# rekursiv.ai -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Joshua V. Dillon spent 13 years at Google Research and DeepMind, while Dan Kondratyuk led Luma AI’s World Models group and authored an ICML 2024 Best Paper. That makes founder conviction rekursiv.ai’s highest dimension; autonomous AI research infrastructure—already occupied by Sakana AI, Periodic Labs, Edison Scientific, and Lila Sciences—is rekursiv.ai’s lowest. The analytical signature is an elite missionary-researcher asset bolted to the most factory-farmed financing category in 2026: unusually credible people pursuing an unusually consensus-shaped venture.

**Drop.io** is the high rung for asymmetric conviction: years of operating through an illegible market until repeated tacks produced a Facebook acquisition. Rekursiv.ai shares that crank-turning mechanism—eight system attempts, 984 experiments over 30 days, and 684 more experiments before reaching 100% on the Sudoku stress test. Rekursiv.ai diverges because those tacks establish technical obsession, not yet customer obsession: no paying researcher, recurring workflow, or manual Wizard-of-Oz service appears in the dossier. Dillon and Kondratyuk are missionaries, but rekursiv.ai has not shown that anyone outside their laboratory needs the mission.

**OpenAI-of-X** is the low-rung failure mechanism for category fit: foundation-model exposure repackaged as a startup at precisely the moment everyone agrees the category matters. Rekursiv.ai is more substantive than an agent shell—235 ARC experiments and published evidence tracing matter—but rekursiv.ai still sits inside the AI-scientist capital race against Sakana AI’s $412M, Periodic Labs’ $300M, and Lila Sciences’ $550M. None of the unstated sleeves load: no pre-consensus crypto economics, DTC margin, prescription rail, creator asset, holdco, or manual-service wedge. “AI scientists whose breakthroughs accelerate the next” is technically impressive and allocation-theoretically backwards for me.

**$5M raised from Y Combinator** is not the normal $500K pre-seed financing tack; absent disclosed valuation or visible tier-one passes, it reads closer to a hot AI round than value-seed mispricing. The counterfactual is simple: rekursiv.ai would move into my invest range if rekursiv.ai showed that major funds passed, the SAFE remained below the batch median, or the capital purchased a proprietary asset unavailable to Sakana AI and Edison Scientific. None appears here, so the default nine points for silence actually overstates the evidence.

**Forty-eight-hour ARC experiments** reveal how rekursiv.ai’s capital structure could fail over the next 18 months. Each new customer workload would multiply model and experiment costs before rekursiv.ai has published pricing, revenue, or proof that consumption margins improve with use; meanwhile Apache-2.0 releases such as sagent and configgle leak some learning back to other builders. A proprietary corpus of failed hypotheses could eventually compound into an illegible asset worth financing, but rekursiv.ai currently compounds compute expense faster than defensibility. That is a five-round regatta unless the research loop becomes dramatically cheaper or customers finance it through high-margin contracts.

**John Fish / Bookshelved** had owned distribution before Slow supplied capital; the creator and the acquisition channel were the same asset. Rekursiv.ai has the opposite profile on this axis: Kondratyuk has roughly 2,000 X followers and a 1,200-star repository, yet rekursiv.ai’s organization has 14 followers and sagent has 25 stars. Those credentials can recruit researchers, but rekursiv.ai does not have a community on its cap table, and the financing announcement’s 31 likes does not become distribution merely because the founders are respected.

**235 ARC experiments** support a specific 36-month forecast: by July 2029, rekursiv.ai will have pivoted from a broad autonomous-scientist platform into enterprise ML evaluation and experiment orchestration, reaching $3M–$8M ARR while remaining independent. Rekursiv.ai’s high-conviction founders should find a useful product through repeated tacks, but rekursiv.ai’s weak sleeve fit, commoditizing stack, and capitalized competitors make ownership of the broader “AI scientist” category unlikely.

**71.4%–75.5% ARC-AGI-1 accuracy** is the strongest bull objection because it says rekursiv.ai may already possess the research engine rather than merely pitching one. The Pinterest exception supports paying for missionary founders, and the Ro exception shows that capital intensity can become a moat when unavoidable complexity blocks entrants; Dillon and Kondratyuk are credible enough to trigger both arguments. The objection loses today because rekursiv.ai’s results remain company-run public benchmarks, literal transfer from Sudoku failed on ARC, no customer revenue is disclosed, and open-source competitors attack the same software boundary. I would flip to Invest if rekursiv.ai secured three unaffiliated ML laboratories paying at least $250K ARR each, with repeat improvements across two distinct research domains.
