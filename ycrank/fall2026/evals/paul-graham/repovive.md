# Repovive -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The question I cannot answer about Repovive is what the founders did when something important broke. Their programming extension was delisted on August 21, 2026. Browser-based contests continued afterward, but the dossier does not establish that these were a workaround for the delisting, or describe how quickly the founders responded. I cannot turn that sequence into evidence of resourcefulness. This matters because I am investing in the people’s ability to get through the next obstacle. The most important part of my investment case remains unproved.

The strongest argument for writing a check is the reported **$100K in contracts across two formalization RL environments**. A lab buying an environment is a much better signal than an audience applauding research. Repovive has identified a narrow customer type with a specific job: obtaining training material for machine-checkable reasoning. The separate **$200K coding-data LOI** adds provisional evidence of demand. But two environments need not mean two customers, and neither collected cash nor repeat purchases are documented. The reported 12,000-person medalist community could help supply difficult tasks; it does not establish that labs will keep buying them.

There is enough technical substance to take the company seriously. Repovive reports an **8.3-percentage-point pass-rate improvement** using GRPO on Leanstral 1.5. Its earlier extension also reached 879 installs, establishing that the team has shipped functioning software. The formalization result deserves partial credit while benchmark publication is pending. My pattern of backing technical founders with concrete proof applies here only provisionally: an internal improvement leaves important questions about reproducibility and specification quality unanswered. Related research demonstrates specification hacking. A solver can improve its measured result without improving the property a customer actually needs verified.

The founders have relevant experience: Shayan worked on Lean-verified datasets, and Amir worked on model post-training at Google and YouTube. That makes their choice intelligible. The dossier does not describe the personal encounter that produced this particular idea or the neglected operational burden they learned to handle. Likewise, the movement from educational tools documented in December 2025 to lab-facing work by November 2026 could represent good learning, but the observation that caused the change is missing. I give both origin and adaptability their absent-evidence defaults. There is also no evidence that the cofounder relationship was hastily assembled or that sales depend on an unspecified major partner. The Cognition contest sponsorship alone establishes neither red flag.

I would pass on the evidence available. The objection to my decision is substantial: Repovive may already have found paying demand for expertise its founders genuinely possess, while competitors such as Harmonic pursue a different immediate product. My exceptions allow working products, unmistakable pull, or independently validated proof to override an uncertain founder prior. Here, the contracts are promising, but delivery, renewal, and independent technical validation remain undocumented; they do not yet supply that override. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: **a referenceable lab renewal accompanied by a dated account of the customer’s blocking problem, the founders’ shipped workaround, and the resulting continued use**. That would connect the commercial promise to the founder behavior I need to see.
