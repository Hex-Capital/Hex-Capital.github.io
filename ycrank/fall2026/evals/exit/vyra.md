# Vyra -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Vyra’s strongest path. A video-editing vendor such as Adobe could buy Vyra’s reference-guided short-form editor to bring creators and their recurring editing workflows into its own product. Vyra combines editable timelines, searchable footage, and reusable style references. Ownership could be worth more to a buyer than building or licensing the editing features if Vyra brings a retained paying audience that the buyer would struggle to acquire on its own. The memo provides no buyer interest or measured benefit to a buyer. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

The memo records published subscription prices, a shipped hosted editing integration, and 14 iPhone ratings. Vyra displays a count of 104,982 creators without defining the count, and its founders report more than 40 million views on Vyra-edited content. Those claims leave paid conversion and retention unmeasured. A plausible seed milestone is a six-month paid cohort showing repeat use of stored footage or style references and stronger retention among creators who reuse them. Vyra could measure that with its existing product. The memo gives no burn, runway, or funded test budget to establish the capital needed to reach buyer-relevant scale. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*)

A software acquisition is a plausible structure. Vyra uses revocable, project-scoped authorization for external agents, and the memo leaves IP assignment and any rights or consents needed to carry creator projects into a buyer’s product unverified. The other routes lack independent support. The memo provides no cash generation to underwrite a sponsor buyout, no demand for eligible shares in a secondary or tender, no operating scale or reporting readiness for an IPO, and no excess cash or payout commitment for dividends or buybacks. The reported SAFE investment provides a financing signal. (*Key Risks*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

Actual terms for the modeled holder are unavailable. Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million for the holder, or $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership. The equity hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% case requires $88.3 million in enterprise value before fees, senior claims, or investor taxes. The memo quantifies none of those deductions or provides a basis for an acquisition price range. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

At year eight, a hypothetical $25 million sale yields $600,000 gross to the holder, or 1.2×. A hypothetical $100 million sale yields $2.85 million, or 5.7×, using the same net debt and ownership assumptions. If Vyra’s growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public-market scale remains out of reach, and the company reinvests all available cash without a secondary or payout, the holder realizes $0 and retains the shares. These scenarios show what sale prices would clear the hurdle and how continued private operation could leave the holder without cash.

### Countercase

Descript already supports editing through MCP, Adobe has previewed third-party agent control inside Premiere, and OpusClip offers reusable editing recipes. A buyer could serve much of the same need inside its existing product. If creators can readily move their workflow or have little reason to keep paying for Vyra, a buyer has little incentive to pay for its user base or stored projects. The substitution evidence weakens the acquisition case, while the memo leaves open whether Vyra’s retained customers could justify a sale that meets the holder’s objective. That uncertainty supports Watch. The Pass gate requires a supported structural block or materially inadequate proceeds under the strongest credible economics. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Vyra show that repeat use of stored footage and style references retains enough paying creators to support an acquisition above the holder’s modeled hurdle? A six-month paid cohort paired with a buyer-specific estimate of the incremental subscription or retention value from owning that workflow would answer it. A defensible price range that clears the hurdle at plausible diluted ownership would move the action to Reach out.
