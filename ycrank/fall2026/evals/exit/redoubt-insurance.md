# Redoubt Insurance -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition by a transportation insurance brokerage** is the strongest path. A buyer could acquire Redoubt’s commission-producing fleet book, its account histories, and the workflows used to place and service policies. Ownership could be worth a premium if those assets transfer together and measurably improve renewal economics or reduce servicing costs across the buyer’s existing book. Redoubt describes a shared account record and reports one customer saving $56,000 on insurance within two days. That testimonial supports customer value. The buyer’s potential savings and the advantage of owning the workflow still need evidence. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)

The founder reported 19 accounts after the October launch and $37,600 in expected annual net commissions. The memo records a reported $500,000 accelerator investment. Those signals support testing the first renewal cohort, with runway, realized renewal revenue, and servicing costs still unknown. The acquisition case assumes Redoubt remains a brokerage. Taking underwriting risk would introduce separate authorization and capital requirements. Rights to transfer the software, account data, and carrier relationships are unverified. (Market & Traction; Key Facts; Founders & Team; Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The memo supplies no terms for the check being evaluated. Using the thesis’s illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year target is $2.5 million in gross cash, or 5×. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The expected commissions provide an operating baseline. The memo supplies no usable acquisition pricing evidence. (Key Facts; Market & Traction)

No independent fallback earns credit above the default. (Market & Traction; Key Facts)

### Countercase

A transportation brokerage could buy comparable intake and quoting automation from a vendor such as Covera and keep its own book and carrier relationships. If that delivers comparable servicing economics, a buyer may value Redoubt mainly for its commission book. The reported customer savings leave both the ownership premium and the book’s renewal value unresolved. This supports Watch. The memo establishes neither equivalent results from the vendor substitute nor a price ceiling that would trigger a structural Pass. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Redoubt’s first renewal cohort show a servicing advantage that a transportation brokerage would pay to own? Realized renewal commissions, retention, and servicing costs benchmarked against a vendor-equipped brokerage would answer it. Move to Reach out if those results support a buyer’s economic case for ownership and a plausible path to at least $83.3 million in distributable equity within eight years at the assumed 3% stake.
