# STASIS -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an eyewear brand is the strongest supported route. STASIS says its glasses detect eye fatigue and respond with automatic tint, break prompts, and screen adjustments. An eyewear brand could use that combination to add a differentiated wellness feature. The founder reports discussions with eyewear brands, but the memo records no measured benefit for a partner or evidence that a brand would pay to own the product. (The Idea)

The memo records prototype coverage, an open reservation checkout, and YC backing. March 2027 is the company’s scheduled first shipment. A plausible next milestone is to ship the first batch, measure paid conversion and sustained use, and validate fatigue detection in everyday conditions. Getting there would require capital for manufacturing, fulfillment, and field validation. Refundable deposits leave paid demand uncertain, and the memo gives no confirmed budget or runway for the test. (Market & Traction; Key Facts; Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The illustrative scenarios below assume a cash sale within eight years, full participation by the holder, and pro rata distribution after debt and deal adjustments, with no senior preference or deferred consideration.

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40M less $5M in assumed debt and deal adjustments = $35M | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M less $10M in assumed debt and deal adjustments = $90M | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operations with cash reinvested and no share sale or payout | $0 (0×) |

At $90 million in distributable equity, a sale covering only 80% of the holder’s stake would pay $2.16 million (4.32×). The memo supplies no basis for an acquisition price, leaves data ownership and transfer rights unverified, and identifies no independent route to holder liquidity. Proceeds, feasibility, and alternatives therefore receive their defaults. (Defensibility; Key Facts)

### Countercase

An eyewear brand could build or source much of the functionality itself. The memo identifies available inward-tracking components and published blink-tracking code, while software-only blink training addresses part of the customer problem. STASIS reports over 90% accuracy for fatigue and cognitive load without publishing its reference measure or validation protocol. If real-world performance is easy to match, a buyer has little reason to pay a premium. That weakens the acquisition case, but the memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a supportable sale-price range that falls short even in the strongest credible case. The Pass gate remains untriggered. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a production pilot with an eyewear brand show enough incremental value over readily available alternatives to support an acquisition that clears the holder’s hurdle? Measured field performance and sustained paid use, paired with the brand’s quantified economics for buying versus building, would answer it. Move to Reach out if those results support a credible path to roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity. Move to Pass if they show that the strongest credible buyer economics leave the holder materially short.
