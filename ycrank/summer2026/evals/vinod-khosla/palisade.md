# Palisade -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Palisade has demonstrated that its software can move commerce: more than $2.7 million in marketplace GMV since the Summer 2026 batch began, with Shorebreak and Vaulted Atelier live in production. That is unusually informative for a one-person pre-seed company. But moving purchases between listings is not yet a consequence-of-success case. The dossier quantifies a $3.0 billion conversational-AI market, not the sales-labor cost, abandoned-commerce pool, or marketplace failure rate Palisade could reduce by an order of magnitude. The ambition currently reads as better conversion, not a 100x societal or economic discontinuity.

The product is broader than a chatbot. Buyer agents retain preferences across visits, seller agents assist supply, and staff agents propose listing, pricing, interface, and code changes while connecting to catalogs, booking engines, property-management systems, and CRMs. That breadth could make Palisade an operating layer rather than a point feature. Yet Rep AI, Constructor, and Nosto already address adjacent discovery and conversion workflows, while general-purpose computer-use APIs provide interface control. Palisade supplies no measured conversion lift, cost reduction, accuracy advantage, or task-completion benchmark against any of them. Functional breadth is not technical discontinuity.

The bull case is Jonathan Salama. Amazon Traffic & Marketing, consumer-shopping agents at Sage AI Labs, and roughly one million subscribers for Lulus TV put product, distribution, and marketplace behavior in one founder. Producing $2.7 million of attributed GMV with a solo team suggests a doer who ships, not a pontificator. This resembles the exception I would make for an execution-heavy financial or marketplace wedge: a modest initial mechanism can expand into infrastructure when it attacks a large existing value pool. The material difference is that Palisade has not shown either the value pool it can capture or evidence that its agent layer compounds faster than incumbent products can copy it.

The largest kill risk is replication. Persistent histories and integrations may create switching costs, but the dossier establishes no exclusive data rights, proprietary model, or performance learning curve. The other risks—customer-data exposure and erroneous checkout, repricing, interface, or code actions—are mitigated through human approval and handoff, which limits damage but may also limit labor displacement. Palisade names these hazards without supplying the decisive experiment, numerical threshold, cost, timing, or stop condition that capital should fund first.

Software gross margins make unsubsidized operation plausible, but plausibility is not a scale model. Prices, Palisade’s revenue, inference and integration costs, support burden, and GMV attribution methodology are undisclosed. A system that “drives” $2.7 million of GMV could be highly valuable, or it could be receiving credit for transactions that search and merchandising software would have captured anyway. The red flag is not weak traction; it is confusing attributed volume with incremental value while competing through integrations and implementation around commodity models.

I pass today. Within 36 months, my base case is that Palisade pivots toward a narrower managed sales-automation product for high-value vertical marketplaces rather than becoming their general operating layer. The single flip condition is a controlled benchmark across at least ten marketplaces showing Palisade produces five times the incremental gross profit per software dollar of the best available incumbent, after subtracting discounts, human intervention, inference costs, and agent-error losses. That would convert an impressive deployment story into a technical and economic discontinuity worth purchasing.
