# Agnost AI -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Shubham Palriwala and Parth Ajmera are an unusually strong founder pair whose weakest signal is not ability but origin: Agnost AI processes more than 1 million events daily, yet the dossier never says either founder originally built Agnost AI to solve his own recurring problem. Eight years of friendship, open-source contributions, data-pipeline experience, and a product already opening useful pull requests make Agnost AI look like two hackers who can build almost anything. The unresolved question is whether they had to build this particular thing.

**Stripe** (2010) is the high rung for co-founder configuration: two brothers with years of shared history, complementary abilities, and commerce problems they had already attacked together. Agnost AI has the first two ingredients because Palriwala and Ajmera are childhood friends of eight years, with Palriwala covering analytics and open source while Ajmera brings Microsoft pipelines and GPU engineering. Agnost AI diverges from Stripe because the dossier establishes friendship, not years of building together before the startup; the relationship is strong enough for the check, but the working chemistry remains less proven than the personal chemistry.

**Loopt** (2005) is the low rung for an idea insufficiently grounded in immediate founder pain: mobile location was early, weakly pulled by users, and rescued mainly by Sam Altman’s force of personality. Agnost AI avoids Loopt’s infrastructure-timing problem because conversational agents already generate more than 1 million daily events inside Agnost AI, but Agnost AI never shows Palriwala or Ajmera personally operating an agent, suffering through failed conversations, and building the first version for themselves. Cisco analytics, Formbricks, and Microsoft pipelines are adjacent experience. Adjacent is not organic. If Agnost AI’s founders discovered the problem through customer interviews rather than their own work, the idea could become a good business without becoming a Dropbox-like inevitability.

More than 1 million daily events and 16 of 18 Lopus AI pull requests merged are the best evidence that Agnost AI’s founders are animals rather than merely credentialed programmers. Over the next 18 months, that resourcefulness can compound: more conversations produce better failure clusters, accepted fixes generate outcome data, and outcome data makes the next fix easier to trust. Agnost AI has also raised $250,000 from Entrepreneurs First and recruited users associated with Exa, Corgi Insurance, and Odysser before Summer 2026. What is missing is an Airbnb cereal-box moment—some bizarre, unscalable act showing that Palriwala and Ajmera will manufacture survival when the obvious channel stops working.

Enterprise VPC deployments, audit logs, custom retention, and production-conversation access are the beginnings of a real schlep, but Agnost AI has not yet proved it enjoys the schlep. Agnost AI could move up a full tier here if Palriwala and Ajmera personally handled security reviews, regulated-data deployments, and bespoke integrations that LangSmith did not want to support. Without that evidence, Agnost AI remains mostly a polished software product in one of the most crowded parts of AI. The unpleasant enterprise work may become the moat, but presently it is a pricing-table clause.

**Reddit** (2005) is the useful single-anchor parallel for hacker genealogy: technical lineage appeared before the business looked important. Palriwala contributed to Bitcoin, OWASP, and Linux Foundation projects and was Formbricks’ first engineer; Ajmera built Spark pipelines at Microsoft and led graphics and GPU engineering at Infurnia. Agnost AI therefore passes the hacker test independently of its AI vocabulary. Agnost AI lacks a visible Aaron Swartz-scale artifact or a public Ajmera repository, but shipped infrastructure and upstream contributions are substantially stronger evidence than two computer-science degrees.

More than 1 million events per day makes a falsifiable 36-month forecast possible: by July 2029, Agnost AI will reach $4 million to $8 million in annual recurring revenue as a conversation-product-analytics layer, but Agnost AI will not become the default general observability platform because its founders’ strongest evidence concerns building and distribution rather than a uniquely organic insight. The mechanism is customer-specific conversation history plus accepted-pull-request feedback, which can create a valuable narrow product even while LangSmith owns the broader agent-development surface.

**LangSmith** is the objection that stings: LangChain already has framework distribution, $260 million in funding, failure clustering, trace-to-code diagnosis, and proposed fixes, so Agnost AI could be reduced to a feature before customer-specific data has time to compound. Agnost AI’s answer is behavioral rather than rhetorical—Odysser received 1,247 surfaced feature requests, Lopus AI merged 16 of 18 proposed pull requests, and Agnost AI already handles more than 1 million daily events. Those are artifacts, not a market slide, so I would invest. I would flip to Pass if, by July 2027, Agnost AI still cannot disclose at least 20 paying customers with six-month retention and the Lopus AI acceptance rate remains a one-customer anecdote rather than a repeatable result.
