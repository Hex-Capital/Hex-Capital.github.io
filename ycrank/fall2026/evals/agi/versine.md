# Versine -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Versine is a plausible seed fit. It says an assistant can initiate up to 100 person-to-person transfers per prompt, subject to passkey approval or standing rules, and send bank claim links to recipients without accounts. One scenario is a recurring group organizer who currently settles only larger expenses because individual transfers take too much time. Batch execution could make smaller, more frequent settlements worthwhile if it reduces human effort per correctly completed transfer after approvals, retries, and corrections. The memo provides no like-for-like delivery trial or evidence that this segment has begun making those transfers. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

The launch recorded 182 Product Hunt upvotes and an Android listing with 5+ downloads. The company offered a $100 sending credit, and repeat use without incentives remains unknown. The founders’ experience in payments, observability, and AI tooling is relevant to the launch, while reliable handling of payment exceptions remains untested in the memo. (*Market & Traction*, *The Idea*, *Founders & Team*)

Under the disclosed pricing, gross fee revenue per completed transfer would be $1 multiplied by the number of paid instant payouts per completed transfer. Standard payouts and transfers are free. The memo gives no paid payout rate or marginal costs for identity checks, settlement, support, and payment errors. That leaves the contribution from additional transfers unknown. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Versine may make existing payments more convenient without creating a new class of transactions. With equally capable assistants, a payment incumbent could add comparable approvals and claim links. Versine’s saved rules and contacts could give users a reason to stay, but there is no evidence of sustained use or preference at parity. Bridge handles identity verification and bank payouts, and the memo identifies no scarce rail Versine controls to support Path A. Both Path B conditions remain unproven, so the outreach gate stays closed and a seed-stage trial can still change the decision. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will recurring group organizers make and pay for settlements they previously skipped, then keep choosing Versine when a competing payment workflow offers equivalent assistant capabilities? An unsubsidized trial should compare human time per correctly completed settlement with the manual workflow, including approvals and exceptions, then measure repeat paid use against the matched alternative. Verified labor savings and repeat preference at pricing that covers delivery costs would support Reach out if the updated score clears 65. A credible trial showing no newly viable settlements or no reason to choose Versine at parity would move the action to Pass.
