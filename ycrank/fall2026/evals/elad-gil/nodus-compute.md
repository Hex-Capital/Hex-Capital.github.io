# Nodus Compute -- Elad Gil Evaluation

AI teams running open-weight models now have more infrastructure choices, and Nodus Compute is selling execution across those choices; the controlling variable is whether it can lower **cost per successfully completed workload, after fees and recovery overhead**, enough to become the customer’s default execution layer. That is a plausible market thesis. The dossier identifies cost-to-completion routing, but does not establish the savings threshold that makes switching worthwhile or the economics that let Nodus retain a meaningful margin. Cheap GPU supply alone does not answer either question.

The strongest economic evidence is the founder’s October 2026 claim of greater than 30% compute savings. That would matter if measured across comparable completed workloads. The evidence comes through a mirrored search snippet, however, without a baseline or measurement methodology. Published H100 SXM supply at $2.600 per hour is explicitly before Nodus fees. Maturing vLLM and SGLang infrastructure creates a credible adoption mechanism, but the claimed savings do not yet demonstrate how much value reaches the customer versus how much Nodus captures.

The product has more substance than a pricing dashboard. Nodus executes containers, restores saved files on replacement machines, and documents checkpoint integrity checks and generation fencing. Those controls put it inside an operational workflow with state and responsibility. Better models could increase demand for that infrastructure without reproducing the execution system itself. The limitation is important: customer code must reload checkpoints, ephemeral workloads are not resumed, and pilot service has no default SLA. The team has documented useful recovery machinery; it has not demonstrated dependable completion across the workloads needed to support a broad execution platform.

The bull case is that ten startup pilots expose enough real workloads to turn this machinery into a compounding placement advantage. That fits my pattern of funding infrastructure around a technology discontinuity, and an execution relationship could become distribution for adjacent optimization products. But ten founder-reported pilots establish experimentation, with payment, repeat usage, and expansion still unspecified. Meanwhile, Modal reports more than $1 billion in annualized revenue and already pools capacity across clouds; SF Compute supports capacity resale. This market is crystallizing. Nodus needs evidence that its completed-workload economics survive those alternatives. Its proposed workload-to-research feedback loop remains a research agenda, so I would not award it a data moat today.

Kotra and Varshney have relevant infrastructure exposure, including Varshney’s Google cost-optimization work. Internships do not establish second-time founder learning or costly personal commitment, so that dimension stays at the missing-evidence default. Nor does the dossier support a frontier-research exception to weak commercial pull: it documents neither field-defining research nor a corresponding capital path. The physical-margin exception is also unsupported because Nodus’s retained margin is unknown. I would pass on writing a personal check today, while keeping the company under observation. One result would change that: **an independently auditable 90-day cohort showing at least five paying customers repeatedly achieving over 30% lower cost per equivalent completed workload, after Nodus fees and retries, with positive Nodus contribution margin.** That would connect the product’s strongest evidence—execution control—to the economic proposition that makes the market work.
