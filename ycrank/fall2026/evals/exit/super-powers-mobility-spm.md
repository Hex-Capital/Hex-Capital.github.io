# Super Powers Mobility (SPM) -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is SPM’s strongest Fall 2026 exit path. A defense power supplier such as EnerSys could buy SPM to add integrated vehicle battery packs, power electronics, and power export. The memo records EnerSys’s $208 million acquisition of Bren-Tronics, which sold portable military batteries and charging systems. Ownership could make sense if SPM’s validated designs and production relationships save a buyer measurable integration time across multiple vehicle programs. That benefit remains an inference. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is a repeat fleet production order following accepted, paid deliveries, with documented unit margins and a funded capacity plan. SPM reports participation in four federal contracts and bookings into February 2027. It reports $421,815 in realized revenue for one month and separately describes over $400,000 in contracted revenue for one month. The realized figure speaks more directly to sales, but the memo leaves the figures unreconciled and repeat demand unverified. The $1.3 million pre-seed preceded the pivot, and the capital required for production scale-up remains open. In-house design supports a plausible asset transfer, while IP ownership and any customer or federal contract consents remain unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash target within eight years. The target requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% hurdle is $88.3 million in enterprise value. At year eight, a $25 million sale yields $600,000 gross (1.2×) and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×), using simple pro rata allocation before investor taxes. SPM’s debt, fees, and senior claims are unverified. Bren-Tronics had approximately $100 million in annual sales at the reported $208 million purchase price, so the deal provides only thin pricing evidence for SPM. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and SPM reinvests all available cash for eight years, the holder realizes $0 and retains the shares. The Inventus Power buyout establishes sponsor activity in the sector, while the memo leaves SPM’s sustainable cash generation and financing capacity unestablished. Eligible secondary demand, a public resale path, and funded payouts are also unevidenced. A wind-down recovery is unquantifiable without assets, obligations, and preferences. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

A prime can contract for SPM’s engineering and buy packs as needed, while Inventus already supplies complete military battery and power systems. If SPM’s edge stays confined to custom jobs, a buyer has little reason to pay for ownership. That makes the acquisition price the decisive gap. The memo leaves open whether a substitute can match SPM’s vehicle integration and whether a sale could clear the holder’s hurdle, so the Pass gate does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A buyer-backed estimate of the time and cost saved by owning the system, tied to a valuation range that clears the hurdle at plausible diluted ownership, would move the action to **Reach out**.
