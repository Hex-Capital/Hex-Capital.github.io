# Munito AI -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years forward, I can imagine a European business switching models without asking permission to move its confidential work. Munito AI offers a small piece of that world: familiar APIs, an EU endpoint, and zero prompt/output retention by default. But imagining the destination is my contribution. The dossier does not establish the intervening unlocks or an early behavior that makes that future more inevitable. Regional inference is already available from competitors. I need to distinguish a useful service arriving today from an artifact of what comes next.

Alexey Eryshev is the strongest reason to keep looking. Leading inference at Mistral and building Le Chat’s Deep Research are substantive operating accomplishments, not decorative employer names. He has worked inside the machinery Munito must make reliable. That earns meaningful founder credit. It does not establish the independently pursued obsession, early experiments, or ability to recruit first believers that would make me follow him into a difficult infrastructure bet almost regardless of the initial product. Those are unanswered questions, not evidence that he lacks them.

Why should a developer need a sales conversation to choose where inference runs? Munito’s self-service EU offering removes a concrete gate in its comparisons with Together AI and Fireworks. Default zero retention also gives customers a useful starting position instead of another configuration chore. That returns some control. The economics remain less established: the dossier does not quantify procurement time saved, a gatekeeper’s rent, or the cost of operating an equivalent service. Pricing DeepSeek-V4-Flash at $0.11 per million input tokens and $0.28 per million output tokens tells me what Munito charges, not what advantage it owns.

The strongest case against passing is that this founder may know exactly where inference providers waste money and frustrate customers. Munito launched its free tier on October 7 and paid tiers on October 15; by October 27, demanding a long revenue history would be silly. Compatible interfaces and published agent integrations create practical ways to try the product. I will back infrastructure before revenue when the technical artifact and founder evidence make the destination convincing. Here, however, the self-improving inference stack remains under development, and the dossier documents no repeated customer action. Launch dates establish availability; they do not establish that anyone has entrusted a recurring workload to it.

Munito fits my pattern of looking beneath applications, but stack position alone is insufficient. There is no demonstrated scarce asset or serving advantage yet, while Mistral already offers regional endpoints and third-party models. The privacy architecture deserves credit for explicit boundaries: unpinned global requests can leave the EU, and account and billing services are excluded from residency coverage. Those disclosures do not establish a severe privacy red flag. They do mean “private by default” needs to survive contact with the customer’s actual configuration. Missing audits and standard service guarantees matter when that customer tries to move sensitive work into production.

I pass on Munito AI today. The founder’s relevant execution history is substantially stronger than the evidence for a distinctive future or controlled bottleneck. My pre-revenue exceptions do not bridge that gap: this dossier lacks the combination of demonstrated technical differentiation and committed user behavior that would justify one. One verifiable result would change my decision: a named customer’s sustained paid production deployment demonstrating that Munito’s own serving technology delivers a repeatable cost advantage over an equivalent EU, zero-retention alternative. Then I could underwrite something the company owns.
