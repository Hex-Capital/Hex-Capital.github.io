# Hickory & Company -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with its initial bank and fintech buyers, Hickory’s most plausible growth path is to reuse its investigation workflow across institutions. Hickory describes agents that retrieve cited evidence, draft case documentation against institution-specific criteria, and log actions for human approval. These functions could travel to comparable compliance teams if local policies can be configured and customers can use the available data-processing options. The memo leaves both conditions unresolved. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The documented buying path runs through demos and organization-provisioned accounts under written agreements. Segment-specific outreach and a banking conference give Hickory channels it can reuse, while the ordinary buyer still depends on a sales-led purchase. Recurring investigations, configured policies, and accumulated audit histories could support continued use. Institution-level licensing is an inference, and the memo establishes neither billing cadence nor a way for an existing customer’s spend to grow. The October 30 launch establishes public availability of the pitch, with paid production use and revenue status unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under a licensing scenario, incremental contribution would be the next institution’s fee less attributable sales, setup, support, hosted processing, and storage costs. Reusing policy configurations could reduce setup effort if the same configurations work across institutions. The memo provides no fees, workload costs, or deployment effort to assess that contribution. Hickory’s claimed 10× queue speedup has no published benchmark method in the memo and speaks to a potential customer benefit. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Hickory’s monitoring-replacement pitch faces concrete limits. Its terms exclude payment-rail integrations, say the software is unsuitable for consumer financial-account information, and allow customers to restrict or disable hosted processing that may occur outside their jurisdiction. If routine investigations require those inputs or processing options, expansion could require custom integration or deployment work for each institution or market. The structural downside gate remains open because the narrower evidence-gathering and case-documentation workflow is plausible, and the memo leaves its dependency on that work unresolved. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a bank outside the U.S. run a paid production investigation workflow on the current product through policy configuration and standard setup? A deployment record showing what changed, how data was processed, and how much Hickory staff time setup took would answer it. Bounded setup would sustain Watch and strengthen the portability case. Evidence that this workflow routinely requires a market-specific rebuild or ongoing bespoke delivery would change the action to Pass.
