# Kebra -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

$6.1B in field-service software, growing to $13.8B by 2033, gives Kebra room to move; zero evidence of user-added network value gives Kebra nothing that compounds across customers. The signature is a large-market workflow company with single-customer economics. Kebra can build a useful business here. Useful is not the same as asymmetric. Software scales, but distribution and data architecture determine whether upside scales faster than effort.

Uber began with black cars and expanded into UberX because the market contained multiple pivots and every expansion enlarged the network. Kebra shares the broad operational surface: documentation can lead into warranty claims, parts procurement, billing, diagnostics, and eventually the proposed “Company Brain.” The analogy stops where Kebra’s $6.1B field-service-management category narrows around home-services administration and ServiceTitan-dependent workflows. Kebra has pivot-room, but not Uber-shaped optionality; the adjacent products remain enterprise software sold to the same operators.

Perplexity’s low rung on network compounding was survivable because extreme founder-specific AI knowledge and permissionless product distribution overrode the missing marketplace. Kebra has no equivalent override yet. One plumbing operator adopting Kebra does not automatically improve Kebra for the next HVAC operator, and no marketplace connects technicians, suppliers, manufacturers, or warranty providers. The inferred Company Brain creates switching costs inside each account, not compounding value between accounts. Kebra is single-sided SaaS until the architecture proves otherwise.

ServiceTitan’s $961M FY2026 revenue reveals both the opportunity and Kebra’s leverage problem. Over 18 months, Kebra’s code can process more recordings at low marginal cost, but acquiring accounts through 30-minute demos and maintaining integrations with ServiceTitan, QuickBooks, warranty portals, suppliers, Gmail, and Messages compounds labor and gatekeeper dependence. Kebra’s dossier shows none of the unstated pattern I look for: no open-source pull, creator distribution, organic technical audience, or free self-serve product. Zero YC job postings means Kebra has not yet committed the premature-SDR red flag, but the architecture already points toward sales leverage rather than permissionless leverage.

Luca Hadife built multimodal AI systems at Microsoft, while Mohamed Hosny taught computer architecture and signal processing at Berkeley and worked on data-center switches at Arista. Kebra therefore clears generic technical competence and has credible audio-processing adjacency. Kebra does not yet clear specific knowledge: neither founder is documented as having closed field-service jobs, administered warranties, or operated a trades business. The score flips into invest territory if Hadife or Hosny can show a shipped artifact or years of lived workflow knowledge that incumbents cannot hire from the same Berkeley talent pool. Specific knowledge is discovered through obsession, not awarded through credentials.

Notion was built from Ivan Zhao’s long-running obsession with rebuilding tools, giving the founder and product the same source code. Kebra instead begins with two technically strong Berkeley graduates and no public repos, prior field-service employment, clean prior venture, or long-form domain writing. Kebra’s two founders studied together from 2022 through 2026, which supports energy and relationship durability, and nothing raises an integrity concern. Intelligence reads positive; energy is plausible; integrity is simply untested. I can underwrite a small check without perfect information, but I cannot yet call Kebra a demonstrated 10-year partnership.

ServiceTitan’s overlapping AI back-office products make my 36-month forecast specific: by July 2029, Kebra reaches roughly $2M-$5M ARR as a job-close automation layer and is acquired by ServiceTitan for under $75M, rather than becoming an independent network. Kebra’s recording-to-action wedge can win customers faster than ServiceTitan ships every specialized workflow, while Kebra’s reliance on ServiceTitan interfaces makes the incumbent the natural buyer and limits bargaining power. That is a credible outcome. It is not the 1x-downside, 10,000x-upside geometry I buy.

Rilla’s estimated $70M ARR is the strongest bull case: field audio can be a much larger wedge than it first appears, and Kebra goes beyond coaching by executing warranty, parts, and billing work. Perplexity proves that a single-sided product can become an exceptional investment when permissionless distribution and rare founder knowledge compensate for the absent network. Kebra has demonstrated neither compensation: the public motion is a scheduled walkthrough, the moat section contains no current defensibility signal, and ServiceTitan already offers overlapping automation. I pass today. I flip if Kebra reaches $1M ARR with more than half of new accounts arriving organically or by referral while cross-customer data measurably improves workflow accuracy.
