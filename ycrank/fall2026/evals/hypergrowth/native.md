# Native -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Reach out  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Native’s strongest path is **A. Product-led adoption**. Small businesses without a marketing team need to decide what to post, produce it, and publish it every week. Native offers a free preview built from the buyer’s website, then sells a subscription that ranks ideas, generates creative, and publishes with optional approval. Paid ads bring buyers to the product. The growth hypothesis is that the preview can turn those buyers into paying publishers with little manual setup, and that recurring publishing can retain them and lead some to add brands or client workspaces. The listed plans support that expansion, while realized upgrades remain unverified. (*The Idea*)

Native reports more than $2M ARR and 2,200+ paying customers across 20+ countries. That is a concrete signal for outreach, though the memo gives no country-level buyer mix or activation data. A second-market test would check whether the same journey works with local language, creative conventions, and channel requirements. For initial fit, HAVN Storage’s company-published case records 9,495 Facebook and Instagram impressions versus 2,322 in the preceding comparison period. It provides a specific usage example, with the effect on sales and the durability of use still open. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Paid ads may be buying a stream of short-lived subscriptions. Native reports that acquisition cost fell from roughly NOK 1,800 to NOK 800 per new customer, but the memo gives no cohort renewal data or cost per active account. Each new buyer adds acquisition spend and may need help with setup. Continued publishing adds generation, publishing, and support costs. Meta also offers overlapping creative tools, and Native’s research leaves conversion lift unmeasured. The pass gate remains open because the memo establishes neither a structural demand ceiling nor proportional costs at scale. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Native show that paid-ad cohorts of small businesses in two countries move from preview to paid publishing with little manual help, then keep publishing and renewing long enough to cover acquisition, generation, publishing, and support costs? Cohort records showing that pattern as acquisition volume rises would support continuing diligence. If the cohorts are too young or the records cannot show the pattern, move to Watch pending a mature cohort.
