# Autoresearch, Inc. -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Autoresearch, Inc. has shown me a research result; it has not shown me what my check buys. Post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and the experiment funded by this round are all undisclosed. YC and Peak XV backing establish neither an auction nor scarce capital. I use my missing-evidence defaults for both financing dimensions. The distinction matters here: successfully running thousands of experiments does not establish that the next dollar reveals a commercially valuable truth.

The technical work deserves attention. Autoresearch reports **4,188 experiments over two days on 50 B200 GPUs**, followed by **25 interleaved confirmation runs** supporting median validation bits-per-byte of **0.9016**. Repeated verification is substantially more persuasive than a selected best run. But the demonstrated mechanism is benchmark improvement through organized search. The investable mechanism would be customers repeatedly paying more for those improvements than they cost to produce. The dossier supplies no observed purchase, renewal, or proprietary channel connecting the two. Paid-plan terms establish how the company can charge, not that anyone has paid.

The strongest argument against passing is the team’s ability to make this operationally difficult service work. Karan Brar and Thomas Boser bring production ML infrastructure experience from Reducto and other employers; Jad Ghalayini adds compilers and formal-verification expertise. Their released research provides a shipped receipt beyond job titles. That combination fits my preference for founders who connect systems that otherwise work poorly together: customer ML operations, automated experimentation, and trustworthy measurement. It earns the strongest dimension score here. Still, the dossier does not establish proprietary customer access or repeated commercial deployments, so I would not treat technical competence as evidence of distribution.

Customer ownership creates the central tradeoff. Running inside customer infrastructure could make Autoresearch useful precisely where sensitive data prevents a generic hosted tool from working. Yet customer ownership of research artifacts and enterprise data restrictions constrain the cross-customer experiment memory that might become its proprietary asset. Meanwhile, Karpathy’s open-source loop supplies a substitute starting point. The team could build a valuable execution business around orchestration and verification, but its durable advantage must survive customers taking the resulting recipes home. My AI exceptions require demonstrated conversion, retained usage, or cash economics strong enough to overcome this concern. Autoresearch’s benchmark is credible technical evidence; it does not yet supply that commercial override.

Delete the next financing and the economics remain unknown: no disclosed margins, burn, runway, or seed-funded break-even plan. Fifty B200 GPUs do not prove bad economics, especially without knowing who pays for compute, but experiment count cannot serve as the cash metric. I also see no evidence of an auction, equal-founder paralysis, or a plan dependent on errors disappearing; those red flags remain unestablished. **I pass on the supplied evidence.** My one verifiable flip condition is a documented seed proposal below **$10 million post-money** funding a **six-month, customer-paid campaign test**, with predefined production-performance thresholds and positive contribution profit after compute and human review. That would connect price, a finite venture moment, and an operating business in one underwritable proposition.
