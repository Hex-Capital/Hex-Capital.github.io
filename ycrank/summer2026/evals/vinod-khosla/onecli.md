# OneCLI -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

ChartDB reached 22.5K stars and more than $10K MRR before Jonathan Fishner and Guy Ben-Aharon pivoted to OneCLI. That makes OneCLI a high-learning-rate, low-required-capital security-infrastructure bet: excellent at relocating toward a newly important boundary, but weak as a Khosla-scale option. At maximum success, credentials stop entering agent processes. Useful. Yet OneCLI currently protects the coming agent economy rather than redirecting it; the economic mechanism improved is secrets management, while no civilizational system clearly breaks.

**Slash** (2024) is the high rung on learning rate because its documented Kanye West pivot exposed founders who changed the thesis when reality changed. OneCLI shares the load-bearing mechanism: Fishner and Ben-Aharon abandoned a working ChartDB product with 22.5K stars and more than $10K MRR to pursue the credential boundary created by autonomous agents. OneCLI diverges because the dossier documents the destination but not the learning event—what became false about ChartDB, what the founders observed, and why OneCLI was the necessary response. That keeps OneCLI just below the Slash rung rather than beside it.

**DoorDash** (2013) failed my capital-fit test: its early roadmap did not require the concentrated $50M-$115M checks that made OpenAI and Commonwealth Fusion my kind of asymmetric bet. OneCLI repeats that low-rung mechanism. OneCLI is Apache-2.0 software priced from $199 per month to $1,499 per month for managed VPC deployment, with no identified training cluster, fleet, plant, drug pipeline, or technical milestone requiring more than $50M. Enterprise security sales may consume capital, but sales expense is not capital-hostile science. I am over-resourced for OneCLI.

The projected agentic-security market reaches $13.52B in 2032, but market growth is not consequence. Over the next 18 months, OneCLI could compound integrations, organization policies, and audit history until OneCLI becomes mandatory egress infrastructure for agents; then credential delivery into workloads begins to disappear. The mechanism presently leaks, however: traffic can bypass the proxy, permitted actions can still cause damage, and gateway-root access exposes decrypted credentials in memory. OneCLI can capture a growing security budget without collapsing the secrets-management regime. That is an optimization play until proven otherwise.

**Physical Intelligence** (2024) required foundation-model, robotics, and physical-world deployment breakthroughs—the technical risk itself repelled consensus capital. OneCLI faces serious engineering problems, including enforced routing, gateway isolation, HSM support, and policy correctness, but OneCLI does not require a comparable miracle. Infisical released an MIT-licensed Agent Vault using the same HTTP-proxy and credential-injection pattern in April 2026. When a funded competitor can reproduce the central architecture within months, the problem may be important while the technical wager remains ordinary.

Jonathan Fishner was Principal Security Architect at Axis Security, while Guy Ben-Aharon worked at Argon Security, Aqua Security, Wix, and Unit 8200. OneCLI therefore reads as expert continuity: security specialists applying the established proxy-and-policy playbook to agents. OneCLI would flip this dimension into an Invest range if Fishner and Ben-Aharon demonstrated that conventional vault architectures are structurally incapable of governing autonomous agents and published evidence that network-layer authorization remains enforceable despite proxy bypass and permitted-action misuse. Today the dossier shows capable experts moving with the market, not instigators contradicting their field.

The claimed 320K downloads, 2.9K GitHub stars, and NanoClaw default integration support a falsifiable forecast: by July 2029, 1Password will acquire OneCLI after OneCLI reaches roughly $2M-$5M ARR, primarily to absorb its agent-gateway distribution rather than because OneCLI became an independent identity platform. High learning rate should let the founders package the emerging boundary quickly; low capital intensity and competitive convergence should make strategic sale more rational than prolonged independence. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself” does not mean every useful product becomes a civilizational company.

NanoClaw’s default integration is the strongest objection to my forecast because defaults can compound into control points, and **DoorDash** proves I sometimes underestimate capital-light systems that reshape an economic flow. OneCLI also has founders who previously built ChartDB to 22.5K stars and more than $10K MRR, so the bull case is that OneCLI becomes the neutral credential standard through developer distribution before Oasis, Astrix, or 1Password understands the channel. The objection stings. It still loses today because Infisical already offers the same proxy architecture, the repository is Apache-2.0, no structural barrier is identified, and no OneCLI revenue or retention evidence is public. I pass. Fifty Fortune 500 companies enforcing all agent egress through OneCLI would flip the verdict because that would demonstrate a new control plane rather than another secrets product.
