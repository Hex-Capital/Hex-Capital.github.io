# RealPact -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I need are missing: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether YC’s $500,000 was genuinely the first scarce check. Leap Year, Dartmouth Venture Studio, and real-estate angels are named, but the dossier supplies neither terms nor evidence that investors dismissed the company. I will not infer an auction; I also will not pretend an undocumented YC round is cheap. The deal gate remains unproven.

The capital plan is equally undefined. RealPact has a plausible experiment available—prove that address-to-contract automation can remain accurate across state-specific forms, MLS permissions, and compliance regimes—but the round is not tied to a dated rollout, accuracy threshold, paid-conversion target, or other pass/fail result. “Nationwide brokerage expansion” is a direction, not a venture moment. I cannot tell what discrete truth this check purchases.

The strongest evidence is that customers are already acting. More than 10 brokerages are running the product, Four Seasons Sotheby’s has a firm-wide pilot, and RealPact reports current-product sales rather than waitlist enthusiasm. Turning an address or voice command into a signature-ready New Hampshire agreement in under five minutes attacks a real workflow that reportedly consumes 30–40 minutes per contract. The bull case stings: those deployments could generate the state templates, integration knowledge, and transaction feedback that eventually become a proprietary operating layer.

But that dark pool is not established yet. No retention, paid-conversion rate, transaction volume, or evidence that RealPact’s accumulated workflow data caused another purchase is disclosed. SkySlope already reaches 900,000 professionals and processes 3 million annual transactions while marketing overlapping AI automation; general voice, vision, and language models commoditize much of the visible product. The relevant exception would require Ando-like operating proof—measured accuracy, complete conversion, and complete retention—not merely the resemblance of an AI workflow product to one. RealPact has promising deployments, but not that override.

The founders have shipped together: CourseMe reached 4,000-plus MAU, reportedly served 90% of Dartmouth students, and was acquired; SignPact then evolved into this product. Those are real software receipts. They are not receipts from operating a brokerage, managing compliance, or controlling MLS access. Customer research and access to Four Seasons partially bridge the gap, but the team has not yet shipped on both sides of the system. The equal co-founder structure also leaves unanswered who has final authority over product, financing, and the liabilities created by contract errors.

The business may be useful without being a good personal angel investment at unknown terms. Revenue exists, but its amount, pricing, gross margin, burn, and route to break-even are absent; state-by-state compliance and SOC 2 work could make the next round structurally necessary. My 36-month base case is that RealPact remains below $3 million ARR and either becomes a SkySlope acquisition target or is compressed into a feature of a broader transaction platform. I would flip to investing if the company documents a non-auctioned price below a $10 million post-money valuation and shows at least 80% paid retention across 10 brokerages after twelve months, with positive contribution margin and a specific seed-funded milestone for entering three additional states. Until then, I pass.
