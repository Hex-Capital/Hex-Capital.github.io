# Rational -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is not revenue. It is what broke. Rational has shipped a substantial product, but the dossier never names an obstacle the founders encountered, the workaround they built, or how quickly it changed the result. Three hundred integrations and firm-specific approval gates imply a great deal of ugly work. They do not prove how Jibril Moinuddin and Christ Xu behave when that work resists them. Xu’s OpenAI, Jane Street, Tencent, and prior-exit credentials make technical ability plausible; credentials are not evidence of resourcefulness.

The technical proof is much better. Rational reports 92% strict exact-return accuracy and 96.3% strict line accuracy on TaxCalcBench TY25, against 58% for the tested GPT-5.6 configuration. Tenace separately reports 98% accuracy on configured reconciliations. This looks like a real deterministic-plus-model system rather than a wrapper with an accounting vocabulary. But the benchmark is company-run, the API remains private during security testing, and accounting errors are unusually asymmetric: 92 correct returns do not make the other eight harmless. I give substantial credit for the artifact, not full credit for independent proof.

There is also a narrow well of users. Tenace’s roughly 50-person team says each person reclaimed about 20 hours per week while revenue run-rate approximately doubled. An anonymized Fortune Global 500 customer reports a 50% reduction in close time, 80% of routine communications handled, and approximately doubled transaction volume without added finance headcount. Those are unusually concrete pre-seed outcomes. The weakness is provenance: both are Rational-published case studies, with no disclosed payments, deployment duration, retention, or repeated expansion. Design partners can be users, but they can also be elaborate pilots.

The strongest bull case is that I am demanding the wrong evidence too early. Rational already has the pattern I often like: a difficult proprietary substrate built before a large round, followed by users reporting large operational effects. Accounting is full of schlep blindness—permissions, integrations, exception handling, security, and human approval—and the founders appear willing to enter it. If Tenace’s results reflect sustained production use, the working product and unmistakable user pull could override the missing founder-obstacle story.

I do not think the override is established yet. Neither founder publicly came from accounting, so the dossier does not show that this idea grew from a problem they personally encountered. Nor does it show a rejected hypothesis and a materially revised route. In a market containing Basis, Truewind, Numeric, Digits, and Fieldguide, technical competence alone is not enough: several heavily financed teams can build overlapping workflows. Rational’s likely destination in 36 months is a narrower reconciliation or tax-operations product at roughly $2–6 million ARR, rather than a general “AI employees for accounting” platform. The broad product claim is ahead of the evidence.

I would not write the check today. The operational gate fails because Rational has not shown the founders turning a named blocker into a shipped workaround, and neither the benchmark nor the customer pull is independently strong enough to replace that evidence. One verifiable fact would flip me: a named accounting firm showing six months of paid production use, expanding Rational across at least three workflows after the founders personally solved a documented deployment blocker.
