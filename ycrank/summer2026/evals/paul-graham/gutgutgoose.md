# Gutgutgoose -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The most important fact is missing: what broke, and what did the founders do next? The dossier shows that Anis Mihrshahi built the mechanistic algorithm, Leon Mojarrabi brought pharmacy and GTM experience, and the childhood friends completed an alpha cohort. But it never names a technical, regulatory, or acquisition obstacle followed by a founder-built workaround. At pre-seed, missing growth history is normal. Missing evidence of motion under resistance is more consequential. I cannot infer resourcefulness from credentials or activity.

The product is nevertheless real enough to take seriously. Gutgutgoose sequences more than 300 strains, models competition and metabolic pathways, compounds personalized formulations, and retests customers. The reported 16-fold colonization and 31-fold persistence results are measured claims, not a diagram. But they remain company-reported, unpublished, and dependent on stool testing even though published research questions whether stool detection represents mucosal colonization. The March 2026 provisional patent protects a description of the method; it does not validate the method. This is an intermediate technical proof, not the unusually concrete pre-round proof spike that has predicted my recent checks.

The best evidence is the small well of users. At an endometriosis event, 20 of 50 attendees joined the waitlist, seven paid, and five referrals followed. Payment at $93 or $183 per month is more informative than the broader 120-person waitlist. Referrals are better still. These users appear willing to tolerate an elaborate first product involving stool collection, sequencing, compounding, and months of treatment. But subscriptions that renew every 90 days are only recurring revenue after customers actually renew. Gutgutgoose has shown initial pull, not yet durable pull.

There is real schlep here. Personalized formulation requires sequencing logistics, strain sourcing, compounding, retesting, and careful navigation of supplement claims. Mojarrabi’s pharmacy experience makes this more credible than a generic microbiome pitch, while Mihrshahi supplies relevant AI and data-science work. What is absent is the organic origin: neither founder is shown encountering the problem personally or building the first version to solve a need arising from prior work. And competitors already possess formidable substrates—Floré reports more than 40,000 formulations and Viome more than one million analyzed microbiomes. Gutgutgoose must prove that its simulation predicts an outcome those datasets cannot, rather than merely adding another personalization layer.

The strongest bull case is that seven customers and five referrals already validate the wedge, while the alpha and beta cohorts provide the beginnings of a proprietary intervention dataset. Long founder history also removes the hastily assembled-team risk. That is enough to keep this from being a pass. But it does not qualify for an exception to my resourcefulness gate: the technical result is not independently validated, the central measurement may not establish the claimed biological outcome, and there is no documented rapid correction after contrary evidence. Meanwhile, recruiting a Growth employee and leaning on clinic partnerships this early risk insulating the founders from learning personally why customers buy, renew, or stop.

I would not write the check today. Gutgutgoose has found more genuine demand than most personalized-health startups at this stage, but the company has not yet shown the founder behavior or validated proof needed to turn that demand into an investable logical argument. One verifiable event would flip me: the founders encounter a specific failure in the next cohort, ship a corrective change themselves within weeks, and show that the affected customers subsequently achieve independently validated colonization and renew at day 90.
