# Boxes.dev -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is **New Economies**. Boxes.dev could make large parallel coding runs viable for teams whose engineers would otherwise spend too much time setting up and tending separate environments. Its product copies a working development environment into a template and forks isolated cloud machines that can keep running when a laptop sleeps. The memo describes the prior workflow’s costs in engineer setup time and shared CPU, memory, and ports. The market-opening scenario assumes that cloning cuts setup and supervision time enough to lower the all-in cost per accepted code change, after cloud charges and review. The memo gives no before-and-after cost data or evidence that a team previously ruled out such a workload on cost grounds. (*The Idea*)

The founders report that an unnamed customer ran more than 200 agents on over 100 machines for one software build. The memo also records 10,611 standalone CLI downloads in one week. These signals show reported use at meaningful scale and measurable distribution, while payment, repeat use, and completed outcomes remain unknown. The founders previously built engineering systems together at Gem and shipped a CLI and mobile apps with a two-person team. Production reliability and credential handling under sustained team use remain open. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*)

Versioned team templates and startup scripts could create switching work. Whether that translates into paid preference when alternatives run the same agents and support comparable workflows is untested. The awake box-hour is a clear expansion unit. Customers supply model access, and paid-plan overages are listed at $0.40 per awake box-hour. Contribution depends on realized revenue per hour after included usage and trials, less compute, storage, and support. The memo provides no realized cost or utilization figures. (*Defensibility*, *The Idea*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Boxes.dev may make an existing cloud development workflow easier to set up while competitors or model providers capture the economics. Replicas already runs native coding agents in isolated VMs, Coder offers customer-hosted workspaces, and OpenAI’s acquisition of Ona creates a route to bundle workspaces into Codex. Anthropic’s authentication terms also limit an access method Boxes.dev relies on. Existing substitutes defeat the scarce-complement case for generic isolated execution. The New Economies case remains open because the memo establishes neither that snapshot cloning opens a market nor that buyers switch readily once they have a comparable option. (*Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent engineering teams that previously ruled out large parallel coding runs on cost grounds repeatedly pay for Boxes.dev when a comparable hosted workspace runs the same agents? A paid cohort should compare all-in cost per accepted change against the teams’ prior workflow and then record which workspace they choose at unsubsidized prices. Reach out if the cost reduction enables those teams to run the workload and they repeatedly choose Boxes.dev. Pass if the comparison shows the workload remains uneconomic or buyers readily switch to a comparable workspace.
