I assign the missing-evidence default rather than treating inferred substitution risk as a proven defect.

The strongest argument against passing is that this team may already be discovering that asset through use. Mirrors displays 711,933 agent sessions, and its public documentation describes a concrete integration surface. But the session count has no period, customer breakdown, or attribution between the older replay product and current context delivery. It cannot establish repeated preference for today’s product. The cited $8.3 billion context-layer estimate includes substantial adjacent categories, however; Mirrors has not established which additional buyers its present capability unlocks. Demo-led, custom pricing identifies an intended commercial relationship, but supplies no realized price or revenue evidence. Likewise, customer-authored guidance may improve that customer’s agent; the dossier shows no mechanism through which one customer improves another customer’s utility or distribution. My patterns of outsiders contributing reusable supply and individuals creating scalable output remain possibilities here.

I find no documented integrity problem, artificial financing pressure, or dependence on investor reassurance. The change from replay to runtime guidance alone establishes none of those red flags. I also allow exceptions for an exceptional founder response, a demonstrated product with unusual capital efficiency, or a network already forming before monetization. The dossier does not establish those override conditions strongly enough. **I pass today.** My verifiable condition for reopening is a controlled demonstration that a deployment-derived dataset unavailable to competitors produces sustained improvement on held-out customer workflows over the same underlying model with generic context routing. That would establish something Mirrors owns which compounds as it operates.
