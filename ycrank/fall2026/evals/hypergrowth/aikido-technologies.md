# Aikido Technologies -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B, repeatable sales with account expansion.** A power-constrained neocloud is the clearest first buyer. Aikido could win an initial capacity agreement through direct sales, bring the buyer’s workloads online on a standard floating platform, and sell more capacity as the buyer grows. The memo describes a global power bottleneck and a planned Norway pilot. Together they support some geographic potential, while demand for Aikido’s capacity remains untested across markets. A second geography would test whether the same design can meet local permitting, grid-independent power, connectivity, and marine servicing requirements. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The capacity inquiry form is a concrete sales entry point, and Aikido reports platform permits for its pilot retrofit. Aikido also says prospective GPU renters are lined up. The form is nonbinding, and the memo gives no buyer names, payment terms, live workloads, or realized revenue. At this pilot stage, the immediate test of fit is whether one independent buyer will pay to run a real workload. Repeatable activation and expansion remain untested. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Scenario assumption.** If the platform has spare reliable capacity, additional paid workloads could spread fixed costs across more revenue. The contribution test starts with revenue from capacity actually delivered and deducts sales and onboarding effort, backup fuel, and offshore support. Once spare capacity runs out, the next module also brings fabrication, deployment, and financing costs. Neither the pilot nor the full-scale design has a reported cost breakdown that shows how contribution changes at that step. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Each new block of capacity could require roughly proportional capital and site work. Wind variability and vessel-dependent maintenance could also erode billable uptime, while full-scale certification is still planned. The company-reported assembly of its earlier floating wind platform in under 40 working hours gives Aikido a plausible lever on assembly effort. The memo leaves the integrated data center’s cost and uptime open, so the structural-cost Pass gate has yet to be met. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent neocloud pay for and run a real workload on the pilot under terms Aikido could offer the next buyer? An executed paid agreement, workload and uptime records, and itemized onboarding and operating costs would answer it. Move to **Reach out** if the pilot meets the buyer’s service requirements and the measured costs support a credible path to positive contribution on a repeat deployment.
