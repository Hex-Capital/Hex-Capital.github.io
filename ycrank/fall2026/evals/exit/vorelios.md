# Vorelios -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest path. An engineering simulation or CAD platform could buy Vorelios to own its model, training pipeline, and automated design search. Ownership could matter if exclusive control gave the buyer a durable advantage in delivering faster design cycles across its customer base. The memo documents Siemens’s use of a PhysicsX partnership to develop an aerodynamics model, which shows commercial interest in the capability. The economic advantage of owning Vorelios remains an inference. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

Vorelios reports subsecond predictions from clean CAD and thousands of candidates searched in initial autonomous runs, with predictions within a few percent of an independent solver in those runs. It also reports that a previous architecture failed on unfamiliar design families. A production pilot on unseen customer designs could establish whether the workflow saves engineering time at acceptable accuracy after CAD preparation and validation. YC participation is confirmed and the memo reports $125,000 raised. The pilot budget, runway, and IP transfer rights are unverified. The memo supplies no usable acquisition pricing or independently supported route to cash for this holder. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *Defensibility*, *Key Facts*)

**Proceeds bridge.** The illustrative investor makes one $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation and dilutes from 5% to 3%. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the investor, or $83.3 million of distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale prices below are scenario assumptions. Both sales assume zero net debt, transaction costs, and other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. Both assume a simple pro rata waterfall with no preference deductions. All consideration is cash at closing, with $0 each in stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation.

| Scenario | Timing | Distributable equity proceeds | Investor cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $40 million | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100 million | $3 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | $0 distributed | $0, shares retained | 0.0× realized |

The strong sale clears the hurdle at 3%. At 2%, it pays $2 million, or 4.0×. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The memo provides too little evidence to treat that scenario as probable or either sale price as underwritten.

### Countercase

A platform may get enough of the benefit by partnering or building in-house. The Siemens–PhysicsX collaboration demonstrates the partnership route, and NVIDIA’s open-source PhysicsNeMo supplies tools for internal development. Vorelios’s clean-CAD requirement and reported difficulty with unfamiliar design families could further narrow the savings a buyer would pay to own. This supports Watch. The memo leaves open whether those substitutes can match Vorelios on a production workflow where interacting physical effects matter, so the structural Pass gate is unproven. (*Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production pilot on an unfamiliar aerospace design family show enough incremental value over a prospective buyer’s best build or partnership option to justify ownership at the investor’s proceeds hurdle? A buyer-validated comparison of end-to-end engineering time and solver-verified accuracy, translated into economics that support a purchase near or above the $83.3 million equity hurdle at 3%, would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that the substitute delivers comparable value and caps the price below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
