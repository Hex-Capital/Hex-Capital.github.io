# Avoca Systems -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The strongest evidence is what the founders have done, not where they worked before. Max Shand was Afterpay’s first employee and sold Serenade in 2024; Campbell Mercer-Butcher previously led engineering teams, but neither biography establishes prior radiology knowledge ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/avoca-systems); [Vinyl Group](https://data-api.marketindex.com.au/api/v1/announcements/XASX%3AVNL%3A2A1551729/pdf/inline)). Avoca itself changes the prior. They have shipped into radiology networks, integrated clinical workflows, and produced an 86% call-resolution rate, 4.6/5 satisfaction, and a reported 90% reduction in abandoned calls. That is specific knowledge acquired through action, although the dossier does not separate founder contribution from the eight-person team.

The product solves a real coordination problem. Radiology booking is not merely answering a telephone: protocols, preparation instructions, referrals, site rules, and RIS write-back must agree. Avoca has already connected Epic, Oracle Health, MEDITECH, Karisma, Aura, and Visage, while earning SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 credentials ([Avoca integrations](https://www.avocasystems.com/integrations); [Avoca security](https://www.avocasystems.com/security-and-governance)). Those integrations and clinic-specific rules create implementation depth. They do not yet create defensibility. If openDoctor, AbbaDox, or an installed RIS vendor copies the interface and voice layer, Avoca has no disclosed proprietary model, exclusive dataset, hardware, or rights that prevent the customer from choosing the incumbent.

The bull case is stronger than the absence of revenue suggests. Lumus Imaging, Western Radiology, ForHealth, and other named operators are using the system; one customer reported 30 converted bookings in half a day, and deployments include networks operating more than 150 clinics ([Avoca](https://www.avocasystems.com/)). Enterprise healthcare software rarely reaches that operational surface area by accident. Accumulated exception histories and booking outcomes could eventually become a proprietary operational model. But “could accumulate” is not “has accumulated,” and customer-specific configuration can remain services-heavy switching friction rather than a compounding asset.

Avoca also lacks the participation loop I prefer. One clinic does not make the product more useful or easier to acquire for another clinic; each deployment appears to require its own integrations, rules, compliance work, and enterprise sale. This is centralized workflow automation, not a permissionless network. The published $780 million radiology-scheduling market is respectable but bounded, and the dossier does not establish a causal expansion from radiology into adjacent specialties. Assort Health and Prosper AI are already pursuing that broader patient journey with substantially more capital.

No evidence raises an integrity, founder-stability, or artificial-deadline red flag. The problem is structural rather than personal. The known exceptions to missing monetization or network evidence require either an unusually durable prototype, exceptional founder-specific knowledge, or already self-reinforcing adoption. Avoca’s deployments support execution, but not yet one of those override mechanisms. Australia-only hosting during a U.S. expansion and unfinished athenahealth and eClinicalWorks integrations add execution gates precisely where installed competitors have leverage.

My 36-month base case is that Avoca becomes a credible $6–12 million ARR radiology-workflow vendor, but remains a sold-and-serviced enterprise product rather than a defining network. I would not write the check today. The single flip condition is verifiable: show that deployment data from existing networks produces a proprietary scheduling model that measurably improves accuracy or deployment time for each subsequent customer, alongside at least $2 million ARR. Then the integrations become a data flywheel rather than implementation inventory.
