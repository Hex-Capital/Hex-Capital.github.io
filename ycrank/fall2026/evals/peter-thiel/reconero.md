# Reconero -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Reconero needs a secret about why police software can be rebuilt that its competitors have failed to understand. The dossier establishes an expensive problem: paperwork reportedly consumes over 40% of an officer’s day. It also describes an intelligible product—search departmental evidence, extract body-camera narratives, prefill reports. But which important belief does this overturn? No disputed proposition or falsifying experiment is documented. I leave the secret unproved; a factual dossier’s silence cannot establish that the founders have none.

The competitive evidence is more decisive. Mark43 already combines records and dispatch; Axon already generates reports from body-camera audio; Abel offers AI tools within departmental workflows. Reconero proposes combining overlapping functions while also handling legacy migration. That creates a dangerous bargain: assume the implementation burden of a system replacement before demonstrating a proprietary advantage sufficient to compel replacement. No bounded initial segment, exclusive data, measured technical superiority, or installed network establishes a monopoly mechanism. Department-specific configuration might eventually create switching costs, but the dossier supplies no deployment or retention evidence supporting that possibility.

The strongest argument against passing is the founding team. Azeroual and Liu met through their high school’s Hack Club, Liu left Stanford to build the company, and the founders co-developed ReconeroRMS. Those are affirmative signs of shared work and commitment. They counter the red flag of founders assembled for an accelerator application. Ownership, compensation, and both founders’ full-time status remain unverified. Azeroual’s reported $150K ARR from a previous sentiment-analysis business makes dismissing their commercial ability uncomfortable, although it establishes neither Reconero revenue nor a repeatable police-sales channel.

Reconero also fits an unstated preference for making contested information operationally legible. Police-unit trials and iterative officer feedback, reported in September 2026, give that connection substance. The bull case is that these founders discover an unusually neglected departmental workflow through direct use, then build the surrounding system around it. Yet the agencies are unnamed and payment status is unspecified. My exceptions to competitive or execution concerns require independent technical proof, persistent transaction demand, or a demonstrated deployment path. The reported trials establish none of those overrides. Errors documented in a comparable tool make accuracy a necessary test; they do not establish that Reconero itself fails it.

Departmental demos using agency data provide a concrete acquisition route, earning modest credit for distribution. Sales ownership, procurement duration, implementation cost, and contract value remain unknown, so its economics are unresolved. Likewise, subscriptions are inferred, and the broader $20.4B market supplies no customer-count, pricing, or margin arithmetic for Reconero’s own revenue engine. I would pass with my own money. The decisive obstacle is demonstrated competition without demonstrated value capture. One verifiable milestone would reopen the decision: a named department’s paid renewal following an independently measured, order-of-magnitude improvement over its incumbent workflow, at comparable factual accuracy, attributable to Reconero’s proprietary technology.
