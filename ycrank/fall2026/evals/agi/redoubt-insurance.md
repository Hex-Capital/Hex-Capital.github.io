# Redoubt Insurance -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Small fleets can lose loads while waiting for a broker to issue a certificate or change a policy. Redoubt’s agents use a shared account record to prepare submissions, compare quotes, and handle servicing. The investment hypothesis is that reducing broker labor per correct, on-time transaction makes responsive service economical for small fleets whose commissions previously supported slower service. Redoubt reports one customer saved $56,000 in premiums within two days of contacting it. That anecdote suggests customer value. The memo provides no comparison of broker labor or evidence that the customer previously went without timely service. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

Under the scenario assumption that rival brokers have equally capable models, fleets could still choose Redoubt for reliable placement and servicing. The memo describes an operating brokerage run by a licensed founder with prior experience building AI for medical coding and legal workflows. Redoubt’s account history could make repeat servicing easier, though its effect on reliability and retention remains untested. The founder reports 19 accounts since the October launch, with repeat demand from previously underserved fleets still unknown. (*Defensibility*, *Founders & Team*, *Market & Traction*)

If the reported $37,600 in expected annual net commissions covers those 19 accounts, it implies about $1,980 per account. That scenario leaves contribution unmeasured. The comparison with conventional brokerage needs the full cost of acquiring, placing, and servicing an account, including model usage, licensed review, retries, exception handling, and correction of filing or coverage errors. The projected commission base and absence of a representative cost comparison support a preliminary case with low confidence. (*The Idea*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Redoubt may be making conventional brokerage faster for fleets that already buy coverage and receive adequate servicing. Existing brokers can adopt similar automation through vendors such as Covera, and Redoubt depends on third-party carriers and wholesalers for placement. The memo identifies no structural barrier or enforceable control of scarce carrier capacity. That leaves Path A unsupported and makes substitution credible under Path B. The memo contains no direct comparison that settles either Path B condition, so Watch applies. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Redoubt profitably deliver repeat, time-sensitive servicing to small fleets whose prior brokers routinely missed their deadlines, and retain those accounts when a broker with equivalent AI and comparable carrier access is available? A comparative pilot should track on-time, correct outcomes, fully loaded delivery costs, realized commissions, and repeat account choice. Reach out if the pilot shows newly viable service and fleets repeatedly choose Redoubt under those conditions. Pass if it shows that the prior model already serves the use economically or that an equivalent broker can preserve the same outcome and win the accounts.
