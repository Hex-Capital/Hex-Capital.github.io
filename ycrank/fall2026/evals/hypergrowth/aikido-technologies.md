# Aikido Technologies -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with AI labs and cloud operators seeking compute capacity, Aikido could serve the same recurring job in multiple markets. Its proposed AO60DC combines a modular floating platform, wind generation, backup power, and seawater cooling. Those components could carry over to new sites, but the memo records permits for one pilot retrofit, and Aikido still describes full-scale certification as planned. The extent of site-specific engineering and approvals remains uncertain. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The capacity inquiry form gives prospects a repeatable entry point. Ordinary buyers still need a separate agreement for price, capacity, and delivery, and first use depends on physical deployment. Colocation could retain customers because they install their own hardware, but recurring billing terms and a way to earn more from existing accounts are unverified. Aikido reports prospective GPU renters and pilot progress since the early-2025 pivot. The memo leaves both realized revenue and pre-revenue status unconfirmed, so initial traction takes the default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The cost test is the contracted revenue from the next deployment minus the incremental cost of acquiring and onboarding the buyer, adding and operating the platform, providing backup power and vessel servicing, and supplying GPUs where Aikido provides them. In a favorable scenario, reusing the platform design reduces engineering and assembly costs, while onsite wind and seawater cooling reduce operating costs. Those are design assumptions. The memo gives no contracted prices, utilization, or representative operating costs to establish positive contribution. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Even with strong buyer demand, each increment of capacity could require another platform, site approvals, and vessel-dependent maintenance. Wind variability could also increase backup costs or reduce billable availability. If those burdens recur at each site, modularity may do little to improve contribution as volume grows. The structural downside gate remains open because the pilot evidence leaves both the extent of site-specific work and the economics of a repeat deployment unresolved. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a second paid deployment reuse the core platform design and earn positive incremental contribution at realistic utilization? The signed capacity agreement and actual engineering, deployment, backup power, and servicing costs would answer it. If required site work or capacity costs make repeat deployments uneconomic at contracted prices without a credible improvement lever, change Watch to Pass. Positive contribution with bounded site work would clear the main uncertainty and warrant rescoring. (The Idea, Key Risks)
