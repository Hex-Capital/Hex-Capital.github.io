# Telamont -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Telamont’s strongest path is repeatable direct sales to general contractors. The recurring job is checking revised drawings, specifications, and contracts for conflicts before work proceeds. The memo documents an Autodesk Forma listing alongside direct demos and requests for warm introductions. Telamont describes a workflow that syncs revisions and assigns source-linked findings. The same document review workflow could carry across markets, assuming local codes and contract requirements can be accommodated within the existing product. Paid adoption in a second market remains unverified. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

Telamont reports $1.1 million in foundation rework avoided and $400,000 in missing structural scope flagged. Those examples suggest contractors have a reason to put live project documents through the product. The memo leaves buyer payments and recurring workflow commitments unverified. The first likely break, even if buyers see strong value, is getting the next contractor from demo to paid use through a standard rollout. The listing and demos establish routes to discovery, while the memo gives no account-level record linking either route to paid activation. New revisions create a reason to return, and additional projects offer a plausible expansion route. Repeat paid use, implementation effort, and marginal delivery costs remain unmeasured. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

The reported savings could come from a few unusually costly issues, while routine deployments still require hands-on setup and support. Telamont solicits warm introductions, files outside supported connectors require manual upload, and customers must approve notices. The risk is that Telamont has to step in repeatedly to assemble a complete project record or make findings actionable. The memo provides no affirmative evidence that Telamont’s labor rises in proportion to revenue, so the structural downside gate does not apply. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a non-founder take a second independent general contractor from a direct demo to a paid live-project rollout using the same onboarding steps and comparable staff hours as the first? A paid order, a source-linked finding the contractor acts on, and time logs from both rollouts would move the action to Reach out if they show the process can be handed off and repeated.
