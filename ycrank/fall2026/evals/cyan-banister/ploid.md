# Ploid -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine asking an agent to find a collaborator and receiving a current, evidenced answer that the person can correct or withdraw. Getting there requires reliable identity matching, continuous refresh, and permissions that survive redistribution. Ploid’s MCP interface makes that future easier to access. It does not yet show how the company gets through those intervening steps. Connecting an agent to a people database is a present-day artifact; becoming the trusted identity layer requires considerably more.

Why are people still exporting CSVs and cleaning contact records? Ploid identifies a real irritation: information fragments across sites and goes stale. Its $200 and $600 monthly plans offer a concrete way to buy back research time. But the dossier does not identify an artificial restriction, quantify the cleaning burden, or establish who captures a rent that Ploid removes. Faster prospecting gives the researcher some agency. The person being researched needs agency too.

The strongest argument for investing is underneath the sales interface. Cross-site identity resolution could serve recruiting, research, and sales without owning those applications. That fits my preference for concealed infrastructure beneath a visible beachhead. The company’s claimed two-billion-person index and its reported 81% accuracy across 1,000 queries deserve attention, particularly against the reported 63% for Exa and 23% for Apollo. If that advantage survives independent testing, dismissing Ploid as another contact database would be expensive. But these are company-published results graded by Gemini 3.1 Pro, and neither exclusive inputs nor a compounding correction advantage is established. Monid combining several providers also gives buyers a way to substitute suppliers.

Hernandez’s prior work on recommendation systems and embedding pipelines at Wander is relevant technical preparation. His public `synapse` repository adds an actual artifact to the biography. Karic brings research experience and handles customers and partnerships. I see plausible complementary abilities, but little documented evidence of a people-data obsession predating Ploid or unusual recruitment power. On product behavior, the September 4, 2026 Monid integration is more informative than follower counts: someone undertook integration work. “Thousands” of companies, without cohorts or paid usage detail, does not yet establish repeated costly behavior.

Privacy is the question that could change the entire mechanism. Ploid prohibits personal surveillance and provides source URLs and field-level provenance; those are useful boundaries and verification tools. Its privacy policy nevertheless says it does not control some contact vendors’ sourcing or refresh practices. That leaves consent and custody unresolved rather than proving misconduct. I would also verify decision authority: Hernandez is consistently CEO, while Karic’s COO/CGO variation alone does not establish a leadership shell game. My exceptions for pre-revenue investments remain available, but this dossier lacks the exceptional founder history or demonstrated user commitment that would activate them.

I pass with my own money today. Missing revenue is not the reason; the missing connection between useful retrieval and a durable, rights-respecting identity asset is. One verifiable flip condition would be an independently audited, renewing production deployment demonstrating that Ploid’s own identity corrections sustain a material accuracy advantage and that those corrections—including deletion requests—propagate through customer outputs. That would show infrastructure earning trust through use.
