# The Subvocal Company -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Product-led adoption is the strongest path. Frequent voice-dictation users could use the glasses to compose prompts, emails, and messages in places where speaking aloud is disruptive. That recurring job plausibly travels across markets. Preorders were limited to the U.S., and recognition across wearers and the privacy and connectivity constraints of cloud decoding remain unresolved. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The launch demonstrated silent speech-to-text on a prototype and directed interested viewers to web reservations. The company reported roughly 750 preorders backed by refundable $199 deposits toward the $499 early-bird price. That is a concrete early buyer commitment. The memo gives no evidence of buyers paying the balance, setting up independently, or returning to dictate regularly. The growth scenario assumes buyers can get useful text from production glasses without hands-on calibration and that frequent users eventually pay for the planned unlimited tier. Repeatable activation is the first unproven link. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

A viral demo can attract refundable reservations even if buyers ultimately prefer typing. The company reported 85–92% recognition accuracy in its showcase, and changes to production sensor placement or power design can alter the signal the model receives. If those differences require hands-on tuning for each wearer, support effort could rise with every sale. The structural downside gate remains untriggered because production rollout effort and marginal costs are still unmeasured. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of frequent dictation users acquired through direct web sales after the launch surge complete payment, set up production glasses without founder help, and use them for everyday dictation week after week? Observing that sequence in the same cohort would move the action to Reach out.
