# Daqstra -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Daqstra has shown me the machine, but not the deal. There is no proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is a genuinely scarce first check. For my money, that is not administrative detail: a heavily financed field already contains Nominal with $182.5 million raised and Sift with $67 million, so an undocumented YC round could offer cheap exposure to a discarded integration problem or expensive exposure to a newly fashionable category. I cannot tell which. Round terms therefore receive the explicit missing-evidence default, not the benefit of optimism.

The strongest fact is that this is not an AI wrapper looking for a workflow. Daqstra has operated live LOX/IPA static-fire campaigns, connected NI, Siemens, Modbus, OPC-UA, and custom-TCP equipment, deployed across multiple rocket-engine test stands, and entered an industrial-energy pilot targeting more than $100,000 in annual customer savings. Active aerospace programs reportedly use the system day to day. That is meaningful pre-seed proof: heterogeneous hardware is being joined inside safety-critical operations, and the product can command sequences, redlines, valves, and abort logic rather than merely displaying telemetry.

The founders are unusually credible translators. Darell Chua worked on systems performance at Rivian and co-authored research on distributed acquisition for cryogenic rockets; Anchit Kumar built autonomy for Mars cave-exploration drones, worked on robot safety, and participated in more than ten Mojave test campaigns. They met building and testing liquid rocket engines, then shipped software into the same physical environments. Daqstra’s best asset today is not “AI-native” anything. It is the accumulated judgment required to make incompatible industrial systems cooperate without turning a software error into damaged hardware.

The bull case is that those deployments already form the beginning of a proprietary workflow dark pool: configurations, calibration lineage, safety logic, and test histories can become painful to remove once Daqstra sits between engineers and live equipment. Day-to-day aerospace use and a signed energy pilot are stronger than followers or generic LOIs. But the dossier does not establish paid conversion, retention duration, or whether Daqstra owns this workflow rather than repeatedly performing expensive custom integration. Nominal, Sift, and incumbent instrumentation vendors can attack the same control layer; supporting every bespoke controller can also turn software leverage into a services burden.

The missing venture moment is decisive. No use-of-funds plan identifies one hypothesis, metric, or deadline. “Onboard more hardware teams” would be a capital factory, not a test. I want to know whether one pre-seed check can prove that a repeatable connector-and-deployment package converts live pilots into high-margin contracts without financing a new engineering project for every customer. The dossier likewise supplies no pricing, revenue, gross margin, burn, or runway, so the $100,000 savings target demonstrates customer value but says nothing about Daqstra’s own cash economics.

I am neutral and would not write the check today. The operating receipts keep this from being a pass, including against my red flag for generic AI applications: the base product already works on physical systems, and AI is not carrying the underwriting. The verifiable flip condition is conversion of the current energy pilot and aerospace deployments into at least $500,000 of annualized contracted software revenue at positive gross margin by August 2027 without another financing. That would show the embedded workflow causes purchases, the integrations repeat, and one scarce check can reveal a valuable truth.
