# Versine -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Versine lets an agent move money. What consequential bottleneck disappears when it works? Reimbursements and recurring bills are real tasks, but the dossier supplies no baseline for their cost, population affected, or economic activity prevented by manual approval. Autonomous financial infrastructure could matter enormously. The disclosed consumer use cases do not yet establish that consequence. I would pass with my own money.

The technical question is whether Versine makes safe delegation possible where existing systems fail. Its disclosed differentiation is personal transfers and account-free recipient links, assembled with MCP/API connections, Coinbase wallets, and external funding and payout providers. That is affirmative evidence of convenience, but not a measured capability discontinuity. Stripe already offers agent payment infrastructure. Versine provides no benchmark showing that its authorization system solves an incumbent constraint substantially better. I would score the disclosed differentiation below the missing-evidence default; undisclosed engineering cannot earn credit.

The strongest objection to my pass is that the integration itself could become the control point. Harshith’s Stripe engineering experience is relevant. Param’s work on research evaluations could help distinguish legitimate agent instructions from manipulation. A consumer payment relationship, saved recipients, and developer integrations could make Versine difficult to replace. My actual investment pattern permits execution-heavy fintech; scientific novelty is not compulsory. The payments-platform exception also deserves consideration: a small initial use case can expand into financial infrastructure. Here, however, that expansion remains an inferred possibility, without observed adoption or a documented founder insight strong enough to override the technical gap.

The largest immediate kill risk is whether delegated authority remains safe while removing meaningful human work. Versine’s terms acknowledge manipulated agents, irreversible blockchain payments, and user responsibility for permitted transactions. Passkeys and capped rules are relevant controls, but their existence does not establish their effectiveness. The dossier contains no decisive experiment, numerical threshold, budget, or response to failure. That earns the default for missing milestone evidence, not a penalty for being pre-launch. Likewise, the September–October shift from authentication to payments shows a changed product direction; without the evidence that caused it, I cannot award points for fast belief revision or risk-specific recruiting.

The economics are equally unresolved. Accounts, sending, and bank payouts are free; funding-provider charges may apply. Free to the user does not establish low delivery cost. Versine discloses neither its revenue mechanism nor contribution economics after provider charges, support, and payment losses. I see no evidence of permanent subsidy dependence, so this receives the absent-evidence default. The company’s 1,076 LinkedIn followers and consumer/developer waitlists do not resolve those economics or demonstrate repeated delegation.

One verifiable result would make me reopen the decision: a paid deployment independently demonstrating at least a 5x reduction in human handling time against the customer’s existing payment workflow, at matched authorization safety and including exception handling. That would establish a capability worth buying rather than merely another route to sending money. Until then, Versine offers relevant engineering backgrounds and a plausible infrastructure opportunity, but neither the quantified consequence nor the demonstrated mechanism that makes an improbable outcome worth purchasing.
