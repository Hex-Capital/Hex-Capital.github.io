# Meteoric -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Meteoric begins with specific knowledge, not a fashionable climate story. Mete Karslioglu worked on cloud-reflectivity nozzles and atmospheric instruments; Eric Nilsson worked on digital microfluidics, droplet removal, control systems, and the same class of measurement equipment. Together they produced an artifact: a chamber prototype that dissipated an artificial cloud by 13% ([Meteoric](https://meteoric.earth/); [Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/meteoric)). The result is narrow and unreplicated, but it proves the founders have acted inside the exact problem. Two new graduates with unusually concentrated knowledge can be more interesting than ten experienced executives renting a thesis.

The substrate is real, but the advantage is not yet real. Drones operating at 1–5 km, droplet manipulation, sensing, and closed-loop flight control cannot be reproduced merely by copying an application repository. Yet Meteoric discloses no proprietary mechanism, patent, field dataset, permit portfolio, or natural-cloud performance. Rainmaker already possesses purpose-built weather drones, radar, forecasting, and mission-validation infrastructure ([Rainmaker](https://www.rainmaker.com/technology)). If Rainmaker can reproduce the treatment after seeing field evidence, Meteoric has performed expensive category discovery for a better-capitalized competitor. Difficult engineering creates a head start; only accumulated deployment knowledge turns it into leverage.

The solar wedge has an appealing asymmetry. Overhead clouds can reduce incoming sunlight by 73–82%, while adding generation must contend with a 773 GW U.S. interconnection queue ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/meteoric)). Recovering output from installed panels avoids waiting for new grid capacity, and electric-drone access at a claimed $30–60 per hour changes the experimental cost. But the company has not connected chamber dissipation to recovered MWh, named a paying operator, or shown how many drone-hours are required per MW. The market is therefore physically large but economically undefined. A 10–30% modeled annual uplift is a hypothesis until a customer meter records it.

The hurricane ambition currently subtracts clarity. Meteoric targets a first storm operation in late 2028 even though the World Meteorological Organization says there is no generally accepted evidence that tropical cyclones can be modified ([WMO](https://wmo.int/content/wmo-statement-weather-modification)). Solar-farm cloud clearing and hurricane weakening share atmospheric physics, but not a buyer, regulatory regime, operating scale, or validation path. The broad market cannot be assembled by placing an extraordinary second claim beside an unvalidated first one.

The strongest bull case is that founder-specific knowledge should override early wedge ambiguity. The team has relevant research, complementary controls expertise, a working chamber result, cheaper aerial hardware, and a direct route to utility-scale solar operators. A single natural-cloud trial showing measurable incremental generation could collapse several uncertainties at once. I accept that exception in principle, but it does not yet override the missing compounding mechanism: every treated site appears to require another fleet, another authorization, and more operating labor. No customer makes the service better or distributes it to the next customer.

I am neutral today. There is no disclosed integrity issue, artificial financing pressure, or founder-behavior red flag; the weakness is structural evidence, not character. I would reopen decisively if Meteoric completes a permitted natural-cloud deployment for a named solar operator that produces statistically attributable generation uplift and paid repeat usage. That one result would establish practical physics, value capture, and the beginning of a proprietary site-response dataset. Until then, the company has proven that two capable founders can alter an artificial cloud—not that the effect becomes a durable business.
