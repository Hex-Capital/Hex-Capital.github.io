# Kanopy -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**Path B. Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** Electric utility vegetation teams repeatedly need to find clearance hazards, plan trimming, and verify completed work. ACRT introduced Arborcision, which combines Kanopy’s video analysis with ACRT’s vegetation management services. Kanopy’s credentialed SDK provides a reusable way to upload footage and retrieve inspection results. Together these give Kanopy a concrete distribution and onboarding route. The memo does not show a utility reaching paid activation through that route. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)

Kanopy reports 3,093 cumulative miles mapped, including more than 300 across four states in two weeks. That gives modest evidence that the capture and reconstruction workflow travels within the US. Separately, six utilities or contractors reportedly signed LOIs or master service agreements covering 270,000 potential miles. Those agreements signal buyer interest. Paid orders, customer-level usage, and revenue remain unverified. (Market & Traction; Key Risks; Key Facts)

If utilities accept video-derived measurements for routine work, accounts could expand through repeat surveys and additional circuits using the same workflow. In a second country, the test is whether local capture practices, clearance rules, validation requirements, and work-order formats can be accommodated through configuration. Contribution could improve if the utility or contractor handles capture and Kanopy can process added miles with limited manual review. Capture, compute, quality checks, field verification, and partner fees would all affect the contribution from the next rollout. The memo gives no measured rollout costs. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Countercase

The strongest downside is that utilities need ground checks often enough that Kanopy adds a processing step while leaving much of the fieldwork in place. The memo describes a utility plan that calls for validating remote measurements against field observations and retaining ground inspections where remote methods fall short. If that pattern applies to Kanopy, each new territory could require its own field validation and each survey could add labor alongside compute. The memo establishes a credible risk. The Pass gate requires observed evidence of a structural ceiling or proportional scaling costs, and neither has been established for Kanopy. (Key Risks; Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent utility outside the US pay for and put a partner-led vegetation survey into routine use through Kanopy’s existing capture-to-results workflow? A paid order, utility acceptance of the results for trimming decisions, and a rollout record showing reusable setup and limited field rechecks would move the action to **Reach out**.
