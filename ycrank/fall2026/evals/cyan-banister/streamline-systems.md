# Streamline Systems -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

By 2032, I can imagine a drone operator treating satellite positioning as an optional input rather than permission to fly. Getting there requires navigation that survives interference, hardware cheap enough to lose, and manufacturing that reproduces the demonstration reliably. Streamline Systems reports autonomous flight without satellites and a handoff to GPS: a small, concrete leak from that world. But only the first transition has supporting evidence. The recruitment listing explicitly says hardening, productization and manufacturability remain unfinished. I can see the path; I cannot yet see how much of it this team has crossed.

The drop-in module is what interests me most. Integrations with ArduPilot, INAV and PX4 put Streamline beneath several aircraft platforms, where one useful component could improve both commercial and military operations. That fits my pattern of finding concealed infrastructure under a more visible market. Yet controlling a module is different from controlling a scarce enabling constraint. The sensing mechanism is undisclosed, and Advanced Navigation and ANELLO already offer alternative architectures. Without accuracy, drift, duration or cost comparisons, I cannot tell whether Streamline removes a bottleneck or supplies another way around it.

Why does an operator need an expensive navigation suite? Which expense disappears with this module, and who gets to fly because it disappears? The dossier identifies jamming, spoofing and costly alternatives, but supplies no module price or quantified operating savings. Restoring navigational independence is useful; the evidence does not yet establish a particular institutional rent being removed or an individual gaining economic control. That distinction matters to my check.

Gabriel Poraj has built a content agency using Premiere Pro automation and worked as a senior software developer. Those are tangible signs of initiative beyond a university credential. Charlie Jessett brings RAF reserve exposure and a complementary operating role. But neither biography documents a sustained navigation obsession before Streamline, and advertised engineering vacancies establish recruiting intent rather than recruited believers. Naming Gabriel CEO and Charlie COO gives me an accountable leadership structure on paper; ownership and contractual decision rights remain unknown. Likewise, “no RF signature” describes an operating property, not evidence about consent or data custody. Neither unknown earns a red flag or affirmative credit.

The strongest objection to passing is that this is already flying hardware, with signed LOIs and government trials scheduled. Those are meaningful pre-seed signals. **CosmicBrain AI** offers a relevant exception: a functioning prototype and industrial commitments can justify investing before revenue. The material difference is its documented lifelong founder obsession; Streamline’s dossier does not supply that bridge from prototype to unusually credible team. Here, counterparties and trial results are also undisclosed. The LOIs support interest, but they do not establish repeated customer expenditure or completed field use.

I pass at this evidence level. Streamline Systems has a plausible place in a future I want to exist, but the present artifact carries more of the investment argument than it can independently prove. My one verifiable flip condition is a named operator’s completed, paid repeat deployment demonstrating that the module meets a disclosed mission accuracy requirement under GPS denial at a lower installed cost than its incumbent solution. That would connect the flying object to an actual change in what someone can afford to do.
