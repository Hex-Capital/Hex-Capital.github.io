# Care GP -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. A primary-care clinic no longer employs people to shuttle information between a fax inbox, a telephone, and practice-management software; software agents complete routine work, prove what they did, and hand humans only genuine ambiguity. Care GP has one excellent leak from that future: more than 3 million documents processed, with one clinic reducing allocation work from six hours to under 30 minutes. What is missing is the bridge. The dossier does not show how document filing becomes trusted authority over calls, billing, referrals, and records transfer—or how liability, permissions, and error recovery evolve as the agents act rather than sort.

The working artifact is the strongest evidence here. Care GP reached US$200,000 ARR after 3.5 months, reported an 85% pilot conversion rate, US$5,000 ACV, profitability, zero churn, and eventually more than 400 clinic operators. Those clinics are spending money, changing workflows, and allowing the product to touch sensitive operational systems. Even allowing for the fact that several metrics come from company materials and lack independent retention cohorts, this is far beyond a waitlist wearing a lab coat.

The broken system is also concrete, but it is operational rather than institutional. Emails, calls, faxes, and medical documents consume scarce staff time; Care GP can compress a six-hour task into 30 minutes. That returns time and attention to receptionists and clinicians. But the dossier does not identify a regulation, gatekeeper, or artificial rent that Care GP removes. It is repairing expensive fragmentation, not yet transferring ownership or occupational control from an institution to an individual.

Melvin Chen brings commercial command: prior company-building, investment experience, an 18,000-person LinkedIn audience, and enough recruiting ability to assemble a ten-person team. I do not see the pre-company healthcare obsession. There is no dated operating wound inside a clinic, independently built medical artifact, or clinical apprenticeship explaining why this founder had to solve this problem. More concerningly, Startmate named Ethan Atkins and Andy Bui as founders in 2025, while YC now lists Chen alone. That may be an innocent evolution, but founder roles and decision authority need a clean explanation before my money goes in.

The bull case is sharp: three million processed documents and a Bp Premier integration could make the document agent a beachhead for a clinic-wide operating layer. Word-of-mouth growth and profitability suggest the product already understands something competitors do not. The bear case stings more than normal because Facere offers overlapping document and voice automation inside the same practice-management ecosystem, while Care GP still reports a 5%–20% human edit rate. ISO 27001, Australian residency, encryption, and MFA address security; they do not by themselves explain patient consent, correction-data ownership, retention, or secondary model use. In healthcare, the difference between secure surveillance and trusted infrastructure lives in those details.

I am neutral and would not write the check today. Care GP has unusually strong behavior for this stage, but the evidence currently describes a successful vertical application suite rather than a controlled enabling layer six years ahead of the market. One condition would flip me: an independently verified cohort of at least 50 clinics retaining Care GP for twelve months while actively using three or more agents. That would show the document wedge is becoming the clinic’s operating substrate rather than merely a very good automation product.
