# STASIS -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A, product-led adoption through direct web checkout, is the strongest candidate.** The inferred first buyer is a screen-heavy consumer who regularly experiences eye fatigue. STASIS says its glasses detect fatigue and respond with tint changes, break prompts, and screen adjustments. If those interventions make screen work more comfortable, buyers could wear the glasses on most workdays, recommend them to others, and optionally pay for Premium. The same job could recur abroad. A second-market rollout would test whether the same product and checkout can work with local payments, prescription fulfillment, shipping, and returns. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The memo records an open reservation checkout and approximately 1.62 million launch-post views. The listed terms require a refundable $99 deposit toward a $299 first-batch price, with shipment scheduled for March 2027. The memo gives no reservation count, paid conversion, or evidence of regular wear. The launch shows that the founder can attract attention once. The optometry and eyewear discussions are too preliminary to support a second distribution path. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

In a hardware-only scenario, a completed first-batch order brings in $299, including the $99 deposit and $200 balance. Assume zero Premium revenue. Each additional order then has to cover acquisition, manufacturing, prescription lenses where needed, fulfillment, returns, and support. More orders could also tie up more cash in inventory. The memo provides prices and terms, leaving per-order contribution and its behavior at higher volume unknown. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A software-only intervention could relieve enough discomfort that buyers see little reason to pay for dedicated glasses or wear them regularly. The memo describes symptom improvements from smartphone-based blink training, while STASIS reports over 90% accuracy for fatigue and cognitive load without disclosing the reference measure or validation protocol. That makes full payment and sustained wear the decisive test. The evidence does not establish a structural ceiling on demand or show that delivery costs worsen with scale, so the Pass gate is unmet. (Key Risks)

Does the first shipped cohort show that independent buyers with recurring screen fatigue will pay the full first-batch price and keep using the glasses on screen-heavy days? Order and usage records showing several buyers completing payment through the same checkout and wearing the glasses regularly through the first month would move the action to **Reach out** to assess repeatable acquisition, contribution, and a second market.
