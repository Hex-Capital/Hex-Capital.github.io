# RightNow -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

RightNow’s potentially important claim is that recurring coding sessions create enough reusable context to support a structurally better inference business. What would prove that wrong? A representative production comparison showing that cache misses, GPU utilization, and session routing erase the apparent savings. The company’s methodology says displayed cache rates come from the highest qualifying account, while its historical benchmarks lack runtime details and an available comparison harness. This leaves the proposition unproved. The dossier also identifies no prevailing belief that the founders explicitly contest; a useful optimization does not automatically establish a secret.

The more decisive problem is ownership of the improvement. Coding-agent developers form a recognizable initial customer segment, but Together AI already develops kernels, compilers, and inference infrastructure. RightNow’s Autokernel implementation and correctness harness demonstrate substantive engineering, yet their MIT license permits reuse. Session-aware routing could create customer value while the resulting profits accrue to providers with greater infrastructure scale. The competitive overlap and reusable implementation are affirmative reasons to doubt monopoly formation, beyond the ordinary uncertainty of a pre-seed company.

The strongest objection to passing is that the founders are already building the infrastructure that the expanding AI stack requires. Autokernel has approximately 1,600 GitHub stars, both founders coauthored model research, and the commercial product has shipped. This fits my observed preference for infrastructure beneath AI applications. An independently demonstrated technical discontinuity could justify accepting uncertain market ownership; persistent paid customer behavior could establish an advantage that public architecture descriptions miss. RightNow currently supplies neither kind of exception evidence. OpenFang’s approximately 18,200 stars belong to a separate project, and RunInfra’s Product Hunt rankings establish launch attention without establishing production dependence. The technical bull case deserves an experiment, but it cannot yet override the competitive red flag.

Distribution is the most concrete part of this business. Developers can buy prepaid usage starting at $5, choose $10/$29/$99 monthly plans, connect existing coding agents, and discover the service through launches or startup credits. That is a plausible acquisition route for small accounts, although conversion, retention, and acquisition costs remain unestablished. The same pricing makes the scale question exacting: at the listed $99 monthly price, approximately 84,200 continuously paying workspaces would generate $100 million in annual subscription revenue before serving costs. Usage revenue could change that arithmetic, but the dossier provides no customer expansion or gross-margin evidence supporting a fund-scale outcome.

The founding structure remains an open diligence question. Shared research establishes some working relationship; the dossier does not establish its duration, both founders’ full-time status, or their economic exposure. I assign the stated default rather than infer misalignment. I would pass on the present evidence because RightNow has a credible route to selling a service whose durable advantage remains doubtful. One verifiable condition would reopen the case: an independent production audit showing a sustained 10x advantage in fully loaded serving cost against Together AI on representative recurring coding workloads, with matched models, output quality, and latency. That would turn an optimization claim into evidence of proprietary technical advantage.
