# Degla Inc -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a rescue coordinator saying, “Search this watershed,” and aircraft dividing the terrain, finding someone, and returning safely without a pilot choreographing every movement. Degla Inc’s reported October 2026 voice-commanded VTOL search demonstration is a small leak from that world. The distance matters: autonomous docking is scheduled for Q1 2027, operating permissions remain necessary, and recovery from communications or aircraft failures is unproven. There is a future here I want to inhabit, but several essential transitions still need evidence.

Aziz Hanafi gives me a reason to keep looking. His work on autonomous-system coordination at InstaDeep and neural-network research at MIT MSEAS, beginning in spring 2024, puts relevant technical experience before this company. UChallengeMe reportedly reached more than 10,000 users before closing and selling its codebase. That is useful evidence of building something people adopted and finding a commercial endpoint. It does not establish a lifelong aerospace obsession or an ability to recruit a complementary team, but his biography contains more substance than an AI credential attached to a drone pitch.

Why does each aircraft need so much human choreography? Part of the answer is an explicit operating constraint: baseline Part 107 rules prohibit one pilot from simultaneously operating multiple drones, while visual-contact requirements further constrain deployment. Degla could return scarce operator time to search teams and infrastructure crews. But these restrictions address safety, and software does not itself remove them. The dossier identifies the rule without quantifying the coordination cost or showing who captures an avoidable rent. Skydio’s report of approvals for 12 additional agencies also means the waiver pathway is available to an established competitor.

The strongest case for investing now is that Hanafi understands coordination, has already put an aircraft through a public demonstration, and could turn mission execution into infrastructure serving several industries. That fits my recurring attraction to the concealed machinery beneath a visible product. Reported onboard inference is a meaningful integration choice. Yet Degla has not demonstrated control of a scarce technical dependency, exclusive mission data, or an aircraft-independent platform that downstream operators require. The software layer could become valuable; today, Skydio and Auterion already offer overlapping coordination capabilities.

I would allow a pre-revenue exception for exceptional founder evidence and a convincing working system. Degla supplies pieces of that case, but no signed industrial proofs of concept or repeated customer behavior. FBI, CBP, and DIU representatives observing a demonstration establishes an audience. It leaves unanswered whether an operator will entrust recurring missions to the product. The vehicle-tracking use case also activates my privacy gate: the dossier supplies no consent, retention, or access architecture. That is an unresolved question rather than evidence of abuse. Hanafi is identified as CEO; there is likewise no basis here to invent a leadership or equal-control problem.

I am **Neutral** on Degla Inc. The technical founder is currently more convincing than the evidence that users will reorganize their work around his system. My verifiable flip condition is an independently documented, paid 90-day deployment with a named search-and-rescue operator, showing recurring missions under applicable authorization, measured human interventions and recoveries, and explicit data-access limits. That single deployment would reveal whether Degla actually returns control and time to the people doing the work. I would want to spend a shift beside that operator.
