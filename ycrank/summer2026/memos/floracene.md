## The Idea

- **Problem:** Independent surgery centers lack price and clinical-equivalence information when surgeons select implants based on training, brand familiarity, or device-representative input, while implants account for approximately 40–60% of orthopedic and spine procedure costs ([Y Combinator, Jul 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/floracene)).
- **Approach:** Floracene uses each center’s manufacturer contracts to present surgeons with three or more clinically equivalent implant options and their prices for a procedure ([Y Combinator, Jul 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/floracene)).
- **Differentiation:** Floracene recommends contract-specific equivalent implants at the selection point, whereas Hybrent comparison-shops general supplies, AssistIQ captures supplies already used, and AlloDirect directly sources allografts ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/floracene), [Hybrent](https://hybrent.com/markets/), [AssistIQ](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250528906219/en/AssistIQ-Raises-%2411.5-Million-to-Fix-Supply-Blind-Spots-with-AI-Preventing-Missed-Charges-and-Avoidable-Costs-in-Surgical-and-Procedural-Care), [Orthopedics This Week](https://orthotw.com/press-releases/allodirect-opens-its-allograft-procurement-platform-to-hospitals-and-idns-nationwide-1781282095742)).
- **Business Model:** [Inferred]: Floracene could charge surgery centers a subscription, savings-linked fee, or procurement commission because the product uses center-specific contracts to identify lower-cost implant choices, but no pricing model is published.
- **TAM/SAM:** Floracene states that U.S. surgery centers spend approximately $7 billion annually on implants and estimates 20–30% is recoverable through equivalent-device substitution, but no independently sourced growth rate or specific SAM was found ([Y Combinator, Jul 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/floracene)).
- **GTM / Distribution:** [Inferred]: Direct outreach to independent orthopedic and spine surgery centers is the likely path because physician-owners control implant economics, while the public website currently provides only a “Get in touch” contact action ([Floracene, Jul 2026](https://www.floracene.com/)).

## Key Risks

- **Clinical-equivalence validation:** Floracene’s product depends on determining which implants are clinically interchangeable, but the 90%+ interchangeability figure is attributed only to company interviews with orthopedic surgeons and no validation methodology is published ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/floracene)).
- **Contract-data completeness:** Recommendations require accurate center-specific manufacturer contract terms, yet no public source describes Floracene’s contract ingestion, normalization, or price-update process ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/floracene)).
- **Surgeon behavior:** The company says implant selection is influenced by training, habit, brand comfort, and device representatives, creating adoption friction even when a cheaper option is displayed ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/floracene)).
- **Workflow expansion by incumbents:** Hybrent already comparison-shops supplies and tracks physician costs, while SourcePlus PCX receives supplier pricing updates and integrates with ASC systems, placing both in adjacent workflows ([Hybrent](https://hybrent.com/markets/), [Surgical Information Systems](https://www.sisfirst.com/advanced-supply-chain-management)).
