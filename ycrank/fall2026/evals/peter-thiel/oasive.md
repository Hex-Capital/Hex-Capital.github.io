# Oasive -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Oasive’s possible secret is that board minutes and local news reveal municipal credit changes before conventional financial datasets do. That proposition is testable: compare its research with incumbent coverage and measure whether analysts discover material information earlier. But this is my reconstruction. The dossier supplies neither a named prevailing belief that Howland rejects nor results testing that disagreement. Source-linked research establishes a useful product design; it leaves the secret unproved.

The initial customer is reasonably specific: municipal credit analysts researching borrowers. The ownership mechanism is harder to identify. DPC DATA already supplies obligor financials, disclosure archives, and issuer news; MuniPro generates source-based credit memos. Oasive’s borrower pages could be substantially better, but the dossier documents no exclusive municipal inputs, measured technical advantage, or accumulating network effect. Its advertised proprietary prepayment models concern Agency MBS. Transferring that claim to municipal research would manufacture the central investment evidence.

Anaïs Howland provides the strongest reason to take the company seriously. Wells Fargo MBS research, Google Treasury portfolio management, and Google AI strategy put her inside both the customer’s problem and the enabling technology. This fits my revealed willingness to back financial infrastructure built from operating experience. However, expertise does not establish founding alignment. YC lists a sole founder, which makes co-founder history inapplicable; full-time status, ownership, and responsibility for core engineering remain undisclosed. Her earlier venture does not establish divided commitment. I see no documented founding-structure red flag.

The strongest objection to passing is Oasive’s company-reported use across **15 institutional desks** at its September 2026 MBS/rates launch. Reaching global banks, large asset managers, and corporate treasury teams is meaningful evidence at this stage. Institutional demos and a paid-pilot offering also fit the buying process better than an unexplained promise of viral adoption. The limitation is specific: these desks establish access to adjacent fixed-income customers, while municipal adoption, paid conversion, and repeatable acquisition remain unreported. My expectation is that, within 36 months, Oasive will principally sell MBS/rates research rather than establish a dominant municipal research business.

The economics reinforce that expectation. Macro & Rates has a concrete **$200/user/month** price; Agency MBS offers custom pilots and annual contracts. Municipal pricing is undisclosed. At the published seat price, $100 million in annual revenue would require roughly **41,667 paying seats**. Enterprise contracts could change that arithmetic, but their values and margins are unknown. Multiple fixed-income tools are not inherently incoherent; they do leave the dominant revenue engine unresolved. Licensed-data termination and export restrictions further constrain control over important inputs without proving the business unworkable.

I would pass on investing my own money today. The commodity-workflow warning deserves attention, although the evidence does not establish that Oasive is interchangeable with its competitors. I also find no exception sufficient to override the missing monopoly mechanism: no independently demonstrated technical breakthrough, persistent municipal transaction behavior, or validated deployment economics. **One result would reopen the decision: a paid municipal cohort renewing after replacing an incumbent because Oasive’s proprietary borrower histories repeatedly surfaced material credit developments the incumbent missed.** That would connect the secret to customer behavior and value capture. The current dossier establishes relevant expertise and institutional access, but does not yet establish that connection.
