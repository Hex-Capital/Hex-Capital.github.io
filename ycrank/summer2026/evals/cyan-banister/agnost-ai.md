# Agnost AI -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032: software agents handle meaningful work, every completed task becomes training material, and product teams manage behavior rather than pages. Agnost AI has found a leak from that world. An agent can return HTTP 200 while the human repeats herself, abandons the task, or quietly loses trust; clustering those conversations and tracing them back to tool calls is a more honest product signal. But the dossier does not map the intervening transitions or show which indispensable piece Agnost will own. I can see the future. I cannot yet see why this company captures it.

The working artifact is real. More than one million daily events, reported use by teams at Google, Exa Labs, and Corgi Insurance, and an integration statement from a Google engineer are meaningful at pre-seed. The earlier product also processed roughly 2.5 million MCP calls monthly. Still, event volume is not user devotion: the dossier gives no paid cohort, renewal, repeated workflow, or example of a team shipping a reviewed pull request and returning because it worked. Founder-reported usage therefore reaches the behavioral-proof cap, not the high band.

Shubham and Parth are credible builders. Cisco analytics, Formbricks product experience, terabyte-scale Microsoft pipelines, and open-source contributions all fit the technical problem. Their eight-year relationship and the announced Growth Engineer suggest some recruiting ability. What is missing is the dated first-believer story: no pre-company experiment, operating wound, or independently built artifact shows that either founder was obsessed with this failure mode before agent observability became a crowded category. The move from MCP analytics to conversation analytics may be intelligent learning, but it may also be a fast pivot within the hottest present wave.

The broken rule is unusually crisp: infrastructure treats technical completion as user success. Agnost restores visibility to teams that otherwise cannot see frustration or unmet demand, and its $49, $499, and Enterprise tiers make the commercial path legible. But the rent remains unnamed. We do not know how much failed-agent behavior costs customers, who presently captures that waste, or whether fixing it changes retention enough to command a durable budget rather than an observability add-on.

The strongest bull case stings: conversation-derived intent plus trace-linked, historically tested pull requests could become the improvement loop for every agent, and one million events per day could compound into customer-specific failure histories. The bear case is more concrete today. LangSmith, Braintrust, Arize, and Langfuse already control adjacent workflows; no source establishes pooled proprietary learning or a scarce technical asset; and published retention can be as short as seven days. Agnost also ingests conversations, generations, handoffs, and tool calls. VPC deployment and audit logs help, but explicit consent and custody boundaries are not yet disclosed clearly enough for my privacy gate.

I am Neutral. My 36-month base case is that Agnost remains below $2 million ARR and repositions around automated agent QA because conversation analytics is absorbed into larger observability suites. I would flip to Invest on one verifiable condition: ten independent customers each paying at least $2,000 monthly and renewing after twelve months specifically because the conversation-to-pull-request loop produced fixes they shipped.
