# rekursiv.ai -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is: coding agents and frontier models now let ML research teams buy autonomous experimentation, and the economic variable is whether rekursiv.ai can produce independently verified research improvements per dollar and day materially faster than human-led teams. The company has identified the right mechanism—hypothesis generation, experiment execution, failure diagnosis, and evidence-based iteration—but not the threshold that makes it commercially decisive. Its 235 ARC experiments across 15 directions and 684 Sudoku experiments in four days show throughput; they do not yet compare discovery cost or researcher time against a human baseline.

The technology shift is real. During November–December 2025, four agents using Claude Max and one GPU reportedly produced an ML paper for roughly $500. The subsequent ARC and Sudoku campaigns demonstrate that this is an operating system rather than a static demo. Yet the strongest results remain company-reported: ARC used public evaluation, rekursiv.ai expects a four-to-six-point semi-private decline, and its Sudoku method did not transfer directly to ARC. The product has therefore proved that autonomous experimentation can generate candidates, not that it reliably generates valuable, generalizable discoveries.

The strongest part of the company is workflow depth. Multiple agents propose hypotheses, define evaluations, run code, review one another, terminate weak experiments, update leaderboards, and preserve claim-to-evidence traces. Better foundation models should improve this loop rather than absorb the entire product. The unresolved red flag is the supposed data moat: discoveries and components are Apache-licensed, Sakana AI publishes competing code, and rekursiv.ai has not quantified what remains proprietary or what it would cost a well-funded model lab to reproduce its experiment history and orchestration.

Commercial pull is the missing layer. The $5 million financing is not customer evidence; neither an access-request form nor a GitHub organization with 16 followers and a top repository at 29 stars demonstrates recurring use. There is no pricing, revenue, named customer, or external lab repeatedly entrusting research decisions to the system. Meanwhile, Sakana AI, Periodic Labs, Lila Sciences, and Edison Scientific have collectively raised substantial capital around adjacent AI-scientist markets. This validates the discontinuity but also suggests rapid market crystallization, where technical breadth alone may not determine value capture.

Dillon and Kondratyuk are unusually credible builders for this problem: TensorFlow Probability, Veo, VideoPoet, foundation-model pre-training, and world-model leadership are directly relevant. That rare technical supply is the strongest case for overriding absent product pull. The **Anthropic** exception does not cleanly apply, however: both had scarce researchers during a discontinuity, but rekursiv.ai has not shown a frontier-model oligopoly, differentiated capital requirement, or research asset that independently establishes strategic control. Nor does the dossier document prior startup learning, costly personal commitment, or the desperation signal required for additional founder credit.

My 36-month base case is that rekursiv.ai narrows from “AI scientist” into experimentation and evaluation infrastructure for model-development teams, reaching $3–8 million ARR if it finds a repeatable buyer. I would monitor rather than invest today. One condition flips me: three unaffiliated ML labs each renew at $250,000 or more annually after documenting at least a threefold reduction in cost per independently reproduced model improvement. That would connect the impressive workflow to the controlling market variable and show that the first product has become distribution rather than research theater.
