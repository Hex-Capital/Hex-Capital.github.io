# Phone Harness -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Phone Harness could let agents perform work inside applications that offer no usable API. That is a potentially important bottleneck: software cannot replace human work it cannot reach. But the dossier does not identify a particular labor pool, its current cost, or how much of it this product can eliminate. Four thousand GitHub stars measure developer attention. They do not measure the consequence of success. I cannot yet attach an enormous payoff to even a small probability of this working.

The engineering removes a real installation obstacle. Phone Harness uses iPhone Mirroring and OCR instead of the Xcode and XCTest runner required by iphone-use; Android control uses accessibility information. But eliminating installation steps does not establish a discontinuity in completed work. There is no comparative task-success, latency, or intervention benchmark. Multitouch, camera, and Face ID flows are excluded, and unlabeled controls require a vision-capable model. The decisive technical question is whether the harness enables reliable execution through an entire workflow. Minitap’s reported completion of 116 AndroidWorld tasks belongs to Minitap. It supplies no performance evidence for Phone Harness.

The strongest argument against passing is that Shawn Pana may already understand how to turn an accessible developer tool into infrastructure. His Browser Use growth experience is relevant, and approximately 4,000 stars, 403 forks, and an available cloud beta are meaningful accomplishments for one person. My actual investing pattern allows execution-led platforms; a scientific invention is not compulsory. An open-source control layer could attract developers before its most valuable applications become apparent. But forks do not establish recurring production dependence, and the dossier documents neither a proprietary learning loop nor founder behavior showing rapid belief revision or complementary recruiting. The exceptions I make for an extraordinary instigator addressing a quantified giant value pool, or for demonstrated exponential capability progress, lack comparable evidence here.

I would spend the next dollar testing dependable, trusted execution. Platform permissions, unsupported authentication flows, and the September 16, 2026 telemetry issue could each interrupt that mechanism. The issue reports transmission of scripts, OCR output, and unredacted typing arguments; current remediation has not been independently verified. That is a specific unresolved concern for a product handling logged-in phones, rather than proof that the present version remains unsafe. The dossier supplies no experiment with a budget, numerical threshold, and stop-or-change decision against these risks. Shipping a beta is progress, but it does not establish disciplined risk removal.

The economics remain equally open. At $0.05 per phone-minute, cloud access costs $3 per phone-hour before any separately incurred agent inference or human correction. Cheap device time can still produce expensive completed work when attempts fail. No utilization, retry, support, or fully loaded task-cost model establishes parity with human execution. Free personal use and $5 promotional credit do not establish permanent subsidy dependence either. My one verifiable flip condition is a paid-customer production trial of a recurring, permitted workflow showing at least 95% unattended completion and a tenfold reduction in fully loaded cost against measured human execution, with independently verified telemetry remediation as a prerequisite. That would connect capability, consequence, and economics. Today I pass with my own money.
