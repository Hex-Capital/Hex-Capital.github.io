# Sutrix -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B. Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** Hospital and academic research teams need to reconcile patient records and apply study-specific rules before they can analyze the data. If a biostatistics core can bring its first study live and reuse the data mappings, protocol rules, and reviewer decisions for subsequent studies, Sutrix could serve more research teams within the same institution with less setup per study. The memo documents an offer of software access to cores. It gives no example of a core bringing a buyer through paid activation. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Emory’s Winship Cancer Institute reported faster preparation of interpretable results, and BlueRock Therapeutics reported less manual reconciliation with steps visible for expert review. Sutrix reports paying teams at 10+ institutions and $150K+ in revenue over the months before the software release. That supports early willingness to pay for dataset preparation. The reported 15+ labs and 50+ studies are aggregate counts, so they leave repeat buying and paid expansion at individual accounts unresolved. Confidence is low because the evidence of buyer value is more specific than the evidence for the growth mechanism. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

**Second-market scenario.** If another market’s research teams have the same job and can provide approved data exports that the same product can process, the core workflow could travel. The memo gives no verified second-market rollout. The next institution could require fresh security and data-access approvals, source mapping, or institution-cloud setup. The next study could still add protocol configuration, compute, and exception review. Contribution improves if reuse cuts enough of that work to leave more of each study fee after delivery. The memo gives no measured costs for that test. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Sutrix may have found demand for a valuable study-preparation service whose delivery effort grows with each engagement. Scope, pricing, and timelines are set per engagement. Direct FHIR connectivity is described for sandboxes, production access may require approved endpoints or exports, and investigators still verify ambiguous records. Those facts make proportional delivery effort plausible, while leaving actual labor and cost per deployment unknown. The structural Pass gate requires evidence that the work scales proportionally without a supported improvement path. A paid rollout that measures reuse can resolve the uncertainty, so the action is Watch. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a biostatistics core in a second geography, acquired through the intended channel, complete a first paid study and add a second paid study using the same deployment? Paid invoices and deployment records showing reuse of the data connection and materially lower incremental setup and review costs would move the action to **Reach out**. A rollout that still depends on a fresh integration or comparable manual work per study would keep the action at **Watch**.
