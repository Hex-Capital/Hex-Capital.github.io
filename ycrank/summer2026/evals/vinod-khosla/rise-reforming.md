# Rise Reforming -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is plausible but unquantified. Rise Reforming points to almost 90% of U.S. wastewater biogas remaining flared or trapped in low-margin uses, alongside $41.6 billion of methanol and $11.09 billion of DME demand. But it never converts that opportunity into feedstock volume, chemical yield, avoided resource consumption, or a defensible U.S. serviceable market. Turning waste gas into chemicals could matter. The dossier does not show a 100x consequence.

The technical evidence is real. More than 1,800 hours of stable syngas production, a proprietary process, an IP filing, and a container already delivered separate this from climate PowerPoint. But stable syngas is not yet stable chemical production from dirty wastewater biogas. Rise supplies no measured yield, uptime, energy consumption, contaminant tolerance, or production-cost advantage against M2X, CHP, or pipeline RNG. M2X has already completed field deployments; CRecTech advertises a 10,000-tonne module. A process can be real without being a 5x discontinuity.

The use of capital is more intelligent than the technical benchmark. Rise has raised over $1.7 million and directed it toward a live-feedstock pilot, the correct kill risk given moisture, hydrogen sulfide, VOCs, siloxanes, and catalyst poisoning. The binding supply agreement and conditional DME offtake connect both ends of the experiment. It also avoids the red flag of spending primarily on customer acquisition while the process remains unproven. What is missing is a falsifiable operating plan: required runtime, allowable catalyst degradation, target conversion efficiency, pilot cost, and the pivot-or-stop response if those thresholds fail.

The founders provide the best signal. Three molecular-engineering students formed the company in 2023, built the bench reformer, and changed feedstock from plastic waste to biogas in 2025. That is evidence of tactical flexibility, although the dossier does not establish what data caused the revision. Their technical backgrounds are relevant, but the founding team comes from one institution and the advertised senior process-engineer role is not yet a difficult recruit won. Advisors cannot own scale-up risk.

The bull case is substantial. Rise fits an unstated pattern I value: inserting difficult chemistry into installed industrial infrastructure, with host supply and downstream offtake rather than asking consumers to adopt a green product. At pre-seed, 1,800 operating hours, a delivered container, and a binding supply agreement are unusually concrete. The known-exception argument would waive incomplete economics for an adaptable technical team purchasing an option on a giant existing value pool. I cannot apply it here because neither the captured value pool nor the scale consequence has been quantified.

Economics therefore remain the fatal gate. Rise intends to finance equipment, maintain dispersed installations, purchase biogas, absorb cleanup and utilization risk, and sell commodity chemicals, yet provides no bottom-up cost model or pricing. My 36-month base case is that by August 2029 Rise has narrowed from DME-plus-methanol to methanol, remains pilot-stage, and produces less than $1 million in annualized chemical revenue. I pass today. I would flip if the 2027 wastewater pilot completes 1,000 continuous hours on live biogas while demonstrating a fully loaded, unsubsidized production cost at or below fossil-derived methanol.
