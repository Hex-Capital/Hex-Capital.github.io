# Covera -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Covera has evidence that its founders can build and sell software. It has no disclosed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or indication that my check would supply genuinely scarce capital. Those are different underwriting questions. I give the missing deal terms the prescribed default; YC membership does not establish an auction. But I cannot price a personal investment from the quality of the product alone.

The strongest case is the bridge Venet and Regerat have already crossed. They met at Pretto in 2020, built Simio together, and sold it in 2024. Brokerage exposure plus a shipped CRM business is substantially more useful here than an impressive engineering résumé. This fits the pattern of founders translating between systems whose participants struggle to communicate. The remaining distinction matters: borrower-insurance experience does not establish mastery of independent US P&C agencies and their carrier relationships.

Hello Risk provides the most useful product evidence: 155 tasks over two weeks and a 90-second median quote-preparation time. Someone is repeatedly putting work through Covera. Persistent account context, document interpretation, and portal execution could make the product increasingly embedded in an agency’s daily operations. That earns intermediate credit for workflow adoption. The reported 50-plus agencies does not establish paid conversion, however, and the estimated 10 hours saved rests on assumed manual task durations. Covera has demonstrated activity inside a customer workflow; it has yet to demonstrate ownership of an economically defensible distribution channel.

The bull case stings because these founders have already completed a build-and-exit cycle, and a named agency is using the next product repeatedly. Human approval also makes the proposition more credible than promising unattended insurance submissions. But two corrected values show why review remains part of delivery economics. Meanwhile, Adhoc lacks write-back and BubbleIn imports omit policies. FurtherAI and Liberate already offer overlapping execution capabilities. The relevant AI red flag is whether maintaining connectors and correcting exceptions consumes the savings Covera sells. There is no evidence that it does, but no measured margin evidence resolves that question either.

Delete the next financing from Covera’s plan. Public pricing, paid revenue, burn, and runway are unavailable, so the profitable-path dimension receives its default. Customer ROI claims cannot substitute for Covera’s own cash economics. Likewise, the dossier supplies no round-funded hypothesis with a budget, deadline, and measurable result; recruiting a founding account executive does not establish one. The known exceptions to my usual gates require unusually hard conversion and retention evidence, external acquisition validation, or an explicit financing structure that avoids repeated raises. Covera’s prior founder exit strengthens the team assessment, but does not supply those missing company-level receipts.

I pass at the disclosed evidence level. This could become useful, durable agency software; the unresolved question is whether outside equity buys a finite discovery or subsidizes an integration and sales operation. One verifiable condition would reopen underwriting: an executable financing package below $10 million post-money, with a funded 12-month break-even plan supported by contracted agency revenue and measured inference, review, connector-maintenance, and support costs. That would connect the deal, the experiment, and the functioning business.
