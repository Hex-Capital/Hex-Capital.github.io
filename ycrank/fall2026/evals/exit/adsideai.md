# Adside.ai -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic sale to a paid-media agency that manages campaigns across several platforms. Adside’s documented workflow combines creative production, campaign setup, and monitoring. An agency could buy it to reduce operator hours across its client base, but the memo provides no measured savings or buyer interest. Adside reports 30 companies live at launch, and its published pricing gives it a way to recruit paid pilot customers. A plausible seed milestone is to demonstrate repeatable labor savings across Meta and Google while maintaining campaign performance. The full seed round size and the capital available for that work remain unconfirmed. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in cash within eight years, which requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The founders’ seven-figure Botdog sale involved a different product and leaves Adside’s acquisition price unanchored. (Founders & Team)

- **Modest sale, illustrative.** A $30 million enterprise value less $5 million in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $25 million in equity. Assuming no senior claims and an eligible sale of the full stake for cash within eight years, the holder receives $750,000, or 1.5×.
- **Strong sale, illustrative.** At $100 million in enterprise value with the same assumptions, $95 million in equity yields $2.85 million, or 5.7×. If only 80% of the stake is purchased within eight years at the same per-share price, realized cash falls to $2.28 million, or 4.56×.
- **Prolonged private.** If Adside keeps operating and reinvests available cash, the holder realizes $0 within eight years. The memo supplies no evidence of a funded payout or an eligible secondary purchase.

### Countercase

Meta’s direct agent access and Google’s native campaign tools could make it cheaper for an agency to assemble its own workflow. Adside’s cross-channel coordination is the strongest reason to buy, but the memo provides no measured benefit that would establish a premium over those alternatives. Campaign operations also depend on delegated account access, and the permissions needed after a change of control are unverified. These risks leave price and closing feasibility open. The memo establishes neither a structural block nor inadequate proceeds across the strongest credible route, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Adside demonstrate enough repeatable savings in cross-channel campaign operations to support an acquisition that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A paid agency pilot showing sustained labor savings at comparable campaign performance, paired with credible buyer pricing that supports roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity after deal adjustments, would move the action to Reach out if the holder can sell the full stake for cash within eight years.
