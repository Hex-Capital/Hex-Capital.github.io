# Hoplite -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and identity of the first institutional check are all missing. YC affiliation does not prove an auction, but it does not establish scarce capital either. I am being asked to buy into the most consensus-saturated software category of 2026 without knowing the strike price. Pre-seed explains the financing silence; it does not turn silence into favorable terms.

The product has a legible experiment hiding inside it, but the round does not name that experiment. Hoplite’s first installation converted ignored Sentry errors into proactive pull requests and continuously merged lower-priority tickets. A valid venture moment might be: automate the founders’ manual installation process and prove by a fixed date that ten businesses will let those agents merge production changes repeatedly. The dossier provides neither that milestone nor a deadline, however. “Deploy cloud software factories” is a direction; it is not a capital-allocation test.

The Sentry installation is the strongest fact here because it demonstrates causal workflow activity rather than audience theater. The 582 X followers and 329 LinkedIn followers have caused no documented purchase, while one customer’s error stream did cause useful code changes. The bull case is that hands-on installations expose Hoplite to proprietary environments, credentials, sessions, and failure data that could become a defensible dark pool. But one installation, with no payment or retention evidence, is access borrowed from a design partner—not yet access the company owns.

This is also where the competitive structure bites. Cursor already offers local-to-cloud handoff, reusable environments, Slack initiation, and parallel agents; Factory offers persistent machines, alerts, mobile control, and customer-hosted deployment. Hoplite’s iMessage and Sentry triggers are useful, but presently look like features available to vastly better-capitalized platforms. Ando is the relevant exception to my skepticism about AI applications: measurable accuracy, complete pilot-to-paid conversion, and complete retention overrode the category risk. Hoplite has the beginning of that mechanism but none of the conversion proof.

Morrissey and Redmond have built together before, and CarbonCopy reportedly reached 250 public-beta users and $855,000 in trading volume. Their Stripe and AWS internships, deployment-monitoring projects, and one working Hoplite installation show more shipping ability than résumés alone. They have not yet demonstrated scaled operation on both sides of the bridge, however: managing a customer’s software workflow is one side; running secure, economical cloud-agent infrastructure across many customers is the other.

Delete the next financing and the model disappears. There is no revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or paid tier; the visible offer is a free week containing $100 of credits, while founder-led installations add labor to uncertain compute economics. My 36-month base case is that Hoplite narrows into a Sentry-triggered remediation product and remains below $1 million ARR rather than winning the general cloud-agent layer. I pass today. One verifiable condition flips me: a disclosed sub-$10 million post-money first institutional round funding self-serve installation after at least ten businesses have paid for 90 days, with eight renewing at positive contribution margin without founder deployment work.
