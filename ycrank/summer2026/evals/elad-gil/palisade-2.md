# Palisade -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Three named CVE remediations make **Palisade** look most like workflow infrastructure on a compounding security category, while no customers, revenue, or repeat usage make revenue velocity its weakest dimension. **Palisade** has built an interesting mechanism before proving buying behavior: kernel telemetry becomes fleet-wide enforcement in minutes rather than weeks. That can become an index on increasingly complex device fleets, but today it is a technical demonstration, not something “just working.”

**Stripe** is the high rung on index-like positioning because every new internet merchant strengthened its developer-channel path into becoming the payments default. **Palisade** shares the load-bearing mechanism: every additional server, GPU, and cloud device creates more kernel events, vulnerabilities, and remediation work that could flow through one control plane. **Palisade** diverges because Cisco’s Tetragon already supplies eBPF observability and in-kernel enforcement, while Falco and Tracee occupy adjacent workflow paths; without proprietary deployment data or a distribution wedge, Palisade must win share rather than automatically ride fleet growth.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism for traction: visible adoption can still be a false signal when the company lacks structurally superior economics or position. **Palisade** is earlier and the signal is weaker—three CVE demonstrations, 79 LinkedIn followers, and five YC launch votes show that Palisade can produce artifacts, but none establishes actual buying behavior. There is no evidence of revenue velocity or a usage curve, so I am applying the 8/25 absent-evidence default; for an AI security product, production usage would be unusually easy to describe if it existed.

**eBPF/LSM-BPF enforcement** and AI processing of syscall, memory, process, and packet telemetry provide a plausible catalyst for **Palisade**, but the dossier does not establish that either capability changed the endpoint-security market specifically within the last 24 months. Over the next 18 months, the catalyst compounds only if **Palisade** converts kernel-policy generation into a safe remediation loop that incumbents cannot ship quickly; otherwise Cisco, Aqua Security, and Sysdig absorb the same technology into existing distribution. This is closer to a technical opening than a true Stripe-after-merchant-accounts market re-opening.

**Fnu Prince** has worked in offensive and defensive security since age 16 and Palisade has shipped working demonstrations against CVE-2024-1086, CVE-2025-6019, and CVE-2025-32463. That places **Palisade** above a credential-only founder profile, but below the Perplexity pattern because there are no public repositories, user-feedback loops, or dated iterations between customer conversations. The score flips into the invest range if Prince can show three production design partners and a sequence of weekly releases responding to their false-positive, rollback, and coverage requirements.

**Color Genomics** worked because commodity sequencing was already available and the remaining company was a workflow and data problem. **Palisade** is not yet that clean: Palisade needs AI-generated rules to be accurate, kernel enforcement to avoid fleet-wide outages, security buyers to trust autonomous remediation, and coverage to expand beyond the Linux/eBPF evidence disclosed. Those are partially coupled, but automated-remediation safety is an independent miracle from detection quality; the absence of false-positive, rollback, availability, and supported-platform measurements makes this closer to a science project than the Color architecture.

**Cisco’s Tetragon** makes the 36-month path for **Palisade** narrower than the pitch: I expect Palisade to retreat from broad endpoint fleets into Linux cloud-workload remediation, reach $1 million to $3 million ARR, and remain an acquisition candidate for Cisco or Aqua rather than become an independent endpoint platform. **Palisade** can monetize its strongest axis—the kernel enforcement workflow—inside a tractable environment, but the absent revenue curve and incumbent distribution prevent me from underwriting a broad-market outcome today.

Three CVE demonstrations support the strongest bull case: Prince has unusually early security depth, **Palisade** already ships rather than merely pitching, and security remains one of the open AI markets in *AI Market Clarity*. The Mistral exception is relevant—pre-revenue can be investable when the catalyst has happened and the founder has shipped the underlying capability—but **Palisade** lacks Mistral’s prior Llama-scale artifact and Palisade faces open-source substitutes already offering enforcement. I would flip to Invest if Palisade documents at least three production fleets, one named customer, and 90 days without a remediation-caused outage while endpoint usage grows above 20% month over month.
