# Vexo -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is Vexo’s strongest path. The Band captures conversations through a paired phone, turns them into searchable memory, and drafts email or calendar actions for approval. Vexo documents a permissioned connector that lets users retrieve memories with source references from assistants they already use. The seed-stage hypothesis is that professionals who routinely skip follow-up on informal commitments could act on them more often if capture and drafting save enough time after consent, review, and corrections. The memo gives no like-for-like measure of labor per correctly completed follow-up. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The memo records a $199 checkout charge that is refundable before shipment. The founder reports distributing prototypes to preorder customers, with the count undisclosed. That is an early willingness-to-pay signal, with repeat use and fulfilled sales still unknown. The founders’ embedded systems and consumer software experience fits the prototype stage. Consumer hardware fulfillment and reliable operation in the field remain to be shown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

**Scale scenario.** Assuming one $199 sale per retained buyer and no recurring fee, each sale must cover hardware, fulfillment, returns, support, and ongoing transcription, storage, and inference. More daily use could increase customer value and raise service costs on the same sale. The memo establishes neither the reachable buyer count nor contribution per buyer at sustained usage. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

With cheap, comparable models and well-funded competitors, Vexo may offer a better way to handle an existing workflow while retaining little of its value. Bee already offers wrist capture and follow-up, and Omi makes wearable firmware and agent access reusable. Vexo’s cross-assistant memory is a plausible reason to choose it, but the memo has no evidence that buyers stay for it once a rival provides comparable capture and AI. It also identifies no scarce input Vexo controls for Path A. These facts leave both Path B conditions unproven. The substitution evidence is substantial enough to withhold outreach, while the lack of a direct buyer comparison leaves the override untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

In a paid field trial with professionals who routinely skip informal follow-ups, does Vexo make those follow-ups viable and earn continued paid preference over a wearable with comparable capture and model capability? Compare both devices with the manual workflow at equivalent accuracy, counting consent handling, review, corrections, and retries. Repeat completion of previously skipped follow-ups with materially less labor, plus a durable choice to keep Vexo at a viable price, would support Reach out if the updated evidence clears the score and confidence gates. A clear failure on either condition would move the action to Pass.
