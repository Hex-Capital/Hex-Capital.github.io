# Kebra -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The proposed post-money valuation is unknown, the competing-term-sheet count is unknown, and YC plus SV Angel does not tell me whether this is the first scarce check or another efficiently auctioned batch company. That financing silence matters. Kebra may be inexpensive, but I am not going to invent cheapness from the absence of disclosed terms. More importantly, the dossier names no finite experiment the round buys—only a broad ambition to make field-service companies AI-native.

The strongest evidence is operational rather than promotional. Kebra is live with companies across Florida, Pennsylvania, and California, and Quality Air is specifically using it to capture jobs and automate post-job work. That is materially better than 395 LinkedIn followers or nine Launch YC votes. The product sits inside a real workflow where incomplete technician evidence delays claims, parts, invoices, and customer follow-up. This is a plausible translation layer between field technicians, back-office staff, and fragmented software systems.

But the dark pool is prospective. Kebra’s Company Brain could accumulate equipment histories, repair decisions, communications, images, and technician behavior; today there is no disclosed data volume, retention, exclusivity, paid conversion, or evidence that this context has caused a purchase. ServiceTitan, Jobber, and Housecall Pro already own installed distribution and offer overlapping AI functions. The counterfactual is ugly: if an incumbent adds competent post-job evidence capture, Kebra must own customer-specific workflow depth that cannot be replicated from the incumbent’s system of record. Nothing public yet establishes that depth.

The AI exception does not rescue the underwriting. Ando earned an exception through greater than 90% forecast accuracy, complete pilot-to-paid conversion, and complete retention. Kebra shares the embedded-enterprise-workflow mechanism, but not the measured accuracy, conversion, or retention that made model risk secondary to a functioning business. Kebra itself warns that outputs may be incomplete or inaccurate, and no gross margin reveals what review steps, inference, calls, and computer-use agents cost. Delete the next financing and I cannot yet see the controllable cash metric.

Hadife has built multimodal AI systems at Microsoft, while Hosny brings systems work from Arista and Berkeley teaching experience. That supports the technical side of the bridge. The dossier does not show either founder previously shipping or operating inside HVAC, plumbing, claims, parts distribution, or field-service administration. Their current deployments may be producing that access quickly, but current exposure is not the same as prior operating receipts. It is also unclear whether the nominal CEO has final authority when product, accuracy, and financing decisions collide.

I am passing at this evidence level. Kebra has found a real, messy workflow and has more validation than a generic AI wrapper, but I am being asked to underwrite unknown pricing, an unnamed capital experiment, unmeasured customer economics, and a moat that incumbent FSM vendors are structurally positioned to contest. I would flip if Kebra offered a sub-$10 million post-money round financing a dated test in which ten named deployments produce paid 90-day renewals at positive contribution margin.
