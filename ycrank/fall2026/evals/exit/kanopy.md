# Kanopy -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Kanopy’s strongest route is a strategic sale to a grid-software vendor. ACRT’s launch of Arborcision shows that a vegetation-management provider can integrate Kanopy’s video-derived clearance measurements into an existing service. Schneider Electric’s announced AiDASH acquisition shows strategic interest in adjacent vegetation software. A grid-software buyer could acquire Kanopy to add clearance measurement from ordinary video. The value of owning that capability depends on whether it reduces fieldwork enough to justify buying it. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

The seed-stage milestone is repeat paid deployments that demonstrate field-validated clearance accuracy and measurable fieldwork savings. Kanopy reports mapping 3,093 miles, and six utilities or contractors reportedly signed LOIs or master service agreements covering 270,000 miles of potential scope. That supports a path to testing the milestone. Paid repeat volume and the capital needed to reach it remain unknown. The hosted models and credentialed SDK make integration plausible, while assignment of customer agreements and transfer of footage rights remain unverified. (*Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The following are illustrative scenarios. Both sale cases assume $5 million in net debt and other deal adjustments, no preference diversion, and an eligible holder selling its full stake for cash at closing within eight years.

| Scenario | Bridge to holder cash |
|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in adjustments leaves $35M of distributable equity. The holder receives $1.05M, or 2.1×. |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $5M in adjustments leaves $95M of distributable equity. The holder receives $2.85M, or 5.7×. At 2.5% ownership, the same deal pays $2.375M, or 4.75×. |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with reinvestment and no eligible share sale or payout yields $0 in realized cash. |

The memo provides no acquisition price or quantified buyer economics to support the strong case, and it leaves independent liquidity routes unevidenced. (*Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Arborcision shows that a partner can already access Kanopy’s capability through a commercial integration. Competing inspection and grid-modeling tools give prospective acquirers other options, and utilities may retain field checks where remote measurements fall short. If those checks remain common, the savings from owning Kanopy could be too small to justify a price that clears the holder’s hurdle. The memo leaves both effective substitution and the attainable sale price unresolved, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Do repeat paid utility deployments show field-validated savings large enough to justify a grid-software buyer paying roughly $88 million in enterprise value under the assumed $5 million in adjustments? Customer-verified accuracy and fieldwork savings, paired with a credible estimate of how much of that value a buyer could capture through ownership, would move the action to Reach out if they support a price that clears the hurdle.
