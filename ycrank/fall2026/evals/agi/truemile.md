# TrueMile -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** The memo describes independent owner-operators booking freight one load at a time and sometimes driving back empty. TrueMile leases on owner-operators and plans outbound and return loads across the fleet. The market-opening hypothesis is that pooled dispatch and fewer empty miles could make reliable, recurring truckload service economical on a Texas or Sunbelt lane where shippers previously went without it. Whether such a lane exists is a scenario assumption. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

TrueMile reports that a dispatcher can manage nine times as many trucks. The relevant test is labor cost per completed, on-time shipment under both delivery models, including broker calls, required human approvals, rebooking, and exception handling. The full cost comparison must also account for empty miles, driver compensation, and carrier compliance. TrueMile separately reports 10% more monthly miles per truck, an early utilization signal. The memo leaves contribution per truck and the ability to repeat it on another lane unresolved. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

Under model parity, a shipper could still pay TrueMile to commit leased-on capacity, handle disruptions, and take responsibility for delivery. Running the carrier operation gives TrueMile some control over execution. How reliably it can secure that capacity, and whether it can do so on better terms than another carrier, remain open. TrueMile reports $57K in freight bookings over three weeks and solicited two dedicated-lane partnerships. Completed, collected, and repeat business from newly served shippers remains unverified. Dhaliwal’s reported family-fleet experience and deployment of his own solver support seed-stage execution credit. The decisive economics and buyer behavior are still supported mainly by early company reports and inference. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*)

### Countercase

TrueMile may be improving dispatch for freight that existing carriers already serve. Thin backhaul availability can leave trucks empty even with better planning, and cancellations can unwind linked loads and add manual work to a process that already requires human approval on every load. CloudTrucks and FleetWorks offer parts of the workflow, making it plausible that another carrier with equivalent models could match the service. The evidence leaves both Path B gates open. Neither a newly viable lane nor buyer preference under model parity has been demonstrated, and the memo contains no representative comparison that defeats either condition. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent shipper renew at a price that gives TrueMile positive contribution on a recurring lane that was uneconomic under conventional dispatch, when a carrier with equivalent model capabilities can bid? A paid lane trial would need to establish the prior economics, measure dispatch labor and full costs per completed shipment, and show that the shipper renews because TrueMile can reliably commit trucks and handle disruptions. That result would move the action to **Reach out**. A representative trial showing that the fully loaded savings cannot open the lane, or that the shipper readily switches to equivalent service, would move it to **Pass**. An inconclusive trial would keep it at **Watch**.
