### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Factory teams manually reconcile records across email, spreadsheets, and plant systems when a quality hold interrupts production. Horizontal describes agents that assemble the evidence and coordinate batch release across those systems, with human sign-off for critical actions. One plausible opening is a plant that can afford prompt investigation only for its most costly holds. If automation reduces the full cost of a successful, compliant investigation, that plant might pay to investigate routine holds promptly as well. This is a scenario assumption. Horizontal reports testing in operating plants and advertises 50% shorter release times. The memo gives no baseline, measured labor savings, or buyer evidence that faster release made a previously uneconomic workflow viable. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

Under model parity, a plant might still pay Horizontal for validated integrations and approval workflows that keep investigations reliable across its existing systems. Horizontal reports on-premises deployment and ISO 27001 certification, but the memo provides no buyer comparison showing that those capabilities drive preference when a rival can deliver equivalent outcomes. The proposed expansion unit is one workflow at one plant. Its economics depend on whether recurring hold volume can cover integration and validation costs after accounting for inference, human review, retries, exception handling, and ongoing support. The memo supplies no measured cost bridge. The public launch and the founders’ prior industrial sales and modeling work support seed-stage execution, while production reliability remains unverified. (*Defensibility*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*)

### Countercase

Horizontal may help existing factory teams clear the same holds faster while sign-off, exception handling, and site-specific validation preserve much of the old delivery cost. SAP’s announced shortage detection and rescheduling features give some customers an embedded option for overlapping work. The NVIDIA reference blueprint gives other vendors a starting point for coordinating factory agents. The memo establishes neither equivalence for batch release nor a failed labor-cost comparison. Both Path B conditions remain open to testing, which supports Watch at seed. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)
