# Asakana -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with seafood distributors, Asakana’s multilingual order capture, catalog matching, and exception review could serve the same recurring job abroad. The reusable part is the conversion of restaurant messages, calls, and notes into structured orders. How much of the ERP connection and messaging setup transfers across markets remains uncertain. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The documented buying path runs through demos, workflow inquiries, and individualized email quotes. The terms specify a commission on processed transaction volume. At a constant rate, routing more orders from an existing distributor through Asakana would increase its recurring bill, and accumulated order history could make the product more useful. Asakana reports live U.S. seafood deployments and says they returned 40% or more of order-desk time to selling. Payment status, realized revenue growth, and repeat billing are undisclosed, so commercial traction receives the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under a shared-ERP scenario, reusing a connector could reduce setup labor for each additional distributor. The contribution test is whether the commission covers attributable sales and onboarding labor, order-processing costs, and ongoing support. The memo establishes the billing unit and identifies custom ERP work as a potential cost driver. It leaves commission rates and labor per deployment unknown. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Individual quotes and custom connections for ERPs without APIs could keep growth dependent on hands-on selling and implementation. Staff also have to resolve ambiguous orders, and outbound messaging requires supplier-managed consent. If these dependencies recur across most target distributors, multilingual capture alone would do little to speed rollout. The structural gate remains open because the memo ties required custom connections to systems without APIs, leaving a credible route through distributors on compatible ERPs. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of paying distributors in the U.S. and one additional market get to live ERP posting through the same connector with mostly customer-run setup and positive incremental contribution? Deployment records, recurring invoices, and attributable sales, setup, processing, and support costs would answer it. A demonstrated rollout that raises the score to at least 65 with Medium or High confidence would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that substantial bespoke work is unavoidable across the viable target segment and makes faster growth uneconomic would move it to Pass.
