# Axiomat -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Ethan Kharitonov’s work on formal methods and AI verification at AWS is the strongest reason to take Axiomat seriously. He has worked inside the problem. Jack Naimer’s AI research and Elad Perlman’s utility-control software experience add relevant technical depth. But the dossier identifies no specific verification system Ethan personally shipped, its users, or its measured results. I give the team credit for relevant experience; exceptional specific knowledge still needs an artifact.

The decisive question is what Axiomat’s proof actually proves. Its product converts documentation and schemas into a logical business model, then verifies queries against that model. A mathematically valid query can still answer the wrong business question if “revenue” was encoded incorrectly. The scarce capability may therefore be constructing and maintaining the specification. Axiomat’s “never hallucinates” claim needs explicit boundaries around that capability. This is an unresolved technical claim, not evidence of dishonesty; the dossier supplies no founder-integrity veto.

The strongest bull case is that these founders can turn specification-building into infrastructure that every enterprise agent needs. That fits my recent preference for companies attacking underlying technical bottlenecks. Customer corrections could eventually produce an asset competitors cannot reproduce by copying the code. But today that asset is hypothetical: no proprietary model, exclusive correction corpus, or demonstrated switching cost is documented. AWS’s existing reasoning checks and the March 2026 SpotIt+ research show that other capable teams are attacking related problems. Neither establishes equivalence to Axiomat, but technical difficulty alone does not establish ownership of the solution.

Business-data queries provide a concrete wedge. Extending verified business definitions into other agent workflows is conceivable, yet the dossier supplies no demonstrated adjacent buyer, transaction, or spending path. Competitor financing establishes investor interest rather than customer economics. Axiomat currently solicits demos and conducts email and LinkedIn outreach; there is no evidence that one customer improves another customer’s product or acquisition. Nor is there a disclosed payer, price, or completed transaction. These are unknowns receiving the stated defaults, not evidence that a three-person pre-seed company has failed.

I pass with my own money today. My exceptions for unusually capable founders or strange early products require demonstrated recruiting behavior, a working prototype, exceptional capital efficiency, or an emerging user loop; none is established here strongly enough to override the missing durable asset. Axiomat also lacks the outsider-contribution mechanism behind my preference for permissionless networks. The team could build useful verification software, but the dossier has not shown what compounds beyond its labor. I would reopen on one verifiable condition: an independently audited deployment study showing that customer corrections accumulate into an exclusive, reusable verification asset that measurably improves subsequent customers’ deployments.
