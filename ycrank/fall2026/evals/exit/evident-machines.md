# Evident Machines -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a frontier AI lab is the strongest path. The memo records the launch of FrontierEE Bench and describes a physical lab that captures instrument readings, hardware failures, and expert workflows. The company reported a 29.3% average physical pass rate across 10 tasks that models passed at 100% in simulation. The standalone results were unavailable for verification. The reported gap points to a problem a lab could pay to solve. The ownership case is still an inference. A lab could pay to control a growing library of hardware failures and the ability to generate new ones if that control produces repeatable training gains on unseen equipment and gives the buyer a durable advantage over licensing or commissioning the work. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is a paid lab pilot that demonstrates those gains against a partner-built comparison and measures how much bench work each new task requires. Shipping the benchmark makes the test plausible. Manual wiring and probe placement create throughput risk, and the memo does not establish the cost of the pilot or the runway to complete it. A sale would also depend on transferable rights to the data, task library, and tooling. The memo describes paid outside contributions and leaves the assignment terms open. It supplies no independently supported route to cash for this holder if the acquisition fails. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Using the thesis’s illustrative seed terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million in gross holder cash, requiring $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. The memo provides no usable evidence of acquisition pricing or buyer savings, so the sale prices below are scenario assumptions. Both sales assume zero net debt and other closing adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. Both assume full cash payment at close, simple pro rata allocation with no preference impact or fees, and zero stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, or compensation. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

- **Modest sale in year 5.** $40 million of enterprise value yields $1.2 million in holder cash, or 2.4×.
- **Strong sale in year 7.** $100 million of enterprise value yields $3 million, or 6×. At 2% ownership it yields $2 million, or 4×.
- **Prolonged private through year 8.** Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The investor retains the shares and realizes $0, or 0×.

### Countercase

Anthropic’s collaboration with Diode shows that a frontier lab can build overlapping electronics evaluations with a hardware partner. If a lab can obtain comparable training gains through that arrangement or a license, the value of buying Evident Machines could fall below the proceeds hurdle. The collaboration establishes a credible substitute for some of the work. The memo leaves open whether it can match Evident’s physical failure data or what a lab would pay for the difference. That gap warrants Watch. The evidence falls short of triggering a Pass override. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid frontier-lab pilot show that owning Evident’s data and bench capability would be worth materially more than licensing it or commissioning equivalent work? Move to Reach out if the lab confirms repeatable training gains on unseen hardware against its best available substitute and quantifies an ownership advantage that could credibly support the $83.3 million equity hurdle under the base ownership assumption.
