# Definite -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The founders have collided with the problem. Gurshabd saw manual attestations and approval controls at BitGo; Mazin saw regulatory numbers repeatedly checked at Optiver and built financial-analysis agents at Boosted.ai. This is stronger than an “AI for accounting” idea manufactured from a market report. It also exposes a real schlep: dirty source data, policy encoding, evidence lineage, deterministic calculations, and approval workflows. These are precisely the details that general automation tools prefer to assume away.

But encountering the problem is not the same as proving resourcefulness. The dossier does not name a Definite deployment that broke, what the founders did next, how long the workaround took, or what changed for a user. I cannot substitute their Waterloo, Meta, Optiver, and BitGo credentials for that sequence. Nor can I count building the verification engine twice: the engine is technical evidence, while the missing response to resistance is founder evidence.

The technical case is fairly good for pre-seed. Definite has separated document interpretation from deterministic financial math and reports that its verification engine with DeepSeek V3 beat GPT-5 on FinBalance at roughly half the cost, as well as published FinAuditing results. That is a functioning, measured claim rather than a diagram. But it remains company-reported and unreplicated. There is no disclosed production report, completed close, or customer workpaper against which to test the benchmark’s relevance.

The strongest bull case is that these three founders have known one another since their first year at Waterloo, possess unusually relevant technical and financial experience, and have already assembled the beginning of a difficult substrate. Customer-specific mappings, policies, evidence relationships, and approval logic could become sticky. Custom order forms with advance payment terms also show that they understand this is enterprise software, not a consumer toy. Yet no named bank, credit union, fintech, or insurer is shown using or paying for it. The 170 LinkedIn followers, 11 YC Launch votes, and roundup coverage are attention, not pull.

The route changes are ambiguous. Ledgix became Bylaw, then bank regulatory reporting, then broader reconciliation and control workflows. This might be a running back searching quickly for the right opening. It might also be three adjacent nouns without a user observation deciding among them. The dossier supplies no rejected hypothesis, usage result, or customer request that caused any of these changes, so I cannot reward the motion merely because it occurred.

The red flag is not competition by itself. Numeric, Stacks, and Basis have raised $38 million, $35 million, and $137.6 million respectively, but a small team can still win by learning faster. The problem is that Definite’s evidence does not yet show that learning loop. Its highest signal is founder-originated schlep; its lowest is users pulling the crude product back. That combination often produces an impressive system in search of the exact job people will urgently buy. I am passing now. One event would flip me: a named regulated institution completes three recurring reporting cycles after the founders personally repair a broken data integration within two weeks, with the resulting evidence-linked workpapers accepted by its finance team.
