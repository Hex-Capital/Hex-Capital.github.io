# screenpipe -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. An agent does not wait for you to summarize a meeting or connect the right application; it remembers the contract clause you viewed, the objection someone voiced, and the sequence of clicks that completed a task. screenpipe has captured a small leak from that world: a working, cross-platform memory layer connected to agents through MCP. Microsoft Recall and the disappearance of Rewind also confirm the demand. What is missing is the full path from personal recall to trusted institutional memory—especially who may record whom, which memories an agent may use, and how those permissions survive inside a company.

Louis Beaumont looks like a first believer rather than a tourist. He says he maintained an AI second brain beginning in 2020, before the current agent rush, and his infrastructure, machine-learning, computer-use, and intelligence work plausibly supplied the operating tuition. The 12,176-commit repository is the better biography: cross-platform capture, accessibility extraction, transcription, recovery, encryption, search, and permissions are difficult, unglamorous work. Twenty thousand nine hundred stars and 2,100 forks show he recruited technical belief, although the dossier does not establish complementary leadership or who owns enterprise sales and privacy governance.

The strongest evidence is behavioral. By February 2025, 202 people were paying $3,480 MRR, more than 4,000 copies were downloaded monthly, and 320 people were active weekly. Users are permitting a functioning product to observe screens and audio all day, consuming 5–10% CPU and 5–10 GB each month. That is costly and weird behavior. The founder’s later claims of sixfold active-user growth and 20–30% weekly revenue growth are not sufficiently quantified, but they sit on top of a real paid baseline rather than a waitlist.

This could become enabling infrastructure below the crowded agent interface layer. The controlled asset is not OCR, Whisper, SQLite, or MCP individually; those are available to everyone. It is the reliable cross-platform pipeline that turns messy human activity into permissioned, queryable context. APIs and scheduled pipes allow many downstream workflows, while local storage and model choice reduce dependence on a closed provider. Yet Microsoft can bundle overlapping capture, and exportability limits data lock-in. The moat must become trusted operation, not merely comprehensive recording.

Here is where the bet bites me. screenpipe describes disconnected applications as the broken system, but it does not identify a rent, artificial restriction, or gatekeeper whose power is transferred to the user. More seriously, its enterprise product adds MDM and device controls to a system capturing microphones, communications, and secrets. Local storage, exclusions, encryption, retention, and PII filtering address custody; they do not prove that coworkers and customers meaningfully consent to being recorded. The bull case says 300,000 claimed users, accelerating revenue, and deep open technical adoption can make this the neutral memory substrate before incumbents react. The bear case says the same dataset becomes workplace surveillance, and the enterprise buyer—not the individual—gains the agency.

I am neutral with my own money today. The artifact and founder obsession are unusually strong, but privacy is part of the product architecture, not a compliance task to complete later. One condition flips me: three deployments of at least 100 seats must renew after independent privacy review while enforcing visible participant consent, role-scoped agent access, and auditable deletion. That would show screenpipe can preserve the strange user value without turning ambient memory into an institutional trap.
