# Coasty -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Prateek Jannu’s ML-inference work and Nitish Kovuru’s computer-vision and agent-automation background make Coasty a specific-knowledge company without a network. That is Coasty’s signature: technically unusual founders attacking computer use at the moment models cross a capability threshold, but every customer currently consumes an independent automation service. The code may compound. The users do not.

**Stack Overflow** is the high rung for specific knowledge: developers building the tool they already needed, with shipped work preceding the financing story. Coasty approaches that rung through Prateek’s fault-tolerant ML pipelines, Nitish’s graduate AI teaching, prior agentic-sales automation, and an 82.81% OSWorld Verified result. Coasty diverges because neither founder is documented as having spent years inside accounting, insurance, healthcare, or compliance—the workflows where Coasty claims its sharpest pain. They know the machine exceptionally well. They know the user less clearly.

**Perplexity** is the low network rung: a single-sided product where one additional user does not automatically improve the prior user’s experience. Coasty has the same structural absence. Thousands of paying users across four continents may generate failure trajectories, but Coasty has not shown that customers contribute supply, attract demand, or create shared infrastructure by default. The Perplexity exception could apply, but only when permissionless distribution, rare founder knowledge, and category timing all clear emphatically. Coasty clears founder knowledge and timing; its bespoke proof-of-concept motion leaves permissionless distribution unresolved.

**82.81% OSWorld Verified** demonstrates code leverage in production mechanics, not yet in distribution. Over 18 months, Coasty’s $0.03 grounding requests and $0.04–$0.10 actions could turn every integration into a self-serve API call with near-zero replication cost. The present motion—soliciting workflows and building proofs of concept for operators in regulated industries—compounds labor before code. Coasty’s 111-star open-cowork repository matches my builder-product pattern weakly, while Product Hunt’s 84 upvotes and three comments show that open distribution has not yet become the dominant engine.

**$54.8 billion by 2034** suggests enough market surface for Coasty to move from legacy desktops into browser infrastructure, testing agents, compliance automation, or a general computer-control layer. Coasty earns pivot-room because the same perception, state, verification, and recovery primitives can cross verticals. The score would fall a tier if healthcare, tax, and insurance each require separate compliance stacks, human approval systems, and bespoke integrations; it would rise if one API demonstrably controls both desktop and browser workflows across those categories without vertical implementation work.

**Notion** is the relevant founder-trio parallel: intelligent builders who kept rebuilding before the market understood the product. Prateek leaving Stanford, Nitish teaching graduate AI as an undergraduate, and Coasty moving from an “AI employee” VM product to developer-controlled automation read as intelligence and energy rather than résumé theater. Coasty provides no comparable evidence about clean prior ventures, treatment of colleagues, or ten-year relationship behavior, so integrity remains at the absent-evidence default. Silence is not a red flag. It is not a reference check either.

**Thousands of active users and paying customers** lead me to forecast that by July 2029 Coasty reaches $8–15 million ARR as a developer API for reliable legacy-software automation, while remaining a single-sided infrastructure business rather than becoming a network. Coasty’s mechanism is metered code leverage: the 82.81% benchmark attracts developers, production failures improve recovery, and usage expands through prepaid actions and VM-hours. The ceiling is large, but without user-added compounding the outcome geometry looks more like valuable infrastructure than Uber-scale network optionality.

**UiPath’s $1.43 billion FY2025 revenue** supplies the strongest bear case: Coasty’s benchmark lead is temporary, foundation models absorb grounding and recovery, and regulated customers reject Coasty’s terms permitting customer-content processing through infrastructure providers. Coasty could then become a thin orchestration layer trapped between model vendors and enterprise procurement. The dossier pushes back with verified benchmark leadership, paying customers on four continents, and a two-person operation—real technical leverage before heavy financing. I remain Neutral because distribution is still the missing proof. I would flip to Invest if Coasty documents at least $1 million ARR with a majority of new usage acquired self-serve and no founder-built proof of concept.
