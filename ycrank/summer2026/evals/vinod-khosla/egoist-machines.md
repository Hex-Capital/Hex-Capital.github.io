# Egoist Machines -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Egoist Machines offers better consent plumbing for AI memory, but the dossier does not show an order-of-magnitude consequence. It names no spending pool, affected population, or expert bottleneck that AI Passport could collapse. Users avoiding repeated explanations across ChatGPT and Claude is useful. It is not yet a 100x societal outcome. A user-controlled identity layer could eventually become consequential, but “could” is not evidence.

The product has thoughtful mechanics: field-level requests, duration limits, approval, revocation, and access receipts. Those distinguish it conceptually from OpenAI’s native memory and developer-controlled systems such as Mem0 and Letta. But there is no measured improvement in security, onboarding time, context accuracy, or application retention; no disclosed integration partner; and no evidence that the architecture is difficult to reproduce. Today this is a permissions interface surrounded by better-funded memory infrastructure and platform owners that control the necessary APIs.

The team is the strongest part of the option. David Khachaturov brings a Cambridge computer-science PhD, OpenAI PaperBench work, AI-security experience, and a 4,700-star GitHub project. Erin McGurk ranked first in her Cambridge cohort and has built educational channels reaching more than one million followers. That is unusually complementary technical and consumer-distribution capacity for two people. What is missing is the behavior I care about more than credentials: a documented belief revised after evidence, or a difficult recruit brought in to own ecosystem adoption, security assurance, or developer distribution.

The largest kill risk is coordination, not implementation. A passport without applications is a database; applications without users will not integrate it. Egoist has opened a consumer waitlist and developer-pilot form, but 268 company LinkedIn followers and press coverage do not test that loop. The next dollars should buy one decisive result: several independent AI applications integrating the passport and generating repeat, user-approved context transfers. Feature development, university visits, and free bagels do not remove the existential risk unless they produce that behavior.

The bull case is real and uncomfortable enough to take seriously. Native AI memory expanded during 2025–2026, the founders combine security depth with unusual audience reach, and a trusted neutral permissions standard could accumulate switching costs across applications. But the dossier currently shows no active users, partner commitments, proprietary learning loop, or economic model. “Free for life” on the consumer side may accelerate adoption, yet paid developer access remains inferred and there is no bottom-up path to sustainable cost parity.

I pass. My 36-month base case is that Egoist either shuts down or pivots into developer-facing consent and identity infrastructure because consumer Passport adoption cannot overcome platform substitution and integration friction. One result flips me: three independent AI applications in production, each generating repeat passport usage from at least 10,000 monthly users, with developers paying because portable context measurably improves activation or retention. That would convert an elegant interface into infrastructure and identify the large value pool currently absent from the dossier.
