# Ooak Data -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Seven-figure contracts with the two largest AI labs make Ooak Data a high-potential market bet with a low-permissionless-leverage architecture. Ooak Data sits inside a training-data market forecast to grow from $3.59B to $23.18B, but growth currently requires negotiated contracts, purchased workflow rights, and human GTM. The market compounds. The distribution does not. This is AI infrastructure built with capital and relationships rather than code escaping into the world.

**Uber** is the high rung on pivot-room: black cars became UberX, delivery, and a global transportation network without abandoning the underlying market. Ooak Data has comparable conceptual room across training data, agent evaluations, workflow simulation, and enterprise digital twins. The $23.18B market forecast supports that optionality. Ooak Data diverges where Uber’s marketplace could recruit supply incrementally; acquiring more than 300 company ecosystems at up to $300K each means every pivot may require fresh capital, fresh permissions, and fresh data rights.

**Anchorage Digital** is the low rung on permissionless leverage because regulatory permission was not friction around the product—it was the product’s moat. Ooak Data also depends on permission: enterprises must release emails, documents, financial figures, and collaboration data, while AI labs buy through seven-figure contracts. But Ooak Data has disclosed no equivalent charter, exclusivity, or structural barrier that converts permission into defensibility. Six open roles spanning sales, marketing, US GTM, and operations activate my red flag: labor leverage is arriving before permissionless distribution. Ooak Data could build valuable code, but valuable code is not automatically code leverage.

Up to $300K paid per acquired ecosystem makes Ooak Data a library, not yet a network. Over 18 months, each additional workflow can improve product breadth and reuse economics, but one supplier does not automatically recruit another supplier or increase the next supplier’s incentive to join. Ooak Data must buy, anonymize, calibrate, and sell each increment. The proposed 300 acquisitions may create a proprietary corpus, yet no completed ecosystem count, exclusivity, or customer-to-supplier loop is disclosed. Data accumulation can compound internally. Networks compound externally.

**Perplexity** cleared my specific-knowledge exception because Aravind Srinivas brought unusually deep retrieval expertise into a permissionlessly distributed AI product. Ooak Data has a credible but less singular parallel: Thomas Aubry was Samsung applied-ML scientist and PayLead Head of Data, Grégoire Lamy ran Ops & Data at Epsor, and Pierre-Louis Vouteau previously built a product reporting 15,000 users. Ooak Data therefore has relevant operating knowledge across sourcing, data operations, and ML. Thomas’s largest disclosed GitHub repository has 23 stars, however, and the dossier shows no multi-year obsession with RL environments or proprietary technical artifact predating the financing story. Specific knowledge is present. Inevitability is not.

Pierre-Louis began Ooak Data alone, Grégoire joined roughly a year before YC, and Thomas joined later without a shared prior employer or university. That sequence demonstrates recruiting energy, while seven-figure contracts demonstrate execution intelligence; no integrity warning appears. The 10-year-partner score would flip upward if Ooak Data showed that the three founders had previously shipped through conflict together, treated former collaborators well, or divided authority through an operating history rather than titles. Until then, intelligence and energy are observable. Integrity and partnership durability remain appropriately unscored rather than presumed.

**Scale AI** already sells adjacent RL environments, and Ooak Data’s customer concentration and procurement-heavy architecture point toward consolidation rather than a permissionless breakout. My falsifiable 36-month forecast is that by July 2029 Ooak Data reaches $12M-$30M ARR and is acquired by Scale AI for less than $500M, primarily for its reusable workflow rights and anonymization pipeline. Ooak Data can become a successful supplier because two seven-figure lab contracts validate demand. The same buyer concentration and capital-intensive sourcing make a stand-alone 10,000x network outcome less likely.

Seven-figure contracts with the two largest AI labs are the strongest case against that forecast: Ooak Data may already have found the scarce input frontier labs cannot manufacture internally. **Anchorage Digital** shows that permission can become a moat, while **Perplexity** shows that an AI product can overcome the absence of a network. The bull case stings because 300 exclusive company ecosystems could become irreplaceable training infrastructure. The dossier does not yet win that case: Ooak Data reports neither exclusivity nor completed ecosystem count, offers suppliers up to $300K, and faces Scale, Surge, Mercor, and Snorkel. I would flip to Invest if Ooak Data verifies at least 100 reusable, contractually exclusive ecosystems and shows that a majority of new revenue comes from standardized repeat licensing rather than custom enterprise work. Until then, a good business is not necessarily my business.
