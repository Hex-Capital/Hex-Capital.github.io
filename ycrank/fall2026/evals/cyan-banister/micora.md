# Micora -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I would like a remote factory to order electricity the way it orders a forklift: equipment arrives, unfolds, and starts earning its keep. Micora’s stackable solar modules and battery container make that future tangible. But the important transition is from delivering equipment to delivering dependable power with very little site-specific work. The dossier does not yet establish the intervening steps or show that autonomous deployment has crossed that gap. That future is my hypothesis, rather than something I can credit Micora with having demonstrated.

Why does the factory need diesel deliveries in the first place? Because the grid does not reach it, and fuel can travel where transmission infrastructure cannot. Micora attacks that logistical dependency and the labor required to install an alternative. That could give remote operators meaningful control over their electricity supply. But fuel delivery and installation are real services, not demonstrated artificial rents. The purchase and 12- or 24-month lease options make access plausible; final pricing after site review, with land and ongoing operations excluded, leaves the actual transfer of economic control unmeasured.

The founders give me more confidence than the installation headline. Akemoto previously shipped heat pumps as Normal Robotics CTO. Choo designed drivetrains and handled manufacturing at Serve Robotics, then brings solar/BESS deployment experience from Tesla. Those are useful encounters with physical reality: things must move, survive, and get installed. Mareels adds finance and startup operating experience. This is substantive preparation for the job, although the dossier does not establish a long, independently pursued obsession or demonstrate how this particular team recruits first believers.

The most consequential question sits between “forklift-placeable” and “autonomous installation of 100 kWp daily.” Those descriptions could fit successive stages of the same process; they are not proof of dishonesty. But they leave the claimed advantage unverified. The October 21, 2026 LAKI Robotics coverage concerns planned robotic deployment. Micora’s reported Pacific-island and manufacturing deployments suggest equipment has reached users, but unnamed sites without dates, operating records, or repeat orders provide limited behavioral proof. BoxPower already supplies prefabricated microgrids. If Micora still needs comparable site labor, the customer may be buying another capable integration package.

Micora does fit my recurring attraction to concealed physical infrastructure: electricity makes other businesses possible. Yet standard LFP batteries and an integrated package do not establish control over a scarce enabling constraint. The strongest argument against waiting is that hardware progress often precedes tidy commercial evidence. My **CosmicBrain AI** exception shared that robotics-and-physical-infrastructure mechanism, but its functioning prototype, signed industrial proofs of concept, and documented lifelong founder obsession supplied specific substitutes for revenue. Micora’s relevant builders are encouraging; the dossier does not supply equivalent substitutes. Akemoto is clearly identified as CEO, and no affirmative governance or privacy violation appears, so the unresolved issue is operational proof.

I land at **Neutral** with my own money. Missing revenue is not what stops me; the uncertainty is whether automation changes deployment economics enough to create a different business. My flip condition is a customer-verified record of repeat deployments showing that Micora’s automated process consistently achieves the claimed 100 kWp/day while reducing fully accounted installation cost against the customer’s conventional alternative. That would turn the most interesting sentence in the pitch into something I could underwrite.
