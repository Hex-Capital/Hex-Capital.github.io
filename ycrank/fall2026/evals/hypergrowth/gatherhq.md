# GatherHQ -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Reach out  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with enterprise marketing teams, GatherHQ can plausibly serve the same recurring job across markets. Its product uses existing customer evidence to answer buyer questions and commissions fresh interviews when coverage is thin. One customer’s co-published report surveyed 1,392 consumers across seven countries, demonstrating cross-border fieldwork for a single account. Slack and the API provide reusable interfaces for the core workflow. Simulation quality in each new segment still depends on relevant, permissioned buyer data. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Slack Marketplace gives buyers a way to discover and install Emma for free, and published usage rates give them a clear price for the first task. The memo leaves open whether a typical enterprise team can connect its evidence, pay, and get a useful result without staff help. If teams use Gather for recurring campaign and research decisions, metered billing lets existing accounts spend more as workloads grow. The company reported upsells in April, around the shift toward market research. Whether expansion on the current product can offset churn remains uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Gather reported 50-plus customers in September and 60 in October, and the memo lists 65-plus enterprise marketing teams in November alongside a nine-person team. Those figures support early adoption. The paid share is unclear, the reported 10× growth has an unspecified metric, and the April report of more than doubled quarterly revenue leaves attribution to the current product unresolved. The pace of paid adoption remains uncertain. (GatherHQ, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

At list price, a $10 simulation has positive incremental contribution if inference, data handling, and attributable acquisition, onboarding, and support cost less than $10 per run after allocation. A separately billed $50 consumer interview or $100 B2B interview must also cover recruitment, any participant incentives, and delivery. Reusing permissioned research could reduce the need for new fieldwork as usage grows. The memo gives no measured costs for that workload mix. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Enterprise demos may still do most of the work of converting buyers, and teams with sparse or restricted data may need frequent fresh interviews before simulations are useful. If that is the ordinary workflow, staff time and recruitment costs could rise alongside revenue, while research demand could arrive in bursts. The structural downside gate stays open. The memo documents a reusable software workflow and a way to draw on existing evidence, with the amount of human work and fieldwork still unresolved. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a typical enterprise marketing team go from Slack install to repeat paid simulations at positive incremental contribution with minimal Gather staff time? Recent account records linking installation, first useful output, repeat billing, staff time, and associated fieldwork costs would answer it. A repeatable positive result would sustain Reach out.
