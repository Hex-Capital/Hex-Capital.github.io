# Kiwi -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Kiwi’s strongest path. A CRM or agent-workflow platform could buy Kiwi to make cross-tool agent runs auditable and catch regressions before workflow changes reach production. Kiwi describes an engine that replays the SQL behind a run against versioned data and uses reviewer corrections as regression tests. Chen reports spending 20 hours in one week reviewing and fixing agent work at a prior employer. That illustrates the problem. The memo documents Kiwi’s public launch, while production savings and buyer willingness to pay remain unverified. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

The operating milestone is repeatable, measured reductions in review time and escaped errors across paid production deployments, compared with native tools. The founders’ post-sales and SQL engineering experience supports a credible test. Paid production use, the cost of reaching that milestone, and available runway remain unverified. Any acquisition would also need to establish what IP and customer agreements can transfer, and whether continued use of customer data would require consent. The memo supplies no separate fallback that could pay this holder. (*Founders & Team*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** A $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× hurdle is $2.5 million in holder cash, requiring $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt and other price adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. They assume pro rata allocation after conversion, with all consideration paid in cash at closing and zero stock, escrow, earnout, rollover, or compensation. The prices illustrate the hurdle. Kiwi’s memo provides no usable pricing evidence. (*Key Facts*)

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value and equity consideration | Gross holder cash at 3% |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest acquisition | Year 5 | $40 million | $1.2 million (2.4×) |
| Strong acquisition | Year 7 | $100 million | $3 million (6×) |

### Countercase

HubSpot already uses MCP for cross-tool account summaries, and Kiwi itself distributes through MCP. A platform could cover enough of the workflow natively or license Kiwi’s capability, leaving little reason to pay a price that clears the holder’s hurdle. The overlap is documented. Whether native tools or a license can deliver equivalent replay and regression testing in production remains open, so the structural Pass gate is unproven. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

Move to **Reach out** if a buyer-side build-versus-license analysis uses measured production results to support a purchase price that could deliver $2.5 million to the holder within eight years.
