# Native -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Native reports $2M ARR within six months, but I cannot price my personal check from that number. The proposed post-money, competing offers, and whether my capital is actually scarce are all undisclosed. Those are missing facts, not evidence of an auction. The dossier also names no experiment the round will fund, with a deadline and a result that changes the company’s value. Native has demonstrated a business people buy; it has not established what investment opportunity I am being offered.

The strongest argument for writing the check is the founders’ bridge between content production and software. Bekken has produced content for OBOS, NTE, and BERRE; Hauga founded a marketing business; Gråsberg was CTO of Wepost. Together they shipped Wepost to a reported NOK80,000 MRR before starting Native. That fits my unstated preference for people who can translate between operating worlds. These are operating receipts, rather than three biographies that happen to contain “AI” and “marketing.” Native also names a CEO; three founders alone do not establish the equal-founder decision paralysis I worry about.

The potential asset is the feedback inside the publishing workflow. Native reports 2,200-plus paying customers and 40,000-plus posts in one month. Customers are purchasing an integrated process that selects, generates, and publishes content, creating opportunities to collect approvals, edits, rejections, and outcomes. That earns meaningful workflow credit. It does not yet establish that proprietary feedback causes purchases or retention. Training opt-outs weaken accumulation, and Blaze already offers a comparable strategy-through-publishing loop. Founder followers and company followers add nothing without evidence that their attention converts.

Here is the objection that makes passing uncomfortable: customers could be buying useful marketing labor today while Norn becomes an inexpensive upside option. At $79 per month, Native need not prove some grand theory of artificial intuition to offer value. My known exceptions for AI businesses permit exactly that separation when conversion, retention, and cash economics support the base business. But Native’s $2M ARR is company-reported, and the dossier supplies no gross margin, acquisition cost, churn, burn, or runway. The Briggs on Homes engagement comparison—5.5% versus 2.9%—does not establish incremental sales, and Native’s research acknowledges selection and survivorship biases. I cannot turn that into either predictive validation or a profitable route through the next three years.

I pass on the investment as documented. Native has stronger founder evidence than financing evidence, and enough commercial activity to deserve serious underwriting. The unresolved question is whether the team owns a compounding customer asset or sells a useful service whose surplus goes to model providers, publishing infrastructure, and customer acquisition. The verifiable flip condition is a financeable offer below $10M post-money whose costed proceeds fund a dated, controlled test showing that Norn’s feedback advantage produces incremental retained gross profit sufficient to reach break-even. That would connect the price, the venture moment, and a business that can survive without the next round.
