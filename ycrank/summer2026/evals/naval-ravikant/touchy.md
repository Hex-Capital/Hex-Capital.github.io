# Touchy -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The strongest evidence is the team’s ability to build. Rohit researches agent alignment, Andrew built educational software used by roughly 500,000 students, and Kevin contributed to Daft and SkyPilot before shipping a repository with about 2,500 GitHub stars. Touchy itself is live in the App Store with voice, camera, memory, and more than 1,000 tool integrations. This is action, not credential theater. But it is only partial evidence of specific knowledge in consumer assistants: the founders’ corroborated work is concentrated in research, language tools, and infrastructure, while the live product has too few ratings for Apple to summarize adoption.

The fatal question is what remains when Touchy’s code is copied. Today, the answer is unclear. Memories and preferences might create switching costs, but the dossier shows no retained cohorts, exclusive data, proprietary model, or user-generated network. Apple controls the operating system and announced Siri AI with personal context, camera understanding, and system-level cross-app actions in June 2026. A native iPhone interface is distribution rented from the competitor most capable of absorbing the product.

The market is large, but the argument is top-down. An $18.60 billion global AI-assistant category growing at 18.8% does not establish Touchy’s room to maneuver. The current wedge names an iPhone user who wants hands-free cross-app execution; it does not identify an adjacent buyer, transaction, or shared capability that Touchy can own after Apple and well-funded assistants such as Poke, Rabbit, Pally, and TwinMind converge on the same behavior. The 1,000 integrations widen utility, but integrations available to everyone are compatibility, not leverage.

The product is also solitary. One user’s conversations, preferences, and camera input do not improve another user’s assistant. There is no contribution, referral, liquidity, or matching evidence. Touchy Plus at $14.99 at least places a price against the utility, but the billing interval is undisclosed and there is no evidence of a paying customer, recurring revenue, or retention. Eleven thousand launch-post views and 63 likes prove attention, not value capture.

The bull case is that this is exactly when public evidence understates a technically exceptional team. The founders have already crossed from infrastructure research into a working consumer product, and declining inference costs could make persistent assistance practical before incumbents execute well. A recent pivot from Tokenless may represent rapid learning rather than weak conviction. The known exception for an unusually capable team with a demonstrated prototype therefore deserves examination—but Touchy has not yet shown the capital efficiency, obsessive usage, or distinctive product language needed to invoke it. A prototype can excuse missing revenue; it cannot excuse a missing substrate.

I see no evidence of dishonesty, artificial financing pressure, reassurance-seeking, or mistreatment of principals, so the pivot is not itself a founder red flag. The contradiction is structural: my recent pattern favors infrastructure, proprietary models, hardware, or permissionless networks, while Touchy is an app-layer assistant dependent on third-party models and Apple’s platform. My base case is that within 36 months the consumer app is discontinued or the team pivots back toward agent infrastructure. I would reopen on one verifiable condition: a published cohort showing at least 30% twelve-month paid retention alongside a proprietary interaction dataset that measurably improves task completion beyond commodity models.
