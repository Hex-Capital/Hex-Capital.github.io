# Shire Intelligence -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Shire’s strongest path is to sell camera-based floor management to full-service restaurant groups, then use POS resellers to reach more operators. Conditional on strong product-market fit, tracking table readiness and server workload is a recurring job that plausibly travels across markets. Shire’s use of existing CCTV gives it a reusable starting point. The work needed to accommodate different POS systems, camera layouts, and video governance requirements remains uncertain. Shire publishes a demo flow, quote-based plans, and a reseller inquiry path. These point to a sales-led motion, with reseller-led acquisition and repeatable setup still unproven. (The Idea; Key Risks)

Shire reported 13 live restaurants at its October launch and a 12% average reduction in table-turn time. The memo gives no comparison baseline or measurement window for the improvement. Payment status and commercial growth are unknown, so the deployment count establishes reported adoption and the traction score uses the commercial-evidence default. Using Shire every shift could support retention. Groups could add locations, and POS customers could generate more billable transactions if their payment volume grows. Both are inferred expansion paths. Billing cadence, renewals, and realized expansion remain unknown. (Market & Traction; Key Risks; The Idea)

Incremental contribution would come from fees attributable to an added site and any net payment-processing revenue after subtracting acquisition, camera and POS setup, inference, and support costs. Existing cameras could reduce hardware needs. The memo gives too little detail on site revenue or deployment effort to estimate contribution. (The Idea; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Camera blind spots and inconsistent classifications in the prototype raise the possibility that each location needs hands-on calibration. If setup stays labor-intensive, a reseller could bring in leads while Shire still carries the rollout burden. Toast’s bundled table status also gives operators a way to cover part of the workflow through their existing POS. The structural gate stays open because the memo leaves room for a standard deployment across locations. (The Idea; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a POS reseller bring successive cohorts of restaurant groups in two markets to paid use with the same setup and limited Shire staff time? Growing paid cohorts, existing groups paying to add locations, and positive contribution after deployment and support would support moving to Reach out if the results lift the score to at least 65. Evidence that each site requires bespoke work that makes faster growth economically infeasible would move the action to Pass.
