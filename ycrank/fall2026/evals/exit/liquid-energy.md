# Liquid Energy -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic sale to a managed inference provider. Liquid Energy owns and deploys the NanoDC hardware and sells inference capacity. Its self-contained solar and battery design could give an acquirer capacity it can deploy where grid access would delay service. That buyer benefit is an inference. The memo records a November 5 launch and contains no confirmed go-live. The company reports a four-week deployment timeline, 20 units on hand, manufacturing capacity of 2 MW per month, and agreements with unnamed inference platforms. Those reports support a path to paid field trials. The funding needed to build and operate a larger fleet remains unquantified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Using the rubric benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3%, with no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity. Both sale scenarios below assume an all-cash sale within eight years, $10 million in net debt and deal deductions, and an eligible holder cashing out the full stake pro rata with no preference reducing the payout.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Holder cash |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $10M in deductions leaves $40M in equity | $1.20M (2.4×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in deductions leaves $90M in equity | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no share sale or distribution | $0 (0×) |

The sale prices are scenario assumptions. At an illustrative 2× annual revenue and the company’s modeled $1,500 in annual compute revenue per NanoDC, the strong case would require roughly 33,000 revenue-generating units. The memo records no realized revenue or published pricing. Even at the strong-case price, dilution to 2% would cut holder cash to $1.8 million. (Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could partner for modular capacity instead of acquiring Liquid Energy. Greenference already markets solar-and-battery inference containers, and Armada manufactures deployable GPU modules with Johnson Controls. Liquid Energy’s integrated array could justify ownership if it delivered a measurable advantage, but the memo contains no field data on battery autonomy or sustained token throughput and identifies Starlink interruptions as a delivery risk. The company’s own revenue model also makes fleet economics consequential. The evidence leaves both buyer demand and a hurdle-clearing price unresolved, without establishing a structural block or materially inadequate proceeds across the strongest route. The Pass gate is therefore untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid multi-site deployment show enough economic benefit for an inference platform to justify an acquisition that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Customer-verified deployment lead times, delivered token throughput through day and night, and fully loaded fleet costs would answer it. Move to Reach out if those results support a financeable fleet and a strategic price that clears the hurdle after deductions. Keep Watch if the deployment proves uptime while buyer economics remain unresolved.
