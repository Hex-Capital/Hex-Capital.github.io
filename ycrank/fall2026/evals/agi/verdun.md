# Verdun -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is Verdun’s strongest route. Verdun uses AI to triage alarms from existing cameras and routes threats and uncertain events to operators who can intervene. Verdun reports that AI closed about 85% of alerts in a 14-day sample. That supports the feasibility of automated triage. The proposed new transaction is staffed monitoring for commercial sites that previously found it too expensive. That remains a scenario. The memo leaves operator minutes saved, threat recall, and response times versus conventional monitoring unmeasured. Verdun’s claim that incumbent queues can exceed 20 minutes during spikes makes peak performance especially important. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

With equally capable models available to competitors, a buyer could still value Verdun’s site-specific response protocols, incident records, and staffed intervention. The switching cost is inferred. The memo leaves open whether buyers would give up anything material by moving to another staffed provider. An anonymized screenshot shows activity presented as coming from customer sites. The free parallel trial and switching discount establish the offer. Paid conversion, repeat demand, and uptake among previously unserved buyers remain unknown. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, The Idea)

The San Francisco–based Fall 2026 launch is a plausible seed-stage fit. Tai previously built and sold physical-security computer vision at Actuate, and Hoang built its first gun-detection model. Their prior work together supports execution credit, while Verdun’s ability to maintain threat recall under real operating loads still needs testing. The expansion unit is the next site’s installed cameras. **Scenario assumption:** At a price that brings a previously unserved site into the market, recurring per-camera revenue would need to cover inference, exception review, staffed peak coverage, and the per-site cost of setup and support. Existing cameras could limit installation work. Pricing, staffing requirements, and site-level contribution remain undisclosed. (Key Facts, Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Verdun could be a faster monitoring vendor serving the same buyers at similar prices. Its switching offer targets properties that already buy monitoring, and Deep Sentinel and Blue Eye already combine AI detection with live intervention. Even a high automated closure rate could leave savings too small to open a market if difficult incidents consume most operator time, peak staffing stays high, or missed threats erode service quality. With abundant capital, competitors could add operators and integrations. The memo identifies no scarce capacity Verdun controls that would support a separate Path A case. Both Path B conditions remain unproven, which keeps outreach gated. The competitor evidence establishes credible substitutes, while buyer behavior under comparable service remains unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will commercial sites that previously declined staffed monitoring on price choose and renew Verdun at a price that covers delivery when a comparably capable AI-plus-staffed alternative is available? A paid side-by-side trial with independent buyers should measure operator time including exceptions, peak staffing, threat recall, response times, and site-level contribution. Reach out if buyers renew at viable pricing, the labor savings hold at comparable quality, and buyers show a preference for Verdun’s site-specific operations under parity, with the updated score and confidence clearing the outreach threshold. Pass if the trial shows that equivalent-quality delivery cannot serve those buyers economically or that buyers readily switch under parity.
