# River -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is River’s strongest path. River’s workspace combines document, spreadsheet, and slide editors with an agent that reads related files and makes reviewable edits. **Scenario assumption:** Independent consultants could profitably offer recurring reports to smaller clients if River cuts the time required to assemble source files, reconcile updates, and review the finished report. The memo establishes a workflow that could save labor. It leaves open whether those savings survive retries, exceptions, and review at equivalent quality, and whether they make a previously uneconomic engagement viable. (The Idea, Key Risks)

**Company claims:** River reports more than 100,000 creators. Its desktop launch reported more than 50,000 beta users and 200,000 files created. That suggests early use, with paid conversion, repeat use, and demand from newly served buyers still unknown. River’s public launch and Supple’s enterprise AI engineering experience support seed-stage execution. The memo leaves current team capacity unclear. A San Francisco–based Fall 2026 YC company is a plausible seed mandate fit. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

**Scale inference:** Self-serve distribution makes the next paying seat a plausible unit of expansion. River’s $14–$100 monthly plans and billed AI overages give it a way to recover heavy usage costs. Contribution per seat still depends on realized revenue after model calls, tool use, retries, and support. The memo gives no measured inputs for that calculation or count of reachable buyers for the proposed reporting use. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

River may make existing report work faster while leaving customers free to get the same outcome elsewhere. Genspark already combines office editors, agents, and a browser. Microsoft can edit in place using customers’ existing tenant context and controls. Those are concrete substitutes for parts of River’s workflow. River’s shared files and workspace memory could make switching costly, but the memo provides no buyer preference or retention evidence under functional parity. It also identifies no scarce input River controls for Path A. The competing products leave the Path B outreach gate unmet. A representative paid trial can still test both conditions, supporting Watch. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can independent consultants repeatedly deliver and sell a recurring report they previously declined as uneconomic, then choose to keep paying for River when a comparably capable alternative produces the same quality? A paid head-to-head trial should compare both tools with the prior workflow, count all human time through delivery, and measure repeat paid use at a price that covers River’s incremental costs. Success would support Reach out if the evidence lifts the score to at least 65 with medium or high confidence. A representative trial showing that the reports remain uneconomic or that buyers readily switch at parity would move the action to Pass.
