# OpEra -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

A 38% reduction in delivery costs would matter enormously if it survived deployment across real fleets. But OpEra’s result comes from a historical backtest against single-order dispatch from the nearest store. That establishes an opportunity to improve a weak operating policy. It does not establish a breakthrough against competent optimization. My money would be buying the possibility that scarce operations-research expertise becomes radically cheaper. The dossier has not yet measured that possibility.

The technical evidence deserves some credit. The founders built tariff-aware routing with a heuristic assignment layer and mathematical optimization using JuMP/Gurobi; this is more substance than a language interface demonstration. But the binding constraint may be constructing and maintaining each customer’s operational model. OpEra’s natural-language interface must preserve equipment, staffing, capacity, and business rules when conditions change. There is no disclosed accuracy benchmark or measured reduction in modeling effort against Nextmv or another named alternative. A better solver output and a cheaper way to formulate the problem are different achievements. Only the first has preliminary evidence here ([implementation methodology](https://github.com/bchalita/retail-last-mile-optimizer)).

The strongest argument against passing is that Rossignol and Chalita have already encountered these problems directly. Their MIT operations-research collaboration, McKinsey ML engineering, and BMW and Sanofi experience give them relevant tools. The additional company-reported results—10.4% higher sugar-sales premium and 54% fewer unfulfilled marketplace orders—suggest their ability extends beyond one routing exercise. My unstated preference for turning scarce expert workflows into repeatable platforms is relevant. If these projects share a reusable modeling engine, bespoke deployments could be the learning mechanism that builds the platform. But the dossier does not establish that reuse, a proprietary learning loop, or an observed instance of belief revision or difficult complementary recruiting. Credentials establish preparation; they do not establish those behaviors.

The consequence and economics remain unquantified. The adjacent $11.03 billion software category is not a measurement of industrial resources OpEra could release. There is no disclosed baseline for planner labor, implementation expense, or population of economically reachable sites. Nor is there pricing or a fully loaded deployment-cost model. Software controlling physical operations need not manufacture anything to encounter economic gravity: expensive integration and continuing expert maintenance can consume the customer’s savings. Those are unresolved questions, not evidence that OpEra has bad economics.

I also cannot credit a risk-removal plan that has not been disclosed. The retailer backtest is useful work, but it does not specify a decisive test of reliable constraint translation or repeatable deployment economics, with a budget, threshold, and stop condition. The red flag to investigate is whether implementation labor remains the differentiation. The dossier does not prove that it does. I checked the known-exception logic: a giant existing value pool paired with an exceptional instigator can justify buying an option before conventional milestones. Here, relevant founders and useful operating results make the bull case credible, but neither a quantified extraordinary consequence nor comparable evidence of an overriding founder commitment is documented.

I pass with my own money today. OpEra could become a valuable optimization business; the current evidence supports that possibility more strongly than an orders-of-magnitude collapse in the cost of operational expertise. One verifiable result would reopen my decision: an independently audited live deployment showing a 10x reduction in fully loaded cost per valid replanning decision versus a competent Nextmv-based implementation, including integration, human review, and maintenance, while preserving operational feasibility. That would test the mechanism worth financing.
