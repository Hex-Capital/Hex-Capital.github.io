# OpenHack -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A, product-led adoption.** The initial buyer is an engineering or application-security team that ships frequently and needs to identify which findings warrant a fix. The memo records a self-serve tier and 444 GitHub stars for the open-source scanner. OpenHack named six users at launch and reported that Papermark fixed a cloud-storage exposure it flagged. That remediation is an early signal of buyer value. The memo leaves open how Papermark activated, whether it paid, and whether it used OpenHack again. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Growth scenario.** If developers can move from the CLI or self-serve signup to a useful finding without founder help, the same workflow could bring in teams in other markets. Recurring reviews as code ships could support paid expansion to more repositories. The first cross-market test is whether teams can approve private-repository access through the standard setup. OpenHack lists read-only access, post-scan deletion, and an on-premises Enterprise option. If buyers need on-premises deployments, setup and support could rise with each account. Scanning and model compute could also rise with each repository or review. The memo provides no measured contribution, paid expansion, or cross-market activation. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The free scanner could attract developers while paid adoption depends on founder-led enterprise sales and deployment. Managed scans require temporary access to private repositories, GitHub offers overlapping remediation inside its existing workflow, and OpenHack reports finding 277 of 761 planted vulnerabilities in its own white-box benchmark. Buyers could therefore find individual fixes useful while seeing too little incremental value to approve a recurring paid rollout. The memo establishes neither a geographic ceiling nor proportional delivery costs, so the structural Pass gate remains unmet. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of independent engineering teams outside the U.S. go from self-serve signup to a useful finding, convert to paid use, and keep scanning over subsequent release cycles? Cohort records showing activation without founder-led setup, repeat paid usage, and scan and support costs covered by paid usage would move the action to Reach out. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)
