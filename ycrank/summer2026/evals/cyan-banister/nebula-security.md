# Nebula Security -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Eten Zou earned a cybersecurity PhD, researched security at Microsoft, and helped turn kernel exploitation into $400,000-plus of Google bounty rewards. Nebula Security is therefore a founder-origin company trapped inside a product category: highest on lived experience, lowest on physical-world verifiability. Nebula Security’s founders are unmistakably *of* security, not visiting it, but VEGA remains an abstraction that ordinary users cannot touch, photograph, or feel.

Anduril is the high rung because Palmer Luckey had already shipped frontier technology before translating that lived technical obsession into defense. Nebula Security shares the load-bearing mechanism: Eten Zou, Yuan Tan, Frank Wu, and Xiaochuan Yu accumulated 1,162 validated findings, eight Chrome zero-days, 98 public CVEs, DEF CON finals, and Black Hat appearances before packaging VEGA. Nebula Security diverges because those achievements prove extraordinary research capability, not that Eten has previously converted a comparable product into a scaled organization. The Tiz is blindingly obvious; the Biz and Rizz remain less observable.

DeepMind exposed the low-rung problem on physical verifiability: abstract intelligence cannot be ridden like an Uber or spotted like a Flock camera, so the check required a polymath exception. Nebula Security has inspectable artifacts—a 1,186-star exploit repository, public CVEs, and human-written patches—but Nebula Security cannot be evaluated by a normal human within 48 hours. A security researcher can touch VEGA’s work; an enterprise buyer must largely trust the founders’ testimony until VEGA survives a real repository, a real release cycle, and a real incident.

XBOW’s $120 million Series C, Semgrep’s $204 million raised, and OpenAI’s Codex Security launch make “AI security” consensus rather than magically weird. Nebula Security can escape that gravity over the next 18 months only if researcher validation compounds into a proprietary exploit-and-remediation corpus that competitors cannot reproduce. Nebula Security currently limits report volume because every finding and patch receives human review, so the same researchers creating trust may become the throughput ceiling. I am never playing the game I seem to be playing—but here too many sophisticated people are visibly playing the same game.

Eten Zou is the named CEO, yet Nebula Security has four founders connected through UC Riverside research and r3kapig competition without disclosed voting control, equity allocation, or conflict protocol. Nebula Security would flip this governance score upward if Eten held explicit operating tiebreaker authority with founder vesting and a written deadlock mechanism. Without that evidence, “CEO” is a title rather than proof that four elite researchers have decided who wins the argument when product velocity conflicts with publication-grade validation.

Vigil Labs is the useful single-anchor parallel: unsexy security and regulated financial infrastructure both reward trust, domain fluency, and technical evidence before conventional growth polish. Nebula Security has the ugly, adversarial work—kernel bugs, exploit demonstrations, human validation—but not Vigil’s regulatory moat or a physical infrastructure burden. Nebula Security is software-only in a market where Gecko, ZeroPath, Semgrep, XBOW, and OpenAI can approach the same buyer, so difficulty compounds mainly through researcher judgment rather than regulation or capital intensity.

The $400,000-plus in bounty rewards predicts that Nebula Security will still possess valuable technical capability in 36 months, while the absence of disclosed revenue predicts that capability will not automatically become an independent platform. My falsifiable forecast is that Nebula Security reaches $3 million to $7 million in ARR by July 2029, remains materially human-assisted, and is acquired by Semgrep for its exploit corpus and research talent. Nebula Security’s high founder authenticity creates the asset; Nebula Security’s low tactile distribution and crowded channel make absorption more likely than category ownership.

OpenAI’s Codex Security overlap supplies the bear case, but the stronger objection to my modest forecast is bullish: Nebula Security may be the DeepMind exception—a cluster of polymaths whose 1,162 validated findings and eight Chrome zero-days are early product proof hiding inside research metrics. That case stings because $400,000 in bounties is earned evidence, not pitch-deck cosplay. Nebula Security still has no disclosed paying-customer count, its human-review process constrains throughput, and customer-derived data has not yet demonstrated compounding advantage; technical applause does not prove repeat enterprise use. Ten paying enterprises completing repeat scans by July 2027, with most validated findings processed without founder review, would flip my verdict to Invest.
