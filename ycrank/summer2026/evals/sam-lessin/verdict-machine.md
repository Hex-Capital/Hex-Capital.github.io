# Verdict Machine -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I want are missing: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. YC admission is not evidence that capital is scarce; it can create the opposite dynamic. With no round terms or competitive context, I cannot tell whether Verdict Machine is a cheap non-consensus bet or an already-auctioned security story. That gate stays at its low prior.

The product does contain a plausible venture moment: can an institution-specific model turn fragmented inventory, screening, and policy into reliable pre-execution control? Success would matter because these transactions are irreversible. But the dossier never connects round proceeds to a dated test, a production threshold, or even one controllable metric. “Run POCs and hire” is not a card flip. I need something like one bank processing a defined transaction volume under enforcement with a measured false-positive rate by a fixed date.

The founders are the real asset. Dan Danay and Isaac Grossman built keyTango together; Danay and Liran Kogan then led blockchain-security work at Check Point, including an AI-assisted blockchain firewall. This is unusually direct shipping evidence on both sides of the bridge: blockchain infrastructure and institutional security. The POCs with asset managers and infrastructure providers moving billions weekly, plus evaluations inside large-bank digital-asset teams, suggest that their prior operating relationships can reopen difficult enterprise doors.

But access is not yet a dark pool. No institution has disclosed a purchase, production deployment, proprietary dataset, or retained usage. The 125 LinkedIn reactions are noise. Meanwhile, Blockaid processes more than 180 million transactions monthly, and Hexagate combines pre-signing prevention with Chainalysis data across more than 75 chains. Verdict Machine proposes a broader institution-specific model, but today the competitors own the observed data while Verdict Machine owns a product description and promising meetings.

The strongest bull case is that this resembles the early enterprise-AI pattern where domain credibility creates unusually fast POCs and the workflow data compounds after deployment. Ando is the relevant analogy because AI sat atop a consequential enterprise decision loop; the material difference is that Ando disclosed greater than 90% accuracy, complete pilot-to-paid conversion, and complete retention. Verdict Machine discloses none of those. Here, model error can block legitimate transfers or permit irreversible ones, so “AI enforcement” is not optionality until the base control system is proven.

There is also no revenue, pricing, gross margin, burn, runway, or seed-funded path to break-even. My 36-month base case is therefore a product acquisition by Check Point before Verdict Machine reaches $3 million ARR, rather than an independent category leader. The team is credible enough to build something valuable, but the current evidence does not show that company equity captures a durable standalone asset. I am passing with my own money. One verifiable event flips me: a named global bank converts its evaluation into a paid production deployment and reports 90 days of pre-execution enforcement with fewer than 0.1% false positives.
