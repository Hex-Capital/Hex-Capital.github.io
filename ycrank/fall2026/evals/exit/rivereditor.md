# River -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is River’s strongest path. A productivity software vendor could buy River’s native editors and reviewable cross-file agent to add workflows across documents, spreadsheets, slides, and connected apps. Owning the product could also bring paying teams if River demonstrates durable adoption. The memo documents a desktop launch and paid plans. River claims more than 100,000 creators, but the memo gives no paid conversion or retention figures, measured buyer benefit, or acquisition price evidence. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

A proposed seed-stage milestone is a paid team cohort still using River for recurring cross-suite work after 90 days, with measured time savings against incumbent tools. The launched product and paid plans make that test plausible. The reported $500,000 YC convertible note gives a financing signal, while burn, runway, and additional capital needs are unknown. A buyer would also need to verify rights to the core software and continuity of model and app integrations under new ownership. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

**Illustrative proceeds.** With actual holder terms unavailable, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The eight-year target is $2.5 million in gross cash. That requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. Assuming $5 million of net debt for the sale scenarios, the 3% enterprise-value hurdle is about $88.3 million. These figures use simple pro rata allocation before investor taxes. Actual debt, fees, and preference terms are unknown. (*Market & Traction*)

| Eight-year scenario | Gross cash to the holder at 3% |
|---|---:|
| Modest sale at $25 million enterprise value | $600,000 (1.2×) |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | $2.85 million (5.7×) |
| Growth plateaus, buyer interest fails to materialize, public scale stays out of reach, and River reinvests all available cash | $0, with shares retained |

The sale prices are scenario inputs. The memo provides no basis for estimating wind-down recovery after obligations and preferences. It also supplies no evidence of cash generation for a sponsor buyout, demand for an eligible secondary, public-market scale and resale readiness, or excess cash for dividends or buybacks. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Microsoft already performs cross-file work inside Microsoft 365 under existing tenant controls. Genspark already combines native office apps, agents, and a browser. Anthropic offers overlapping workflows and supplies models to River. Those shipping products give potential buyers credible build or integration alternatives, while River’s reported creator count leaves the value of its customer base unresolved. The evidence weakens the case for paying for ownership. A structural closing block or materially inadequate proceeds under supported sale economics remains unestablished, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Could owning River’s cross-suite workflow business justify an acquisition price above the modeled $88.3 million enterprise-value hurdle? A retained paid-team cohort with benchmarked time savings, paired with buyer-specific economics that support a price above the hurdle and explain why acquiring River beats building or licensing, would move the action to **Reach out**.
