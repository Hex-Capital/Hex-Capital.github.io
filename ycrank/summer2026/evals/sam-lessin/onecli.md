# OneCLI -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three cells are blank: proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. That is not evidence of bad terms, but it earns exactly the low prior my price gate requires. More importantly, the dossier never says what this round will prove. “Give every employee a secured, sandboxed pro assistant agent” is a product ambition; it is not a finite experiment with a metric, deadline, and truth-revealing result. I cannot tell whether my money buys one decisive test or merely more integrations, distribution, and runway.

The founders are the strongest part of the case. Jonathan Fishner built zero-trust network access at Axis Security before its HPE acquisition; Guy Ben-Aharon was Argon Security’s first engineer before its Aqua acquisition. Together they shipped ChartDB to roughly 22.5K GitHub stars and more than $10K MRR. This is the outsider-insider translation layer I care about: security operators who have also demonstrated open-source product distribution, now bridging human identity systems and autonomous-agent execution. They have shipped on both sides. The unresolved co-founder question is who owns the final product, hiring, and financing decision when the two disagree.

The product mechanism is real. OneCLI keeps credentials outside the agent environment, injects them at the network layer, attaches calls to employee identities, and can pause designated actions for approval. NanoClaw making OneCLI its sole credential path is stronger than a logo or download: another product has embedded the gateway into an operating workflow. But it does not yet show a purchase, retained paid usage, or control of a proprietary dark pool. Apache-2.0 licensing, Aembit’s credential broker, Pomerium’s per-request gateway, and Cisco’s acquisition of Astrix all say the architecture can become a feature before OneCLI owns the identity and policy history around it.

The traction presentation leans into precisely the metric confusion I distrust. The 350K downloads combine the old credential vault with the current harness; the 3.3K stars belong to a shared pre-v2/v2 repository; Product Hunt produced 143 points and a #7 daily rank. The “Trusted by” list includes Docker, Zoho, Kakao Entertainment, and others, but the dossier does not say whether any paid, deployed broadly, or returned. Those numbers prove attention and some distribution skill. They do not establish that the current product causes an economic action.

The bull case stings because the founders have more operating receipts than most pre-seed teams, NanoClaw supplies independent technical adoption, and the security flaw is concrete enough to create a valuable control point. A known-exception override would be plausible if current-product usage converted into unmistakable enterprise demand. It does not yet: the exception standard requires hard evidence such as paid enterprise adoption or similarly costly external validation, not blended downloads and customer logos. OneCLI also records a credential-injection host-enforcement bypass, while proxy-unaware tools can evade controls unless egress lockdown—off by default—is enabled. For a security gateway, enforcement is the product, not a cleanup item.

The $149 and $499 monthly plans show a monetization shape, not economics. There is no reported revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or seed-funded route to break-even. Delete the Series A and I cannot model the business. My 36-month base case is that OneCLI retreats from the broad per-employee assistant harness toward an agent-credential gateway and remains below $3M ARR as larger security platforms bundle overlapping controls. I pass today. I would flip if at least five of the named organizations were verified to be paying $499 per month or more and retained for six consecutive months; that would show the embedded workflow causes purchases rather than merely GitHub attention.
