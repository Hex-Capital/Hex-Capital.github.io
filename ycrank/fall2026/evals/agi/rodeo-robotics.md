# Rodeo Robotics -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** Rodeo’s strongest case is that cheaper intelligence could expand usable service capacity at repair shops constrained by technician time. Rodeo says shops turn away work and reports 96% autonomous execution time on its initial tasks. That metric gives the mechanism some seed-stage support. Its denominator is execution time on selected tasks, leaving successful jobs per bay-hour and total technician minutes per completed job unknown. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

Under equal access to capable models, shops would still need hardware and workflows that can safely perform physical service across their vehicle mix. Rodeo’s reported custom hardware and integration of inspection, diagnosis, and service provide a plausible basis for retaining value. The memo documents no demonstrated switching cost or improvement across deployments. Path B remains unproven because the memo identifies no newly viable buyer segment, use, price point, or service frequency. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*)

The San Francisco team is a plausible seed fit. Both founders have relevant vehicle autonomy deployment experience, with shop deployment still ahead. The waitlist and January 2027 rollout target describe Rodeo’s recruiting and plans. Buyer sign-ups, committed shop time, and payment go unreported. (*Founders & Team*, *The Idea*, *Key Facts*)

**Scenario assumption.** If technician time limits a shop’s output and the shop can fill freed capacity, the value per bay comes from the contribution on additional completed jobs and any realizable labor savings, less robot ownership or lease costs, integration, maintenance, and downtime. Supervision, interventions, and rework belong in the labor calculation. The memo provides no site-level inputs to establish whether that bridge works. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Vehicle variation and safety exceptions could keep technicians tied to the bay and erase the throughput gain. With abundant capital and comparable models, competitors could supply much of the same capacity through specialized systems. PitPro already has a tire-changing deployment, Automated Tire offers overlapping tasks, and Rodeo uses off-the-shelf hardware where available. The memo leaves open whether those substitutes can match Rodeo’s broader workflow. These risks leave both bottleneck relief and durable control unproven. The Path A override remains untriggered, and a representative shop trial gives Rodeo a credible path to resolving the gap. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent repair shop complete more same-quality jobs per bay-hour at viable economics with Rodeo on its normal vehicle mix? A paid pilot against the shop’s existing workflow should log technician minutes, exceptions, rework, downtime, and all-in cost per successful job. Paid continuation because Rodeo’s integrated workflow outperforms available substitutes would support Reach out if the updated score clears 65 with at least medium confidence.
