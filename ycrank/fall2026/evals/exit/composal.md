# Composal -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition of Verify by a coding-agent platform. Verify documents reusable browser test scenarios and recorded failure evidence. A platform such as Cognition could use those capabilities to add customer-journey checks to its coding workflow. That buyer rationale is an inference. Composal reports finding more than 1,500 bugs in customer apps, but the memo gives no measure of engineering time saved, paid retention, or buyer willingness to pay. Sparks is at the beta waitlist stage and adds little support for an exit today. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A production pilot that repeatedly runs authenticated customer journeys and measures engineering time saved is a plausible seed-stage milestone. The early-access Recorder rollout and documented support for dedicated test accounts and private networks give Composal a way to run it. The memo gives no pilot budget, runway, or evidence that those access measures have resolved the setup friction. It also gives no evidence on IP transfer, required consents, or holder eligibility, and identifies no independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-ons. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring about $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3%. Both sale cases below assume the holder is eligible, sells the entire stake, and receives pro rata cash at closing within eight years, with no preference claims ahead of the holder. Prices and deductions are illustrative. The memo supplies no acquisition pricing evidence.

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Cash to holder by year eight |
|----------|-------------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Modest sale | $30M less $2M in assumed debt and deal adjustments = $28M | $840K (1.68×) |
| Strong sale | $90M less $5M in assumed debt and deal adjustments = $85M | $2.55M (5.1×). If only 90% is paid within eight years, cash falls to $2.295M (4.59×). |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or funded payout | $0 realized |

### Countercase

A platform could build its own browser verification or integrate an existing tool. Composal says nine teams it interviewed had already built internal QA agents and that five of ten setup calls stalled on authentication or network controls. That weakens the case that Verify is worth buying and that an acquirer could roll it out broadly. The memo leaves a specific gap in buyer economics. It establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported sale-price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle, so the Pass gate does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a coding-agent platform’s production pilot show enough incremental value from owning Verify to support a deal that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? Buyer-validated savings from repeated authenticated runs, measured against the cost of building or integrating a substitute, would move the action to Reach out if those economics support a price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity under the benchmark terms.
