# Hemlock -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B, direct sales with account expansion.** The initial buyer is a robotics training team working on force-sensitive manipulation. Hemlock offers samples by email and has shown synchronized video with contact, hand-pose, and depth overlays. Hemlock reports that adding force data raised egg-placement success from 10% to 100% and lightbulb-screwing success from 30% to 80% in its tests. Those results give a lab a concrete reason to test the data. Buyer validation, paid activation, and repeat orders remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Scenario assumption.** A lab pays for data for one task, sees a training benefit, and orders data for more tasks in the same format. If labs abroad can use the same library and ingest the labels through the same workflow, each additional license could reuse the original collection work. If a buyer needs local workers, tools, or task demonstrations, the next order could add wearable deployment, worker time, calibration, and label quality checks. Watch hinges on whether a second paid workload can reuse enough of the first rollout to improve contribution. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Hemlock could become a custom data collection service. The reported gains cover two tasks, and published wrist-EMG work cited in the memo uses per-worker calibration. If Hemlock needs similar calibration and fresh collection for each task, expansion could add delivery labor roughly in step with revenue. Labs can also choose direct tactile capture or build on published methods. The memo provides no measured Hemlock rollout costs or observed structural limit on demand, so the Pass gate is unmet. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Will an independent robotics lab pay for a second production-relevant task after validating the training benefit on the first?
