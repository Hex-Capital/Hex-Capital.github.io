# Capveon -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Capveon could make it economical for infrastructure equipment sellers to pursue smaller public projects they currently skip because finding them early and checking equipment eligibility takes too much staff time. Capveon says it connects planning documents to project records, checks specifications and addenda against a seller’s capabilities, and cites the relevant pages. The October launch establishes that the company brought the offering to market. Earlier company copy claims paying customers for a workflow-handoff product. Paid repeat use of the current bid workflow and review time at equivalent accuracy remain unverified. The San Francisco location and Fall 2026 YC launch make seed entry plausible. Lancaster’s search engineering and Moorhead’s B2B sales experience support early execution. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team)

**Scenario assumption.** A seller starts pursuing smaller bids if expected gross profit from a pursuit exceeds its all-in pursuit cost after automation. The comparison needs to include time spent verifying citations, checking addenda, correcting errors, and drafting bid documents at the same eligibility accuracy as manual review. For Capveon, the next customer’s subscription must cover incremental feed maintenance, inference, onboarding, and support. Quote-based plans and reported tender coverage leave that contribution unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Cheaper intelligence may make existing bid teams faster without changing which projects they can afford to pursue. Missed addenda or incorrect eligibility calls could also consume the time saved. Under model parity, GovSpend already covers meeting signals and GovSignals covers cited bid review and drafting. Capveon’s reported agency coverage shows breadth, while the proposed switching costs from customer bid history remain an inference. The memo identifies no structural barrier or separate Path A case based on control of a scarce input. Both Path B conditions remain unproven. The overlap is material, but full substitution is untested, so the evidence supports Watch. (Defensibility, Key Risks, The Idea)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent equipment sellers repeatedly pay Capveon to pursue smaller bids they previously skipped when they can also use a comparably capable model on the same agency documents? A paid comparative pilot should record which bids sellers actually pursue, all-in staff time at matched eligibility accuracy, and Capveon’s incremental delivery cost. Repeat paid preference at a price that covers delivery, together with measured labor savings that open those pursuits, would support Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 with at least medium confidence. If the savings fail to open new pursuits or sellers switch readily at parity, move to Pass.
