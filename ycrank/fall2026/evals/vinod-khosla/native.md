# Native -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Native reports $2M ARR in six months. That establishes something valuable: small businesses will pay to delegate marketing. It does not establish the consequence I need to buy this option. If Norn works, how much human expertise becomes accessible, and at what reduction in cost? The dossier supplies a $36.4B social-media-management market estimate, but no baseline connecting Native’s mechanism to an order-of-magnitude change in that spending. I see demonstrated demand and an unquantified consequence. I would pass.

The technical question sits between prediction and generation. Native uses frontier models to generate assets; its potential invention is selecting what deserves to be generated. Approval, rejection, and subsequent performance could make that selection progressively better. That deserves more attention than another content interface. But collecting feedback is a mechanism for learning, not evidence of predictive superiority. Briggs on Homes’ 5.5% engagement versus 2.9% for its own posts is encouraging customer evidence, not a controlled benchmark against Blaze or Jasper. Native’s research acknowledges selection and survivorship biases. There is no demonstrated 5–10x technical discontinuity here ([research](https://native.no/research/the-taste-layer)).

The strongest argument against my pass is that the founders have already converted relevant experience into unusually substantial pre-seed demand: 2,200+ paying customers across 20+ countries, according to Native. Bekken brings content production, Gråsberg brings AI/ML, and Hauga brings agency operations. They also abandoned Wepost after it reached reported NOK80,000 MRR and started Native in January 2026. Walking away from a functioning product is an observed change of route. I give that modest credit for adaptability, without inventing the evidence that caused the decision. Their complementary skills help; the dossier does not yet show difficult recruits won to resolve specific weaknesses ([founder biography](https://native.no/about-us/benjamin-bekken)).

An execution-led business can qualify without a scientific breakthrough. But the exception requires a consequential value pool and unusually persuasive evidence that this team can reconstruct it. Native’s current commercial success does not establish that larger case. Its feedback loop also differs from the scientific learning systems I favor: customer approval and engagement can improve marketing without producing scarce, defensible technical knowledge. Marketing optimization sits close to my advertising-tool exclusion, although this dossier describes organic publishing rather than establishing a paid-advertising business. I would not turn that proximity into an unsupported categorical veto.

The next dollar should test whether Norn’s selection actually creates incremental customer value. The dossier contains no costed experiment, numerical stop condition, or ranked plan for resolving that uncertainty, so milestone discipline receives the absent-evidence score. Likewise, $79/month pricing establishes an offer, not unsubsidized economics: inference, publishing, support costs, and displaced human work remain unquantified. My single flip condition is an independently auditable, randomized customer trial showing Norn delivers at least 5x more attributable gross profit per fully loaded marketing dollar than the existing workflow, sustained for six months. That would connect prediction, economic leverage, and consequence. Until then, I am passing on an unproven investment mechanism despite meaningful commercial traction.
