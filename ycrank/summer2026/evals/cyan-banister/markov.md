# Markov -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. Software agents operate CAD tools, browsers, spreadsheets, and development environments, but they still need a reliable way to practice tasks and prove that the resulting files are correct. Markov has found a leak from that future: 48,478 recordings covering roughly 12,300 hours, distributed into a market already pulling the dataset 17,814 times per month. What is missing is the causal map between this artifact and a durable company. The dossier dates the arrival of public computer-use products, but it does not explain which remaining technical transitions Markov uniquely unlocks.

The AutoCAD environment is the most important evidence here. Retaining files, screenshots, model turns, and input events moves Markov below the visible agent interface and toward training and evaluation infrastructure. That mechanism can extend across CAD, coding, design, browsers, and spreadsheets. Yet the current evaluator can pass geometry that looks right while being semantically wrong, and the flagship internet-sourced corpus has unresolved chain-of-title questions. This is a real enabling layer, but Markov does not yet control the scarce part of it.

The founders have paid some technical tuition. Dev Mandal worked at Sarvam AI, DevRev, and Telekinesis and shipped doubt.ai; Harish Ashok previously built a voice-operated robotics-development assistant. Together, two very young founders assembled hundreds of gigabytes of usable material and an auditable application environment. That is first-believer behavior, not a recent category label pasted onto a deck. I still cannot see the full Rizz–Tizz–Biz pattern: there is no documented co-founder history, recruiting proof, buyer relationship, or evidence that either founder has learned the commercial machinery of contracted data work.

The strongest bull case is hiding inside the free distribution. More than 150,000 cumulative Hugging Face downloads could seed Markov’s formats, environments, and evaluators across research teams before established vendors notice that computer use requires more than generic annotation. If those teams return with proprietary workflows, Markov could migrate from downloadable recordings into paid evaluation environments. My 36-month expectation is precisely that pivot, reaching roughly $1 million to $3 million in ARR by August 2029.

But the bear case stings. Scale AI, Mercor, Turing, and Invisible already possess customer relationships and expert networks; researchers have also reported useful results from only 312 human trajectories augmented with synthetic actions. Markov’s largest corpus is public, permissively licensed, and apparently assembled from internet recordings whose underlying rights remain unclear. The company may be manufacturing abundant raw material while the valuable bottleneck migrates to task design, semantic verification, and buyer-owned proprietary workflows.

There is also no broken institutional rule yet. Markov identifies an engineering bottleneck, but not who captures an artificial rent, what quantified delay customers endure, or whose economic agency is restored. The 150,000 downloads demonstrate curiosity and repeated use, not costly behavior: no named customer, contract, renewal, or sensitive workflow has crossed the system. I would not write the check today. The verifiable flip condition is one named AI lab renewing a paid contract for an application environment whose semantic evaluator measurably outperforms screenshot-based grading; that would show Markov controls the difficult layer rather than merely publishing useful data.
