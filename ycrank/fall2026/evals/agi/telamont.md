# Telamont -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**New Economies** is the strongest path. Telamont checks new drawings, specifications, and contracts against the project record as revisions arrive, then assigns source-linked findings for resolution. A plausible new use is continuous review on smaller projects where contractors could previously justify only selective manual checks. The relevant comparison is the cost per correctly resolved conflict, including staff time spent finding and cross-checking revisions, validating findings, and approving notices, at comparable detection quality. Telamont reports $1.1M in avoided foundation rework, which illustrates the potential value of catching a conflict. The memo leaves baseline review costs, detection rates, and savings per resolved conflict unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The Autodesk Forma marketplace listing documents a shipped integration, and the founders bring construction, software, and industrial AI experience. Telamont reports monitoring more than $2B in construction project value, an early usage signal. The memo documents paid offerings but leaves actual payments, repeat orders, and adoption by previously underserved buyers unverified. In a per-project rollout scenario, fees would need to cover document processing, connector upkeep, and support for exceptions. Contractor time spent verifying findings and approving notices would still count toward the buyer’s cost per resolved conflict. The memo provides no project-level pricing or cost data to test either side of that calculation. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Telamont may make an existing review workflow more efficient while competing for the same buyers and budgets. Trimble already offers overlapping drawing, specification, and contract review, and Buildcheck offers review agents tailored to contractors’ own playbooks. Telamont’s source-linked decision history could give teams a reason to renew, but preference under functional parity and switching costs across projects remain untested. The overlap makes substitution credible without establishing parity or observed switching. Both New Economies conditions remain open, and the memo supports no independent scarce-complement path. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent contractors who previously skipped continuous review pay a price that covers delivery costs and choose Telamont again when a functionally comparable alternative is available? A paid cohort that tracks contractor time, review costs, and correctly resolved conflicts against the prior workflow would answer this if buyers also make a paid choice on their next project after trying the alternative. Move to Reach out if the cost reduction makes continuous review viable for those buyers and they choose Telamont again at a sustainable price. Move to Pass if the trial shows the use remains uneconomic or buyers readily switch at functional parity.
