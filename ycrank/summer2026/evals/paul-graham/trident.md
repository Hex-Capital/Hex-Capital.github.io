# Trident -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what happened when Trident failed. The dossier says the product reproduces vulnerabilities, traces them through cloud identities, opens patch pull requests, and reruns exploits. But it never names a blocker the founders encountered, the workaround they built, or how quickly that changed a customer outcome. Junaid Mahmood may be resourceful, but writing game exploits at 15 and working at Tesla are biographical evidence, not the running-back test. On my controlling criterion, the evidence is simply absent.

There is nevertheless a real artifact here. Trident has apparently tested customer systems, reports finding critical vulnerabilities in 97% of companies it worked with, and does not accept model output until an exploit is reproduced. Connecting web and API findings to AWS, Azure, and GCP identities, then generating and verifying patches, is considerably more than an AI security presentation. But the measurements are company-reported, with no denominator, benchmark, named customer, or independently inspected result. This looks like a fierce technical team that has built something; it does not yet show how much better that thing is than existing tools.

Who wants the crude version now? “Companies” and “customers” are not a niche. There is no public revenue, repeated-use figure, retention result, or named security team willing to describe what Trident found. Finding at least one vulnerability in every black-box customer proves that the software ran, but not that customers were surprised, paid, returned, or changed their workflow. The 347 LinkedIn followers and an event with Cursor and Fondo are attention, not pull.

The most worrying fact is the July 2026 movement from AI-agent monitoring and red-teaming toward web/API pentesting and cloud attack paths. Changing routes can be excellent, but only when the observation causing the change is visible. Here the stable goal, rejected hypothesis, and customer evidence behind the revision are missing. In a market where Horizon3.ai has run more than 150,000 pentests for over 3,000 organizations and Pentera reports more than $100 million ARR, an unexplained repositioning could be learning—or merely searching for an unoccupied sentence on a website.

The bull case is sharp. These founders built together before Trident, Mahmood has made exploits since adolescence, and the team has already assembled an unusually broad loop from exploitation to cloud reachability to remediation. Enterprise trust work, multi-cloud integrations, false-positive control, and SOC 2 are precisely the sort of unpleasant work that can hide a good company. Funded competitors also prove that buyers spend real money here. If Trident’s private customer evidence is substantially stronger than its public record, the dossier understates the company.

But I invest in demonstrated motion under resistance, not plausible hidden evidence. Trident fails my operational gate because the founder-workaround score is below 18/31 and the demand evidence is below 11/18. I would pass today. One verifiable condition would flip me: a dated case study showing that a named customer’s missed or false finding forced the founders to ship a workaround within two weeks, after which that customer paid for repeated monthly tests.
