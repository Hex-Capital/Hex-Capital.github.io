# Pomerado -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Pomerado sits between growing demand for agents and websites that still lack APIs. The buyer is an agent developer blocked from completing a customer workflow. The controlling variable is the fully loaded cost per successful production operation, including authentication failures, website changes, and repair. Compiling browser exploration into reusable code could improve that cost substantially. Whether Pomerado can retain the economic advantage after maintenance determines whether this becomes a company worth owning.

The architecture supplies a credible mechanism. A model explores the website, generates and tests operations, and passes them through a separate review model; subsequent execution requires no further model requests. That removes repeated inference from the execution path. MCP’s November 2024 introduction also supplies a common interface through which those integrations can reach agent developers. But zero runtime model calls is only one component of customer economics. The dossier does not establish the execution volume needed to amortize generation and repair, or comparative costs once websites change. I can identify the governing variable without claiming Pomerado has validated it.

The strongest bull case is that reliable execution becomes more valuable as agents improve. Better models could generate more integrations while Pomerado retains authentication, saved operations, and responsibility for execution. That is materially deeper than formatting model output. A developer adopting one integration could subsequently buy many through the same channel. The $50 monthly Premium plan makes initial adoption inexpensive, although its allowance of 1,000 calls per day tells me nothing about realized usage or gross margins. The opportunity is to become the maintained execution layer across otherwise inaccessible systems.

Competition makes that outcome harder. Browserbase already offers generated scripts, Browser Use offers replayable scripts, and Skyvern describes compilation and repair after website changes. Pomerado’s MIT-licensed implementation makes the core mechanism reproducible. Open source can help distribution, but it does not establish that customers will pay this team to operate the software. This category is crystallizing around several overlapping suppliers. My preference for infrastructure across an AI transition supports looking closely; my concern about reproducible technical assets prevents treating participation in the transition as a winner case. The maintenance commitment also lacks a universal restoration deadline, leaving the economically decisive service burden unresolved.

The launch evidence establishes attention. On October 27, the repository showed 1.1k stars and 100 forks; Product Hunt ranked the product fifth on October 16. Those are useful acquisition signals, but they do not establish repeated production use or willingness to pay. Manglik’s enterprise-agent work at Scale AI, including Mayo Clinic deployments, gives the problem selection credibility. Ma brings enterprise data and AI experience, and their earlier SafeSip collaboration establishes that they have built together. None of that documents costly founder commitment or lessons from operating a prior startup, so I retain the founder default rather than infer motivation from employers.

I would pass today. The research-stage exception does not apply: this is an execution-infrastructure business competing with working alternatives, without evidence of scarce frontier research that substitutes for customer pull. The bull case remains credible because the product already separates expensive discovery from reusable execution. My single flip condition is a six-month paid production cohort demonstrating lower fully loaded cost per successful operation than the customers’ previous browser-agent approach, including repair labor. That would connect the technical architecture to the market variable that matters.
