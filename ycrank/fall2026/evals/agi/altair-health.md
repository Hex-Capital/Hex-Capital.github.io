# Altair Health -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Altair could make managed billing affordable for small independent practices that currently do the work themselves because outsourcing is too expensive. Altair says it checks proposed claim actions against formalized payer rules on the practice’s existing systems and routes exceptions to in-house billers. The market-opening scenario assumes those checks reduce total biller time per successfully resolved claim at comparable accuracy and reimbursement speed, after counting review, retries, compliance, and follow-up. The memo documents the workflow and leaves the labor savings and affordability threshold unmeasured. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Altair reports reusing payer-specific resolutions on later claims. If that history transfers across practices and improves exception resolution, buyers could have a reason to pay Altair for handling claims even when they can access equivalent models. The memo provides no measured cross-practice improvement or buyer preference under parity. It also identifies no scarce billing input Altair controls that would support a separate Path A case. (Defensibility, The Idea)

The memo records the launch of an earlier co-pilot. The CEO reported use at an Arkansas clinic and attributed avoided receivables delays to claims prechecks. That offers limited demand evidence for the earlier product. Paid, repeat use of the current managed service by previously unserved practices remains unverified. The founders’ enterprise software sales and clinical EHR machine-learning experience support the ability to run a seed-stage trial. Delivery of the full billing workflow remains unproven. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

The scale test is whether fees from the next practice cover inference, policy upkeep, biller time, compliance, and onboarding at a price that practice will pay. The memo gives conflicting pricing listings and no usable unit economics, so scale economics receives the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Altair may be improving billing for practices already served by existing billers. Manual exceptions and changing payer rules could erase enough labor savings to keep outsourcing unaffordable for smaller practices. With abundant capital and comparable models, Tebra could offer similar checks within its existing practice platform. Candid Health’s configurable rules and agents are further evidence that competitors can build similar features, although its offering targets enterprise providers. The memo does not establish equivalent service outcomes or easy switching. Both Path B conditions remain unproven, which blocks Reach out and leaves a feasible test for Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent practices that previously handled billing in-house because outsourcing was too expensive renew Altair at a price that yields positive contribution when they have a credible alternative using comparably capable models? A paid comparative pilot should measure total human time per successfully resolved claim against the existing workflow, at comparable accuracy and reimbursement speed, and record renewal at the tested price. Repeat paid use, measured labor savings, and demonstrated preference under parity would support Reach out if the updated evaluation clears the score and confidence gates. A representative trial showing that either condition fails would move the action to Pass.
