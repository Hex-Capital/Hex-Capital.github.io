# ClaimKit -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit, ClaimKit’s most plausible growth path is to reuse evidence collection and project-to-cost matching across countries while adapting claim preparation to local rules. Finance and engineering teams face the same recurring documentation job, and ClaimKit describes an offering in the US, UK, and Australia. The work needed to add another filing regime remains unclear. Direct signup, a US credit calculator, and reusable integrations give buyers a way to start. Completing a claim still requires specialist review, and the Australian fees are individually quoted. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

ClaimKit reports 80+ clients across three countries seven months after product launch, with a listed team of four and specialist involvement. Edexia’s testimonial describes faster completion after switching providers mid-project. These are early uptake signals. The number of paying clients, realized fees, and commercial growth rate remain unknown. (ClaimKit, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

Continuous evidence collection gives customers a reason to return each tax year. If Australian customers file again under the same terms, the minimum tax-agent fee can support repeat revenue and a larger eligible benefit can raise the bill above that floor. Renewals and same-account expansion remain unverified. For the next Australian filing, contribution is the quoted software fee plus the filing fee, less attributable acquisition and onboarding, evidence processing, specialist review, and filing support. Automated evidence matching could reduce preparation time. The fee levels and specialist hours needed to assess that margin are unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

ClaimKit may scale as a tax practice whose throughput depends on specialists in each jurisdiction. Every claim requires review, and the three existing markets have distinct filing workflows. The structural gate stays open because the memo supports a reusable evidence workflow and leaves both the extent of market-specific rebuilding and the economics of specialist review unresolved. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What would ClaimKit have to rebuild to complete a paid filing in a fourth jurisdiction? A rollout showing that the existing evidence pipeline works with bounded changes to rules and forms would support continued Watch while paid growth and contribution are verified. Evidence that each new market requires bespoke rebuilding of the core workflow, with no credible near-term way to reuse it, would trigger Pass.
