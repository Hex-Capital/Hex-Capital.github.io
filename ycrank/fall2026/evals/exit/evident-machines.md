# Evident Machines -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a frontier AI lab. Owning a growing library of physical experiments, failure data, and expert corrections could give a lab a reason to buy if the data improves model performance and is costly to reproduce. Evident reports that models averaged a 29.3% pass rate across 10 physical tasks they passed at 100% in simulation. The standalone results page was inaccessible during memo research. The reported gap identifies a problem worth testing. Whether training on Evident’s data improves performance on unseen hardware remains open. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

The benchmark launch and contributor recruitment give Evident a starting point for a paid pilot. Manual wiring and probe placement leave delivery cost and throughput uncertain, and the memo records YC backing while leaving total funding and runway unconfirmed. A sale would also depend on whether the company can transfer rights to contributed tasks and experiment data. The memo leaves those rights and any independent liquidity route unverified. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. Illustrative sale cases assume the holder can sell the full stake for cash within that horizon and receives 3% pro rata after debt and deal adjustments, with no senior claims.

- **Modest sale.** At $40 million in enterprise value with zero net deductions, the holder receives $1.2 million, or 2.4×.
- **Strong sale.** At $100 million in enterprise value with zero net deductions, the holder receives $3 million, or 6×. Net deductions above roughly $16.7 million would bring the payout below target even before any senior claims.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested yields zero realized proceeds in the comparison case.

The sale values are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no company-specific pricing evidence to support a sale above the hurdle. (Market & Traction; Key Facts)

### Countercase

Anthropic’s collaboration with Diode shows that a frontier lab can commission overlapping electronics evaluations through a hardware partner. If that approach covers enough of the physical testing a lab needs, the lab can get the benefit without buying Evident. Evident’s manual bench work could also make it expensive to build a sufficiently broad library. The collaboration establishes a credible substitute for part of the workflow. Whether it can match Evident’s physical experiment coverage remains open, and the memo establishes neither a structural block nor a credible sale range that consistently falls short. The Pass gate is untriggered. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid frontier-lab pilot show repeatable performance gains on held-out physical experiments over the lab’s existing workflow at a measured delivery cost? Move to Reach out if the pilot demonstrates those gains and the lab identifies an economic reason to own the dataset and workflow. Keep Watch if the pilot shows benchmark demand while the value of ownership remains unresolved.
