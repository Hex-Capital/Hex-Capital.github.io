# Waddle Labs -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Yiding Song and Hanming Ye are Harvard roommates who met at MIT’s Research Science Institute, which makes Waddle Labs a strong founder pair pursuing a problem that is less clearly their own. Waddle Labs has the configuration I like most and the origin story I trust least: two young hackers with six months of robot traces, but no evidence that either first needed Waddle as a customer. This is a hacker-built research artifact still searching for an organic startup idea.

**Stripe** (2010) is the high rung for co-founder configuration: two brothers who had built together before and whose skills compounded under pressure. Waddle Labs resembles Stripe structurally. Song brings program induction and multimodal research; Ye brings robot-policy adaptation; they met at RSI and later became roommates. But Waddle Labs diverges where the Collisons were strongest: years of shared commerce hacking had already tested both the relationship and the problem. Waddle Labs has the pair, but not yet the scar tissue.

**Loopt** (2005) exposed the failure mechanism at the low end of organic ideas: impressive founders can outrun the lived need for a product, and only an exceptional animal like Sam Altman makes me suspend that objection. Waddle Labs sits above that rung because Ye developed Value-Guided Denoising for robot diffusion policies and Song researched in-context program induction. But research adjacency is not the same as Drew Houston repeatedly forgetting his USB stick. Waddle Labs’s dossier never says Song or Ye needed to reprogram robots for their own recurring work, so I cannot give them the organic-idea credit their technical fit initially tempts me to give.

Waddle Labs generated a working policy in roughly 20 minutes and ran about 1,000 randomized LEGO pick-and-place repetitions overnight. That is a real artifact, not a deck. Over the next 18 months, animal resourcefulness would compound if Song and Ye personally install Waddle Labs on awkward customer hardware, debug failures at 3 a.m., and turn every intervention into a reusable skill. But the dossier contains no cereal-box stunt, hand-built customer operation, revenue improvisation, or rejection-defying act. The founders have shipped; they have not yet left unmistakable animal tracks.

Waddle Labs has six months of robot data and intervention traces, but the schlep remains mostly inside its own laboratory. The score would move into **Coinbase** (2012) territory if Waddle Labs had to support five incompatible robot stacks, enter factories, satisfy safety reviews, and keep production lines running while customers blamed it for every mechanical failure. The score would fall sharply if the preview API remains a clean wrapper around CaP-X, ASPIRE, and frontier-model calls. Physical deployment could become the moat. Waddle Labs has not proved it is willing to swallow that schlep yet.

Waddle Labs’s hacker genealogy is closest to **Reddit** (2005): young technical founders with credible intellectual output before conventional business credentials. Song co-authored multimodal astrophysics research and maintains `dudocode`; Ye published topology work and a NeurIPS workshop method for parameter-free robot-policy adaptation. The GitHub footprints—12 stars and 8 stars—are small, but Waddle Labs has already converted research ability into a working physical system. Hacker taste is present. Hacker-community pull is not yet.

Physical Intelligence has raised $1.07 billion and Skild AI about $1.7 billion, while Waddle Labs is offering a preview API with no public revenue. My 36-month prediction is that by July 2029 Waddle Labs will have abandoned the promise of prompting arbitrary robots, narrowed into programming and recovery software for a specific class of manufacturing workcells, and reached $1 million to $3 million in ARR. Waddle Labs’s strong pair can iterate into that narrower product; the weak organic origin makes a narrowing pivot more likely than a straight line.

Waddle Labs depends on larger reasoning models, builds on published mechanisms, lacks standardized external benchmarks, and has founders constrained by international-student visa and 12-credit requirements. The bear case stings: Physical Intelligence or Skild AI could absorb code generation into their models before Waddle Labs ever escapes preview, leaving Waddle Labs as a clever paper implementation with no independent distribution. I still invest because the founders have complementary technical depth, a working robot loop, and six months of intervention data—more artifact than most pre-seed robotics pitches contain. If no external customer is running a Waddle Labs-generated policy weekly on production hardware by July 2027, I would flip the verdict to Pass.
