# Confetti -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Confetti’s possible secret is that beginners want completed physical inventions enough to pay for a system that absorbs the engineering between an idea and a working assembly. That proposition could be tested: do novices successfully build things and return to build again? The dossier establishes an advertised workflow, but neither that behavior nor a prevailing belief the founders have disproved. “Build hardware using plain-English prompts” describes an interface. The important truth would concern what that interface makes economically possible.

Who owns the resulting value? Confetti combines design, component selection, printed-part ordering, and community remixing. Its consumer focus differs from Adam’s engineering workflows and Flux’s electronics emphasis. Yet Zoo already offers conversational geometry inspection and debugging, while Flux includes sourcing and manufacturing outputs. These are substantive overlaps, although they do not establish that the complete products are interchangeable. Confetti has identified a customer type; it has not demonstrated a bounded market it can dominate. The proposed library of successful assemblies could become valuable proprietary infrastructure, but that library and its compounding advantage remain hypotheses.

The founding relationship is the strongest evidence here. Tianyi Evans Gu and Lucas Yuan both attended Penn and coauthored EngineLab, accepted at the 2026 ICML NExT-Game workshop. That establishes collaboration beyond simply meeting to apply to YC. It earns affirmative credit for alignment, without establishing full-time status, ownership, or control of essential engineering. None of those unknowns proves a founding defect. Likewise, the inferred transition from Asteria does not establish mercenary behavior or an inability to commit.

The strongest objection to passing is that Confetti may be organizing the wrong-looking pieces into the right business. Gu’s software research, Yuan’s research collaboration and Electric Racing exposure, and a product extending through physical delivery could let this team discover where novices actually fail. Successful projects could then become reusable, purchasable building blocks. My willingness to back consumer businesses and complete physical systems makes that possibility relevant. But the decisive economic link is still absent: transaction fees are inferred, pricing is unverified, and there is no documented contribution margin or repeat purchasing. Community remixing is an advertised feature, not yet an observed acquisition mechanism. The 58 LinkedIn followers establish a public presence and little else.

I would pass on Confetti today. The relevant red flags are feature convergence and the need to coordinate design correctness, compatibility, and fulfillment; the dossier does not establish outsourced engineering, misaligned ownership, or a false technical claim. My known exceptions depend on demonstrated technical breakthroughs, persistent transaction demand, or a concrete deployment path. None supplies an evidence-based override here. One verifiable result would change my decision to investigate: an independently checked cohort of 100 novice customers in which at least 80 receive functioning projects and at least 30 purchase a second project, with positive contribution margin after fabrication, shipping, and support. That would establish that Confetti is beginning to own a recurring transaction rather than merely simplifying a design step.
