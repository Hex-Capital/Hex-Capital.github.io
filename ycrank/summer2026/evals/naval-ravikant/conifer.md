# Conifer -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The strongest evidence is what Charles Muehlberger learned by doing. He researched edge-device multimodal inference, built edge-AI hardware for RF brain-injury modeling, and now ships a local inference engine. Michael Jeffords previously built an applied computer-vision pipeline used in screenings reaching more than 10,000 people. The claimed 60% decode-speed advantage over llama.cpp on Apple Silicon, a working installer, and 248 GitHub stars turn this from résumé proximity into an artifact. The founders have specific knowledge of edge inference, even if they have not yet proved equal knowledge of selling AI infrastructure.

The engine is real technical work, but performance is not yet a durable asset. Conifer distributes proprietary binaries and reports its own benchmark; the dossier provides neither independent reproduction nor evidence that the advantage compounds with deployments. If Ollama adds difficulty-based routing, or OpenRouter adds a local execution tier, Conifer cannot answer merely by writing more code. Its hypothetical routing dataset could become proprietary leverage, but no routing volume, accuracy history, or outcome dataset exists publicly. Today, competitors can copy the architecture even if they cannot immediately copy every optimization.

The wedge is broader than a single application. Coding agents and customer-support systems share the same need to allocate requests across local, efficient-cloud, and frontier models, while OpenRouter’s estimated $50 million annualized revenue independently proves spending on the routing layer. Yet Conifer has not shown which adjacent buyer follows the first one, how Windows and Linux fleets participate when full local execution centers on Apple Silicon, or whether an 80% token-cost reduction leaves enough gross profit after cloud usage. A large parent market is not the same as room to maneuver.

The company resembles a permissionless system because it recruits idle customer hardware, but the resemblance stops at ownership. One customer’s laptop does not make another customer’s routing better. There is no demonstrated contribution, referral, liquidity, or shared-learning loop. The current product captures value directly through consolidated pay-as-you-go cloud billing, but it discloses no payer, price, take rate, completed transaction, or recurring revenue. Conifer may reduce token spend while failing to capture much of the savings.

The bull case stings: three young founders have shipped a difficult engine, found more than 100 developers for the prior Sage launch, accumulated 248 stars, and entered precisely when local models are becoming useful on Apple Silicon. The relevant known exception permits investing before an observable user loop when a distinctive prototype and capital-efficient team already exist. Conifer has part of that exception. It does not yet have the part that matters most here: proof that the product becomes harder to reproduce as it is used. The repeated Sage–Juniper–Conifer renaming is not enough to infer founder instability, and the dossier shows no integrity, artificial-deadline, or reassurance-seeking red flag. But absence of founder red flags cannot manufacture product leverage.

I am neutral. This is credible edge-inference engineering inside a strategically exposed routing feature. The likely path is a pivot toward an enterprise edge-inference runtime or an acquisition driven by the team, unless usage creates a proprietary learning system before the surrounding platforms absorb the feature. I would flip to Invest when at least 10 paying teams collectively route one million monthly requests and Conifer shows that its proprietary outcome data improves task success per dollar by at least 20% over a frozen router using the same underlying models.
