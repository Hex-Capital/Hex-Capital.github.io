# Elite -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: real-time voice models now let healthcare practices automate front-office work, but Elite becomes a large company only if resolved-interaction pricing produces reliably lower costs and better access without creating clinical or EHR-action failures. That controlling variable is falsifiable, yet the dossier supplies neither a resolution-rate threshold nor customer-level cost, retention, or expansion data. A $13.7 billion category projected to reach $106.7 billion does not answer whether Elite captures it.

The product is more substantial than a voice wrapper. Protocols for 18 specialties, write-back across more than 19 EHRs, contextual escalation, audit logs, and responsibility for scheduling, referrals, refills, eligibility, intake, and billing define an owned operational workflow. Better models should improve task completion while Elite retains integrations, permissions, protocol state, and the completed action. This is the company’s strongest evidence.

The hard counterargument to passing is that a two-person team has assembled unusual implementation breadth, promises go-live in days, and prices per resolved interaction rather than per seat. Assort, Hyro, and Syllable have collectively attracted hundreds of millions of dollars, validating a real healthcare-access budget rather than an invented category. A fast-moving team could enter smaller practices beneath those health-system-focused competitors, use the first EHR workflow as distribution, and cross-sell adjacent administrative work.

But the same comparison shows a crystallizing market in which Elite has not identified its winner variable. Assort already claims 23-plus specialties and patient-journey memory; Hyro has health-system deployments and explainability controls; Syllable spans the same channels. Elite discloses no current customer, usage, revenue, proprietary dataset, or structural barrier. Outcome pricing could align incentives, but without resolution accuracy and gross-margin evidence it may simply transfer exception-handling and liability risk onto Elite.

The founders show credible execution potential, not yet healthcare-specific inevitability. Vihaan was founding engineer number three at Cadastral before its acquisition and generated more than 1,000 signups for a Perplexity campaign; Raj brings Deel GTM experience. However, neither has documented clinical-practice operating experience, and the move from a government application-review product launched July 29, 2026 to a healthcare HIPAA notice effective August 22 is evidence of speed but also of an unsettled problem commitment. None of the known-exception patterns applies: this is not a scarce frontier-model team, a newly proven scientific mechanism, or a physical-market reconstruction with demonstrated margin change.

My base case is that within 36 months Elite pivots from healthcare front-office automation into a less regulated administrative workflow rather than reaching $3 million ARR. I pass today. The single condition that flips the decision is a documented cohort of at least 20 practices retaining Elite for six months while autonomously resolving more than 70% of interactions at positive gross margin; that would validate the economic variable, product pull, and safety-adjusted workflow advantage simultaneously.
