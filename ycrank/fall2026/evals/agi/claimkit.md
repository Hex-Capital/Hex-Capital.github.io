# ClaimKit -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Finance and engineering teams reconstruct projects and payroll for R&D tax claims, either internally or with specialist advisers. ClaimKit says it collects evidence from connected tools, maps costs to R&D activities, and sends each claim to a tax specialist for review. Edexia reports lower cost and faster completion after switching from its accountants. Fluer reports that its team could hand over raw data and skip writing the claim report. These customer accounts show where labor can fall. The proposed new market is eligible companies that previously skipped filing because the credit was too small to justify staff time and adviser fees. The memo leaves that market opening unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The Australian terms document an upfront software fee and a tax-agent fee due at lodgement. The relevant comparison is full cost per successful claim, including customer time, specialist review, rework, compliance, and filing. The fee structure provides a starting point for testing contribution per claim. Realized fees, review hours, exception rates, and successful claim volumes remain unknown. Huang’s R&D tax experience, Mahon’s engineering automation experience, and the launched product with a named tax review lead support seed-stage execution. Edexia’s switch and ClaimKit’s reported 80+ clients show early adoption. Paid repeat demand among first-time filers remains unverified. (The Idea, Key Risks, Founders & Team, Market & Traction)

**Model-parity assumption.** If rivals have equivalent AI, buyers may still pay ClaimKit to turn incomplete engineering and payroll records into a specialist-reviewed filing. ClaimKit has organized that service, but its ability to deliver it more reliably or cheaply than an equivalent provider remains untested. Confidence is low because the customer accounts are qualitative and neither required condition has been tested with the proposed new buyers. (The Idea, Defensibility, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

ClaimKit may primarily win existing tax-credit work from accountants. Edexia was already pursuing a claim, specialist review still applies to every filing, and incomplete records can create rework. Neo.Tax already automates work from source records, RAD already collects evidence continuously, and ClaimKit lets customers export their evidence files. If those alternatives can match ClaimKit’s reviewed filing service, buyers may have little reason to stay under model parity. The memo leaves both gates open for testing and supplies no independent evidence of a scarce input ClaimKit controls. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can ClaimKit win a paid cohort of eligible companies in one jurisdiction that previously skipped filing on cost grounds, at fees that leave positive contribution after review and rework, when those buyers have a functionally equivalent AI-enabled option? Move to **Reach out** if the cohort shows lower full cost per successful filing than the prior route and buyers commit to the next filing cycle because they value ClaimKit’s specialist execution. Move to **Pass** if a representative comparison shows that full costs still price those buyers out or that they readily switch to an equivalent service.
