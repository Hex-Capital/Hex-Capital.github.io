# Particularity -- Paul Graham Evaluation

What happened when an investigator first tried to use Particularity? That is the missing story I care about. Kumaravel worked on consumer protection at the Senate Banking Committee. Bannon built security and compliance infrastructure at AWS. Those are useful backgrounds for this problem. But the dossier names no obstacle they encountered together, workaround they shipped, or time it took to change the situation. Resourcefulness gets its absent-evidence score. That is uncertainty about the founders, not evidence against them.

The product contains a sensible distinction: finding a suspicious checkout is easier than producing evidence an investigator can use. Particularity proposes navigating websites, testing cancellation flows, preserving timestamped and hashed records, and mapping findings to statutory elements. That last mile could contain the company. Hashing establishes that a record has not changed; it does not establish that the agent understood what happened. The launch describes an architecture, without a demonstrated investigation or measured reliability. Bannon’s public Sailor framework supplies some independent evidence that he builds software, so technical proof deserves limited credit above default. It does not validate this system.

The strongest case for investing is the combination of policy experience and engineering experience applied to work that is both tedious and consequential. Kumaravel has worked near the intended users’ problem, and Bannon has worked on compliance infrastructure. Their friendship offers more reassurance than two strangers assembling a team for an application, although shared execution remains undocumented. There is a plausible schlep here: turning changing websites into usable investigation records. I give the problem’s origin partial credit for relevant prior work, while withholding the stronger claim that a specific firsthand frustration produced Particularity. One investigator repeatedly requesting new investigations would make this proposition substantially more interesting.

But Particularity has not yet shown that investigator. Its October 15, 2026 emergence and subsequent launch coverage establish public activity, not user pull. Requests for introductions to state attorneys general and city consumer-affairs departments identify a sensible hunting ground; they do not establish repeated use. Pricing and revenue are undisclosed, so I cannot judge the subscription hypothesis. More immediately, UNCTAD documents regulators already using mystery-shopping and recording tools. Particularity must make an investigator sufficiently more effective to displace that workflow. The dossier shows neither that response nor an evidence-driven product revision, so demand and adaptability receive their defaults.

I would pass today. The team fits my pattern of technical founders approaching an administratively ugly problem, but Particularity lacks the concrete proof spike or demonstrated programmable substrate that strengthens that pattern. I see no affirmative evidence of partner dependence, premature delegation, or launch theater; having launched is not itself a red flag. I also checked the exceptions: there is no demonstrated prototype, unmistakable user pull, or independently validated result strong enough to override the failed resourcefulness gate. My flip condition is one documented agency deployment in which a named investigator’s blocker causes the founders to ship a workaround within two weeks, followed by that investigator repeatedly using the resulting evidence in actual investigations. That would answer the founder question and the user question with separate, observable facts.
