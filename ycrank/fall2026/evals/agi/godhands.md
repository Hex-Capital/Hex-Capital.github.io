# GodHands -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** GodHands could make exception-heavy workflows economical to automate for teams that currently rely on senior staff, shadowing, and manual playbooks. An unnamed fintech customer reported that a replacement controller closed the books on time in month two, compared with two quarters previously. That account suggests faster handover on an existing workflow. The market-opening scenario is that a team could afford to automate a recurring workflow it previously handled manually because documenting and maintaining its exceptions took too much expert time. The memo gives no before-and-after labor cost per successful outcome. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Under model parity, a buyer might keep paying GodHands to preserve its accumulated, source-linked record of exceptions after raw recordings expire. That switching cost is inferred. The memo reports no retention or migration comparison. An unnamed logistics customer reported documenting its escalation process within two weeks, an early usage signal with payment and repeat use unverified. (Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The founders’ prior systems work and October product launch support seed-stage execution credit. A plausible expansion unit is one additional workflow within a customer account. Capture without API integrations could lower setup work, while mandatory approvals, retries, privacy controls, and support could limit contribution per workflow. The memo documents subscription terms and leaves realized pricing and delivery costs unknown. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

GodHands may make existing handovers faster while leaving expert review expensive enough that the proposed new use remains uneconomic. Scribe and Skan AI already offer overlapping workflow capture and agent capabilities, giving buyers credible alternatives if the same models are widely available. The memo establishes neither the cost threshold for market opening nor buyer preference under parity. It also contains no representative cost comparison or observed switching that closes the path, so the action is Watch. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent support team renew at a price that covers delivery costs after using GodHands to automate a recurring escalation workflow it previously could not afford to automate? Compare the former manual workflow, GodHands, and a credible substitute using the same model and comparable access to workflow context. Count setup, review, retries, and human exceptions per correctly resolved escalation. Move to Reach out if the savings make the workflow economical and the buyer renews because GodHands handles accumulated exceptions better than the substitute. Pass if the comparison shows that the workflow remains uneconomic or that the substitute preserves the outcome with negligible switching effort.
