# Viraj Aero -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic sale to a turbine OEM or packager. Viraj reports a patent on its exhaust-water condenser and publishes GENA targets of 10 MW, 45% electrical efficiency, and 2 ppm NOx. A buyer could gain an advantage at sites that need both on-site power and cooling water if the combined system lowers installed and operating costs. That advantage is an inference from the design. Its value depends on measured water yield, efficiency, and emissions under continuous duty. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

A continuous-duty data center pilot is the next milestone. Viraj reports that the first unit is under construction and that letters of intent cover more than 1.3 GW. The memo also records an announced €4.5 million seed round and grant support for aviation. These are execution signals, while GENA’s pilot budget, funding, and schedule remain open. A sale would also depend on IP ownership, transfer rights, and holder participation. The Safran proof of concept shows industrial engagement in aviation, with certification still ahead and an independent holder payout path unsubstantiated. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale cases below assume the holder can participate, sells its entire stake for cash within eight years, and shares pro rata with no senior claims or deferred consideration. The prices and deductions are illustrative assumptions. Viraj’s reported paying engine manufacturer provides an early commercial signal, with contract scope and payment undisclosed. (Market & Traction)

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Cash to holder |
|----------|-----------------|----------------|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M in assumed net debt and deal costs leaves $45M in distributable equity | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $120M enterprise value less $10M in assumed net debt and deal costs leaves $110M in distributable equity | $3.30M (6.6×) |
| Prolonged private | Operating cash is reinvested, with no distribution or eligible share sale within eight years | $0 |

The strong case clears the objective at 3% ownership. At 2% ownership, the same sale pays $2.20 million, below the hurdle.

### Countercase

Packagers can already serve on-site power demand with turbine or reciprocating-engine offerings. If GENA’s water recovery saves too little after integration and operating costs, a buyer could meet customer demand with existing equipment and have little reason to pay for Viraj. The first unit is still reported to be under construction, leaving that economic comparison open. This supports Watch. The Pass gates require a supported structural block or evidence that even the strongest credible route pays materially below the objective. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price range could a turbine packager justify using measured results from a continuous-duty GENA pilot? A packager’s build-versus-buy analysis using metered power, usable water yield, emissions, and installed and operating costs would answer it. Move to Reach out if the supported range can deliver $2.5 million in cash to the benchmark holder within eight years after deal deductions.
