# Kairox Systems -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a defense integrator is the strongest path. Kairox says maritime teams use separate air and underwater fleets, with separate operators and logistics. It reports Marine Corps engagement and military end-user concept validation. An integrator could pay to own Tempest if field results show that combining the two jobs materially lowers operating costs and that ownership captures more of that value than a supply agreement. The memo records an adjacent civilian procurement award for uncrewed marine systems, which supports demand for related equipment. The ownership advantage and willingness to pay for Kairox remain inferences. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Kairox reports lab-stage validation and describes procurement as a future milestone. The memo records $500,000 in reported accelerator funding and relevant hardware and qualification experience on the founding team. Those signals support a plausible path to an integrated field test. The test budget, runway, IP ownership, and transfer rights remain unverified. The memo’s unquantified revenue signal supplies no basis for underwriting a buyout or funded shareholder payout, and there is no evidenced demand for this holder’s shares. (Key Risks, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts, Defensibility)

**Illustrative proceeds.** With no actual check terms supplied, assume a $500,000 seed check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the investor, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The equity hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

For both sale scenarios, assume zero net debt, transaction fees, and other price adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. Apply a simple pro rata waterfall before investor taxes, with no preference effects. All consideration is cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and separate compensation are each zero. The modeled prices are sensitivity points. The memo supplies no acquisition pricing or quantified buyer economics to support a range. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Existing fleets already cover both jobs. Even if Tempest qualifies, an integrator could obtain the operating benefit through a supply agreement, leaving little reason to pay an acquisition premium. Kairox has reported concept feedback and lab validation, while integrated field performance and procurement conversion remain unverified. The substitute is credible, but the memo does not establish that it caps the acquisition price below the hurdle. The $0 prolonged-private scenario is a downside case, with insufficient evidence to treat it as the probable outcome. The Pass override does not apply. (Defensibility, Key Risks, Market & Traction)
