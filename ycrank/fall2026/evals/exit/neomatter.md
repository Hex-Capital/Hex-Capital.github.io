# Neomatter -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest path. An established legal software platform could acquire Neomatter’s intake and drafting software to bring lawyer-approved, precedent-based workflows to its existing customers. Ownership could accelerate rollout and give the buyer control over integrations and product development if Neomatter can demonstrate a durable performance advantage. That advantage is an inference. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The memo documents an October launch and named-seat subscription terms. The CEO reports 73% weekly user growth from an undisclosed base and says one unnamed customer saved dozens of hours weekly and thousands of dollars in outside counsel spending. The launch gives Neomatter a starting point for testing repeatable savings across paid deployments. The funding and runway available for those deployments are unknown, as are the rights to transfer software and continue using customer playbooks after a sale. The memo gives no usable evidence of cash flow for a buyout or payout, demand for this holder’s shares, or a path to public-market scale. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Illustrative proceeds.** With no deal terms supplied, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× target requires $2.5 million in holder cash, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% ownership and $62.5 million at 4%.

| Scenario | Timing and enterprise-to-equity bridge | Holder cash and gross multiple |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | Year 5. $50M enterprise value becomes $50M distributable equity. | $1.5M, 3× |
| Strong sale | Year 7. $100M enterprise value becomes $100M distributable equity. | $3.0M, 6× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. Shares remain held. | $0, 0× |

Both sale scenarios assume zero net debt, no other price adjustments or preference effects, and pro rata cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each modeled at $0. The sale prices and dates are assumptions. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction evidence to anchor them. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Legora already routes Slack requests into configured workflows, and LegalOn offers intake and drafting agents. An incumbent could extend those capabilities or license an integration, especially if the customer-specific playbooks cannot transfer. The overlap makes an acquisition premium hard to justify today. The memo leaves open whether Neomatter’s automatic intake and precedent-based drafts outperform those substitutes in production, so the structural Pass gate remains untriggered. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a strategic buyer justify paying enough to clear the holder’s proceeds hurdle based on what ownership adds over building or licensing? A buyer-backed comparison using repeatable results from paid deployments, with a quantified ownership benefit that supports at least $83.3 million in distributable equity under the illustrative 3% case, would move the action to Reach out.
