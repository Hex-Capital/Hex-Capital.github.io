# Tire Swing -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with U.S. affordable housing operators, Tire Swing can grow by automating recurring eligibility and certification work. Document extraction and evidence review could transfer abroad, but the current product applies program-specific eligibility rules and produces certifications in operators’ existing forms. Even within the U.S., different HOTMA deadlines require separate rule configurations. A rollout into a different national housing system would likely require substantial work to implement and validate local rules and forms. That dependency triggers the structural downside gate. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Founder introductions, industry events, and demos using a prospect’s own file give Tire Swing a repeatable sales approach. The documented path still depends on founder involvement, and the memo gives no evidence that buyers can purchase and go live independently. Auto-renewing subscriptions fit recurring certification work, and accumulated review histories could support renewals. The terms do not establish whether adding properties or processing more files increases an existing customer’s bill, so expansion revenue remains uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The two founders reported dozens of live properties across eight states by November 2026, following the July launch of the housing product. That establishes a reported deployment footprint. Payment status, the date of first paid availability, and commercial growth are unknown, so initial traction uses the absent-evidence default. For incremental contribution, the relevant bridge is subscription revenue less attributable sales and setup time, third-party AI and OCR processing, and any rule maintenance required by the next account. Reusing existing checks for properties in a supported program could reduce setup and maintenance per property. The memo leaves the size and durability of that benefit uncertain. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Facts, The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The strongest case for a scalable path is that the same document and review workflow already spans several U.S. programs and states, according to the founders. If new rules and forms can be added through ordinary configuration, the core product could travel with less work than its current U.S. focus suggests. The reported domestic footprint supports reuse within the current market. The structural gate still applies because the memo establishes no credible near-term path to adapting the rules and forms for a different national system with bounded work. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an operator in a non-U.S. housing program pay for and complete a live certification cycle using the existing product with ordinary rule and form configuration? A paid deployment that records the configuration changes and vendor hours, and produces certifications accepted through the operator’s normal approval process, would support moving to Watch if the work proves bounded and reusable.
