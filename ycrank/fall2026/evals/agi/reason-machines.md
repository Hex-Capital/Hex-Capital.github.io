# Reason Machines -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest path. Reason ships a coding-agent harness that can take a triggered issue through to a pull request. The company reports 22% lower cost per solved task than Codex using the same model on fixed benchmark tasks. That result supports a narrower inference-efficiency claim. The seed-stage hypothesis is that teams could afford to handle recurring, low-priority repository work they currently leave undone. Testing that hypothesis requires measuring human review, retries, and rework per merged pull request at equivalent quality. *The Idea, Key Risks.*

The memo records 112 Chrome extension users, while Reason reports 20,000 users in its first four weeks. These are early adoption signals. Paid repeat use by teams taking on previously uneconomic work remains unknown. The founders have relevant developer-tooling and production ML experience and shipped successive Reason releases after Ara. The team is based in San Francisco and says it is raising a seed round, which fits the stated mandate. *Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts.*

The $50 and $200 monthly seat plans, credit allowances, and paid top-ups give Reason a way to charge as usage grows. Contribution from the next team depends on seat and top-up revenue after Reason’s inference, cloud, and support costs. Actual usage and contribution remain unknown. *The Idea, Key Facts.*

### Countercase

Reason may be making existing coding-agent work cheaper in a market where buyers can switch once alternatives match its workflow. Reason builds on the public Pi harness, Cursor already offers isolated cloud execution, and Devin has its own cost-optimization approach. Subscription providers also control the quotas that make rollover valuable. Reason’s persistent sessions and connected workflows could give teams a reason to stay, but the memo contains no evidence of buyer preference at functional parity. With abundant capital, competitors have credible routes to matching the harness, and the memo identifies no scarce capability Reason controls that would support Path A. *Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks.*

The competitive evidence leaves both Path B conditions open. The benchmark measures inference cost, and buyer behavior under parity is untested. That keeps the action at Watch with low confidence.

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent teams repeatedly pay Reason at a price that covers delivery costs to handle repository work they previously deferred, even when they can choose a functionally equivalent agent using the same model? A paid production trial should compare human time and total cost per merged pull request at equivalent quality, then give buyers a real choice between Reason and the alternative on comparable terms. Repeat purchases with measured labor savings would support Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 and confidence reaches medium. If the savings serve only existing work or buyers readily switch under parity, move to Pass.
