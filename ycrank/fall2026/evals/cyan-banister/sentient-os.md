# Sentient OS -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I want to close my laptop and wake up to finished work without having handed a stranger the contents of my life. Sentient OS has an interesting piece of that future: selected sources become editable Markdown knowledge on my own machine. Work backward and three things must happen: personal context becomes usable, delegation becomes dependable, and permissions remain understandable as the assistant gains agency. The dossier shows a concrete approach to the first transition. Drafting and Sidekick suggest the next step, but dependable completion remains unfinished. That is enough to make me curious about what is coming.

Jesai Tarun’s Writing Tools and Bliss AI projects, alongside his on-device inference work, matter more to me than the fundraising list. These are artifacts of someone repeatedly working around a related problem. WritingTools’ 2.5K GitHub stars provide outside evidence that one artifact reached people; its reported 30,000 users belong to that project, not Sentient OS. Aditya Vellanki brings complementary software experience and a Contexto project. I see a credible builder trajectory, although the dossier does not establish a dated, long-running obsession or demonstrate how the team turns enterprise interest into a business. Calling each other best friends establishes neither a governance problem nor a contractual tiebreaker.

Why must I reconstruct my life every time I ask software for help? Sentient OS identifies a real tax: context scattered across email, messages, notes, and files becomes work the user must perform before delegation can begin. Portable knowledge could return some control to that user. But the dossier neither measures this tax nor identifies an artificial restriction sustaining it. My attraction to businesses that restore individual agency applies here, with less force than it would to a demonstrated gatekeeper being removed. Free consumer access and initially free teams also leave the proposed enterprise licensing and support economics untested.

The strongest argument for writing a check is that people are already letting this thing into their computers. Sentient OS reports 10,000+ users, and a dated October 21 GitHub issue describes overnight processing repeatedly failing on long items. That complaint is more informative than a waitlist: someone attempted the actual workflow and cared enough to report where it broke. It still does not establish repeated successful delegation. My willingness to back a pre-revenue prototype applies; revenue is not the missing ingredient. The missing evidence is that the intended overnight ritual reliably gives users something they return for.

The product also fits my pattern of looking underneath the visible assistant, but only partway. Customized LiteRT-LM integration, Metal acceleration, speculative decoding, and engine reuse show engineering substance. They do not yet establish control of a scarce enabling constraint. Screenpipe already offers local, model-agnostic context, and portable Markdown deliberately makes the resulting knowledge easier to move. I like that choice for users; I cannot count it as exclusive company leverage. Nor does the dossier establish dependence on one closed model supplier. The sharper red-flag test is privacy: optional cloud features can transmit knowledge and screenshots, while local models and claimed Zero Data Retention provide limiting mechanisms. That warrants inspecting the actual permission boundary, rather than treating “on-device” as a complete answer.

I am **Neutral** on Sentient OS with my own money. The founders and the editable personal-memory artifact earn attention; the evidence does not yet show that this becomes an indispensable layer. My flip condition is one independently inspectable, 30-day team deployment demonstrating repeated successful overnight work with sensitive context confined to the users’ chosen boundary. That would connect the appealing future to a lived habit—and show whether enterprise support has something valuable to support.
