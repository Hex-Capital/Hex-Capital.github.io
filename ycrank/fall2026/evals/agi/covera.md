# Covera -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**New Economies** is Covera’s strongest path. It uses AI to assemble risk files and prefill carrier portals, with agency staff reviewing the output. Covera says manual intake and re-entry can take hours and reports three minutes from email to quote. That timing measures elapsed time. The stronger labor signal is Hello Risk’s testimonial, which reports 155 tasks over two weeks and estimates 10 hours saved per week. The memo leaves the change in labor cost per usable quote unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Scenario assumption.** Independent brokers may decline small commercial submissions when expected commissions cannot justify the staff time. Covera could make those submissions economical if it reduces fully loaded cost per usable, agency-approved quote enough to cover its fee. A matched comparison would divide total processing cost by usable quotes delivered, counting document follow-up, review, corrections, and failed attempts in both workflows. Whether brokers would take on those submissions and keep paying Covera with comparable alternatives available remains open. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The memo records 174 Chrome extension users, and Covera separately reports 90+ live brokers. Alongside Hello Risk’s repeated task use, these support early adoption. Paid conversion and repeat demand for previously uneconomic submissions remain unverified. The founders previously worked together in brokerage and built Simio through acquisition. The shipped extension and documented approval controls add seed-stage execution evidence. Each additional agency is a plausible expansion unit if portal mappings carry across customers. Per-agency contribution depends on paid pricing, inference, onboarding, support, and portal maintenance, which the memo leaves unquantified. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The strongest countercase is that Covera speeds up quotes brokers already write, while comparable models and established agency software narrow its advantage. Broker Buddha already combines intake with CoverForce rating. Covera’s reported 100+ portal connections and documented agency management integrations offer practical breadth, but the extension uses agencies’ existing carrier logins and depends on mappings that can break when portals change. The memo provides no evidence of exclusive carrier access, preference under functional parity, or a network effect. These gaps leave both New Economies gates unresolved. The competing workflow does not establish functional parity or easy switching, so Watch remains appropriate. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent brokers repeatedly pay Covera at a contribution-positive price to quote submissions they previously declined on labor cost, when a functionally comparable alternative is available?

A paid cohort with documented prior declines, matched costs per usable quote, and renewal after access to the alternative would answer it. Consistent evidence of newly viable submissions and repeat paid preference would move the action to Reach out. Direct evidence that the savings only apply to already viable work, or that buyers readily switch at parity, would move it to Pass.
