# Workers IO -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what Chaitanya Choudhary did when something refused to work. Workers IO has a technically interesting proposition: make production failures reproducible by running unmodified systems inside deterministic virtual machines. But the dossier contains no particular obstacle, founder-built workaround, and elapsed time. Choudhary reports selling The Timelinears after reaching 3 million monthly active viewers. That makes him worth meeting. It does not establish how he responds when a customer’s software breaks his simulator ([YC biography](https://www.ycombinator.com/founders/chaitanya-choudhary)). I would pass on the present evidence.

The strongest argument against passing is the implementation work. His August 2026 article describes seeded execution, intercepted I/O, and virtual clocks. Those are concrete mechanisms for tackling a difficult problem, and they earn more credit than a product description alone. But the published seeded-Bun implementation does not establish deterministic execution across arbitrary unmodified systems. There is no independently reproduced benchmark or customer failure replay connecting the narrower implementation to the broader promise ([technical article](https://workers.io/blog/deterministic-simulation-testing/)). Workers IO fits my recurring interest in programmable infrastructure. It has not yet supplied the numerical proof spike that makes an unfamiliar technical bet easier to underwrite. The strongest evidence concerns how the product might work; the weakest concerns what its founder does under resistance.

Who needs this badly enough to install it now? Payments and trading engineers have an intelligible problem: failures involving retries, timing, and partial outages. Yet the 208 stars and 32 forks belong to the open-source agent skills package. They do not establish repeated use of the simulation backend. The roughly 64,000 launch views establish attention even less directly. Professional pricing of $2,500 a month gives Workers IO a concrete offer to test, but it supplies no evidence that anyone accepted it ([GitHub](https://github.com/workersio/skills), [launch tracking](https://www.launchvideo.com/directory/workers-io), [pricing](https://workers.io/pricing/)). A single engineering team repeatedly bringing its hardest failures back would tell me much more.

Antithesis already offers overlapping deterministic testing. That does not disqualify Workers IO, but it makes the first customer’s reason for choosing it especially informative. Easier installation or better fault models could matter enormously; neither advantage is demonstrated here. Choudhary’s engineering roles establish relevant experience, but the dossier does not connect a particular incident in that work to this company’s origin. Nor does it document a rejected hypothesis and subsequent product change. Those are missing observations, not evidence of a manufactured idea or stubborn founder. Likewise, the systems-engineer vacancy does not prove he is waiting for a hire, and launch coverage does not prove he mistakes publicity for distribution.

I cannot justify an exception on the available record. The prototype, independently validated result, or unmistakable user pull that could override uncertainty about founder behavior remains unestablished. Being a solo founder is not itself a reason to pass. My one flip condition is a documented customer deployment in which Choudhary encounters a specific integration blocker, ships a workaround within two weeks, reproduces that customer’s real failure, and the customer continues paying and using Workers IO. That sequence would turn several attractive possibilities into evidence of the company I would actually be buying.
