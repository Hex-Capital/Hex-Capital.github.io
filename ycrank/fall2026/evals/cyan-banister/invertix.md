# Invertix -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years forward, I want a renewable-energy operator to add another hundred plants without adding another room of people chasing alarms and maintenance tickets. Invertix points toward that world. But someone must first connect equipment records, distinguish a real fault from bad telemetry, and turn a diagnosis into an authorized repair. The dossier describes pieces of that path; it does not establish the transitions between them. “Energy Superintelligence” is a destination sign. I need evidence that the road reaches it.

Why are engineers manually reconciling plant data? Because equipment, records, and responsibilities live in different systems. Invertix attacks that specific operating wound by embedding engineers and mapping the organization before deploying agents. That respects hidden complexity, which matters to me. The agency returned is an engineer’s ability to investigate and coordinate work without assembling the context by hand. But there is no quantified reconciliation cost, measured time returned, or artificial gatekeeper collecting a rent. This is a credible workflow repair; the dossier has not shown a deeper change in who controls the economics.

The strongest argument against passing is that customers apparently already care enough to pay and expose operational information. In May 2026, Vireo reported more than **1.8 GW** managed through Invertix’s AI workers; June coverage reported **“multiple six figures” in ARR**. Those are meaningful pre-seed signals, even with company-reported revenue and no disclosed currency. TotalEnergies independently confirms program participation, although that establishes access rather than a customer contract. I would not confuse customers’ **50 GW** of portfolios with Invertix deployments. What remains missing is the behavior inside those deployments: repeated investigations completed, maintenance actions accepted, or customers expanding after experiencing the product. The dated launch-heading ambiguity also keeps the ontology description provisional rather than independently verified technical proof.

Invertix does fit my pattern of finding concealed infrastructure beneath a fashionable wave: renewable operations could become a necessary enabling layer for much more electricity-intensive activity. Customer-specific ontologies offer a plausible foothold. Yet control over a scarce asset is unproven, and Delfos already combines operational data, domain rules, models, and workflow automation. Customer-directed data use limits the assumption that every installation automatically strengthens a shared intelligence. That is sensible custody, not a defect to engineer away. Likewise, customer-defined permissions and professional sign-off are constructive boundaries; the dossier establishes neither a severe privacy violation nor dependence on one closed model supplier. The commercial question is whether approximately **60 days from contract to deployment** purchases reusable infrastructure or another installation that needs substantial bespoke labor.

Perrotta’s Cubbit experience and Durmaz’s computer-vision and differential-privacy background provide useful complementary skills. Their meeting at Brightdale in early 2025 and subsequent life as roommates explain how the partnership formed. They do not establish a pre-company obsession with energy operations, a succession of independently built artifacts, or unusual recruitment power. I would not turn that silence into an accusation of opportunism. But my exceptions for pre-revenue technical ambition require stronger prototype and lifelong-founder evidence; my exceptions for extraordinary user behavior require the behavior itself. Neither overrides the gaps here. A named CEO provides accountability on paper; equal-control governance remains unknown, not a demonstrated red flag.

I pass with my own money today. Invertix may build a useful, paying business while remaining dependent on customer-by-customer implementation, and that distinction determines my conviction. My one verifiable flip condition is **an independently documented three-customer deployment cohort showing the same core system repeatedly completing authorized fault-to-maintenance workflows, with materially less engineering effort on successive installations**. That would connect the reported commercial interest to the enabling layer I actually want to own.
