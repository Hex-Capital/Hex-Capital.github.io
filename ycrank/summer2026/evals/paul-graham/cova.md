# Cova -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Cova is a schlep-rich company with an organic-idea gap: Cova has crossed into Michigan provider licensure, Medicaid enrollment, electronic-visit-verification compliance, and caregiver operations, but neither Jordan Ibe nor Ansh Tandon is documented as having personally needed home care. That pairing defines Cova. The founders found an ugly workflow by spending a year building AI for agencies and hospitals, then moved closer to the ugliness by becoming the agency. I like the direction of travel. I do not yet know whether Cova is something they had to build or merely something they discovered they could build.

**Coinbase** (2012) is the high rung for schlep blindness because Brian Armstrong treated compliance as the product while other hackers treated it as contamination. Cova shares that mechanism: the founders accepted Michigan licensure, benefits coordination, caregiver training, Medicaid billing, and EVV enforcement beginning April 1, 2026. Cova even faces a rule requiring at least 80% of certain Medicaid HCBS payments to reach direct-care workers. The divergence is evidence of endurance. Coinbase had Armstrong’s rejected application and hand-built wallet; Cova is licensed and live, but Cova has disclosed no case volume, reimbursement history, or compliance crisis the founders personally fought through.

**Loopt** (2005) exposed the low-rung organic-idea failure mechanism: force of personality could override a product whose enabling infrastructure was not yet usable, but the exception depended on Sam Altman being an unmistakable animal. Cova’s dossier never says Jordan Ibe cared for a relative, struggled to obtain Medicaid reimbursement, or built Cova for his own household. Ibe’s VA medical-center work and Cova’s prior year inside home-care workflows are better than market tourism, but Cova remains one step removed from the Dropbox USB-stick pattern. The founders encountered the problem professionally; the evidence does not show the problem chasing them home.

Jordan Ibe and Ansh Tandon spent a year building AI systems for home-care agencies and hospitals, then Cova abandoned the easier software-vendor position and became a licensed provider. That is the strongest animal track in the dossier. Over the next 18 months, this choice compounds because every denied claim, missed visit, caregiver departure, and confused family teaches Cova something a horizontal AI vendor cannot learn from an API. It also exposes whether the founders are animals: Cova has no revenue, named customer, or unscalable acquisition stunt yet, so licensure earns substantial credit without earning the Airbnb cereal-box score.

Ansh Tandon’s public GitHub trail tops out at a two-star ECG fatigue-monitoring repository, while Jordan Ibe has no public repository. Cova therefore has technical credentials and a year of shipped AI work, but not the hacker genealogy that made Stripe legible before Stripe was large. The score would flip upward if Cova published a non-trivial agent architecture, demonstrated unusually reliable Medicaid workflow automation, or showed software processing hundreds of real visits with fewer billing failures. Until then, Cova’s “AI-native” label is a claim supported by operating proximity, not by a visible technical artifact.

**Stripe** (2010) is the useful parallel for Cova’s founder configuration: two founders spent meaningful time working on the underlying problem before committing to the final form. Ibe and Tandon report a year together building healthcare AI, and Cova combines Ibe’s VA exposure with Tandon’s bioengineering and data-science background. Cova does not have the Collison brothers’ lifelong relationship or Auctomatic history, but Cova is a real pair rather than two strangers assembled around a pitch. One year is enough to reduce cofounder risk, not enough to erase it.

Cova will reach roughly $4 million to $8 million in annualized revenue by July 2029, primarily from Michigan care delivery, while remaining an independent provider rather than retreating to agency software. Cova’s mechanism is not an AI moat in the abstract. It is accumulated knowledge from intake, eligibility, EVV, billing, and caregiver operations that gradually makes each additional case cheaper to administer. The forecast follows the signature: Cova can become a meaningful regional agency because it embraces the schlep, even without proving that the idea began as personal pain.

Honor’s scale, Michigan’s direct-caregiver enrollment alternative, and the Medicaid 80% compensation rule form the strongest bear case against Cova. Cova could discover that AI removes clerical labor but cannot remove caregiver shortages, reimbursement delays, or thin regulated margins; the weak GitHub trail and absence of current-product revenue make “AI-native” vulnerable to becoming manager-mode decoration. What keeps me on the invest side is that Cova already chose the harder provider pivot and obtained licensure instead of promising to hire operators after funding. I would reverse the verdict if Cova cannot document at least 50 recurring paid care relationships with positive contribution margin by July 2027.
