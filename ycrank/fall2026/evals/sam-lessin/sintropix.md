# Sintropix -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Sintropix has ten active clients, but I cannot price the check. The expected $500,000 YC investment tells me neither the proposed angel post-money nor how many investors are competing. Nothing establishes an auction; nothing establishes scarce capital either. The second missing piece matters just as much: what specific uncertainty would my money resolve? The dossier documents a business being built, but no round-funded experiment with a deadline and a measurable result. Both gates receive my missing-evidence defaults.

The strongest reason to keep looking is how Sintropix acquired its accounting customers: referrals, followed by direct accountant outreach. Someone trusted the team enough to hand over a consequential workflow. That is substantially more useful than the claimed 100-plus integrations or 789 LinkedIn followers. Ten clients across Chile, Mexico, Brazil, and the United States establish actual customer activity, although the dossier does not establish how many pay, renew, or refer others. The potential proprietary asset is the customer relationship and accumulated accounting context. Integration count does not establish ownership of either.

There is also an operating bridge here. Alfonso and Borja moved from manual bookkeeping in April 2026 to a proprietary ERP described in September. Doing the work before automating it gives them direct exposure to accounting exceptions alongside experience building the software. That earns more credit than Alfonso’s Toku product title or Borja’s AI background alone. It fits my preference for teams connecting systems whose participants struggle to understand one another. It does not yet establish deep expertise across four accounting jurisdictions, particularly with Mexico services still described as beta.

The bull case is uncomfortable enough to take seriously: Sintropix may already have the trusted service relationship through which increasingly cheap software can improve delivery economics. Its reported reduction in closing time from day 20 to day 1, and onboarding from 30 days to 24 hours, could make that relationship much more profitable. But those are company-reported workflow outcomes, partly retrieved through search snippets. Services advertised from 20 UF per month tell me the asking price; they do not tell me collections, human review costs, gross margin, or burn. Delete the next financing from the spreadsheet and the dossier cannot tell me whether Sintropix survives.

The AI risk is specific. Automated journal posting creates liabilities when errors propagate, while Campfire and Rillet already offer overlapping accounting workflows. Sintropix could still win through trusted local delivery, but its economics must include exception handling rather than assume it disappears. Nor do I infer an equal-founder governance problem merely because the founders are brothers; ownership and decision rights are undisclosed. I checked the exception case to my financing gates: documented conversion, retention, cash economics, or unusually strong external validation could outweigh missing deal information. Ten active clients provide a foothold, but the dossier does not supply that override.

I pass on writing a personal check today. Sintropix has stronger evidence of customer access than of an investable financing proposition. My verifiable flip condition is a documented seed-to-break-even plan: a priced round funding a dated cash break-even milestone, with the calculation supported by actual customer collections and fully loaded servicing costs. That would connect the promising customer relationships to what my capital buys.
