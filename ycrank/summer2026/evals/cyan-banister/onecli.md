# OneCLI -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Jonathan Fishner’s security-architect career and OneCLI’s 320K claimed downloads make OneCLI a founder-authentic infrastructure bet whose strongest feature is lived problem ownership and whose weakest is physical-world observability. Fishner and Guy Ben-Aharon did not discover credential leakage in a TAM memo: they came through Axis Security, Argon Security, Aqua Security, Unit 8200, and jointly built ChartDB to 22.7K stars and more than $10K MRR. OneCLI passes my “of the place, not visiting it” test. OneCLI does not yet pass my hotel-Wi-Fi test; an HTTPS credential gateway is inspectable by an engineer, but a normal human cannot ride it, photograph it, or feel it working.

**Niantic** is the high rung on founder origin because John Hanke spent a decade living inside location infrastructure before Pokémon Go materialized. OneCLI shares that load-bearing mechanism: Fishner built Unit 8200’s first Big Data team and worked as a Principal Security Architect, while Ben-Aharon was Argon Security’s first engineer before its acquisition. OneCLI diverges from Niantic because the dossier gives me professional adjacency, not the specific autobiographical moment when Fishner or Ben-Aharon personally felt an agent expose a credential. The unstated pattern still lands strongly: OneCLI’s founders shipped ChartDB together before OneCLI, and prior-product persistence matters more to me than their institutional badges.

The consensus YC AI-agent darling fails my physical-world rung because the abstraction can only be validated through founder testimony. That failure mechanism appears partly in OneCLI: OneCLI is software plumbing, and its most visible artifact is policy enforcement inside an outbound proxy. Yet OneCLI is not vapor. A developer can install the Apache-2.0 repository, route an agent through it, inspect the blocked request, and observe that the decrypted credential never enters the agent process. The 2.9K GitHub stars, 170 forks, and 4,523 weekly SDK downloads lift OneCLI above the pure-deck low rung, but the **HQ Trivia** exception does not apply—OneCLI has no tactile consumer pull explosive enough to override the abstraction penalty.

Infisical’s April 2026 Agent Vault determines whether OneCLI remains magically weird over the next 18 months. OneCLI’s network-layer injection can compound as integrations, organization policies, approval histories, and NanoClaw’s default installation turn a clever proxy into a security control plane. The opposing mechanism is brutal: Infisical already implements a similar open-source HTTP credential proxy, while Oasis, Astrix, and Entro sell to the same security buyer. OneCLI is non-consensus in implementation, not category; 320K downloads and a crowded agent-security narrative mean OneCLI is already becoming legible. This is a rebuttal, not a hard no, but the round is only underpriced if paid organizations attach to the gateway faster than incumbents can copy it.

Jonathan Fishner is explicitly OneCLI’s CEO, while Guy Ben-Aharon is explicitly OneCLI’s CTO. That is better than the **HQ Trivia** pattern of authority dissolving into a founder feud, but titles are not a tiebreaker protocol: the dossier provides no equity split, board arrangement, vesting terms, or exit-conflict mechanism. OneCLI would move up a full tier for me if Fishner holds documented final operating authority and the founders can describe what happens when product velocity conflicts with Ben-Aharon’s security standard. Until then, OneCLI gets credit for clear roles, not for governance evidence I have not seen.

The $1,499-per-month managed VPC tier puts OneCLI closer to **Vigil Labs** than to another disposable AI wrapper: both sell control inside a domain where mistakes become expensive and policy accumulates. OneCLI nevertheless lacks Vigil’s regulated-finance friction, and OneCLI has none of the capital intensity that made SpaceX or Crusoe aesthetically uninvestable. Proxy bypass, root access to decrypted memory, and future HSM or trusted-execution-environment support create real technical hardness, but today those are security constraints around software—not frontier hardware or a regulatory moat. The ugly, unsexy part is present; the capital-intensive swing is not.

OneCLI’s prior ChartDB execution and NanoClaw distribution lead me to a falsifiable forecast: by July 2029, OneCLI reaches $5M–$10M ARR and is acquired by **1Password** for its agent-identity gateway rather than becoming an independent category leader. OneCLI can reach that outcome because Fishner and Ben-Aharon already demonstrated open-source distribution and monetization together, while OneCLI’s $199, $499, and $1,499 price points provide a direct ladder from developer adoption to security-budget revenue. The same low tactile score that limits my conviction also makes acquisition logical: OneCLI can become valuable infrastructure without becoming a beloved standalone destination.

Infisical is the bear mechanism that stings: OneCLI’s core is Apache-2.0, Infisical has an MIT-licensed competing architecture, Oasis has raised $195M, and OneCLI discloses pricing but no revenue. The 320K downloads could be NanoClaw-driven installation noise, and OneCLI could become a feature before it becomes a business. I still write a small personal check because OneCLI’s founders have lived security, shipped together, reached $10K MRR before, and earned default distribution before Demo Day—the **DeepMind** exception is unnecessary because the core founder-origin criterion already passes. My verdict flips to Pass if OneCLI has fewer than 25 paying organizations or less than $50K ARR by July 2027; that would show that developer fascination is not crossing the enterprise identity boundary.
