# Egoist Machines -- Elad Gil Evaluation

AI assistants now accumulate durable user context inside separate platforms; independent AI applications may want portable context, but Egoist Machines becomes large only if enough applications adopt its permission layer that repeated cross-app context reads outweigh integration and consent friction. That is the controlling variable. The dossier identifies the mechanism, but not the threshold: no integration partners, successful passport reads, or evidence that developers will pay for access are disclosed.

The product is more thoughtful than a generic memory wrapper. Field-level requests, duration limits, user approval, and access receipts create permission state that individual applications should not control. Better models could increase the quantity and value of persistent context, strengthening this layer. But Egoist Machines does not yet own an end-to-end workflow: applications must request and honor its passes, while OpenAI and Anthropic control the underlying interfaces. Mem0, Supermemory, and Letta can also reproduce much of the memory infrastructure. The architecture is differentiated; its control point is unproven.

Demand evidence is presently weak even by pre-seed standards. The Passport waitlist opened on July 13, 2026, but there are no active-user, repeat-usage, integration, or revenue figures. The consumer product is advertised as free for life, and the developer page solicits pilots without published pricing. The contrast between Erin McGurk’s reported audience of more than one million followers and only 268 company LinkedIn followers is particularly useful: the team possesses distribution inventory, but has not yet demonstrated distribution conversion. Press coverage and YC selection do not validate product-market pull.

The bull case is real. David Khachaturov combines a Cambridge computer-science PhD, OpenAI PaperBench work, AI-security experience, and a 4.7K-star GitHub project; McGurk brings consumer reach and an ability to explain unfamiliar products. As platform memory expands, privacy pressure could force a neutral consent and audit layer into the stack. This also resembles my unstated pattern of buying multiple layers around a technical discontinuity before the market fully crystallizes. The objection is that this market is already partly crystallizing around powerful platform owners and funded developer-memory vendors, so Egoist Machines now needs a sharper winner case than “user ownership” alone.

The no-traction exception does not apply yet. This is not scarce frontier research whose technical team and capital path can override absent demand; it is an ecosystem-coordination product whose core risk can only be resolved through ecosystem adoption. The relevant red flags are platform dependency and an inferred data or network moat with no demonstrated reproduction cost. There is no evidence of premature hiring, founder liquidity, or fundraising as status, but neither founder biography documents prior startup learning or the costly desperation that would justify additional founder credit.

My base case is that within 36 months Egoist Machines pivots from a consumer Passport to a developer-facing consent and audit SDK and remains below $1 million ARR. I would pass today. One verifiable milestone would flip the decision: five independent AI applications each completing at least 1,000 Passport reads per month for three consecutive months. That would prove the neutral layer is reducing friction rather than adding another approval surface, and it would create a plausible path from the first integration to reusable distribution.
