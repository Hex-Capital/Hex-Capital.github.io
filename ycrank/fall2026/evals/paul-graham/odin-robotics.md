# Odin Robotics -- Paul Graham Evaluation

I want to know what happened when Odin Robotics first tried to pick up a badly positioned part. Did the founders spend the night fixing it? Did they change the gripper, the training data, or the task? The dossier identifies the obstacle—variation defeats fixed automation—and shows CNC-tending and conveyor-packing demonstrations. It supplies no specific episode connecting a founder’s intervention to a successful workaround. That leaves my most important question unanswered ([company website](https://odin-robotics.com)).

The demonstrations still count. Samriddhi Singh and Alper Sametoglu have built something that moves physical objects. Singh’s industrial-robot experience and Sametoglu’s propulsion and hardware work make the technical ambition credible. Combining programmed precision with learned handling also sounds sensible. But the public evidence stops before cycle times, intervention rates, or sustained operation under changing conditions. I give the functioning demonstrations intermediate technical credit. I cannot infer production reliability or exceptional resourcefulness from them.

The strongest case for investing is that Odin is willing to do the unpleasant work. Existing factories have machines, safety requirements, operators, and inconvenient layouts. Installing a useful robot means dealing with all of them. Satisfaction-contingent pilot payment puts some performance risk on Odin; monthly rental could let a small manufacturer buy an outcome without committing to equipment ownership. Those choices could expose the founders to unusually useful feedback. Yet the payment terms describe an offer. The dossier identifies no factory repeatedly using it or paying for it. CNC operators are a plausible narrow well; their urgency remains untested in the available evidence.

This is where my unstated preference for concrete proof matters. Odin claims reusable skills and integrations, but reports no reduction in installation time across deployments. That missing measurement separates the possible scalable business from a succession of custom installations. RobCo already combines industrial hardware and learned execution, so Odin will have to discover something through serving factories that competitors cannot obtain merely by adopting similar software. The founders’ relevant experience helps them attempt that discovery. It does not establish that they personally encountered this problem, or that customer evidence has already changed their route ([YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/WJg-odin-robotics-robot-arms-that-do-repetitive-manual-work-in-existing-factories)).

I checked the exceptions because working hardware deserves a serious hearing. The prototype exception could matter here, but the dossier lacks the accompanying evidence of unusually rapid founder correction. The independently validated technical-proof exception also remains unavailable. There is no unmistakable user pull to substitute for either. Conversely, I see no affirmative evidence of founders waiting for permission, delegating away the product, or persisting against contrary feedback. Unknown cofounder history and a public launch establish none of those red flags.

I would pass on Odin Robotics today. The technical artifact is its strongest signal; founder behavior under resistance remains largely unobserved. My flip condition is one documented, founder-led factory installation where a named production blocker prompted a shipped fix within weeks and the customer subsequently paid for a month of repeated operation. That single case would connect the robot, the founders, and a real need.
