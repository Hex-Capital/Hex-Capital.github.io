# Ample Computer -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Ample Computer lets an agent deploy an application before a human claims it. That is a potentially consequential place to sit: whoever provisions the application may choose its infrastructure before a developer ever compares hosting vendors. But what am I paying for that possibility? The dossier supplies no post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that my check fills a scarce capital need. YC backing establishes none of those things. I cannot underwrite cheap optionality without the strike price, and financing silence is not evidence of an auction either.

The product question is whether that first deployment becomes an owned customer relationship. Ample provisions isolated microVMs, databases, storage, and an HTTPS endpoint through its MCP connector or CLI. If those applications persist, Ample could collect recurring infrastructure spend after the initial agent interaction. But the Anthropic-verified connector added in October 2026 and the Hermes plugin establish availability through somebody else’s distribution. Six showcased applications establish examples; their ownership, payment status, and repeat usage remain undisclosed. There is no demonstrated chain from proprietary access to purchase. Railway and Render already expose infrastructure operations to agents, so merely speaking the agent’s language does not establish control over its choice of provider.

The strongest argument against passing is that the founders have already shipped enough to make that ownership transition plausible. Ravi has founded prior products and has a reported earlier acquisition; Ishan published the protocol implementation report describing production operation on October 17, 2026. Their Shopify backgrounds give the team relevant product context, while the current integration work supplies a concrete technical receipt beyond job titles. This fits my interest in people translating between systems: autonomous software creation on one side, persistent application operation on the other. The unresolved issue is whether they can make that bridge economically privileged. The dossier demonstrates shipping, but does not establish prior operation of infrastructure at scale or exclusive access to agent-generated workloads.

Usage billing is a sensible starting mechanism for capturing value. Per-second compute metering, a one-minute minimum, and monthly invoices could connect customer activity directly to cash. They do not tell me the spread after compute costs, support, and reliability work. There is no disclosed margin, burn, runway, or seed-funded break-even plan, so I assign the economics default rather than inventing either efficiency or distress. Likewise, the dossier names no capital-funded experiment with a deadline and a measurable result. A test of retained, paying agent-originated deployments would be useful; it is my proposed test, not evidence of the founders’ financing plan.

I pass on the evidence available. Ample has a functioning product and credible builders, but neither the deal nor the discrete truth my money buys is specified. My exceptions do not resolve that: there is no documented conversion and retention record, acquisition alternative, or financing structure that supplies an independent reason to override the missing gates. Ravi’s reported prior exit earns founder credit; it cannot also establish capital efficiency. My one verifiable flip condition is a sub-$10 million post-money financing backed by a costed six-month experiment that actually reaches cash break-even on retained, independently paying deployments. That would connect price, a finite venture moment, and a business capable of surviving without the next round.
