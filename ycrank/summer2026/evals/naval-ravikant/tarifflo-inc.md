# Tarifflo Inc. -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Bryce Judy has acquired specific knowledge through costly action. He classified imports at KPMG, earned a CBP customs-broker license, published HTS benchmarking research, obtained an ABI filer code, and helped build a system that has audited more than 1 million products. The $1.3 million recovered for Orly after reviewing 10,000-plus items is stronger evidence than a résumé: the founder’s knowledge has already produced an economically meaningful outcome.

The harder question is what remains after a competitor copies the workflow. Direct CBP connectivity and licensed-broker review create real implementation friction, while 10 million linked shipment records could become proprietary intelligence. But “could” is not a moat. The dossier does not establish exclusive ownership, superior accuracy from accumulated outcomes, or switching costs, and Zonos, KlearNow, iCustoms, and Altana already possess overlapping customs infrastructure, data, and government relationships. Tarifflo has built more than a commodity AI wrapper, but it has not yet proved that deployments compound into an advantage competitors cannot reproduce.

The market has room to maneuver. Free filing can acquire U.S. importers; recovery audits monetize historical mistakes; the same rule engine and entry data can extend into classification, supplier intelligence, broker workflows, and eventually other customs jurisdictions. Importers, freight brokers, and brokerage partners are identifiable buyers or channels, and the underlying spend is concrete: a $5.48 billion U.S. brokerage market inside a $23.78 billion global market. The U.S. wedge is credible, although its direct-CBP integration does not automatically transfer abroad.

Economics are promising but partially hidden. Contingency pricing aligns payment with recovered value, and a $1.3 million customer recovery demonstrates a large value pool. Yet Tarifflo discloses neither its take rate nor collected revenue, while free filing and refund-dependent payment can create long working-capital cycles around correction and protest deadlines. Customer savings are not company revenue. The business has demonstrated value creation more clearly than value capture.

The missing leverage is user-to-user compounding. Brokerage partners may distribute the product, but the dossier shows no referral, contribution, liquidity, or matching loop through which one importer improves Tarifflo for another. Its 10 million shipment records might eventually support such a loop, but no measured learning effect or customer contribution mechanism is public. The unstated pattern only partially fits: Tarifflo digitizes fragmented compliance work, but it is currently a centralized software-and-broker service rather than a permissionless network.

The strongest bear case is that policy complexity forces permanent expert labor while funded competitors duplicate the software, leaving Tarifflo as a contingency consultancy with delayed cash flows. No disclosed integrity, reassurance-seeking, artificial-deadline, or other founder red flag strengthens that case, and the shipped product plus Orly result justify preserving option value. I would invest a bounded pre-seed check, not underwrite a defining network yet. My verifiable flip condition is financial: if Tarifflo has not collected at least $1 million in cumulative contingency fees by August 2027—not merely identified or recovered savings for customers—I pass on further capital.
