# Ryhla -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Ryhla’s strongest exit route is a sale to a utility software vendor seeking to add developer-side feeder screening and application management. Ryhla reports that screening out eight of ten planned battery projects saved one customer about $160,000 in application fees. The memo records E.ON’s majority acquisition of distribution-grid software provider envelio, showing that a strategic buyer has acquired an adjacent asset. The inference is that owning Ryhla could help a buyer win more interconnection work by combining early site screening with filing management. How much of the reported customer savings a buyer could capture remains unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is a paid rollout across several utility territories that measures fee savings and checks both recommended and rejected sites against utility outcomes. Ryhla reports live models in 11 territories and a live Midwest solar deployment, giving it a starting point for the test. Its reported 91% approval rate covers recommended sites, with sample size and false negatives undisclosed. The cost and timing of validation and expansion remain uncertain. Transfer rights for feeder data and agreements remain unverified. The memo supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility)

The proceeds comparison assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× objective is $2.5 million in cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million of distributable equity on a full pro rata sale. The sale cases below assume an eligible holder sells its entire stake for cash within eight years, after $5 million in net debt and deal adjustments, with the remaining equity distributed pro rata and no preference diversion.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Holder cash |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M in adjustments leaves $45M for equity holders | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $5M in adjustments leaves $95M for equity holders | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested and no eligible share purchase or distribution occurs within eight years | $0 |

In the strong case, selling only 80% of the stake reduces realized cash to $2.28 million, below the objective. The sale prices are illustrative assumptions. The memo provides no acquisition price, revenue, or holder terms that would substantiate the strong case. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A strategic buyer could cover enough of the workflow through existing products and partnerships. PowerClerk already integrates envelio’s utility-side modeling and technical reviews, and public hosting-capacity maps can cover initial screening where data are adequate. Territory-specific calibration could further limit what a buyer can deploy economically. This weakens the case for an acquisition premium. The memo establishes neither a structural block to a sale nor materially inadequate proceeds under the strongest credible route, so the Pass gate remains open. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What could a strategic buyer justify paying for Ryhla after a paid rollout demonstrates repeatable savings across several utility territories? A buyer-backed valuation range tied to the economics of owning the workflow would move the action to Reach out if it supports $2.5 million in holder cash after deal adjustments, the payout waterfall, and any limits on the stake sold.
