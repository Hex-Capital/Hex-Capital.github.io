# Prescience, Inc. -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The contested truth is that an insurer can use longitudinal medical histories to choose better care trajectories and share the resulting savings, making superior clinical intelligence—not risk selection—the basis of a profitable health plan. What would prove this wrong? If matched employer cohorts show no material reduction in claims after Time Machine routes care, the company is merely adding an AI interface to conventional benefits administration. Prescience has stated a falsifiable technical idea, but it has published no model validation, clinical outcomes, or customer-level savings study. For now, this is a secret asserted, not discovered.

The strongest evidence is foundational. Rishab and Aditya Jain are brothers who have built projects together for ten years; one brings more than ten ML and health papers and company-reported acquisitions, while the other combines an MD with biomedical engineering and medical-AI work. This is unusually coherent founder formation for a two-person pre-seed company. Yet the dossier does not establish that both are full-time, how much they own, or whether essential engineering remains entirely in-house. Long alignment is affirmative evidence; economic exposure remains unknown.

The monopoly question is much worse. Prescience holds California Administrator License #6019366 and reports integrations across banking, HSAs, provider networks, compliance, risk, and plan administration. Those assets may accelerate entry, but they do not yet preserve value. Angle Health already combines carrier, TPA, underwriting, and AI; Collective Health offers AI navigation and plan-administration automation; Sana sells level-funded plans to small and midsize employers. Prescience names no bounded segment it already dominates, no exclusive data, no measured technical advantage, and no structural barrier. This is presently competition with a sophisticated product, not a monopoly with competitors.

The savings-linked model is directionally intelligent: charging no administrative fee makes the buyer’s downside legible and could align Prescience with the employer against healthcare inflation. But neither the savings percentage, contract minimum, gross margin, nor number of served employers is public. A $1.6446 trillion spending pool does not supply the missing arithmetic. Nor is distribution solved: the company solicits introductions to founders, CFOs, benefits managers, brokers, HRIS platforms, and medical officers, which is a list of possible routes rather than evidence of one repeatable route with a known sales cycle.

The bull case stings. Two long-aligned founders combine model-building and clinical judgment; Prescience already has a California license, claims to serve U.S. employers, and accepts payment only after producing savings. If each deployment generates proprietary claims, navigation, price, and outcome data, the product could improve while incumbents remain trapped in fragmented incentives. This resembles the monopoly uncertainty around OpenAI only superficially: OpenAI’s uncertainty was overridden by a publicly observable zero-to-one technical event, whereas Time Machine has no disclosed independent result. The known exception therefore does not apply.

The red flag is coordination masquerading as integration. Prescience must validate its clinical model, protect PHI without a completed SOC 2 examination, navigate state-by-state regulation, integrate plan administration, acquire employers through intermediaries, and demonstrate savings before it can earn meaningful revenue. Its application-layer position also runs against the stronger unstated pattern of owning a critical layer of the AI production stack; inferred future data advantages cannot substitute for present technical proof. I pass today. One condition would flip the decision: an independently verified twelve-month cohort showing at least 15% claims-cost reduction across ten named employers, accompanied by renewals. That would simultaneously turn the secret into evidence and the license-and-data bundle into the beginning of a monopoly mechanism.
