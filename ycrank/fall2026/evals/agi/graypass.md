# GrayPass -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** GrayPass could supply reliable assurance about who is directing a sensitive action inside an authenticated session. The likely buyers are application teams responsible for payouts, data exports, or permission changes. The thesis depends on a scenario in which cheaper browser agents increase the volume of delegated actions and make session-level identity less useful at the moment of execution. GrayPass documents a beta decision API, with behavioral continuity signals limited to sandbox and shadow evaluation. That supports a plausible seed-stage mechanism. Its ability to catch unauthorized actions at an acceptable rate of challenges for legitimate users remains unmeasured in the memo. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

GrayPass documents short-lived proofs bound to an exact action and application, along with customer-scoped behavioral templates. Enrollment and integration with customer policies could give buyers a reason to stay if those features improve live decisions. The memo offers no retention or migration data to establish that advantage. GrayPass reports more than 900,000 customer-app sessions and $50,000 in October 2026 revenue as of launch. Those claims suggest early use and payment, while the number of independent paying buyers, repeat purchases, and the share of use in live enforcement remain unknown. Mathur’s application engineering and Ranjan’s computational research are relevant to the next milestone. The memo provides limited evidence of their experience qualifying a security product for production enforcement. The Toronto listing and reported move to San Francisco leave a plausible geographic fit for a primarily U.S. seed strategy. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team)

The expansion unit is another protected workflow. GrayPass lists pricing from $500 per month and meters usage by assured actions or active Brainprint months. In an expansion scenario, added usage earns incremental fees while integration and consent work per workflow fall. Contribution depends on those fees covering decision processing, integration support, consent handling, and exception review. The memo leaves those costs and repeat rollout economics unknown. Path B has weaker support because the memo does not identify a previously uneconomic buyer or transaction made viable by measured delivery labor savings. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Castle already combines behavioral and device evidence with application-defined decisions, and LexisNexis bundles behavioral analytics with identity and device intelligence. With ample capital and access to the same models, a competitor could seek the same customer telemetry and server-side integration. Customers control which workflows enforce the decision, and GrayPass’s founder says incumbents could build the capability. If an incumbent delivers equivalent protection at comparable cost and user friction, GrayPass’s action-bound proof may offer little reason to buy a separate product. The memo establishes meaningful overlap and leaves equivalence on an exact protected action unresolved. That keeps the Path A override open and supports Watch. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent payout platform pay to keep GrayPass in live enforcement after a head-to-head trial against its best available incumbent option? A persuasive seed-stage result would show that GrayPass catches materially more unauthorized payouts at comparable rates of disruption to legitimate users and at an acceptable integration and operating cost, followed by a paid production commitment. That would establish relief of the bottleneck and a reason to choose GrayPass, reopening the case for Reach out if rescoring clears the 65-point gate. Equivalent results at comparable cost and friction, with the buyer seeing no reason to pay for GrayPass, would move the action to Pass.
