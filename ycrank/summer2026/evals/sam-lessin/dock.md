# Dock -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The founders are substantially more convincing than the deal. Govind Kavaturi helped Branch grow from zero to $100 million in revenue, Mike Molinet co-founded and operated it, and the pair subsequently built Thena together. They understand how B2B teams coordinate work and buy software. Dock’s shared tables, agent-specific credentials, workspace permissions, approval gates, and replayable attribution history are a shipped translation layer between human organizations and autonomous software—not merely two résumés attached to an agent-workspace pitch.

But I invest in a security, not an abstractly good team. The dossier gives me no post-money valuation, no competing-term-sheet count, and no evidence that this is a scarce first check rather than another YC auction. It also never says what the round will prove. “Run your company” contains several hypotheses—general-purpose artifacts, multi-agent coordination, enterprise permissions, and cross-functional adoption—without one dated experiment or pass/fail metric. Capital that simultaneously funds product, compliance, distribution, and category creation is not a venture moment; it is a small capital factory.

The strongest bull case is that the product is already technically coherent before distribution arrives. Separate keys, scoped roles, REST/MCP access, real-time synchronization, human approval, and attributed event history address real failure modes as task-specific agents spread through enterprise applications. The founders have operated the customer side of that system, while Dock itself provides an initial receipt on the agent side. A cheap round aimed at proving that one operating team will move a recurring workflow into Dock could therefore be unusually efficient.

The current evidence does not establish that loop. One GitHub follower, one star on the strongest visible repository, zero Toolify reviews, and no reported customer, purchase, repeat-use, or retention signal mean there is no demonstrated proprietary workflow causing economic action. The founders’ combined LinkedIn reach of roughly 26,000 is rented attention until it converts. Meanwhile Linear, Cursor, Lindy, Replit, Hex, and Relevance AI can extend adjacent workspaces into the same general surface. Dock’s accumulated schemas and audit history become a dark pool only after customers entrust important work to it; today that moat is hypothetical.

The economics are equally unresolved. Charging $19 or $49 per organization could support a capital-efficient self-serve product, but there is no revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or route to break-even without another financing. Low pricing may instead make the enterprise security burden fatal: Dock lacks SOC 2 Type II, penetration testing, HIPAA eligibility, and ISO certification, while recoverable webhook secrets and browser tokens remain server-side. If serious customers require enterprise assurance, a $49 plan will not finance the work needed to win them.

I expect the general-purpose “workspace to run your company” framing to narrow into a specific agent-operations or audit workflow within 36 months, because founder operating depth is real but horizontal distribution and purchase evidence are not. The verifiable flip condition is simple: show three unaffiliated organizations paying for the same recurring workflow, retaining for six months, and name a round-funded milestone—with a date—that gets Dock to break-even without assuming a Series A. Without that, I am passing on a credible team packaged inside an unpriced, unbounded AI thesis.
