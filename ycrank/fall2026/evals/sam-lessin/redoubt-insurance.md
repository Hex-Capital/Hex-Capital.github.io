# Redoubt Insurance -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Redoubt Insurance sits close to money already changing hands: a contractor needs insurance documents before work can start. That is a useful place to build a business. But the reported $500,000 YC investment tells me neither the proposed post-money valuation nor how much competition there is for this round. I cannot establish that my check buys an overlooked belief at a sensible price. Financing silence is missing evidence; it does not establish an auction or a bargain.

The strongest case is the ordinary agency underneath the automation ambition. Redoubt publishes Utah license 1116212 and relationships with Progressive Commercial, Cover Whale, Smart Choice, and Burns & Wilcox. Its contractor setup checker and licensing guides address a specific moment when someone might need to purchase coverage. This fits my preference for businesses that connect difficult systems and can become economically meaningful within a local market. The objection to passing is substantial: a useful brokerage could earn commissions while gradually automating its own work, without first winning some grand contest in AI.

What stops that argument is the missing link between access and purchase. The dossier documents the tools and relationships, but no resulting bound policy, paid customer, referral, or repeat servicing interaction. The 115 LinkedIn followers do not answer that question. Carrier access is useful infrastructure, but the disclosed relationships are not exclusive, and publishing a guide does not yet establish an owned customer relationship. Harper already advertises automated brokerage workflows across more than 165 underwriters. Redoubt’s possible advantage is earning and retaining contractor trust; equivalent automation alone would leave that advantage unproven.

Andre Beukers brings something more concrete than impressive affiliations: his public reinforcement-learning research code demonstrates implementation work, and Redoubt has published practical insurance tools. That earns modest founder credit. It does not establish a history of successfully operating across both insurance and software. The company’s distinction between certificate wording and actual policy coverage is also sensible. It recognizes that a document can look correct while failing the customer’s contractual requirement. I would preserve that operational judgment when automating; I would not underwrite margins that require consequential errors to disappear.

There is no disclosed capital-funded experiment with a metric and deadline, and no commission revenue, servicing cost, burn, or break-even plan. Those omissions receive my stated defaults rather than an additional penalty for being pre-seed. They also leave the exceptions to my normal gates unsupported: there is no documented conversion or cash performance strong enough to override the missing deal and capital plan. **I pass on the evidence available.** One result would reopen the conversation: a measured Utah contractor cohort whose collected commissions exceed fully loaded acquisition and servicing costs through its first renewal. That would establish that Redoubt owns an economically productive relationship and give me something concrete to price.
