# Stacktrace -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Stacktrace could remove a constraint on autonomous work: every agent that needs continuous human supervision carries a hidden labor cost. Eliminating that supervision could matter far beyond security spending. But the dossier does not quantify supervision hours, losses avoided, or additional work safely automated. The projected $1.8 billion agent-security market measures vendor spending, not the consequence of solving the problem. I cannot purchase a world-changing option on a market-growth forecast alone.

The product has substance. Local transcript processing, component attribution, and deterministic detection rules constitute an inspectable implementation. The credential-leak demonstration gives prospective customers something concrete to test. But Stacktrace’s endpoint sidecar reads logs and cannot itself stop calls; blocking requires the inline gateway. That distinction determines whether the product reduces investigation effort or enables unattended execution. Neither deployment has a disclosed performance benchmark against Noma’s contextual enforcement or Oligo’s runtime instrumentation. Different instrumentation does not establish a technical discontinuity. I assign the technical evidence its missing-benchmark default rather than assume either superiority or inferiority.

The strongest argument against passing is the team’s proximity to the problem. Kone owned software supply-chain security at Harness; Benedict operated infrastructure, security, and AI platforms at Airtable; Ganta brings AWS and generative-AI operating experience. These founders could recognize deployment failures before outsiders can describe them. Design partners running agents locally and in the cloud provide access to those failures. My actual investing pattern allows execution-driven infrastructure businesses, and open-source collection could become distribution. Nevertheless, nine OpenACA stars do not establish a committed technical community, and career histories do not document belief revision or difficult complementary recruiting. The founder criterion remains at its default. The exceptional-case override also fails: the dossier establishes neither a quantified giant value pool unlocked by this mechanism nor an extraordinary consequence sufficient to waive the technical gate.

The next dollar should establish whether Stacktrace makes unattended operation acceptably safe. CLI version 0.17.0, released October 27, demonstrates shipped software; it does not establish that the largest deployment risk has been retired. There is no disclosed experiment with a failure threshold, budget, or stop decision, so the milestone criterion receives its default. Economics are similarly unresolved. Free access for 100 agents and 200 session-hours encourages experimentation, but custom annual Enterprise and Gateway pricing reveals neither delivery cost nor customer savings. I see no evidence of permanent subsidy dependence. I also see no evidence supporting cost parity, leaving that criterion at its default.

I pass with my own money today. Stacktrace has credible builders and a real operational problem, but the evidence does not yet connect its instrumentation to an order-of-magnitude outcome. The concern is overlapping control functionality without demonstrated capability advantage; the dossier does not establish that this is merely an AI wrapper. One result would reopen the decision: an independently reproducible production comparison against Noma showing at least 10x fewer human supervision hours at an equal or lower rate of successful harmful actions, with Stacktrace’s full deployment cost below the labor cost removed. That would connect the product to a consequential economic mechanism.
