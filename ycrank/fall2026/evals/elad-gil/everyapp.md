# EveryApp -- Elad Gil Evaluation

EveryApp is betting that cheaper application creation shifts the bottleneck toward deployment, identity, and operations. Developers and small businesses become the buyers; recurring gross profit per customer determines whether this becomes a large company. The critical belief is that customers will pay EveryApp to operate an expanding collection of applications even when the software itself is free. The dossier establishes the deployment mechanism, but provides no evidence yet of that economic relationship. An announced $10/month managed service is a starting price, not demonstrated value capture.

The most compelling asset is the shared Gateway. Applications run in the customer’s Cloudflare account, while the Gateway handles sign-in, permissions, and API credentials across Workers, D1, R2, and KV. That is meaningful operational functionality. Better coding models could increase the supply of compatible applications and make this infrastructure more useful. Customer databases and configured permissions also give the product workflow state beyond generated code. However, EveryApp’s [early-adopter documentation](https://everyapp.com/docs/early-adopters) declines to promise backward compatibility. Preserving data and creating restore points help, but reliable upgrades remain part of the operating burden customers would be paying to remove.

The economic step change is less established. The dossier’s coding-agent explanation is explicitly inferred, and there is no quantified reduction in deployment time, maintenance cost, or customer spending. Cloudron already packages applications with centralized users, updates, and backups; Coolify supports broader deployment targets and agent access through MCP. EveryApp’s Cloudflare standardization could simplify operations, but it also narrows compatibility. I cannot award an economic discontinuity premium merely because application generation is improving.

The strongest argument against passing is distribution. OpenSEO has 25.3K GitHub stars and claims more than 3,000 entrepreneurs. EveryApp’s October 26 launch attracted 128,561 post views by the following day. An existing application could become the acquisition channel for shared infrastructure, then introduce customers to additional applications without reacquiring them. That fits my preference for a first product becoming distribution. But OpenSEO is separately identified, its paying population is unknown, and the new platform’s 351 stars and 16 forks establish attention rather than repeat operational dependence. I would not transfer the standalone product’s audience into platform product-market fit.

Ben Senescu has more relevant experience than an employer list alone conveys. He previously co-founded artst, launched an initial Gateway, built OpenSEO, and returned to the broader platform. Those are concrete startup iterations and direct experience building on his own infrastructure. They deserve credit without inventing a prior exit or personal sacrifice. This also fits the pattern of backing tooling around a technology transition. The counterweight is reproducibility: MIT licensing permits competing managed offerings, and the dossier does not establish an integration ecosystem or operating advantage that would make those copies difficult to displace. The research-stage exception does not resolve this gap; there is no documented frontier-research asset or scarce model-building capability supporting that override.

I would pass on a personal check today. EveryApp has credible workflow infrastructure, but the evidence is stronger for what it operates than for why customers will repeatedly pay it. My single flip condition is a dated cohort of at least 20 independent paying organizations that renew for three consecutive months while running multiple applications through the Gateway at positive contribution margin. That would directly test whether the shared infrastructure creates a commercial relationship beyond one application and its launch audience.
