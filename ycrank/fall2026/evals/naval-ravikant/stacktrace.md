# Stacktrace -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Stacktrace’s founders have earned the right to investigate this problem. Kone operated infrastructure and held software supply-chain security responsibility at Harness; Benedict ran infrastructure, security, and AI platforms at Airtable; Ganta has demonstrated credential leakage in an unattended agent workflow. There is also a shipped artifact: CLI version 0.17.0, released October 27, 2026. This is specific knowledge expressed through action. It earns substantial founder credit, although the dossier does not establish that they have already sold or operated agent security successfully at scale.

What survives if someone copies the implementation? Stacktrace’s session collection and composition-analysis foundations are Apache-2.0 licensed. The complete product is proprietary, but proprietary ownership alone does not make software difficult to reproduce. Local processing, component attribution, and deterministic rules describe useful capabilities; they do not establish an exclusive asset. There is no demonstrated proprietary model, unavailable dataset, or accumulated switching cost. Open source could attract contributors, but the dossier shows no contribution or referral loop through which another customer makes the product better for existing customers. Permissionless installation is a distribution choice. Its compounding effect remains unproven.

The technical boundary matters economically. Stacktrace’s endpoint sidecar reads logs and cannot itself stop calls. Blocking requires the inline gateway, and dynamic steering remains forthcoming. This creates an adoption tradeoff: the easy deployment observes; the deployment that controls must enter the execution path. Noma already advertises enforcement, steering, masking, and kill switches. Deterministic detection can be a sensible engineering choice, but Stacktrace still needs a reason an enterprise will select and retain its control layer when competitors offer overlapping capabilities.

The expansion path is credible enough to keep my attention. Start with engineers supervising local and cloud agents, then extend the same session attribution into organization-wide security policies and gateway enforcement. Those are identifiable adjacent buyers and workflows. The estimated $1.8 billion agentic-security market supports the existence of a category, but does not establish Stacktrace’s obtainable spend. Enterprise session-hour commitments and annual Gateway pricing identify a payer and a charging mechanism. They do not prove value capture: the Team tier supplies 100 agents and 200 session-hours monthly for free, while realized revenue, paid conversions, and contribution economics remain undisclosed. Unnamed design partners establish experimentation, not willingness to pay.

The strongest objection to withholding a check is that these founders may be building the instrumentation layer before anyone knows which security policies matter. Their operating experience, working CLI, local processing, and production design partners could put them close to the failures that define the category. That is a serious pre-seed argument. My exceptions for demonstrated prototypes and unusually capable founders leave room for it. But the dossier does not establish exceptional capital efficiency or recruiting behavior strong enough to override the structural gap. Stacktrace fits my pattern of turning technical knowledge into scalable tools; it has yet to demonstrate the customer-created value that makes those tools compound. No integrity, artificial-deadline, or founder-instability red flag is documented. The concern is durability, not character.

I am neutral and would hold my personal check. My flip condition is a measured deployment study showing that learning from one customer’s incidents improves detection for other customers, with documented rights making that learning unavailable to competitors. That would turn Stacktrace’s proximity to agent failures into an accumulating asset. Today, the founders are the strongest evidence; the product has not yet shown how their advantage grows while they sleep.
