# Waddle Labs -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Approximately 1,000 randomized LEGO pick-and-place repetitions make **Waddle Labs** a recently catalyzed robotics-infrastructure bet whose technical loop is already visible, but whose commercial loop is absent. That high-catalyst, low-revenue pairing is the analytical signature: **Waddle Labs** may be skating for the puck technically, while no customer evidence yet shows that the puck is moving there commercially.

**Stripe** (2010) is the high rung on market re-opening: APIs transformed an existing payments market by removing merchant-account friction. **Waddle Labs** shares the mechanism—a new abstraction layer becomes possible after frontier reasoning models plus 2026 closed-loop methods such as CaP-X and ASPIRE—but the analogy stops at timing. Stripe’s catalyst immediately unlocked developer demand; **Waddle Labs** has only an inferred catalyst, a preview API, and no evidence that industrial robotics buyers recently changed budgets or deployment behavior.

**Lyft** at Series C exposed the low-rung failure mechanism: apparent activity is a false signal when it does not demonstrate improving market position or economics. **Waddle Labs** reports no revenue, signed LOIs, repeat external usage, or named customers; 20-minute policy generation and 1,000 internal repetitions validate a prototype, not buying behavior. At pre-seed I do not require meaningful ARR, but for an AI company I require some evidence that usage is “just working.” **Waddle Labs** provides no such evidence, so I apply the absent-evidence default.

Six months of robot data and intervention traces could let **Waddle Labs** compound over the next 18 months: each successful deployment adds parameterized skills, failure recoveries, and cross-robot knowledge that lower the cost of the next deployment. The problem is that an index on robotics must sit in a workflow every robotics developer touches. **Waddle Labs** currently distributes through an early-access form to developers supplying their own hardware, while CaP-X and ASPIRE expose overlapping mechanisms and Physical Intelligence, Skild AI, and Dyna Robotics control far larger data pipelines. The skill library can compound, but **Waddle Labs** has not established that it will own the compounding layer.

CaP-X, ASPIRE, and larger upstream reasoning models reveal a multi-miracle risk inside **Waddle Labs**: frontier models must generate safe control code, skills must transfer across hardware and workspaces, and task economics must beat fine-tuning or classical programming. The score flips into Invest territory if **Waddle Labs** demonstrates that one unchanged API can solve production tasks across three materially different robot embodiments with bounded intervention cost; that would collapse the architecture into one validated capability followed by product and GTM execution. Today the upstream-model dependency and non-standardized benchmarks leave more than one independent uncertainty.

**Yiding Song** has worked on in-context program induction, while Hanming Ye developed parameter-free adaptation for robot diffusion policies; **Waddle Labs** therefore has considerably more founder-market fit than two generic AI founders wrapping an API. The relevant parallel is **Perplexity** (2022): Aravind Srinivas repeatedly converted conversations into shipped artifacts. **Waddle Labs** has shipped a working control loop and accumulated six months of traces, but the dossier shows neither a prior independently scaled product nor iterations between external user conversations. Waddle Labs has builders; the Perplexity execution rhythm remains unproven.

The preview API positions **Waddle Labs** closer to robotics developer tooling than to an autonomous-robotics platform. My 36-month forecast is that by July 2029, **Waddle Labs** will have pivoted from general-purpose robot control into simulation, testing, and failure-recovery tooling for robotics developers, reaching $1 million to $3 million ARR rather than becoming the default cross-robot control layer. The catalyst creates valuable technology, while the missing revenue curve and hardware heterogeneity force **Waddle Labs** toward a narrower workflow where reliability can be sold without owning physical execution.

**Physical Intelligence’s $1.07 billion raised** and Skild AI’s roughly $1.7 billion establish that capital believes general robot intelligence has reopened, while Waddle Labs’ 20-minute policy generation suggests a dramatically cheaper layer above those models. The strongest bull case is the **Color Genomics** exception: **Waddle Labs** is workflow infrastructure on top of the hard thing, not a science project, and its shared skill library could become an index on every downstream robot model. The evidence that wins today is that all validation remains internal, replication paths are published, and both founders face visa-related course-load and work constraints. Three named industrial customers using **Waddle Labs** weekly, with paid usage growing above 20% month-over-month for four consecutive months, would flip my verdict to Invest.
