# Proto -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The most useful question about Proto is what happened when a designer tried to use it and got stuck. The dossier doesn't contain that episode. It contains Maayan Albert identifying error, loading, and empty states as a problem, followed by demonstrations of automatic state generation. That establishes a specific product idea. It doesn't yet establish the founder's response to resistance. I can't infer the latter from a good résumé, even when the résumé fits the product unusually well.

Albert has been working near this problem for years. He co-created Stamper, an artboard-oriented programming environment documented by [CMU in May 2020](https://www.design.cmu.edu/news/design-alumni-develop-creative-coding-tool), and later launched Browserbase's Director. Proto's August and September demonstrations describe contextual comments that restore prototype states and decomposition into pull requests. These are concrete behaviors worth investigating. They also fit my unstated preference for founders building programmable interfaces. But the evidence remains founder-published demonstrations; there is no independently inspected system or measured result here. I give the technical work partial credit, without turning that same work into evidence of resourcefulness or customer demand.

The strongest case for investing is that Albert understands the seam between drawing an interface and making it behave. Proto could make neglected states cheap enough that designers explore them routinely, then preserve those decisions through engineering handoff. That would be more useful than another way to generate an attractive screen. Figma's March 2026 agent-editable canvas and Magic Patterns' October MagicOS release make this distinction consequential: generating and handing off designs already has substitutes. Proto needs users who specifically miss its treatment of behavior when they return to those substitutes.

Who are those users? Alpha access through founder DMs is a sensible way to find them, but the dossier supplies no repeat usage, payment, or unsolicited referral. The company's 65 followers and October 31 coverage cannot answer that question. Unpublished pricing and revenue are ordinary at this stage; they don't establish bad economics. The missing observation is whether a small group of designers repeatedly chooses Proto. Nor does Albert's relevant work history establish that he personally encountered this exact problem, or that Proto is tackling an ugly integration burden others refuse. Engineers still completing the connections after a submitted pull request leaves that possibility open.

I would pass on the present evidence. I checked the prototype exception represented by Dropbox: both involve a technical founder offering a different interface in territory with existing alternatives, but the decisive difference is the strength of the working-product evidence and demonstrated rapid correction. Proto's demonstrations don't yet supply an equivalent override. There is no documented partner dependency, premature delegation, or obstinacy here; unknown cofounder history is also not evidence of a hastily assembled relationship. There simply isn't an observed blocker-and-response sequence or evidence-driven route change. My flip condition is one documented four-week pilot with five product designers: a concrete workflow failure prompts a founder-built revision within days, the revised behavior works in their projects, and those designers subsequently use it weekly and pay. That single experiment could establish the founder behavior, technical proof, and narrow well of demand that would make me invest.
