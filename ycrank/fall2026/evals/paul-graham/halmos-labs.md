# Halmos Labs -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The most revealing detail about Halmos Labs is that its system repeatedly proposed experiments outside a collaborator’s permitted range. What did the founders change when this happened? The dossier describes experimentalists retaining control, but no shipped correction or elapsed time. That leaves my main founder question unanswered. Akke’s research at MIT, AstraZeneca, and Cambridge establishes relevant experience. It does not establish how he behaves when something breaks. I give resourcefulness the missing-evidence default, rather than treating an incomplete account as evidence of passive founders.

There is something here worth investigating. Halmos reports matching a bioprocessing campaign’s best performance with **30% fewer evaluations across repeated simulations**. Its retrospective epitope test placed a validated target within the top twenty predictions for each of five TESLA patients. These are specific results from an implemented system, which is substantially better than a description of what agents might eventually do. But the measurements are company-reported, and simulated savings do not establish savings at the laboratory bench. I give the technical work intermediate credit after the certainty discount. Automatic deductive checks can catch inconsistent reasoning while leaving a biologically wrong assumption intact.

The fermentation example is the strongest commercial evidence. An unnamed customer confirmed a missing nutrient variable and revised its experimental plan. Someone changed what they intended to do because of Halmos’s output. That earns demand credit beyond the default. The unanswered question is whether that customer returns with the next problem. A useful finding can sell a consulting project; repeated dependence can build a company. Direct outreach for scoped projects is a sensible way to discover that distinction. Published prices and revenue are absent, so the dossier does not yet establish which business is emerging.

The strongest argument for investing is that these founders already possess the ingredients to compress this learning cycle. Akke understands biochemical research; Löfgren brings quantitative modeling and LLM-security experience. Their collaboration results suggest they can get close enough to experimentalists to affect real decisions. This fits my pattern of backing technical teams that produce numerical evidence in unfamiliar scientific markets. The relevant exception is **Bindwell**: both teams connect computational methods to biological problems, but laboratory validation supplied the decisive additional evidence there. Halmos’s simulations and retrospective rankings do not yet supply an equivalent reason to override the missing founder evidence.

I also see no documented product revision tied to a falsified hypothesis. The customer’s revised experiment is evidence of customer response; it is not evidence that Halmos revised its own route. That dimension stays at its default. Akke’s prior research makes an organic origin plausible, but the dossier never identifies the particular problem he encountered that caused him to start Halmos. Nor does it establish a neglected operational burden the team has already undertaken. Meanwhile, Atinary already offers experiment optimization and instrument integration. Halmos needs to discover the narrow research job where customers repeatedly prefer its judgment. Its launch coverage cannot answer that question, though its direct customer work could.

I would pass today. The decisive issue is the failed founder-action gate, with neither overwhelming customer pull nor independently validated science to justify an exception. This is an interesting technical team with an incomplete investment proof. My one verifiable flip condition is a documented customer cycle in which the founders correct the out-of-range recommendations, ship the correction within weeks, and earn a paid repeat engagement after a prospective laboratory campaign demonstrates fewer experiments at comparable performance. That would connect resistance, action, and user dependence—the connection I am buying.
