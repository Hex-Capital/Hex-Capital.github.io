# Veeza AI -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Veeza AI charges EGP 1,500 for Schengen assistance and EGP 4,000 for UK or US applications. People reportedly pay. That establishes a service worth examining. It does not establish a security I want to buy. The proposed post-money, competing offers, and reason my capital is scarce are all undisclosed. I will not infer an auction from YC membership, but I cannot underwrite a cheap first check from financing silence. There is also no stated use of proceeds identifying one experiment, a deadline, and a result that materially changes the business’s value.

The interesting mechanism is translating between anxious travelers and unforgiving government systems. Arabic/English assistance, document checking, portal navigation, and human review could make that bridge useful. This fits my preference for businesses connecting systems that do not communicate well. But Veeza controls the assistance layer; it does not control appointment inventory or government decisions. Its own terms acknowledge portal changes and unavailable appointments. Better computer use can reduce clerical work without resolving either bottleneck.

The strongest bull case is that Moursy already has the beginnings of trusted local distribution. Veeza reports growth from 1,000 to 7,000-plus users, and its roughly 400-member WhatsApp community generated more than 10,000 feedback messages. Paying customers make this more substantial than launch attention. But the dossier does not connect community membership, founder content, or referrals to purchases or retained usage. The 253,000 launch views and vendor-reported revenue doubling supply neither that causal link nor a useful denominator. Owned trust becoming commerce would interest me considerably; these facts have not yet demonstrated it.

Moursy has shipped the product through closed and public betas, following product work at Bell and Efreshli and co-founding Dlvvr. His Egyptian-passport experience gives him direct knowledge of the customer’s frustration. That earns some founder credit. It does not establish operating access inside visa processing, a prior scaled outcome, or proprietary agency relationships. Meanwhile, Atlys and iVisa/HelloGov already combine assistance with overlapping automation. Veeza’s regional focus could support a good business, but geography and language alone do not establish an advantage competitors cannot purchase.

Delete the next financing and the decisive question becomes cash retained per completed application. Published fees and paying customers provide modest affirmative evidence. Human-review time, acquisition expense, refunds, inference costs, burn, and runway remain unknown. Human review sensibly addresses the red flag of assuming agent errors disappear; its cost still has to fit inside those fees. The documented exceptions to my usual gates require hard conversion and retention evidence, unusually strong external validation, or a financing structure that avoids repeated raises. Veeza has not supplied comparable evidence.

I pass on the present record. A useful regional visa service could emerge here, and forcing it into an expensive software narrative could damage the opportunity. My single condition for reopening is three consecutive months of positive operating cash flow from visa services, after acquisition, human review, refunds, technology costs, and overhead. That would demonstrate an independently functioning base business strong enough to reconsider the missing financing gates.
