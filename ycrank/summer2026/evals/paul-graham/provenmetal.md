# ProvenMetal -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The obstacle I want to see is not “American PCBs take too long.” It is the moment a supplier missed a deadline, a board failed inspection, or a component vanished—and what the founders shipped in response. ProvenMetal has built a real service around these problems, but the dossier never identifies such an incident. Thirty-plus customer conversations, a five-day offering, and one-hour quoting show activity, not yet the relentlessly resourceful behavior I use as my first gate. I cannot infer it from founder quality, so this criterion remains at its missing-evidence default.

The technical evidence is better. The KiCad 9+ plugin runs on three operating systems, checks live inventory and lead times, and returns sourcing results in about a minute ([ProvenMetal](https://provenmetal.com/plugins/kicad)). Doyle and Carkner also previously shipped a 17-day Syncra pilot that collected over 20 million datapoints and reportedly reduced phantom energy use by 95%. This looks like a pair of fierce nerds who build before asking permission. But the measured result belongs to the previous product, while ProvenMetal has not published order-cycle performance, inspection yield, plugin installations, or a completed-board result independently showing that its control layer improves the factory underneath it.

The narrow well is plausible but not proven. Five LOIs after more than 30 industry conversations are more informative than a waitlist, especially at pre-seed, but the customers are unnamed and the LOIs may concern the earlier X-ray-inspection concept rather than the August turnkey service. There is no payment or repeat order. Meanwhile, Network PCB already offers direct three-day turnkey service and performs ProvenMetal’s assembly. This creates the exact dangerous kind of partnership: the supplier controls the scarce operational capability, can sell directly to the same customers, and is faster on the advertised metric. The KiCad interface could become a valuable programmable layer, but without installations or retained usage it is still an artifact rather than pull.

The founders did encounter this schlep organically. Doyle deployed IoT hardware, Carkner built PCB-testing systems, and the pair worked together on Syncra before ProvenMetal. They know that component sourcing, inspection, documentation, and customer handholding are the product, not administrative residue. But their running-back evidence is weak. The route moved from IoT power management to benchtop X-ray inspection and then to partner-manufactured PCBA, without a disclosed customer or technical observation explaining either change. Flexible founders revise a route when evidence breaks it; unexplained revisions can also be search without learning.

The bull case is that this team’s prior measured pilot, existing plugin, shared execution history, and direct hardware exposure form the exception: young founders with numerical proof can outrun an initially thin customer record. The problem is that the 95% result validates Syncra, not ProvenMetal, and the current factory economics belong substantially to Network PCB. My 36-month base case is therefore that ProvenMetal has shifted toward procurement-and-inspection workflow software for American hardware teams, reaching $1–3 million ARR, rather than becoming a scaled PCB manufacturer. I am not writing the check today. One event would flip me: a named customer placing a second paid order after the founders personally recover a delayed or defective first build within the promised five-day window. That would simultaneously reveal the obstacle, the workaround, and genuine pull.
