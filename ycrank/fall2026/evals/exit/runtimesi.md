# Runtime.si -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a robot OEM or automation platform is the strongest supported route. Runtime’s planner searches candidate actions in simulation before executing them. If that cuts the time and labor needed to commission new manipulation tasks, a buyer could spread those savings across its installed base and gain a reason to own the planner. RoboSkillHub documents reusable manipulation components and records which were verified on physical hardware. Runtime separately claims its current policy matched or exceeded unnamed models on tasks including rope threading and egg grasping, with trial counts and success rates undisclosed. The buyer savings remain an inference. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

A paid production pilot that measures commissioning time, task success, and live planning latency is a plausible seed-stage milestone. Runtime says early access opens in January, and the founders have relevant manipulation and controls experience. The memo leaves the current policy’s pilot funding, runway, and integration cost unresolved. IP assignment and closing consents are also unverified, and the memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (*The Idea*, *Founders & Team*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% pro rata. The sale scenarios assume an eligible holder sells the full stake for cash at closing within eight years, with no preference or other senior equity claim reducing the holder’s share.

| Scenario | Assumed proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest acquisition | $50M enterprise value less $4M net debt and $1M transaction costs leaves $45M for equity | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong acquisition | $110M enterprise value less $8M net debt and $2M transaction costs leaves $100M for equity | $3.00M (6.0×) |
| Prolonged private | Operating cash is reinvested throughout the eight-year window, with no eligible share purchase or payout | $0 (0×) |

If only 80% of the strong-case payment arrives within eight years, realized proceeds fall to $2.4 million (4.8×). The sale values are illustrative assumptions. The memo provides no buyer pricing, relevant acquisition comparables, or measured deployment economics to support either value. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

An OEM could build a planner with open GPU physics tools or adopt an existing policy that adapts from demonstrations. Simify’s reported physical rearrangement result makes the build option credible, and Runtime’s acknowledged live-loop latency could erase commissioning savings in production. This weakens the case for paying an acquisition premium. The evidence leaves both the availability of a production-ready substitute and the attainable sale price unresolved, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a robot OEM demonstrate enough repeatable commissioning savings in a paid production pilot to justify buying Runtime at a price that clears the holder’s hurdle? Pilot logs across multiple task changes should quantify setup labor, task success, and live planning latency. An OEM’s estimate of fleet-wide savings and the cost of building internally would test whether those results could support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity. Evidence supporting that range would move Watch to Reach out. If the strongest credible acquisition case still leaves the holder materially short, move to Pass.
