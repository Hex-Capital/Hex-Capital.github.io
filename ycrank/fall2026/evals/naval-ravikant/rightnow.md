# RightNow -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

RightNow’s founders have acquired specific knowledge through building. Autokernel exposes a GPU-kernel optimization implementation and correctness harness; both founders coauthored model research, including RightNow-Arabic in May 2026. RunInfra launched in July. These are inspectable artifacts in the problem domain, substantially stronger evidence than employer affiliations. The approximately 1,600 Autokernel stars support developer interest in that work. They do not establish paying inference customers.

The investment turns on what the founders can keep after competitors learn from them. Autokernel is MIT-licensed. RunInfra serves open models, and Together AI already develops kernels and inference infrastructure. Session-aware routing can reduce redundant computation, but the dossier establishes no exclusive workload data, proprietary model, hardware advantage, or switching cost. If another provider copies the optimization and matches the routing, what continues compounding for RightNow? The public evidence leaves very little. Technical difficulty can create a head start; ownership of the resulting advantage determines its duration.

The strongest objection to passing is that these founders could discover the durable asset through deployment. They have shipped across models, kernels, and developer products, and repeated coding sessions supply a concrete optimization target. My exception for backing an unusual working prototype before a distribution loop exists deserves consideration here. But RightNow has not demonstrated the economic advantage that would make that exception persuasive: its historical benchmarks lack runtime details, its comparison harness and raw data are unavailable, and advertised cache rates describe the highest qualifying account. Disclosing those limitations is useful evidence of candor. It leaves performance unresolved; it supplies no integrity veto.

RunInfra also fits my pattern of tools that let individuals produce more without institutional permission. Self-service APIs and coding-agent integrations provide that access. Yet permissionless access alone creates no user-to-user compounding. One developer’s cached conversation has no demonstrated benefit for another developer. The October 20 launch’s 115 Product Hunt points show attention, while OpenFang’s approximately 18,200 stars belong to a separate project. Neither establishes referrals, reusable customer contributions, or improving network utility.

There is real spending around RightNow’s wedge: Fireworks reports more than $1 billion in annualized revenue, and Together’s revenue is estimated near that level. Compatible model APIs offer room beyond coding, but the dossier does not identify a specific adjacent buyer and an established route into that buyer’s budget. The $132.7 billion inference estimate includes substantial spending outside RunInfra’s reach. Meanwhile, $5 prepaid credits and $10/$29/$99 subscriptions establish a direct charging mechanism, with no disclosed completed purchases or contribution margins. That is enough to understand the proposed business, insufficient to establish value capture.

I pass with my own money. RightNow has credible builders, but its strongest demonstrated asset is knowledge competitors can absorb. **My flip condition is independently reproducible production evidence that an exclusive asset accumulated through customer deployments produces a sustained cost advantage at matched model quality and latency.** That would connect the founders’ specific knowledge to something the company can own.
