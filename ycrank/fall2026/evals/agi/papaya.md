# Papaya -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is Papaya’s strongest path. Papaya turns production traces into ranked fixes and can open pull requests for approved changes. The seed-stage hypothesis is that small engineering teams could fix regressions routinely that they currently leave unresolved because manual trace review takes too long. The company reports $25K+ in annualized savings per large workflow, but the memo gives no like-for-like measure of engineer time per successful fix after review, retries, and regression checks. The teams newly able to afford routine remediation remain hypothetical. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The founders report first paying customers. Sunny Health reports fewer inference errors, and Hamlet reports faster diagnosis with the trace viewer. Those are early signs of utility. The memo leaves repeat paid remediation and buyer preference under model parity unproven. The team has relevant agent evaluation, data infrastructure, and enterprise sales experience, and it shipped SDK updates after turning its internal optimization harness into Papaya. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

At the listed $500 monthly Business price, a customer using the full 50,000-trace allowance would yield 1¢ in subscription revenue per trace before costs. That scenario gives a starting point for testing contribution as usage rises. Actual trace utilization, inference costs, and onboarding and support effort are unknown. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Papaya may make existing observability work faster without opening a new market. Braintrust already automates recurring trace diagnosis, and Papaya’s run-linked recommendations and pull requests could be matched by an incumbent with equally capable models and access to the same traces. Customer-side filtering, partial exports, and review of proposed changes could also erode the labor savings. The overlap is concrete, but equivalent remediation and easy substitution remain untested. Neither Path B condition has cleared its gate, and the memo identifies no scarce complement Papaya controls that would support Path A. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will small teams that previously deferred routine remediation repeatedly pay for Papaya when their existing observability stack has access to an equally capable model? A paid, multi-cycle trial should compare engineer time per successfully deployed fix at equivalent quality, including filtering, review, retries, and regression checks, then measure which workflow buyers keep at viable pricing. Repeat paid use with material labor savings and a demonstrated preference for Papaya would support Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 with at least medium confidence. A reliable finding that either condition fails would move the action to Pass.
