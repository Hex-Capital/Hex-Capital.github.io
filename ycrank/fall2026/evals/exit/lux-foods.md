# Lux Foods -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Lux’s strongest exit route is a strategic sale to an established produce operator. The memo records Blue Road Capital’s acquisition of NatureSweet and investor backing for the Village Fresh / Verdexa carve-out. These transactions show willingness to buy or fund established tomato businesses. A produce operator could have a reason to acquire Lux if its growing and handling system delivers repeatable margins on specialty tomatoes through the operator’s existing distribution network. Lux reports 90% lower light intensity and says its first San Francisco farm is under development. Commercial yield and all-in production costs remain unverified. The reported $500,000 convertible note is an early funding signal, with the pilot budget and capital needed for scale still open. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale scenarios below assume an eligible holder sells its entire diluted stake for cash at closing within eight years, with a simple pro rata waterfall after assumed debt and deal adjustments.

| Scenario | Assumed enterprise-to-equity bridge | Holder cash |
|----------|--------------------------------------|-------------|
| Modest sale | $50 million enterprise value less $10 million in debt and deal adjustments leaves $40 million | $1.2 million, or 2.4× |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value less $10 million in debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million | $2.7 million, or 5.4×. At 2.5% ownership, this falls to $2.25 million, or 4.5× |
| Prolonged private | Lux continues operating and reinvests available cash throughout the eight-year horizon | $0 realized |

The sale prices are sensitivity assumptions. The adjacent deals provide no usable valuation benchmark, and the memo gives no terms to determine the note’s treatment, the holder’s waterfall, or eligibility. The memo is also silent on an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Established greenhouse suppliers already offer year-round specialty tomatoes, and Oishii already sells tomatoes from automated indoor farms. A strategic buyer could meet its supply needs through those alternatives. Lux says its flagship yield and vessel-to-module design still need validation, and its lower-light claim leaves the cost of sugar feedstock and total facility economics unresolved. The compact-plant patent is assigned to the University of California Regents, and Lux’s unverified license terms create a material closing risk if the buyer needs those rights. The memo supports neither a confirmed transfer block nor a credible sale-price ceiling below the holder hurdle. Watch fits the unresolved economics and rights. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would repeatable results from a paid commercial-module pilot give a strategic buyer an economic reason to acquire Lux at a price that clears the holder’s $2.5 million cash hurdle? The milestone is repeated harvests with measured saleable yield, paid prices, and all-in costs, followed by buyer feedback that ties those results to a plausible acquisition range. Move to Reach out if that range can clear the hurdle after deal adjustments and diligence shows a credible path to transferring the needed rights. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
