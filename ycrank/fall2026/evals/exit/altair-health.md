# Altair Health -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to a practice billing platform is Altair’s strongest supported route. Tebra already bundles billing with its EHR for independent practices and has announced investment in AI billing. Altair says its product checks proposed claim actions against formalized payer policies on a practice’s existing EHR and clearinghouse, with in-house billers handling exceptions. Altair also reports tracking policies across 3,400+ payers. A platform could have a reason to buy if the payer rules and resolution history improve collections enough to justify the purchase and integration costs. The memo records an earlier co-pilot launch and a founder-reported clinic visit. The founder attributed millions of dollars in avoided receivables delays to claim prechecks. That claim leaves incremental collections, labor savings, and repeatability across practices unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. Both illustrative sale cases assume an eligible holder sells the full stake for cash at closing within eight years, receives a pro rata distribution, and faces no preference claims, escrow, or earnout.

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40M less $5M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments = $35M | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M less $10M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments = $90M | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Available cash is reinvested and no shares are sold within eight years | $0 (0×) |

The sale values are scenario assumptions. At 2.5% ownership, the strong case falls to $2.25 million (4.5×). The memo’s directory pricing has unclear billing units, leaving realized revenue and margins unverified. It supplies no buyer valuation or transaction pricing to support either sale value. It also supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route that could pay this holder. (The Idea, Key Facts)

A production rollout across several independent practices could measure incremental collections and biller hours per claim for the current product. The earlier co-pilot activity makes that test plausible. The memo leaves its funding, timeline, and staffing needs open. Existing EHR and clearinghouse integrations make a product acquisition plausible in principle. Altair reports using business associate agreements and access controls for patient data, while the rights to transfer customer contracts, integrations, and relevant data remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Tebra could build similar checks into its existing practice platform, and Candid already markets configurable rules and billing agents. Altair’s human exception workload could also erode the savings a buyer would pay for. The earlier co-pilot claim concerns receivables timing and leaves the current product’s economics unresolved. These risks weaken the case for a purchase price that clears the proceeds hurdle. The memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a credible price ceiling below the hurdle, so the Pass gate stays open and the action is Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a practice billing platform underwrite a price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity based on the current product’s results? A buyer-side valuation supported by repeatable net collections gains across several practices, after biller costs and integration costs, would move the action to Reach out if it clears the hurdle under plausible deal terms. A supported ceiling materially below the hurdle for the strongest route would trigger Pass unless an independent route could meet the objective.
