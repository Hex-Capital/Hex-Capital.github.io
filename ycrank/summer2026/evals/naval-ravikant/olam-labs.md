# Olam Labs -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The founders have earned the right to work on this problem. Om Buddhdev built agent infrastructure and product systems as a Staff Engineer at Latitude/AI Dungeon; Shreshth Sharma worked in reinforcement learning and production data science at RBC. More importantly, they have already converted that knowledge into an operating system producing more than 1,000 matches and nearly one billion tokens of activity per day. Specific knowledge is visible in the artifact, not the biography.

The durable asset is not the multiplayer code. A funded evaluation platform could reproduce the environments. The potential asset is the joined corpus: deterministic game states, identical human and agent action spaces, transcripts, reasoning traces, and 86,729 graded table-talk turns. Every public match adds behavioral data while users supply both participation and comparison. This resembles a permissionless contribution system, but the compounding claim remains incomplete: Olam has not shown that another 100,000 turns make its predictions more accurate or its benchmark harder for customers to leave.

The bear case is serious. Current rankings are preliminary and LLM-graded, transfer from social poker to deployed agents is unproven, and Patronus AI has $70 million behind overlapping simulation infrastructure. Nearly one billion daily tokens also make a free arena a potentially expensive data-acquisition machine. If game performance does not predict production behavior, Olam is collecting a large proprietary dataset about the wrong world. Code can be copied; irrelevant data does not become a moat through volume.

The wedge can expand from social-strategy evaluation into private pre-deployment testing, custom environments, datasets, and training simulations. Those products address a reported $2.25 billion evaluation-services market growing 29.7% annually. But this is still a top-down market story: no frontier lab is identified as a payer, no private evaluation has been priced, and the public product generates no disclosed revenue. Thousands of users prove curiosity and repeat activity; they do not yet prove direct value capture.

I find no evidence of the behavioral red flags that would veto the option—no artificial deadline, unfair founder dealing, or direction changes manufactured for approval—but absence is not integrity evidence. The strongest revealed-preference signal is the permissionless arena producing useful work before institutional approval. The weakest is monetization. Nor does a known-exception argument excuse that weakness yet: Olam has activity, but it has not demonstrated a self-reinforcing network whose utility clearly rises for each participant.

I would invest a small personal check. The asymmetry comes from founders with exact-domain execution, an already-growing behavioral corpus, and a plausible transition from evaluation into training infrastructure. My 36-month base case is that Olam becomes a private simulation-and-evaluation vendor serving frontier labs at $2 million–$6 million ARR, rather than remaining a consumer game arena. My single flip condition is a paid renewal from one frontier-model lab within twelve months; without that, the corpus is evidence of engagement rather than evidence of value.
