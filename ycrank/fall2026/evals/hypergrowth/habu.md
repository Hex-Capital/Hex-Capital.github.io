# HABU -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Even assuming strong demand from institutional investors and family offices, HABU’s current offering is to develop apartment projects. HABU reports a working parcel-screening and first-pass underwriting module. In Phoenix, the team manually collects local approval knowledge and favors parcels where the intended use is already permitted. The screening workflow could transfer, but its current dependence on local zoning and approval practice implies substantial market-specific work. Investor introductions and an inquiry form provide reusable intake. Each project still requires hands-on diligence, negotiation, and financing. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The memo lists a two-person team and records a September launch followed by a first Phoenix site under contract in October. It also records $1.126 million in corporate securities sales. The site contract shows early execution, and the securities sales show fundraising. The memo leaves commercial revenue unknown. Development fees and contingent profit shares are tied to individual projects. A returning investor could finance another deal, but that would require a new commitment, and the memo documents no repeat commitments. (HABU, Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Facts)

For the next project, incremental contribution starts with the development fee and any profit share actually earned, less the staff time and tools needed to source, underwrite, close, and manage the deal. Sponsored projects also require project-specific debt and equity and can tie up HABU’s own capital. Screening automation could reduce labor before a site goes under contract. HABU’s targets of five times as many projects per manager and up to 20% faster delivery remain unverified by a completed-project comparison. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The strongest alternative is to reuse the underwriting module on fee-only engagements or work with local co-GP partners. Fee-only terms could reduce HABU’s capital commitment, and local partners could supply approval knowledge. The memo establishes that HABU offers these structures, while the current Phoenix workflow still depends on manually gathered local knowledge. Scaling the current development offering broadly would require rebuilding that capability market by market, with no demonstrated near-term way to contain the work. That triggers the structural downside gate. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
