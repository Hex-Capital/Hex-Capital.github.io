# Sutrix -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Sutrix’s supported AGI path is making study datasets available often enough to inform follow-up decisions. The old workflow can take weeks or months as research staff review charts and reconcile records in spreadsheets. Sutrix uses AI to link records, apply study rules, and route unresolved cases to reviewers with source evidence. The Rugby Research and Injury Prevention Group reported getting from questionnaire records to findings in days instead of weeks. BlueRock reported less manual reconciliation and said experts could inspect the preparation steps. Sutrix separately claims an 80% cut in preparation time. The customer accounts support a shorter workflow, while the size and cost of the savings remain uncertain. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The seed-stage scenario is a research team that needs a refreshed dataset before its next follow-up decision and cannot meet that deadline with manual preparation. The reported turnaround makes that use plausible. Its viability depends on whether the team can get an investigator-approved refresh on time and within budget after accounting for data access, exception review, and corrections. Under model parity, Sutrix could still deliver a distinct outcome through record linkage, versioned study rules, field-level provenance, and investigator review. Those features could also make repeat work easier to keep with Sutrix, though renewals and switching costs remain unproven. The building-blocks path scores zero because the memo describes a research workflow and supplies no evidence that Sutrix relieves a scarce input to AI deployment. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Sutrix reports paying teams at more than 10 institutions and over $150K in revenue in the months before its software release. That supports demand for the delivered dataset work. Repeat paid software use remains undisclosed. The release and the founders’ prior clinical research and hospital software delivery support execution credit. Production EHR access and reliable exception handling remain open, and the memo gives too little cost data to estimate contribution on the next accepted dataset. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The strongest countercase is that Sutrix speeds up studies that customers could already run, while bespoke integration and expert review absorb enough of the savings to keep a higher refresh cadence uneconomic. The revenue predates the software release, direct FHIR connectivity is described for sandboxes, and competing tools already automate parts of chart abstraction. The customer reports establish practical workflow gains but leave the new-use threshold open. That gap triggers Watch. If the gains prove limited to routine acceleration of existing studies, both AGI paths would score zero and the action would become Pass. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Has a paying research team used Sutrix to meet a recurring follow-up deadline that its manual workflow could not meet within budget? A customer-confirmed comparison showing repeat, investigator-approved refreshes on time and within budget, including integration, review, and correction costs, would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that the same cadence was already affordable and achievable would move it to Pass.
