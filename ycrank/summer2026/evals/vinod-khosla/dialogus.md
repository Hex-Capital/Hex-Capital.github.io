# Dialogus -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The demand pool is real, but the consequence is not yet extraordinary. Contact centers consume substantial labor and impose wait times, yet Dialogus quantifies only a $2.54 billion voice-agent market—not the labor spending, population served, or cost collapse its technology could produce. Thousands of calls for Fortune 500 enterprises establish relevance. They do not establish a 100x societal consequence. This currently looks like automating an expensive workflow, not rewriting the economics of expert service.

The strongest evidence is operational. Dialogus recognizes callers, acts inside enterprise systems, constrains tools by policy, records audit trails, and replays production failures as regression tests. SOC 2 compliance and deployments associated with Papa Johns, KFC, and Totalplay are meaningful for a three-person pre-seed team. But there is no resolution rate, action-error rate, latency benchmark, or cost per completed call. Production proves that the system works sometimes; it does not prove a 5–10x discontinuity against Salesforce, a human agent, or PolyAI.

The bear case is commoditization disguised by implementation difficulty. OpenAI supplies realtime speech, SIP connectivity, and tool use. Salesforce bundles telephony, CRM data, AI agents, and human handoffs. Sierra already reports more than $150 million ARR, while PolyAI reports over 2,000 deployments. Dialogus may execute unusually well in Mexican Spanish and constrained workflows, but customer-specific integrations and failure traces become a moat only if they improve outcomes faster than competitors can reproduce them. Several thousand calls are too little evidence for that learning advantage.

The founders have enough technical aptitude to earn attention. Hans Ibarra left Georgia Tech and Google and Amazon internships; Rodrigo Terán Hernández trained across Meta, Microsoft, and Google. Leaving protected paths is a positive signal. Yet the dossier shows no material belief revised after customer evidence, no complementary recruit won to own enterprise reliability or contact-center operations, and little public evidence about Juberth Rodriguez. This is a capable software team; it is not yet a demonstrated risk-tuned talent architecture.

The strongest exception is the possibility that a technically incremental wedge becomes a broad financial or operational platform, as happened with **Block**. Dialogus could evolve from answering calls into the policy-controlled transaction layer for orders, collections, and customer records. The material difference is that the dossier neither quantifies that value pool nor documents the founders changing the plan toward it, while several heavily financed competitors already occupy the platform position. The exception is conceivable, but it is not evidenced.

My 36-month base case is a $3–8 million ARR enterprise integration business by August 2029, differentiated regionally and operationally but not foundational infrastructure. That is a respectable company and an unattractive option-value bet for my money. I pass. One result would flip me: an independently verifiable enterprise cohort showing at least a 5x reduction in cost per successfully resolved call versus the incumbent stack, with no increase in wrongful actions across one million production calls and a renewed annual contract.
