# Nines -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Nines’s candidate secret is that fiber commissioning can become a sufficiently standardized robotic task to release valuable compute faster and more cheaply than technicians can. What would prove that wrong? Repeated deployments in which site adaptation, human intervention, or cabling errors consume the savings. This is a testable proposition, but the dossier supplies neither a named prevailing belief it overturns nor a physical experiment establishing it. Microsoft’s 2024 manipulation research shows that sophisticated operators already see the opportunity. Nines still has to establish what it knows that these operators have missed.

The commissioning wedge deserves attention. It gives the founders a bounded task to solve before attempting drive swaps, optics reseating, and server restarts. But who owns the resulting advantage? Exclaim already targets cabling and optics; Boost and Proprio approach adjacent workflows; Microsoft develops overlapping capabilities internally. These are concrete competing routes to automation. Task selection alone cannot protect Nines’s economics, and the proposed deployment-data moat remains an inference. Successful automation could create substantial value while operators and hardware suppliers retain most of it.

The strongest argument for investing is that the founders are approaching an expensive physical constraint inside the AI production stack. Li’s reported large-scale inference responsibility at Meta and autonomous-underwater-vehicle experience give that argument substance. Eom brings software delivery experience from Patreon. This fits my preference for extending technology into physical infrastructure, and the commissioning focus could reduce the coordination burden of starting with maintenance in operating racks. Yet the public website sequence is explicitly simulated inspection. There is no independently demonstrated technical event, persistent customer usage, or validated deployment path sufficient to activate an exception to the competitive concern. The simulation is honestly disclosed; it establishes no physical reliability.

Founding structure remains open. Both founders studied computer science at Cornell, but shared education does not establish a history of working together. Full-time status, ownership, and responsibility for essential engineering are undocumented. I assign the missing-evidence default without interpreting an indexed Meta affiliation as proof of part-time commitment. Commercially, Nines names a buyer and solicits operator introductions through YC, which is a concrete starting route. The approximately 252,000 launch views measure attention. They establish neither access to a commissioning project nor an economically repeatable sale. Pricing, service margins, and customer expansion are also absent, leaving the $16.2 billion broader-market estimate disconnected from a revenue engine I can underwrite.

I pass on writing a personal check today. Nines has identified a valuable task, but the evidence presently supports an automation opportunity more strongly than an ownable company. My flip condition is one independently verified, paid commissioning deployment showing that Nines’s proprietary control system completes fiber patching across representative racks at materially lower total cost than technicians, including adaptation, interventions, and error remediation. That result would begin to establish both the technical insight and the mechanism for retaining its value.
