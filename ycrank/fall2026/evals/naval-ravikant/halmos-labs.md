# Halmos Labs -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Halmos Labs has encountered something more valuable than a research credential: an experimentalist who confirmed its finding and changed a fermentation plan. That is contact with reality. Akke’s biochemical research at MIT, AstraZeneca, and Cambridge gives this work relevant context; Löfgren brings quantitative modeling and security experience. But the dossier does not identify a dated research result personally attributable to either founder that establishes exceptional mastery of experiment design. I see credible specific knowledge, with some evidence of application. I do not yet see an exceptional founder advantage.

The decisive question is what Halmos owns after a competitor reproduces its orchestration and deductive checks. The reported 30% reduction in evaluations came from repeated simulations. The five-patient TESLA results are retrospective. These are useful demonstrations of capability, but neither establishes proprietary models, exclusive experimental data, or an asset that compounds with deployment. Atinary already occupies experiment optimization and announced a Bruker collaboration in August 2026. Halmos must explain why better orchestration becomes enduring ownership before an incumbent adds comparable research agents.

The strongest argument against passing is that Halmos could discover important variables precisely because it examines assumptions across disciplines. The fermentation finding makes that possibility concrete. I can accept missing revenue when an unusual prototype reveals something others overlook; my prototype-led exceptions leave room for this team. But those exceptions require exceptional product evidence or founder execution. Here, the system also repeatedly proposed experiments outside the collaboration’s permitted range. Logical consistency does not establish experimental usefulness. The laboratory remains the judge.

There is room to maneuver: experiment selection can extend from biotech R&D into assay development, bioprocessing, and manufacturing. Those are identifiable adjacent buyers using a shared capability. The cited $4.6 billion AI-in-biotechnology market, however, does not establish how much of their budgets this product can capture. Nor does the current project model demonstrate my preferred pattern of customers creating value for one another. Halmos solicits scoped engagements; no contribution or distribution loop is documented. Paid-offering terms establish that charging is contemplated, while realized revenue and pricing remain unknown.

I would pass with my own money today. There is no documented integrity problem, artificial financing deadline, or other founder-behavior veto; this is a question of what the business accumulates. The team has shown promising research assistance without establishing that each engagement makes the next one structurally better. My flip condition is one prospective, multi-customer laboratory study demonstrating that accumulated experimental feedback, retained under exclusive usage rights, improves results for subsequent customers against the same system without that feedback. That would turn today’s inferred learning loop into a measured asset.
