# Oasive -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Oasive could make municipal credit expertise cheaper to deliver. Cities, school districts, hospitals, and airports leave analysts searching scattered financial statements, board minutes, and news. Automating that work has value. But the dossier does not quantify analyst spending, hours displaced, or how cheaper research changes access to financing. I cannot turn a useful research page into a 100x societal consequence by assuming the missing arithmetic. I pass at this point.

The technical question is whether Oasive can eliminate substantial research labor while preserving credit judgment. Source-linked numerical claims are a sensible product choice, but citations alone do not establish completeness, freshness, or correct interpretation. MuniPro already generates source-based credit memos; DPC DATA supplies standardized financials and issuer news, including AI-ready datasets released in March 2026. Oasive’s board-meeting coverage could uncover information those workflows miss. There is no comparative result showing that it does. Advertised proprietary MBS models are evidence about another product, not proof of a municipal research discontinuity. Nor does the dossier establish that Oasive merely wraps a standard model; that red flag remains unproven.

The strongest objection to my pass is Anaïs Howland herself. Wells Fargo MBS analysis, Google Treasury portfolio management, and Google AI strategy put relevant expertise inside one founder. Company-reported use across 15 institutional desks at the September 2026 MBS/rates launch suggests she can get sophisticated buyers to try what she builds. My actual investing pattern includes execution-heavy financial businesses; scientific novelty is not an absolute requirement. But those desks are not disclosed municipal customers, their assets under management are not Oasive revenue, and the founder’s credentials do not document belief revision or complementary recruiting. The exception cases require an unusually credible instigator tied to a demonstrated giant value pool, or exceptional evidence of transformative capability. This dossier establishes domain credibility without establishing either override.

Accuracy is the first risk I would spend money to resolve. Oasive’s October 26 terms warn that outputs may be inaccurate, incomplete, or outdated and arrive without prior review. That language is not evidence of defective performance. It identifies the economic question: how much checking must the analyst still do? If verification consumes the saved research time, the labor multiplier disappears. The dossier supplies no decisive experiment, numerical threshold, budget, or response to failure. Fifteen desks provide some evidence of interest; they do not answer this kill risk.

The $200/user/month Macro & Rates price and Agency MBS paid-pilot offering establish commercial packaging, not municipal cost parity. Municipal pricing, data costs, inference costs, and residual analyst labor remain undisclosed. Licensed-data continuity and export restrictions also constrain how independently Oasive can deliver its service. I would reopen the decision after **one independently audited, paid municipal deployment across 100 issuers demonstrates at least 10x lower fully loaded research cost, including human verification, without increasing material credit omissions versus the existing workflow**. That would establish a consequential expertise multiplier. Today I have evidence of a credible specialist founder and an institutional research product, with the central multiplier still unmeasured.
