# Quippy -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Quippy’s strongest path. A consumer learning platform could buy Quippy to add daily social rehearsal and retain paying users. Quippy says it uses saved practice history and optional connected-app context to tailor later sessions. One reviewer reported forming a new friend group after practicing with the app. The ownership case is an inference: if accumulated history measurably improves paid retention, buying the product and its user relationships could be worth more than building a similar roleplay feature. The memo gives no measured retention lift or evidence of buyer interest. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A seed-stage milestone would be a paid-cohort comparison that isolates the retention benefit of personalized practice over generic roleplay. Quippy has shipped on iOS and Android, has 92 U.S. iOS ratings, and has an estimated 10,000-plus lifetime iOS installs. That gives it a live product on which to run the test. The memo reports a $500,000 seed entry but gives no paid cohort size, burn, or runway to judge how quickly Quippy could reach a reliable result. (Market & Traction)

The memo supplies no terms for the check being evaluated, so the proceeds bridge uses the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3%, with no follow-ons. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, or 5×. Under a simple pro rata waterfall, that requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. Both sale scenarios assume zero net debt and no other price adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. They assume no senior claims and full cash payment at close. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each $0. Actual investor rights and the waterfall are unverified.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Enterprise value and equity cash | Holder cash on $500,000 total invested |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $30 million | $900,000 (1.8×) |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100 million | $3 million (6.0×) |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No sale or payout | $0 (0×), with shares retained |

The sale prices are scenario assumptions. The strong sale clears the hurdle at 3% ownership and yields 4× at 2%. In the prolonged-private case, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and Quippy reinvests its cash. Listed subscription prices provide no evidence of distributable cash or a funded payout, and the memo supports no independent liquidity route for this holder. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

A buyer could build voice rehearsal using commercial speech tools, as the competing simulations suggest. If generic roleplay delivers comparable retention, Quippy’s practice history gives a buyer little reason to pay an acquisition premium. Even if personalization proves valuable, the ability to transfer the relevant data is unverified. Quippy says users can disconnect integrations and delete account data. These concerns support Watch. The memo establishes neither an adequate substitute for effective longitudinal personalization nor a binding transfer restriction, and it gives no basis to conclude that every credible strong outcome falls short. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Does Quippy’s accumulated practice history improve paid retention enough to make ownership more valuable to a consumer learning platform than building generic voice roleplay? A controlled paid-cohort comparison would move the action to Reach out if the measured lift supports buyer economics at roughly $83 million in distributable equity value under the 3% ownership scenario.
