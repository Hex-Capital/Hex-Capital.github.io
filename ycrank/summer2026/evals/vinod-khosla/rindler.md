# Rindler -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is missing. Rindler names real manual work across finance, recruiting, compliance, and government portals, but it does not quantify the labor pool, affected population, or cost that disappears if every website becomes callable by an agent. “The translation layer between AI agents and the web” could describe important infrastructure or a maintenance-intensive middleware business. Without a baseline, I cannot price the upside as a 100x option.

The strongest evidence is technical. Across 38 runs on two grocery tasks, Rindler reports 4x faster completion, 3x lower execution cost, and 2x fewer failures than Browser Use. Verified screens, typed records, and centrally repaired routes are more substantive than another prompt wrapper. But 38 homogeneous runs do not establish a 5–10x discontinuity on authenticated, bot-defended portals—the exact environment where reliability matters. Kernel, Browserbase, Browser Use, and Deck are well-funded, and two of them already provide infrastructure beneath or adjacent to Rindler. They can move up the stack unless accumulated mappings become demonstrably difficult to reproduce.

The bull case stings. One maintained mapping can serve every tenant of Greenhouse, Lever, or Ashby, converting site-specific repair labor into a reusable production asset. Starter at $100 per month and Teams at $1,000 provide a direct route from a working MCP endpoint to paid deployment. This is the kind of small-team, execution-heavy platform wedge that can expand far beyond its first feature. It also escapes the worst AI red flag: the product adds verification, schemas, and repair infrastructure rather than merely placing a new interface over a standard model. The exception still does not clear the gate because there is no disclosed revenue, repeat customer usage, mapping count, or giant quantified value pool.

Capital should now attack one kill risk: whether Rindler can remain reliable and economical across diverse hostile websites without accumulating linear human maintenance. The dossier provides no experiment threshold, repair-hours metric, cost, timing, or stop condition. SOC 2, HIPAA, and PCI are absent; customers remain exposed to website terms; authenticated sessions traverse Kernel, Browserbase, BrightData, or Capsolver. Spending first on sales or mapping breadth would conceal the risk rather than remove it.

Arthur De Los Santos and Michael Serrano have relevant technical preparation through MIT CSAIL, physics, computer science, and Roblox machine learning. That supports the ability to build the current product. It does not yet show rapid belief revision, a difficult complementary recruit, or someone explicitly owning security procurement, browser-policy risk, and production operations. Their biographies receive the stage-appropriate prior, not founder-learning credit that the dossier has not earned.

My 36-month forecast is that Rindler narrows from a horizontal web translation layer into recruiting-operations automation around Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby, remaining below $3 million ARR. I pass with my own money today. One condition would flip the decision: $1 million ARR from at least five unrelated enterprises retained for six months while completing production workflows across 20 authenticated portals with fewer than one human repair-hour per 10,000 runs. That single operating result would demonstrate demand, reliability, reusable learning, and non-linear economics.
