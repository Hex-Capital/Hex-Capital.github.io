# Amulet -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Amulet’s contested truth is precise: autonomous agents need a Git-like, durable filesystem detached from ephemeral compute, and isolated forks with conflict-checked publishing are superior to putting every worker on one shared volume. What would prove this wrong? If production teams can achieve the same reliability with sandbox snapshots, object-store mounts, or ordinary distributed filesystems, the company is a feature rather than a new infrastructure layer. The thesis is falsifiable, but Amulet has not shown that named peers reject it or that customers have validated it. Its sub-100-ms mounts, 1.9-ms p99 reads, and 8.4-GB/s throughput are self-published results, not yet independent proof of a secret.

The beachhead is appropriately narrow: automated-research labs, cloud-agent providers, and data-intensive AI teams running parallel disposable workers. Content-addressed commits, copy-on-write forks, workspace-scoped credentials, and compare-and-swap publishing form a coherent technical system rather than an arbitrary feature list. But technical complexity is not yet a monopoly mechanism. Archil has raised $18 million, E2B $32 million, and Daytona at least $29 million; each controls an adjacent layer from which it could integrate storage. With no public retention, exclusive data, dominant integration, or independently measured advantage, Amulet has demonstrated differentiation but not value capture.

The founders are the best evidence in the dossier. Nithik Bala worked in Meta web infrastructure and interned at Rockset; Varun Puru worked at Meta and has backend and cloud-infrastructure experience from Amazon. Both now identify as Amulet founders, and the core system appears to be built in-house. Yet “both worked at Meta” does not establish that they shipped together, while ownership, salaries, and prior working history remain unknown. More troublingly, the July 2026 LinkedIn description still presents a prior post-sales product. A fast pivot can reveal reality; it can also reveal that the company was assembled before its important truth was found.

The strongest bull case is that the team has identified an infrastructure boundary just as agent execution is becoming abundant: E2B reported hundreds of millions of sandbox sessions, while Amulet separates persistent state from whichever sandbox happens to run the agent. The $500 monthly Growth plan and $0.20 per GiB-month usage charge could combine software subscription economics with storage expansion. If workspace history becomes operationally indispensable, switching costs could compound. This is the unstated-pattern case for owning part of the AI production stack, but it remains an inference: private-beta access, 84 LinkedIn followers, and no disclosed production users do not show that anyone will buy the filesystem separately from compute.

Distribution is therefore the decisive weakness. “Request access” and “book a demo” name interfaces, not a route to market. The dossier supplies no sales owner, conversion rate, sales cycle, deployment burden, or evidence that the targeted labs will pay Amulet rather than demand the capability from E2B, Daytona, or a cloud provider. Published pricing establishes an engine but not its arithmetic: there are no margins, customer counts, expansion results, or credible path from $500 accounts to a fund-returning outcome. My 36-month base case is that Amulet remains below $3 million ARR and pivots toward embedded storage sold through agent-compute platforms rather than becoming an independent filesystem standard.

No known-exception override applies today. A verified zero-to-one technical event could overcome crowded market structure, but self-published benchmarks and private-beta availability are not such an event. I would investigate, not invest. One condition flips the decision: three independent production customers must each run Amulet across at least two compute providers for six months, pay at least $5,000 monthly, and document that conflict-safe workspace history—not generic storage performance—prevented them from replacing it with their sandbox vendor. Until then, this is capable infrastructure in search of proof: possible secret, unproved monopoly, unresolved distribution.
