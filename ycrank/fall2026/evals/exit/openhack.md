# OpenHack -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest path. A code-security platform could buy OpenHack’s application-mapping and verification engine to add business-impact triage across code and infrastructure. Ownership could make sense if deep integration lets the buyer cut customer triage time or find material issues its existing tools miss, and if that benefit exceeds what it could get by building, licensing, or partnering. OpenHack reports that its custom scanner led six configurations on F1 in its own benchmark. That supports a potential technical advantage, while the buyer benefit and the case for ownership remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The operating milestone is **multiple paid enterprise renewals with customer-verified gains over incumbent tools**. The memo records 444 stars for the public scanner and reports $500,000 in accelerator financing. OpenHack names six launch users and says Papermark fixed a cloud-storage exposure it reported. Those signals make a production test plausible. Paying customer count, retention, burn, and the capital needed to reach the milestone remain unknown. The public CLI and OpenHack’s stated temporary repository access and post-scan deletion policies also leave core IP ownership and customer-contract transfer rights unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% relative to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× target is $2.5 million gross cash within eight years. It requires $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. At 3% with illustrative net debt of $5 million, the hurdle is $88.3 million in enterprise value before any fees or senior claims. The memo supplies neither actual shareholder terms nor evidence of an achievable sale price. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Gross cash to the holder |
|---|---|
| Modest sale at $25 million enterprise value in year eight | $600,000, or 1.2×, after illustrative net debt |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value in year eight | $2.85 million, or 5.7×, after illustrative net debt. At 2% ownership, the same sale pays $1.9 million, or 3.8×. |
| Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and OpenHack reinvests available cash for eight years | $0 realized, with shares still held. Wind-down proceeds would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo does not quantify. |

A sponsor buyout lacks evidence of cash generation and financing capacity. A secondary lacks evidence of eligible share demand, price, and sale fraction. An IPO lacks evidence of operating scale and resale capacity. Dividends or buybacks lack evidence of distributable cash and a funded policy. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A platform may already have enough of the workflow to keep building in-house. GitHub offers native remediation, and Strix already combines scheduled testing, remediation pull requests, and retesting. OpenHack’s own white-box evaluation found 277 of 761 planted vulnerabilities, leaving substantial room for a buyer to question production coverage. Together, those facts make building or partnering credible alternatives to acquisition. They establish neither a structural closing barrier nor a supported sale price that leaves the holder materially short. The Pass gate is therefore untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a code-security platform’s buy-versus-build analysis support a price above the illustrative $88.3 million enterprise-value hurdle? A buyer-supported valuation range grounded in customer-verified gains across renewed paid deployments would move the action to Reach out if the range could clear the holder’s 5× target within eight years. Until then, Watch.
