# RATLOG -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with satellite operators, RATLOG’s best growth path is to sell payload capacity by the kilogram on shared flights. Operators in different markets could use the same launcher for the same job from a single site if payload integration and access can be standardized. The memo identifies the buyer and use case. Site selection, cross-border access, and integration requirements remain unresolved. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

The documented sales route directs buyers to the CEO by call or email. Shared flights give RATLOG a common offer, while payment and payload onboarding remain unverified. Constellation operators could return for successive deployments and spend more as their booked payload mass grows. That is a plausible expansion mechanism under the planned per-kilogram pricing. Repeat purchases, recurring commitments, and same-customer spending are unverified. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

RATLOG reports $400 million in signed reservations, with customer identities, deposits, and cancellation terms undisclosed. Its four-person team also reports reaching a prototype hot-fire in four months for under $30,000. These signals support buyer interest and prototype execution. Realized launch sales and paid production use remain unverified, and the company targets its first orbital flight for 2029 or 2030. (*Key Facts*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Even with strong demand, growth could require mission-specific work for each operator and substantial new capacity as flights fill. In a favorable scenario, an extra payload on a scheduled flight contributes its contracted price per kilogram times its billed mass, less attributable sales, integration, and handling costs. Once demand requires another flight, the relevant costs also include launch operations, recovery, refurbishment, and any additional vehicle or site capacity. The reported hot-fire provides prototype evidence. The proposed lower part count and reuse leave flight-level costs and turnaround unproven. The structural gate stays open because shared capacity offers a credible path to spreading flight costs and the memo does not establish that added volume would be economically infeasible. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can RATLOG earn positive incremental contribution on a repeat paid rideshare at a realistic payload load? Collected revenue and actual sales, integration, launch, recovery, and refurbishment costs from a reflight, with any required capacity step-up accounted for, would answer it. If the mission loses money even at realistic high utilization and the loss comes from unavoidable delivery or capacity costs, move to Pass. Positive contribution would keep RATLOG on Watch pending evidence of repeatable acquisition and commercial growth.
