# Evardi -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B. Repeatable sales with account expansion.** The initial buyer is a distribution utility’s operations team, which repeatedly checks switching orders before operators approve them. Evardi reports that its software caught 60% of errors that passed manual review in a simulation on public data. It also reports deployments at utilities serving 10M+ electricity customers across 180,000+ line miles. That footprint describes the utilities’ service networks. The memo gives no utility buyer count, paid conversion, or recurring production usage. The simulation gives an early-stage reason to test the product in the field. Buyer commitment remains unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Growth scenario.** CEO outreach and demos could bring utilities into a paid switching-check deployment. If Evardi can reuse the validated network model, the same account could add feeders or adopt storm restoration. In a second geography, the same software would need to ingest local data, map the network, and accommodate the utility’s operating limits, approval workflow, and security requirements. Contribution could improve if those steps become reusable and each added feeder takes less engineering and support effort. The memo supplies no cross-market rollout, repeatable onboarding, realized expansion, or delivery-cost measurements. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

SMUD has deployed meter-based transformer monitoring and integrated AspenTech for coordinated switching and restoration. That demonstrates demand for the workflow and gives utilities an incumbent route to overlapping capabilities. Evardi could face a fresh sales and security review, data integration, and topology and operating-limit validation at every utility. If those steps require comparable engineering effort on each rollout, a recurring buyer problem could still yield slow, labor-intensive growth. The memo establishes the risk and leaves the actual effort unmeasured, so the structural Pass gate does not apply. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent distribution utility in a second geography pay for switching checks and use them routinely with the same core product? A rollout that documents payment, repeated use in operator-approved workflows, and the engineering and support hours required would answer it. If those records show a paid, recurring workflow with mostly reusable setup, move to **Reach out**.
