# Orca Aerospace -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Orca Aerospace has a credible reason to exist: spacecraft cannot always wait for the next ground contact to make a decision. What I cannot price is the investment. Proposed post-money, competing term sheets, and whether my check enables something otherwise unfundable are all undisclosed. YC membership establishes none of those facts. I will not invent an auction, but I also cannot underwrite cheap, non-consensus ownership from financing silence.

The founders are the strongest evidence here. Jacob Ososke spent more than a decade in Lockheed Martin guidance, navigation, and control and founded its first AI-GNC team; Hakan Chunton combines Lockheed GNC experience with MIT trajectory and collision-avoidance research. That gives Orca substance behind its proposed bridge between AI reasoning and spacecraft control. It fits my pattern of backing people who can translate between difficult operating systems. However, the dossier does not identify a flight deployment or customer operating result attributable to either founder. Relevant work earns credit; an assumed flight heritage does not.

The architecture makes the bull case considerably stronger than “put a language model on a satellite.” AI selects objectives, a deterministic planner constructs actions, and guardrails reject unacceptable commands. That separation could let customers buy autonomy without surrendering control of execution. Orca’s concept-level integration study with Lyra also exposes a concrete deployment constraint: model memory drives board requirements, and board power drives spacecraft sizing. This is meaningful engineering engagement. It does not yet establish that proprietary access caused a purchase, pilot commitment, or retained operational use. The 302 LinkedIn followers and government-contract registration add no conversion evidence.

The objection to passing is that demanding commercial proof now could miss precisely the hard, neglected infrastructure business these founders are equipped to build. I take that seriously. But Shield AI and Sedaro reported 189 approved commands executed aboard a NOVI satellite in August 2026, including an onboard validation framework. Orca’s architectural distinction therefore needs customer-specific validation to become an owned advantage. My exceptions to skepticism about AI and missing financing gates rest on demonstrated conversion, operating economics, or unusually hard external validation. A concept study supplies a useful opening, but does not supply that override. Nor does deterministic command checking establish safety across an entire sequence of mission decisions.

I pass on the investment described here. Public pricing, revenue, burn, margins, and runway are unavailable, so I cannot determine whether integration work funds a durable software business or consumes the next financing. That is an unanswered economic question, not evidence of negative margins. Likewise, no disclosed use of proceeds ties this round to a dated, measurable venture moment; the engineering vacancy does not establish the capital plan. My one verifiable flip condition is a documented financing package below $10 million post-money that funds a signed customer flight-validation contract, with dated acceptance thresholds and a cash budget reaching acceptance without another raise. That would connect price, technical truth, customer ownership, and survival in one underwritable transaction.
