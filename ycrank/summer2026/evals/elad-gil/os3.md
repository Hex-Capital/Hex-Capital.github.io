# OS3 -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Rishabh Chanana and Chris Hailey built Observee in six hours and turned it into a reported $10K MRR business, making OS3 a high-shipping, low-current-curve bet. That pairing is OS3’s analytical signature: the founders have demonstrated unusually fast iteration, but OS3’s only present traction is an unnamed hospitality deployment with no unit count, contract value, paid status, or usage curve. I believe the founders can keep skating for the puck; I do not yet know whether customers are buying the puck.

**Perplexity** is the high rung for founder iteration because Aravind Srinivas repeatedly returned a week later with the discussed product built. OS3 shares the underlying mechanism: Chanana and Hailey met at an MCP hackathon, shipped Observee in six hours, won three categories, and subsequently reached $10K MRR. OS3 diverges because the visible iteration crosses from software into mobile dual-arm hardware, where a fast prototype does not demonstrate reliable deployment, manufacturing, or servicing; the archived Observee repository and rapid robotics pivot establish speed, but not yet Perplexity-style compounding around one product.

**Lyft** is the low rung on revenue quality because growth that fails to alter winner-take-all economics can be a false signal rather than evidence of “just working.” OS3 is below even that evidentiary threshold: OS3 claims a hospitality deployment but names no hotel, revenue, robot count, uptime, intervention rate, or repeat use. Pre-seed status excuses limited history, not the absence of one legible buying signal when OS3 is already using the word “deployed.” The prior $10K MRR from Observee proves selling ability, but it cannot substitute for current-product revenue velocity.

**NVIDIA’s March 2025 GR00T N1 release** is a real recent catalyst: open foundation models and synthetic-data tooling reduce the software and data burden for video-trained robotics. Over the next 18 months, that catalyst can let OS3 iterate manipulation policies faster, but it also gives the same leverage to Figure, Apptronik, 1X, and dozens of better-capitalized entrants. OS3 therefore benefits from a market re-opening without owning it; unless hotel labor shortages convert the 65% staffing-gap statistic into rapid purchasing, the catalyst compounds competition faster than OS3’s distribution.

**Figure’s $1.745B-plus funding** frames OS3’s index problem: OS3 currently sells a vertically integrated wheeled robot into hotels, so OS3 must win deployments against heavily funded platforms rather than automatically benefiting from every robotics deployment. The score flips upward if OS3 becomes the hospitality manipulation, workflow, or fleet-data layer used across multiple robot bodies—an index on physical-labor automation rather than one hardware vendor. Evidence of integrations with two outside robot manufacturers or a hotel-chain workflow standard would move OS3 an entire tier on this dimension.

**Color Genomics** worked because commodity sequencing was the hard layer and Color owned the workflow above it. OS3 currently inverts that architecture: OS3 proposes in-house hardware, internet-video pretraining, reinforcement learning, limited real-robot data, safe hotel operation, manufacturing, and direct deployment. That is not a single miracle; it is several correlated execution problems plus at least two independent miracles—reliable general manipulation and economical field operations. The wheeled form factor removes the unnecessary bipedal miracle, but OS3 remains closer to a science project than to Color’s workflow-on-top-of-a-validated-hard-thing pattern.

**OS3’s unnamed hospitality deployment** leads me to predict that by July 2029 OS3 will have pivoted from general hotel-kitchen labor to a narrower warehouse or production-line manipulation workflow and will remain below $1M ARR. OS3’s founders have enough shipping velocity to find a more constrained task, while the missing customer curve, 5 kg payload per arm, undisclosed intervention metrics, and capital intensity make broad hospitality autonomy unlikely to reach repeatable economics within 36 months.

**Anduril** is the strongest objection because it shows that a hardware-heavy company can escape my science-project red flag when a recent catalyst, shipped systems, and workflow distribution collapse the risk into one miracle. The bull case is real: OS3 has a specific 2025 foundation-model catalyst, Chanana has robotics and ML experience, Hailey shipped production systems at Coinbase, Observee reached $10K MRR, and OS3 claims an operating hospitality deployment. The dossier still loses on the distinction that mattered for Anduril: OS3 provides no named buyer, contract economics, reliability data, or procurement path, while three competitors have raised between $125M and nearly $1.75B. I would not write the check today. Ten paid sites at one named hotel group, paired with greater than 20% monthly revenue growth and declining human interventions over six months, would flip my verdict.
