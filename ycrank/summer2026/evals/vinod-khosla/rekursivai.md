# rekursiv.ai -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

If rekursiv.ai works, machine-learning research changes from serial expert labor into parallel, self-correcting experimentation. That could accelerate every downstream model breakthrough. But the dossier does not quantify the present research cost pool, researcher-hours displaced, affected population, or resulting rate of progress. “AI scientists whose breakthroughs accelerate the next” describes recursive leverage, not yet a 100x societal consequence.

The technical evidence is the reason to keep reading. The system coordinated 235 ARC experiments across 15 directions and 684 Sudoku experiments in four days. It reached 100% on Sudoku-Extreme and a 99,768-puzzle stress test, while reporting ARC performance at 16–11,600x lower computational cost than selected accuracy peers. Those are unusually concrete pre-seed results. They are not yet a clean incumbent benchmark: ARC used public evaluation, the team expects a four-to-six-point semi-private decline, and its Sudoku method failed to transfer directly to ARC. This proves a powerful experimentation engine on benchmarks, not autonomous scientific productivity on a customer’s expensive workflow.

The bear case stings. One reported paper used four agents, Claude Max, one GPU, and roughly $500 of resources. Discoveries and some components are Apache-licensed; Sakana AI also publishes competing AI-scientist code. With only 16 GitHub followers, no exclusive dataset, and no documented performance improvement from accumulated experiment history, rekursiv.ai may be excellent orchestration around increasingly commoditized models. Periodic Labs, Lila Sciences, Edison Scientific, and Sakana have collectively raised far more capital. If the orchestration layer does not learn faster from proprietary experiments, technical novelty becomes a feature available to every frontier-model customer.

Dillon and Kondratyuk are unusually credible builders: TensorFlow Probability, Veo, VideoPoet, Dream Machine, foundation-model pre-training, and world-model leadership directly match the problem. More important, they published a failure: Hypothesis-Pinning Search did not transfer from Sudoku to ARC, so they retained only its feedback-and-repair mechanism. That is evidence of tactical revision, and the completed experiment campaigns spend effort against generalization and throughput risk. But the dossier supplies no ranked kill-risk plan, experiment budget, stop threshold, or complementary recruit already won. Two founders from the same Google Research–Luma lineage and two open roles are not yet risk-tuned gene-pool engineering.

The serious exception argument resembles OpenAI: visible technical progress could justify buying the option before a business model exists. The material difference is that rekursiv.ai currently depends on externally supplied frontier models, exposes important components openly, and has not demonstrated that its own learning loop compounds. There is no pricing, revenue, named customer, or bottom-up scale model. The $500 research-paper experiment and low-cost ARC results suggest favorable economics, but hundreds of experiments and runtime as the acknowledged bottleneck could also make useful discoveries expensive. The $5 million financing validates investor appetite, not unsubsidized customer value.

I would not write the personal check today. This is a technically impressive research system without a quantified consequence or demonstrated economic loop—a 1x-loss option whose 100x side remains asserted rather than measured. One condition flips me: a named external ML team must independently show that rekursiv.ai produces a validated improvement on its private production problem with at least 10x fewer researcher-hours at an all-in cost below the displaced human work. That single result would connect the benchmark discontinuity to proprietary demand and real economics.
