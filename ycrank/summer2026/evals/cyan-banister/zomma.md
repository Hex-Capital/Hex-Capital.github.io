# Zomma -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Jihyun Kim helped launch a $1B multi-strategy hedge fund, yet Zomma has three founders without a documented tiebreaker. That pairing is Zomma’s signature: an authentically finance-native founder wrapped in a structurally fragile founding arrangement. Zomma did not discover reconciliation in a TAM memo; Kim lived among the portals, PDFs, and desktop systems. My hesitation is not whether Zomma knows the problem. It is whether everyone knows who decides when Kim’s operating instincts collide with Varun Arumugam’s agent architecture or Cashel Fitzgerald’s vision approach.

**Anduril** is the high rung because Palmer Luckey carried an entire prior life of building physical systems into defense. Kim gives Zomma the same load-bearing mechanism: Goldman Sachs, Endowus, and Arrowpoint put her inside the workflows Zomma automates, while unnamed hedge funds and asset managers are already using Zomma. The divergence is depth of demonstrated founder-product compounding. Luckey had shipped Oculus before Anduril; Zomma’s dossier does not show Kim previously shipping workflow software to scale, and Zomma’s two technical founders have adjacent ML and verification experience rather than the same lived financial-operations origin.

**GameCrush** is the low rung because multiple founders and investor pressure eventually stripped away the product’s DNA when authority became contested. Zomma names Kim as CEO, which is materially better than shared titles, but Zomma provides no equity split, operating agreement, vesting protocol, or conflict tiebreaker across three founders. “CEO” on a YC page is a role; authority is what survives the first impossible disagreement. Zomma is above the GameCrush rung, but not far enough above it for me to stop hearing the floorboards creak.

**Zalos** has already raised $3.6M, Eloquent AI has raised $7.4M, and Stacks has raised $35M; over the next 18 months, that density turns Zomma’s computer-use layer from novelty into a commodity. Zomma can compound only by learning the exception histories, audit requirements, and approval paths peculiar to investment operations faster than general models improve. Without proprietary reliability data or retention evidence, Zomma looks less “magically weird” than professionally well-positioned. This is a rebuttal, not a hard no, but I am never playing the game I seem to be playing—and the obvious game here is crowded AI-agent infrastructure.

**SOC 2 remains in progress** while Zomma handles vaulted credentials, KYC, reconciliation, and approval-gated financial actions. Zomma would flip into my frontier-and-regulated tier if its product assumed accountable control of a regulated workflow—producing audit-ready records, carrying contractual liability, or obtaining a compliance authorization competitors could not quickly copy. Today Zomma sells software into regulated institutions; Zomma is not yet itself a regulated-finance artifact. That is closer to an unsexy wedge than a **Vigil Labs**-style moat.

**Flock Safety** could be encountered as a camera and discovered through a Wi-Fi signal before anyone explained its market. Zomma’s agents can be observed clicking custodian portals, downloading PDFs, and pausing for approval, so Zomma is more tangible than a developer API whose value exists only in a benchmark. Yet a normal person cannot install Zomma within 48 hours, and the forward-deployed enterprise model makes every observation mediated by a sales demonstration. Zomma has an observable artifact, but not the accidental, environmental verifiability that made Flock impossible for me to ignore.

**Unnamed hedge funds and asset managers already using Zomma** support a narrow forecast: by July 2029, Zomma will have concentrated on managed reconciliation for investment firms, reached $2M–$5M ARR, and lost at least one of its three original founders. Zomma’s finance-native origin should produce real contracts, while commodity computer-use models will force Zomma toward services-heavy exception handling rather than a horizontal platform. The unresolved authority structure is most likely to surface when that narrowing demands choosing between product purity and forward-deployed revenue.

**Kim’s Arrowpoint experience and Zomma’s live institutional users** make the strongest bull case sting: Zomma could convert every correction and approval into proprietary workflow memory, preserve complementary founder roles, and become the trusted operating layer while general agents remain too unsafe for finance. **HQ Trivia** is the known exception—explosive product pull can temporarily override ugly governance. Zomma does not yet have that override: usage is unnamed, revenue is undisclosed, SOC 2 is unfinished, and no retention or moat signal appears in the dossier. I would flip to Invest if Zomma documents Kim’s final operating authority and shows at least $1M ARR with 90%+ gross retention from named or independently verified investment firms.
