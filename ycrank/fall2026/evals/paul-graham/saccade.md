# Saccade -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Saccade has built something harder than a pitch deck. UCSB documents custom cameras, illumination, a chassis, and thermal management for an eye-tracking attachment. Bhardwaj’s mechanical-engineering and soft-robotics background fits that work. But I cannot find the founder story I most want: what failed, what he changed, and how quickly the change worked. The attachment earns technical credit. It cannot also supply an undocumented story about resourcefulness ([UCSB](https://capstone.engineering.ucsb.edu/projects/saccade)).

The important distinction is between measuring eye movements and detecting Alzheimer’s pathology. Saccade has evidence for engineering the former. Its own homepage says the technology is unvalidated for diagnosis, individual risk prediction, and treatment decisions. That leaves the central claim—detection up to 15 years before symptoms—unproved. A beautifully engineered instrument can measure something accurately without that measurement answering the question customers care about. I would give the hardware substantial partial credit, but not the credit due a measured biological result ([Saccade](https://saccade.health/), [YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Vff-saccade-world-s-earliest-test-for-alzheimer-s-biomarkers)).

Longevity clinics are a plausible place to find the first users. They contain people willing to try an unusual assessment before conventional symptoms appear. The October 2026 launch reports deployment in San Francisco clinics, which is a little evidence beyond expressions of interest. But no clinic is named, and there are no assessment volumes, repeat orders, or payments. Meanwhile, the homepage recruits participants for future research. Those statements may describe different stages of availability; they do not establish that anyone keeps using the product. Nor does the $7,500 competition award answer that question. I want to know whether a clinician requests the next assessment after seeing the first result.

The strongest case for investing is that this two-person team has already undertaken difficult physical engineering, and the clinical experiment could reveal something valuable. Bindwell is a relevant exception: young technical founders earned credibility through laboratory validation of their scientific claim. The shared mechanism is building evidence before asking investors to believe an unfamiliar proposition. The material difference is that Saccade’s documented artifact establishes an ability to collect signals, while its disease-specific predictive value remains unvalidated. My pattern of backing concrete scientific proof therefore supports taking this team seriously, but does not yet support writing the check.

The neglected work here could become valuable: collecting recordings linked to confirmed pathology, controlling measurement variation, and fitting the assessment into clinical decisions. But that is prospective work, not an existing dataset advantage. ViewMind already measures eye movements, and the FDA-cleared blood test provides another comparator. Saccade must establish what its result changes, especially when an asymptomatic result does not itself establish treatment eligibility. Bhardwaj’s prior device work supports his ability to build the instrument; it does not document how he personally encountered this need. There is also no documented evidence-driven product revision. Both dimensions receive their missing-evidence defaults. I see no basis to infer partner dependence, premature delegation, or founder obstinacy from this dossier.

I would pass today. Saccade misses my founder-action gate, and neither independently validated disease detection nor unmistakable user pull supplies an exception. The conflicting availability statements require clarification, but do not prove misconduct or a pivot. My single flip condition is an independently documented clinic study showing a named measurement failure, the founder’s dated fix, and held-out biomarker discrimination meeting a prespecified clinically useful threshold. That would connect the promising engineering to both resourcefulness and the actual diagnostic claim.
