# twentyfour26 -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I want a hardware engineer to change a design over breakfast and test the replacement part before going home. twentyfour26 touches something necessary for that world: the repeated quoting, programming, setup, and inspection that make each new geometry another little project. But automated planning must connect to reliable physical execution, then inspection, without the founders rescuing every unfamiliar part. The dossier establishes the starting machinery; it does not establish that those transitions are happening. I can imagine the destination more clearly than I can underwrite the path.

What attracts me is that the founders operate the shop. twentyfour26 owns its software and runs its CNC, production, and inspection rather than handing execution to a supplier network. That puts mistakes where the team can learn from them. It fits my pattern of looking beneath a visible product for an enabling layer: faster physical iteration could serve many hardware businesses. The important distinction is between possessing that learning opportunity and possessing a demonstrated advantage. Proprietary process data is explicitly an inference here. CloudNC already distributes overlapping programming automation to more than 1,000 shops, so owning a machine does not by itself establish a scarce capability.

Why does the customer wait? The dossier gives a useful answer: production-oriented shops repeatedly perform preparatory work for prototype orders. Removing that repetition could return engineering time and control over iteration schedules. It does not identify a quantified intermediary rent or artificial restriction, however; some setup and inspection are necessary work. The commercial evidence is similarly narrow. Sam reports taking manufacturing orders in the October 2, 2026 interview, which is more informative than a waitlist, but there are no disclosed completed-order counts, repeat customers, or observed delivery improvements. Individually negotiated quotes establish a selling mechanism, not its economics.

Hemant’s aerospace MS and experience at AGCO and Grain & Protein Technologies give the team relevant industrial context. Sam has shipped software, including the publicly visible lead-enrich extension, and their eight-year relationship offers some evidence of durable collaboration. What I cannot find is a dated machining obsession, prior autonomous-production experiments, or evidence of recruiting others into this particular mission. Leaving school is not itself proof of independently derived conviction; neither is a pivot evidence of opportunism. My CosmicBrain AI exception matters here: both bets concern physical automation, but that commitment had lifelong robotics evidence and signed industrial proofs of concept. Those were affirmative reasons to bridge the missing revenue. This dossier supplies less support for that bridge.

The strongest argument against passing is that running real orders could teach twentyfour26 exactly what software-only competitors cannot observe. A narrow 6061-T6 aluminum envelope could help the founders master a repeatable process before expanding. That is a credible experiment, and acknowledging unfinished autonomy avoids the red flag of pretending manufacturing is trivial. But the observed business could still become a capable, founder-dependent prototype shop. No severe privacy violation or leadership shell game is documented; no exception removes the need to establish the automation mechanism. I pass with my own money today. My flip condition is one auditable cohort of repeat customers whose successive, previously unseen parts move through planning, machining, and inspection with declining founder intervention while meeting their quoted tolerances and delivery dates.
