# Cyclon -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Cyclon’s strongest path. An Android handset maker could buy Cyclon’s OS-level agent to bring background workflows across signed-in apps and on-device access to custom, user-generated tools to its phones sooner. Cyclon describes both capabilities and offers a refurbished Pixel 8a for closed-beta feedback. The memo documents the launch and a refundable $199 reservation toward an estimated $699 handset price. The buyer rationale is an inference. Ownership could give a handset maker more control over OS integration and the product roadmap if Cyclon can demonstrate a meaningful lead over building or licensing the features. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The route-specific milestone is a refurbished-Pixel beta showing sustained repeat use and a measurable advantage in successful cross-app task completion over stock Android. The beta offer gives Cyclon a plausible way to run that test before committing to custom handset production. The memo lists $125,000 in financing and gives no beta budget or burn rate. Software and data transfer rights are also unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Under the illustrative terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-ons. The 5× gross cash target within eight years requires $2.5 million for the holder, or $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership. That equity hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% case requires about $88.3 million of enterprise value. On a simple pro rata basis before investor taxes, a $25 million sale at year eight yields $600,000 (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). The memo supplies no usable acquisition price evidence or company-specific debt, fee, or preference terms.

If growth plateaus, buyer interest fails to materialize, public-market scale stays out of reach, and Cyclon reinvests all available cash, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. The memo supplies no separate support for a sponsor buyout, an eligible secondary, an IPO resale, or a funded payout. Any wind-down proceeds would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Google already automates supported tasks on existing Android phones, and Nothing already offers user-generated apps. Those products give handset makers build or integration alternatives for parts of Cyclon’s offering. App compatibility limits and execution errors could further narrow Cyclon’s advantage on recurring workflows. That weakens the case for paying the required price. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the memo leaves both the attainable price and any structural barrier to an OS acquisition unresolved. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would an Android handset maker value ownership of Cyclon’s OS agent at a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle after a successful beta? Move to Reach out if a handset maker uses measured beta results to quantify the advantage of ownership over building or licensing and supports a valuation near or above the illustrative $88.3 million enterprise value hurdle at 3% ownership.
