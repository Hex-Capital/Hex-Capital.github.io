# TrueMile -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with its initial U.S. buyers, TrueMile has a plausible way to improve lane economics. Its solver plans outbound and return loads across leased-on trucks, and its agents handle broker outreach and dispatch coordination. Shippers enter through inquiries and booked calls, while owner-operators join through a lease-on process. Both are reusable channels that still require human involvement. Dedicated lanes could generate recurring paid loads, and existing shippers could spend more by tendering more freight. The two solicited partnerships leave repeat billing and account expansion unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

TrueMile reports booking $57K of freight in three weeks. The memo gives no completed or billed load figures, so the booking figure leaves the realized commercial slope unknown. In an illustrative per-load model, contribution equals the freight charge less operator settlement, any deadhead costs TrueMile bears, AI and communications costs, human approval and exception handling, and attributable onboarding and compliance work. Fewer empty miles and more trucks per dispatcher could improve contribution. The required sign-off on every load preserves a labor cost, and the memo gives no rates or settlements to size the margin. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

The structural downside gate applies to the current carrier model. The solver may transfer, but serving shippers in another country would likely require rebuilding substantial parts of the lease-on, safety and compliance, and local freight operations around it. The memo establishes a U.S. carrier workflow and gives no credible near-term path to transferring that operating model with bounded changes. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

TrueMile could grow quickly by adding density in Texas and the Sunbelt. More recurring outbound freight could give the solver more opportunities to match return loads, and the company’s reported 9× dispatcher capacity suggests a way to spread dispatch labor across a larger fleet. The required human sign-off alone does not trigger the gate. The domestic case leaves the geographic constraint in place. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can TrueMile repeatedly complete and bill freight in a second country using the same dispatch product and a repeatable local carrier arrangement? A live lane with repeat invoices and a rollout record showing bounded changes to lease-on, safety, and compliance would move the action to Watch.
