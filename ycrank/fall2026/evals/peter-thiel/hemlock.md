# Hemlock -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Hemlock’s possible secret is that wrist muscle signals can supply the missing supervision for dexterous robot learning without instrumenting the hand itself. That is a precise technical proposition. It would be substantially weakened if the advantage disappeared on unfamiliar workers and tasks. The reported 10× improvement in egg handling and 2.7× improvement in lightbulb installation make this worth examining. But the October 2026 FACT research also recognizes the importance of force supervision. Hemlock has evidence for a useful signal; it has not established which important belief its competitors reject.

The decisive question is whether Hemlock can own the resulting business. Its wrist-worn Bracelet offers a concrete architectural difference from MANUS and StretchSense gloves and Contactile’s robot-mounted sensors. A less intrusive collection device could reach demonstrations those systems cannot economically capture. Yet the dossier establishes neither that collection advantage nor exclusive access to the workers producing the data. “Proprietary” describes the Bracelet; it does not establish a monopoly. The four claimed wearable patents lack identifiers and demonstrated company ownership. I assign some credit to an existing differentiated device, but none to the hypothetical exclusive corpus.

Julian Olschwang is the strongest reason to resist an easy dismissal. His hand-sensing project earned a $25,000 Davidson Fellowship in 2024, and his UCLA work involved generating military robotics training data. Those are unusually relevant antecedents to this particular product. They make the bull case uncomfortable: Hemlock may be an inventor discovering a valuable measurement primitive before the commercial category exists. Nevertheless, domain continuity does not establish the founding structure. A solo founder has no co-founder alignment problem to prove, but full-time status, ownership, and control of essential engineering remain undocumented. I find no affirmative evidence of the part-time, outsourced-engineering, or distorted-cap-table red flags.

Hemlock also fits the less obvious preference for infrastructure beneath the complete AI production stack. The attractive version supplies indispensable training inputs to multiple robotics developers rather than betting on one robot manufacturer. The dangerous version performs commissioned collection that customers eventually internalize. Public pricing, margins, licensing terms, and customer expansion are unavailable, so I cannot distinguish those businesses economically. The broader $1.05 billion training-data estimate includes simulation and internal collection; it supplies no arithmetic for Hemlock’s value capture. Its YC launch identifies plausible buyers, but requests for introductions and an inquiry form establish prospecting, not a repeatable distribution engine.

Could technical proof override the ownership concern? The relevant known-exception mechanism is a demonstrated technical discontinuity strong enough to justify unresolved monopoly economics. Hemlock’s two company-reported task improvements are the beginning of that argument, but relative gains without absolute success rates, cross-worker results, or independent replication cannot carry it. Nor does this dossier show a validated production-and-demand path that would justify accepting several simultaneous hardware and commercialization risks. Conversely, adjacent sensing suppliers are not proof that Hemlock is a commodity clone. The uncertainty is real in both directions.

I would pass on a personal check today, while keeping the technical result under observation. My concern is the gap between discovering valuable information and controlling its supply. The single verifiable event that would move me to investigate is an independently documented renewal by a robotics lab for an exclusive Hemlock dataset, with the renewal attributable to gains on held-out workers and tasks. That would connect technical usefulness to durable purchasing behavior. Today, Hemlock has a credible experiment and a relevant inventor; the monopoly remains a hypothesis.
