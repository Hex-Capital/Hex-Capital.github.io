# Snowstone -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Snowstone’s candidate secret is that a company’s operating records reveal financially important failures that interviews and consultant reports systematically miss. That is a testable proposition: compare its discoveries with an independent diagnostic, then measure the improvements management actually realizes. But the dossier does not establish which informed peers reject this proposition or show that Snowstone has beaten their approach. Its private benchmark—dollar estimates within 20% of ground truth in 66% of cases—provides a measurement, not yet proof of a secret. The remaining cases need explanation before estimated EBITDA becomes an investment thesis.

The founding structure supplies the strongest affirmative evidence. Moreland and Baur met on their first school day and subsequently built projects and competed in hackathons together. That is a relationship with productive history, rather than a team assembled around an application deadline. Baur’s Winterberg diligence experience also gives Snowstone some contact with the buyer’s actual work. But Waldorf’s history with the other founders, everyone’s full-time status, and economic ownership remain undocumented. I credit the demonstrated working relationship without treating Harvard credentials as evidence of alignment.

Private equity is a recognizable beachhead. Whether it can become a monopoly is a different question. Snowstone preserves reviewed entity matches and source/version histories, but those features do not establish exclusive knowledge, customer dependence, or improving economics with scale. Celonis, Skan, and Mimica approach overlapping problems through different observation methods; the dossier does not prove that they are interchangeable with Snowstone. It also does not show why they could not serve this buyer. A PE-specific financial output could distinguish the product while leaving its economic position contestable. The monopoly mechanism remains unproved, rather than refuted.

The strongest bull case is that Snowstone becomes the diagnostic standard across a sponsor’s entire portfolio. Its reported $2B+ of transaction value reviewed and $50M+ of opportunities identified suggest exposure to substantial operating problems, although neither figure establishes payment or realized gains. This fits two patterns I take seriously: finance can support consequential companies, and systems that make disputed institutional reality legible can become valuable infrastructure. Source-linked findings could let an operating partner challenge management’s account with evidence. If validated decisions accumulate across repeated engagements, the business could become more durable than its initial diagnostic.

But the bridge from that possibility to an investable company is still missing. The demo funnel and reported collaboration with Corporate Lifecycles MD and Grey Matter Global identify a plausible customer route; they do not establish repeatable conversion or delivery economics. Read-only access reduces intervention risk while still requiring permission to inspect sensitive communications and financial systems. Pricing, margins, and expansion contracts are undisclosed, so I cannot construct power-law arithmetic from the current evidence. My known exceptions for competitive markets require a demonstrated technical discontinuity or persistent customer transactions. Neither the private benchmark nor potential savings supplies that override. There is also no documented part-time founder, outsourced core engineering, or investor-dominated capitalization that would justify invoking those red flags.

I pass on writing a check today. Snowstone has a credible founding relationship and a potentially useful way to expose operational truth, but no demonstrated mechanism for owning the value it uncovers. Missing evidence does not prove failure; it leaves an investigation rather than a conviction. One verifiable event would reopen the decision: an independently audited sponsor renewal extending Snowstone to at least two additional portfolio companies after the first deployment produces realized savings exceeding both its fees and implementation costs. That would establish that this buyer purchases demonstrated outcomes repeatedly.
