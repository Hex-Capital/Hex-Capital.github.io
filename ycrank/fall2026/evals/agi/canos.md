# Canos -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** The strongest candidate is repeat monitoring for dogs whose owners would otherwise forgo frequent imaging. The memo describes CT as costly and requiring sedation. Canos offers a one-draw sequencing workflow that compares results over time. The investment hypothesis is that automated analysis could reduce labor enough to make serial monitoring viable for those owners. Canos reports 90.6% sensitivity at 95.1% specificity in retrospective cross-validation on a 303-dog diagnostic subset. That supports technical plausibility. Prospective monitoring utility and labor savings at equivalent clinical quality remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

With equally capable models available to competitors, clinics would still need a dependable service for sample collection, processing, and interpretation over time. Canos reports a longitudinal analysis of 117 dogs across 514 time points, and its founder reports a patent filing. The longitudinal work gives Canos a starting point for building a harder-to-reproduce service. Control over collection or lab capacity, enforceable patent rights, and a buyer preference for Canos remain unestablished. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

Canos reports ten clinics and mobile vets onboarding. Paid conversion and repeat orders are unreported, and the advertised free trial leaves willingness to pay open. The founders bring complementary veterinary diagnostics and computational pathology experience and previously worked together on a veterinary pathology platform. The launch and U.S. clinic onboarding support evaluating Canos as a seed-stage prospect. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, The Idea)

The scale unit is an active clinic multiplied by eligible dogs per clinic and paid tests per dog. Assuming Canos realizes the listed $399 OncoTrack price, contribution per usable result depends on collection, shipping, sequencing, computation, review, and redraw costs. The buyer’s economics also depend on the workup a result triggers. The memo gives no realized price, cost per usable result, or repeat-testing rate. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Canos could add another paid diagnostic step while owners still need the same imaging or follow-up. CanCan already offers sequencing-based monitoring, and VolitionRx has in-clinic distribution for blood-based screening. PetDx’s former sequencing service stopped processing samples after substantial fundraising, though the memo does not establish why. Together, these facts make adoption and substitution material risks. The memo establishes neither equivalent competing performance nor failed economics for Canos, so the override remains untriggered. Neither Path B condition clears the outreach gate, and the memo identifies no separate bottleneck Canos controls for Path A. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a prospective, paid trial across several clinics show that owners who previously skipped serial monitoring pay for repeat OncoTrack tests because Canos delivers a clinically useful result with less total labor and a service they prefer when comparable analytical capability is available? Measure labor, redraws, and downstream workups against usual care, and test paid repeat choice against a credible alternative with comparable analytical performance. A result that establishes both conditions and supports viable contribution would justify rescoring for Reach out. Evidence that the labor savings disappear or buyers readily switch under parity would close Path B and move the action to Pass.
