# Asakana -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Yudai has handled the fish. That matters more to me than the MBA. Seafood distribution teaches you why a restaurant’s shorthand is intelligible to an experienced salesperson and ambiguous to software. Ben’s work on Gemini and Project Mariner supplies a complementary ability to automate the interaction. Asakana has carried that combination into live distributor deployments. This is specific knowledge expressed through action, with substantially more substance than two impressive résumés ([Columbia](https://business.columbia.edu/insights/recent-columbia-business-school-grads-y-combinator), [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/asakana)).

The investment turns on who owns the learning. Asakana interprets orders using customer histories, preferences, and catalogs, then escalates uncertainty before entering the ERP. Those are useful capabilities. The dossier does not establish that the resulting knowledge belongs exclusively to Asakana, transfers across deployments, or makes replacement materially harder. Choco already offers message-to-ERP automation and claims over 200 ERP connections. A competitor can therefore deliver the central workflow. The unanswered question is whether Asakana accumulates something the competitor cannot obtain by connecting to the same distributor’s records ([Asakana](https://www.asakana.co/), [Choco](https://choco.com/us/stories/suppliers/from-restaurant-app-to-ai-partner-for-distributors-the-evolution-of-choco)).

The strongest argument for investing is that these founders may discover that asset through deployment. One distributor receives approximately 60 orders daily, some exceeding 75 lines; Asakana reports returning over 40% of order-desk time to selling. That is consequential work to automate. Broadline customers also provide concrete evidence that the seafood wedge can travel. Order interpretation and ERP entry extend into broader food distribution, while competitors’ commerce and payment products identify adjacent transactions. I give that expansion path credit without inventing a market-size estimate or treating competitors’ financing as customer spend ([YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/VrR-asakana-automating-sales-and-the-entire-back-office-for-food-distributors), [founder announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/yudai-matsuoka_asakana-the-company-i-founded-has-been-activity-7496684951208202241-qcIw)).

But automating fragmented offline commerce does not automatically create a network. The dossier shows no additional distributor making another distributor’s product better, or customers generating distribution for one another. Columbia introductions are a useful founder-led acquisition channel; they do not establish permissionless participation or a customer-created loop. Transaction-volume commissions align the proposed business model with activity, but negotiated rates, collected revenue, and contribution margins remain undisclosed. Reported labor savings demonstrate customer utility; they do not establish how much of that utility Asakana captures ([terms](https://www.asakana.co/terms-of-service.html)).

My exceptions for exceptional founders and working prototypes keep this company interesting. Exceptions for an already compounding network do not apply. Nothing disclosed establishes an integrity problem, artificial financing urgency, or founder instability; the concern is structural. Asakana could become a valuable business while integration maintenance and incumbent bundling constrain the return on my money. At **52/100, I am Neutral and would hold my check**. My flip condition is a documented customer cohort choosing to renew Asakana over a cheaper equivalent because accumulated order context demonstrably cannot be migrated without losing accuracy. That would turn today’s useful implementation into evidence of an asset.
