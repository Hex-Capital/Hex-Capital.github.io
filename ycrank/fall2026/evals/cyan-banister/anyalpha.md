# AnyAlpha -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

By 2032, I can imagine someone expressing a belief about the world, setting a loss limit, and letting software watch the relevant markets while they go live their life. AnyAlpha’s thesis-to-agent workflow points toward that behavior. But getting there requires reliable translation of intent, trustworthy live execution, and permissioned access across venues. The dossier establishes a proposed workflow; it does not yet show those transitions happening. Automating today’s trading screen is useful. The larger future still needs its first convincing artifact.

The founders are the strongest reason to keep looking. Shirley built trading systems at Coinbase and LSEG; Jerry reports six years working across quantitative data, models, backtests, and execution. They met at a Microsoft poker tournament five years before launch and have built together for three years. That is a more interesting origin than two strangers discovering prediction markets during an accelerator. Their reported $500K ARR from earlier apps also suggests some understanding of distribution and commercial levers. But Jerry’s personal trading returns and the prior-app revenue remain founder-reported claims, and the dossier supplies limited independently corroborated build history. I give the team credit without turning their biographies into verified investment performance.

Why does the trader need AnyAlpha? Because the market keeps moving while the trader is somewhere else. Research scattered across news, X, Discord, and market listings creates a real attention burden; conditional execution could return hours and control over availability. That fits my interest in ordinary operating frustrations. Yet the dossier identifies no quantified toll, artificial restriction, or gatekeeper whose power the product removes. AnyAlpha still depends on venue access, including geographically separated Polymarket accounts. Its agency benefit is plausible and specific, but narrower than changing who controls the underlying system.

The $100 agent that reportedly made $53 at an October 2026 YC event is a small, tangible experiment. I would want to touch it. One profitable demonstration, however, tells me little about whether strangers will repeatedly entrust money to the product. The 42 iOS ratings and 100-plus Android downloads span earlier positioning, so they cannot establish current-agent devotion. Listed purchases of $4.99–$79.99 establish a checkout mechanism, with unclear billing periods and entitlements. My strongest bull case is that experienced trading builders with prior consumer-app success have found an unusually accessible way to recruit individual traders. That could become a business before it becomes defensible.

What would AnyAlpha accumulate as those traders arrive? The dossier establishes neither exclusive execution data nor a controlled technical bottleneck. Composer and Capitalise.ai already demonstrate adjacent strategy-creation, testing, and execution workflows; Olas addresses autonomous prediction-market trading directly. My pattern of backing mundane workflow repair gives this team a hearing, but my pattern of finding concealed infrastructure supplies no additional conviction here. The sports-information terms alongside agent execution also leave consent and authorization unclear. This does not establish a severe privacy violation, and Shirley is clearly identified as CEO; I would not invent a governance or custody failure from silence.

I pass with my own money today. My exceptions for pre-revenue technical obsession or extraordinary consumer behavior require affirmative evidence that outweighs the missing commercial history. AnyAlpha’s relevant founders and funded demonstration make that exception worth considering, but do not establish it. One verifiable flip condition would reopen the decision: an auditable 90-day cohort of 20 unaffiliated traders who keep capital allocated and repeatedly run their own agents without founder intervention. That would turn an attractive automation promise into evidence that people have actually changed how they live.
