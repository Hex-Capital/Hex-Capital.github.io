# Tokenless -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine companies buying completed work from agents while quietly employing another set of agents to check whether “completed” means anything. Tokenless has found a wonderfully irritating crack in that world: the machine can get the answer wrong and still collect its reward. But imagining that future is my contribution. The dossier establishes defective graders today; it does not establish the intervening steps by which auditing them becomes an enduring, independent business. That distinction matters when I am writing the check.

Why does a wrong answer receive credit? Because the grader has become the authority, and checking a reference solution does not exhaust the ways that authority can be fooled. Tokenless attacks a specific institutional failure rather than merely calling evaluation broken. Its agents inspect graders, probe tasks in separate sandboxes, and require objective checks or human review to confirm findings ([EnvCheck](https://usetokenless.com/blog/envcheck/)). Fixing that machinery could return control to teams otherwise optimizing against misleading scores. The economics remain unmeasured, though: the dossier does not quantify wasted training expenditure, remediation savings, or what a buyer will pay to recover that control.

The strongest case for investing is that Tokenless has already built something beneath the visible AI wave. Its reported **110 defects across DeepSWE, Terminal-Bench, and BFCL**, accompanied by **66 write-ups**, give the technical claim something to stand on. Public reproducible findings and a working grader are substantially more interesting than a safety presentation. This fits my pattern of finding concealed infrastructure beneath an attention-grabbing application. Yet the infrastructure here is reproducible without demonstrated exclusivity, and the specialized auditor model remains future work. Tokenless also acknowledges that its own judge could be exploited. The company must turn discovering holes into a dependable capability that customers cannot cheaply maintain themselves.

The founders have relevant operating ingredients: Rohit Agarwal researches agent alignment at Princeton, Kev Wang built GPU infrastructure at SkyPilot and worked at Eventual, and Andrew Liu researched language-documentation tools. That earns credit for substance beyond school names. It does not yet show the dated experiments, unusual persistence, or first-believer recruitment that would make me trust this particular team to traverse a six-year map. The September–October 2026 transition from Touchy to Tokenless is not evidence of opportunism. Equally, I cannot treat the consumer assistant as a deliberate beachhead financing infrastructure. The dossier does not connect those dots.

I would **pass today**, with genuine interest in the artifact. My pre-revenue exception applies when a working prototype, exceptional founder obsession, and a concrete future thesis reinforce one another; Tokenless currently supplies the prototype most clearly. Unnamed partners “exploring” reward hacking do not establish repeated customer sacrifice, and Touchy’s ratings or purchase price cannot supply that evidence for this product. Nor does the dossier establish the severe privacy, governance, or single-supplier dependence that would justify an active-mismatch verdict. The objection to my pass is serious: laboratories may urgently need an independent party precisely because their internal scores are compromised. **One named laboratory renewing a paid EnvCheck deployment after documenting a consequential defect its internal checks missed would make me reopen the investment.** That would show somebody rearranging a real training workflow around Tokenless, rather than simply appreciating its findings.
