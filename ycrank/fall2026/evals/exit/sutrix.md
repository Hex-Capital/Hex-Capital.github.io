# Sutrix -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an EHR-integrated clinical research platform is the strongest path. Sutrix describes a workflow that turns records from multiple systems into study-ready datasets using versioned protocol rules, field-level provenance, and reviewer decisions. BlueRock reported less manual reconciliation with the steps visible for expert review, and Emory’s Winship Cancer Institute reported faster preparation of interpretable results. A buyer could pay to own the workflow if embedding it across its installed base saves materially more time and integration work than building or licensing it. That ownership advantage remains an inference. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

The operating milestone is paid renewals across several institutions using the same production workflow, with verified preparation-time savings and limited bespoke work. Sutrix reports paying teams at 10+ institutions, an 80% reduction in preparation time, and $150K+ in revenue over a few months before the software release. The memo documents engagement-specific pricing. It leaves recurring software revenue, the cost of securing production integrations, and runway to reach the milestone unknown. IP ownership and the transferability of customer contracts and data-use permissions are also unresolved. The memo provides no usable acquisition-price evidence or independently supported route to holder cash. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

**Proceeds bridge.** The illustrative seed check is $500,000 at a $10 million post-money valuation. A 40% relative dilution takes ownership from 5% to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale scenarios below are assumptions. They use zero net debt, transaction fees, and other adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. The waterfall assumes pro rata allocation with no senior preference claims. All sale consideration is cash at close, with $0 each in stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation.

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value and distributable equity | Holder cash and gross multiple |
|-----------|--------|-------------------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $50 million | $1.5 million, 3× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100 million | $3 million, 6× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No sale or payout | $0, 0×, with shares retained |

The strong sale clears the hurdle at 3% and yields only $2 million, or 4×, at 2%. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and the company reinvests its cash. The memo gives no usable basis for pricing either sale or treating the prolonged-private outcome as probable.

### Countercase

Sutrix’s early revenue may reflect study-specific delivery work. The software’s contribution to recurring revenue is undisclosed, direct FHIR connectivity is described for sandboxes, and competing tools already automate parts of EHR abstraction. A platform could license Sutrix for the studies that need it and use existing tools for the rest. That would cap what it could justify paying for ownership. The memo supports this as a credible risk while leaving the cost and adequacy of those substitutes unresolved. The structural Pass override is therefore untriggered. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Would owning Sutrix create enough incremental value over licensing it for a clinical research platform to support a price that leaves about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds? A quantified buyer-side comparison grounded in repeat paid production deployments would answer it. Economics that support that hurdle would move the action to Reach out.
