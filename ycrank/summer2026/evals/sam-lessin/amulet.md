# Amulet -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The deal is a blank: no post-money valuation, no competing-term-sheet count, and no evidence this is genuinely the first scarce check. YC participation does not prove an auction, but it does not make price irrelevant. I am being asked to evaluate a security without knowing the strike price, so the round-terms gate stays at its missing-evidence default.

The product contains a plausible venture moment, but the financing does not yet name one. Amulet could test whether data-intensive agent teams will replace copied datasets and sandbox-local storage with sub-100-ms, copy-on-write workspaces that survive compute sessions and prevent conflicting writes. That would be a sharp experiment if the team specified a dated workload, a measurable publishing or retention threshold, and what capital buys to reach it. Private-beta access in small batches is a distribution mechanism, not a capital plan.

The strongest bull case is architectural. Hundreds of millions of E2B sandbox sessions suggest ephemeral compute is producing a real state problem, while Amulet’s content-addressed commits, per-run provenance, workspace-scoped credentials, and compare-and-swap publishing address integrity rather than merely offering another mounted volume. This also fits the translation-layer pattern I care about: durable object data on one side, disposable agent compute on the other. But Archil has raised $18 million, E2B $32 million, and Daytona at least $29 million. Amulet’s 1.9-ms p99 reads and 8.4-GB/s throughput remain self-published, and the product deliberately excludes live same-file multi-writer workloads. The technical wedge is credible; its independent value is unproven.

The commercial evidence is weaker. A $500 monthly Growth plan and $0.20 per actively used GiB-month establish a pricing surface, not a purchase. The dossier supplies no paid customer, named pilot, repeat workload, retention measure, or evidence that Amulet owns access to research labs or agent providers. I assign no value to 84 LinkedIn followers, especially when the profile still describes the prior post-sales product. The known high-price exception represented by **NanoCo** does not apply: the shared mechanism would be a technical product spreading through demanding users, but Amulet lacks NanoCo’s thousands of users, early enterprise customers, and rejected acquisition offer.

Nithik Bala and Varun Puru have relevant infrastructure résumés—Meta web infrastructure, Rockset, backend systems, and cloud internships—but the dossier does not document a shipped distributed-storage product, scaled operating result, or direct work inside an automated-research lab. They have credentials on the infrastructure side of the bridge, not receipts from both ecosystems. The two equal “Founder” titles also leave final product and financing authority unresolved, which matters more after a recent product pivot.

The published storage pricing could support a capital-efficient business, but there is no gross margin, burn, runway, revenue, or controllable cash metric from which to delete the next financing. My base case is therefore that Amulet becomes a useful storage feature inside a better-funded agent-compute platform rather than a standalone system of record. I am passing with my own money. The one verifiable flip condition is three independent agent teams paying for Growth and renewing after 90 days while using conflict-checked publishing every week; that would demonstrate that the workflow—not the AI-infrastructure story—causes durable purchases.
