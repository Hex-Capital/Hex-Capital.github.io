# Stratum Industries -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I start with three blanks: no proposed post-money valuation, no competing-term-sheet count, and no evidence that this is the first scarce check. Those blanks do not prove a YC auction, but they prevent me from underwriting non-consensus pricing. Stratum Industries may be cheap; the dossier simply gives me no basis to say so.

The product also names a mission rather than a venture moment. Reading applications, applying agency rubrics, requesting missing information, and producing cited summaries is coherent. But “clear the U.S. government backlog” spans permits, patents, drug approvals, grants, licenses, and benefits—each with different buyers, rules, error costs, and procurement paths. There is no named agency, dated deployment, accuracy threshold, or adjudication-time target that this round will test. Capital would currently finance several uncertainties at once rather than flip one card.

The strongest case is the team. Vihaan shipped document-oriented AI at Cadastral before its June 2026 acquisition by Legora; Raj reports averaging 220% of quota at Deel and has built prior automation projects. That joins real technical construction with real enterprise selling. The material gap is the government side: neither founder has documented procurement access, adjudication experience, or an agency relationship. Their LinkedIn reach and YC Launch post may produce meetings, but an introduction is not a proprietary channel and no owned workflow has yet caused a pilot, purchase, or repeat use.

The April and May 2025 federal policies create a legitimate opening, and the human-decision architecture is directionally correct for high-impact AI. The bull case is that a technically fast team with strong outbound ability lands one neglected agency workflow before larger vendors notice. It stings because GovWell’s 150 agencies prove governments will buy modernization software. But they also expose the difference: Stratum has disclosed no deployed customer, proprietary case history, accuracy result, or procurement foothold, while GovWell, Polimorphic, and Clariti already occupy overlapping channels. The desired dark pool is still a future possibility.

Delete the next financing and the model becomes harder. There is no contract value, gross margin, burn, runway, or controllable cash metric, and cross-domain adjudication carries implementation and compliance costs before repeatability is established. My 36-month base case is that Stratum either pivots into one narrower permitting or licensing workflow or remains below $1 million ARR; the broad federal-to-local adjudication platform will not survive contact with procurement and program-specific accuracy requirements.

I am passing, not declaring the company dead. Missing pre-seed traction is normal; missing price, a finite capital experiment, and proprietary agency access simultaneously is not enough for my own money. One condition flips me: a paid deployment with a named agency that processes at least 1,000 cases, cuts median adjudication time by 50% or more, and clears the agency’s documented accuracy and appeal thresholds. That single result would turn the policy story into owned workflow, technical proof, and a measurable venture moment.
