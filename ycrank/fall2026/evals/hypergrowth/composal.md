# Composal -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Verify’s strongest path is **usage-based expansion**. The initial buyer is an engineering team that needs to check customer-facing browser journeys before each release. Verify offers a discovery scan before checkout, saves journeys as reusable scenarios, and returns recordings and reproduction steps. The growth hypothesis is that teams start with a few critical journeys, rerun them as they ship, and eventually pay for more browser minutes as they widen coverage. The same job and product could travel across markets where teams can grant access to test environments. The memo gives no buyer geography or evidence of paid adoption in a second market. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Composal reports finding more than 1,500 bugs in customer apps, and the memo records an early-access rollout of its Chrome Recorder. These are early signs that teams are willing to test Verify on real workflows. The aggregate bug count leaves paid conversion, repeat use at release cadence, and account-level expansion unresolved. (Market & Traction)

Verify lists a $500 monthly plan with 3,000 browser minutes and prepaid add-ons at $1 per minute. In a **full-utilization scenario**, the subscription yields about $0.17 in revenue per included minute before browser infrastructure, inference, setup, and support costs. The memo gives no realized add-on purchases or marginal cost measurements, so contribution from greater usage remains unknown. (The Idea)

### Countercase

Activation is the first link likely to break even if teams value the tests. The YC launch and discovery scan provide a route to first value, but Composal reports that five of ten setup calls stalled on authentication or network controls. The memo gives no account-level example of a standard setup carrying a buyer through to payment. Composal also reports that nine teams it spoke with had already built QA agents internally, giving buyers a way to meet the recurring need without adopting Verify. (The Idea, Key Risks, Defensibility)

The setup stalls support an adverse acquisition score. They leave a testable question about whether dedicated test accounts and private-network connections can make activation repeatable. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo does not establish that each rollout requires bespoke work that grows in proportion to revenue. (Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can two independent engineering teams with authentication or network controls use the same documented setup without founder intervention, convert to paid Verify, and rerun saved scenarios over several normal release cycles? Two such rollouts would move the action to **Reach out** by showing that the activation barrier can be cleared and that the workload recurs on a paid account.
