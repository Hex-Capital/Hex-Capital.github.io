# Constor -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Constor has customers putting money down before there is much public evidence to admire. Five paying design partners and approximately twelve prepaid orders matter more to me than its “AI native OS” description. But the proposed post-money, competing offers, and reason this needs my particular check are all undocumented. I cannot infer an auction from YC membership. I also cannot infer a cheap entry from a small team. There is a plausible business here; the investment proposition is still missing.

The strongest asset is the bridge between drawings and working contractors. Louis has done takeoffs in his father’s insulation business; Elio previously shipped a product handling tens of thousands of medical-office calls; Dany secured the first deal. Those are operating receipts across the construction and software sides, rather than three impressive school names. Constor fits my preference for founders who can translate between systems. Approximately fifteen contractors reportedly using the product gives that bridge some commercial reality, although the paying partners, prepaid orders, and users may overlap.

The potential dark pool is the customer relationship through which drawings, corrections, and subsequent bids flow. Paying design partners supplying historical data and feedback are a useful beginning. They do not establish exclusive data rights, repeat purchasing, or a distribution advantage. Beam AI already offers overlapping HVAC and plumbing takeoffs. Constor therefore needs something that survives competitors gaining comparable extraction capabilities. Its 3D checking and trade focus could help, but neither has a documented accuracy or retention advantage. The customer’s required quantity review is especially consequential: the economic product is time saved **after checking**, not quantities generated.

The best objection to passing is that Constor may already be building exactly the modest, profitable business I say I want. The founder claims profitability from inception, customers prepaid, and A2C Isolation’s owner reports preparing more bids and winning more jobs. That deserves credit at pre-seed. It also makes the AI exception worth considering: customers are purchasing a functioning workflow rather than merely expressing interest in automation. But the dossier supplies neither the cash figures needed to substantiate independence from another round nor the measured conversion and retention that would justify overriding my financing gates. The estimated 680 hours saved in seven days does not answer what remains after support, inference, and customer review costs. There is no evidence of auction pressure, equal-founder paralysis, or dependence on eliminating human checking; I will not manufacture those red flags.

I pass on writing a personal check from this dossier. Constor’s strongest evidence concerns the founders’ ability to build and sell; its weakest concerns what the investment buys and what finite truth the proceeds reveal. No named experiment, budget, or deadline is documented. My single verifiable flip condition is a written sub-$10 million post-money offer whose proceeds fund a defined 90-day experiment: ten new HVAC contractors completing a second paid billing period at positive contribution margin after all review and support costs. That would connect price, a venture moment, and a controllable cash driver. Today, those connections remain unproven.
