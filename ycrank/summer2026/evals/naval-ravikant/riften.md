# Riften -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

An unknown founder is not a bad founder. It is an unpriced variable. Riften discloses no founder identity, prior product, customer work, research, repository, or domain artifact. The YC profile proves that someone has framed the problem; it does not prove specific knowledge acquired by routing production traffic or training models. I assign the founder prior exactly—uncertainty, not condemnation.

The proposed progression is coherent: replace one environment variable, route requests to cheaper models, collect production outcomes, and train private open-weight models that earn more traffic. The problem is that only the first step is evidenced, and it is the copyable one. Not Diamond already trains custom routers from customer evaluations, OpenRouter operates a much larger model gateway, and an open-source framework offers more than 16 routing methods. Riften has published no benchmark, customer dataset, proprietary model, or deployment result. When the code is copied, the durable asset remains hypothetical.

The strongest bull case is that the gateway is merely the acquisition wedge. If Riften captures high-quality outcome and escalation data inside customer workflows, it could convert commodity routing into proprietary models with measurable cost and quality advantages. The one-variable integration reduces trial friction, open weights broaden the available model supply, and the overall inference market was estimated at $106.15 billion in 2025. But this is a resemblance to compounding, not evidence of compounding. LLMRouterBench found that several routing systems failed to beat simple baselines, and adversarial suffixes can steer routers toward expensive models. The technical claim needs production proof precisely where competitors are weakest.

The market is broad enough to permit movement from routing into evaluation, training, hosting, or private inference. That earns more than the absent-market prior, but the cited $254.98 billion 2030 forecast is a parent-market estimate rather than a causal map from Riften’s initial buyer to adjacent spend. Economics are even less developed: there is no disclosed payer, price, revenue, signed agreement, or usage. Routed inference and private-model hosting are inferred business models, not transactions.

Riften also lacks the recurring mechanism I look for beneath the stated criteria. Customers appear to contribute private evidence to their own models, not permissionless supply that makes the system better for other customers. There is no observed referral, contribution, liquidity, or shared-data loop. No founder-integrity, artificial-deadline, or reassurance-seeking red flag is disclosed, but the known exceptions do not rescue the company: the dossier shows neither an already-forming network, a demonstrated capital-efficient prototype, nor founder action strong enough to override an illegible wedge.

My 36-month base case is that Riften either shuts down or pivots into an enterprise AI evaluation and observability gateway after private-model training proves too data-hungry for most customers. I pass today. One result flips the decision: a published production cohort showing that at least five paying customers trained private models which maintained their quality threshold while reducing inference cost by 50% or more over three consecutive months. That would turn the claimed flywheel into an earned asset.
