# Agnost AI -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The decisive missing fact is what happened when something blocked the founders. The dossier shows capable builders, but not a named obstacle followed by a dated workaround and observable result. Processing more than 1 million events daily is evidence that they shipped; it is not evidence of what they did when the obvious route failed. For a people-first decision, that distinction matters. I cannot infer resourcefulness from Cisco, Microsoft, IIT Madras, or open-source credentials.

The technical proof is much stronger. Agnost already ingests conversations through an SDK, a skill, and OpenTelemetry; connects failures to events and tool calls; and tests proposed pull requests against historical conversations. The earlier product processed roughly 2.5 million MCP calls monthly in January 2026, while the current product reported more than 1 million events daily by July. This is a fierce-nerd signal: the founders built an operating system before waiting for the category to settle. It also matches the useful unstated pattern of a concrete proof spike before the round.

The harder question is who wants the crude product now. Google, Exa Labs, and Corgi Insurance are named, and a Google engineer describes an integration with MCP Toolbox for Databases. That is more informative than the 77 Hacker News points or 2,885 LinkedIn followers. But the dossier does not say which teams repeatedly inspect Agnost’s findings, whether anyone pays $49 or $499 monthly, or whether a customer merged a generated pull request. One million events could represent deep use or merely cheap ingestion. Pricing is not revenue, and event volume is not retention.

The move from MCP analytics in January to conversation-derived product analytics in July suggests the founders can change routes while keeping the goal of agent improvement. But the dossier never identifies the observation that caused the change. It could be rapid learning; it could be repositioning into a larger-sounding market. Their prior analytics and data-pipeline work is relevant, yet there is no documented founder-originated collision with this problem. Handling sensitive conversations, VPC deployments, retention policies, and uncertain automated fixes contains real schlep, but the evidence does not show the founders encountered and defeated any particular part of it.

The strongest bull case is that this is already a developer product with substantial production throughput, two childhood friends who have known each other for eight years, founder-led outreach, and named sophisticated users. There is no dependence on a ceremonial launch or an unspecified distribution partner, and the founders have not delegated the product itself. The bear case stings more: LangSmith, Braintrust, Arize, and Langfuse can absorb conversation analytics into suites customers already use, while Agnost has disclosed neither paid pull nor a proprietary cross-customer data advantage. Hiring a Growth Engineer before demonstrating repeat paid usage risks trying to amplify something the founders have not yet proved people need.

None of the known exceptions supplies an override. There is a working product, but not yet unmistakable user behavior strong enough to replace the failed resourcefulness gate. My one flip condition is a dated customer case showing that, after a specific product or distribution blocker, the founders shipped a workaround within two weeks and three named customers subsequently renewed while merging Agnost-generated fixes. Until then, I would not write the check.
