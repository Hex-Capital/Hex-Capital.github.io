# Contour -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Contour’s interesting choice is to own the consulting firm. If Cortex works, the owner captures the labor savings and retains the customer relationship instead of selling another software subscription. That fits my preference for businesses that own their workflows and for software that reorganizes neglected physical-world operations. But proposed ownership is not owned access. Contour has no documented completed acquisition, customer conversion, or proprietary project dataset. The website’s invitation to practice owners establishes a sourcing channel; it does not establish that anyone has sold.

Before writing a check, I need the price and the experiment. Contour’s post-money valuation, competing offers, and financing structure are undisclosed. YC participation tells me none of those things; I will not invent an auction. Likewise, acquiring firms and embedding engineers describes an operating strategy, but the dossier supplies no dated, measurable test that this round funds. Can Cortex reduce the fully loaded cost of an accepted permit application while preserving professional accountability? That is a candidate venture moment. It is not yet a documented capital plan.

The strongest case against passing is that Contour could acquire a functioning base business and make AI the upside option. Environmental consultants already perform work that customers need, and permanent ownership could preserve the local relationships that an external software vendor struggles to reach. The broader market was estimated at $27.0 billion in 2024, though that is not Contour’s addressable market. Even if Paces or Transect matches document-generation capabilities, Contour could retain value through acquired client relationships and execution. That argument stings because it makes model commoditization less decisive. It still requires an acquired business with demonstrated cash economics.

Delete the next financing from the model. What remains? The dossier provides no acquisition price, seller financing, gross margin, debt service, or holding-company burn. I cannot conclude that this requires serial fundraising, but I also cannot credit a profitable path merely because the intended acquisitions are service businesses. On-site engineers and professional review have to be paid from somewhere. Cortex’s relevant metric is cash earned per accepted engagement after review and rework, not documents generated. No operating evidence establishes that metric yet.

Agrawal’s work building enterprise search at Rezolve AI provides a concrete software-building receipt. Lubar’s Lazard experience is relevant preparation for transactions, but it does not establish that he has acquired and operated a consulting practice. No founder has documented environmental-permitting operating experience in this dossier. That leaves the proposed bridge between software, acquisitions, and regulated professional work only partially evidenced. Four founders do not establish an equal-control committee, and Simpson is identified as CEO; I would not manufacture that red flag. Nor does the dossier claim that regulatory errors simply disappear with scale.

I pass at **29/100**. The known exceptions to my financing gates depend on demonstrated conversion, operating results, or a prior exit paired with an explicit capital structure; Contour supplies none of those overrides. The ownership model earns attention, but resemblance to a business I want is not evidence that this one works. My one verifiable condition for reopening the investment is a closed first acquisition whose six-month accounts show retained client revenue covering practice expenses, embedded engineering, debt service, and holding-company burn without another equity injection. That would turn the central claim into an operating fact; price would still determine whether I buy.
