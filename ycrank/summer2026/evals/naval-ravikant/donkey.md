# Donkey -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Benjamin Martindale has already paid tuition in the exact market. Prelude OS acquired 31 paying Chinese factories in 39 days and became cash-flow positive; he speaks Mandarin, advised Sany and Sinoboom on overseas expansion, and is now driving between independent US distributors rather than learning import trade from a spreadsheet. James Tan is building the customs-record classification that separates factories from shells and forwarders. This is specific knowledge revealed through action, not credentials arranged around a fashionable idea.

The product turns fragmented factory supply into a price-discoverable transaction. A buyer submits a photograph; Donkey identifies the manufacturer, quotes one delivered-duty-paid price, takes title, inspects the goods, clears customs, and delivers them. The first week produced $110,000 in quotes, including one 15% below the buyer’s existing supplier from the same factory. Purchase orders described as “signing now” are not revenue, but they demonstrate that the price discrepancy is real enough to initiate transactions.

The difficult question is what remains after Sourcify, Flexport, or Zetwerk copies the software. Donkey has inspectors across seven Chinese provinces, assumes title and credit risk, and says completed orders accumulate factory-pricing and reliability data. Those are better foundations than an AI interface, but the dossier does not establish a proprietary dataset, superior classification benchmark, exclusive supplier access, or improving unit economics. Operational complexity can deter competitors; it can also imprison the company inside proportional labor and working capital.

That distinction defines the outcome. The unstated-pattern fit is real: Donkey is exposing opaque offline supply and making prices discoverable. But it is not yet a permissionless network. Buyers do not create supply, suppliers do not acquire buyers, and each order still requires inspection, financing, customs work, and delivery. My 36-month base case is therefore $10–25 million of annualized merchandise volume but less than $3 million of annualized gross profit, with growth still constrained by credit capacity and operations rather than compounding user leverage.

The strongest opposing case is that the trading operation is merely the data-acquisition wedge. Martindale has already sold factory software quickly, Tan is resolving entities from shipment records, and every settled order could improve pricing, defects, lead times, and supplier selection. If those observations become proprietary and measurably improve every subsequent quote, Donkey could turn manual trade into a compounding intelligence network. That possibility matters, but a future data flywheel is not a present moat. None of the known-exception patterns overrides the missing proof: there is neither demonstrated self-reinforcing adoption nor evidence that exceptional capital efficiency neutralizes the balance-sheet burden.

I see no disclosed integrity problem, artificial deadline, or founder dependence on reassurance; the pivot from Prelude instead looks like learning transferred into a larger transaction. The stale tariff example is nevertheless a precision warning: when the product sells landed-cost certainty, an expired surcharge directly weakens trust. I am neutral today. The verifiable flip condition is ten or more repeat buyers generating at least $10 million of annualized volume while quote accuracy and gross margin improve without proportional growth in inspection headcount or working capital.
