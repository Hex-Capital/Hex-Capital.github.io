# Reconero -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Reconero’s strongest path. A records, dispatch, or evidence-platform vendor could buy Reconero’s workflow to add timestamp-linked video analysis, department-specific form completion, and policy guidance to products it already sells to agencies. Reconero claims those capabilities. The buyer rationale is an inference. Ownership could be worth more than building, licensing, or partnering if Reconero demonstrates that its integrations and form mappings can be deployed across agencies and generate enough incremental revenue or reduce deployment costs. The memo leaves that benefit, buyer interest, and comparable acquisition prices unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The memo records a public launch and YC backing. Reconero reports consulting more than 100 officers, and its CEO reports that several police units tested an earlier ReconeroRMS beta. That progress makes a paid production pilot plausible at seed. The capital required to run it and roll out across agencies remains unknown. Control of the core IP and transferability of agency contracts and data rights are also unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** Under the shared scenario, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million gross before investor taxes. It requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% case requires $88.3 million of enterprise value. A $25 million sale at year eight yields $600,000 (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×), both using simple pro rata allocation after that debt assumption. Reconero’s actual debt, fees, and senior claims are unknown. The sale prices are sensitivities, and the memo provides no basis for a realizable price range. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

If agency adoption plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. Sponsor financing lacks demonstrated cash generation. A secondary lacks evidenced share demand and holder eligibility. An IPO lacks evidence of operating scale and reporting readiness. Dividends or buybacks lack evidence of excess cash and a funded payout. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Axon already bundles report drafting into its evidence platform, and Code Four offers source-linked drafting across camera vendors and integrates with records and dispatch systems. A randomized trial of a competing tool found no time savings, while Reconero has disclosed no measured improvement. A buyer could therefore see little incremental value in acquiring the workflow. These are concrete substitutes and a reason to wait. They establish neither a structural closing barrier nor a supported sale price that would trigger the proceeds gate. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production pilot show enough incremental value over an agency’s existing workflow to support a strategic purchase? Move to Reach out if the pilot measures officer time saved after review and corrections, maintains report accuracy, and supplies paid pricing and deployment costs that support buyer economics consistent with the modeled proceeds hurdle. Otherwise, stay on Watch.
