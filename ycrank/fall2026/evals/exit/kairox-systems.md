# Kairox Systems -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a maritime robotics integrator. Kairox says Tempest combines aerial and underwater operation in one vehicle and reports Marine Corps engagement and concept validation with military end users. An integrator could buy Tempest to offer customers a single system if field results show it reduces operating and logistics costs. The memo documents a $21.6 million procurement award in adjacent civilian marine robotics. That establishes adjacent customer spending. Acquisition interest and pricing remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

A customer-funded field evaluation that measures operating costs against separate fleets would strengthen the acquisition case. MassRobotics residency and reported accelerator funding of $500,000 provide some support for early development. The trial budget, committed funding, and qualification schedule remain unknown. Transfer rights, required consents, holder eligibility, and independent liquidity routes are also unevidenced. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The scenarios below are illustrative. Both sales assume cash at closing within eight years, $5 million in combined debt and deal deductions, a pro rata waterfall with no further senior claims, and full holder participation.

| Scenario | Enterprise value | Distributable equity | Holder cash |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | $50M | $45M | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M | $95M | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private with cash reinvested | — | — | $0 realized |

The strong sale clears the hurdle under those assumptions. If the holder can sell only half its stake, cash at closing falls to $1.425 million. The memo supplies no acquisition price evidence to establish either sale case as realizable.

### Countercase

Customers can keep operating separate fleets. Kairox reports laboratory-stage validation, and the memo describes procurement as a future milestone. If Tempest delivers little savings in the field, an integrator has little reason to pay for it. The memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a credible price range that falls materially short, so the Pass gate remains untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

Would a maritime robotics integrator’s buy-versus-build analysis support a price of roughly $88.3 million or more in enterprise value if Tempest demonstrates measurable operating savings in a customer-funded field evaluation? A buyer-side analysis grounded in those results that clears the hurdle under plausible holder terms would move the action to Reach out.
