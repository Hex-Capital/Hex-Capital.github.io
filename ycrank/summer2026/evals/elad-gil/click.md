# Click -- Elad Gil Evaluation

MCP became a standard interface with more than 10,000 active public servers by December 2025, creating demand from ChatGPT and Claude users for broader data access; Click becomes large only if aggregating external connectors produces materially better retrieval and lower integration cost than native platforms or individual providers. That is the controlling belief. The dossier supplies no accuracy, latency, cost, or retention threshold—and no present evidence that Click clears one.

The market change is real. MCP moved from Anthropic’s November 2024 launch to OpenAI remote-server support in May 2025, making one-install access to Exa, Crustdata, Google Maps, Reddit, X, YouTube, and financial datasets technically feasible. But the same standard lowers integration costs for competitors and customers. OpenAI already provides native web search, while Exa, Tavily, Linkup, and Parallel have collectively raised substantial capital. The discontinuity expands adoption but has not visibly improved Click’s margins or bargaining power.

The strongest bull case is breadth. A technically credible solo founder with Ethereum consensus, formal-verification, and agent-context experience could turn cross-provider routing into infrastructure: observe query performance, select the best connector, and make every additional provider improve the system. The $49 monthly plan offers a clean self-service wedge, and one installation is simpler than contracting with ten vendors. This could become useful distribution before the market crystallizes.

The current product, however, owns neither the underlying data nor an end-to-end customer workflow. Provider pricing and access can change; requests and results pass through third parties; and no proprietary routing dataset, evaluation loop, permissions layer, or operational action system is documented. Stronger native search and easier MCP installation therefore compress rather than deepen the present product. Click’s shifts from assistant frontend to operations automation to research connectors reinforce the concern that it is searching for a durable workflow around an interface layer.

There is also no product-market-pull evidence yet. The 157 LinkedIn followers and NVIDIA Inception membership establish visibility, not usage; a published $49 price establishes monetization intent, not revenue. Pre-seed status makes this absence unsurprising, but it cannot satisfy a market-first underwriting gate. Asgaonkar’s technical background is relevant execution evidence, yet the dossier documents neither prior startup learning nor the costly commitment or personal need that would support a founder override. The known talent exception does not apply: this is credible adjacent expertise, not scarce frontier technology already built.

I would pass. My 36-month base case is that Click pivots back toward operations automation or becomes inactive as connector aggregation is absorbed by model platforms and well-funded retrieval providers. The verifiable flip condition is ten business customers each paying at least $2,000 per month and renewing after six months because Click’s measured routing quality or integration economics outperform both native OpenAI search and direct provider connections.
