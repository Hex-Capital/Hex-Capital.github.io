# Osseus -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

$47.90 billion of PLM spending gives **Osseus** a real economic surface, but **Osseus** is consequence-rich and capital-poor: its strongest dimension is the possibility of replacing fragmented robotics engineering memory with a permissioned, machine-readable decision graph, while its weakest is a rollout model requiring no disclosed $50 million technical milestone. The economic mechanism Osseus could break is the human coordination tax across CAD, electronics, software, tests, and suppliers. That matters. It is not yet civilizationally distinct from better PLM.

**OpenAI** (2019) is the high rung on consequence because success meant automating a meaningful fraction of economically valuable cognition, not improving one engineering workflow. Osseus shares the load-bearing idea that accumulated machine context can replace expensive human reconstruction: Osseus anchors findings to parts, nets, connectors, dimensions, and revision evidence. Osseus diverges because the dossier names no labor pool eliminated, robotics category unlocked, or incumbent regime made obsolete. “Intelligent design platform” currently means fewer engineering mistakes, not a world-state comparable to OpenAI.

**DoorDash** (2013) is the low rung on required-capital fit because ordinary venture financing could fund its early software and logistics network; capital profile alone would never have triggered my check. Osseus sits even closer to that mechanism: forward-deployed integrations, custom rollout pricing, managed subscriptions, customer model keys, and VPC deployment describe enterprise-software economics. Osseus discloses no foundation-model training program, hardware fleet, plant, or scientific milestone requiring more than $50 million. I am over-resourced for the deal.

**CoLab’s $72 million Series C** shows how Osseus’s technical position could compound over 18 months: every connected CAD revision, failed test, supplier constraint, and approved finding can make Osseus’s graph harder to replace. Osseus could build valuable workflow depth. But connector maintenance across CAD, electronics, code, simulation, suppliers, and issue systems is integration breadth, not technical risk that repels consensus capital. CoLab already has AI peer checking, while Duro and Bild are adding adjacent automation. Osseus requires execution and trust, not a falsifiable scientific miracle.

**Anish Mariathasan’s biomedical-engineering and computer-vision background** and Ayush Patel’s Apple agentic-QA work give Osseus credible exposure to physical systems, but they do not document defiance of expert consensus. Osseus would move from 8/20 into an invest-level founder-defiance score if Mariathasan could demonstrate that robotics experts reject a specific technical thesis—perhaps that cross-domain dependency graphs can predict failures before simulation—and then prove that thesis on real robot programs. Today Osseus is entering a direction that CoLab and Duro commercialized during the preceding 12–18 months. Experts may be wrong, but Osseus has not yet told me which experts are wrong.

**Anish Mariathasan’s prior compliance-AI company** is evidence of entrepreneurial exposure, not evidence of a documented pivot or corrected thesis. **Slash** earned an exception because its Kanye West pivot exposed learning rate under pressure; Osseus provides no comparable “we believed X, observed Y, and rebuilt around Z” event. Osseus therefore receives the absent-evidence default rather than a penalty for being young. Learning rate above everything else must be demonstrated, not inferred from two Imperial degrees.

**CoLab’s $72 million financing** makes my 36-month forecast concrete: Osseus will pivot from robotics-specific design intelligence into a broader hardware-R&D knowledge layer and reach roughly $1 million–$3 million ARR, but Osseus will remain a feature-contested enterprise product rather than the default engineering system of record. The mechanism is straightforward. Custom connectors pull Osseus toward whichever adjacent hardware customers will pay, while CoLab, Duro, and Bild compress standalone review functionality. Integration demand broadens the market and dilutes the original robotics wedge.

**Osseus’s permissioned, version-aware artifact graph** is the strongest opposing case. If Osseus becomes the trusted context layer joining designs, software, validation results, suppliers, and owners, customer-specific history could create Glean-like switching costs; DoorDash also proves that lack of science risk need not preclude an exceptional outcome. The objection stings because robotics complexity is rising and Osseus’s default-deny memory promotion addresses a real adoption barrier. The dossier still shows invited access but no named pilot, repeat usage, revenue, or structural barrier, while three funded competitors already occupy adjacent territory. One condition flips my verdict: within 12 months, Osseus must show three named robotics customers repeatedly using the graph to identify cross-domain failures that their PLM and review tools missed.
