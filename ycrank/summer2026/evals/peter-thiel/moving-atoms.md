# Moving Atoms -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The contested truth is that robot policies can be evaluated faster and more broadly by an action-conditioned world model than by physical testing or manually constructed simulation. What would prove it wrong? Atom 1’s predicted failures would fail to correlate with failures on real robots. Moving Atoms has not yet run that decisive test. Atom Harness scored 56.6 across 198 Physics-IQ cases, second overall, but Physics-IQ measures generated-video prediction—not deployed policy performance—and the submission remains under review as a composite harness using Seedance 2.5 and Claude Opus 5. This is a falsifiable secret with an experiment adjacent to it, not yet an experiment of it.

The founders are the strongest evidence. Shehryar and Ahmad Saroya are brothers with a long relationship rather than co-founders assembled for YC; Ahmad left Oxford during his second year, while Shehryar brings mathematics, computer science, and production video-model experience from TikTok. That suggests commitment and technical coherence. But the dossier provides no ownership, compensation, or explicit evidence that the core world-model technology is proprietary and built entirely in-house. A committed team can discover a secret; commitment cannot substitute for one.

The initial market is sensibly narrow: robot-policy teams submitting a checkpoint or inference endpoint plus example episodes for automated evaluation. Yet a beachhead is not a monopoly. No proprietary dataset, exclusive customer relationship, retention mechanism, or measured technical advantage is documented. More damagingly, the current benchmark result depends on external model vendors, while NVIDIA Cosmos, Google’s robotics models, Genesis AI, and heavily financed robot-foundation-model companies can overlap the capability. If Seedance supplies the imagined world and Claude grades it, Moving Atoms presently owns the orchestration layer. That is a feature boundary, not a last-mover position.

The bull case is sharper than the current score: robot evaluation could become a critical layer of the AI production stack, and every customer rollout could generate paired observations, actions, failures, and labels unavailable to general model vendors. The brothers could use a vendor-neutral harness as the wedge, accumulate proprietary failure data, and eventually train the model they currently assemble. This would fit the unstated pattern of investing across the complete AI stack. But the flywheel is inferred; there are no disclosed design partners, repeat evaluations, data rights, or paid contracts. The exception for a verified zero-to-one technical event also does not apply: an open, disputed video benchmark cannot override the robot-validity gap.

Economically, the company has an engagement shape but not yet a revenue engine. Founder-led outreach to a limited design-partner cohort identifies the buyer and required inputs, but pricing, sales-cycle length, integration cost, gross margin, and conversion are absent. The prior shift from hosted MolmoAct2 access to Atom 1 evaluation compounds the uncertainty: it may show rapid learning, or it may show that the product has not stabilized around a durable customer need. The large physical-AI TAM does not answer whether this layer captures value rather than transferring it to model providers.

I pass today. Moving Atoms has serious founders and a potentially important question, but the lowest-scoring fact is also the decisive one: there is no demonstrated monopoly mechanism between a benchmark harness and capitalized upstream competitors. The verifiable flip condition is three independent robotics customers showing that Atom 1 predicts real-world policy failures materially better or faster than Isaac Sim or physical testing, signing annual contracts, and granting Moving Atoms reusable rights to the resulting evaluation data. That would convert a fashionable world-model claim into a proprietary feedback system.
