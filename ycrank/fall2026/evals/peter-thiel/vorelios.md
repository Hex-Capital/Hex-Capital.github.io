# Vorelios -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Vorelios has demonstrated a computational result; it has not yet identified the secret that makes this a great business. Its strongest possible proposition is that aerospace engineers can make consequential design decisions using predictions that arrive in milliseconds instead of waiting hours for conventional simulation. That proposition is testable: give the model unfamiliar aircraft geometries and ask whether its predictions preserve the engineering decisions a validated solver or physical experiment would produce. The dossier reports agreement with solver outputs, not that decisive test. Nor does it identify a prevailing belief that Vorelios uniquely rejects. Luminary and PhysicsX already believe physics AI can transform engineering.

The technical evidence nevertheless deserves attention. Vorelios reports 6.37% pressure error and 3.81% skin-friction error on the NASA Common Research Model, with inference times across aircraft benchmarks of 1.1–31 milliseconds on an H100. It also reports outperforming Transolver-3 and AB-UPT. Those are concrete results that could be challenged, rather than an aspiration to “solve engineering.” They give proprietary technology the strongest affirmative evidence among my five criteria. But superiority to research models does not establish superiority to the alternatives an aerospace buyer would purchase. Luminary already offers SHIFT models for aircraft wings. Vorelios has a recognizable initial application; it has not demonstrated a mechanism for owning it.

The strongest objection to passing is that the founders may already have built the beginning of that mechanism. Internally generated simulations could let a two-person team improve its models without waiting for customers to contribute scarce engineering data. McNeil’s physics background and Kapadia’s 2025–2026 autonomy research at Florida are relevant preparation. This also fits my interest in AI extending into physical production. But the corpus’s exclusivity and expanding coverage remain hypotheses. My exception for an independently demonstrated technical breakthrough cannot be invoked merely because the company publishes encouraging benchmarks. There is also no documented customer behavior or realized economics that independently resolves the competitive question.

The founding structure remains unknown rather than defective. Shared University of Florida affiliations do not establish that McNeil and Kapadia previously shipped together, and the dossier does not establish full-time status, ownership, or economic exposure. I would apply the stated cautious prior. Nothing here supports accusing them of convenience matching, outsourced engineering, or divided commitment. Likewise, the multiphysics roadmap creates a coordination concern only if Vorelios must deliver aerodynamics, heat transfer, and structural mechanics together to win its first customer. A useful aerodynamics product could stand alone; the team should prove that before making “everything else” part of the investment case.

The commercial problem is more basic than missing pre-seed revenue. Aerospace teams and a founder contact form identify a prospect and an initial contact route, but establish neither a repeatable sales process nor delivery economics. No price, contract structure, or expansion mechanism supports the arithmetic of a power-law outcome. The $14.2 billion CAE market estimate cannot supply those missing links. Faster engineering may create enormous value while existing simulation platforms, customers, or competing model developers capture most of it.

I would pass on the present evidence, while keeping the technical result open to revision. My single flip condition is an independently verified paid aerospace deployment in which engineers repeatedly use Vorelios on previously unseen geometries, meet pre-agreed engineering tolerances, and choose it over a named commercial alternative. That would turn benchmark performance into evidence of a valuable position. Today, Vorelios has a plausible technical advantage and an unproved route from that advantage to ownership of a market.
