# Orvixo -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Ethan Phillips and Kolya Schwinge are Cambridge physicists attempting technically difficult factory planning without a capital-intensive technical moat. That pairing is Orvixo’s signature: Orvixo is strongest where live factory constraints make the software harder than a commodity AI wrapper, and weakest where the roadmap requires neither a $50M check nor a scientific miracle. Orvixo may build useful software. Useful is not the same as “too important not to do.”

**Commonwealth Fusion** is the high rung on technical risk because fusion required investor-repelling physics, a $115M Series A, and falsifiable breakthroughs before commercialization. Orvixo shares the need to solve interacting constraints—machines, labor, materials, tooling, and capacity—but Orvixo names no comparable breakthrough, patent, proprietary dataset, or customer-validated reliability result. Orvixo therefore has real systems difficulty, not consensus-repelling science. Integration complexity is not a miracle.

**DoorDash** is the low rung on required capital: capital-light logistics lacked a scientific milestone, and only extraordinary ambition and control of consumer-commerce flow justified the exception. Orvixo shows the same missing-capital pattern without the exception signal. Orvixo appears to be founder-led enterprise software sold through negotiated deployments; nothing in the dossier requires more than $50M before product-market fit, much less the plant, fleet, model-training, or drug-pipeline scale where my capital changes the outcome.

The **$1.744B global APS market** confines Orvixo’s stated consequence to improving an existing planning category rather than collapsing an economic regime. Over 18 months, Orvixo could compound factory knowledge through constraint libraries and model-change histories, making each deployment faster and each schedule more reliable. But the dossier explicitly provides no evidence that Orvixo controls or can reuse those assets across customers. Without that compounding mechanism, Orvixo remains an optimization play for planners—potentially valuable, but closer to 20–30% better scheduling than to removing an incumbent system.

Ethan Phillips brings quantum-computing research and Kolya Schwinge brings applied ML plus an IFS engineering role, but Orvixo provides no documented instance where either founder contradicted factory-planning orthodoxy. The score flips upward if Orvixo demonstrates that incumbent APS specialists are wrong about the need for specialist model configuration—for example, if factory operators can encode and safely change production constraints in ordinary language without consultants. Until Orvixo proves that claim in a live plant, two physicists entering manufacturing are outsiders, not yet instigators. Experts extrapolate the past, but merely lacking factory experience is not evidence of seeing the future.

**Slash** earned the learning-rate exception through a specific, publicly documented pivot; Orvixo offers no comparable “we were wrong about X” moment. Orvixo’s movement from physics, astronomy, and enterprise ML into factory optimization suggests intellectual range, but biography is not demonstrated learning rate. With no prior exit, product pivot, or thesis revision in the dossier, Orvixo receives the absent-evidence default rather than credit for a story that has not happened.

**IFS**, Kolya Schwinge’s former employer, is the most likely endpoint: by July 2029, I expect IFS to acquire Orvixo for under $100M after Orvixo reaches $2–5M ARR in factory scheduling software. Orvixo’s constraint-modeling capability can produce a credible specialist product, while Orvixo’s integration burden and lack of a structural moat make an incumbent’s installed base more valuable than remaining independent. The likely outcome is a useful APS capability absorbed into an enterprise suite—not a new industrial operating system.

**Glean** is the strongest objection because enterprise information asymmetry became consequential enough to override a conventional founder profile; DoorDash also proves that capital-light software can become enormous when it reorganizes economic flow. Orvixo’s bull case combines a 20.4% market CAGR, Cambridge physics talent, Kolya’s IFS experience, and natural-language configuration that could turn every planner into an optimization engineer. The objection fails today because Orvixo has no disclosed customer, revenue, deployment result, reusable cross-factory dataset, or measured advantage over Siemens, Asprova, or Sight Machine. I would flip to Invest if three named multi-site manufacturers deployed Orvixo across at least ten plants and reported greater than 30% throughput or schedule-adherence improvement from a reusable Orvixo constraint layer. Until then, I pass. Make a great company and the money takes care of itself; Orvixo has not yet shown that the economic mechanism breaks when Orvixo wins.
