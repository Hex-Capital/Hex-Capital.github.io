# Whitespace -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Alex Tung grew up around wholesale distribution, advised distributors at BCG, and helped Ankar reach seven-figure ARR; Leon Yao combines the same lived exposure with a UCL machine-learning MSc and logistics AI work at InstaDeep. Whitespace is a specific-knowledge-heavy, network-light vertical AI operator. Whitespace has founders who know where the work is buried, but every customer currently remains an island. Knowledge can produce a good business. Networks produce asymmetric outcomes.

**Stack Overflow** is the high rung for specific knowledge: developers built for developers after living the problem publicly. Whitespace shares the lived-problem mechanism—both founders come from wholesale families, Tung worked with distributors, and Yao built logistics AI—but Whitespace diverges where the evidence becomes artifact rather than biography. Yao has an IROS paper and a prior product serving 1,000 users, yet Whitespace has no public repository, organic builder community, or years-old wholesale product; three months earlier, Yao was launching an AI slide maker. Founder-market fit is unusually strong. Founder-product proof is not yet Stack Overflow strong.

**Perplexity** exposes the low-rung network mechanism: each user can receive value without making the product directly more valuable for the previous user. Perplexity escaped that weakness through permissionless product distribution, extreme retrieval expertise, and pre-consensus AI timing. Whitespace is even more single-sided: Kingfisher Direct’s corrections remain private to Kingfisher Direct, and Whitespace explicitly does not train across customers. No marketplace, shared data asset, supplier graph, or user-created corpus compounds with adoption. The Perplexity exception does not carry because Whitespace also lacks Perplexity’s product-led distribution and category-timing advantage.

Two named customers—Kingfisher Direct and Warwick—show that Whitespace’s code can produce 80% productivity gains, but code leverage is not the same as permissionless distribution. Over 18 months, Whitespace must integrate SAP, NetSuite, Epicor, Infor, Dynamics, Sage, and several CRMs while calibrating approval thresholds customer by customer. That architecture risks converting software into implementation labor. Whitespace’s “Book a call” funnel, founder-led introductions, and private customer configurations contradict the unstated portfolio pattern behind Twitter, Notion, and Replit: software that distributes before salespeople ask permission.

The $60 trillion wholesale market gives Whitespace more pivot-room than the narrow label suggests: order entry can expand into inventory planning, procurement, quoting, customer service, and accounting. Whitespace would move into the top market rung if one reusable data model could unlock those adjacent workflows without another integration project for every customer. Today the serviceable market remains unquantified, competitors such as Proton.ai, Distro, and Stockline already occupy adjacent wedges, and the recent slide-maker pivot leaves the operating-system claim unproven. The ocean is large. Whitespace has not yet shown that one boat can traverse it.

**Twitter** passed the long-term-partner test because Jack Dorsey shipped while Ev Williams carried a clean prior operating record. Whitespace offers a credible parallel: Tung and Yao have known each other for 12 years, lived and worked together, and bring complementary commercial and technical energy. Tung’s Ankar record and Yao’s IROS authorship demonstrate intelligence and execution. Whitespace provides no comparable evidence about prior-team treatment or completed ventures, so integrity receives the neutral default rather than an inferred halo. Play long-term games with long-term people—but verify that they have already played one.

Kingfisher Direct and Warwick make my 36-month forecast concrete: by July 2029, Whitespace will reach roughly $4 million to $8 million in ARR selling implementation-assisted ERP agents to wholesale distributors, but Whitespace will remain a vertical SaaS operator rather than becoming a marketplace or network. Domain-specific knowledge should win enough workflows to create a real business. Customer-isolated learning and integration-heavy distribution should cap the leverage geometry before it becomes a defining platform.

Distro, Proton.ai, Stockline, and Fresho make the bear case sting: Whitespace could remain below $1 million ARR as two founders drown in ERP maintenance, agent errors, and procurement cycles while incumbents copy the visible workflows. The three-month pivot from slide generation strengthens that objection, and no Known Exception rescues Whitespace—there is no Anchorage-like regulatory moat or Perplexity-like permissionless pull. The evidence preventing a Pass is that Whitespace already names two customers, reports two-to-four-week deployments, and claims three-month payback rather than presenting only a prototype. If Whitespace reaches 25 live distributors with at least 120% net revenue retention while keeping median deployment below four weeks, I move to Invest.
