# Agent Relay -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest fit. Agent Relay shipped messaging and searchable session history across coding tools and teammates’ machines. The company describes engineers manually copying decisions and handoffs between agent sessions and Slack. The seed-stage hypothesis is that automating those handoffs makes it economical for teams to run more agents across tools than engineers could coordinate manually. The memo documents the shipped workflow. The market opening remains an inference. (The Idea)

Pomerado reports using Agent Relay to coordinate 80+ agents across 38,000+ messages and claims 2–4× faster product improvement. The message volume supports actual use. The speed claim leaves open how much engineer time the relay saved at equivalent quality after review, retries, and failed handoffs. A founder reports willingness to pay from a second, unnamed prospect. Payment, renewal, and whether either team previously had to limit parallel work remain unverified. This is enough evidence to justify a seed-stage trial with target buyers. (Market & Traction)

The founders bring relevant engineering leadership and integrations experience, and the team shipped a cross-provider SDK before launching team messaging. For a scale scenario, assume the paid unit is a team workspace and more connected agents drive upgrades. Contribution per team would be workspace revenue less hosting, search, support, and onboarding costs. The memo identifies paid desktop and enterprise offerings, with prices, conversion, and costs per team still unknown. (Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Agent Relay may improve an existing engineering workflow without making a previously uneconomic use viable. Its strongest potential reason to pay is accumulated, searchable handoff context across tools, but the memo provides no retention, migration, or buyer preference evidence under functional parity. Native messaging already covers Claude Code’s own agent teams, and the open A2A standard could make cross-tool messaging easier for well-funded platforms to add. That leaves Path B’s parity gate open and outreach unwarranted. Path A has no independently supported case because the memo identifies no scarce rail Agent Relay controls. (Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent engineering teams that previously capped cross-tool agent work repeatedly pay for Agent Relay when equivalent messaging and search are available elsewhere? A costed trial should compare engineer minutes per quality-accepted handoff against manual coordination, including review, retries, and exceptions, then offer a functionally equivalent alternative at comparable pricing. Newly viable workflows and repeat paid preference would support Reach out if the updated score clears 65. A trial showing that labor savings fail to unlock new use or that buyers readily switch at parity would move the action to Pass.
