# Ekpa -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Ekpa’s $3.4M in linked assets reveals a regulated-finance experiment without a clearly empowered human at the center. Ekpa scores highest as an unsexy-regulated swing and lowest on founder authority: two MIT founders offer the market suggestions with exact share counts, yet neither Anant Asthana nor Yuga Patel is identified as CEO. That pairing makes Ekpa a sharp regulatory edge wrapped around a soft organizational center. I can tolerate strange. I cannot tolerate not knowing who breaks the tie when the strange thing catches fire.

**SpaceX** made capital intensity and regulatory difficulty compound into a moat because the physical system became harder to reproduce with every launch. Ekpa shares the willingness to enter a category other investors may avoid: Ekpa calls itself a non-adviser while generating position-specific actions, and the SEC generally treats algorithmic recommendation providers as advisers. The divergence is load-bearing. Ekpa owns no rocket, custody layer, license, or proprietary market-data system; SnapTrade supplies brokerage connectivity while Public and PortfolioPilot can bundle similar research inside regulated or execution-capable platforms. Ekpa receives some credit for walking toward the tripwire, but merely standing near regulation does not make regulation a moat.

**HQ Trivia** exposed what happens when an addictive product temporarily conceals unresolved co-founder authority: the founder conflict eventually reaches the product. Ekpa presents Anant Asthana and Yuga Patel simply as founders, names no CEO, documents no tiebreaker, and shows no earlier shared employer or co-founder history. Ekpa therefore sits near the lowest governance rung, without HQ Trivia’s explosive consumer pull to justify an exception. Fourteen Launch YC votes and 2,400 analyses are not “the closest we ever came to world peace.” They are not enough love to make me ignore the empty chair at the head of the table.

**Anant Asthana’s** agentic drug-discovery pipelines and Yuga Patel’s investment exposure give Ekpa technical adjacency, not a lived origin. Over 18 months, lived experience compounds through better questions, stranger customer insight, and persistence when the obvious product fails; coursework compounds mostly into competent implementation. Ekpa’s dossier supplies no specific moment when Asthana or Patel personally suffered the retail-investor problem, managed money professionally, or built a prior investing product to scale. Two MIT students combining AI and finance looks more like a market discovered in a memo than a founder who was *of* the place.

**Public’s** portfolio agents, Composer’s SoFi acquisition, and PortfolioPilot’s registered-adviser model make Ekpa’s category crowded rather than magically weird. For Ekpa to flip this score into invest territory, the non-consensus truth would need to be that read-only, cross-broker research is a trusted product category rather than a temporary feature between brokerage dashboards and registered advice. Ekpa would then need evidence that sophisticated investors or incumbents rejected that architecture for the wrong reason. The current evidence—38 supported brokerages, no disclosed revenue, and no defensibility signal—says Ekpa is early, but “early” and “non-consensus” are different animals.

**Niantic** could be felt before it could be modeled: I played Ingress, watched humans move through streets, and understood the artifact in my bones. Ekpa is more observable than a buried developer API because a user can connect an account and inspect portfolio-specific alerts, but Ekpa remains a screen interpreting other people’s assets and data. The 2,400 completed analyses prove that something runs; they do not show repeat use, trusted actions, or a changed financial outcome. Ekpa can be touched with a browser, but it cannot yet be observed changing behavior the way a ride, delivery, camera, or location game can.

**2,400 analyses across $3.4M of reported assets** lead me to a falsifiable 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Ekpa will have pivoted from consumer portfolio recommendations into compliance-oriented research copilots for registered investment advisers, producing less than $1M ARR. Ekpa’s regulatory exposure is its most distinctive raw material, while Ekpa’s weak consumer distribution, bundled competition, and unresolved founder authority make a durable standalone retail product unlikely. I’m never playing the game I seem to be playing; the consumer wrapper may be how Ekpa discovers a safer B2B workflow.

**$3.4M in linked assets and 38 brokerage integrations** form the strongest bull case: Ekpa may already have a useful, inspectable product whose read-only architecture earns trust, while 2,400 analyses could be the first twitch of HQ Trivia-style product pull that overrides governance. The case stings because pre-seed usage can matter more than revenue, and Ekpa has shipped beyond a deck. It still loses today because Ekpa discloses neither retention nor paid demand, Public and PortfolioPilot can bundle the function, and neither founder owns the final decision. One verifiable condition would flip my Pass to Neutral: a signed founder-control agreement naming Asthana or Patel as sole CEO with explicit tiebreaker authority.
