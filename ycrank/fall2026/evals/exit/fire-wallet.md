# Fire Wallet -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest path. A payments platform could buy Fire to add private per-request settlement, agent spending controls, and merchant checkout. My inference is that ownership would be worth paying for if Fire built merchant integrations with recurring payment volume that a buyer could capture faster by acquiring Fire than by building or partnering. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The memo documents a WordPress beta and records $500,000 in YC funding. Fire reports a sub-two-second mainnet payment test, a signed Trustboop LOI, and an enterprise merchant onboarding. The reported $1.5 million per month is the merchant’s existing volume. The $100 million per year is potential volume associated with introductions. The memo gives no volume processed through Fire. A funded production deployment with repeat e402 payments would test whether Fire can turn onboarding into an asset a buyer values. The test and onboarding make that milestone plausible, while its cost, runway, and timing remain unclear. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

**Illustrative proceeds.** A $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, assuming no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership. At 2% and 4% ownership, the equity hurdles are $125 million and $62.5 million. With $5 million in illustrative net debt, a $25 million enterprise-value sale at year eight pays the holder $600,000 gross (1.2×), and a $100 million sale pays $2.85 million (5.7×). The 3% case requires about $88.3 million in enterprise value with that debt assumption. The bridge assumes cash at closing and pro rata distribution after net debt, before investor taxes. Fire’s actual investor terms, net debt, fees, senior claims, and realizable sale price are unknown. The disclosed financing supplies no valuation or ownership terms. (Market & Traction)

If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and Fire reinvests all available cash, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. Fire’s advertised fee schedule provides no evidence of cash generation for a sponsor buyout or a company-funded payout. The memo also gives no evidence of eligible secondary demand or operating scale that would support an IPO and subsequent resale. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could assemble much of the capability through existing products. Crossmint offers programmable agent wallets and per-request payments, Hinkal offers confidential settlement, and Fire documents that merchants can offer e402 alongside x402. That makes building or partnering credible. Fire’s reliance on MobileCoin, MoonPay, and 0x also leaves open which capabilities and arrangements a buyer could carry forward. Fire reports that MobileCoin was acquired, with buyer and terms undisclosed, so the transaction establishes no price benchmark for Fire. The evidence weakens the ownership premium and leaves closing conditions open. It establishes neither a structural block nor a proceeds ceiling, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a payments platform pay enough to clear the holder’s cash hurdle once Fire’s onboarding merchant routes repeat payments through e402? Measured production volume and a platform’s quantified buy-versus-build or partnership analysis would move the action to Reach out if they support a purchase price that can deliver $2.5 million to the holder within eight years under credible ownership and closing terms.
