# Kanopy -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Kanopy says it reconstructs 3D clearance measurements from drone, vehicle-mounted, or handheld video. The seed-stage opportunity is to make repeat vegetation surveys affordable on feeder segments that utilities currently inspect infrequently. **Scenario assumption.** At the same accepted clearance accuracy and hazard-detection quality, video capture and processing, human review, repeat captures, and required field checks would together cost less than the manual survey workflow. The next-mile economics depend on repeat survey revenue covering capture, inference, review, and field verification. Kanopy reports mapping more than 300 miles across four states in two weeks. That indicates potential throughput, while accepted mileage and delivery costs remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The memo documents ACRT’s introduction of an offering built with Kanopy and a credentialed SDK for delivering results into utility applications. The founders bring utility consulting and computer vision experience. Six utilities or contractors reportedly signed LOIs or master service agreements covering 270,000 miles of potential scope. Committed volumes, payment, and repeat orders remain unknown. Under model parity, **the inference** is that utilities might keep paying Kanopy if they value continuity of inspection records and the integration needed to use them. The memo provides no retention or buyer preference evidence to establish that advantage. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Field verification could absorb the labor savings and leave Kanopy selling a more efficient version of an existing survey. The memo describes a utility validation plan that retains ground inspections where remote methods fall short. Satellite and LiDAR alternatives already serve adjacent vegetation workflows. With cheap vision models and ample capital, competitors could offer equivalent clearances and workflow integration. Field access and utility acceptance could still constrain delivery, but the memo establishes no durable Kanopy control over either for Path A. The evidence leaves both Path B conditions open and therefore supports Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will a utility place a paid repeat order for surveys on feeder segments it previously surveyed less often because of cost, after a pilot shows lower fully loaded delivery cost at the same field-validated quality and the utility can choose a functionally equivalent alternative? A repeat order at a price that covers delivery, with the buyer identifying what it would lose by switching, would support Reach out if the revised score reaches 65 and confidence reaches Medium. A representative trial showing that field checks erase the savings or that buyers readily switch at parity would support Pass.
