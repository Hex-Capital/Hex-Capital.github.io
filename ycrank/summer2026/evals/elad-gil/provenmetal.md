# ProvenMetal -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Policy pressure is making drone, defense, and industrial buyers reconsider domestic electronics supply, but ProvenMetal becomes a large company only if its embedded procurement workflow generates high repeat gross profit per engineering account after the contract manufacturer takes its share. The dossier provides no threshold, retention cohort, pricing, or gross-margin evidence for that variable. More damagingly, “fast-turn, American made” is not yet a controlling advantage: Network PCB offers three-day turnkey service, Sierra Circuits advertises faster fabrication, and ProvenMetal’s fabrication may remain overseas unless customers select a fully domestic route.

The economic step change is therefore unproven. North American PCB sales were $16.12 billion in 2025, but 5.48% projected annual growth describes a mature market, not a discontinuity. ProvenMetal does not own production; Network PCB performs assembly while accepting competing RFQs directly. No evidence shows that the software lowers manufacturing cost, changes commodity margins, or makes previously uneconomic orders feasible. This currently looks like a software-aware intermediary exposed to supplier concentration and disintermediation—the central physical-market red flag.

The KiCad plugin is the strongest product signal. It connects projects to customer accounts, checks live distributor inventory and lead times, and produces sourcing results in roughly one minute across macOS, Windows, and Linux. If engineers begin every revision there, quoting could become distribution and BOM, inspection, and supplier-performance records could create workflow state. But version 0.2.0 has no published installation, reorder, or retention figures, while the fabrication, assembly, and testing loop remains controlled by partners. The prospective data moat is inferred; its reproduction cost is unknown.

Demand evidence is suggestive but weakly transferable. Five LOIs after more than 30 conversations would matter if they covered today’s turnkey service, yet they preceded the documented transition from an AI-enabled X-ray inspection product. There is no revenue, repeat ordering, or demonstrated capacity constraint. I do give real credit to the team: Doyle and Carkner previously built Syncra together, ran a 17-day office pilot producing more than 20 million datapoints, and then abandoned that product. Carkner also worked on PCB testing. That is costly learning and relevant operating experience, not résumé decoration.

The strongest bull case is that the founders discovered the pain firsthand, use KiCad to aggregate engineer demand, and gradually make individual manufacturers replaceable while every completed order compounds revision and quality data. The policy environment and five early LOIs make that plausible, and zero job listings suggest they are not scaling headcount before proof. But none of the relevant exceptions clears the present evidence: there is no frontier technical asset, scarce research team, or documented reconstruction of incumbent unit economics. The plugin may improve acquisition while leaving value capture with the factory.

My 36-month base case is that ProvenMetal pivots from turnkey PCBA brokerage into inspection and traceability software for domestic contract manufacturers, with less than $3 million in ARR at the transition. I pass today. One condition would flip the decision: a named cohort of at least ten customers completing three orders each within six months while ProvenMetal sustains gross margins above 40%. That would establish repeat pull, workflow ownership, and changed economics through the same controlling variable.
