# Hoplite -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The founders have acted, but not yet deeply enough inside this exact problem. Ryan Morrissey and Bence Redmond built products and freelanced together; Morrissey worked at Stripe, Redmond at AWS and built developer tooling, and their prior CarbonCopy product processed $855,000 in prediction-market volume. That is better evidence than credentials alone. It proves they can ship and recruit usage. It does not yet prove unusual knowledge about operating cloud coding agents across a business.

Hoplite’s first installation is the strongest current signal. Sentry errors that had been ignored became proactive pull requests, and lower-priority tickets were continuously merged. The founders are also traveling to customers and configuring these systems themselves. Manual work is acceptable when it buys specific knowledge; the danger is mistaking service labor for product leverage. One installation shows that the pain exists, not that the solution compounds.

The fatal question is what remains after Cursor or Factory copies the interface. Local-to-cloud handoff, reusable environments, Slack initiation, persistent remote computers, and alert-triggered work already exist across well-capitalized competitors. Hoplite adds MCP migration, iMessage, and Sentry triggers, but these are features, not substrates. The dossier identifies no proprietary model, exclusive data, hardware, network, or demonstrated workflow lock-in. Customer credentials and retained environments could become switching costs, but a possible future asset is not a present advantage.

The bull case is real: two young founders repeatedly ship, personally install the product, and have already observed one business turn neglected operational work into merged code. Customer-specific environments and workflow history might eventually produce a proprietary execution layer. The market is also expanding—AI coding assistants were estimated at $8.51 billion in 2025—but Hoplite has not shown the causal path from its installation wedge into a broader transaction layer. A large parent category does not give every entrant room to maneuver.

There is no user-created loop today. Each new customer appears to require another founder-led installation, while company followers and YC distribution do not make the product more useful for the next team. Economics are similarly unproven: a free week with $100 in credits and an inferred prepaid Stripe system establish a proposed meter, not a payer or realized value capture. No known exception overrides both missing durability and missing adoption: there is neither a self-reinforcing network nor evidence of a prototype so strange and loved that conventional moat analysis should be suspended.

I would pass. This is a capable team entering a valuable market with a useful product, but the option is crowded and presently terminates at copyable code plus founder labor. My flip condition is verifiable: show at least ten paying businesses whose retained environments and workflow history measurably improve agent success over time and produce six-month retention despite access to Cursor and Factory.
