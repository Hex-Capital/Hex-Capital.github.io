# screenpipe -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

screenpipe’s 250,000 users make screenpipe a tactile memory machine with a governance-shaped hole in the middle. A normal human can install screenpipe, watch yesterday’s screen and speech become searchable, and feel the magic before reading a market slide. Yet Louis Beaumont is identified only as “Founder,” with no public evidence of CEO authority, founder vesting, or a conflict tiebreaker. The analytical signature is unusually observable product love attached to an organizational black box.

**Niantic** is the high rung for physical-world verifiability: I played Ingress, watched location software alter human behavior, and knew the artifact before the spreadsheet. screenpipe shares that mechanism because its local capture stack, REST API, MCP access, and programmable pipes can be installed and observed within 48 hours; 250,000 users and 18,900 GitHub stars say people already touch screenpipe rather than merely applaud a demo. screenpipe diverges from Niantic because recording desktop history is solitary utility, not “the closest we ever came to world peace”; the dossier proves adoption, but not repeat usage, retention, or a human ritual strong enough to become culture.

**HQ Trivia** is the low governance rung because intoxicating product pull briefly concealed an unresolved founder tiebreaker, and the bill arrived later. screenpipe names Louis Beaumont as Founder but never names a CEO, co-founder structure, equity split, or operating agreement—no evidence, so I apply the absent-evidence default. The HQ Trivia exception does partially apply: screenpipe’s hundreds of thousands of installs, sixfold active-user growth in two months, and tens of thousands of dollars earned within weeks are powerful enough to tolerate uncertainty temporarily. They are not permission for screenpipe to postpone the conversation about who decides when privacy, licensing, community, and enterprise demands collide.

Louis Beaumont’s work in French intelligence, defense AI, LLM retrieval, and FAISS compounds into founder-market fit over the next 18 months because screenpipe must reconcile hostile operating systems, multimodal indexing, privacy controls, and agent interfaces—not merely wrap a model. screenpipe also has 358 releases, which looks like builder persistence rather than pitch-deck tourism. The missing link is autobiographical: the dossier never gives the physical moment when Beaumont personally needed total recall. He is demonstrably *of the technology*, but screenpipe has not shown that he is *of the user’s pain* in the Anduril or Crusoe sense.

screenpipe’s 18,900 GitHub stars place it in an awkward zone between magically weird and already legible. Local, continuous recording of everything seen and heard is aesthetically transgressive, and default telemetry beside sensitive screen capture gives screenpipe the sort of discomfort that makes conventional investors recoil. Yet 250,000 users, YC admission, Granola’s $67.3 million funding, and Meta’s acquisition of Limitless show that personal AI memory is no longer lonely terrain. The score flips upward if screenpipe documents sophisticated investors passing specifically because local-first capture looks too invasive or commercially strange; an oversubscribed round pitched as “the open-source Recall” would flip screenpipe downward into the consensus AI-agent darling I avoid.

**SpaceX** made capital intensity and frontier risk the moat itself; screenpipe does the opposite by shipping a $25 monthly software product on commodity computers. screenpipe’s sensitive-data exclusions, on-device redaction, per-pipe permissions, and optional local processing introduce an unsexy compliance surface, but the dossier identifies no regulated authorization, hardware breakthrough, or structural barrier. screenpipe is not SpaceX hiding inside desktop software. The more interesting disguise is enterprise control infrastructure hiding inside a consumer recorder, but Microsoft Recall can bundle the basic behavior while screenpipe bears the privacy burden.

screenpipe’s $50-per-seat Pro plan and $150-per-seat Enterprise plan support a falsifiable forecast: within 36 months, screenpipe reaches $8–15 million ARR and is acquired by Atlassian to supply local cross-application memory for enterprise agents rather than remaining an independent horizontal desktop platform. screenpipe’s observable utility produces the adoption wedge; its REST API, MCP access, and deployment-specific pipes produce the enterprise integration layer. Low governance visibility and weak structural barriers make acquisition more probable than a durable standalone category winner.

Microsoft Recall is the bear mechanism that stings: Microsoft can bundle local history into Windows, Granola can own meetings, Otter.ai already has $100 million ARR, and screenpipe’s license change can alienate the open-source contributors who created its 1,800 forks. screenpipe also consumes 5–10% CPU and 5–10 GB monthly while continuously handling passwords, financial data, and private communications—a single credible breach could erase the trust behind screenpipe’s physical-product advantage. The dossier’s sixfold active-user growth and early revenue win today because they show behavior, not applause, but one verifiable condition flips my verdict: if twelve-month paid retention is below 40% after excluding free-source users, I pass regardless of download count.
