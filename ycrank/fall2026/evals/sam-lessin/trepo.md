# Trepo -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Trepo reports six sold-out hardware batches, but I cannot price the investment: post-money valuation, competing offers, and whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital are all undisclosed. YC participation establishes none of those things. The use of proceeds is also missing. I can identify an important experiment here—whether a kitchen device makes household inventory reliable enough to drive repeat grocery purchases—but Trepo has not documented a capital-funded test with a deadline and a pass/fail threshold. An investor’s proposed experiment earns no credit as the company’s financing plan.

The interesting asset is access to what happens between grocery deliveries. Instacart’s public APIs can help build a basket; they do not know which ingredients a household consumed or discarded. Trepo’s external device could capture that information across existing refrigerators. That fits my pattern of backing bridges between physical operations and software. But the household must keep supplying accurate updates. If recording food becomes another chore, inventory deteriorates, recommendations lose credibility, and the supposed proprietary data becomes an unreliable record. Samsung’s overlapping functionality makes reliable participation more consequential than recognition technology alone.

The strongest argument against passing is that people are already taking action. Six reported sold-out batches are more informative than a waitlist, and 150,000-plus item check-ins suggest somebody is doing the recording work. A 4.9 rating from 56 App Store reviews adds evidence of satisfaction. Those facts deserve distribution and workflow credit. They do not yet establish how many devices were paid for and delivered, whether the same households keep participating, or whether inventory context causes subsequent purchases. Founder TikTok activity could be an effective acquisition channel, but its conversion economics are undisclosed. The exceptional-case override would require demonstrated commercial pull or retained usage strong enough to outweigh missing financing evidence; aggregate check-ins and unspecified batch sizes do not establish that.

Taylor’s Tesla and Nuro hardware experience, alongside a released app and beta device offering, gives Trepo more substance than an agent concept. Slaughter brings consumer marketing experience, and the reported grocery pilots provide some access to the other ecosystem. Still, the dossier supplies no attributable prior deployment results or demonstrated grocery operating outcomes. CEO and COO roles identify leadership responsibilities; knowing each other through college water polo gives me no reason to infer an equal-founder decision problem. The real operating question is whether this two-person team can support hardware while maintaining a useful consumer service.

Delete the next financing from the model. The app is free, subscription prices are unpublished, and device margins, burn, and runway are unknown. That earns the stipulated economics default, without assuming hardware is inherently uneconomic. **I pass on the evidence available.** My verifiable flip condition is an offered round below $10 million post-money accompanied by a cohort-supported cash plan demonstrating that its proceeds reach break-even using observed retention and device margins after fulfillment, returns, and support. Trepo has evidence that people want to try the product. I need an investable connection between that behavior, durable household access, and cash.
