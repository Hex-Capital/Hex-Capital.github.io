# Aviern -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic sale to an aerospace manufacturer or logistics integrator. A buyer could acquire Aviern’s airframe and autonomy stack to serve smaller airports and shorten the ground leg for urgent freight. Aviern reports more than 150 autonomous miles on a scaled demonstrator and says it built the airframe and software in-house. Its planned 1,000 lb payload and estimated $100 per flight hour remain unvalidated at commercial scale. The memo records Grid Aero’s manufacturing partnership with Oshkosh, which shows industrial interest in the category. FedEx, UPS and DHL appear in Aviern’s requests for carrier introductions. Buyer interest in owning Aviern remains an inference. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Aviern has a larger 50 lb test aircraft in development and targets a purpose-built cargo aircraft flight by Q1 2028. The scaled flight record supports further testing. The capital and time needed to progress from that flight to authorized, repeatable feeder operations remain uncertain. IP assignment and transaction consents are unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, dilution from 5% to 3%, and no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale scenarios below assume a full cash acquisition closing within eight years, $10 million in net debt and other deal deductions, and a pro rata waterfall with no senior preference leakage or deferred consideration.

- **Modest sale.** $50 million enterprise value yields $40 million in distributable equity and $1.2 million to the holder, or 2.4×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million enterprise value yields $90 million in distributable equity and $2.7 million to the holder, or 5.4×. At 2% ownership, the same sale yields $1.8 million, or 3.6×.
- **Prolonged private operation.** If Aviern reinvests available cash and the holder has no eligible share sale, realized proceeds are $0. The memo provides no evidence for an independent liquidity route.

The sale prices are sensitivity assumptions. The memo supplies neither a priced acquisition comparable nor measured carrier economics from which to estimate realizable proceeds. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Carriers could buy feeder capacity from a retrofit provider and avoid owning an aircraft platform. Reliable Robotics is already pursuing Caravan certification and was selected for an FAA pilot program covering regional cargo. Aviern also faces a classification risk. If the proposed 110 lb total-weight cap for certificated package delivery applies to its feeder routes, the planned 1,000 lb payload alone exceeds it. Neither Pass gate is met. The classification remains unresolved, and the memo gives no supported sale-price range from which to conclude that even the strongest credible route leaves the holder materially short. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a carrier-backed pilot on a representative feeder route show that owning Aviern’s platform is worth enough to clear the holder’s proceeds hurdle? A pilot conducted under documented FAA authorization would need to measure cost, delivery time and reliability against the carrier’s current service. If those results and credible rollout volumes supported a purchase price above the hurdle after deal deductions, the action would move to Reach out.
