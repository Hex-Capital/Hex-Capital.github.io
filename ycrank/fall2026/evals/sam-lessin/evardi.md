# Evardi -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Evardi wants access to the utility’s operating model. That could be valuable. But what am I paying, and what does my check prove? The dossier supplies neither post-money valuation nor competing-term-sheet count, and no defined use of proceeds. YC, SF1 and Browder Capital being listed tells me somebody invested; it establishes neither scarce capital nor an auction. Both financing dimensions receive their missing-evidence defaults. I cannot underwrite a cheap, truth-revealing experiment from a list of backers.

The strongest argument for Evardi is the bridge its founders can build. Diva Shah has worked in Kenya Power grid operations and Autodesk utility integrations, and self-reports selling prior businesses or infrastructure. Those are more useful receipts than simply having studied energy. Aarya Borele adds engineering and an offshore-wind venture, although the dossier does not establish a scaled deployment. This fits my pattern of backing people who translate between physical operations and software. Founder evidence is the strongest dimension here, with partial rather than full credit because the shipped outcomes remain thinly documented.

The product gives that bridge substance. Combining GIS, SCADA, billing and meter records into a power-flow model, then checking recommendations before operator approval, addresses a difficult operating workflow. Human approval also means Evardi need not assume agent errors disappear to deliver value. The bull case stings: a trusted model inside a utility could become expensive to replace, and transformer-level visibility could make that access economically consequential. But whose dark pool is it? The utility supplies the feeder map and meter history. Evardi has not yet demonstrated that its integrations produce proprietary access or repeat purchasing that Neara or Bidgely/Grid4C cannot contest.

The reported footprint—utilities serving 10M-plus customers across 180,000-plus miles of line—is evidence of claimed reach, not ten million Evardi customers. The published 61% inspection hit rate versus 6% from billing flags is promising, but the studies explicitly use simulations on public data. They do not establish paid conversion or retained operational use. My exceptions for AI businesses require measured customer behavior that survives contact with production; those receipts are missing here. The founder-exit financing exception also depended on a specific instrument and capital plan, neither documented for Evardi. I see no evidence of auction pressure or an equal-founder committee, so I will not invent those red flags.

Delete the next financing from Evardi’s model and the underwriting remains unresolved: no disclosed revenue, implementation margin, burn or route to break-even. That earns the economics default, not a claim that a four-person pre-seed company is already uneconomic. Utility-specific modelling could support durable licensing, but it could also consume substantial engineering labor before each purchase. I pass on the evidence available. My verifiable reconsideration trigger is a named utility renewing a paid deployment at positive contribution margin after including integration and engineering labor. That would show the operating bridge earns money rather than merely producing persuasive simulations.
