# Synara -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Synara has a founder who has already built and sold technology inside this market. What I cannot price is the investment. Proposed post-money, competing term sheets, and whether my check enables something otherwise unfundable are all undisclosed. YC membership does not establish an auction; financing silence does not establish a bargain. There is also no stated use of proceeds identifying one experiment, a deadline, and the result that changes the business’s value. Those two missing gates matter more to my personal check than the size of the monitoring market.

Boris Ploix is the strongest argument against passing. He co-founded Calipsa as CTO in 2016, worked on machine learning for surveillance, and reached Motorola’s acquisition in April 2022. That is a shipped operating asset with an external buyer, not résumé decoration. Synara also fits my unstated preference for founders connecting software with difficult physical operations: detection is useful only if somebody verifies the incident and takes responsibility for responding. Ploix has credible experience connecting AI with video monitoring. The dossier does not establish that he has operated the human-response service now being proposed, which keeps the founder score below the ceiling.

The commercial architecture is where this gets uncomfortable. Security integrators supply their installed camera bases, bill under their own brands, and retain the customer relationship. That could make distribution efficient, but whose dark pool am I buying? Synara has no documented integrator-driven purchase, retained deployment, exclusive relationship, or proprietary incident-data rights. A channel strategy is not yet owned access. Meanwhile, Alarm.com acquired a majority stake in CHeKT in February 2025 and already has relationships with the service providers Synara needs. Ploix’s experience makes this competitive problem legible; it does not make those relationships Synara’s property.

I give the product credit for explicitly retaining human verification rather than pretending errors disappear. But that also makes operator workload central to the economics. Wholesale pricing, operator cost per site, gross margin, burn, and a seed-funded route to break-even are undisclosed. The launch and October 2026 coverage establish availability, not operating performance. There is no evidence of negative contribution economics, so I will not invent it. Equally, there is no functioning cash business here that I can separately value beneath the AI option. The relevant AI red flag is exposure to overlapping incumbent capabilities; the dossier does not establish dependence on miraculous future automation.

I pass at this evidence level. The strongest bull case is that a founder who already sold surveillance AI can use integrators to build a capital-efficient monitoring business while others keep selling components. That is substantial enough to warrant attention. But the known exceptions to my pricing and capital-plan gates depended on demonstrated customer conversion, operating economics, or an explicit alternative financing structure; Synara supplies none of those overrides yet. One verifiable result would make me reopen diligence: a paid integrator cohort that remains deployed for 90 days and generates positive contribution after actual verification and intervention labor. That would establish that the proposed distribution and response workflow produce cash together. Round price would still determine whether I buy it.
