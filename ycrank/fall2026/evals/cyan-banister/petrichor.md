# Petrichor -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

The failures are the interesting part. Petrichor proposes returning photographs, raw instrument files and timestamped actions while scientists execute experiments—including the experiments that go wrong. Move the clock to 2032: a biological model should be able to propose an experiment, inspect what actually happened and choose its next experiment without its owner first building a laboratory. That requires reliable execution, records a model can interpret, and feedback that improves the next decision. Petrichor describes the connective tissue. Lila’s reported hundreds of thousands of AI-driven experiments in September 2025 suggest that this future has begun, but they do not establish that Petrichor can deliver its part.

Why should every model company become a laboratory operator? The dossier identifies construction timelines measured in years. Buying experimental capacity could return those years to a small research team and let it pursue questions without owning the building. That is a concrete release of agency. But laboratories also take time because physical execution is difficult; the dossier has not exposed an artificial restriction or quantified an incumbent’s rent. Outsourcing moves that operating burden onto Petrichor. Someone still has to make the experiment work.

The strongest argument for investing is that scientists may be the sensible bridge into this future. They can execute unusual protocols before those protocols justify automation, while intermediate records could make the service more useful to model builders than a final results file. This fits my preference for the concealed infrastructure beneath a fashionable application. The uncomfortable counterargument is that Emerald Cloud Lab and Strateos already operate remote experimentation infrastructure. Petrichor has not yet demonstrated controlled laboratory capacity, superior records or a technical dependency those alternatives cannot supply. Its position in the stack interests me; ownership of a scarce bottleneck remains unproved.

Wilhelm Hedenskog, Jesper Johansson and Elias Reinfeldt previously worked together on Qokedas’s scientific instrument-data offering. That is relevant continuity, and their high-school relationship predates this company. It does not establish a longstanding experimental obsession, laboratory operating skill or the ability to recruit scientists. Nor does the described workflow establish a completed customer experiment or repeated use. My willingness to back a pre-revenue technical company applies here, but the exception needs something affirmative—a demonstrated prototype and credible commitments, for example. YC Fall 2026 participation supplies neither. Missing revenue is ordinary at this stage; missing execution evidence leaves the central promise unanswered.

Petrichor’s customer-ownership and no-shared-training commitments deserve credit as confidentiality safeguards. I would not ask the founders to weaken them to manufacture a data moat. They do mean the investment must work through execution quality, integrations and accumulated operating knowledge rather than presumed ownership of customers’ experimental results. Pricing and contract terms are undisclosed, so I cannot tell whether scientist labor becomes an increasingly efficient service or remains the constraint. The dossier identifies Wilhelm as CEO and supplies no affirmative evidence of concealed leadership, equal-control deadlock or dismissive claims about incumbent complexity; those red flags should not be invented.

I am interested, but I would keep my own money in my pocket today. Petrichor has a plausible place in a future I want to inhabit, with little evidence yet that this team can operate the bridge. My one verifiable flip condition is an auditable customer deployment showing three successive paid model–experiment–model cycles, where Petrichor’s intermediate records demonstrably change the customer’s next experiment. That would turn a persuasive service description into evidence that somebody is already living in this future.
