# Hickory & Company -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years forward, I can imagine a small bank overseeing dozens of fintech partners without adding a compliance analyst for every new relationship. Evidence arrives continuously, exceptions reach the right human, and an examination does not begin with a scavenger hunt. Hickory & Company’s source-linked research and acceptance-driven agents point toward that world. But getting there requires reliable evidence collection, then institutional acceptance of the resulting records. The dossier does not establish those transitions. An unnamed live-bank integration is an early deployment claim; it does not yet reveal that larger future.

Why does the scavenger hunt exist? Sponsor banks remain accountable for partners whose daily operations they cannot control. That separation matters: the bank carries responsibility while the evidence lives elsewhere. Hickory gathers documentation, drafts workpapers, and leaves approval with employees ([sponsor-bank product](https://hickory.co/for/sponsor-banks)). That could return time and practical control to compliance staff. Yet there is no measured collection delay, labor cost, or partner restriction showing how much agency changes hands. I see a specific coordination problem, with the economic benefit still unmeasured.

Joshua Howland is the reason I would take the meeting. He previously built Seis as co-founder and CTO; his reported 500,000 accounts and $10 million revenue within 16 months describe substantial contact with banking operations, even though Seis subsequently closed. Those are prior-company results, not Hickory traction. Scott Grow worked with him there and brings more than 20 years of compliance experience; Kevin Anderson adds banking-systems engineering ([founder biography](https://www.ycombinator.com/founders/josh-howland), [team](https://hickory.co/team)). This is meaningful builder and recruiting evidence. The missing link is whether a specific operating wound at Seis became Hickory’s specification. That would fit my pattern of backing operators who discover a universal problem inside their own misery; shared industry experience alone does not establish it.

The strongest case against passing is that this team may already know exactly which tedious work a bank will entrust to software. A live integration could represent considerable institutional effort. I will back a pre-revenue company when a working artifact and unusually deep founder history make the destination believable; revenue is not the gate here. But Hickory’s unnamed integration supplies no completed workflow, repeated analyst use, or customer commitment that lets me test that exception. The 22 launch videos demonstrate organized selling, not user dependence. I want to watch an analyst prepare an actual examination package and discover what they refuse to do without Hickory.

There is also a difference between repairing compliance work and controlling infrastructure that other products must use. Institution-specific policies and retained records might eventually make Hickory difficult to replace, but that mechanism remains inferred. Parcha already offers configurable agents and audit logs, while Hummingbird spans research, review, and orchestration. Hickory has not demonstrated a scarce dependency beneath those applications. Its data controls deserve a fair reading: tenant isolation, DLP screening, and restrictions on hosted models address my privacy concern rather than proving a severe privacy violation. Nor does the dossier establish dependence on one closed model supplier or a founder-control problem.

I would pass with my own money today. Hickory & Company has credible people repairing a real institutional seam, but the evidence currently supports workflow competence more strongly than a distinctive future or controlled enabling layer. One verifiable result would change my decision: a named sponsor bank renewing a paid deployment after using Hickory through an examination, with documented reductions in evidence-preparation hours across multiple fintech partners. That would show the product becoming something the institution relies on, and give me a concrete place to begin working backward from the future.
