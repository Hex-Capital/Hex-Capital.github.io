# Prodigy Research -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. Specialized models continuously form hypotheses, test them against markets, execute within risk constraints, and learn from their own impact. Human quants supervise systems rather than hand-building every strategy. Prodigy Research may be an early leak from that world: a proprietary finance model and agent harness are already trading company capital. But the dossier does not map the intervening steps—how the system survives regime changes, distinguishes genuine alpha from a transient pattern, or scales without trading away its advantage. I can see the future; Prodigy has not yet shown me the bridge.

The most interesting artifact is not the benchmark against GPT-5.6 Sol or Claude Fable. It is the claimed 108% live return over two months using delta-neutral strategies, with no down week. The team has allowed software to take costly action with its own capital, which is materially better than a rendering or waitlist. But this is also exactly where a professional daydreamer has to become painfully literal. There is no independent audit, daily return series, volatility, drawdown, capital base, or account of market impact. Two months can reveal a strange machine; it cannot yet distinguish a durable machine from one lucky regime.

Michael Wang and Yuhua Wang are unusually well matched to the technical task. Michael reportedly trained Gemini 3.1 Pro at Google DeepMind and generated billions in P&L as a Jane Street quant and licensed trader; Yuhua reportedly built AI infrastructure serving billions of Apple users and millions of daily Salesforce queries. That covers model construction, trading, and production systems with almost suspicious neatness. Still, these claims come from Prodigy’s own website, and the dossier contains no dated pre-company experiments, public artifacts, recruitment history, or evidence explaining why these two became first believers together. The credentials are excellent. The biography of obsession is not yet visible.

Then I ask the irritating systems question: whose rule is broken, who collects the rent, and who gets agency back? The dossier offers no answer. Prodigy appears to trade company capital rather than give an individual or institution new occupational control. There is also no disclosed pricing, fund structure, software product, or distribution path. That absence is not a pre-seed traction penalty; it is an unresolved identity question. Is this a venture-scale model company, a proprietary desk, or a future fund? A profitable trading engine could be wonderful while remaining a poor angel investment if its economics cannot be shared without destroying its edge.

The bull case stings. A former DeepMind researcher and Jane Street trader paired with a hyperscale infrastructure engineer may have built real alpha before acquiring the vocabulary, customers, or audited history needed to explain it publicly. If 108% is genuine, obsessing over software ARR would miss the more valuable asset. But Standard Signal already runs an end-to-end AI hedge fund, EquiLibre reports systems trading billions of dollars daily, and Numerai has $550 million in AUM. Prodigy has not yet demonstrated the controlled data advantage, execution bottleneck, or capacity behavior that makes its model an enabling layer rather than another participant riding the same increasingly correlated signals.

My base case is that by August 2029 Prodigy remains primarily a proprietary trading operation with less than $1 million in external software ARR, even if the founders produce respectable trading returns. I pass today because the strongest evidence is a working artifact while the weakest evidence is the economic system it changes. One condition flips me: an independent administrator verifies at least twelve months of live, capacity-scaled performance at $25 million or more in capital, including drawdowns, fees, factor exposures, and market-impact attribution. That would turn the 108% claim from a shiny number into evidence that the future actually arrived early.
