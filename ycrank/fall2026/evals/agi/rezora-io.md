# Rezora IO -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Rezora’s strongest case is automated follow-up on consented real-estate leads that small teams would otherwise leave untouched because manual dialing costs too much per qualified meeting. The current platform launched in January 2026 with outbound calling and calendar booking. Rezora requires documented consent, which limits the pool of eligible leads. Whether the platform opens a new transaction depends on the human time still needed for retries, review, and handoffs at equivalent meeting quality. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The founders reported $40,000 in 40 days from the prior custom-agent pilot and 1,000 meetings booked on the current platform. The pilot is an early payment signal for the prior delivery model. The current booking count leaves buyer payment, repeat use, and the share of meetings from previously unworked leads unknown. At seed, building the first agent around a workflow Matsay knew from real-estate sales and shipping a self-serve platform show relevant execution. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team)

**Scale scenario.** At an assumed 200 outbound minutes per account per month, the two published pricing schedules imply monthly billings of $309 or $329. Contribution per added account depends on telephony, inference, onboarding, support, and exception handling. Buyer economics depend on the subscription and remaining staff time per attended, qualified meeting versus manual follow-up at equivalent quality. The memo leaves those costs, the number of reachable buyers, and repeat usage unmeasured. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Rezora may save brokers time on outreach they already do, while buyers with access to equally capable models can assemble basic calling and booking from commercial tools. Matsay reports doing exactly that for the pilot. The company reports training agents for specific real-estate conversations, but buyer preference at functional parity and measurable network effects remain untested. The memo identifies no scarce input Rezora controls under Path A. The pilot’s reproducibility weighs against durable value capture. Substitution at equivalent quality remains unproven, so Path B stays open for a test and falls short of the outreach gate. (Defensibility, The Idea)

### Decisive diligence question

Will small brokerage teams pay and renew for Rezora to work consented leads they previously left untouched when they can choose an equally capable alternative? A paid comparative trial should measure fully loaded cost per attended, qualified meeting against manual follow-up, including consent checks, review, retries, and handoffs. It should then give buyers an equivalent automated option at a comparable price. Lower delivery cost that makes the previously skipped work viable, positive contribution per account, and repeat paid preference for Rezora would support Reach out if the updated score and confidence clear the outreach gate. If the savings disappear or buyers readily switch at parity, Pass.
