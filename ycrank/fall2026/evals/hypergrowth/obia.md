# Obia -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Even assuming strong product-market fit, Obia’s current service depends on local property data, zoning rules, and brokers. The tenant’s job of finding space with the right permitted use, loading, and power can recur across markets. Serving that job with the current product would likely require substantial work to adapt screening to local rules and data and arrange compliant brokerage. The memo documents a cooperative certificate requirement for out-of-state brokers working with Nevada brokers. Online inquiries and referral offers provide reusable ways to find tenants. Brokers still review uncertain findings, lead tours, and negotiate terms. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Obia describes a landlord-paid fee tied to each completed lease. A tenant could return for another site, but the memo establishes a transaction-based model with little basis for recurring revenue or expansion within an account. Cho reports $1.05 million in closed lease value over 22 days, with some clients moving in within two weeks. That indicates early deal activity for the four-person team. The memo gives no earned fees, paying-customer count, or time since commercial launch, leaving the pace of realized revenue unscorable beyond the default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Incremental contribution is the landlord fee earned per closing less attributable screening and broker labor, data and processing costs, and any referral payout. Automating pre-tour screening could reduce labor per search, including searches that never close. The memo provides neither fee amounts nor representative costs, so the size and durability of that improvement remain uncertain. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Obia reports screening sites for a logistics operator by permitted use, service-area coverage, and projected throughput per dollar of rent. If that screening workflow can be reused with configurable local inputs and a standard broker partnership, Obia could expand while concentrating broker time on tours and negotiations. The reported deployment supports the usefulness of the screening workflow. The current evidence still points to substantial market-by-market adaptation without a credible near-term transfer path, which triggers the structural downside gate. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Obia complete a fee-paying lease in a materially different overseas jurisdiction using the same screening product, with bounded local data and rule configuration and a standard broker partnership? A completed lease with a collected fee, a record of the adaptation required, and positive contribution after attributable broker and onboarding costs would move the action to Watch.
