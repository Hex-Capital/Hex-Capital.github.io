# Boxes.dev -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Boxes.dev has shipped a useful product. What am I being offered ownership of, and at what price? The dossier supplies neither a proposed post-money valuation nor competing-term-sheet data. YC membership and prior founder success establish neither an auction nor scarce capital. I cannot underwrite cheap uncertainty from an undisclosed price. There is also no disclosed capital plan naming the experiment, deadline, and result this check would buy. Is the money testing whether developers will pay to keep their environments here, or merely keeping another agent-hosting service running? Those are different investments.

The strongest argument for writing the check is the team’s operating history. Nick Bushak co-founded and served as CTO of Gem; Drew Regitsky was its first engineer. They built together before, now use Claude Code and Codex themselves, and have shipped persistent Linux environments with snapshots and mobile access. That fits my preference for people who can translate between systems: software builders addressing the operational friction of software-building agents. Their experience deserves substantial credit. It does not establish proprietary cloud infrastructure or customer access that competing teams cannot reproduce.

The product’s attractive mechanism is that better agents could make persistent execution environments more valuable. Boxes.dev need not win a model race; it could own the place where developers keep work running as models change. That is the bull case that makes this pass uncomfortable. But Conductor already offers cloud microVMs, native agents, subscription support, and mobile access. Boxes.dev uses E2B underneath. Prepared environments could become an embedded workflow, but the dossier documents no retained usage or purchases caused by that workflow. Product Hunt’s 113 launch points and 165 followers, Android’s 10+ downloads, and one iOS rating establish visibility—not an owned pool of demand.

Deleting the next financing from the model leaves the economics unanswered. Starter advertises $19 for 50 compute hours; Pro is $99 for 250, with homepage overages at $0.60 an hour. Those are prices, not contribution margins. Without infrastructure costs, utilization, realized revenue, or burn, I cannot tell whether persistent environments create profitable attachment or an accumulating compute bill. Conflicting homepage and help-page allowances add uncertainty about the actual offer. None of this proves an uneconomic business, and a two-person pre-seed team need not have mature reporting. It does mean the profitable-path dimension receives its missing-evidence default.

I pass. The documented team and functioning product support the translation-layer pattern, but the exceptions that could override missing financing gates require demonstrated conversion, retention, cash economics, or proprietary access; Boxes.dev supplies none yet. The relevant AI red flag is exposure to application substitution, although the founders do not claim that falling model costs alone create a moat. Nor does the dossier establish financing pressure or confused founder authority. My concern is narrower: capable builders have entered an overlapping market without evidence that their workflow captures durable economics. One verifiable condition would reverse my pass: an available entry below $10 million post-money into Boxes.dev after retained paying customers have sustained company-wide cash break-even for three consecutive months. That would make the investment a priced claim on a working business, with agent growth providing the option.
