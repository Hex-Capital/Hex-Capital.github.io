# 0.security -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Automating security expertise could let a two-person engineering team defend software that previously required a dedicated security department. That is the consequence worth underwriting at 0.security. But the dossier does not quantify that expansion in access or the reduction in expert labor. A $10.65 billion application-security market establishes existing spending. Software reaching more than 3 billion devices establishes potential exposure. Neither measures the consequence of this product succeeding.

The strongest evidence is an actual vulnerability discovery credited to Doruk, combined with a product that joins deterministic scanning, repository context, execution sandboxes and remediation agents. There is engineering substance here. The Spotify engineer’s report of scanning almost 30,000 files in under a minute is encouraging, but scanning throughput does not establish vulnerability detection or safe remediation. Against Aikido’s overlapping autonomous-testing offering, 0.security has not demonstrated a measured discontinuity in useful security work. That distinction determines whether these founders are building scarce capability or competing to package increasingly available models.

The bull case deserves attention. Two founders have attracted 526 stars and 58 forks, report work with EY and other demanding organizations, and have published evidence of security research. Doruk’s cyber-defense experience gives the product a credible technical starting point; Fabian’s finance and business-development background could complement it commercially. This fits my unstated pattern of backing tiny teams with an emerging technical community. Open source can accelerate testing, contribution and distribution. It does not need exclusive source code to create value. But the dossier establishes neither a committed contributor engine nor a proprietary learning advantage from successive investigations.

I would not dismiss this because revenue is undisclosed or the team has only two people. Those are normal pre-seed conditions. My exceptions for execution-led platforms and pre-product technical bets still need a compelling instigator or an extraordinary consequence that justifies buying the uncertainty. The reported prior exits supply limited information about either. There is no documented belief revision or difficult complementary recruit. Equally, the evidence does not justify accusing the founders of stubbornness, premature scaling or building only an interface wrapper. The relevant AI red flag remains unresolved: can their capability advance faster than the underlying models and established security vendors?

The next dollar should test autonomous remediation reliability. The dossier supplies no experiment with a pass threshold, budget or stop decision, so I cannot credit a demonstrated risk-removal process. Economics are similarly unproven: a $30 subscription and usage credits disclose customer prices, not compute costs, human review requirements or cost per verified fix. Cheap scans could still produce expensive security work if experts must investigate every result.

I pass with my own money today. My single verifiable flip condition is an independently audited, matched customer-code trial showing at least 10x more confirmed, safely remediated high-severity vulnerabilities per fully loaded dollar than Aikido, with equivalent scope and explicit controls for false positives and regressions. That would connect technical capability to expert-labor economics. Until then, 0.security has credible builders and useful early evidence, but the extraordinary outcome remains unestablished.
