# Contour -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Contour could make environmental expertise abundant enough to accelerate infrastructure construction. That consequence interests me. But the dossier establishes a **$27.0 billion U.S. environmental consulting market in 2024**, not how much infrastructure Contour can unlock or how sharply it can reduce the resources required. Producing a report faster only accelerates construction if report production is the binding constraint. Field studies, agency review, and project-specific disputes may still determine the schedule. I cannot price a world-changing option from the size of the consulting market alone.

The acquisition model is the strongest argument for taking Contour seriously. Owning practices could give Cortex access to actual workflows, customer relationships, and responsibility for completed work. Embedding engineers beside practitioners could expose failures that a software vendor never sees. This fits my unstated preference for turning scarce expert workflows into repeatable production systems. The distinction is consequential: ownership could create a learning loop. However, completed acquisitions, rights to reusable project records, and measured improvements are all undisclosed. Paces and Transect already address parts of permitting and environmental diligence. Contour has not demonstrated which constraint Cortex breaks, or a 5–10x advantage against an existing method. That earns the technical absent-evidence score; it does not establish that Cortex is merely a wrapper.

The founders have ingredients relevant to this strategy. Aarush Agrawal built enterprise search at Rezolve AI; Zack Lubar’s Lazard experience relates to acquisition execution. Those are useful capabilities for combining document automation with firm ownership. They do not yet demonstrate rapid belief revision or recruiting someone exceptional to own environmental accuracy. Likewise, YC participation supplies no evidence of customer acceptance or risk removal. I assign the prescribed defaults here. Four founders and undisclosed revenue are ordinary pre-seed conditions.

The largest investment question is whether automation survives the cost of making the output dependable. A report produced cheaply can become expensive after expert correction, fieldwork, and liability are included. Acquisition financing adds another claim on the savings. Contour discloses neither a decisive accuracy experiment nor fully loaded delivery economics. There is also no evidence of permanent subsidy dependence, premature scaling expenditure, or founders ignoring contrary evidence; those red flags remain unestablished. My exceptions for execution-heavy businesses and unusually consequential missions keep the ownership strategy in consideration, but neither substitutes for demonstrated transformation of this workflow.

**I pass on the present evidence.** The bull case is uncomfortable: controlling delivery could let this team improve expert productivity while competitors remain outside the practice. That could become a substantial business. My one verifiable flip condition is an independently reviewed, matched-project pilot showing Cortex delivers accepted environmental reports at **one-tenth the fully loaded cost**, including expert review, rework, field inputs, and allocated acquisition costs, without worse material-error rates. That result would demonstrate a technical discontinuity with economic substance. Today, the dossier supplies a plausible route to learning and no measured result from it.
