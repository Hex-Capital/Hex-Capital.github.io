# Snowstone -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Snowstone’s $50M of identified opportunities could matter to its customers. It does not yet establish what changes by orders of magnitude if the company succeeds. The $2B reviewed is transaction value, not an operating-cost baseline; identified opportunities are not realized savings. The consequential version of this business would make expert operational diagnosis radically cheaper across ordinary companies. The dossier does not quantify that reach or demonstrate that cost collapse. I can lose my entire check on an ambitious experiment. First, I need evidence of what the experiment could unlock.

The product has a sensible mechanism: reconstruct workflows from emails, documents, ERP, and CRM records, then connect recommendations to underlying sources. But PE-specific financial diagnosis establishes a customer focus, not technical superiority over Celonis, Skan, or Mimica. Persisted entity matches and version histories make findings more auditable. They do not demonstrate a proprietary learning advantage or a 5–10x improvement against an incumbent constraint. Snowstone has not earned that technical-discontinuity credit. Equally, the dossier does not establish that it is merely an API wrapper; that red flag remains unproven.

The private benchmark is the most useful evidence here because the team has subjected a central product risk to measurement. Dollar estimates fell within 20% of ground truth in 66% of cases. The remaining third matters enormously when recommendations determine where management spends money. Source citations help someone inspect a calculation; they do not establish that the proposed intervention causes the projected EBITDA improvement. This is partial risk removal, worth more than a feature roadmap, but the dossier supplies no decisive acceptance threshold, experiment cost, or response to failure.

The strongest argument against passing is that Snowstone may already have an unusually effective route into expensive work. Its reported analysis scope suggests access to consequential transactions, and the collaboration involving Corporate Lifecycles MD and Grey Matter Global could provide distribution. Baur brings documented PE diligence and LBO-modeling exposure; Waldorf and Moreland contribute physics, engineering, and agentic-systems backgrounds. That is a more relevant combination than three generic AI résumés. Nevertheless, complementary studies are not evidence of difficult recruiting, and the Compendium-to-Cerenovus-to-Snowstone history does not reveal which belief changed after customer evidence. I would not turn rebranding into proof of learning rate.

Snowstone also fits part of my broader pattern: an execution-heavy financial workflow can become a valuable platform without a scientific breakthrough. Reviewed matches could make subsequent engagements easier. The missing link is evidence that repeated work compounds into reliable automation rather than repeated analyst effort. My exceptions to the technical gate require a documented instigator addressing an enormous existing value pool, or demonstrated progress with extraordinary consequences. This dossier establishes neither sufficiently. Conversely, it contains no evidence of founder liquidity obsession, ignored disconfirming evidence, or reckless scaling. This is a Pass for insufficient fit, not an indictment of the founders.

I would revisit Snowstone after **one independently audited, paid deployment demonstrates at least a 5x reduction in fully loaded diagnostic cost against the customer’s existing process, with recommendations producing verified savings within 20% of their forecasts**. Include integration, inference, and human review in that cost. That result would connect the technical mechanism to customer economics and attack the largest uncertainty directly. Public pricing and delivery costs are currently absent, so unsubsidized competitiveness remains unproven. Until then, I am being asked to purchase an option on a promising diagnostic business whose transformative mechanism has yet to be demonstrated. I pass.
