# Lyon -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Twenty-eight billion transactions is an input. What becomes radically cheaper because Lyon can learn from them? The consequential possibility is replacing months of bespoke prediction engineering with reusable models that make financial expertise inexpensive across credit, fraud, and insurance. A fintech serving tens of millions of active users gives that possibility substance. But the dossier quantifies neither the engineering cost displaced nor the improvement in access to financial services. I can see a potentially important mechanism. I cannot yet price the consequence of success.

The strongest argument for investing is that Lyon has already crossed a barrier many research startups never approach: training on a real institution’s transaction history. Its reported **4× precision** improvement in premium-card conversion deserves attention, particularly from a two-person team. Customer-specific event pretraining, reusable embeddings, and downstream adapters could turn isolated prediction projects into a production platform. That fits my preference for expensive specialist workflows becoming repeatable systems. However, the comparison is against undisclosed rules, without a published testing method. It establishes neither superiority to a strong task-specific model nor transfer into credit. Fundamental and SAP-owned Prior Labs make that distinction consequential: Lyon must beat credible model alternatives, not merely an institution’s oldest rules.

Noya’s NVIDIA and financial-sector affiliations, alongside Höhn’s mathematics and machine-learning studies, suggest relevant technical ingredients. They do not document how either founder changes a belief after contradictory evidence or recruits someone to own a critical weakness. I would not confuse Stanford credentials with demonstrated learning rate. Equally, the dossier gives me no evidence of stubbornness, prestige-driven hiring, or premature organizational expansion. The founder dimension stays at its missing-evidence default. The unfinished credit deployment also supplies no disclosed experimental threshold, budget, or stop condition; it earns no separate credit for disciplined risk removal.

The economic question is sharper because Lyon trains a separate model inside each customer’s VPC. That can satisfy data-control requirements, but it also creates a possible cost floor: repeated training, integration, monitoring, and support for every institution. Customer ownership does not establish an exclusive data advantage or a learning loop that compounds across customers. There is no public pricing or bottom-up delivery-cost model, so unsubsidized economic superiority remains unproven. Missing revenue is ordinary at this stage. Missing evidence that the architecture makes deployment progressively cheaper leaves the central platform claim unresolved.

My exceptions matter here. Execution-heavy financial businesses can justify investment without a scientific discontinuity, and an overwhelming consequence can justify funding before formal milestones exist. Lyon’s access to substantial transaction data is the strongest argument for that override. But customer scale alone does not establish the value Lyon can capture or the societal cost it can collapse. Nor does the dossier establish an interface-wrapper red flag: these founders report training their own models. I pass because the demonstrated result remains narrower than the proposed infrastructure business, while the exceptional consequence needed to override the other gaps is unquantified.

One verifiable result would change my decision: a customer-audited credit deployment showing at least **5× lower fully loaded cost per decision at matched approval and loss rates**, against that institution’s strongest production model, with training and integration costs amortized over disclosed volume. That would connect technical capability, economic consequence, and a completed risk-removal milestone in one experiment. Until then, I am being asked to purchase several possible breakthroughs on the evidence of one promising conversion result.
