# Petrichor -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a frontier AI lab is Petrichor’s strongest path to investor cash. The memo records a $300 million commitment by model developers to a broader biological training-data initiative. Petrichor says building a wet lab in-house takes years and that its staff run experiments in parallel and deliver training records that customers own. A buyer could pay to control dedicated capacity and turnaround if Petrichor demonstrates a repeatable advantage over outsourced providers. The memo supplies no measured customer benefit or evidence that a buyer would pay for that control. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Petrichor’s public launch and YC backing support a paid production test that measures turnaround and reproducibility across repeat orders. The memo discloses no repeat usage, revenue, test budget, or runway. An acquisition would depend on transferring the lab operation and its workflows, with existing training records remaining customer-owned. Transfer rights for the operating assets are unverified. The memo supplies no independently supported fallback that could pay this investor. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Defensibility, The Idea)

**Proceeds bridge.** Using the thesis’s illustrative seed terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios below assume all-cash consideration, zero net debt, fees, and other price adjustments, and a simple pro rata waterfall with no preference claims. Under those assumptions, enterprise value equals distributable equity proceeds. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. The prices and timing are scenario inputs. The memo provides no usable valuation evidence. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Timing and distributable equity proceeds | Investor cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 4, $40 million | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7, $100 million | $3.0 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested | $0, shares retained | 0× realized |

The strong sale clears the objective at 3% and 4% ownership but yields only $2 million, or 4×, at 2%. The decisive gaps are what a buyer could justify paying and how much ownership the investor would retain.

### Countercase

A frontier AI lab may be able to buy equivalent capacity as a service, including from Petrichor. Ginkgo reports that OpenAI uses its cloud lab, and Emerald already captures structured experimental metadata. Petrichor’s customer-owned records also weaken the case for acquiring a proprietary dataset, while staff-run execution creates unresolved throughput and reproducibility risks. This evidence makes the ownership premium uncertain enough to warrant Watch. The Pass override remains untriggered because the memo establishes neither an equivalent substitute for Petrichor’s proposed workflow nor a price ceiling below the proceeds hurdle. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid, repeat production deployment show enough buyer-specific economic value from owning Petrichor’s operation to support a sale near or above the modeled proceeds hurdle? Customer-verified turnaround and reproducibility results against an available outsourcing option, paired with a buyer-side estimate of the value of owning the operation, would answer it. Evidence that the ownership advantage could support the hurdle would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that readily available services deliver equivalent economics and cap the price below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
