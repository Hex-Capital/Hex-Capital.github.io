# Ryhla -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Ryhla has a plausible building-blocks path through **faster access to usable grid power for small AI facilities**. The memo documents a national dashboard for benchmarking distribution interconnection timelines and costs. Ryhla reports that developers can wait months for a utility study before learning whether a site can support the requested load or requires expensive upgrades. Its feeder models estimate available capacity, timelines, and upgrade costs, and its filing service carries that work into the application. If AI inference deployments add demand from facilities within Ryhla’s 1–50 MW project range, earlier screening could steer developers toward feeders with headroom before they spend time and money on unsuitable sites. That scenario assumes the workload can move between candidate sites. The memo leaves AI-specific queue pressure, the time to add equivalent supply, and Ryhla’s effect on energization dates unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The industry-enabled path scores zero. The existing delivery model relies on utility studies and applications, and Ryhla’s reported savings concern developers already pursuing projects. The memo identifies no previously excluded buyer or use that AI makes affordable, and provides no cost comparison showing that a distinct service crosses such a threshold. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Ryhla’s claimed proprietary feeder data and calibration against utility studies support early defensibility, with durability dependent on continued data access and an accuracy advantage over alternatives. Buyer evidence consists of a reported live solar deployment and a battery developer’s reported avoidance of about $160,000 in fees after screening out eight of ten projects. Payment and repeat use remain unverified. Both founders researched grid modeling, and Ryhla reports live models in 11 utility territories. Its 91% approval figure covers recommended sites, with sample size and outcomes for rejected sites undisclosed. Pricing and the cost of screening, filing, and adding territory coverage are absent, so scale economics stays at the default. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Ryhla may save developers application fees while leaving the date they can draw power unchanged. Utilities control approvals and upgrades, and public capacity maps and existing interconnection software cover parts of the workflow. The disclosed customer results address solar and storage, leaving open whether Ryhla can find a viable connection for a small AI facility sooner than the developer’s existing process. That gap warrants Watch. A confirmed ability to redirect an AI project to usable capacity sooner would strengthen the building-blocks case. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Ryhla identify a feeder that gets a 1–50 MW AI facility connected materially sooner than the developer’s existing screening process? A prospective customer comparison with the same load requirements and utility-confirmed capacity, upgrade costs, and connection dates would answer it. A material connection-time advantage would move the action to Reach out. A result limited to earlier screening or lower application fees would keep it at Watch.
