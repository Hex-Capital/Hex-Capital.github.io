# Caladh -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Caladh’s proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is the first scarce check are all unknown. YC participation establishes none of those things. I will not invent an auction because the company is in the batch, but I cannot underwrite a cheap, non-consensus investment without a price. The more interesting question is what my money would prove: can Caladh actually deny an underwater vehicle’s mission under realistic harbor conditions?

Kristen Kita gives that question substance. Her MIT–WHOI doctorate and February 2022 research demonstrate passive tracking of an underwater vehicle amid passing boats. That is an experimental receipt, stronger than a defense résumé. But tracking a vehicle and defeating its navigation, communications, or sensing are different accomplishments. Eclipse and Cloak advertise the latter; the dossier supplies no measured denial performance. Kita has demonstrated competence on the underwater acoustics side of the bridge. Her STR roles do not, by themselves, establish access to procurement decisions or successful operational deployments.

The strongest bull case is that Caladh can turn that expertise into a narrow experiment with a dramatic result. Sonardyne’s Sentinel advertises underwater detection at 1,200 metres; Caladh proposes intervention and concealment. A working non-kinetic system could complement installed detection infrastructure rather than require replacing it. This fits my willingness to finance difficult physical systems when a founder understands the neglected operating problem. The material uncertainty is whether Caladh has built that bridge: neither the product descriptions nor adjacent suppliers’ capabilities demonstrate that its denial mechanism works.

That distinction matters for the capital plan. A bounded sea trial could be a venture moment, but the dossier does not document proceeds, a deadline, a pass/fail threshold, or why customer funding cannot finance the experiment. I cannot award points for designing the company’s financing hypothesis myself. Nor do briefing and pilot request forms establish a proprietary customer relationship. There is no documented purchase, retained deployment, or exclusive operating access here. Future deployment data might become a dark pool; today that remains a hypothesis.

Delete the next financing and Caladh’s operating model is also unresolved. Hardware sales with deployment services are inferred, while pricing, margins, burn, and a route to break-even are undisclosed. Those are missing facts, not evidence of bad economics. Equally, there is no documented auction pressure, compulsory serial financing, or founder-control conflict to justify an active red-flag penalty. The exceptions that could override my missing financing gates require hard evidence—customer conversion, operating economics, proprietary access, or demonstrated execution across the systems being joined. Caladh’s acoustic research is meaningful, but it does not supply those commercial receipts.

I pass on the evidence available. My verifiable flip condition is a signed financing-and-pilot package: a sub-$10 million post-money round that funds a named buyer’s independently witnessed underwater-denial trial within 12 months, with an agreed performance threshold and purchase commitment if it succeeds. That would connect price, a finite technical risk, and an actual purchasing decision. Today, Caladh offers a credible researcher and a consequential proposition; the investable transaction is still unspecified.
