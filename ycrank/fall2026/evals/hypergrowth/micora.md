# Micora -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Repeatable direct sales is Micora’s strongest path. The initial buyer is a remote industrial site operator who needs dependable power despite limited grid access. The memo documents a configurator that collects system size, location, and schedule and offers purchase or lease terms subject to site review. Micora reports deployments underway at Pacific island and manufacturing sites. Its stackable solar blocks and containerized batteries could serve the same job across markets, but the deployments are still underway and the extent of local adaptation is unresolved. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Assuming strong fit with remote industrial operators, the first link likely to break is the handoff from a qualified inquiry to paid, commissioned power. Drone surveys and robotic placement could make installation repeatable, but the memo gives no record tracing an independent buyer from inquiry to payment and go-live. It also gives no usable measure of rollout labor or marginal contribution, and the construction reports leave buyer funding and workflow commitment unverified. Recurring power demand could lead to lease renewals or orders for additional sites, but neither is evidenced. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Each sale could require fresh engineering and fieldwork. Terrain and weather can affect placement and yield, and battery sizing and backup generation depend on the site. Micora’s claimed deployment time of under three weeks for most setups is conditional on site review and gives no measure of crew effort or delivery cost. If those costs rise roughly in proportion to deployments, robotic placement alone may offer little operating leverage. The structural downside gate does not apply because the memo does not establish that those costs are unavoidable or proportional. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a second independent remote industrial buyer go from inquiry to paid, commissioned power using the same deployment process? Comparable records from two sites showing payment, a successful handoff to a non-founder, and lower engineering and field effort after accounting for freight, hardware, and any capital tied up in a lease would move Micora to Reach out. If both rollouts show that unavoidable site-specific work and capital costs scale in proportion to revenue, with no credible way to reduce them, move to Pass.
