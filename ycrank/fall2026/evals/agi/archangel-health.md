# Archangel Health -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Archangel has a narrow building-blocks case in training and qualifying clinical AI agents that must take actions inside an electronic health record. The memo documents a failure that static tests can miss. An agent can document an intended order without placing it. Archangel describes a sandbox with de-identified charts, stepwise physician grading, and follow-up outcomes for multi-visit tasks. If developers need to retest more workflows as deployments expand, access to longitudinal cases and specialist grading could limit how quickly they can qualify releases. The documented testing gap supports the product-to-workload match. Supply scarcity and the cost of obtaining equivalent coverage remain unverified. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The industry-enabled path scores zero. The memo describes licensing, evaluation, and planned post-training for health systems. It identifies no previously excluded buyer or clinical use that becomes viable through a lower cost per successful outcome. The sandbox tests whether agents can carry out clinical tasks. Whether those results transfer to live care remains open. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The strongest commercial case is custom clinical-agent evaluation. Archangel’s reported specialist grading and provenance tracking support modest credit for operating know-how, with reuse rights and defensibility still unproven. The public listing and the founders’ reported prior delivery in clinical agents and training data support seed-stage execution credit. Archangel reports use by frontier labs and medical AI developers, but the memo gives no defined trial, paid commitment, or repeat purchase. Undisclosed pricing and delivery costs leave the economics of adding another graded workflow untested. (Defensibility, Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

PhysicianBench already covers multi-step EHR workflows, and competing suppliers offer medical AI environments and annotation. If buyers can combine those alternatives to match Archangel’s coverage at the required cadence, the testing problem is real but the input is readily substitutable. The memo establishes that alternatives exist and leaves equivalence open. That gap supports Watch. Demonstrated ready substitution would trigger Pass because both paths would then score zero. (Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Can a paying clinical AI developer show, in a side-by-side test on a workflow it is preparing to deploy, that Archangel catches consequential execution failures its best available alternative misses and that matching the coverage would exceed its release budget or timeline?
