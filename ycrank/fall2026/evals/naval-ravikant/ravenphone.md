# Raven -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Isa Usmanov has built something in the exact problem he wants to own. The organizer-confirmed agentic-phone prototype searches flights, generates interfaces, and changes calendar events. That is specific knowledge acquired through action. His earlier businesses—droov’s reported $3.4M GMV and 32K users, an ed-tech company’s reported $1.1M ARR, and the Reinio sale—support execution experience, although those outcomes belong to earlier products. The strongest evidence here is the working phone, independently demonstrated in 2026.

The investment question is what Raven owns after another team reproduces the demonstration. A physical device can carry copyable software. The dossier establishes no proprietary silicon, exclusive model, accumulated workflow asset, or distribution right. Brain already has SoftBank distribution; Rabbit executes across existing devices. These alternatives make the distinction consequential: Raven must earn the inconvenience of adopting another phone. Personal context could eventually create switching costs, but that is a hypothesis today. I give missing defensibility its default rather than treating an unproven architecture as a proven commodity wrapper.

The strongest argument against passing is that Usmanov may be discovering a new interface through unusually fast iteration. Founder-reported initial shipments, 160,000 demo views, and hundreds of community signups give him people to learn from. My exceptions allow a strange working prototype to precede observable network effects, particularly when the founder demonstrates unusual capital efficiency. Raven has the prototype; capital efficiency and persistent usage remain unestablished. Attention supplies an opportunity to discover customer love. It does not establish that love already exists.

Raven fits my unstated pattern of tools that turn individual knowledge into greater productive output: completing a Gmail-to-Drive-to-Slack workflow could let one employee accomplish more without adding coordination labor. But the other compounding mechanism is absent. No evidence shows one customer contributing workflows, distribution, or useful supply for another. Hundreds of community signups do not establish a user-to-user loop. There is also no documented integrity, artificial-deadline, or founder-behavior red flag supplying a veto; the concern is the business’s structure.

The employee wedge has a plausible extension into team workflows because the same cross-application execution capability serves both. The $393.3B GenAI-smartphone forecast does little to establish demand for that extension: it includes enormous spending Raven may never address. Device-request forms identify prospective buyers without revealing their willingness to pay. Initial shipments have undisclosed payment status, so I cannot count them as realized transactions. Missing prices and revenue receive the monetization default, without importing revenue from Usmanov’s previous companies.

I pass at this stage. Raven has demonstrated a founder’s ability to build; it has yet to demonstrate an asset that compounds as customers use it. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: a controlled customer migration test showing that accumulated Raven workflow context produces a persistent task-completion advantage over a competing agent given the same application permissions. That would turn the proposed switching cost into evidence.
