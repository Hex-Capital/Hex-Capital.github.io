# Lantern AI -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Lantern AI’s opportunity is to turn AI-driven screening capacity into a lower cost per qualified hire for employers. The controlling variable is whether carrying a company’s hiring judgment upstream into sourcing improves hiring outcomes enough to win additional budget. Customer requests for candidate discovery make that proposition plausible. The dossier does not establish the improvement, its economic threshold, or whether Lantern delivers it better than existing recruiting platforms. I would keep that underwriting question open.

The strongest evidence is that employers already buy something from this team. The founder reports crossing **$1M ARR** in October 2026; the interviewer has conducted **100K+ cumulative interviews**, and the historical customer list includes Crypto.com, Hyatt and Gammon across ten markets. Those are substantial pre-seed signals, even after discounting company-reported figures. Product allocation matters: interview revenue validates demand for interviewing, while the newer sourcing platform still needs its own evidence. Separately, Gammon’s reported **40% faster time-to-shortlist** and Hyatt’s **threefold increase in weekly screening capacity** suggest a real delivery improvement. Neither measures cost per successful hire or Lantern’s gross margin.

The product architecture gives Lantern more substance than an interview summary generator. Shared hiring criteria, feedback and decision history could allow stronger models to improve sourcing and screening while Lantern retains customer-specific workflow state. Its privacy policy also documents stored communications and meeting records associated with Google access. But owning records is different from owning the hiring decision loop. Gem already offers shared-context agents across ATS and CRM history; Juicebox offers feedback-adaptive sourcing. The relevant data-moat question is how much time and money those competitors would need to reproduce Lantern’s customer-specific judgment. The dossier cannot yet answer it.

The strongest case against withholding a check is that Lantern already has the distribution channel its expansion needs. Existing interview customers requested upstream discovery, and Thomas Chan has operated two prior recruiting startups with reported “2M+” sales, albeit without a stated currency. That fits my preference for prior startup learning and for a first product becoming distribution for adjacent products. Successful cross-selling could make the sourcing expansion much cheaper than acquiring recruiting buyers from scratch. But requests establish an opening; they do not establish paid adoption or durable advantage. The scarce frontier-research-team exception does not apply here, and the red flag worth testing is whether an asserted data advantage survives competition from platforms already holding recruiting history.

I would monitor Lantern AI and hold off on a personal check. Its commercial evidence is stronger than its demonstrated explanation for winning the broader category. My flip condition is a paid, six-month customer cohort showing that adding Lantern’s sourcing to its interviewer reduces cost per qualified hire by at least **20%**, with qualification standards held constant. That would connect the existing customer channel, shared judgment and workflow expansion to one measurable economic result.
