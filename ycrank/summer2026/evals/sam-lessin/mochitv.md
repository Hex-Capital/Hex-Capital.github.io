# MOCHI.TV -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first numbers I need are missing: proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. That forces the deal score to its default. The capital plan is equally undefined. MOCHI.TV has not named one finite experiment, deadline, or pass/fail metric for the round; “make more anime and grow the feed” would finance activity, not reveal a discrete truth.

The strongest asset is the distribution machine. The team reports 300M+ organic views, 900K+ social followers, 100K+ users, and 100 original series released daily. But those numbers do not yet form a cash equation. YC materials alternate between 200M and 300M views, Google Play shows only 10K+ Android downloads, and no source reports payer conversion, retention, revenue, or which social cohort caused an in-app purchase. The $7.99 weekly subscription and coin packs prove monetization exists in the product, not that the audience buys. This is dangerously close to metric theater: very large public-platform numbers with the economically important arrows left blank.

The founders are the reason I do not dismiss it as another AI-content wrapper. Robert Qiu reports building a game to $200K in monthly revenue and products used by more than 100K people; Michael Ninh has operated consumer growth and hundreds of video creatives while bringing direct anime fluency. Together they have shipped across the technical-production and consumer-distribution sides of the bridge. That is a real outsider-insider translation layer, although the dossier does not establish who owns final product and financing decisions beyond Qiu’s CEO title.

The bear case is that generative production is not the durable asset. AniShort, Dashtoon, HOLYWATER, and GammaTime have collectively raised substantial capital for overlapping formats, while app reviewers already report unfinished series, authentication failures, and broken ads. There are no disclosed exclusive IP rights, training-data licenses, switching costs, or creator-retention figures. If the next model release makes one-day anime production commonplace, MOCHI.TV is left owning rented social distribution and an unreliable app. Creator publishing and viewing data could become a proprietary dark pool, but “could become” earns no credit until it changes purchasing or retained behavior.

NanoCo is the relevant exception to my price discipline: extreme seed pricing was offset by thousands of users, early enterprise customers, prominent technical adoption, and a rejected acquisition offer. MOCHI.TV has a similarly striking top-of-funnel number, but not the hard conversion evidence that made the exception rational. The bull case still stings—Qiu has previously monetized a consumer game, the current audience was reportedly acquired organically, and even modest payer conversion could produce a meaningful business. The dossier simply does not show that conversion or a route to break-even without another round.

I pass today. One verifiable flip condition would change the decision: a written sub-$10M post-money financing plan ring-fencing the round to a dated experiment that converts at least 2% of an identified organic cohort into 90-day retained paying users. That would connect scarce price, a venture moment, and the audience’s claimed purchasing power. Without it, I am being asked to finance impressive attention while guessing at both the deal and the business.
