# Locke -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Parth Badhwar’s White House, Deloitte policy, and campaign background makes **Locke** a domain-continuity services business whose best dimension is merely untested founder learning rate and whose worst is consensus-repelling technical risk. **Locke** combines lobbyists with AI monitoring, briefs, stakeholder maps, and proposal drafting, but no economic mechanism breaks if it works. Government affairs becomes cheaper and more integrated. It does not disappear. That is optimization, not consequence.

**Slash** (2024) is the high rung on learning rate because a documented pivot converted surprise into a new business and justified my $100M exception check. **Locke** offers no comparable pivot, thesis reversal, prior exit, or “we were wrong” moment; Parth Badhwar moved linearly from politics and policy into government-affairs software and services. I will not penalize **Locke** merely for being pre-seed, but “no evidence—applying absent-evidence default” is not evidence of learning. Locke sits well below Slash and earns only the option value of an unknown founder response.

**DoorDash** (2013) failed my capital-hostile-science test because consumer logistics required no scientific miracle and could attract generalist capital; ambition and a $72B outcome ultimately overrode that weakness. **Locke** shows the same dimensional absence without DoorDash’s demonstrated network mechanism: Quorum already serves more than 2,000 organizations, FiscalNote already sells AI policy intelligence, and Plural starts at $59 per month. Locke’s required miracles are zero in physics, zero in biology, and perhaps one in distribution. Calling general-purpose models “agents” does not manufacture technical risk.

**FiscalNote’s $84.1M ARR** demonstrates that policy intelligence can support a real business, but **Locke** compounds over the next 18 months primarily by adding relationships and service labor. Each Locke engagement may create records connecting legislation, stakeholders, and contracting outcomes, yet the dossier identifies no proprietary dataset, verification system, or product loop that improves automatically across customers. The maximum current success-state is a more efficient lobbying firm. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself” does not mean make government affairs 30% more convenient.

**Parth Badhwar’s** career across the White House, Deloitte policy, the Harris campaign, and Clipbook GTM is strong founder-market fit and weak expert defiance. **Locke** could flip this dimension if Badhwar articulated and demonstrated a controversial thesis—perhaps that autonomous agents can execute most government-affairs work with auditable accuracy while human lobbyists become an exception layer. The present Locke model instead preserves lobbyists, adds familiar monitoring tools, and accepts the industry’s existing workflow. Experts extrapolate the past. Here, the founder is productizing the past.

**Locke’s two openings**, offering as much as $200,000 in salary and up to 3.00% equity, imply an ordinary software-and-services hiring plan rather than a capital program suited to my balance sheet. **OpenAI** (2019) needed concentrated capital before revenue because model training itself was the wager; Locke needs policy staff, technical staff, and founder-led sales. Locke’s low required-capital profile is not intrinsically bad, but it means my capital supplies no special advantage and any capable seed fund can finance the roadmap.

**Quorum’s more than 2,000 customers** and FiscalNote’s $84.1M ARR make my 36-month forecast falsifiable: **Locke** will pivot toward a narrower AI-enabled government-contracting consultancy and remain below $3M ARR, rather than become a category-defining government-affairs platform. Locke’s consultation-based sales motion, undisclosed “first few partners,” human lobbyists, and absent moat produce that outcome. Services revenue may arrive, but labor scales with revenue while incumbents absorb the agent features.

**Parth Badhwar’s approximately 4,000 LinkedIn followers** and policy network form the strongest bull case: **Locke** could resemble the DoorDash exception, using operational execution and founder distribution to beat technically undifferentiated incumbents. Locke’s full-service combination of monitoring, lobbying, and contracting could also generate a proprietary outcome graph that software-only competitors lack. The objection stings because distribution sometimes matters more than science. It still loses today because Locke names no customer, revenue, repeat usage, proprietary record set, or verification process, while lobbying compliance is central to the product—an explicit red flag in my framework. I would flip to Invest if Locke reaches $5M ARR within 36 months while showing that a proprietary stakeholder-and-outcome dataset materially improves win rates without proportional growth in lobbyist headcount.
