# Agentcard -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I have three blank cells: proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. YC exposure and angels from Stripe, OpenAI, Vercel, Shopify, and dLocal do not prove an auction, but they do not prove non-consensus capital either. In an agentic-payments category already occupied by Crossmint, Skyfire, Stripe, and a $545.5 million-funded Alchemy parent, financing silence matters. I am not underwriting cheap convexity until the price is disclosed.

The second blank is what this round actually proves. Agentcard has built OAuth, MCP, CLI, and API surfaces for merchant- and amount-restricted Visa credentials, but the dossier names no use-of-funds milestone, deadline, or pass/fail experiment. “Integrate a platform serving 1.5 million users” could become a venture moment if the test is, for example, a dated threshold of completed purchases with bounded fraud and positive contribution margin. As presented, it is an integration announcement—not a capital plan or a truth-revealing result.

The strongest evidence is the translation layer. Karen Serfaty founded the expense-card company Atlas, sold it to Remote in January 2026, and then ran Cards there; Agentcard now operates an issuing program, card vault, KYC, fraud, disputes, and chargebacks for agent developers. Felipe Abello adds operating experience from Rappi and several startups. This is not a generic AI wrapper built by tourists. The team has shipped inside one side of the bridge and has already assembled a real product on the other, although direct evidence that it has mastered agent-side purchasing behavior remains thinner than the card credentials.

The dark pool is still missing. Ten “Trusted by” logos, 560 Product Hunt followers, 205 votes, and an unnamed large-platform integration show attention and possible workflow access, but none establishes a completed purchase, paid company subscription, repeat usage, or proprietary transaction data. The economics are equally unproven: $5,000 monthly company pricing is a price list, not revenue, and there is no gross margin, burn, runway, fraud-loss rate, or route to break-even without another round. Delete the Series A and I cannot yet see the business.

The bull case stings because Serfaty has already built and sold in cards, Visa opened its network to AI developers, and the product tackles ugly operational work that software tourists will underestimate. A single large integration could generate the authorization history that becomes proprietary risk infrastructure. But Agentcard presently depends on Third National, Visa, Sumsub, Stripe, MoonPay, Crossmint, and other providers while better-capitalized companies can combine the same components. The debit-card marketing versus fully collateralized credit-account terms, pending SOC 2 and PCI DSS status, and Alchemy’s identical name deepen the substitution risk. The known exception to an expensive or fashionable seed requires hard external validation—observed enterprise demand, retained usage, or an acquisition-grade asset—not launch votes and displayed logos.

I pass today. The founders are substantially better than the current evidence, but founder quality cannot manufacture scarce terms, a finite experiment, causal purchase data, or cash economics. The verifiable flip condition is one signed company contract showing at least three months of paid usage, repeat agent-initiated purchases, disclosed authorization and fraud-loss rates, and positive contribution margin—paired with a stated post-money valuation and a round plan that funds that exact test.
