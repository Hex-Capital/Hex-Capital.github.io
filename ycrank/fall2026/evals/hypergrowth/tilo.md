# Tilo -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with teams automating recurring multi-app workflows, Tilo’s strongest growth path is to serve teams whose workflows already fit its Linux app catalog. The memo documents typed MCP actions and shared workspace state across about 50 native apps. That combination could serve the same job across markets with modest geographic adaptation. The browser demo, public source, and Rust SDK let developers discover and try the product independently. The path from trial to paid production remains unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Persistent workspace history and saved integrations give teams a reason to return. Paid hosting is a scenario assumption. The memo establishes neither recurring billing nor a way for growing accounts to spend more. It also leaves revenue status, paid usage, and commercial growth unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

In the hosted scenario, contribution per workspace would be the hosting fee less incremental compute, storage, onboarding, and support. Tilo reports roughly 60 MB per agent in a 50-agent laptop test and about one twenty-ninth of the execution cost on one same-model task whose comparison run failed. These tests suggest a possible compute savings lever. A hosting price and representative production delivery costs are still needed to judge contribution. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The workflows buyers value most may depend on third-party apps outside Tilo’s native catalog. If ordinary deployments require custom integrations to preserve typed actions and shared history, and the early-alpha durability issues require ongoing support, founder effort could rise with each account. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo identifies a viable path through workflows the current catalog already covers and leaves the extent of required custom work unresolved. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of independent teams in multiple markets self-onboard to paid hosting and run a recurring production workflow using the current app catalog? Repeat invoices and measured delivery costs would show whether those accounts can generate positive contribution without bespoke founder work. A cohort that demonstrates this and raises the score to at least 65 with Medium or High confidence would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that ordinary deployments require economically prohibitive custom work would move it to Pass.
