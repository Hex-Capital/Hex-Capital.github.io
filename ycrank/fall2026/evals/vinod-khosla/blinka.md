# Blinka -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Blinka’s strongest possibility is making reliable dollar banking available to businesses that existing providers exclude. The CEO experienced the failure directly: his foreign-operated LLC lost its account and received its balance by check. Fixing that could matter considerably. But the dossier does not quantify the affected population, the financial burden, or a mechanism that reduces either by an order of magnitude. I can identify a painful problem. I cannot yet price an option on a consequential transformation.

The strongest argument for writing a check is actual usage. Blinka reports more than $10 million processed in its first two months, 25 clients before launch, and customers including Bricksave and Dapta. One customer reportedly manages over 200 entities through its portal. Those are meaningful pre-seed signals: somebody has entrusted the product with real financial operations. The more than 150 companies opening accounts represent additional interest, not necessarily active customers. Payment volume is also not revenue. These distinctions matter because the attractive hypothesis is that Blinka becomes essential financial infrastructure; the evidence currently establishes demand for its initial service.

The brothers have demonstrated tactical flexibility. They moved through mortgage automation, lending-broker software, and real-estate banking before the 2026 international-business pivot. Juan Pablo brings legal experience and firsthand account-access pain; Sebastián brings engineering and lending-software experience. That combination deserves more credit than prestigious biographies alone. But the record does not establish how quickly they revised beliefs after specific evidence, or whether they can recruit exceptional owners for banking compliance and payment reliability. This is the strongest dimension, with incomplete evidence.

Execution-heavy fintech is a genuine exception to my preference for scientific discontinuities. A narrow financial wedge can expand into a much larger platform. Blinka’s multi-entity usage makes that opposing case credible. However, the exception needs evidence that the wedge unlocks a giant value pool or creates a capability incumbents cannot readily supply. Mercury and Airwallex demonstrate substantial existing demand, not Blinka’s eventual share or advantage. Partner-bank accounts, payouts, cards, and accounting automation establish functionality; they do not establish a measured technical discontinuity. The AI tag supplies no such evidence either. I would not call this a proven wrapper, but I would not finance undisclosed invention.

The largest kill risk is whether Blinka can durably serve these customers under its partners’ actual eligibility rules. Public marketing suggests non-US entity access while the terms require a US entity or qualifying sole proprietor. That inconsistency needs resolution before customer acquisition compounds the exposure. There is no disclosed experiment with a threshold, budget, or stop condition addressing it. Nor does the dossier disclose customer pricing, partner charges, compliance costs, or loss exposure sufficient to establish unsubsidized economics. These omissions receive the stated defaults; I am not assuming misconduct, permanent subsidy dependence, or reckless spending.

I pass with my own money. Blinka has more evidence of entrepreneurial adaptation than of a mechanism capable of changing banking economics. The mission-scale exception and the execution-led financial-platform exception do not yet overcome that gap. One verifiable flip condition would be an independently audited production benchmark showing that Blinka’s own system delivers compliant cross-border treasury operations at one-tenth the fully loaded cost of an incumbent alternative, including partner and compliance expenses, with positive contribution margin. That would turn a useful bundle into evidence of a cost curve worth backing.
