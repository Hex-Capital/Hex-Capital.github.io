# Adalat AI -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** A High Court can authorize one rollout across many trial courtrooms, where judges and staff have a recurring need to turn testimony into editable records. The memo documents Kerala’s designation of Adalat AI as the primary tool for recording witness depositions. Adalat AI reports that Andhra Pradesh and Sikkim also issued mandates and that Kerala reached about 1,100 monthly active users and transcribed more than 25,600 hours in the first year. That supports recurring use at the court-system level. Whether the same users stay active or courts expand into additional workflows remains open. A magistrate’s account of hearing five to six witnesses a day, up from two to three, gives a specific signal of value in the first segment. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)

The announced Zambia launch and work with local judicial institutions provide an early test of whether the same buyer and workflow recur abroad. **Scenario assumption:** If another judicial authority can reuse the core transcription product, rollout work would center on local speech accuracy, record approval and signing rules, and staff training. A paid rollout could compound if that authority adds courtrooms or workloads and the additional revenue exceeds the added procurement effort, training, support, and transcription costs. The memo gives no paid or sustained usage data for Zambia and no rollout cost breakdown. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Countercase

The strongest case for Pass is that Adalat AI can scale courtroom usage without scaling buyer revenue. The reviewed court agreements specify pro bono deployment, the nonprofit is grant-funded, and Adalat AI reports that Kerala still needed in-person training, refreshers, and workflow changes after the mandate. Under those terms, additional court usage generates no court revenue to offset delivery costs. The Pass gate remains unmet because the memo does not establish that other judicial authorities cannot pay or that delivery effort grows in proportion to courtroom volume. (The Idea; Key Facts; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can one judicial authority outside India fund a rollout from its own budget and sustain routine deposition use with a reusable implementation? A paid agreement, usage records through the court’s normal operating cadence, and a rollout cost breakdown showing a credible path for buyer revenue to cover incremental costs would move the action to **Reach out**.
