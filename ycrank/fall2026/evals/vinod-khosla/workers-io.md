# Workers IO -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Workers IO could make failures in payments and infrastructure reproducible before they become expensive incidents. That is consequential work. But the investment question is how much expert debugging it eliminates and at what cost. The dossier supplies no baseline for engineering hours, incident losses, or the fraction of failures this system can prevent. I can see the mechanism for substantial value; I cannot yet underwrite an orders-of-magnitude consequence. Missing revenue at pre-seed does not concern me. The missing connection between technical capability and consequence does.

The strongest evidence is the implementation work. Seeded execution, intercepted I/O, and virtual clocks describe a concrete approach to controlling nondeterminism. This deserves more attention than an AI interface with a testing label. But the published seeded-Bun implementation does not establish deterministic execution of arbitrary unmodified systems. More importantly, Antithesis already sells whole-system deterministic testing. Workers IO must demonstrate a capability or cost discontinuity against that alternative. Vendor-built fault models and replayable traces establish a product direction; neither establishes a measured advantage.

The bull case has teeth. Chaitanya Choudhary reports a prior company sale and engineering experience at Grit and OnDeck. A two-person team is tackling difficult infrastructure, and its agent-skills repository has attracted 208 stars and 32 forks. Workers IO fits my recurring interest in turning scarce technical expertise into a repeatable platform. If coding agents could use its simulations to find and repair failures autonomously, the product could remove a meaningful expert bottleneck. However, the community evidence concerns the skills package, while the decisive asset is the simulation backend. The dossier documents neither repeated backend usage nor a proprietary learning loop. The founder’s experience also does not establish rapid belief revision or successful recruiting against a named technical weakness; an open hypervisor-and-kernel vacancy is evidence of recruiting intent.

I checked whether this deserves an exception to the technical-discontinuity gate. An exceptional instigator addressing a demonstrated giant value pool can justify buying an option before benchmarks exist. Workers IO has credible builder evidence, but the dossier does not establish that override. Equally, I see no basis for accusing this team of the red flags I reject: acquisition-first ambition, refusal to learn, or spending heavily on distribution while ignoring technical risk. The narrower problem is that no disclosed milestone identifies the largest kill risk, a decisive threshold, its cost, and what happens after failure. Cross-runtime determinism and useful failure discovery remain separate uncertainties.

The economics need the same discipline. Professional pricing is $2,500 monthly for 2,500 runtime hours, with $3.60 hourly overage. Those are selling prices. They reveal neither simulation delivery cost nor customer cost per useful failure discovered. I pass at **28/100**. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: a customer-run comparison on the same unmodified payments workload showing at least 10x lower fully loaded cost per independently validated, reproducible failure than Antithesis, including integration and compute costs. That experiment would turn the central claim into evidence worth financing.
