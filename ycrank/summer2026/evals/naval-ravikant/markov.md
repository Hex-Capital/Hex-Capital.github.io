# Markov -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Markov’s 150,000 Hugging Face downloads reveal its signature: permissionless research distribution attached to a business with almost no demonstrated user-value compounding. Markov can replicate code and data globally at near-zero marginal distribution cost, but each download does not yet make the corpus, contributor supply, or customer experience better for the next user. Permissionless attention is real. A network is still hypothetical.

Stack Overflow is the high rung: developers created public knowledge, public knowledge attracted developers, and distribution improved the product itself. Markov shares the permissionless front end—48,478 CC-BY-4.0 videos produced 45,000 first-month downloads—but diverges where the analogy matters. Markov’s users consume a fixed corpus rather than continuously improving it, while “Talk to us” suggests customized datasets may require negotiated contracts and human collection. Code and media are leverage; bespoke data operations are labor wearing a software jacket. The organic adoption still places Markov well above ordinary enterprise data vendors on this dimension.

Perplexity’s low-rung weakness was single-sided value: one user’s search did not directly improve another user’s experience, and only extreme founder-specific knowledge plus pre-consensus AI timing justified the exception. Markov shows the same weakness without Aravind Srinivas’s documented Berkeley retrieval depth: the proposed contributor network and task library are inferred, while no customer retention or proprietary collection growth is documented. Markov’s 10,000 recorded hours are inventory, not a network. The Perplexity exception therefore warns against scoring the absence generously; it does not rescue Markov.

Dev Mandal’s Sarvam AI experience and prior doubt.ai launch provide the mechanism by which specific knowledge could compound over the next 18 months: Markov can learn which GUI trajectories actually improve computer-use models, convert those findings into RL environments, and make every customer engagement refine the collection system. Harish Ashok adds robotics and hardware-workflow experience, while Markov already spans Salesforce, AutoCAD, Blender, Photoshop, Excel, and VS Code. The evidence is stronger than pedigree-as-thesis, but neither founder has yet shown a multi-year public obsession, major open-source artifact, or deployed computer-use model. Specific knowledge is emerging through shipping, not yet proven as non-replicable.

The $3.2 billion training-dataset market is projected to reach $16.3 billion by 2033, giving Markov room to move from raw recordings into evaluation benchmarks, RL environments, contributor infrastructure, or agent observability. Markov would flip into the top tier on pivot-room if the 10,000-hour corpus became a reusable computer-use evaluation platform serving several model families rather than a specialized data supplier selling projects. Without that proof, Scale AI, Surge AI, and Turing define the uncomfortable counterfactual: a growing market can still reduce Markov to a feature inside a much larger vendor.

Twitter had Ev Williams’s clean Blogger exit and Jack Dorsey’s visible shipping record before the asymmetric payoff was obvious. Markov has energy signals—two founders, zero active job listings, 150,000 downloads, and a 12,300-hour public release—but no public co-founder history, prior-team record, or evidence bearing on integrity. Markov therefore clears intelligence and energy provisionally while leaving the ten-year-partner test unresolved. Silence on integrity is not guilt. It is unfinished diligence when investing one’s own money.

Scale AI’s $870 million of 2024 revenue shows that data supply can become enormous, but Markov’s highest signal is distribution while its lowest is compounding user value. By July 2029, I expect Markov to have pivoted from selling raw GUI recordings into hosted computer-use evaluation and RL environments, reaching $8 million to $15 million ARR while remaining an independent specialist. Markov’s Hugging Face reach supplies the top of the funnel; reusable environments must replace custom collection labor for the forecast to hold.

Markov’s 48,478-video release is the strongest bear case against that forecast: CC-BY-4.0 makes the flagship corpus reusable by competitors, Turing has already delivered 10,000 supervised GUI tasks, and no paying customer, contract, price, or revenue is disclosed. The unstated builder-tool pattern helps Markov, but the red flag is clear—organic adoption may be research curiosity feeding a labor-intensive enterprise service rather than product pull. Anchorage Digital’s permission-as-moat exception does not apply because Markov has no regulatory barrier, and Perplexity’s specific-knowledge exception is not yet strong enough. I would still write a small option check on the permissionless distribution and category timing; I would flip to Pass if Markov cannot show repeat paid use of the same evaluation or RL environment by at least two independent AI labs within twelve months.
