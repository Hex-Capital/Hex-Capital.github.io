# Rodeo Robotics -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A shop-equipment supplier could buy Rodeo’s integrated inspection, diagnosis, and routine-service system and sell it across its distribution network. Ownership could justify a premium over licensing if control of the hardware and software lets the buyer expand vehicle and task coverage faster and capture more of the resulting sales. That benefit is inferred. The memo describes the system and Rodeo’s reported custom hardware, but gives no measured buyer benefit, acquisition interest, or relevant sale price. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Rodeo reports 96% autonomous execution time on initial tasks and targets January 2027 customer deployments. Those claims support a plausible production test. The milestone is a paid shop deployment showing repeatable gains in completed jobs per bay and technician time saved across representative vehicles, after accounting for supervision and interventions. The memo lists a $500,000 convertible note, with funding availability, test costs, and runway unverified. Ownership and transferability of the core technology and data rights are also unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility)

**Proceeds bridge.** The shared scenario assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million in gross holder cash. That requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. At 3%, a $25 million enterprise-value sale in year eight yields $600,000 (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×), each assuming $5 million of illustrative net debt and simple pro rata distribution before investor taxes. On those debt assumptions, the hurdle is $88.3 million in enterprise value. The listed note’s conversion and priority terms are unknown. Neither sale price is supported by buyer economics or transaction evidence in the memo. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

If deployment growth plateaus, strategic buyers stay away, public scale stays out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. The memo provides no cash-generation evidence for a sponsor buyout or company-funded payout, no evidence of an eligible secondary, and no evidence of IPO readiness. Wind-down recovery would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantifiable. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

PitPro has installed a tire-changing robot, and Automated Tire offers overlapping tire service and inspections. A strategic buyer could use a narrower product or license Rodeo’s system if its broader workflow adds little throughput after technician interventions. The reported autonomy figure measures time on initial tasks and leaves net labor savings in production unresolved. This weakens the case for an acquisition premium. The evidence establishes neither a structural block nor a supported price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle, so the Pass gate stays open. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production pilot show enough incremental value across representative vehicles to support a strategic acquisition that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Measured bay throughput and net technician time saved, translated into a credible buyer-specific valuation range after comparing ownership with licensing, would move the action to Reach out if the range supports $2.5 million in holder cash within eight years.
