# Fabraix -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Fabraix’s 78% AgentHarm attack-success rate, versus 67% for GPT-5.6 Sol, makes Fabraix a technically credible adaptive attacker with almost no fit for my capital. That high-technical-risk, low-required-capital pairing is Fabraix’s signature. The product may expose how unsafe deployed agents are, but the economic mechanism it breaks is manual red-teaming—not the labor economy those agents might eventually replace. Consequence belongs to the agent builders. Fabraix collects a security toll.

**Physical Intelligence** (2024) is the high technical-risk rung because learned agents must master unpredictable state-action spaces across software, hardware, and the physical world. Fabraix shares the adaptive-learning mechanism: 10,000-plus attack strategies, adversarial self-play, retained successful attacks, and black-box testing across chat, voice, browser, and coding agents. Fabraix diverges where the analogy matters most. There is no proprietary foundation model, physical stack, or named scientific breakthrough; Promptfoo, Garak, PyRIT, Lakera, and Mindgard already reproduce parts of the workflow. This is serious engineering, not a miracle consensus VCs cannot underwrite.

**DoorDash** (2013) had the low-rung failure mechanism on capital fit: no greater-than-$50 million technical milestone, so my capital was not the scarce input. DoorDash overcame that mismatch by reshaping consumer-commerce flow and building exceptional distribution. Fabraix shows an even weaker capital requirement—an open-source Playground, no disclosed financing roadmap, no pricing, and no revenue signal. Fabraix has Product Hunt rank #6 and 71 GitHub stars, not the distribution exception that turned DoorDash into a $72 billion outcome. Any competent generalist can finance this roadmap.

Prompt Security’s 2025 acquisition by SentinelOne demonstrates how Fabraix’s consequence mechanism likely compounds over the next 18 months: more deployed agents create more attack paths, Fabraix gathers more trajectories, and enterprise-security platforms gain more reason to bundle the capability. The problem is real, but the value flows toward the platform controlling runtime protection and remediation. Fabraix found vulnerabilities in agents at dozens of Fortune 500 companies, yet none is identified as a paying customer and no remediation outcome is disclosed. Protecting labor-replacing agents is useful. It is not the same as replacing labor. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself” requires Fabraix to own the consequence, not merely audit it.

Ahmed Aly led fraud systems processing more than $1 billion annually, while Ibrahim Abdu built production-debugging agents at Meta. Those credentials establish founder-market fit for Fabraix, but they currently read as expert continuity—the exact pattern experts use to extrapolate the past. The score flips a tier if Fabraix proves that Aly and Abdu rejected a security-industry consensus that static payload libraries or runtime filters were sufficient, then publicly demonstrate an adaptive attack class those systems fundamentally cannot detect. Fabraix’s claim that static rankings diverge from adaptive attack economics points in that direction, but a contrarian result is not yet documented expert defiance.

**Slash** (2024) earned an exception because a documented pivot exposed unusually high founder learning rate. Fabraix has no comparable founder pivot, prior exit, or public “we were wrong” thesis update. Fabraix’s self-play loop retaining successful attacks and the Playground generating more than 50,000 attempts demonstrate product learning, not necessarily Aly’s or Abdu’s learning rate. A short pre-seed history makes absence ambiguous, so I apply the persona default rather than manufacture evidence.

Lakera’s acquisition by Check Point and Prompt Security’s acquisition by SentinelOne predict Fabraix’s likely destination. By July 2029, I expect Fabraix to reach $3 million to $8 million in ARR and be acquired by Palo Alto Networks for less than $250 million, rather than become an independent category-defining platform. Fabraix’s adaptive attacks can become valuable threat intelligence, but platform bundling, open-source replication, and the absence of capital-hostile science make acquisition the natural equilibrium.

Fabraix’s strongest bull case is a proprietary attack-data flywheel: 50,000-plus Playground attempts feed 10,000-plus strategies, adaptive self-play beats GPT-5.6 Sol by 11 percentage points, and vulnerabilities across dozens of Fortune 500 agents create the beginnings of a Gandalf-like distribution engine. DoorDash proves my capital-intensity criterion can be overridden when distribution compounds into a new economic flow. The objection stings, but Fabraix has disclosed no paying Fortune 500 customer, exclusive data arrangement, retention advantage, or structural barrier while Mindgard and Lakera already automate red-teaming. I would flip to Invest if Fabraix secured ten named Fortune 500 customers renewing at least $250,000 of annual contract value each.
