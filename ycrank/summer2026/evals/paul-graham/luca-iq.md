# Luca IQ -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The important obstacle was not persuading people that tax software is old. It was getting a new calculation engine through the machinery required to file real returns. Luca IQ built a deterministic rules engine, preserved a source-level audit trail, produced MeF-compatible output, completed a tax season with customers, and passed IRS Assurance Testing in July 2026. That is shipped work under resistance. But the dossier does not reveal what specifically broke during development, how the founders worked around it, or how long the correction took. I can see the result, not yet the running back. That earns 18 of 31.

The technical proof is stronger. This is not an AI wrapper that produces plausible tax advice. The product calculates returns deterministically, maps source documents to return lines, performs diagnostics, and transmits filings through IRS infrastructure. Federal 1040 authorization is an external threshold rather than a benchmark the founders invented. The limited return coverage and unfinished SOC 2 certification matter, but they describe the remaining distance, not the absence of a working artifact.

The narrow well is CPA firms drowning in re-entry and administrative preparation. One customer reports saving 1,100 hours in a season, reducing administrative preparation by 57%, and reaching review-ready status in 30 minutes on average. Hundreds of firms applied for the pilot, and actual customers used the system through a tax season. Those are much better signals than LinkedIn followers or press coverage. But the strongest numbers come from an anonymous testimonial, and Luca IQ discloses neither paid-customer count nor retention. Applications show curiosity. Renewals would show pull.

Angelo Policicchio did not discover taxes in a market report. He worked as a CPA, an EY public accountant, and an FBI forensic accountant. Akash Sadashivapeth supplies the engineering and data-science half. The wedge also contains genuine schlep blindness: changing tax rules, IRS schemas, assurance testing, audit trails, security reviews, and error liability. Still, the dossier gives me a résumé-shaped origin rather than a dated story of Angelo encountering a specific failure and building the first version for himself. The founder-market fit is credible, but not yet autobiographical proof.

The strongest objection is that this could be two newly acquainted Harvard MBAs packaging a respectable product for a crowded market. April and Column Tax already offer filing infrastructure; Filed and TaxGPT automate adjacent workflows. Luca IQ has hired an outbound-sales intern before disclosing even one paying firm, while the founders’ shared history appears limited to HBS programs in 2026. That activates two real red flags: a recently assembled cofounder relationship and the possibility of delegating customer discovery before the founders have learned sales themselves. The counterweight is the unstated pattern I care about most: a difficult, externally verified proof existed before the round, and customers used it in production. This does not require a known-exception override; the IRS authorization and completed tax season satisfy the ordinary technical gate.

My forecast is that by August 2029 Luca IQ will remain an independent tax-infrastructure company serving CPA firms and developers at $6–12 million ARR, without yet displacing the full tax suites. I would invest, but narrowly. The founders have crossed the resourcefulness gate and the technical-proof gate; they have not yet shown that they can repeatedly sell, retain users, or change route when evidence demands it. One verifiable condition flips me to Pass: fewer than ten paying CPA firms filing at least 100 returns each through Luca IQ during the 2027 tax season.
