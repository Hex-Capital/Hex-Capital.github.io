# Sutrix -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Research staff can spend weeks or months reviewing charts and reconciling EHR, REDCap, and wearable records. Sutrix automates record linking and protocol rules, then sends unresolved records to staff for review with source evidence. A rugby research customer reported getting from questionnaire records to findings in days instead of weeks, and BlueRock reported less manual reconciliation. Sutrix claims an 80% cut in preparation time. These accounts support a plausible labor-saving mechanism, while the cost per accepted dataset and the number of studies the savings made viable remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

**Scenario assumption:** A budget-constrained biostatistics core could run a previously shelved study if the full cost per investigator-accepted dataset falls below its budget. The manual baseline includes chart review, reconciliation, and verification. Sutrix’s cost includes data access and integration, inference, compliance, exception review, rework, and final verification. The unit of expansion is another accepted dataset at an approved institution. Reusing connections and study rules could lower its cost, depending on how much work each new protocol and data source requires. If rivals have equivalent models, buyers might still pay Sutrix to reuse approved rules, field-level provenance, and reviewer decisions. Buyer preference under that comparison remains untested. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Sutrix reports paying teams at 10+ institutions and $150K+ in revenue before releasing its software to biostatistics cores. That supports early willingness to pay for dataset preparation. Repeat software purchases and demand from teams that previously went without remain open. At seed, the software release and the CEO’s prior delivery of a 300-participant IRB-approved study support execution credit. Production data access and reliable protocol handling across institutions are the next execution hurdles. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Sutrix may be speeding up studies that already have funding while delivering them through labor-intensive engagements. Direct FHIR connectivity is listed for sandboxes, and production data access, protocol exceptions, and investigator verification can add work on each deployment. Dyania and Layer already automate adjacent chart-abstraction tasks. If a rival can reproduce the multi-source workflow with equivalent models and buyers can carry over their approved rules and review history, Sutrix’s switching-cost hypothesis weakens. The memo leaves both market opening and value capture under model parity unresolved. The customer reports justify testing the mechanism, while the decisive economics and buyer choice remain unmeasured. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent, budget-constrained biostatistics core place a paid repeat order with Sutrix after using it to complete a study it had shelved because of preparation cost? Move to **Reach out** if the core documents that Sutrix brought the full cost of an accepted dataset within budget and chooses Sutrix again at a price that covers delivery costs after trying a functionally equivalent alternative. If a representative trial shows that full delivery costs still put the study out of reach or that the buyer readily switches under parity, **Pass**.
