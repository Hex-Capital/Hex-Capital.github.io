# Simulithic -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Simulithic has no supported building-block path. Its agents verify application changes after coding agents produce them. The memo describes release verification as a bottleneck for engineering teams, but identifies no scarce input that constrains an AI workload or buildout. (The Idea; Market & Traction)

The supported industry mechanism is per-pull-request testing of multistep journeys across browser and device combinations that a small engineering team could previously afford to check only selectively. That is a seed-stage scenario. The company describes manual checks and Playwright maintenance as the existing workflow and documents agent-based journey discovery, visitor-informed route selection, and paired preview and baseline runs. The inference is that automating journey discovery and replay could reduce labor enough to make broader testing routine. Under comparable model capability, the delivered service would still need app-specific journey data, device execution, and integration into release checks. The memo provides no measured savings or evidence that a buyer has crossed that affordability threshold. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

The visitor SDK and workspace journey maps give Simulithic a plausible switching-cost advantage if accumulated history improves coverage. The memo records one named user's report of trying the product. The founders claim several live deployments and weekly growth on an unspecified metric. Paid conversion and repeat use remain unverified. The documented QA launch and the founders' engineering experience support early execution credit, while dependable regression detection remains unverified. The published $1-per-run credit rate gives a price reference, but realized revenue and the cost of delivering reliable checks are missing, leaving scale economics at the default. These gaps keep evidence confidence Low. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Founders & Team; Key Risks; Key Facts)

### Countercase

Simulithic may make existing QA cheaper without opening a use buyers previously skipped. BrowserStack already combines a device cloud with testing agents, and TestSprite already discovers application flows. If those tools deliver comparable coverage, Simulithic's journey history may carry little switching value. Run counts multiply across flows, devices, and agents, while missed paths and native-device limits can leave teams doing manual verification. The memo therefore supports Watch. If the labor savings fail to make previously skipped coverage routine, the industry path falls to zero and the decision becomes Pass. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid pilot turn a named team's previously skipped browser and device journeys into routine pull-request checks at a cost and reliability the team accepts? Compare the same journeys against the team's current workflow and a comparable AI testing tool, including setup, run charges, reruns, and human triage. Move to Reach out if the team keeps paying for the added coverage and identifies a concrete workflow advantage over the alternative. Move to Pass if the pilot shows that the added coverage still exceeds the team's budget at its required reliability.
