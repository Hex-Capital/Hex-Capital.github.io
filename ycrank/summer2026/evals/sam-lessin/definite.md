# Definite -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I start with three blanks: no proposed post-money valuation, no competing-term-sheet count, and no evidence that this is the first genuinely scarce check. YC participation is not itself proof of an auction, but it is not proof of non-consensus pricing either. When price is part of the thesis, financing silence earns the low prior. I cannot tell whether I am buying cheap uncertainty or paying Demo Day prices for a category that Numeric, Stacks, and Basis have already validated with $38 million, $35 million, and $137.6 million of disclosed funding.

The product contains a credible venture moment, but the round does not name one. Definite could test whether a bank will replace weeks of regulatory-report preparation with evidence-linked workpapers that pass human review, yet the dossier supplies no customer, deadline, acceptance threshold, or use-of-funds plan. The movement from Ledgix to Bylaw, then bank reporting, then broad reconciliation and controls makes that omission more consequential: there are several plausible experiments and no declared card to flip.

The founders are the strongest part of this bet. Gurshabd worked on wallet-policy and approval controls at BitGo; Mazin built quantitative-trading systems at Optiver and financial-analysis agents at Boosted.ai; Farhan worked on recommendations and machine-learning agents at Meta. That is a real translation layer between regulated financial operations and production ML, not three résumés stapled to an accounting idea. The deterministic calculation engine, source mapping, exception rules, and evidence lineage also show that the team understands why a probabilistic model cannot be allowed to invent the books.

But the proprietary workflow is still prospective. Definite reports 170 LinkedIn followers, 11 YC Launch votes, and benchmark results exceeding GPT-5 on FinBalance at roughly 50% lower cost; none demonstrates that owned data or embedded workflow caused a purchase, pilot, referral, or repeat use. The model benchmark is especially fragile as a moat. DeepSeek V3 performance can be repriced or surpassed, while well-funded competitors can build deterministic verification around the same improving models. Customer-specific mappings and policies could become a dark pool, but today they are an inferred switching-cost story without a deployed customer behind it.

The strongest bull case is that this resembles the mechanism that made Ando an exception to my skepticism about AI applications: a measurable system wrapped around a costly enterprise workflow rather than an undifferentiated chat interface. The material difference is that Ando had greater than 90% forecast accuracy alongside complete pilot-to-paid conversion and retention. Definite has a company-reported benchmark and no operating receipts. Custom order forms with advance payment are sensible contract architecture, but they establish neither revenue nor gross margin, burn, runway, or a seed-funded path to break-even.

I am passing, despite liking the team and architecture. My base case is that the founders eventually narrow Definite back toward regulated-bank reporting, because the technical bridge is stronger than the current distribution, but that is not yet an angel-underwritable business. The single flip condition is a signed, prepaid pilot with a named regulated institution that defines a dated reporting workflow, measurable acceptance standard, and conversion into recurring revenue upon success. That would simultaneously prove the venture moment and show that the customer-specific workflow causes a purchase; without it, I am financing product scope rather than revealed truth.
