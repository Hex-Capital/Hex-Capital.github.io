# Touchmark -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: inference demand is growing, model providers need forward financing, and AI companies may prepay for discounts—but Touchmark becomes large only if it creates enough recurring liquidity in each model-and-delivery-month contract for savings and resale certainty to outweigh rapid price depreciation. Liquidity is the controlling variable. The dossier identifies its causal role, but provides no threshold, participant count, executed volume, or completed resale demonstrating that the market works.

The economics currently cut against that belief. One billion GLM 5.2 tokens lists for $3,800 and MiniMax-M3 for $870, but without spot-price comparisons, a disclosed take rate, or evidence of provider-funded discounts, these are prices rather than proven savings. Inference prices declined roughly 600-fold from 2020 to 2026. A contract tied to a named model and future month can therefore become obsolete faster than secondary liquidity develops. Growing inference demand expands the underlying market, but it does not by itself improve Touchmark’s margins or adoption.

The strongest bull case is my tendency to buy coverage across an important technology discontinuity before every layer has settled. The adjacent inference market was estimated at $106.15 billion in 2025, OpenRouter reportedly reached approximately $50 million in annualized revenue, and Ornn and SF Compute have raised $33 million and $40 million respectively. Those facts validate capacity procurement as a category. They also show that the market is already crystallizing around funded exchanges and routing platforms. Touchmark needs more than category correctness when OpenRouter could add reservations and resale through existing distribution.

The product is meaningfully deeper than a pricing page: funded balances, an order book, settlement, OpenAI-compatible delivery, and pre-consumption resale form an integrated transaction workflow. That earns credit. There is nevertheless no documented provider integration, proprietary risk engine, executed-price history, or structural barrier. The named-model inventory also triggers the central model-improvement red flag: a stronger or cheaper release can destroy the value of existing positions rather than make the platform more useful. The probable durable workflow is procurement and routing, not necessarily a forward exchange.

Bolgov’s mathematics-and-finance training and Revolut trading-product experience fit the market-design problem; Yanushevskyi’s Citadel Securities quant work, Lovable AI experience, and IOI gold medal fit pricing and infrastructure implementation. This is a technically strong two-person team, but the dossier documents neither prior startup learning nor costly commitment demonstrating desperation. The known overrides do not resolve the market gate: this is not frontier research led by scarce model builders, nor a physical-market reconstruction with demonstrated margin transformation, nor a technical asset whose scarcity independently creates value.

I pass today. My base case is that by August 2029 Touchmark will have pivoted from resellable named-model futures into brokered spot or committed-use inference procurement rather than exceeding $5 million ARR as a forward exchange. I would flip if it publishes three consecutive months above $1 million in monthly executed notional at a positive take rate, with at least 30% of volume coming from repeat buyers. That would validate the one variable on which the rest of the company depends.
