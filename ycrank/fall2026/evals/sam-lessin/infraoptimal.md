# InfraOptimal -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

InfraOptimal has demonstrated engineering work. It has not yet demonstrated what my money would buy. Proposed post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and whether this is the first genuinely scarce check are all undisclosed. I will not infer an auction from the YC badge, but I cannot infer cheap optionality either. The other primary gate is equally unresolved: the dossier names no capital-funded experiment with a deadline and a measurable result. Optimizing inference is a product objective; it does not establish what truth this particular round reveals.

The strongest reason to keep looking is that the founders have worked below the application layer. Cassidy’s 600-plus RISC-V certification tests and the team’s published compiler experiments are concrete technical receipts. Kocheta’s robotics and research background adds relevant context, although those affiliations do not establish a deployed customer operation. This fits my pattern of backing people who translate between systems: hardware constraints on one side, software workloads on the other. The missing bridge is commercial. There is no documented operating access to inference buyers or evidence that these founders have already delivered production serving for them.

The September 2026 EvoCompiler work makes the bull case more credible than a generic promise that agents will improve everything. A measured 3–5.6% decoder-time reduction on an Apple M2 Pro, reproducible experiments, and a scoped Z3 proof show that the team can find and examine real transformations. Small efficiency gains can matter on expensive infrastructure. But the maintainers’ warning about regressions on microcontrollers and DSPs exposes the central economic question: how much verification and target-specific work must accompany each saving? The proof covers an integer identity, not whole-program correctness. Moving from that research to continuously optimized dedicated endpoints is a substantial additional claim.

Who captures the savings matters as much as whether they exist. AWS already offers kernel-writing, debugging, and profiling agents; Together and Baseten already optimize serving. InfraOptimal’s retained transformations could become proprietary knowledge, but cross-customer compounding remains an inference. A console, coding-agent integration, and consultation bookings establish routes to the product, with no documented purchase or retained usage attributable to them. Prepaid minute billing, including idle time, could help collections, but it establishes neither contribution margin nor a seed-funded route to break-even. Customer-cloud deployment is potentially attractive; I still need to know whether optimization revenue exceeds compute, benchmarking, support, and failure costs.

I would pass on the evidence available. The strongest objection is that a technically capable two-person team could turn a narrow, measurable efficiency advantage into a profitable infrastructure business before broad adoption appears. That possibility is real. The documented exceptions to my usual gates, however, involved observed conversion, retention, enterprise demand, or explicit financing structures; InfraOptimal supplies technical evidence without those commercial overrides. The relevant red flag is incumbent substitution, not an assumed financing auction or a claim that these founders are merely wrapping a model. My verifiable flip condition would be a documented sub-$10 million post-money round that fully funds one dated, paid production experiment demonstrating repeatable customer savings at positive contribution margin. That would connect the engineering result, the capital requirement, and an investable price.
