# Wingman Law -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Even assuming strong product-market fit in California, Wingman’s current offering is direct legal representation. The memo documents California-focused intake and claims guides, a California-admitted managing attorney, and state-specific limits on AI delegation and confidential data handling. Evidence gathering and drafting could transfer across markets. Delivering the full service would likely require locally authorized counsel and substantial adaptation of claims and compliance workflows. The memo establishes no credible near-term path to making those changes repeatable, so the structural downside gate applies. (The Idea, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

Referrals, local guides, and free online intake give clients a reusable way to find Wingman and submit a case. Attorneys still have to take on and handle each matter. The contingency fee is a one-time payment upon recovery, with no documented recurring purchase or expansion mechanism for the same client. The memo reports several six-figure cases in progress but establishes neither fee collections nor pre-revenue status, so initial traction receives the 7-point default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The relevant cost unit is an accepted case. Expected collected fees must cover incremental acquisition, intake, attorney time, and agent operating costs. Wingman says its agents gather evidence and prepare documents and communications for attorney approval. If that saves more staff time than review and rework consume, contribution per case could improve. The memo gives no case-level cost or review-time measurements, and delayed recoveries can stretch the time between incurring costs and collecting fees. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Wingman’s internal platform connects calls, texts, emails, and case files to the same attorney approval workflow. If most casework uses that common workflow, locally authorized attorneys could reuse the platform and handle more cases per attorney. That is the strongest case for scaling the firm. The workflow supports the possibility of labor savings, but the memo leaves both the extent of local adaptation and the net time saved unverified. The structural gate remains in effect. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Wingman complete a cohort of cases in a foreign jurisdiction using the same core platform with limited local configuration? A lawful rollout that documents the changes required, collects contingency fees, and shows positive contribution after local acquisition and attorney review costs would move the action to Watch.
