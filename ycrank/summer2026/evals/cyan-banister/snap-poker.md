# Snap Poker -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. The compelling version of Snap Poker is not merely poker with reels; it is a spectator network where hands become participatory stories, players become creators, and the audience supplies the liquidity that gambling platforms normally purchase. That future has a plausible shape, but the dossier does not map the path to it. Pennsylvania and Michigan enlarging interstate play is a regulatory change, not evidence that poker hands naturally become a social medium. The website was inaccessible on August 26, 2026, and there is no published hand, follower, creator, or repeat-usage count—the small artifact leaking from that future is missing.

The most provocative idea here is zero rake. Why does the casino take money from every pot? Because it provides licensing, geolocation, fraud controls, payments, moderation, and enough simultaneous players to make a table useful. Snap Poker proposes monetizing like a game instead, potentially returning money to players and giving creators ownership of an audience. I like the transfer of agency. But without disclosed purchases or another economic mechanism, zero rake may be the removal of revenue before the team has removed the underlying costs.

The founders have real first-believer energy elsewhere: Dillon Mehta presented at NeurIPS and IEEE IEMDC, Neel Gadde worked as a founding engineer at Nuntius, and Rishan Hemrajani co-authored an open-source-software study. They are young technical builders and best friends. Yet the evidence connects them to CueBench as recently as July 31, 2026, not to years of poker play, gambling operations, or compliance work. A quick pivot can be intelligent, but it does not establish independently derived obsession with this system. Poker hides a spectacular amount of operational tuition behind a simple table.

The strongest bull case is cultural rather than technical. GGPoker and PokerStars already prove that players share hands, while neither source establishes a native public follower graph. If Snap Poker makes publishing a voiceover hand feel native, the content could recruit spectators, spectators could become players, and that loop could solve distribution and liquidity together. The expanded 38-million-person interstate pool gives the loop more room to start. This is the one route by which an apparently thin feature becomes a strange live ritual rather than another poker client.

But I cannot award the ritual before anyone performs it. GGPoker already has SnapCam, voice messages, reactions, and Hand Moments; Snap Poker discloses no controlled technology, proprietary data, content volume, or player behavior. It must also acquire jurisdictional approval, enforce eligible-location controls, moderate voice content, and overcome an existing “Snap Poker” name associated with 888poker. Those are three businesses—regulated operator, liquidity marketplace, and social network—arriving simultaneously, with no evidence yet that one finances the others.

I pass today. My verifiable flip condition is narrow: show a legally operable cohort in which at least 100 players complete three or more sessions, publish at least 500 interactive hands, and bring measurable new players through those hands within 30 days. That would demonstrate both the weird behavior and the audience-to-liquidity mechanism. Until then, zero rake is an attractive broken-rule thesis attached to a concept whose future chain, founder obsession, working artifact, and controlled enabling asset remain unproven.
