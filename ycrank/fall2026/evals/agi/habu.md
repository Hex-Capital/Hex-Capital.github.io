# HABU -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

HABU sponsors Phoenix multifamily developments and uses internal AI agents for parcel screening and first-pass underwriting. The memo describes a housing development workflow and identifies no scarce input that HABU supplies to an AI workload. Building blocks scores 0/50. (The Idea)

Institutional investors and family offices already hire developers to coordinate land searches, diligence, and bids. HABU estimates that a project takes at least 1,000 hours of desk work and reports a working screening module. The memo identifies no buyer or project class that this labor burden previously priced out. HABU’s targets of five times as many projects per manager and up to 20% faster delivery lack a completed-project comparison. Industry enabled scores 0/50, so the two-path gate yields Pass. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

For the existing sponsor business, HABU’s retained parcel decisions and manually gathered Phoenix approval knowledge offer a modest operating advantage. Incumbents could reproduce parts of the screening workflow with public data and their own records. The first site under contract and the founders’ development and AI operations experience support early execution. The corporate raise finances HABU itself, and prospective project checks remain CEO-reported interest. The memo documents no project investor commitment or fee-only engagement. Development fees and a contingent promote describe potential revenue, while the delivery costs and capital required for the next project remain unquantified. (Defensibility; Key Risks; Market & Traction; Founders & Team; The Idea)

### Countercase

If conventional screening and diligence costs make a class of smaller infill projects uneconomic, HABU’s agents could reduce the cost of finding and underwriting a viable site enough to bring those projects into reach. Even with equally capable models, investors would still need a sponsor to secure the site, arrange financing, navigate approvals, and deliver the building. This scenario has a plausible labor-cost lever. It leaves the Pass gate in effect because the memo identifies neither a concrete excluded project class nor savings large enough to bring one above a financing hurdle. (The Idea; Key Risks)
