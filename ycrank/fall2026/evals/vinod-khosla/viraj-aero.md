# Viraj Aero -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Viraj Aero addresses a consequential constraint: electricity availability can prevent a data center from existing. The IEA estimates grid bottlenecks could delay approximately 20% of planned global capacity through 2030. But the investment question is how much of that constraint Viraj can remove, at what cost. The $9.7 billion generator market establishes spending, not transformative consequence. The dossier supplies no quantified bridge from this turbine to an order-of-magnitude increase in affordable power availability. That consequence remains unproven.

The engineering mechanism deserves attention. Recover combustion water, use exhaust heat to generate steam, inject it to increase mass flow and moderate combustion temperatures. The condenser must recover enough water without its cooling requirements and parasitic loads consuming the advantage. Viraj reports full-scale aircraft-prototype integration, which gives the engineering effort substance. However, 45% electrical efficiency and 2 ppm NOx remain development specifications. The differing 30% and 40% additional-power claims lack comparable baselines. Neither establishes a measured 5–10x discontinuity against Boom, Turbotech, or a conventional genset.

The strongest argument against passing is the team. Joseph Risson brings propulsion experience from Beyond Aero and Ascendance; Mathilde Bouilloux developed six power projects totaling more than 120 MW. CTO Emeric Sergent and COO Nicolas Béchet add relevant turbine and aerospace operating experience. That is a useful combination of disciplines around physical risks. It earns credit beyond prestigious biographies, although the dossier documents neither rapid belief revision nor unusually difficult recruiting wins. The €13 million of aviation LOIs and company-reported Safran work strengthen the case that this team can attract industrial interest. Those LOIs still do not validate data-center demand.

Viraj fits two patterns I favor: accepting certification timelines and improving installed industrial infrastructure through a retrofit. A 2027 demonstrator flight and targeted 2029 commercialization are reasonable ambitions for this category. They do not establish a sequence of dollars spent against risk removed. The largest apparent uncertainty is sustained water recovery under hot operating conditions; the dossier gives no completed decisive test, numerical threshold, or experiment budget addressing it. The €4.5 million financing and 15 collaborators do not themselves demonstrate my premature-scaling red flag. Spending allocation is undisclosed, so I award neither execution credit nor a penalty on that assumption.

Economics could overturn the technical objection. A modest component improvement can produce a major customer benefit if it makes previously unavailable power affordable. But Viraj provides no equipment price, condenser cost, maintenance burden, utilization assumptions, or net cost per reliable MWh. Consequently, unsubsidized competitiveness remains missing evidence. I would not infer permanent subsidy dependence from public funding, but I cannot underwrite cost parity from an efficiency target either.

I pass with my own money today. My exceptions allow an extraordinary instigator or overwhelming societal consequence to outweigh incomplete milestones and economics; this dossier establishes relevant builders and real demand, but neither override strongly enough. **My flip condition is one independently witnessed, 1,000-hour hot-ambient comparison showing closed-loop operation without makeup water and lower all-in cost per reliable MWh than a named conventional genset.** That result would connect the condenser to dependable, economically useful power—the missing causal link in Viraj Aero’s investment case.
