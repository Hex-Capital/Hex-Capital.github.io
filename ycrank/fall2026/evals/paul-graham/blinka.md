# Blinka -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The account closure is the most interesting fact about Blinka. Juan Pablo had a US company operated from abroad, lost access to its banking platform, and received the balance by check. That is a rather good way to discover a problem. His legal background and the brothers’ earlier lending-software work put them close to the unpleasant parts of solving it. But the dossier leaves a gap between encountering the obstacle and overcoming it: what did they build themselves, what initially failed, and how quickly did they fix it? I cannot infer resourcefulness from having experienced inconvenience.

The customers make this worth investigating. Blinka reported over $10 million in payments during its first two months, 25 clients before launch, and customers including Bricksave, Dapta, and Creditop ([October 9 report](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/asi-es-la-empresa-que-dos-colombianos-crearon-en-ee-uu-y-que-ya-ha-procesado-mas-de-us10-millones-en-pagos/)). These are businesses entrusting money to a young company. That is substantially stronger than signing up for a waiting list. The additional 150 account openings or applications are less informative. I would give the actual payments considerable demand credit, while keeping their company-reported provenance and unknown concentration in view.

There is also a functioning product underneath the positioning. A customer reportedly manages over 200 entities through one portal. That is a concrete integration job, and more convincing technical evidence than describing Sebastián as a self-taught engineer. It does not establish exceptional proprietary technology. Blinka connects accounts, payments, and financial workflows supplied partly by other providers; the dossier supplies no independent reliability measurements. The brothers have shipped something useful, but we cannot yet tell how much difficult machinery they have mastered.

The earlier moves through mortgage automation, lending brokers, and real-estate banking could describe founders finding their way toward an urgent problem. They could also describe repeated changes without accumulated learning. The [Pygma account](https://pygma.substack.com/p/we-bet-on-a-lawyer-and-a-drop-out) establishes a sequence, but the dossier does not identify the customer observation that killed each hypothesis or the time needed to ship its replacement. I give no extra adaptability credit for the number of pivots. Their prior collaboration as brothers does, however, avoid the concern about a cofounder relationship assembled for an application.

The strongest argument for investing is that Blinka has already found the narrow well: overseas operators whose US entities are awkward customers for ordinary banking platforms. The payment volume resembles the concrete proof spikes I favor. My exceptions also allow a working product and unmistakable user pull to outweigh incomplete founder evidence. Here, the product is real, but aggregate volume does not yet establish that exceptional pull. Nor does the dossier establish a difficult proprietary substrate. Against Mercury, Airwallex, and Slash, the important question is whether Blinka can keep serving customers whom larger providers find troublesome. Its inconsistent public eligibility descriptions make that question particularly concrete. Banking partners are necessary suppliers; the dossier does not show that the founders are waiting for a distribution partner to produce customers.

I would keep talking to these founders, but I would not write a check from this dossier. Payment volume is not revenue, and the unpublished fee schedule leaves the economics unresolved; neither warrants treating an early company as a failure. The binding gap is evidence of founder action under resistance. One verifiable condition would flip my decision: a dated, customer-confirmed account of a blocked onboarding or payment workflow that the brothers personally repaired and deployed within weeks, restoring recurring customer use. That would connect Blinka’s promising demand to the founder behavior on which I actually bet.
