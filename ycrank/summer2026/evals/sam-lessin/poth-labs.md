# Poth Labs -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers are missing: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and the price of this check. That earns no presumption of scarcity. More importantly, Poth Labs has not named what the round will prove. “Build the graph, add integrations, and sell the product” would finance a SaaS roadmap; it is not a finite experiment with a dated, value-changing result. The product may be useful, but the capital case is currently absent.

The strongest evidence is actual usage. Inkle, Sol, Composio, UnCircuit, and Illume appear under “Trusted by teams at,” and Poth says several Summer 2026 YC companies used the product before Ask Poth launched. That is better than followers or a waitlist. It shows that fragmented customer information is painful enough for teams to try a solution. It does not yet show that Poth owns a dark pool: there is no paid conversion, renewal, repeat query volume, or evidence that its entity mappings produce decisions customers cannot obtain elsewhere.

The bull case is the team. Matthew Wong worked on U.S. commercial data systems as a Palantir forward-deployed engineer and deployment strategist; Mojmír Horváth was a data scientist and coauthored work on a 4.5-billion-parameter multimodal model. They understand the technical translation layer between messy enterprise records and usable answers. The adaptive follow-up mechanism is also directionally smart: when existing evidence cannot explain churn or adoption, Poth can collect the missing evidence rather than hallucinating certainty from a static repository.

But the material other side of this bridge is customer research, and the dossier does not show that either founder has shipped a research system, owned a customer-success operation, or validated the methodological accuracy of automated interviews and statistical tests. Entity-resolution errors could contaminate every downstream conclusion. Enterpret already offers cross-source entity resolution, cited answers, more than 50 integrations, and a customer context graph after raising $25.1 million. Dovetail, Sprig, and Marvin attack adjacent workflows with substantially more capital. Falling inference cost helps every one of them; it is not proprietary access.

The economics are equally unresolved. There is no revenue, pricing, gross margin, burn, runway, or controllable cash metric. The inferred demo-led subscription model may eventually support a one-and-done business, but resemblance to SaaS is not proof of a profitable path. The soft pivot from adaptive surveys to a broad customer graph also suggests that the company is still locating its wedge. My 36-month expectation is that Poth narrows into customer-success churn intelligence and remains below $3 million ARR rather than winning as a horizontal customer-knowledge platform.

No known-exception override applies. The dossier contains neither extraordinary conversion nor proprietary adoption evidence strong enough to excuse unknown pricing and an undefined capital experiment. The red flag is not simply “AI wrapper”; it is a crowded AI workflow whose advantage currently depends on integrations, model-mediated analysis, and an unproven accuracy layer. The verifiable flip condition is $500,000 ARR from customers that have renewed after replacing an incumbent product. Until then, I am passing—not because the founders cannot build this, but because I cannot identify what scarce capital buys that ordinary product iteration does not.
