# Faraday -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Faraday has a plausible industry-enabled path in automating order reconciliation across disconnected industrial systems. Employees reconcile orders and exceptions manually, and bespoke automation has historically required on-site or forward-deployed engineering. The underwriting assumption is that some manufacturers have too little volume to justify that engineering but enough recurring exceptions to benefit from daily automation. Faraday’s shared runtime, customer-specific simulations, and job checks could lower the engineering and review time per correctly resolved exception. Under model parity, the customer would still need deployed integrations, workflow rules, and permission controls. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

The memo documents a public benchmark, and Faraday reports that its harness raised accuracy from 38% to 94% with the same underlying model. That supports the technical mechanism in a benchmark environment. The founder also reports an unnamed customer deployment that progressed after a remote-desktop blocker was resolved. That is an early demand signal, with payment and repeat use unverified. The founders’ prior work deploying software at manufacturers supports execution. Customer-specific rules and correction histories could create switching costs, but the memo gives no evidence of retention or pricing power. Scale economics take the absent-evidence default because there is no cost bridge for the next deployment. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Facts*)

The building-blocks path scores zero. Faraday applies AI to industrial workflows, and the memo identifies no scarce input it supplies to a specific AI workload or buildout. Watch reflects the unresolved question of whether Faraday can make cross-system automation affordable for a buyer that previously ruled it out. (*The Idea*)

### Countercase

Faraday could improve an existing workflow while leaving the economics of bespoke automation largely intact. The reported remote-desktop blocker illustrates the integration work each deployment may require, and write-enabled workflows need separate authorization and agreed controls. Infor already covers overlapping order, item, and invoice tasks within its suite. If setup and human review costs keep previously excluded buyers from adopting, or ERP-native agents can deliver the same cross-system outcome, the industry-enabled path falls to zero and the decision becomes Pass. The memo establishes overlap within Infor’s suite and leaves cross-system equivalence open, so that gate has yet to trigger. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a manufacturer that previously ruled out automating cross-system order exceptions sustain a paid Faraday deployment below its affordability threshold? A live pilot would move the action to Reach out if the buyer continues paying and the measured cost per correctly resolved exception, including integration, human review, and rework, clears that threshold at the required accuracy and cadence. If the pilot shows that bespoke effort still prices that buyer out, move to Pass.
