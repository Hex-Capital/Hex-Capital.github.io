# Derya -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

**$80,000 of contracted first-month revenue makes Derya a capital-hungry freight operator whose strongest fit is financing scale and whose weakest fit is technical risk.** Derya can build a useful business by replacing days of quoting, documentation, and carrier coordination with minutes of automation. But the economic mechanism that breaks is not yet freight forwarding; it is merely the staffing model of a freight office. Derya has evidence of demand. Derya does not yet have evidence of a miracle.

**Commonwealth Fusion** required $115 million before consensus investors could even see the commercial milestone; capital itself protected the technical thesis. Derya shares one load-bearing mechanism with that high rung: acting as principal, financing bookings above $20,000, and managing more than $3 million of transactions creates real balance-sheet demand that a lightweight SaaS vendor avoids. Derya diverges where it matters. No disclosed facility, underwriting system, or deployment plan shows that Derya needs more than $50 million to unlock a proprietary breakthrough. Capital may fund receivables rather than invention. That is financing a freight book, not financing fusion.

**The GPT-4 sales-call summarizer fails because commodity intelligence produces commodity differentiation.** That low-rung mechanism appears uncomfortably clearly in Derya: Flexport, Forto, Nuvocargo, and Zencargo already combine freight execution with software, while traditional forwarders can purchase third-party AI tools. Derya reports no proprietary model, measured automation rate, exclusive carrier relationship, or technical result competitors cannot reproduce. Derya assumes operating complexity will become a data moat. Complexity alone is usually overhead wearing a moat costume.

**The $24.5 billion projected digital-freight market gives Derya a respectable consequence, but consequence compounds only if automation changes industry economics over the next 18 months.** If Derya converts its $3.2 million opportunity pipeline into repeat lanes, every shipment could improve pricing, carrier selection, and exception handling, allowing two founders to operate freight volume that once required dozens of employees. If those feedback loops remain unmeasured, Derya merely offers same-day quotes inside the incumbent regime. DoorDash changed consumer-commerce flow; Derya currently accelerates an office. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself” does not mean make an existing office 30% faster.

**Mert Turna’s ship-operations software on more than 30 vessels would flip the founder-defiance score if Derya showed that industry experts believe autonomous forwarding cannot safely handle customs, qualification, and financing—and then published results proving them wrong.** Derya instead presents strong domain continuity: Turna previously built truck and ship software, while Oguzhan Karaca worked in legal at CEVA/CMA CGM. Derya may benefit from expertise, but experts often extrapolate the past; McKinsey forecast fewer than one million U.S. cellphones by 2000 and missed by 108 million. No controversial thesis or rejected orthodoxy is documented here.

**Slash earned an exception because a documented pivot exposed extraordinary learning rate.** Karaca built Ivy Consultants to six-figure ARR, and Turna co-founded Hemut before Derya, so Derya’s founders have shipped, sold, and learned outside a classroom. Their eight-year relationship also reduces ordinary co-founder fragility. But Derya supplies no specific “we were wrong” moment, product pivot, or thesis revision. Prior execution earns Derya credit; it does not establish the learning velocity that justified overriding consequence weakness at Slash.

**$80,000 of contracted revenue in month one leads to a falsifiable 36-month forecast: Derya will reach $8 million to $15 million in annual net revenue by July 2029 and describe itself primarily as a technology-enabled freight forwarder, not a proprietary AI platform.** Derya’s founder-market fit and 20-container commitment can compound into a real book of business. Derya’s nonexclusive partner network, working-capital exposure, and reproducible AI workflow will keep gross margins and defensibility closer to forwarding than software.

**More than $3 million of managed transactions is the strongest objection to that forecast because Derya may be the DoorDash exception: an operational network can become the moat before outsiders recognize it.** Derya has unusually informative pre-seed validation, complementary founders, and direct control of the customer experience rather than dependence on software-seat adoption. The objection still loses today because opportunity volume is not proprietary learning, the separate contracted-revenue figure is only $80,000, and four named digital forwarders already pursue the same regime. One verifiable condition flips my verdict: Derya demonstrates that at least 70% of shipment operations run autonomously while repeat customers produce $5 million or more of annualized net revenue at software-like contribution margins.
