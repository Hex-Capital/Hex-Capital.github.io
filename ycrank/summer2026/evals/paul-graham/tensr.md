# tensr -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The dossier contains no company evidence. It does not identify tensr’s founders, product, users, market, business model, technical artifact, or even the problem being attacked. The sole sentence describes an intended research process, not something the founders did. I cannot convert that into evidence of resourcefulness.

The controlling question is what broke and what the founders shipped in response. No blocker, workaround, elapsed time, or observable result is documented. There is likewise no working system, benchmark, deployment, or other measured proof. These are absences, not adverse facts, so both dimensions receive their specified defaults rather than zero.

No named user niche is shown pulling a crude product back. There is no usage, payment, retention, signed LOI, or even reported interest. Nor is there a before-and-after product decision from which to tell whether the founders behave like running backs or merely defend the first route. Demand and adaptability therefore remain untested.

The origin is equally opaque. Nothing connects a founder’s prior work or personal experience to the problem, and no regulatory, operational, integration, or service burden establishes a schlep-heavy wedge. Market size and competition cannot repair this omission because neither is supplied either.

The strongest charitable case is that a pre-seed company may possess a working product and unusually resourceful founders without leaving a public trail. But none of the known exceptions applies on faith: each requires the missing substitute—an impressive artifact, unmistakable user pull, or independently measured proof. I would pass because the operational gate is not met. One verifiable fact would reopen the decision: evidence that the founders encountered a named blocker, shipped their own workaround within weeks, and produced repeated use by a specific user niche.
