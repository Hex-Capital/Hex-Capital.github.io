# Runtime.si -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic sale to a robot OEM that could use Runtime’s planning software to reduce the engineering time needed to deploy new manipulation tasks. The memo documents a launched policy and a reusable skill library with physical verification records. Runtime reports matching or exceeding unnamed foundation models on rope threading and egg grasping, with trial counts and success rates undisclosed. A buyer could have a reason to own the stack if it repeatedly cuts task setup time while meeting production reliability and cycle-time requirements. The announced January early access offers a way to recruit a pilot partner, while pilot funding, integration cost, and runway remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The benchmark is a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The memo leaves actual financing terms and acquisition pricing unresolved. The following are illustrative sensitivities. Both sale cases assume $5 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, an eligible holder selling the entire stake for cash within eight years, and a pro rata waterfall after senior claims. (Market & Traction)

### Countercase

An OEM could build with open GPU simulation tools or adopt a competing policy. Simify’s research demonstrates a related approach to physical object rearrangement, and Runtime is still working to accelerate live planning. If integration work or latency offsets the savings from avoiding task-specific demonstrations, an OEM has little reason to pay an acquisition premium. The memo leaves buyer economics, transfer rights, and any independent liquidity route unverified. That supports Watch. The evidence has yet to establish a structural block or an exit price that triggers the proceeds gate. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
