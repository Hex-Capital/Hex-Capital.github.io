# Maritime -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Maritime’s strongest path. An agent infrastructure provider such as Daytona could buy Maritime’s scheduler and managed hosting product to add persistent, per-customer agent fleets with message-triggered wakeups. Maritime describes a scheduler that maps saved VM memory and attaches a thin disk clone on wake. Keiki documents running its background agent harness on Maritime, and Maritime reports a snapshot of 1,107 agents with 29 awake concurrently. A buyer could gain paying workloads and shorten its rollout by owning the product, but the memo does not quantify either benefit. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The pre-seed milestone is to retain multiple paying production fleets through a renewal cycle and demonstrate lower cost per agent at comparable wake latency and reliability against an incumbent option. Keiki’s deployment provides a starting point. Maritime’s pre-seed financing amount, burn, and runway are undisclosed, so the capital needed to reach the milestone is uncertain. Closing would also depend on whether the scheduler rights, customer agreements, and infrastructure arrangements can transfer. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

Under the shared scenario, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The 5× target requires $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, or $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3%. The equity hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. With $5 million of illustrative net debt, the 3% hurdle is $88.3 million in enterprise value. A $25 million sale at year eight would yield $600,000 gross (1.2×), and a $100 million sale would yield $2.85 million (5.7×), assuming full cash payment and pro rata distribution before investor taxes. Maritime’s actual debt, fees, and senior claims are unknown, and the memo gives no usable basis for a realizable sale price. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The memo gives no evidence of cash generation to finance a sponsor buyout, demand for an eligible secondary, operating scale for an IPO and resale, or excess cash for a payout. If growth plateaus, buyers stay absent, public scale remains out of reach, and Maritime reinvests all available cash, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. Any wind-down proceeds would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo does not quantify. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

A buyer could build or partner around capabilities already in the market. Fly.io offers idle sleep and persistent checkpoints, Google Cloud offers isolated sandboxes with persistent workspaces, and Daytona already supports reusable snapshots. The memo identifies no structural barrier and provides no measured switching costs. If Maritime’s advantage narrows under sustained awake workloads, a buyer could adopt an existing option or license the scheduler at far lower cost than buying the company. The overlap weakens the reason to pay, but the memo does not establish equivalent economics for Maritime’s full workflow or a price ceiling that would trigger the Pass gate. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could an agent infrastructure buyer justify for owning Maritime after it demonstrates retained paid fleets and a measured cost advantage at comparable wake latency and reliability? A buyer-side estimate of incremental revenue and avoided costs, measured against building, licensing, or partnering, would support Reach out if it implies a credible price range above the $88.3 million illustrative enterprise-value hurdle on plausible holder terms.
