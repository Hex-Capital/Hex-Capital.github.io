# Libra Robotics -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A large installation contractor or construction-equipment supplier could buy Libra to gain control of its reusable robot skills and physical testbeds. Ownership could be worth paying for if it lets the buyer qualify new tasks and adapt to site changes faster than building internally or contracting for completed work. The memo records a live demonstration at FTC Solar’s Robo Day. Libra reports a 10× reduction in task-to-deployment time, with cross-site performance still awaiting an independent benchmark. The ownership case and the price a buyer could justify remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Repeat paid solar installations across different site conditions are the most concrete milestone. Libra reports signed solar agreements and pilots booked through March 2027, giving it a path to measure redeployment time, reliability, and fully loaded installation cost. Contract values, test funding, and runway are undisclosed. The memo also leaves IP ownership and the transferability of jobsite data and customer contracts unverified. It supplies no usable valuation evidence or independently supported route to cash for this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility, Key Facts)

With investment terms undisclosed, use the illustrative seed check of $500,000 for 5%, diluted to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in holder cash, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. For both sale scenarios below, assume full cash payment at close, zero net debt and other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, and a simple pro rata waterfall with no preference effects. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each $0. The prices are scenario assumptions.

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value → distributable equity | Gross holder cash at 3% |
|---|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $50M → $50M | $1.5M (3×) |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100M → $100M | $3.0M (6×) |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No sale or payout | $0 (0×), shares retained |

The strong sale clears the hurdle at 3% and falls to $2 million (4×) at 2%. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public-market scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested.

### Countercase

Contractors can already buy completed installation work from Libra. Sunstall has worked with both Libra and Cosmic, and Gritt offers a retrofit approach using commercially available equipment. If those options deliver comparable results, a buyer has little reason to pay an ownership premium. The memo establishes that alternatives exist, while their relative cost and performance remain unknown. That gap warrants Watch. The evidence does not establish a binding price cap or trigger a Pass override. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a strategic buyer’s build-versus-buy analysis, grounded in repeat paid solar installations across materially different sites, justify an acquisition price that delivers $2.5 million in holder cash within eight years? Move to Reach out if buyer-validated deployment savings and the cost of obtaining comparable results by building or contracting support that price at a credible diluted ownership level. Until then, Watch.
