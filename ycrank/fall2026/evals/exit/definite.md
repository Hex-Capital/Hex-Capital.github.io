# Definite -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest private-company path. A finance software vendor could buy Definite to add auditable reconciliation across multiple ERP systems. The memo records Definite’s shift to a shared finance data layer by September 2026. Definite describes a read-only product that applies deterministic rules and preserves source-level traceability and approvals. Its claim of live bank deployments concerns the earlier regulatory-reporting product. The memo provides no paid deployments or measured customer results for the current platform. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The ownership case depends on whether Definite’s connectors and mapping rules can be reused across customers. If they can, owning the product could let a finance vendor embed and extend the capability faster than building it or relying on a license. A seed-stage milestone would be repeat paid deployments across different ERP systems that show measurable reductions in reconciliation time and reuse of mappings. Definite scopes deployments around one workflow, and the founders have relevant experience in approval controls and financial tooling. Runway and the cost of reaching that milestone remain unknown. The memo does not establish whether the code and customer contract rights could transfer in a sale. It supplies no evidence of an independent route to cash for this holder. (The Idea, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** With deal terms unavailable, the illustrative seed case assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, or 5×. Under a simple pro rata waterfall, the holder needs $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale scenarios are assumptions for testing the hurdle. Both assume zero net debt and zero other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity. All consideration is cash at closing, with $0 each in stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and transaction compensation. The waterfall assumes no senior preference deductions and pays the holder 3% before investor taxes.

| Scenario | Timing and assumed outcome | Gross cash to holder | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 at $40 million enterprise value | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 at $100 million enterprise value | $3 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. The holder retains the shares. | $0 | 0× |

The strong sale clears the objective at 3% ownership and falls to 4× at 2%. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction pricing to support either sale value. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Microsoft already offers native finance agents and ERP data access for overlapping Dynamics workflows. A prospective buyer could build enough of the capability itself or license Definite. Customer-specific mappings and missing connectors could also make implementations too bespoke to give ownership much value. The memo leaves open whether those substitutes can match Definite’s cross-ERP traceability or cap the price below the proceeds hurdle. The structural Pass override therefore does not apply, and the unproven ownership economics keep the action at Watch. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Definite demonstrate a repeatable production result that a finance software buyer values enough to own? Paid deployments across different ERP systems would need to show measured reconciliation savings and reusable mappings, and a prospective buyer would need to quantify an ownership advantage over building or licensing that could support a sale above the modeled proceeds hurdle. That evidence would move the action to Reach out.
