# Sutrix -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Sutrix’s founders learned the problem by doing the work. Ritvik led a 300-participant concussion study and worked in clinical research at HSS and Emory; the brothers built software inside research environments before offering it more broadly. That is specific knowledge exposed through action. Supratik’s prior security-company exit adds execution experience, but the strongest evidence is their direct encounter with clinical data preparation. They understand what happens between receiving records and trusting a dataset.

The commercial evidence deserves respect. Sutrix reports 10+ paying institutions, and the founder reports $150K+ in revenue from work delivered before opening software access. These are actual buyers paying to remove an expensive bottleneck. Individually scoped engagements establish direct value capture, although the undisclosed service/software split prevents me from treating those receipts as recurring software economics. The claimed 80% preparation-time reduction explains the purchase; it does not establish how much labor Sutrix must supply to deliver it.

My central question is what the fiftieth study leaves behind that makes the fifty-first structurally easier. Approved mappings, reusable definitions, and source-linked reviewer decisions are useful product assets. But the dossier does not establish that these assets are exclusive, difficult to transfer, or improving performance across customers. If a competitor copied the code, the customer would still possess the underlying records and study requirements. Layer Health and Carta already demonstrate overlapping abstraction capabilities. Sutrix has shown useful execution; a durable advantage remains unproven.

The strongest case against withholding a check is that delivering research work may be precisely how these founders discover the durable product. Their reported 15+ labs and 50+ studies provide repeated exposure to exceptions that a demo would miss. I can accept an absent network effect when founders have built something unusually good through sustained contact with users. But Sutrix does not yet show the compensating advantage: no measured cross-study improvement, proprietary model, or customer-created distribution loop. PHI approval requirements also mean this cannot simply become an open contribution system. Another institution adds a customer; the evidence does not yet show that it improves the product for existing customers.

There is room beyond the initial wedge. Source reconciliation and auditable extraction could serve registry and observational-research teams, workflows already addressed by competitors. That is a concrete expansion path, although adjacent purchasing and spend remain unestablished for Sutrix. I see no documented integrity or financing-pressure veto. I see capable founders building a potentially valuable business whose compounding mechanism is still unresolved. I would hold my money today. **My flip condition is a documented cohort of repeat institutional studies showing that retained mappings and approved decisions materially reduce Sutrix’s staff hours per study while maintaining independently checked accuracy.** That would show the accumulated work becoming an asset rather than another deliverable.
