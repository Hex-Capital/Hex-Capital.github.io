# PRINCEPS -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: GPU-backed financing and an expected $822 billion of 2026 data-center spending create new outage and collateral risks, but PRINCEPS becomes large only if its models let carriers bind this coverage at sustainable loss ratios. That controlling variable is measurable. The dossier also shows that it is currently unresolved: historical loss tables do not exist, carrier capacity is external, and the company is still recruiting the actuary who would price the risk. This fails my primary market gate today.

The bull case is that insurance supply has lagged the compute buildout. More than $20 billion of GPU-backed debt creates real exposure, while standard policies may exclude SLA breaches, GPU outages, and residual-value losses. Trofimova has worked in data-center project finance, Hector has modeled uncertainty in energy systems, and the Ornn relationship could provide operator access. Those ingredients could make PRINCEPS an underwriting layer for a rapidly expanding asset class rather than another generic AI application.

But there is no demonstrated market vacuum. Forward Compute already markets outage, contractual-liability, and GPU residual-value coverage. American Compute holds more than 600,000 hardware-pricing datapoints, while Parametrix monitors over 7,000 cloud services and 750 data centers. PRINCEPS discloses neither a concrete product difference nor exclusive telemetry. Its workbench combines contracts, market prices, grid data, and failure modes, but combining available inputs is not yet evidence of superior pricing. A well-funded competitor appears able to reproduce the claimed data asset.

The strongest exception would resemble the mechanism that justified Anduril: a physical market whose economics are reconstructed by technology. The material difference is that PRINCEPS has not shown reconstruction. It remains dependent on third-party carriers, has no published commission economics, and has not quantified how its system lowers underwriting costs, improves loss ratios, or unlocks coverage incumbents cannot price. The $4.85 billion market estimate and 31.35% projected CAGR describe potential demand, not value capture.

Product pull is similarly absent rather than disproven. Ornn is a working relationship, but the dossier identifies no bound policy, named insured, paid pilot, repeat usage, or revenue. YC, NVIDIA Inception, a competition finalist slot, and four open roles are institutional and hiring signals, not customer evidence. Hiring two GTM roles before demonstrating carrier-approved pricing also risks putting distribution ahead of proof.

The team is credible but does not provide an override. Trofimova and Hector cover infrastructure finance and energy uncertainty, yet none of the three founders has disclosed actuarial or insurance-underwriting experience. Moe’s prior events business shows some operating exposure, but not second-time learning from an insurance or infrastructure company, and the dossier documents no costly commitment or founder desperation. I pass until PRINCEPS proves one verifiable flip condition: a named carrier binds at least $25 million of coverage across three unrelated compute operators using PRINCEPS-generated pricing, with disclosed economics or an initial renewal result.
