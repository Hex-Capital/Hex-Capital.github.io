# Derya -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

$80,000 of contracted first-month revenue makes Derya a Wizard-of-Oz freight operator wearing an AI label, not another agent shell. Derya’s analytical signature is unusually strong sleeve fit paired with almost no owned distribution: Karaca and Turna are already moving freight through manual, regulated workflows, but 464 company followers and a free-quote form are not a community. That can produce a sound forwarding business; it does not yet produce proprietary demand.

**Fin** is the high rung on category fit because the manual operation was the product laboratory: humans performed difficult work before software absorbed repeatable steps. Derya shares that mechanism by directly quoting, booking, tracking, coordinating customs, and financing shipments rather than selling workflow software to forwarders. Derya diverges from Fin because the dossier reports no automation rate, model accuracy, or expanding margin per shipment; Derya may be automating the office, or merely subsidizing an office with venture dollars.

**Fin’s** personal-assistant incarnation also demonstrates the low-rung distribution failure: a labor-intensive service without an owned audience must repeatedly purchase or hustle for demand, so operational learning never becomes cheap customer acquisition. Derya shows the same vulnerability through founder requests for importer introductions, 464 company followers, and no newsletter, channel, or pre-existing logistics community. Karaca’s 1,422 LinkedIn followers and Turna’s roughly 2,000 give Derya useful networks, but networks are not distribution until customers arrive repeatedly without founder-by-founder selling.

$1.1 million from YC, 212, Umur Cubukcu, and angels places Derya between the canonical hot vertical-agent round and Sublime Security’s pre-consensus entry, closer to the middle than the hot-deal failure case. Derya discloses neither valuation nor visible tier-one bidding, so I cannot call the round mispriced in Derya’s favor; price silence earns only limited credit. Over the next 18 months, $80,000 of contracted revenue can make the asset legible and support a disciplined tack, but the $3.2 million opportunity figure can just as easily manufacture consensus momentum before contribution margins exist.

Oguzhan Karaca built Ivy Consultants to six-figure ARR after working inside CEVA/CMA CGM, while Mert Turna built ship software used on more than 30 vessels representing $200 million of fleet value. Derya therefore has more pre-pitch conviction than public-writing evidence alone suggests: Derya’s founders lived in logistics operations, built adjacent businesses, and maintained an eight-year working relationship. The score would flip into the top rung if Karaca or Turna documented several years of a non-obvious freight thesis—or showed that Derya manually completed the same lane repeatedly before automating it—because that would distinguish missionary conviction from a well-timed AI application.

**Ro** is the relevant capital-structure parallel: operating inside a regulated transaction can make capital intensity the moat rather than the mistake. Derya similarly takes responsibility for customs, carrier coordination, cargo data, and financing bookings above $20,000, so Derya could turn working capital and compliance into an illegible asset other seed investors cannot underwrite. Ro had regulatory rails substantial enough to justify repeated financing tacks; Derya discloses no credit facility, underwriting policy, default history, risk transfer, or exclusive licenses. Software is worthless here—quick walk-back: software without disciplined freight and credit economics is worthless here.

$3.2 million of sourced opportunities will not become the forecast merely because it photographs well in a deck. By July 2029, I expect Derya to be a specialized, operations-heavy forwarder producing $8 million to $15 million of annualized gross revenue, concentrated in a handful of trade lanes, while raising a working-capital facility rather than a software-style Series A. Derya reaches that outcome because its strongest asset is full-stack execution and its weakest is owned distribution: Derya can compound lane expertise and carrier data, but growth remains attached to relationship sales and balance-sheet capacity.

TVO’s signed 20-container commitment is the bull case that stings, and **Ro** is the Known Exception that prevents an easy pass: Derya may be discovering that liability, financing, and partner licenses are precisely the barriers that let a two-person operator consolidate a fragmented market. Derya’s $80,000 contracted in month one and Turna’s 30-vessel deployment history support that reading. The dossier still gives Flexport, Forto, Nuvocargo, and Zencargo the same full-stack strategic map while providing no exclusivity or measured automation advantage for Derya. I would flip to Invest on one verifiable condition: six consecutive months above $1 million in monthly booked freight with positive contribution margin after financing cost, claims, and bad debt.
