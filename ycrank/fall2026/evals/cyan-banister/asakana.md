# Asakana -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, a chef should be able to text “the usual, smaller fish this time” and have the right order arrive without somebody translating that sentence into an ERP. Asakana is handling an early piece of that world: customer shorthand, order history, catalog matching, and escalation when the machine is uncertain. The interesting question is what eliminating that translation enables next. The dossier establishes today’s workflow repair; autonomous purchasing, inventory coordination, and supplier substitution remain possibilities I am imagining. I cannot give Asakana credit for having demonstrated that longer chain.

Why does a distributor need somebody to type an order twice? One customer receives approximately **60 orders daily**, some exceeding **75 lines**, and spends roughly **30% of representative time** processing them ([YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/VrR-asakana-automating-sales-and-the-entire-back-office-for-food-distributors)). The restaurant’s informal language and the distributor’s rigid software do not meet. Employees absorb the difference. Asakana preserves how customers already communicate and returns some control over the working day to the order desk. That is a specific, measurable operating wound, and the strongest part of this investment case. Its transaction-volume commission still needs to leave the distributor meaningfully better off; the negotiated rates are undisclosed.

Yudai has actually worked in seafood distribution and fished for yellowtail. Showing Ben the manual order desk gave a former Google engineer with Gemini and Project Mariner experience a concrete problem to attack ([Asakana About](https://www.asakana.co/about)). That combination gets my attention more than their school names. It fits my pattern of backing unglamorous repairs discovered through lived experience. But domain familiarity and complementary recruitment establish only part of the founder case: the dossier does not document years of independent experiments or repeated attempts to solve this particular problem before Asakana.

The strongest argument for writing a check is that the founders have already crossed into live operations. US seafood distributors are using the product, broadline customers are reported, and the company claims **40%+ of order-desk time returned to selling** ([YC profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/asakana)). Letting software enter commercial orders carries consequences; this is a functioning artifact. However, the savings lack dated cohorts, customer attribution, and repeated behavioral detail, so I keep that evidence within the founder-reported usage ceiling. My willingness to back pre-revenue prototypes applies here in principle. The exception becomes compelling when unusually deep founder obsession and a mapped future compensate for thin adoption evidence; this dossier establishes neither strongly enough.

Asakana could accumulate the quiet infrastructure that food commerce runs through. Customer context and maintained ERP connections are useful ingredients. But **Choco already markets message-to-ERP automation and more than 200 ERP connections**, while Pepper bundles overlapping capture with commerce and payments. Those competitors make it harder to infer that Asakana controls a scarce dependency. Its human escalation mechanism shows respect for operating complexity, which I like. Its privacy policy assigns consent responsibility to suppliers and describes encrypted credentials; that does not establish a severe privacy violation, but it also does not settle how buyer communications are authorized and retained. No equal-control arrangement or leadership deception is documented, so I infer neither.

I am **Neutral** with my own money. Asakana has found work worth removing and assembled a credible team to remove it. My conviction stops at the gap between a useful distributor service and an enabling layer that downstream businesses must use. One verifiable flip condition would move me: **three named distributors renewing after six months at disclosed commission rates, with audited net labor savings and order accuracy**. That would show the product survives its full economic cost and operational consequences—the point where an attractive demonstration becomes something customers organize their businesses around.
