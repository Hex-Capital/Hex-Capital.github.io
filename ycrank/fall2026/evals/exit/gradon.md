# Gradon -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Gradon’s strongest path to investor cash in Fall 2026. An AEC software platform could buy Gradon for its Windows automation and ability to configure engineering workflows across existing tools. Ownership could give a buyer control over that workflow layer and let it distribute the capability across its installed base. That advantage depends on Gradon proving that its software and deployment methods are reusable across firms. The memo documents Autodesk’s overlapping assistant in technical preview. Gradon reports that one project took 180 hours against a 750-hour manual estimate. That comparison suggests potential customer value, while the economics of owning the capability remain untested. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is repeatable time savings across several paying firms after licensed review, with progressively less on-site setup. The CEO reports mid-six-figure contracted ARR in four weeks, an early signal of demand. The memo records a $125,000 seed entry and gives no verified runway or cost to reach the milestone. IP ownership and customer contract assignment rights remain unverified. Sponsor buyouts, secondaries, public resale, and company-funded payouts lack separate evidence of a path to cash for this holder. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The illustrative seed case assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. Both sale scenarios assume enterprise value equals equity consideration, zero net debt or fees, a simple pro rata waterfall with no preference leakage, and all cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and transaction-related compensation are each assumed to be zero.

- **Modest sale, year 5.** At an assumed $40 million enterprise value, the holder receives $1.2 million, or 2.4×.
- **Strong sale, year 7.** At an assumed $100 million enterprise value, the holder receives $3 million, or 6×. At 2% ownership, the same sale pays $2 million, or 4×.
- **Prolonged private, through year 8.** Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. The investor still holds the shares and realizes $0, or 0×.

The sale prices illustrate the hurdle. The memo supplies no usable evidence of what a buyer could justify paying. (Defensibility, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

Autodesk already has a route to bundle overlapping automation in Revit and Civil 3D. If most of Gradon’s value can be replicated inside those applications or obtained through a license, a platform buyer has little reason to pay an acquisition premium. Gradon’s on-site configuration and required licensed review could also reduce the economics of scaling deployments. The preview makes the substitute credible, while comparable production results and a binding price ceiling remain unproven. The structural Pass override is therefore unestablished. (Defensibility, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could an AEC platform justify for owning Gradon’s transferable workflows compared with building or licensing equivalent automation? Referenceable deployments showing consistent net time savings after review and declining setup effort would give a buyer a basis to quantify that advantage. Move to Reach out if those results support a buyer-side price range that clears the holder’s cash hurdle at plausible dilution. Keep watching while the ownership premium remains unsubstantiated.
