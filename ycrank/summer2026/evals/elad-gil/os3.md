# OS3 -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: vision-language-action models may lower the cost of robotic manipulation, hotels with persistent staffing gaps may buy automated labor, and OS3 becomes large only if its fully loaded cost per reliably completed task beats human labor. The dossier never measures that controlling variable. It provides no task-success rate, intervention rate, uptime, customer price, or payback period. A 65% hotel-shortage survey establishes pain, but not whether a two-arm robot can serve that pain economically.

The March 2025 release of new VLA models is a real technical discontinuity. It may reduce the robot data required to learn new tasks, while OS3’s wheeled form factor can avoid some cost and complexity associated with bipeds. But lower model-development barriers help Pudu, Apptronik, and other well-capitalized competitors too. With Pudu already offering a mobile two-arm D7, OS3 has not shown that the shift expands its margins or adoption rather than commoditizing the control layer. The projected $40.5 billion humanoid market is not a substitute for unit economics.

The strongest part of OS3 is the integrated product architecture. The company designs the hardware, trains control models from internet and first-person video, collects deployment data, and feeds failures back into both models and body design. Better models should make this system more capable rather than simply absorb a thin software feature. The claimed data flywheel is nevertheless unproven: no dataset size, learning curve, task benchmark, or deployment count is disclosed. A well-funded competitor’s cost and time to reproduce it remain unknown.

The bull case is meaningful. OS3 reports robots operating in hospitality, Chanana has robotics and ML training experience, and Hailey previously helped build a hackathon product that reportedly reached $10,000 MRR. Two founders building an in-house mobile manipulator at pre-seed is materially harder than assembling an AI demo. If the deployments involve repeated autonomous work in live kitchens, the team may already possess more product pull and technical proof than the public record reveals.

That possibility does not qualify for the physical-business exception yet. OS3 has published payload, sensing, and safety specifications but no evidence that the technology changes incumbent labor economics; it retains hardware production, component sourcing across Korea, Taiwan, and China, on-site deployment, and safety exposure. The unexplained transition from Observee and YC Summer 2025 to OS3 and Summer 2026 also obscures how much of the founders’ prior learning transfers to this market. My 36-month base case is that OS3 narrows from general hotel labor to one constrained manipulation workflow in warehouses or production lines and remains below $1 million ARR.

I would pass today. The single condition that flips the decision is a named hotel customer disclosing a 12-month OS3 deployment whose fully loaded cost per successfully completed task is at least 30% below the human-labor baseline. That result would validate the controlling variable, establish real pull, and show that the integrated hardware-model loop produces differentiated economics rather than an impressive prototype.
