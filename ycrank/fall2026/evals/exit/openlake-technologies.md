# OpenLake Technologies -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. An inference platform such as Baseten could pay to own OpenLake’s workflow for turning live data into model updates, testing those updates, and serving them on the existing deployment. The observed MLPerf Storage submission supports the team’s work on cache and checkpoint infrastructure. OpenLake claims its models halve serving costs on average, but the memo gives no buyer-specific savings or evidence that an acquirer would pay to own the workflow. Its reported 100 billion tokens per day covers all OpenLake products, with no breakdown for continuous post-training. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)

The seed-stage milestone is a paid production deployment that runs repeated updates and measures total operating cost and model quality against a customer-managed workflow. An unnamed team’s testimonial about training and evaluating Security-One for production makes that test plausible. Runway, test funding, and the cost of reaching the milestone remain unknown. A sale would also depend on which parts of the workflow and customer integrations can transfer. The memo establishes that the storage engine is open source and checkpoints are exportable, while ownership of the remaining software and transfer rights for customer data and contracts are unverified. (Market & Traction; Defensibility; Key Risks)

Under the shared scenario, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted by 40% to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year target is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% case requires about $88.3 million in enterprise value. A $25 million sale in year eight yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). These are pro rata scenarios before investor taxes. Actual debt, fees, preferences, and a realizable sale-price range are unverified.

If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and OpenLake reinvests all available cash, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. The memo provides no evidence of cash generation for a sponsor buyout or company payout, demand for an eligible secondary, or operating and reporting readiness for an IPO. (Market & Traction; Key Facts)

### Countercase

Together AI and Fireworks already combine managed post-training and inference, and customers can move trained models using exportable checkpoints. The open-source storage engine also gives a potential buyer access to that component without purchasing the company. Those documented alternatives bear more directly on the build-versus-buy decision than OpenLake’s aggregate cost claim. The memo leaves open whether automating the full workflow creates enough incremental value to justify ownership. It establishes neither a structural block nor a credible price range that leaves the holder materially short, so the Pass gate does not apply. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a plausible acquirer’s build-versus-buy analysis, grounded in a paid production deployment, support a price range that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? Measured operating savings and model quality across repeated updates, applied to the buyer’s own deployment volumes and alternatives, could justify **Reach out** if the resulting range clears about $88.3 million in enterprise value under the modeled 3% ownership and $5 million net debt assumptions.
