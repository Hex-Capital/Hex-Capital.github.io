# Gradon -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Gradon’s strongest path is to start with civil engineering firms that repeatedly produce plan sets and drawings in Civil 3D, then sell more seats within each firm. Assuming strong product-market fit, the Windows agent can reuse its core drafting workflow across firms, while the Firm Brain adapts it to each firm’s project history and standards. The buyer and job plausibly recur abroad. How much adaptation local standards and review requirements would demand remains uncertain. The documented sales path runs through demos or referrals to firm principals, a paid pilot on a live project, and on-site configuration. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The subscription terms specify unlimited use per named seat. A firm that adds engineers can expand its bill, and accumulated standards and corrections give engineers a reason to keep using the product. More work per seat generates no additional revenue. In a scenario where additional engineers can use the same Firm Brain without another on-site rollout, their seat fees could spread the initial deployment cost. The contribution bridge starts with seat fees and subtracts attributable sales and referral costs, on-site setup, inference, infrastructure, and support. The memo gives no prices or workload costs to establish positive contribution under unlimited use. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The CEO reported reaching mid-six-figure contracted ARR from zero in four weeks after the October 13 AEC launch, alongside weekly firm onboarding and 50% week-over-week growth. The reported first customer used Gradon for plan sets, CAD, and reports. That is a promising time-to-contract signal for a small team. The memo lists two team members and records a third co-founder announcement in November, leaving the team size during the ramp uncertain. It also leaves earned revenue, paid production volume, and the metric behind the 50% growth figure unspecified. The commercial slope therefore remains unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team)

### Countercase

Each new firm may require substantial Gradon staff time to configure its standards, files, and live workflows. The memo estimates at least a week on-site per deployment, and licensed professionals must review the output. If that setup persists across firms and heavy usage raises inference costs without raising seat revenue, delivery capacity and contribution could constrain growth even with strong fit. The structural gate stays open because the shared Civil 3D workflow and firm-level setup offer a plausible route to reuse. The memo leaves both the minimum deployment effort and the resulting contribution unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Gradon take a cohort of civil engineering firms in more than one jurisdiction from pilot to paid Civil 3D production using the same remote setup? Paid invoices, deployment logs showing limited Gradon staff time, and positive contribution at representative usage would support Reach out if the resulting evidence raises the score to 65 or above. If those rollouts establish that bespoke rebuilding is unavoidable across markets, or that required deployment labor makes growth uneconomic with no credible lever, move to Pass.
