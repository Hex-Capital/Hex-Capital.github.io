# Quippy -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine someone rehearsing a difficult conversation on the walk to dinner, then putting the phone away and actually having it. Quippy points toward a future I like: software helping people enter human relationships. Voice rehearsal, feedback, and lessons that remember recurring difficulties are plausible ingredients. But the dossier does not establish the intervening steps from practicing convincing lines to developing judgment that transfers across real relationships. That future remains a hypothesis.

The August 12, 2026 review is the little artifact that makes me pause: someone reported finding a new friend group after moving to San Francisco and using Quippy. That is closer to the desired outcome than a beautifully delivered demo. The live iOS and Android products, 24 US iOS ratings averaging 4.8, and 100-plus Android installs establish that people have encountered something usable. They do not yet establish repeated practice or repeatable social improvement. I would want to inhabit this product, especially the moment when its advice meets an awkward human response.

Jonathan Li gives the bull case real weight. His YC biography credits him with product work at Duolingo on an English course reaching 280,000 daily users in four months, and with helping Luvu reach $1.5 million ARR in four months. Those are relevant experiences in teaching, consumer behavior, and commercial execution; neither figure belongs to Quippy. What I cannot find is the earlier obsession: experiments with social learning, an independently developed teaching method, or people recruited around that conviction. My exception for pre-revenue companies still requires affirmative evidence elsewhere. **CosmicBrain AI** combined a functioning artifact with lifelong founder obsession and signed industrial proofs of concept; Quippy shares the artifact, but the other evidence that supported that exception is missing.

Why does someone need Quippy in the first place? Rehearsing privately could give a person confidence without requiring another person’s availability. That fits my pattern of backing products that return individual agency. But the dossier identifies no particular institutional restriction, quantified coaching expense, or gatekeeper whose rent disappears. The $19.99 monthly subscription establishes an asking price, not savings or willingness to pay. Meanwhile, Yoodli already offers adaptive roleplays and personalized feedback. Quippy’s broader personal curriculum could matter enormously, but its teaching quality has to carry that difference.

The strongest argument against passing is that a learning product can become valuable through curriculum and habit before it owns any underlying technology. Li’s background and that friendship testimonial make this possibility credible. Still, personal practice memory is currently an application feature, with no demonstrated downstream dependency or scarce enabling asset. Sensitive practice content also reaches model providers and PostHog for quality review. The stated training restrictions and deletion controls matter; the dossier does not establish a severe privacy violation. I would nevertheless need to understand consent and custody before entrusting the product with intimate rehearsals. There is no evidence of exclusive dependence on one closed supplier, so I would not invent that red flag.

I pass with my own money today. Quippy has a working consumer artifact and a relevant operator, but little evidence yet for my central bet: a specific future becoming visible through a distinctive present behavior. My one verifiable flip condition is an independently assessed, eight-week cohort showing that repeat Quippy practice produces sustained improvement in real conversations relative to a comparison group. That would turn the appealing friendship story into evidence of a teaching mechanism worth backing.
