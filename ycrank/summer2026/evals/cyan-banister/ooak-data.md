# Ooak Data -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Seven-figure contracts with the two largest AI labs make Ooak Data a founder-led data refinery with unusually strong commercial proof and unusually weak physical proof. Ooak Data’s highest signal is Pierre-Louis Vouteau’s unambiguous CEO position; its lowest is that the claimed digital twins cannot be touched, inspected, or distinguished publicly from the 480 Mercor tasks in APEX Explorer. I see Biz and Tiz. The Rizz—and the artifact—remain behind an NDA.

**Anduril** is the high rung for governance because Palmer Luckey’s authority was inseparable from the mission. Ooak Data sits below that rung: Pierre-Louis began alone and remains CEO, while Grégoire Lamy is CPO and COO and Thomas Aubry is CTO. That is far healthier than three people sharing one steering wheel. Ooak Data has not disclosed equity, founder vesting, or an exit-conflict protocol, however, so the titles establish a likely tiebreaker rather than a documented one.

**DeepMind’s** low-rung physical-verifiability problem was that intelligence research could not be ridden like Uber or spotted like Flock Safety; Demis Hassabis’s polymath compounding supplied the exception. Ooak Data presents the same abstraction without equivalent biographical evidence. Ooak Data’s public APEX Explorer contains 480 Mercor tasks, not its own acquired workflows, while the real emails, financial figures, and collaboration data disappear into an anonymization pipeline. I cannot test the claimed fidelity without believing the founder’s description of it.

Pierre-Louis Vouteau’s L’Oréal distribution and quick-commerce history, Grégoire Lamy’s five years at Epsor, and Thomas Aubry’s PayLead and Samsung ML work create adjacent domain competence, not a contrarian origin story. Ooak Data would move up a full tier for me if one founder could show that a failed deployment they personally owned produced the anonymization architecture or the workflow-acquisition model. Without that autobiographical moment, Ooak Data looks discovered through operations and market timing rather than lived before incorporation.

Crusoe turned an ugly input—stranded flare gas—into a scarce computational asset. Ooak Data has a related, genuinely strange move: paying as much as $300,000 for a company ecosystem and transforming its messy operational exhaust into reusable training environments. The analogy stops where the market begins. Scale AI, Surge AI, Mercor, and Snorkel are already pursuing adjacent RL environments, so Ooak Data is magically weird in its sourcing mechanism but consensus in its destination.

More than 300 targeted ecosystem acquisitions over six months make Ooak Data operationally capital-intensive even though no rocket or camera comes out the other end. Over 18 months, Ooak Data’s data rights either compound into a proprietary library that each new AI-lab contract can reuse, or the $300,000 supplier payments compound into bespoke procurement costs and privacy liabilities. Regional storage, deletion rights, automated anonymization, and no human access to raw data make Ooak Data an unsexy regulated swing; undisclosed exclusivity and reuse economics keep it below the SpaceX-shaped end of my portfolio.

Two seven-figure AI-lab contracts lead me to predict that by July 2029 Ooak Data will reach $15 million to $30 million in ARR, with more than half concentrated in three frontier labs, after shifting from a universal workflow library toward bespoke managed RL-environment delivery. Ooak Data’s strong CEO structure can execute that shift, while its weak independently observable artifact pulls it toward services wrapped around proprietary data rather than a self-serve platform. I’m never playing the game I seem to be playing: the product may be the procurement machine, not the benchmark.

Scale AI’s capital, Surge AI’s $1.2 billion of 2024 revenue, Mercor’s 30,000 weekly contractors, and Snorkel’s simulations form the bear case that stings: Ooak Data may be an expensive sourcing layer whose two customers can internalize the work. Ooak Data also carries my red flags—three founders without disclosed conflict rules, no clear lived-problem biography, and a hot AI category crowded with consensus money. The fact that two leading labs nevertheless signed seven-figure contracts is the rebuttal, not decoration; buyers already paid before Ooak Data finished building the moat. I would flip to Pass if either major lab failed to renew and Ooak Data could not verify at least 25 independently acquired ecosystems licensed to more than one customer within 12 months.
