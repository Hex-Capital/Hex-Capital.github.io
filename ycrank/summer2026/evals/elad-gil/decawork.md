# Decawork -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: enterprises are beginning to deploy autonomous agents, IT now needs to govern them as independent actors, and Decawork becomes large only if controlling the agent’s full operational lifecycle proves more valuable than receiving identity and policy features bundled with Okta. The 2026 survey data—88% of organizations reporting confirmed or suspected agent-security incidents while only 22% treat agents as independent identities—supports the timing. It does not establish the controlling threshold: how many production agents, failed actions, or regulated workflows make an enterprise buy a separate control plane.

The strongest evidence is the product architecture. Per-agent identities, scoped credentials, gateway-mediated tool calls, approval gates, attributed logs, failure alerts, and retirement workflows form an operational loop rather than an output wrapper. Better models should increase the number and autonomy of agents passing through this loop, making Decawork more useful. Reported SOC 2 status by July 20, 2026 also indicates that the founders have done meaningful enterprise groundwork unusually early.

The problem is market structure. Okta made agent registration, ownership, credentials, and access governance generally available in April 2026. Astrix, Noma, WitnessAI, and Zenity have each raised more than $55 million for overlapping identity, security, or governance layers. Decawork’s launch and maintenance workflow may be deeper, but the dossier shows neither proprietary technical performance nor a distribution advantage. Cross-enterprise policy data is only a possible future moat, with no disclosed dataset or measured detection benefit. This category is already crystallizing, and Decawork has not yet identified the variable on which it wins.

The bull case is sharper than “agent security is growing.” Sarthak Aggarwal worked on AI systems reportedly deployed at OpenAI and Meta and on enterprise AI used by Microsoft and Hitachi; Aman Raj built compliance tooling used by more than 240 Barclays teams. Their previous autonomous-IT product ran across multiple enterprises in India and the United States. That combination could let them understand the operational boundary between identity administration and agent lifecycle management before a horizontal identity incumbent does.

But prior-product deployments do not demonstrate pull for the current control plane. There are no disclosed current customers, repeat deployments, revenue, or distribution signals. This is not a stage penalty; it means the key proposition remains untested. The known research-stage and rare-talent exceptions do not apply: Decawork is entering a well-funded application and infrastructure market, not creating a frontier technical layer whose scarcity overrides absent adoption.

I would monitor rather than invest today. The verifiable flip condition is three named enterprises running Decawork-controlled production agents, at least one expanding usage after 90 days, with evidence that they pay specifically for deployment, failure response, and retirement workflows that Okta does not provide. That would establish both product pull and the separate-control-plane boundary. Without it, the likely outcome is that Decawork either narrows into an adjacent agent-operations product or is compressed by bundled identity and security platforms.
