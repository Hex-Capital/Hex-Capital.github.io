# Index Law -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years forward, I can imagine a small business carrying its legal memory with it: every negotiated exception, ownership decision, and commercial preference survives the next contract. Index Law’s persistent company context points toward that world. But a memory feature does not yet show the path there. The dossier establishes neither reliable accumulation of legal judgment nor a transition from document assistance to a trusted, continuing relationship. That future is attractive; the evidence connecting Mike to it remains thin.

Why does a routine legal question take weeks? Index Law identifies repeated explanation, specialist coordination, and hourly billing. Firms collect fees while business owners spend time reconstructing context and waiting for answers. Giving those owners control over preparation and access to independent counsel is a meaningful repair. I particularly like that licensed judgment remains available separately: some legal expense buys accountability, and removing that expense indiscriminately would undermine the product. The unresolved economic question is how much coordination Mike actually eliminates once a matter needs a lawyer.

Pativada has experienced this operating wound himself. His prior AI venture’s reported government contracts and $20M-plus booked revenue suggest commercial experience; they are not Mike’s traction. Recruiting Dominic Raab as a senior advisor adds relevant institutional knowledge. That is more substantial than a founder discovering legal AI through a market-size chart. Still, the record does not establish a long-running legal obsession, dated experiments before founding, or independently corroborated execution at the scale claimed. I see commercial promise, with less evidence of the particular compulsion that would make this founder pursue the problem through years of awkward legal edge cases.

The strongest argument against passing is that Index Law may already be making an ordinary business irritation disappear. Its reported contract turnaround below 60 minutes and quotes up to 80% below traditional firms would matter enormously if customers repeatedly experienced them. But the November 12 launch opened a free preview, and the dossier supplies neither customer cohorts nor repeat completed matters. The proposed $149 monthly Pro subscription is a price, not a purchase. Likewise, 191,088 launch-post views show attention; they do not show someone trusting Mike with the next consequential negotiation. My preference for unglamorous workflow repair fits here, but the behavioral proof remains modest.

Underneath the interface, Index Law describes internal harnesses, matter-level reasoning, and evaluations. Those are useful engineering investments, but no scarce enabling asset or demonstrated dependency is established. Harvey and Legora already serve legal professionals, while Crosby integrates delivery with a law firm; direct access for business owners distinguishes Mike’s customer relationship without establishing control of the underlying bottleneck. Confidential documents also pass through external providers, including OpenRouter. Disclosed access controls, contractual restrictions, and encryption are relevant safeguards. They do not establish a severe privacy violation, nor do multiple external providers establish dependence on one closed supplier. Those red flags warrant investigation, not invented findings.

I would pass with my own money today. My pre-revenue exception requires compensating technical and founder evidence; the dossier does not establish that combination. My exceptions for striking product discovery or extraordinary user behavior do not apply to launch attention alone, and nothing here justifies relaxing privacy or governance standards. Index Law’s best route to changing my mind is concrete: an auditable 90-day cohort showing businesses repeatedly paying for completed matters because Mike carries prior negotiation context forward, with licensed escalation when needed. That single result would turn “company memory” from a plausible feature into evidence of a relationship customers choose to keep.
