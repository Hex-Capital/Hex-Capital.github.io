# Cyclon -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition is the strongest path.** An Android handset maker could pay for Cyclon’s OS and agent software if owning it would give the buyer an integration or development lead it could not get through a license or an internal build. Cyclon says its agent can complete tasks in the background across signed-in apps and generate and install new apps. The memo documents a launch and a refundable $199 reservation toward an estimated $699 handset. It gives no completed-sale, repeat-use, or task-reliability data. The ownership rationale is an inference. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The seed milestone is a closed beta on refurbished Pixels that shows users repeatedly and reliably completing valuable workflows beyond what existing Android tools support. Cyclon’s stated beta plan, its CTO’s Android experience, and $125,000 in reported financing make a limited test plausible. The memo leaves the test budget and runway unknown. The refurbished-device plan offers an earlier test while custom handset delivery depends on FCC authorization and carries conflicting dates. IP ownership and transfer terms remain unverified, and the memo supports no independent route to cash for this holder. (The Idea, Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** With no investment terms in the memo, the illustrative $500,000 check buys 5%, diluted to 3%, with no follow-on checks. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The threshold is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, zero fees, and no senior preference claims, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration and cash is allocated pro rata before investor taxes. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. Actual terms are unknown. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction pricing to support either sale value. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Holder cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest, $50 million cash sale | Year 5 | $1.5 million | 3× |
| Strong, $100 million cash sale | Year 7 | $3 million | 6× |
| Prolonged private, with growth plateauing, buyers staying away, public scale out of reach, and cash reinvested | Through year 8 | $0, shares still held | 0× realized |

Even the modeled strong sale yields only $2 million, or 4×, at 2% ownership.

### Countercase

Google already automates supported tasks on existing Android phones and is expanding that capability through updates. Nothing already lets users generate tools on its phones. A buyer could build or license enough of Cyclon’s proposed functionality to make ownership uneconomic. Compatibility gaps in banking, payments, and work apps, along with execution errors, could further narrow the workflows worth buying. The overlap supports waiting for beta evidence. It falls short of establishing an adequate substitute for Cyclon’s full workflow or a price ceiling across credible buyers, so the structural Pass gate remains untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a handset maker’s funded pilot show that users repeatedly complete valuable workflows reliably beyond what its existing tools support, with enough incremental value to justify buying Cyclon’s software? Comparative pilot results and a buyer-supported case for ownership over building or licensing would move the action to **Reach out** if the buyer economics support a price that clears the modeled holder hurdle.
