# OpenLake Technologies -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an inference platform is the strongest path to cash while OpenLake is private. A platform could buy OpenLake to integrate continuous post-training into its own serving stack, gaining control over how live data triggers model updates, how those updates are evaluated, and how they reach production. Ownership could justify a premium if that integration produces durable savings or faster improvements that the buyer would struggle to reproduce through an internal build or a commercial agreement. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The memo records OpenLake’s participation in MLPerf Storage v3.0. OpenLake reports that its models halve serving costs on average, and a company-published testimonial describes an unnamed team training and evaluating Security-One for production. These signals support testing the workflow in production. The ownership premium remains an inference. A paid production deployment that measures the cost of delivering model improvements at comparable quality and regression rates would test it. The reported $125,000 seed entry is unconfirmed, and the funding and runway for that test are unknown. The memo gives no usable evidence on transfer of commercial IP or any customer-data rights a buyer would need, and it supports no independent fallback that could pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** With actual check terms unavailable, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in holder cash, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. The memo gives no usable evidence of an acquisition price or buyer-side dollar economics. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The sale prices and timing below are scenario assumptions. Each sale assumes zero net debt, no other price adjustments, and a simple pro rata waterfall with no preference effects. Enterprise value therefore equals distributable equity consideration, all paid in cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each $0 in both sale cases.

| Scenario | Timing and outcome | Holder cash and gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 at $40 million enterprise value | $1.2 million, 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 at $100 million enterprise value | $3.0 million, 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested | $0, 0×, with shares retained |

Even the strong sale misses the objective at 2% ownership, yielding $2 million, or 4.0×.

### Countercase

An inference platform could assemble enough of the workflow itself or obtain the benefit through a commercial agreement. Together AI and Fireworks already combine managed post-training with inference. OpenLake’s storage engine is open source, and exportable checkpoints make trained models easier to move. That weakens the case for paying an ownership premium. The memo leaves production parity and the price ceiling unresolved, so the structural Pass override does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would owning OpenLake’s continuous post-training workflow create enough incremental value for an inference platform to support a bid that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? A buyer-validated production comparison against the best build or licensing option, with measured cost and model-quality results and a valuation that supports at least $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds under the base assumptions, would move the action to **Reach out**.
