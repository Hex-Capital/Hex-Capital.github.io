# rekursiv.ai -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Five million dollars raised is not a price. rekursiv.ai discloses neither post-money valuation nor competing-term-sheet count, and I cannot tell whether YC supplied the first scarce check or merely certified an already fashionable team. For a Summer 2026 pre-seed company, $5 million is substantial capital; without terms, scarcity, or a reason other investors dismissed the thesis, I am underwriting consensus at an unknown strike price.

The technical experiment is real, but the capital experiment is missing. The system coordinated 235 ARC experiments across 15 directions and 684 Sudoku experiments in four days, reaching 100% reported Sudoku-Extreme accuracy and materially reducing ARC computation costs. Those results establish that agents can iterate on ML problems. They do not specify what this round must prove, by what date, or what outcome dramatically changes enterprise value. Two open roles and an access-request form look like hiring plus exploration, not one named venture moment.

The strongest asset is the team. Joshua Dillon created TensorFlow Probability, built the Veo prototype, and led foundational-model pre-training; Dan Kondratyuk was first author on VideoPoet and led Luma’s World Models team. They have shipped research infrastructure, models, and the current autonomous experimentation loop. There is a plausible translation layer between human research judgment and agent execution, but it remains inside one technical ecosystem rather than connecting two parties whose transaction they uniquely control.

The missing dark pool is commercial behavior. rekursiv.ai has no named customer, paid pilot, pricing, or revenue; its GitHub organization has 16 followers and `sagent` has 29 stars. An experiment-history corpus could become proprietary, but that is inferred while discoveries and components are Apache-licensed and Sakana AI publishes competing code. Periodic Labs, Lila Sciences, Edison Scientific, and Sakana AI have collectively raised enormous capital around automated science. Benchmark leadership in that market can become metric theater unless it causes a lab to purchase, renew, or reorganize its workflow.

The bull case stings: these founders have unusually strong receipts, the product has already generated nontrivial discoveries, and one four-agent campaign reportedly produced a paper for roughly $500. Perhaps technical output compounds into proprietary experiment selection before competitors can reproduce the orchestration. But the known-exception standard requires hard external evidence—conversion, retention, acquisition demand, or an explicit one-and-done capital plan. None appears here. The cross-domain failure of Hypothesis-Pinning Search, expected four-to-six-point decline on semi-private ARC, and experiment runtime bottleneck make the leap from benchmark engine to dependable business especially unresolved.

Delete the next financing and I cannot identify the cash machine: there is no gross margin, burn, runway, or controllable revenue metric, while hundreds of experiments create an obvious compute dependency. My 36-month base case is that rekursiv.ai pivots from an autonomous scientist to internal experiment-orchestration and model-evaluation software for frontier labs, remaining below $3 million ARR by August 2029. I pass today. The verifiable flip condition is a named external ML lab converting into a prepaid contract of at least $250,000 annually after three recurring experiment campaigns; that would demonstrate that the workflow—not merely the benchmark—causes a purchase.
