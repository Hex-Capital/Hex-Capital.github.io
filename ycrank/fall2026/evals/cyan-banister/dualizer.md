# Dualizer -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years forward, I can imagine a process engineer asking a model to redesign a chemical process, inspecting the proposed changes, and deciding which experiment deserves actual plant time. Dualizer’s separation of deterministic simulation from an engineer-approved copilot is a sensible starting architecture. But reaching that future requires models calibrated against measured plant behavior, reliable handling of unfamiliar equipment and kinetics, and engineers trusting the results enough to change decisions. The disclosed demonstrations use synthetic histories. That establishes an experiment worth pursuing; it does not yet establish the little leak from that future that makes me reach for my checkbook.

The founders are the strongest reason to keep looking. Chris Donahue worked at AVEVA and taught simulation certification; Nathan Liu brings controls research, a JACS co-authorship, and a reported 17,000-plus deployments at his separate venture, Emissions Eye. Dongha Kim adds software and Materials Project experience. These are people who have spent time inside the machinery they want to improve. Their backgrounds supply more than fashionable AI credentials, although the dossier does not establish a long, independently pursued obsession with this particular problem or demonstrate their ability to recruit an operating team.

Why must an engineer manually stitch together diagrams, equipment information, operating data, and simulation tools? Dualizer identifies a real coordination burden. It has not yet identified who captures the resulting rent, quantified the delay, or shown how much independent control its product returns to an engineer. I like repairing an irritating system, but I need to understand the system’s economics. Customer-specific kinetics and unit models could make the software valuable while also making every deployment a substantial engineering engagement.

There is an enabling-layer possibility here: Dualizer describes its own deterministic simulation and optimization engine, rather than merely conversational access to someone else’s solver. That fits my pattern of looking underneath the visible AI wave. The uncomfortable counterargument is that its scarce asset remains unproven. AVEVA already supports machine-learning integration, researchers demonstrated conversational control of AVEVA through MCP in January 2026, and DWSIM offers an open-source alternative with an MCP server. If the interface becomes ordinary, Dualizer must win through simulation capability or accumulated calibration knowledge. Neither advantage is demonstrated yet.

The strongest case against passing is that this team could build the difficult engine before commercial evidence becomes visible. Winning $100,000 against 531 teams in September 2026 gives me a reason to take the technical work seriously. My pre-revenue investment in **CosmicBrain AI** shared that mechanism: relevant builders pursuing physical enablement before revenue. The material difference was its functioning prototype and signed industrial proofs of concept. Dualizer’s September account still described the first paid industrial pilot and unseen-case validation as next steps. Competition judges and accelerator selection cannot substitute for an engineer repeatedly entrusting a consequential task to the product.

I would pass today, while keeping the founders close. The dossier shows neither an equal-control deadlock nor the leadership deception that would trigger my governance veto; Dongha’s differing titles alone establish neither. It also does not establish severe privacy abuse or dependence on one closed model supplier. This is an evidence gap in my central investment mechanism, rather than an active rejection of the team. **My flip condition is one documented industrial deployment in which an engineer repeatedly uses Dualizer, calibrated on measured plant data, to make paid process-design decisions with independently checked results.** That would connect the engine, the operating burden, and actual human behavior in one observable artifact.
