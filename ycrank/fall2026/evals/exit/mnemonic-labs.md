# Mnemonic Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a quant trading firm. A buyer could acquire Mnemonic’s model and research pipeline if they produce repeatable trading profits after execution costs at a size the buyer can deploy. The memo records the November 13 launch and both founders’ prior quant trading roles. Mnemonic reports building the pipeline and model architecture in under six weeks and training on more than 25 TB of tokens. Those signals make a live test plausible. The memo supplies no live returns, execution costs, capacity results, or confirmed budget for the test. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Founders & Team; Key Risks)

Under the benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3%, with no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale prices below are illustrative. Both sale scenarios assume a closing within eight years, zero net debt or deal deductions, no senior claims, and an eligible holder selling its full stake for cash pro rata.

- **Modest sale.** $50 million in enterprise value yields $50 million in distributable equity and $1.5 million to the holder, or 3×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value yields $100 million in distributable equity and $3 million to the holder, or 6×. If only 80% of the holder’s allocation is paid in cash within eight years, realized proceeds fall to $2.4 million, or 4.8×.
- **Prolonged private.** If Mnemonic keeps operating and reinvests any trading profits, the holder realizes $0. The memo supplies no evidence of an independent payout route. (The Idea; Market & Traction)

The memo leaves achievable sale value, the actual waterfall, and holder eligibility open. It also flags a potential market-data licensing issue without establishing which terms would apply to a buyer. (Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Countercase

A quant firm could build a similar pipeline in-house using published market-event pretraining methods, and competing teams already report live deployment. Mnemonic’s edge could disappear after execution costs or at larger trade sizes, leaving a buyer with little reason to pay for the pipeline. That case supports Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural barrier to a sale nor a credible price ceiling below the proceeds hurdle. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)
