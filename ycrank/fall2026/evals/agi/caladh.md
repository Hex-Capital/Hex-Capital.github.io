# Caladh -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** Caladh’s strongest case is that qualified maritime drone mitigation could become a bottleneck at sites that can detect incursions but have limited ways to respond. Caladh claims physical barriers are hard to scale and describes Eclipse as a system for disrupting hostile maritime drones. The scenario assumes cheap frontier intelligence increases incursion volume enough to strain existing defenses. If that happens, a site-qualified system could become more valuable to operators. The memo provides no Caladh field results that establish the constraint or show Eclipse relieving it. (The Idea; Key Risks)

The Coast Guard’s request for information on underwater-drone mitigation shows independent buyer interest at the market-research stage. Kita’s doctoral research and undersea systems work support the team’s ability to pursue a field test. A site-specific hardware contract is a plausible unit of expansion, with pricing, installation costs, and ongoing support costs unknown. The memo gives no labor-cost evidence for Path B. (Market & Traction; Founders & Team; The Idea; Key Facts)

### Countercase

Incumbents could meet the same buyer requirements as drone activity rises, leaving Caladh without a durable qualification advantage. Ultra Maritime has demonstrated underwater detection and classification in a port-protection exercise, and D-Fend offers maritime aerial-drone mitigation. These offerings make substitution credible. The memo contains no buyer comparison establishing equivalent performance for Caladh’s proposed use, so the Path A gate remains unresolved and supports Watch. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)
