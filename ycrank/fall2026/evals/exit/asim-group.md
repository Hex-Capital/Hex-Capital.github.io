# Asim -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Asim’s strongest exit path is a strategic sale to a robotics manufacturer that could use its physical testing protocols and held-out scenarios across robot releases. Asim says it varies object properties and operating conditions to surface failures before deployment. A manufacturer could have a reason to own that capability if it repeatedly catches costly failures its existing tests miss. The memo documents a November benchmarking launch and YC backing, with conflicting funding amounts. It provides no paid engagements, repeat usage, measured buyer savings, or acquisition interest. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A plausible next milestone is a paid pilot across two releases that measures failures caught beyond the manufacturer’s baseline, their cost, and the labor required to run the tests. The founders’ robotics and benchmarking experience supports running the pilot. Supervised trials and scene resets make staffing and throughput important, and the memo leaves the pilot budget and runway unresolved. A company sale is structurally plausible. Asim describes separate service agreements, and a buyer would need to verify whether it could retain and reuse customer-derived test results after closing. The memo offers no evidence for a separate liquidity route. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Risks, The Idea, Defensibility)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The eight-year goal is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. Both sale scenarios below are illustrative and assume closing within eight years, $10 million in combined debt and deal adjustments, no senior claims diverting equity proceeds, and an eligible holder selling the full stake for cash pro rata.

- **Modest sale.** $40 million of enterprise value leaves $30 million of distributable equity and $900,000 in holder cash, or 1.8×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million of enterprise value leaves $90 million of distributable equity and $2.7 million in holder cash, or 5.4×. Another $6.7 million in equity deductions would erase the cushion above the objective.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with reinvested cash and no eligible share sale produces $0 in realized proceeds within eight years.

The memo supplies no valuation evidence for either sale scenario, so proceeds receive the absent-evidence default. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A manufacturer could adopt NIST’s planned baseline apparatus for common tests and buy specialized evaluations as needed. Asim’s custom trial design and labor-intensive resets could make each engagement valuable while limiting the savings from owning the whole operation. This supports Watch because a paid pilot could resolve the ownership question. The memo establishes neither a ready substitute for Asim’s held-out testing nor a supported sale price that falls materially short, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What economic benefit would a robotics manufacturer get from owning Asim’s testing capability over running baseline tests in-house and buying specialized tests as needed? A paid pilot repeated across two releases would move the action to Reach out if it quantified avoided costs and informed a buyer-side valuation that could clear the holder’s cash hurdle after deal deductions.
