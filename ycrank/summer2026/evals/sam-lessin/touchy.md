# Touchy -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I do not have the proposed post-money valuation, the competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is genuinely the first scarce check. That does not prove Touchy is being auctioned, but YC plus a fashionable consumer-agent thesis is exactly where price can outrun belief. Without terms, I underwrite the deal at the financing default: I cannot tell whether I am buying cheap uncertainty or paying for a consensus that Apple, Poke, Rabbit, Pally, and TwinMind have already advertised.

The more serious problem is that the round has no named venture moment. Touchy has built a real iPhone product: lock-screen access, voice and camera input, memory, and connections to more than 1,000 tools. But the dossier does not say what the capital will test, by what date, or what measurable result would dramatically change the company’s value. “Models are getting cheaper” is not an experiment. It is an uncontrollable input available to every competitor, and the founders themselves say present models remain insufficient for their intended personalization and require airtight safety before receiving broader access.

The team is the affirmative signal. Andrew Liu shipped educational software used by roughly 500,000 students; Kevin Wang was a founding engineer at Eventual, contributed to Daft and SkyPilot, and has a repository with approximately 2,500 stars; Rohit Agarwal researches AI mathematics and agent alignment at Princeton. Collectively, they have receipts spanning user-facing language software and the infrastructure beneath agent execution. That makes the rapid move from Tokenless infrastructure to Touchy more intelligible—but it also suggests that the durable asset may be the team’s technical capability, not this consumer product or distribution channel.

Touchy does not yet own a dark pool that causes economic behavior. Its distribution is the App Store and a YC launch; the launch produced roughly 11,000 views and 63 likes, while the app has too few ratings for Apple to display an overview. Touchy Plus is listed at $14.99, but there is no evidence of a purchase, repeat use, retention, or proprietary workflow. Apple controls the device, permissions, and system-level actions and announced overlapping Siri AI capabilities in June 2026. User memories might eventually create switching costs, but resemblance to a moat is not a moat.

The strongest bull case is that this is an unusually technical team shipping before the market’s safety and interface problems are solved. Ando also carried AI-model risk, but its exception was earned through greater than 90% forecast accuracy, complete pilot-to-paid conversion, and complete retention. Touchy has none of that override evidence yet. It also provides no revenue, inference margin, burn, runway, or route to profitability without another round. A nominal subscription price cannot establish one-and-done economics when every useful interaction incurs model costs and privileged-data handling risk.

My 36-month base case is that Touchy pivots from a general consumer iPhone assistant into enterprise agent infrastructure—closer to the founders’ prior Tokenless direction—or shuts down before reaching $1 million ARR. I am passing, not because the founders cannot build, but because the product presently depends on rented distribution, commoditizing models, and an operating-system owner launching the same abstraction. One condition flips the decision: a disclosed cohort of 1,000 paying Touchy subscribers with more than 50% still paying after 90 days. That would show the workflow itself causes durable purchases rather than merely attracting launch attention.
