# Agnost AI -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Agnost AI may make agents less frustrating, but the dossier does not quantify a consequential cost pool being rewritten. A $1.23 billion observability market growing to $8.62 billion measures vendor spending, not the value of failed agent interactions or the population affected. Nothing demonstrates a 10x collapse in support labor, software-development cost, or another expert bottleneck. The stated outcome is better product analytics. Useful, yes. Transformative, not yet.

The product has substance beyond a dashboard. It processes more than 1 million events daily, clusters conversation-derived intents and violations, connects them to tool calls, and proposes pull requests tested against historical conversations. But LangSmith, Braintrust, Arize, and Langfuse already own adjacent traces, evaluations, and behavior categorization. Agnost provides no measured accuracy, time-to-diagnosis, or failure-reduction advantage against any of them. Without a 5–10x benchmark or proprietary cross-customer learning loop, conversation-to-PR is a feature competitors can reproduce, not a technical discontinuity.

The largest kill risk is suite encroachment. Yet no milestone attacks it directly. I see no controlled test comparing Agnost with LangSmith, no threshold for acceptable false fixes, and no evidence that historical-conversation testing predicts unseen agent behavior. The reported $250,000 financing funded a working product and impressive event volume, but the dossier does not show dollars being allocated against this existential risk. Hiring a growth engineer while technical defensibility remains unresolved risks scaling distribution before proving why the product survives bundling.

The founders are the best part of the case. Shubham Palriwala brings analytics and first-hire product experience from Formbricks; Parth Ajmera has built terabyte-scale pipelines at Microsoft. Moving from MCP analytics at roughly 2.5 million calls monthly to broader conversation product analytics at more than 1 million daily events is evidence of tactical movement, though not yet documented belief revision after a failed hypothesis. Their eight-year relationship and relevant systems experience reduce execution risk. They have not demonstrated magnet recruiting around the named security, evaluation, or model-quality risks.

The strongest opposing case is real: teams associated with Google, Exa Labs, and Corgi Insurance are reportedly using or engaging with the product; the launch earned 77 Hacker News points and 40 comments; and self-service pricing from $49 to $499 monthly could support rapid adoption. A small software wedge can expand into infrastructure when usage creates a proprietary learning loop. But the plausible exception is not established here: event volume is not revenue, Google evidence appears to be an engineer integration rather than an enterprise commitment, and retention ranges from 7 to 90 days without evidence that customer taxonomies compound into a shared advantage.

The economics are unlikely to require subsidy, but Agnost discloses no inference, storage, support, or gross-margin model showing that $499 per million monthly events remains attractive at scale. My base case is that by August 2029 the company has pivoted from standalone observability into conversation-derived regression testing and remains below $3 million ARR, because the broader suites absorb its analytics features. I pass today. I would flip if three named production customers publish a controlled benchmark showing Agnost reduces undetected agent-task failures by at least 5x versus LangSmith or Braintrust.
