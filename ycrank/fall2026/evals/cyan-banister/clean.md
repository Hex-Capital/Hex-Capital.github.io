# Clean -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032: a small supplier sees a factory expansion and knows which purchase is coming, who controls it, and when to call. That would return something valuable to the little seller: timing that previously required an expensive research team. Clean’s shipment, permit, loan, and contract timelines give me something concrete to examine. But the dossier establishes event aggregation; the connection from those events to a particular purchase remains an inferred future moat. Collecting more records and learning when somebody will buy are different technical achievements. My money depends on the second.

Why are sellers doing this research themselves? Vivameda says half its exported contacts had bounced or changed roles. Somebody still has to inspect the list, find replacements, and decide whether an account deserves attention. Clean attacks that wasted labor and gives sellers time back. This fits my preference for repairing irritating systems. The evidence identifies a genuine workflow failure, though it does not establish a gatekeeper extracting a particular rent or an institutional restriction that Clean removes. I see useful agency returned, with a narrower mechanism than the headline promises.

The strongest argument against passing is that customers apparently care already. Clean reports that Vivameda’s reply rate rose from roughly 4% to 19%, with $60,000 in contracts closed within two weeks. Those are Vivameda’s sales, not Clean’s revenue; the separate founder claim is $240,000 ARR before YC. Even with both claims needing customer verification, this is considerably more informative than a waitlist. Someone used the output to conduct a campaign and close business. Clean could build a worthwhile company simply by delivering better research, even if purchase prediction never becomes its defining capability. What the case study cannot establish is whether the improvement came from timing, fresher contacts, or better targeting.

The founders also have evidence of making things: Pratham’s separate Lokus product has 831 GitHub stars, Pavan brings Cisco enterprise-sales experience, and the team includes prior startup and engineering work. That earns more confidence than credentials alone. The September 2026 EO interview documents a summer pivot from developer infrastructure; it does not establish opportunism, but neither does the dossier establish a longstanding obsession with purchasing behavior. My pre-revenue exception at **CosmicBrain AI** rested on a functioning artifact combined with lifelong founder obsession and a concrete manufacturing thesis. Clean shares the artifact signal and adds reported revenue, but the founder history and future mechanism needed for that override are less developed here.

I also look for the concealed infrastructure beneath the application. Clean claims its own datasets and preserves record provenance, but exclusive rights and a controlled data bottleneck are unproven. Clay’s September 2026 research agents and Apollo’s signal-based outreach put competitive pressure directly on this workflow. That does not make Clean trivial to build; it makes ownership of the learning especially consequential. Privacy is another unresolved gate: optional email connections and encryption are useful safeguards, but teammate visibility into prospect correspondence still requires clear consent and access boundaries. The dossier does not demonstrate a severe violation, so I would not treat one as established.

I pass with my own money today. Clean has stronger evidence that people want its research than that it owns a necessary piece of what comes next. My verifiable condition for reopening the decision is a customer-auditable longitudinal study showing that Clean’s accumulated purchase-outcome history predicts buying windows materially better than the same underlying operational records alone. That would demonstrate that serving customers creates an asset, and give me a reason to believe the distance between Clean and another research workflow grows with use.
