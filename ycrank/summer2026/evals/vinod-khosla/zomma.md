# Zomma -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is too small as documented. Investment operations certainly waste hours moving data across 10–15 disconnected systems, but Zomma has not quantified the labor pool, error losses, assets affected, or cost reduction possible. A $1.96 billion financial-agent market growing at 14.3% is a market-research category, not evidence of a 100x societal consequence. The current upside is credible enterprise automation, not yet a rewritten cost curve.

The technical claim is more promising than the TAM claim. Operating portals, desktop applications, PDFs, and spreadsheets without APIs could eliminate a real constraint that scripted RPA cannot handle. Yet the dossier contains no measured task-completion rate, reviewer burden, accuracy, deployment time, or cost comparison against UiPath or human operators. Browser Use and Asteroid also make the control layer increasingly available. Firm-specific correction memory and seven-year audit logs may create switching costs, but they do not prove a 5–10x discontinuity.

The largest kill risk is reliability under heterogeneous workflows. Varun has correctly named failure handling, reviewer fatigue, credentials, and 2FA, while the company uses approval gates and isolated credential storage. Naming the risks is useful; spending against them is what matters. There is no experiment with a pass/fail threshold, cost, timing, or stop condition. Meanwhile every engagement requires workflow mapping, supervised runs, and a dedicated engineer. That risks scaling implementation labor before proving autonomous reliability.

The strongest case for Zomma is the team. Varun brings ML and verification experience from Bronco AI and Apple; Cashel combines computer vision with backend systems supporting $20 billion in annual transactions; Jihyun has operated inside Goldman Sachs, Endowus, and Arrowpoint. The move from enterprise sales and application support in April 2026 to investment operations by August suggests tactical flexibility, although the dossier does not show what customer evidence caused it. Unquantified usage by hedge funds, asset managers, and fintechs adds a weak but real signal that the new wedge is not imaginary.

The bull case stings: a technically capable, complementary team may have found the ugly no-API work that horizontal agent vendors avoid, and forward deployment could be the temporary bridge through which reusable workflow traces accumulate. But the adverse possibility is equally concrete: corrections remain customer-specific, reviewers remain in the loop, and each new account requires another engineer. Then Zomma is an AI-enabled integration service competing beside well-funded specialists, not a scalable learning system. Within 36 months, I expect it to narrow to one investment-operations workflow and remain below $3 million ARR because deployment labor constrains expansion.

I pass today. The verifiable flip condition is a cohort of at least three financial firms showing a 10x reduction in operator time, fewer than one human intervention per 100 actions, and deployment of each subsequent workflow in under five engineer-days. That would simultaneously establish technical discontinuity, risk removal, and a plausible path beyond services economics.
