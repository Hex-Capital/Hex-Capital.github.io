# ernd -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years from now, a small company might buy AI with the bargaining power of a large enterprise, moving spending among suppliers without surrendering its discounts. I would like that world. But getting there requires pooled spending to become durable negotiating authority, then benefits that survive vendors changing their acquisition budgets. ernd’s advertised rewards establish neither transition yet. “The credit card for companies that pay for AI” identifies a customer today; the evidence does not yet reveal what that customer gains lasting control over.

The doubled Firecrawl usage limits are the most interesting object in the dossier. Why should a startup receive twice the capacity simply by changing its payment relationship? That suggests room to redistribute vendor acquisition spending toward buyers. The strongest bull case is that ernd becomes a purchasing cooperative disguised as a card: recruit buyers, negotiate better terms, attract more buyers. But the distinction between bargaining power and subsidized distribution matters here. The October 27 rewards terms say ernd funds the OpenAI and Anthropic rewards itself, neither provider endorses them, and exhausted budgets can leave new points pending. Those rewards demonstrate a promotional expense; they do not establish that ernd has extracted a durable concession from those suppliers.

The founders give me more confidence than the positioning. Connor Dawson previously generated over $50,000 through Instagram pages; Nikhil Ruia built games exceeding 15,000 downloads; Malcolm Wengel co-founded ARCodeKids, which raised nearly $600,000. Those are separate instances of getting people to do something, pay attention, or commit resources. Ripple stablecoin work and industrial investing also put relevant experience around the table. I see builders with commercial initiative. I do not yet see the pre-company obsession with purchasing economics that would make me believe this particular problem belongs to them. Their prior accomplishments earn founder credit, not ernd revenue credit.

The company-reported ten startups onboarded and five vendor partnerships in the first 48 hours are encouraging evidence of initial execution. They leave the expensive behavior unobserved: customers repeatedly routing meaningful spending through ernd after comparing the actual redemption terms. Cards remain in early access, and rewards cannot reimburse OpenAI or Anthropic charges directly. I make pre-revenue exceptions when a working prototype and an unusually deep founder history carry the bet. Here, the record establishes neither that combination nor the extraordinary repeated user behavior that could independently pull me across the line. Missing revenue is not my objection; the current onboarding count simply answers a smaller question.

The receipt product also asks for something unusually valuable: whole-inbox read access through Gmail or Outlook. Processing only qualifying receipt PDFs, encrypting tokens, and allowing disconnection are useful safeguards, but the permission reaches beyond the intended inputs. That is a concrete tension with returning control to customers. Meanwhile, the concealed infrastructure opportunity I look for remains unproven: no exclusive purchasing rights, controlled technical bottleneck, or scarce asset is established. Ramp already offers AI spending visibility and controls. ernd needs a reason customers entrust it with spending and sensitive access that survives an incumbent matching the offer.

I would pass with my own money today. The team could build a capable vendor acquisition business, but I cannot yet underwrite the leap from distributing benefits to controlling purchasing economics. One verifiable flip condition would bring me back: a documented 90-day cohort repeatedly routing AI spending through ernd, receiving contractually secured vendor-funded savings that remain profitable without ernd subsidies, through a receipt flow that requires no whole-inbox access. That would connect customer agency, repeat behavior, and sustainable bargaining power in one observable mechanism.
