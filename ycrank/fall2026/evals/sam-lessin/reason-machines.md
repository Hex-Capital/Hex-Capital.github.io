# Reason Machines -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Reason Machines reports 10,000 users, but I cannot price the check. Proposed post-money, competing term sheets, and whether my money enables something otherwise unfundable are all undocumented. That does not establish an auction; YC membership does not establish one either. It leaves my primary gate unanswered. The second gate is equally open: the dossier describes a working product, but no capital-funded experiment with a metric and deadline. Building a useful coding agent and identifying a venture moment are separate accomplishments.

The founders provide the strongest affirmative evidence. Adi Singh has built an IDE through Dereference, and Sven Myhre brings production ML experience. Reason’s separation of a persistent controller from interchangeable execution machines addresses a concrete operating problem: keeping work moving across environments without making the engineer supervise every interruption. This fits my pattern of backing people who translate between systems. Actual shipped software earns credit beyond university names and job titles, although the record does not yet demonstrate repeated enterprise deployments on the customer side of that bridge.

The strongest argument against passing is that the team may already have found distribution before anyone has properly measured it. Founder-reported 10,000 users and 300 active customer Slack channels over the preceding 14 days, reported October 22, are substantial early activity for this stage. A persistent teammate inside those channels could accumulate useful context and become expensive to remove. But the dossier establishes neither that switching cost nor that proprietary access caused adoption. The $1,000 YC credit offer also makes payment behavior particularly informative. I cannot turn active channels into paying accounts, repository stars into retained usage, or Bookface access into an owned dark pool.

Reason’s reported 22% lower cost per solved task than Codex is a concrete technical claim worth investigating. It does not establish the economics of the $50 Pro or $200 Max subscriptions. There is no disclosed paid conversion, gross margin, burn, or route to break-even. Meanwhile, the harness builds on Pi, the messaging component permits competitive reuse, and Devin already offers shared context and external agents. That combination activates my concern about AI application advantages being competed away. It does not prove Reason will lose, but it makes ownership of customer workflow more consequential than a benchmark lead.

I checked the exceptions before deciding. The adoption-based exception has some support in the user count, but lacks documented enterprise purchases or comparable external commercial validation. The operating-economics exception lacks measured conversion and retention; the person-level financing exception lacks a proposed structure or capital plan. There is also no evidence of financing pressure, equal-founder paralysis, or dependence on another round, so I am not inventing those red flags. **I pass on the present evidence.** One verifiable result would reopen the decision: a documented cohort showing at least 30 of those 300 Slack-channel customers paying full price, remaining paid for 90 days, and generating positive contribution after model, hosting, and support costs. That would establish a functioning base business worth underwriting; today, its existence remains unresolved.
