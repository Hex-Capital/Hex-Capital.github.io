# Aktoria Robotics -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The important fact is not that Aktoria has robotics researchers from CMU, Zoox, and Microsoft Research. It is that two founders have already assembled 15-plus operators in India who control robots across four U.S. sites around the clock, reporting 158-millisecond latency and takeover in under one second ([LinkedIn, July 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shreenabh_we-just-joined-y-combinator-s26-a-year-activity-7483928525578629121-VINW), [Y Combinator, 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/aktoria-robotics)). That is a real technical proof, not a diagram. But the dossier never names what broke during a deployment, what the founders personally did next, or how quickly their workaround changed the result. Building a difficult system demonstrates ability. It does not yet demonstrate resourcefulness under resistance.

The four operating sites are more informative than Aktoria’s booking funnel, Accel backing, or roughly 59,000 combined LinkedIn followers. Someone is letting these operators control physical machines continuously. Yet the customers are unnamed, and there is no disclosed payment, retention period, intervention volume, or expansion within a fleet. The offer to waive onboarding fees for the next ten customers suggests founder-led selling, which is good, but also suggests that repeatable demand has not been established ([Aktoria](https://aktoria.com/), [LinkedIn, July 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shreenabh_aktoria-robotics-got-into-y-combinator-physical-activity-7483566178548977664-sqBt)). I see a functioning product in a plausible narrow well. I cannot yet tell how deep the well is.

The strongest case for investing is the concrete-proof pattern. Agrawal’s robotics work and Walia’s machine-learning research have become a deployed low-latency stack rather than remaining credentials. The team has also embraced the ugly part: integrations, shift coverage across three countries, operator training, security, and physical-world failure handling. If every intervention becomes training data, the labor service could be scaffolding for a valuable robot-data substrate. That would turn apparent non-scalability into the mechanism by which the scalable product is discovered.

The opposing mechanism is equally concrete. Open teleoperation systems reduce the software barrier, autonomy improves precisely by eliminating unfamiliar cases, and Phantom Auto shut down after raising $95 million despite having deployments ([TechCrunch, March 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/12/remote-driving-startup-phantom-auto-is-shutting-down/)). Aktoria could therefore inherit service-company costs while its customers capture most of the learning. No dataset size, integration count, uptime statistic, recurring price, or data right shows that interventions accumulate into a proprietary asset. My 36-month expectation would be a move toward robot-data collection and intervention-quality infrastructure rather than remaining principally a managed teleoperation service.

The unstated-pattern test is favorable on measured proof and tolerance for operational ugliness. The red-flag test is mostly clean: distribution is founder-led rather than dependent on a ceremonial launch or unspecified large partner, and the founders have not prematurely hired a sales organization. But their prior shared execution is documented only as overlapping graduate study, and there is no evidence that they have encountered contrary product evidence and changed route without losing the goal. The known-exception logic does not rescue this gap. The current measurements are company-reported, and neither independently validated proof nor unmistakable customer pull is strong enough to substitute for the missing founder-behavior evidence.

I would not write the check yet. This is not a rejection of the technology; it is a refusal to infer the founders’ behavior from the artifact they built. One verifiable event would flip me: a named robotics customer confirms that a deployment failure was fixed by the founders within two weeks and that the revised system then remained in paid, repeated operation for at least 90 days. That would connect the three facts currently separated in the dossier—resistance, founder action, and user pull.
