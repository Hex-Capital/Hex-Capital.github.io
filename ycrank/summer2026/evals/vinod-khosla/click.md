# Click -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Click is selling convenience into a $16.28 billion AI-search market, not yet collapsing a consequential cost pool. One installation spanning LinkedIn activity, YouTube transcripts, financial data, Maps, travel fares, Reddit, and X could save agents integration work. But the dossier quantifies neither the labor displaced nor a population receiving radically cheaper expertise. The upside case is therefore a useful retrieval layer, not a 100x societal consequence.

The technical case is weaker. Click assembles Exa, Crustdata, Apify, Fiber AI, Financial Datasets, and other providers behind MCP; no proprietary dataset, architecture, or measured improvement is disclosed. OpenAI already supports native web search and remote MCP servers, while Exa, Tavily, Linkup, and Parallel collectively have raised hundreds of millions of dollars. Click has shown no 5–10x advantage in retrieval quality, latency, coverage, or cost against any of them. This is systems integration exposed to both upstream repricing and downstream platform substitution.

The bull case is Aditya Asgaonkar. An Ethereum Foundation researcher with consensus, formal-verification, distributed-systems, and agent-context experience is substantially more credible than a generic AI-interface founder. Click has also changed from an assistant frontend to operations automation and then research connectors, suggesting tactical flexibility. But the dossier does not connect those changes to customer evidence, document a deliberately revised belief, or show that a complementary recruit owns distribution, data licensing, or retrieval quality. A solo technical founder can change direction quickly; he can also drift quickly.

The largest kill risk is whether aggregation retains value once model vendors and data providers expose their own connectors. Yet there is no decisive experiment, numerical threshold, cost, or stop condition aimed at that risk. The $49 monthly plan with 500 credits establishes a price, not economics: provider costs, gross margin, query utilization, and retention are absent. Capital spent adding connectors before proving durable demand or proprietary routing would increase surface area without removing the existential risk.

The strongest exception is the mutable software wedge: Block began without a scientific discontinuity and expanded far beyond its initial device. The shared mechanism is that a narrow interface can become infrastructure; the material difference is that Block addressed an enormous, already-measured payments value pool with a compact team, while Click has one founder, no reported paying usage, 157 LinkedIn followers, and no evidence that cross-provider activity compounds into a proprietary learning loop. The exception does not apply yet.

I would pass. Click fits the portfolio’s unstated appetite for execution-heavy software only superficially; the present product triggers the more important red flag—prevailing AI infrastructure packaged through connectors and convenience rather than novel capability. One condition flips the decision: demonstrate that proprietary routing delivers at least a 5x measured cost, coverage, or task-success advantage over native OpenAI search and the best single-provider alternative, with repeat paid usage and positive gross margin after connector costs.
