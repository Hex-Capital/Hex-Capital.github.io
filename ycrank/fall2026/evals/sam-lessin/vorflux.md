# Vorflux -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Vorflux has already raised a reported $15 million seed from YC, Peak XV, Powerset, Alliance, and prominent angels. What scarce capital am I supplying? The post-money valuation and competing-term-sheet count are undisclosed; I cannot turn the round size into an invented valuation. But the documented syndicate is affirmative evidence of access to fashionable capital. That puts the deal below my absent-information default on capital scarcity. An impressive engineering team can still be the wrong personal angel investment.

The second question is what this check proves. Vorflux coordinates planning, parallel implementation, cross-model review, and browser QA. Those are concrete capabilities, but the dossier identifies no capital-funded experiment with a deadline and a measurable result that changes the business’s value. Cursor already offers cloud agents with browser verification; Factory supplies model routing and multi-agent workflows. Vorflux needs to establish why customers will keep buying its coordination layer as those alternatives improve. Missing use-of-funds information receives my default score; feature overlap makes the unanswered question consequential.

The strongest objection to passing is that these founders could make delegation work where competitors leave customers supervising software. Prasanna co-founded Rippling and served as CTO; Anant and Haritha bring enterprise infrastructure experience from Databricks and Rubrik. Vorflux has also shipped something people have used: TrashLab implemented specific interface controls, and Supermemory described a two-day optimization session consuming $600 in compute. This fits my pattern of backing teams that connect difficult operating systems. It earns founder credit, although the dossier does not establish independently attributable prior deployment outcomes across both sides of this particular bridge. These are credible builders with a functioning product.

Where that bull case stops is ownership of the customer advantage. Persistent repository knowledge could become a proprietary asset, but no retention or migration evidence establishes that yet. The testimonials document completed work; they do not show that an owned audience, exclusive data, or embedded workflow caused a purchase. The reported trillion tokens measure consumption, not customer dependence. Likewise, prepaid compute pricing of $0.2117–$3.3868 per hour plus model usage describes billing, while leaving gross profit, remediation costs, burn, and break-even unknown. I give the specified defaults for unproven proprietary conversion and cash economics. I do not assume negative margins, disappearing agent errors, or compulsory future financing.

I pass at the evidence available. The exceptions to my price and AI skepticism require hard operating receipts strong enough to overwhelm the gates: retained paid demand, demonstrated economics, or unusually decisive external validation. Vorflux’s named customer work makes that outcome plausible, but does not establish it. Nor do three co-founders establish an equal-control committee; decision rights are simply unknown. My one verifiable condition for reopening the investment is a six-month cohort of ten unrelated paying engineering teams that renew while producing positive contribution margins after compute and human remediation. That would show Vorflux owns a durable customer relationship rather than merely processing expensive work.
