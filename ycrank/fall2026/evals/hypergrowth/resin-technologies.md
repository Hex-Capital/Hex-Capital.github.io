# Resin Technologies -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Product-led adoption.** PCB engineers at hardware teams are the initial buyer segment. They repeatedly select parts, draft schematics, and route boards. Resin offers browser signup, editable KiCad and Altium imports, and a workflow from schematic drafting through routing and design-rule checks. The founder reports that unnamed teams shipped boards in hours versus weeks. If confirmed, shipping a board is a meaningful early test of fit. The memo gives no buyer confirmation, paid conversion, or evidence that those teams activated without founder help. (The Idea, Key Risks, Market & Traction)

The growth scenario assumes engineers can import a live project, get a usable board through their own review, and return for the next design. Reusable workspace libraries could make that second project easier, and advertised per-seat pricing gives Resin a way to monetize adoption by teammates. The same workflow could serve teams abroad if their parts are available and their fabrication rules fit the existing product. The global market estimate gives category context. Cross-market buyer demand, localization effort, and paid expansion remain untested. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Engineers could try Resin on one board and return to their incumbent tools if checking and correcting generated work consumes the time it saves. Resin labels its autorouter beta, warns of wiring, component, and clearance errors, and requires engineering review before fabrication. Incumbent tools also offer routing automation. Review adds work for the buyer. For Resin, the relevant incremental costs are AI and routing compute per project, plus any onboarding, correction support, or security review each new account needs. The memo gives no cost measurements or deployment records that establish proportional vendor effort, so the Pass gate remains unmet. (Key Risks, Defensibility, The Idea)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent cohort of PCB teams outside the US go from standard browser signup to a first fabricated board and then a second paid project at its normal design cadence? Buyer-confirmed fabrication and billing records, paired with measured compute and Resin staff time, would answer it. I would move to Reach out if the cohort repeated that sequence with the existing workflow, without founder-led setup, and with delivery costs that leave room for positive contribution.
