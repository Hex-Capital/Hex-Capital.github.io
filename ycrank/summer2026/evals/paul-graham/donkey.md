# Donkey -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Donkey takes title, clears customs, inspects goods before final payment, and offers qualified buyers net-45 terms, so Donkey is a high-schlep, low-hacker-genealogy bet: more trading animal than software tribe. **Coinbase** (2012) is the high rung for schlep because compliance was not adjacent to the product; compliance was the product. Donkey has the same load-bearing mechanism. Customs valuation, factory verification, credit insurance, and buyer-default exposure are precisely the work clever founders unconsciously avoid. Donkey diverges because Coinbase had Armstrong’s working Android wallet, while Donkey has disclosed neither a completed current-product order nor evidence that its first-sale documentation has survived CBP scrutiny. The schlep is real. Its mastery is not yet proven.

**Loopt** (2005) is the low rung on hacker genealogy: force of personality had to compensate for weak product timing and the absence, in this record, of a pre-check technical artifact that made the bet obvious. Donkey has Minghao Tan building a customs-filings parser, factory-versus-forwarder classification, and trade-operations software, but Donkey has no public repositories, competitive-programming record, open-source trail, or Hacker News artifact. Benjamin Martindale studied finance and supplies the commercial force. That pairing may work, but the unstated pattern cuts against Donkey: hackers usually leave tracks, and the dossier contains credentials plus a description of code, not an independently visible hack.

Benjamin Martindale acquired 31 paying factory customers for Prelude OS in 39 days, reached cash-flow positivity, and later drove from Vermont to San Francisco visiting independent wholesale distributors. Those are animal tracks even though the revenue belongs to Prelude rather than Donkey. Over 18 months, Donkey’s manual visits can compound into an unusually accurate map of reorder behavior, objections, creditworthiness, and factory economics. The danger is metric laundering: Donkey cannot call Prelude’s customers Donkey traction. But someone who sold factories one product quickly and then crossed a continent to sell importers another has demonstrated more than résumé resourcefulness.

Martindale’s Mandarin-language factory work and advice to Sany and Sinoboom place Donkey close to the problem, but proximity is not the same as an organic idea. Donkey’s dossier never says Martindale personally paid a 15–30% trading-company markup, lost a shipment, or built the first version to manage his own import. To move Donkey into the top organic-idea tier, I would need to learn that Martindale encountered the hidden-factory and marked-up-invoice problem inside Prelude, then built Donkey’s photo-to-factory workflow for an order he personally needed to place. Without that fact, founder-market fit is plausible but inferred, so the organic score stays closer to the absent-evidence default.

**Stripe** (2010) is the useful single parallel for Donkey’s cofounder configuration. The Collisons combined a long relationship with complementary technical ability and shared commerce history. Donkey has two University of Toronto founders who worked together on Prelude before Donkey, with Martindale handling factories and selling while Tan builds the customs-data machinery. Donkey therefore has the right shape: a pair with prior operating history and complementary coverage. The missing fact is duration. The dossier cannot establish whether the Donkey founders built together for years or merely overlapped briefly before applying.

Donkey’s inspectors across seven Chinese provinces give Donkey a plausible route to becoming a large managed trading operation, but the low hacker signal means software is more likely to assist the schlep than dissolve it. By July 2029, I predict Donkey will be processing $50–100 million of annualized import volume and earning $6–12 million of annualized net revenue, chiefly as an importer-of-record and inspection service rather than as a self-serve AI software product. Factory reliability records and landed-cost data should improve quoting, while every additional order still creates paperwork, inspections, financing exposure, and exceptions that require humans.

ImportYeti’s 70 million bills of lading and two million-plus users make the bear case sting: Donkey could become a thin-spread commodity trader built atop data others already possess. Donkey has no disclosed current orders, carries goods through delivery, offers net-45 terms, and depends partly on first-sale valuation documentation; one customs dispute or buyer-default cluster could consume many successful orders. The **Airbnb** (2009) exception is not a blank check—Prelude’s 31 customers are not yet Donkey’s cereal boxes. I still invest because ImportYeti does not buy, inspect, insure, clear, and deliver, while Donkey’s founders have already shown they will perform the unpleasant work. If Donkey cannot show at least 10 importers completing two paid orders each, with positive contribution after customs and credit losses, by July 2027, I would flip to Pass.
