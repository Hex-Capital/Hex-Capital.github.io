# Forward -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Forward’s strongest path is to turn debt-advisor referrals into a repeatable private-credit brokerage. Advisors submit financing documents for free. Forward packages them into a brief, matches the deal against current lender mandates, and coordinates submissions and terms. Lenders pay 25 basis points when an introduced loan funds. An advisor testimonial describes introductions to unfamiliar lenders and help securing term sheets, which supports the workflow’s value through that stage. The same screening and coordination tools could serve comparable advisors and funds abroad, with lender coverage, document requirements, and closing workflows still untested in a second market. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The operating evidence shows a repeatable workflow within a sales-led rollout. Forward started with cold calls and LinkedIn outreach, then launched with one exclusive Southern California advisor. It reports that 100 U.S. funds signed fee agreements within three months of its July U.S. arrival. The agreements show willingness to accept the fee structure. The path for an ordinary advisor or lender to join and reach first value independently remains unproven. Retained lenders could pay more by funding more or larger loans through Forward, but the memo leaves repeat funded volume and fees from the same lenders unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The reported $180,000 annualized revenue and $545,000 contracted revenue leave the amount earned on funded loans and the pace of realized growth unclear. Reported deal volume is measured at processing or origination, and the two named transactions were still progressing toward close. The memo therefore lacks a usable baseline for realized commercial traction. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

In an illustrative scenario where one of ten equal US$10 million submissions funds, Forward earns US$25,000 across the ten submissions, or US$2,500 per submission on average. That revenue must cover attributable acquisition and onboarding, document processing, matching, and follow-up across all ten deals. Forward says AI can handle lender discovery and routine coordination. The memo provides no measured staff time or processing cost to establish how much contribution that automation leaves. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Forward could have strong fit with its first advisor and still scale like a relationship-led brokerage. One exclusive partner supplied the initial rollout, lenders must be recruited and their mandates kept current, and Forward can spend time coordinating deals that generate no fee. If each new market requires rebuilding the lender network and each deal continues to demand substantial manual work, growth could consume sales and delivery capacity as fast as it adds revenue. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo leaves both the extent of market-specific rebuilding and the amount of unavoidable manual work unresolved. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a new advisor cohort in a second country use the existing workflow, with bounded localization and little staff help, to generate repeat funded loans profitably? A pilot showing repeat fees from the same lenders and positive contribution after counting acquisition, onboarding, and work on failed deals would support moving to Reach out if the updated evaluation reaches the Invest tier with at least Medium confidence.
