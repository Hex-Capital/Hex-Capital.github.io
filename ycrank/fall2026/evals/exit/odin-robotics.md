# Odin Robotics -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an industrial automation provider is Odin’s strongest supported route. The memo documents CNC machine-tending and conveyor-packing demonstrations. Odin describes a retrofit that lets operators teach new motions on existing lines. An incumbent such as Formic could value the software and machine integrations if they cut the cost of serving factories with frequent part changes. The memo provides no measured savings or evidence of buyer interest. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Odin’s proposed pilot tests cycle time and quality on customer parts, and the founders report on-site factory work. The next milestone is repeatable performance in paid production cells, with measured uptime, changeover time, and installation labor across factories. The memo reports $500,000 in funding and leaves the cost, timeline, and funding for those deployments open. It also leaves IP ownership, third-party permissions, and contract transfer terms unverified. There is no evidenced independent route to holder liquidity. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale scenarios assume a full cash acquisition within eight years, holder eligibility, and a pro rata waterfall with no senior preference reducing the holder’s share.

| Scenario | Assumed proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Modest sale | $40 million enterprise value, with zero net debt or deal adjustments | $1.2 million (2.4×) |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value, with zero net debt or deal adjustments. If net debt and deal adjustments reduce equity value by $20 million, distributable equity falls to $80 million. | $3.0 million (6.0×), or $2.4 million (4.8×) after the assumed deductions |
| Prolonged private | Odin continues operating and reinvests available cash, with no funded payout or eligible share purchase within eight years. | $0 realized |

The sale prices are illustrative. The memo provides no Odin-specific price evidence or investor terms to establish realizable proceeds. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Formic already sells installed automation for overlapping tasks and is working with Physical Intelligence on manufacturing applications. If each Odin installation still requires substantial machine-specific controls, tooling, and guarding work, an incumbent could build or partner for similar capabilities without buying Odin. This weakens the acquisition rationale. The memo establishes neither a ready substitute for Odin’s proposed changeover workflow nor a structural closing barrier or a supported sale range below the holder’s hurdle. The Pass gate is therefore unmet. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Do paid deployments across unrelated factories show repeatable savings large enough to give an automation incumbent a reason to buy Odin at a price that clears the holder’s hurdle? Customer-verified changeover, uptime, and installation-labor data, tied to a defensible buyer valuation after deal deductions, would move the action to Reach out. If the evidence remains limited to demonstrations and founder-reported activity, keep Watch.
