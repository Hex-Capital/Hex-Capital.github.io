# machine0 -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

If machine0 works exactly as described, it makes long-running agents easier to operate; it does not yet rewrite the economics of computation or expert work. The dossier quantifies neither the cost pool displaced nor the number of workers affected. Persistent VMs may remove laptop constraints and credential exposure, but convenience is not a 100x societal consequence. Modal’s $300 million-plus annualized revenue and Daytona’s $5 million establish real demand for agent infrastructure. They do not establish machine0’s consequence of success.

The product is thoughtfully assembled: root access, static endpoints, snapshots, credential profiles, NixOS, and machines extending to eight H200 GPUs. The useful comparison is Modal’s 24-hour standard-sandbox limit. But machine0 provides no measured improvement in cost, provisioning time, reliability, or agent productivity. Its VMs run on DigitalOcean while E2B, Daytona, and Modal already offer overlapping isolation, persistence, and snapshots. Modal added full-VM sandboxes in June 2026. This is feature differentiation inside a converging market, not a demonstrated 5–10x technical discontinuity.

The strongest bull case is the founder. Barnaby Malet researched cloud resource allocation, owned infrastructure and security at Upflow, built Director, and then changed direction when agents began running for six to eight hours. That observed pivot shows more learning capacity than a résumé alone. PACIFIC, a publicly identified customer, and a Show HN launch with 95 points and 36 comments provide modest evidence that he can attract technical users. The known-exception analogy is Block: a tiny team used a narrow infrastructure wedge to enter an enormous existing value pool and repeatedly changed the route. The material difference is that machine0 has not shown a proprietary platform expansion, unusual distribution, or economics that improve with scale rather than merely reselling compute.

Capital should now attack the kill risk: can a one-person company retain production agent fleets when funded competitors copy persistent endpoints? The dossier gives no experiment, threshold, cost, or stop condition for answering that question. Supplier concentration compounds it: DigitalOcean hosts every VM, GPU uptime is only 99%, and machine0’s control plane has no guaranteed uptime. Credential injection and API-retrievable SSH keys make security evidence essential, yet none is presented. Pricing from $0.013 per CPU-hour and $0.836 per GPU-hour proves accessibility, not gross margin or an unsubsidized scale advantage.

My 36-month base case is that machine0 pivots from resold “cloud computers” into an agent-fleet control plane and reaches $1–3 million ARR, because Malet’s belief revision is stronger than the current product moat. I pass today. One verifiable condition would flip me: ten production customers each sustaining at least 1,000 agent-hours per month for three consecutive months at 40% or better gross margin, alongside a measured fivefold reduction in operator time versus Modal or a raw VPS. That would turn a competent wrapper into evidence of a platform.
