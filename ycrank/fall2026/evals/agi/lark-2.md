# Lark -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Lark has no supported path in either AGI bucket. Its agents help wholesale distributors reconcile records, draft replenishment orders, and write approved transactions back to the ERP. This is a downstream application of AI. The memo identifies no AI workload whose deployment Lark could unblock by supplying a scarce input. It also describes wholesale buyers already performing reorder reviews manually. Interline’s testimonial supports labor savings in that existing workflow. The memo identifies no buyer that gained access to replenishment planning or review cadence that became economically viable for the first time. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

The strongest commercial case is automating existing purchasing workflows. Customer-specific item, vendor, and approval-rule mapping could create switching costs. Epicor’s native automation and competing purchasing tools limit confidence that Lark could sustain a price premium. Interline’s reported use supports customer demand, while displayed logos leave payment and repeat use unverified. Lark reportedly delivered Interline’s first recommendations in under a month, and Jason Wang’s prior warehouse-agent work supports execution. Reliable transaction handling across deployments remains unverified. Lark says most setups take one to two months. The memo gives no pricing or delivery-cost data to assess the contribution from the next customer, so scale economics stays at the default. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Lark could make frequent SKU-level replenishment reviews affordable for distributors that previously reviewed parts of their catalog infrequently because of labor cost. That is a scenario assumption. Interline reported a drop from 70 to 10 team hours per week and a 20% reduction in inventory value across more than 60,000 items. Lark separately described the time savings as 60 hours per month. Cross-system reconciliation and controlled ERP write-back give buyers a reason to use a configured product even with access to comparable models. The reported deployment leaves the change in review coverage or frequency and the fully loaded cost per successful review unresolved. The scenario therefore leaves both path scores at zero and the action at Pass. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a distributor sustain paid replenishment reviews at a cadence it previously ruled out on cost grounds, using Lark at an all-in cost its existing ERP workflow cannot match? A paid pilot that documents the prior cadence, shows sustained use at the new cadence, and measures cost per correctly completed review including setup, approvals, and error correction would move the action to Reach out. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)
