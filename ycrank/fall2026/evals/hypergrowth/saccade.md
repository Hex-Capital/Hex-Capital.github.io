# Saccade -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Saccade’s strongest path is to distribute Acuity through longevity clinics and use the same headset and assessment workflow to serve patients who want to track cognitive changes before symptoms. The memo documents a working headset attachment, and Saccade reports testing adults at San Francisco clinics and soliciting referrals to U.S. longevity practices. The headset exam could transfer across clinics. The full service also requires blood draws, lab processing, and physician consultations. Recreating that delivery setup abroad is likely to take substantial local work. Clinic referrals and direct appointment inquiries give buyers two ways in, with staffed visits still required for first value. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

Acuity lists $897 per test on a quarterly billing cadence. Four completed, paid tests would yield $3,588 per continuing patient per year, assuming the listed price holds. Follow-up testing gives patients a reason to return, while the listed bundle offers no clear way for retained patients to spend more. The two-person team reported clinic testing in October, but the memo establishes neither realized Acuity sales nor pre-revenue status. Saccade’s stated research enrollment figure of 2,000 lacks a completed-enrollment count, and research participation is free. The commercial growth rate is unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

At the listed price, contribution per completed test starts at $897 and subtracts attributable acquisition and scheduling, on-site staff time, blood collection and lab processing, physician time, support, and headset costs. If partner clinics can group appointments and share a headset across patients, they could reduce staff downtime and equipment cost per test. The memo gives no costs or utilization figures to establish whether that scenario yields positive contribution. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Even assuming strong initial fit, each quarterly test brings another staffed visit, blood draw, and consultation. Saccade’s terms say the investigational assessment lacks validation for individual risk prediction, which weakens the case for repeat testing if results have limited clinical use. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo does not establish that partner clinics would need bespoke implementation in each market or that the required clinical work makes expansion economically infeasible. The absence of paid uptake and partner delivery evidence keeps confidence low. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can partner clinics in a second country deliver the same Acuity bundle with limited adaptation and positive contribution per paid visit? A rollout run by clinic staff that shows rapid growth in paid visits, quarterly rebooking, and positive contribution after local delivery costs would support rescoring for Reach out. A rollout that exposes unavoidable bespoke work or persistently uneconomic delivery would move the action to Pass.
