# Anywhere -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A social-map or location-sharing platform could pay for an active network of friends who repeatedly save, share, and revisit places, along with the personal map histories that make those interactions useful. Snap’s acquisition of Zenly establishes that a buyer has paid to own an adjacent product. For Anywhere, ownership would make economic sense if bringing its network into the buyer’s product improved retention more than building similar features or connecting to Anywhere through an integration. That benefit remains an inference. (The Idea, Defensibility, Founders & Team)

A seed-stage milestone is 90-day retention in launch cohorts that repeatedly share places with friends and reuse agent-added pins. The iPhone listing shows Anywhere has shipped, and YC reported thousands of first-week signups. The listing’s 2.8/5 from nine ratings is a small, unfavorable signal. Signups give the team a cohort to measure, and Delaroche’s prior mapping work supports its ability to run the test. The memo leaves the cost, runway, retention, and friend-network density open. Anywhere’s terms identify Apple and Google map and place dependencies, and Delaroche says finer agent permissions are planned. A buyer would still need to establish which technology, provider rights, and permissions governing user data could carry over in a sale. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

**Illustrative proceeds.** With a $500,000 check at $10 million post-money, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons, a 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years. That takes $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. Assuming $5 million of net debt, no fees or senior claims, and simple pro rata distribution before investor taxes, a $25 million enterprise-value sale would return $600,000 (1.2×), while a $100 million sale would return $2.85 million (5.7×). The memo leaves Anywhere’s actual shareholder terms and realizable sale price unestablished. Zenly supports the buyer rationale, while pricing Anywhere would require evidence of its own retained network and value to a buyer. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction)

If launch growth plateaus, buyer interest fails to emerge, public-market scale remains out of reach, and Anywhere reinvests all available cash, eight years of private operation would realize $0 and leave the shares held. The app is free, and the memo supplies no cash-flow basis for a sponsor buyout, dividend, or buyback, no evidence of an eligible secondary, and no evidence of IPO readiness. Any wind-down recovery would come from assets left after obligations and preferences, which the memo does not quantify. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Google already offers conversational place discovery, Bump already supports shared visits and friend location, and standard read/write connectors let a platform integrate an external tool. Those options give a buyer credible ways to reproduce much of the experience or partner with Anywhere. The ownership case depends on a retained friend network that a buyer would struggle to build and could benefit from acquiring. The evidence supports Watch while that advantage is unmeasured. A structural transfer block or an inadequate price ceiling remains unestablished, so the Pass gate does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would owning Anywhere’s retained friend network improve a social-map buyer’s engagement enough to justify a price that clears the holder’s return hurdle? Move from Watch to Reach out if 90-day cohorts show sustained reciprocal sharing and reuse of agent-added pins, and a buyer-specific analysis quantifies enough incremental engagement value over building or integrating to support about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership.
