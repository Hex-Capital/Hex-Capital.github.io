# Orca Aerospace -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with its initial fleet operator, Orca addresses a recurring job that plausibly travels across markets: responding to faults while spacecraft are out of contact. Its described onboard planner and command checks give it a reusable core. Transfer to other operators depends on compatible avionics, available memory and power, and how much mission-specific validation is required. Orca’s proposed sales motion starts with direct briefings and requires hands-on flight-software integration and testing. The planned Reflex integration offers a route to reuse across spacecraft on one stack. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

The memo records a signed Orbital letter of intent worth up to $20 million and a Lyra concept study. These show early buyer engagement. The five-person team emerged from stealth in September 2026, while the timing of paid availability, realized revenue, and pre-revenue status are unknown. The proposed per-satellite license gives Orca a way to earn more from Orbital as its fleet grows, assuming the spacecraft deploy and carry the software. License cadence, renewals, and paid expansion remain unverified. (*Market & Traction*, *The Idea*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

In a compatible-stack scenario, the integration fee could cover some upfront engineering and testing, while each added satellite contributes its license fee less incremental validation, telemetry review, and support. Reusing the integration could reduce the engineering cost per satellite. Fee amounts, labor requirements, and achievable fleet volume are unknown, so positive contribution remains a hypothesis. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Even with strong initial fit, each new operator could bring enough avionics, mission limits, and command-authority requirements to require substantial engineering and qualification. Orbital’s planned reuse is within one fleet, and its license opportunity depends on that fleet deploying. The structural gate stays open because a compatible-stack rollout offers a plausible way to spread the upfront work, and the memo leaves the amount and cost of unavoidable work for the next buyer unresolved. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a second operator go live in paid production on an already integrated flight-software stack with bounded qualification work and positive incremental contribution? Measure integration and test hours, ongoing support, and realized fees on that rollout. Move to Pass if it shows that unavoidable bespoke work consumes contribution at realistic fleet volumes. Bounded work and positive contribution would strengthen Watch and justify Reach out if the resulting score reaches 65 with at least Medium confidence.
