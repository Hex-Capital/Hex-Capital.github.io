# Kailash Labs -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales with account expansion.** Kailash targets manufacturers with recurring staffing gaps at existing production stations. Start with a packing station. If it meets the plant’s throughput and uptime requirements, the same cell design and commissioning process could win similar lines at that plant and at other manufacturers. Managed robotics offerings in India and the U.S. make the buyer problem plausible across markets. Portability would depend on reusing the core cell while adapting conveyor interfaces, safety requirements, and field support. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

The memo records a public station demo and open pilot bookings. Kailash advertises installation in days, a seven-day keep-or-return pilot with no customer capital expenditure, and a sales process that starts with a CEO discovery call and an on-site line walk. Paid conversion, recurring production use, and station expansion remain unverified. The demo makes the mechanism testable at this stage. A paid rollout would show whether it works in a buyer’s production line. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Each new station could require fresh sales effort, a site visit, hardware, safety integration, part-specific commissioning, and on-call engineering. A returned pilot could add removal and redeployment costs. If Kailash finances the equipment, each rollout also ties up capital. Reusing the cell design and setup data could reduce engineering effort, but that saving remains unverified across sites. The memo establishes these exposures while leaving the service rate and actual rollout costs unknown. The structural Pass gate therefore remains untriggered. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)
