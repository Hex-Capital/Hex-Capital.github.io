# Sidekick -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I start with three blanks: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. YC participation and named partner backing do not answer them. Without terms, I cannot tell whether I am buying an unfashionable industrial workflow cheaply or paying Demo Day pricing for an AI interface incumbents can reproduce. That is a financing gate, not a judgment on the product.

The real signal is not Sidekick’s 644 LinkedIn followers. It is deployment across 20-plus plants and 600-plus frontline workers through a channel they already use. SMS may eliminate the account creation, app installation, and training friction that weakens factory software. Each question, escalation, work order, and verified resolution can also deepen a facility-specific operating history. This resembles a proprietary workflow beginning to form, but the dossier does not establish that it caused a purchase, retained usage, or measurable reductions in downtime. Paid plans in the terms are not paid customers.

The strongest bull case is that these deployments already contain the evidence the public dossier omits. One founder has reached hundreds of workers in operational environments where integrations, security review, and behavior change are difficult. If those 20 plants are paying and expanding, Sidekick could qualify for the same kind of AI exception as Ando: a functioning enterprise workflow with measured conversion and retention, rather than a model-dependent story. The material difference is that Sidekick discloses none of those economics or outcome measurements, while its terms explicitly require users to verify potentially erroneous answers.

The missing venture moment matters just as much. The product promises document retrieval, work-order routing, escalation, plant-history capture, and PLC/SCADA synchronization. Those are several jobs, not one finite experiment. The dossier gives no use-of-funds plan, dated milestone, or pass/fail metric showing what scarce capital will prove. I would want a narrow card flip: for example, deploy in ten comparable plants by a fixed date and demonstrate a specified reduction in downtime with a defined expansion rate. “Hire, integrate, and grow” would merely finance another SaaS company toward another round.

Justin So has credible access: third-generation manufacturing family, summers on the EDS Manufacturing floor, computer-science training, and finance experience at Arm. He understands both technical and customer language better than a generic AI founder. But the record does not yet show a shipped industrial system, prior deployment, or operating result on both sides of the bridge. The solo-founder structure at least avoids the equal-founder accountability problem.

Delete the Series A and the case becomes difficult to underwrite. Pricing, revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, and customer acquisition economics are all undisclosed, while MaintainX already owns extensive maintenance data and can add SMS after Autodesk’s approximately $3.6 billion acquisition. My 36-month base case is that Autodesk acquires Sidekick for under $100 million before it reaches $5 million ARR, making this a valuable interface but not necessarily a venture-scale independent system. One verifiable fact flips me: evidence that at least ten of the disclosed plants are paying, retained, and expanding usage while the company can reach break-even on the current round. Until then, I pass.
