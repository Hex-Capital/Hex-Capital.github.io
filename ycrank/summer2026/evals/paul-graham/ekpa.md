# Ekpa -- Paul Graham Evaluation

I begin with the missing obstacle. Ekpa has shipped brokerage imports, portfolio analysis, alerts, discovery, and backtesting, but the dossier never identifies what broke, what either founder did next, or how quickly that action changed the product. Founder-led onboarding is a distribution method, not evidence of resourcefulness. The founders may be resourceful. I cannot infer it from MIT, coursework, or a polished product surface.

The strongest evidence is the artifact itself. A two-person team has connected read-only brokerage positions through SnapTrade, supported CSV imports, and run a reported 2,400 analyses across more than $3.4 million in assets. This is meaningfully better than a diagram. It shows they began before permission. But the claimed return above 1.5% in one week has no published methodology or independent verification, and assets connected are not assets earned, retained, or paid for. The technical proof is real but only partly measured.

Who wants the crude version now? “Retail investors with jobs and families” is a demographic, not a narrow well. The 2,400 analyses imply some use, but the dossier gives no user count, cohort, repeat rate, payment, or example of a customer forcing Ekpa into an unplanned workflow. Basic remains free, while the paid Strategy Lab has neither a disclosed price nor reported revenue. This could be ten obsessed investors running hundreds of analyses, which would be excellent, or hundreds of curious users visiting once. The aggregate hides the fact I need.

The bull case stings. These are young technical founders who have already put a functioning research agent in front of real portfolios, and $3.4 million is enough connected capital for harmful errors to matter. Anant previously built agentic drug-discovery pipelines; Yuga combines computing, economics, and investment experience. Cheap inference also makes continuous processing of filings, news, and portfolio context newly practical. If the 2,400 analyses came from repeated voluntary use, this may be a programmable investing primitive whose earliest users are already pulling it into shape.

But Ekpa currently sits between products with stronger natural endpoints. PortfolioPilot provides regulated advice, Composer builds and executes strategies, and Robinhood can place overlapping analysis inside the brokerage itself. Ekpa neither owns execution nor has a documented proprietary substrate. Its cross-broker aggregation is useful, but SnapTrade supplies a critical connector, while the regulatory schlep remains a classification risk rather than a founder-conquered advantage. There is also no dated route change showing that the team rejected a hypothesis after observing users. My 36-month base case is therefore a pivot from consumer portfolio research to a B2B research-and-alerting product for independent advisers, rather than a durable standalone consumer subscription.

I would pass today. The operational gate fails because there is no named-blocker workaround, and the demand evidence stops just short of showing a narrow group pulling the product back. The verifiable flip condition is a cohort of at least 25 paying investors using Ekpa weekly for 12 consecutive weeks after the founders document and ship around one specific brokerage or regulatory blocker. That would reveal both the well and the running back.
