# Shepherd -- Elad Gil Evaluation

**Shepherd’s MCP-based architecture makes the single-miracle test its strongest dimension, while Shepherd’s zero disclosed revenue, customer, or repeat-usage data makes revenue velocity its weakest.** Shepherd is therefore an technically plausible workflow layer whose product could work without new science, but not yet one that is visibly *just working*. The model is permission-aware organizational memory consumed through Slack, CLI, API, and MCP; the missing evidence is whether anyone buys that memory rather than treating it as an interesting demo.

**Color Genomics** is the high rung for single-miracle architecture: workflow and data infrastructure built on commodity sequencing, not a wager on new chemistry. Shepherd shares that mechanism because Shepherd assumes capable models, embeddings, connectors, and MCP already exist; the remaining work is product execution, security, and distribution. Shepherd diverges from Color because its ordinary production controls do not guarantee that employees can never access plaintext, connector-policy changes can interrupt ingestion, and retrieval can be stale or incorrect. Those are three execution surfaces, but they are not three independent miracles. Shepherd passes this test more cleanly than most pre-seed AI infrastructure deals.

**Harvey-clone pitches with no growth curve** are the low rung for revenue velocity: consensus category, plausible product, nothing demonstrating that demand is pulling the product out of the founder’s hands. That failure mechanism appears directly in Shepherd. Shepherd discloses early access, 381 LinkedIn followers, and a founder-demo CTA, but no revenue, signed LOIs, named customers, repeat usage, or deployment expansion. At pre-seed I do not require substantial ARR; I do require one informative buying or usage signal. For an AI company, silence on the curve remains a negative signal even after stage calibration.

**MCP’s growth from its November 2024 release to more than 10,000 public servers by December 2025** is a real recent catalyst, and over the next 18 months it could make a shared organizational-memory endpoint more valuable each time another agent becomes capable of consuming it. Shepherd benefits if multi-agent adoption turns fragmented context into a newly acute systems problem. Shepherd loses that benefit if MCP merely standardizes access for Glean, Dust, Notion, and Guru, because a protocol that lowers integration costs for Shepherd also lowers them for incumbents. This is a genuine re-opening, but the dossier has not shown that decision lineage across hardware, firmware, research, and code is the newly opened segment rather than positioning inside a market growing only 8.9% annually.

**Philip Meng’s four named ML-conference publications, Ishan Ramrakhiani’s DRIFT repository with four stars, and Elijah Renner’s medical-NLP research** establish technical ability but not the Perplexity shipping pattern. Shepherd would move up a full tier on founder execution if Philip, Ishan, or Elijah could show that Shepherd shipped new connectors or decision-tracing workflows weekly in response to user requests, with those users returning. Shepherd currently offers no public artifact history, enterprise-search operating experience, or documented user-feedback loop. Research output is evidence of capability; it is not evidence that Shepherd’s founders ship independently between conversations.

**Stripe** became an index on e-commerce because every new internet business eventually needed payment rails. Shepherd could become an index on enterprise agents if every Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, and internal agent must query the same permission-aware decision graph before acting. Shepherd is not in that position today: Glean already exceeds $100M ARR, Dust sells configurable assistants over internal data, and Notion embeds search within a workspace it already controls. Shepherd must first win share against those distribution owners, so the cross-domain technical-memory wedge is promising but does not yet confer index economics.

**Glean’s greater than $100M ARR and Shepherd’s absence of disclosed customers** point to a falsifiable 36-month forecast: Shepherd will pivot from broad enterprise memory into a narrower engineering decision-lineage product and reach $1M-$3M ARR, but will not establish a standalone enterprise-search category. Shepherd’s mechanism is strongest where schematics, firmware, code, and operating decisions must remain traceable across tools; the broad knowledge layer is already crowded. Shepherd can build a useful business in that narrower workflow, but the current evidence does not support skating for the puck toward a category-defining independent platform.

**Shepherd’s strongest bull case is the Mistral exception:** a young technical group enters immediately after a platform catalyst, treats ecosystem coupling as a feature, and rides adoption faster than incumbent product cycles can respond. MCP adoption, three technically credible founders, early access, and agent-facing CLI/API surfaces make that case sting. Shepherd still lacks what made the exception operative: Mistral’s founders had shipped Llama, while Shepherd’s founders have no documented comparable product, named customer, or usage curve. I would flip to Invest if Shepherd shows either $25K+ ARR growing above 20% month over month or three named technical organizations expanding repeat usage across at least two source domains.
