# GodHands -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest path. GodHands says it captures work in administrator-approved desktop apps and turns it into source-linked playbooks for employees and agents. The seed-stage hypothesis is that capturing an exception once could make it economical to document and handle infrequent escalations that support teams currently leave to experienced staff. Users still approve proposed changes or messages. The labor comparison therefore needs to count capture setup, review, retries, and manual exceptions per correctly resolved case at equivalent quality. The memo provides no such comparison. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

GodHands says it retains customer-specific playbooks after raw recordings expire. An accumulated record of exceptions could give buyers a reason to stay if recreating it elsewhere takes material work. That switching cost is an inference. Buyer preference under model parity and network effects remain unproven. Company-published testimonials report that one support team documented its escalation process in two weeks and that a replacement controller closed the books on time by month two. Those are early use signals, with payment, repeat use, and demand from previously unserved teams still unknown. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

The Fall 2026 seed stage and San Francisco location fit the mandate. The October launch and the founders’ prior work together in quantitative research and low-latency systems support early execution. Reliable deployment in customer environments remains unverified. The quoted subscription gives GodHands a pricing mechanism. Scale would depend on contribution from each added account after setup, security support, inference, and ongoing support. The memo supplies no measured inputs for that bridge. (*Key Facts*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *The Idea*)

### Countercase

GodHands may improve documentation for teams already paying to handle these workflows, while approvals and exception handling absorb the labor savings needed to open a new market. Scribe already offers background capture and agent access, and Skan AI already sells workflow discovery and execution. With comparable models and ample capital, a rival could plausibly reproduce the outcome if customers can rebuild or transfer their playbooks cheaply. The overlap leaves the Path B outreach gate unmet. Actual substitution and failed delivery economics remain unproven, so the override does not trigger. The memo identifies no separately controlled bottleneck for Path A. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent support teams pay for and repeatedly use GodHands on low-volume escalations they previously handled ad hoc, even when a competing workflow tool has comparable model capability? A paid comparative pilot should measure human time per correctly resolved case against the manual baseline, including setup, approvals, retries, and exceptions. It should also track which product teams choose at viable pricing after trying both and what drives that choice. Repeat paid use with material labor savings and preference tied to retained workflow knowledge could move the action to Reach out if the updated score clears the outreach threshold with at least medium confidence. A representative trial showing either no viable new use or ready substitution would move the action to Pass.
