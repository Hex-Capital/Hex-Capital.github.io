# Amorphic Labs -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Amorphic Labs automates hours of sales-engineering labor, but the dossier never converts those hours into a consequential cost pool or an order-of-magnitude outcome. A $6.0 billion sales-enablement market growing to $21.2 billion is a market statistic, not a consequence-of-success case. The beneficiary is a software sales team producing more tailored demos. Useful, yes. Societal or infrastructural leverage, no. This is workflow compression inside an existing sales process, not a 100x change in who can access an essential service.

The technical wedge is more substantive than a generic video wrapper. The agent researches prospects, seeds application data, operates the actual product, records a workflow, edits it, and generates narration. That is meaningfully different from Consensus routing prerecorded segments or Walnut and Reprise customizing captured environments. But Amorphic supplies no measured comparison: no production-time reduction, task-success rate, human-review burden, or evidence that its agent can operate unfamiliar applications reliably. A named difference is not yet a 5–10x discontinuity, especially when well-funded competitors can add the same computer-use primitives.

The strongest bull case is the founders. Frank Li was an OpenAI MTS and project lead with NVIDIA ML-infrastructure experience; Dylan Kelly has quantitative and AI-research credentials, including a NeurIPS 2025 benchmark paper. They have worked together for nearly a decade. This is the right raw material for turning fragile browser automation into a platform, and pre-seed silence on customers is not disqualifying. But credentials do not demonstrate fast belief revision, and the dossier shows neither a difficult complementary recruit nor an observed decision changed after contrary evidence. I underwrite learning behavior, not résumé density.

The largest kill risk is agent reliability under real customer conditions. The company’s own terms concede that actions may be inaccurate or incomplete, while the product handles cookies, session state, integration tokens, passwords, screenshots, and prospect data. Bearer-link sharing adds another failure surface. Yet there is no disclosed experiment, pass/fail threshold, error budget, or stop condition. Capital spent selling subscriptions before proving reliable execution across heterogeneous applications would fund scale before removing the existential risk.

Economics are equally unproven. Subscriptions and metered usage are contemplated, but there is no price, inference-and-browser cost, review-labor requirement, utilization model, or comparison with sales-engineer labor. Software avoids the physical subsidy problem, but economic gravity still applies: if every video requires exception handling and vendor review, the apparent automation becomes a services business hiding behind an agent.

My expectation is that by August 2029 Amorphic Labs will have pivoted from personalized demo videos into broader AI sales-engineer workflow automation and will remain below $3 million ARR. The founders may build a competent company, but the current evidence supports a feature-shaped wedge in a crowded category, not an option on an extreme outcome. I pass with my own money. I would reverse that decision if ten design partners independently demonstrated a sustained 10x reduction in labor per accepted personalized demo with a material-error rate below 1% over 90 days.
