# NebuSec -- Elad Gil Evaluation

AI vulnerability discovery increases both the volume of attacks and enterprises’ need for continuous defense. NebuSec’s opportunity is to make finding and fixing exploitable bugs cheaper than leaving researchers to investigate scanner output. The controlling variable is confirmed, remediated vulnerabilities per dollar of compute and human investigation. Its September 2026 benchmark—88% maximum coverage across 76 known vulnerabilities, versus 80% for Codex Security and 74% for Claude Security—provides a measurable detection claim. But a vendor-run test on known bugs establishes only part of that proposition. It does not establish the cost of producing a useful result on an unfamiliar customer repository.

The product has more substance than a security chatbot. NebuSec documents threat modeling, repository and pull-request scanning, finding verification, and patch generation. Those steps could form a recurring operational loop in which better models improve completion rates while the company retains the customer integration. That is the strongest part of the case. The limitation is consequential: dynamic exploit verification is disabled by default. Finding something suspicious and taking responsibility for a verified repair are different levels of workflow ownership. Cloud and supply-chain products marked “coming soon” receive no credit toward the latter.

[PingCAP’s October 2026 confirmation](https://www.pingcap.com/trust-hub/security/ceo-note-october-2026/) of a completed investigation is meaningful customer evidence. An enterprise trusted this team with an actual security incident. It validates demand for its expertise, although it does not establish recurring demand for its software. The approximately 1.7K CyberMeowfia stars demonstrate research reach rather than purchasing behavior. Fixed scan quotes and subscription tiers provide a commercial mechanism, but the dossier supplies no measured reduction in investigation cost, delivery time, or staffing. I therefore give the economic step-change criterion its absent-evidence default; the benchmark belongs primarily to detection performance.

The strongest bull case is that scarce vulnerability researchers automate their own work before broader platforms learn how. The founders’ exploit development, program-analysis research, automated-repair experience, and Zellic background make that plausible. Tan’s departure from doctoral studies is also concrete commitment, rather than motivation inferred from impressive employers. This fits my pattern of backing technical teams across an emerging technology shift. However, the research-stage exception requires more than domain expertise: the dossier does not establish frontier research or a capital path that would justify overriding weak commercial evidence. Meanwhile, Semgrep and Aikido already combine overlapping capabilities. Better models could improve NebuSec’s product while improving those competitors just as quickly.

I would hold off on a personal check. The concern is that NebuSec becomes a respected investigation practice whose software assists its researchers, while established platforms capture the recurring budget. The dossier does not establish the economics needed to dismiss that outcome, and the inferred future dataset deserves no moat credit without access rights or reproduction costs. One result would change my decision: an independently documented 90-day production comparison at PingCAP showing at least twice as many confirmed, remediated vulnerabilities per dollar of total compute and researcher cost as its existing process. That would connect the technical claim to the market variable that matters.
