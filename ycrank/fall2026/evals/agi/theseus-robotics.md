# Theseus Robotics -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** Theseus could expand overnight shelf-restocking capacity in existing grocery aisles. The memo reports two live robots across two stores and describes launch footage from Zürich. Theseus says grocers struggle to staff overnight shifts and reports peak rates of 400 units per hour restocked and 40 picked. The footage supports seed-stage feasibility. The staffing constraint and sustained throughput at comparable shelf quality remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Even with cheap, equally capable models, grocers would still need someone to execute the work on site. Theseus trains on its own demonstrations and can collect remote-operator corrections. Those corrections could give it an operating advantage if they reduce interventions across stores. The two placements show grocers made aisles available for testing. Payment and repeat orders are undisclosed, and the company’s plan for up to 10 more robots is pipeline. The founders bring learning-based control, hardware supply-chain experience, and prior delivery of a live pick-and-place deployment. Theseus is based in Switzerland and is soliciting U.S. pilots, so U.S. demand remains prospective. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Theseus Robotics, The Idea)

**Scale scenario.** If a robot earns a monthly fee and can pick orders during the day as well as restock at night, the added utilization could spread hardware and deployment costs over more work. Each additional robot’s fee would need to cover hardware, installation, maintenance, and remote staffing. The memo gives no fee amount, sustained utilization, or intervention rate to test that margin. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

With abundant capital and comparable models, another integrator could buy similar hardware. HIVE already pilots overlapping overnight replenishment, and the memo identifies no structural barrier or measured improvement across stores. Theseus could face comparable substitutes while paying for enough remote intervention to erode the labor savings. The memo leaves equivalent substitution and loss of value capture unproven, so the Path A override does not trigger. Path B remains unproven because the memo shows no previously uneconomic transaction made viable by measured labor savings. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
