# Kanopy -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Kanopy has evidence of customer access, but I cannot yet underwrite the check. The reported $200,000 Antler financing tells me neither the proposed post-money nor how much competition there is for this round. YC participation does not establish an auction; a small disclosed financing does not establish cheap equity. Those are missing terms. Equally missing is the specific experiment this capital buys: no dated milestone, pass/fail metric, or explanation of why success changes the business’s value.

The founders make this worth examining. Yann’s utility advisory experience and Nicolas’s computer-vision work at ShapeScale put relevant knowledge on opposite sides of a difficult bridge. Kanopy’s reported 3,093 mapped miles give that pairing substance beyond school names and job titles. This fits my unstated preference for software reorganizing neglected physical operations. The limitation is that utility consulting does not establish ownership of inspection budgets, and the dossier supplies no prior scaled deployment or exit demonstrating that these founders have already crossed this particular bridge.

The strongest argument against passing is the six utilities and contractors signing LOIs or MSAs covering 270,000 miles. At this stage, getting counterparties to sign something matters. Ordinary-camera reconstruction could remove specialized hardware and processing friction from an existing purchasing workflow. But an MSA can establish the rules for buying without requiring anyone to buy, and scoped mileage is not purchased volume. I see commercial access and documented customer action; I do not yet see owned distribution, exclusive data, or an embedded workflow that reliably produces orders. The 312 company followers add nothing to that judgment.

The economic question sits inside the accuracy claim. Kanopy advertises 2–4-inch accuracy, while its release notes describe pole-association and wire-fitting corrections. Those corrections demonstrate active development; they do not establish unacceptable performance. But if each survey needs substantial expert review, avoiding LiDAR may simply move expense from equipment into labor. Public reconstruction models increase the pressure to capture value through reliable work records and customer workflow, while Schneider’s proposed AiDASH acquisition could strengthen an overlapping supplier’s distribution. No disclosed pricing, review cost, margin, burn, or runway lets me determine whether this becomes a business that can fund itself. Missing revenue is ordinary at pre-seed; missing economics still leaves my one-and-done thesis unproven.

I would pass on the evidence presented. I checked the exception case: demonstrated deployment and signed agreements are meaningful, but they do not supply the measured paid conversion and retention that could override missing financing gates. Nor is there a documented prior exit paired with a capital-use plan and unusual security that changes the underwriting. I also see no evidence of a rushed auction, equal-founder paralysis, or an explicit assumption that model errors disappear; the brothers’ relationship proves none of those things. My verifiable flip condition is a proposed sub-$10-million post-money round funding a six-month, customer-contracted test that converts existing counterparties into repeat paid inspections, meets independently checked clearance tolerances, and produces positive contribution margin after human review. That would connect the check to a truth worth financing.
