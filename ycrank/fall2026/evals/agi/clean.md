# Clean -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path B, New Economies, is Clean’s strongest path. Sellers in manufacturing and insurance use contact exports and manual research to find buying windows. Clean says it turns dated records such as permits, shipments, and filings into account timelines with a recommended decision-maker and supporting records. The market-opening hypothesis is that smaller sales teams could continuously monitor accounts they previously skipped because the expected value of an opportunity could not justify repeated manual checks. The November product launch establishes that the team shipped the workflow. The memo contains no direct test of whether it made those accounts economical to serve. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

At seed, the relevant comparison is full cost per verified opportunity identified before supplier selection. The former workflow requires list maintenance, repeated research, and contact verification. Clean could reduce that labor, but the comparison must include data licensing, ingestion, inference, seller review, retries, and time spent on mistimed alerts. The expansion unit is another group of monitored accounts. Its economics depend on how much of the data and verification workflow Clean can reuse and what it costs to extend coverage into another sector. The memo provides no measured costs per verified opportunity. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

Clean reports recurring revenue, and Vivameda’s testimonial reports that replies rose from about 4% to 19% and that it signed $60K in contracts in two weeks. These are early signals of paid demand and customer value, with no evidence tying them to previously excluded accounts or repeat paid use. Under the scenario of cheap, widely available frontier models, buyers could still pay Clean for timely, traceable records that a rival cannot assemble as reliably. Clean reports combining its own datasets with licensed records. Whether that combination produces a sustained advantage remains untested. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Defensibility*)

### Countercase

Clean may improve prospecting economics for teams that already buy sales intelligence while leaving previously skipped accounts uneconomic. The aircraft example shows outreach beginning after a supplier had been selected, which illustrates how an apparently relevant signal can still waste seller time. Salesmotion already aggregates operational signals in CRM workflows, and Clean depends partly on licensed data. If an equivalent-model rival can deliver equally timely, verifiable opportunities, the memo gives little reason for buyers to keep paying Clean. The late alert and overlapping competitor features leave both Path B gates open for testing. (*Key Risks*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent sales team renew at a price that covers Clean’s delivery costs to monitor accounts it previously skipped? A paid pilot would compare full cost per verified, pre-selection opportunity against periodic manual research and compare Clean’s results against a credible competitor using equivalent models at the same monitoring cadence. Move to Reach out if the pilot shows that monitoring those accounts is economical and the buyer renews because Clean delivers timely, verifiable opportunities the competitor misses. Move to Pass if a representative comparison shows that full costs keep the accounts uneconomic or that the buyer can switch and retain the core outcome.
