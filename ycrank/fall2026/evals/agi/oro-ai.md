# ORO AI -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

ORO has an early building-blocks case in evaluating AI agents for wholesale purchasing. Short tests can miss the consequences of a buying decision, such as customers cutting orders after repeated shortages. SMB Bench simulates a 90-day buying cycle and compares matched scenarios with and without delayed customer and supplier effects. ORO reports that no model beat the fixed buying rule in every run and that the best model averaged $7,749 in simulated profit versus $10,795 for the scripted expert. That result shows what the benchmark can measure. The investment assumption is that as more teams deploy purchasing agents, they will need operator-calibrated simulations faster than they can build or source them. The memo establishes the workload and a plausible bottleneck, while buyer delays and the time needed to qualify substitutes remain unverified. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The industry-enabled path earns zero. SMB Bench simulates purchasing while other functions follow fixed rules. The memo gives no evidence that ORO delivers purchasing at a cost that makes it viable for a previously excluded buyer or use. (The Idea, Key Risks)

ORO’s gated grader and experience calibrating the simulation with wholesalers support modest value capture. The founders shipped a working benchmark and bring relevant evaluation and distributed-systems experience. The memo records 670 dataset downloads, while ORO reports 859 runs. Neither figure identifies a prospective buyer conducting a defined trial. Customer demand and scale economics stay at their defaults because the memo provides no buyer commitment or evidence of pricing and the cost to calibrate, maintain, and serve another environment. (Defensibility, Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Teams may need tests tailored to their own tools, records, and workflows. The memo identifies Veris as a way to build those environments. ORO’s published benchmark methods and dataset make replication plausible, and the memo establishes no exclusive operator-data rights. If a buyer can qualify an equivalent test on its deployment timeline, the building-blocks case falls to zero and the decision becomes Pass. Substitution remains plausible, which supports Watch while buyer evidence is pending. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
