# OpEra -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** The strongest initial buyer is a logistics operations leader whose planners repeatedly rebuild delivery plans after disruptions. OpEra publicly launched a founder-led offer to deploy at one site. The company describes connecting to existing systems, modeling local constraints, and letting planners rerun the optimizer as conditions change. The founders’ MIT routing project reportedly cut delivery costs by 38% and increased on-time deliveries by 10% on one retailer’s data. That is an early signal of potential buyer value. Paid production use and recurring reliance remain unverified. *(The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks; Key Facts)*

**Scenario assumption:** A paid first-site deployment could expand to more sites if OpEra can reuse its connectors and routing model while configuring local data and rules. Serving the same buyer in another geography would test whether those changes stay within configuration. The memo establishes neither cross-market buyer demand nor a repeatable route from founder introductions to paid activation. It also gives no evidence of retained workloads, paid expansion, or deployment cost. *(The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks; Key Facts)*

### Countercase

OpEra could produce meaningful savings while scaling like an optimization consultancy. Each new site could require founder sales time, custom data mapping, fresh constraint modeling and validation, and ongoing support and compute. The reported sugar-producer and services-marketplace results show potential value in different workflows, with no evidence that either reused the retailer routing implementation. Site-specific work makes this a credible risk, but the memo does not establish that costs rise proportionally with volume or that buyers in other markets cannot use the product. The Pass gate remains unmet. *(The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)*

### Decisive diligence question

Can OpEra bring a second independent logistics buyer in another geography live on the same paid routing workflow with materially less implementation effort? A buyer-funded deployment with recurring planner use, documented connector and model reuse, and delivery costs measured against the price would move the action to **Reach out**. A rollout that shows proportional custom work with no supported path to reducing it would move the action to **Pass**.
