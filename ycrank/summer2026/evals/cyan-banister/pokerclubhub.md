# PokerClubHub -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. Private poker clubs might operate across borders with programmable money, automated compliance, and instant settlement instead of trusting a local agent with a spreadsheet and a wallet. But PokerClubHub has not mapped that future backward. The dossier gives me an April 2026 Anjouan license, stablecoins on Ethereum and Solana, and Fireblocks custody—ingredients available now—not the intervening changes that make this a much larger system six years from now. The license is an artifact, but without the causal map it is evidence of execution, not evidence that the future arrived early.

The broken rule is much clearer. ClubGG, PokerBROS, and PPPoker separate play-money software from real-money settlement, pushing players toward club owners or agents who manually exchange crypto for chips. Those intermediaries control withdrawals and create delay and loss risk. PokerClubHub moves custody and settlement into a licensed operator while paying club owners affiliate fees. That repairs a real operating wound, but it does not fully return agency to players: control moves from an informal agent to PokerClubHub and its single custody integration. I also cannot see the rent—withdrawal times, loss rates, fees, chargebacks, or labor hours—so I cannot yet measure how painful the leak is.

There is real technical work here. The active license ALSI-202604005-FI1, BMM-certified RNG, Fireblocks integration, and browser product are more substantial than a rendering. The bull case stings because licensing, custody, and gaming compliance are precisely the concealed operations an incumbent cannot reproduce over a weekend. If club owners bring their own players, PokerClubHub could acquire fragmented pools without paying retail customer-acquisition costs. Yet 4,000 launch-post views, 133 forum-thread views, and one reply show attention rather than costly behavior. There are no disclosed clubs, deposits, repeat withdrawals, transaction volumes, or revenue.

The company also sits awkwardly in the stack. It could have become the licensed settlement and compliance layer beneath many poker communities, but today it is another private-club destination competing with products that already have distribution—PokerBROS alone has more than 500,000 Google Play downloads. PokerClubHub controls a license and its operating workflow, but not the underlying custody infrastructure, payment rails, or a demonstrated proprietary compliance asset. Fireblocks is doing the deepest custody work, and no evidence shows that multiple downstream products depend on PokerClubHub.

The hardest gate is the team. YC lists zero team members; no founder biography, operating history, poker obsession, regulatory fluency, or accountable leader is publicly verifiable. The ivan@pokerclubhub.com address tells me a person exists, not whether Ivan built through years of this problem or recently assembled a fashionable crypto-gaming stack. In a business holding player funds across jurisdictions, anonymous leadership is not ordinary missing biography. It prevents me from testing both founder obsession and the governance chain responsible when a withdrawal fails.

I pass. My 36-month expectation is that PokerClubHub either closes or pivots into white-label settlement and compliance software for existing club platforms, remaining below $1 million ARR, because its strongest asset is operational plumbing while its weakest evidence is founder-led distribution and user behavior. The one condition that flips me into diligence is a six-month transaction report showing at least ten named independent clubs collectively processing $1 million per month, with repeat withdrawals and no unresolved custody incidents. That would prove the license is producing trusted behavior rather than merely decorating a working website.
