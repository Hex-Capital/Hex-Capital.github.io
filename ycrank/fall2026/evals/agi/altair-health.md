# Altair Health -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Altair could make managed billing economical for small independent practices that still handle billing manually because outsourcing costs too much. That buyer segment is a scenario assumption. The memo describes practices using spreadsheets and phone calls to reconcile eligibility, authorizations, claims, and denials. Altair says it checks proposed claim actions against formalized payer policies, works within the practice’s existing systems, and assigns exceptions to in-house billers. The strongest field signal is the CEO’s report that an Arkansas behavioral-health clinic’s billing team used the earlier copilot to precheck hundreds of claims. He attributed millions of dollars in avoided receivables delays to those checks. The report provides an early signal for prechecking, while labor savings, payment for the managed service, and repeat demand remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The team launched the copilot and brings enterprise software sales and clinical ML experience. For the managed service, the expansion unit is one practice. The cost test compares staff time and full delivery cost per successfully reimbursed claim under the practice’s former workflow and Altair’s service, accounting for onboarding, policy maintenance, human review, retries, exceptions, and compliance. Altair’s realized fee must cover those costs at the practice’s actual claim volume and exception mix. The directory listings leave both the full-service fee and contribution unknown. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The strongest opposing case is that Altair improves an existing billing workflow while the hard claims still require enough human labor to keep managed billing out of reach for smaller practices. Under model parity, a practice might get comparable rule checks from an incumbent. Altair’s potential advantage is taking responsibility for resolving exceptions within the practice’s existing systems. Its reported tracking of 3,400+ payers and reuse of prior resolutions could help its billers, but the memo contains no comparison showing lower exception costs or a buyer choosing Altair over an equivalent service. Tebra already bundles billing for independent practices, and Candid Health markets configurable rules and agents. Neither required condition has been tested directly, so Watch is warranted. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent practice that previously handled billing manually because managed service was unaffordable renew Altair at a fee that covers its full delivery cost after comparing it with a service using equivalent models? A paid renewal would move the action to Reach out if a representative claim cycle shows lower labor and full cost per successfully reimbursed claim than the former workflow, and the practice chooses Altair for how it handles exceptions. A representative trial showing that exception labor erases the savings or that the practice can switch and retain the same outcome would close Path B and move the action to Pass.
