# OpenScaffold -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

OpenScaffold organizes customer requirements from calls, emails, and tickets, briefs coding agents through MCP, and checks their work against customer rules. That workflow supports AI-assisted implementation, with no memo evidence that scarce deployment context constrains AI workloads or that substitute tooling would be slow to supply. The founders describe engineers spending time reconstructing requirements in existing deployments. The memo identifies no previously excluded buyer or use and gives no evidence that the workflow lowers the fully loaded cost of a successful deployment enough to make one viable. Both AGI paths score zero, which triggers Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Faraday’s CEO publicly identified himself as a customer, and the founder reported testing the product on an unnamed pilot partner’s calls. These are early demand signals, with payment and repeat use unverified. The public demo and the founders’ prior deployment work support seed-stage execution credit. The founder also reported that the product failed to distinguish customer requests from deployment-team requests 82% of the time in one pilot test. The memo gives no cost comparison that accounts for the human review and rework those errors could require, so scale economics stays at the default. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Approved playbooks and deployment history could create switching costs if teams reuse them. The memo reports no evidence of that reuse or retention, and open MCP integrations and overlapping features in Harness Cosmos give buyers a credible route to substitute much of the workflow. The public launch, named customer signal, and reported pilot result support medium evidence confidence, with limited visibility into sustained commercial use. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

If manual scoping and review make smaller implementations uneconomic, source-cited plans and reusable, human-approved playbooks could let a deployment team profitably serve those accounts. Under model parity, buyers could still value a maintained record of approved customer requirements and checks against it. That scenario assumes substantial net labor savings after review and rework. The pilot’s attribution problem and the lack of evidence from a previously excluded account leave the Pass gate in place. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
