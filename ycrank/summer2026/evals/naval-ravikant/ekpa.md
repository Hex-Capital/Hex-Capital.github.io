# Ekpa -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

$3.4M in reported “managed assets” makes Ekpa a code-leveraged research product without a network underneath it. That pairing is Ekpa’s signature: software can replicate portfolio analysis cheaply across 38 brokerages, but one investor’s use does not make the next investor’s product better. Code without compounding is useful. Code with compounding becomes an empire.

**Twitter** is the high rung for permissionless leverage: every user created media, distributed the product, and increased its value at near-zero marginal cost. Ekpa shares the software economics—free self-service signup and 2,400 automated analyses—but Ekpa diverges where distribution meets permission: SnapTrade gates brokerage connectivity, third-party data supplies the inputs, and founder-led trader outreach supplies demand. Ekpa has code leverage inside the product, but not yet permissionless leverage around the product.

**Perplexity** lacked direct user-added network value, but exceptional retrieval expertise and pre-consensus AI timing earned the exception. Ekpa has the same single-sided structure without the same override: portfolio histories may personalize an individual account, yet Ekpa provides no evidence that the Nth account improves research for the first N−1 accounts. Public, Composer, and PortfolioPilot can reproduce the same linked-account loop. Ekpa is a tool, not yet a network.

Anant Asthana built agentic drug-discovery pipelines, while Yuga Patel combines MIT computer science, economics, investment exposure, and automation engineering. **Stack Overflow** was built by developers whose domain obsession predated the company; Ekpa’s founders show technical intelligence but not comparable non-trainable knowledge in trading, brokerage infrastructure, or investment research. Ekpa has capable builders applying AI to finance. The dossier does not yet show finance obsessives expressing themselves through code.

The $3.64B research-software market, projected to reach $9.2B by 2032, gives Ekpa some room but not effortless pivot-room. Ekpa would move up a tier if its portfolio graph naturally expanded into adviser infrastructure, tax intelligence, execution APIs, or an investor strategy marketplace without requiring licenses and enterprise sales. Today Ekpa’s obvious adjacencies are already occupied by PortfolioPilot’s registered advice, Composer’s execution, and Public’s brokerage bundle. A large adjacent market is not the same as a permissionless path into it.

The 2,400 completed analyses demonstrate that Anant Asthana and Yuga Patel can ship, and Ekpa’s read-only design shows sensible restraint around custody risk. Over 18 months, intelligence and energy can compound through faster models, broader brokerage coverage, and tighter alerts; integrity cannot be inferred from MIT credentials or a short operating history. Ekpa earns a neutral 10-year-partner score: no contested history, promotional ethics claim, or unresolved authority is reported, but no multi-year founder relationship or clean prior venture establishes the Buffett trio either.

The 38 supported brokerages will push Ekpa toward a specific outcome by July 2029: Ekpa will pivot from everyday traders to portfolio-monitoring software for RIAs or wealth managers and reach roughly $1M–$3M ARR, rather than becoming a scaled consumer investing network. Ekpa’s software is replicable, but consumer distribution lacks user-added compounding; regulated intermediaries will pay for workflow while individual investors churn among bundled assistants. The architecture predicts the customer.

The $3.4M portfolio footprint and 2,400 analyses form the strongest bull case: Ekpa may resemble the **Perplexity** exception, where a single-sided AI product wins through utility, rapid iteration, and product-led distribution rather than a network. Ekpa’s free entry, read-only posture, and personalized evidence could earn trust before incumbent brokerages move. The case still loses today because Ekpa reports no proprietary moat, public technical artifact, retention evidence, or organic growth loop, while Public already bundles an agent with execution and SnapTrade controls connectivity. I pass at 49, but I would flip to Invest if Ekpa demonstrates at least 40% six-month cohort retention and 20% monthly organic user growth for two consecutive quarters without paid acquisition or founder-led outreach.
