# Kairox Systems -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Conditional on strong product-market fit, the best path is to sell a standard Tempest configuration to allied maritime teams that currently use separate aerial and underwater fleets. Kairox reports concept validation with U.S. and allied military end users. That feedback suggests the job recurs across markets. If buyers can reuse the vehicle configuration and much of the qualification work, Kairox could spread development costs across more orders. Kairox reports laboratory component validation, so field performance and qualification reuse remain assumptions. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The memo records a November public launch and an offer of direct briefings. The path from briefing through qualification and procurement appears buyer-led and high-touch, with little evidence of reusable onboarding. A third-party revenue label leaves paid Tempest deployments and commercial growth unverified. Hardware sales are an inferred business model, and the memo leaves repeat purchasing, unit pricing, and incremental production and support costs unknown. Retention, traction, and cost economics therefore take their defaults. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Buyers can keep their existing fleets, and even strong fit with the first buyer could leave Kairox repeating costly qualification and rollout work for each new customer. Laboratory-stage validation and procurement still described as a future milestone make that a material risk. The structural gate remains open because the extent of buyer-specific work and its effect on contribution are unresolved. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a second allied buyer pay for and field the same Tempest configuration at positive incremental contribution? A paid deployment with a record of qualification, production, and support costs would test the path. Evidence that unavoidable buyer-specific work makes repeat deployments uneconomic, with no credible way to reduce that work, would change the action to Pass.
