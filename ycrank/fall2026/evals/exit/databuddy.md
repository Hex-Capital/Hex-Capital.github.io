# Databuddy -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a product-analytics platform is the strongest private-company path. Databuddy describes a workflow that combines traffic, errors, performance, and revenue data into scheduled investigations with inspectable queries. An analytics platform could buy that workflow to bring cross-tool diagnosis to its installed base. Ownership would make economic sense if production use showed that the integrated workflow improves customer retention or saves enough engineering time to justify buying it over building or licensing it. The memo records one customer’s reported switch from Framer Analytics, which supports demand for the bundle while leaving the value of the investigation workflow unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The memo records 38 active subscriptions and $3,345 in company-wide MRR. Reported financing of more than $560,000 gives the solo founder a plausible starting point for a production test. The memo gives no product-level retention, test budget, burn, or runway, so both the time and capital required remain uncertain. The transaction case also leaves software ownership, integration and customer-data transfer rights, and required consents unverified. The memo provides no separately supported route to cash for this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

The memo gives no terms for the evaluated check, so the bridge uses the illustrative $500,000 check at $10 million post-money. Initial ownership of 5% dilutes to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million in cash before investor taxes, requiring $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% ownership and $62.5 million at 4%. The memo supplies no usable acquisition pricing or quantified buyer economics. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The sale figures below are scenario assumptions. Both assume zero net debt and other price adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. A simple pro rata waterfall allocates the cash paid at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0.

- **Modest sale in year 4.** $30 million in enterprise value yields $900,000 to the holder, or 1.8× gross.
- **Strong sale in year 7.** $100 million in enterprise value yields $3 million to the holder, or 6.0× gross. This clears the hurdle at 3% ownership and misses it at 2%.
- **Prolonged private through year 8.** Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The holder realizes $0, or 0× gross, and retains the shares.

### Countercase

Mixpanel already offers proactive metric monitoring, and PostHog exposes analytics to AI agents. Both can start with customer data they already hold. Databuddy’s strongest countercase is that an incumbent can reproduce enough of the workflow in-house or secure the same benefit through a partnership, leaving little reason to pay an acquisition premium. The memo gives no measured advantage for Databuddy’s full workflow, but the documented overlap covers only parts of it and establishes no price ceiling. That gap warrants Watch. The evidence does not trigger the structural Pass override. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Databuddy show a prospective analytics acquirer that owning the investigation workflow is worth enough, relative to building or licensing it, to support a price that yields at least $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds? A buyer-side production comparison with measured retention gains or engineering savings that support that price would move the action to **Reach out**. Evidence that a readily available substitute caps the price materially below the hurdle would move it to **Pass**.
