# Degla Inc -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Aziz Hanafi has gone beyond describing an autonomous future. Degla Inc reports an October 2026 VTOL demonstration that searched for and tracked a vehicle using voice commands. His autonomous-systems work at InstaDeep and MIT MSEAS research beginning in spring 2024 give that demonstration context. This is evidence of specific knowledge acquired through action. It earns substantial founder credit, although the dossier does not isolate his technical contributions or establish repeated mission reliability.

The investment turns on what accumulates after the demonstration. If a competitor copies Degla’s mission interface, what remains? Onboard inference establishes where computation happens. It does not establish a proprietary model, exclusive flight data, or differentiated aircraft hardware. The dossier identifies none of those assets. I give the team credit for building without assigning the same achievement a second time as defensibility. An aircraft can carry reproducible software.

There is room to maneuver. Search-and-rescue, public safety, and infrastructure inspection share a useful capability: translating an objective into aircraft assignments, coverage, and mission adaptation. Skydio’s reported revenue in the hundreds of millions establishes that buyers spend real money on autonomous aircraft, although it does not establish Degla’s addressable share. That adjacency earns market credit. The broader $28 billion commercial-drone estimate cannot substitute for identifying which missions Degla can perform better than an incumbent with existing customer relationships.

The strongest case against waiting is that embodied autonomy becomes defensible through deployment. Degla’s field demonstration could be the beginning of a reliability advantage that is difficult to reproduce outside real operating conditions. This fits my unstated preference for investing at physical infrastructure bottlenecks. But the other compounding mechanisms are absent: no demonstrated participant-created supply, shared customer utility, or distribution loop. Organizational demonstrations and pilot solicitation are a route to customers; the dossier does not show customers making the product more valuable for one another. FBI, CBP, and DIU attendance establishes an audience, not adoption.

I would consider a founder-led exception before demanding revenue. My exceptions allow an unusually convincing prototype to precede distribution, or a forming network to precede monetization. Degla has meaningful prototype evidence, but neither repeated autonomous performance nor a forming network. Aircraft sales remain an inferred business model, with no public price, completed transaction, or revenue. Those receive the missing-evidence defaults. There is also no documented integrity or financing-pressure red flag; conflicting directory listings do not justify inventing one.

I remain interested, but I would keep my money uncommitted. Degla Inc has stronger evidence of founder capability than of a business whose advantage compounds. Operating permissions, failure recovery, and docking scheduled for Q1 2027 make repeat deployments the next informative evidence. My single flip condition is an independently verified repeat deployment showing that a proprietary model trained on flight data Degla has exclusive rights to use materially improves autonomous mission completion over its prior version. That would turn operating experience into an owned asset—and change the investment.
