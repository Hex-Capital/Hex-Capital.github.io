# Stacktrace -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Stacktrace’s strongest path. An enterprise security platform could buy Fleet and Agent Gateway to add policy enforcement across coding agents and consent controls for consequential actions. The memo records Fleet’s public launch. Stacktrace describes a local sidecar for telemetry and enforcement, plus a gateway that verifies agents, holds actions for consent, and records decisions. Ownership could shorten a buyer’s integration work if production deployments show that Stacktrace covers workflows the buyer would otherwise have to build and maintain. The memo provides no measured buyer benefit or supported acquisition price. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is a paid enterprise deployment that enforces a common policy across multiple coding agents and measures coverage and operator effort against native controls. The launch, founder-reported design partners, and reported $500,000 YC financing make that test plausible. Paid conversion, test cost, burn, and runway are unknown. The memo also leaves IP ownership and contract transfer rights unverified. A sponsor buyout lacks evidence of predictable cash generation. A secondary lacks eligible buyer demand and transfer terms. An IPO lacks evidence of operating scale or reporting readiness. Dividends and buybacks lack evidence of distributable cash. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under the illustrative seed terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The 5× target requires $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, or $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership. The equity hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The reported YC financing is described as a convertible note, with conversion and priority terms unknown. The sale illustrations use simple pro rata allocation before investor taxes and assume $5 million of net debt. Actual fees or senior claims would reduce the amount available to the holder. (Market & Traction)

| Eight-year scenario | Gross cash to the holder at 3% |
|---|---:|
| Modest acquisition at $25 million enterprise value | $600,000 (1.2×) |
| Strong acquisition at $100 million enterprise value | $2.85 million (5.7×) |
| Growth plateaus, buyer demand fails to emerge, public scale stays out of reach, and available cash is reinvested with no secondary or payout | $0 realized, with shares still held |

The target requires about $88.3 million in enterprise value under the illustrative debt assumption. Both sale prices are sensitivities. In a wind-down, only the residual after obligations and preferences could reach this holder, and the memo gives no basis to estimate that residual.

### Countercase

A strategic buyer could build on native controls or partner for the remaining coverage. Anthropic already offers managed permissions and hooks that overlap with Fleet, while Bay deploys through existing security infrastructure and Arcade.dev offers delegated authorization. Stacktrace’s cross-agent policy and consent workflow could still make ownership worthwhile, but the memo supplies no production comparison or retention evidence showing that advantage. These alternatives weaken the reason to buy. The memo establishes neither a structural block nor a supported price ceiling below the holder’s target, so the Pass gate does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid multi-agent deployment demonstrate enough incremental coverage and operating savings over native controls to support a strategic acquisition near the $88.3 million illustrative enterprise-value hurdle? A documented production comparison and a credible buyer-side estimate of the value of owning the product would move the action to Reach out if the implied price could clear the holder’s cash target.
