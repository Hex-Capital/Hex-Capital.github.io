# Hop Aero -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

**Matija Milenovic’s prior propulsion exit and defense-production work make Hop Aero a missionary aerospace company with almost no founder-owned distribution.** That is Hop Aero’s signature: exceptional pre-pitch conviction paired with a distribution asset that begins and ends with procurement. Jacob Balaj worked across the Marine Corps, SpaceX, Virgin Orbit, Masten, and SpinLaunch; Milenovic scaled Ukrainian loitering-munition production from 10 to more than 100 units monthly. Hop Aero has the rizz and tizz to endure Type 2 fun, but 2,256 corporate LinkedIn followers, no public founder writing, and an unreadable website mean none of that conviction compounds through an owned audience.

**Drop.io is the high rung for asymmetric conviction: build the supposedly unreasonable primitive because operating history has made the founder impossible to talk out of it.** Hop Aero shares that mechanism unusually well. Balaj’s launch-vehicle and military-avionics work and Milenovic’s supersonic-UAV and munitions-production experience predate Hop Aero, while a prototype built and tested in under 30 days is missionary behavior rather than strategic-consulting theater. Hop Aero diverges from Drop.io where conviction must become technical proof: a tethered small-scale flight and engine hot-fire remain several brutal tacks short of carrying 250 kilograms for 750 kilometers, surviving hypersonic re-entry, and landing autonomously on unprepared terrain.

**Fin’s weak founder-distribution mechanism was an expensive manual operation without an owned community pulling the product forward, eventually forcing a pivot to workplace analytics.** Hop Aero shows the same absence more starkly: neither Balaj nor Milenovic has a public X account or GitHub footprint, Hop Aero’s website yielded no readable content, and the 2,256 LinkedIn followers belong to Hop Aero rather than to a founder-brand with portable trust. Defense procurement does not require a YouTube channel, obviously, but Hop Aero has no community or personal distribution asset on the cap table; every additional buyer must be won institutionally.

**The $1.25 million U.S. Air Force contract can compound over the next 18 months into flight data, qualifications, and non-dilutive procurement credibility, which is how Hop Aero’s capital structure could become venture-shaped.** The problem is that Hop Aero’s regatta contains propulsion, guidance, re-entry, reusable landing, regulatory approval, manufacturing, and site access before meaningful unit economics appear. Inversion has raised $54 million, Grid Aero $26 million, and Sierra Space at least $1.95 billion; Hop Aero is not a generic five-round SaaS treadmill, but the hardware tacks could still drain founders long before default-alive becomes imaginable.

**No Hop Aero round price or investor roster is disclosed—applying the absent-evidence default on mispricing.** Hop Aero flips up a tier for me only if the SAFE is below the Summer 2026 batch median or credible aerospace funds passed because a container-launched 250-kilogram rocket looked too narrow or illegible. Hop Aero flips down if “defense is hot” has produced a competitive YC round priced off Inversion’s $54 million funding history rather than Hop Aero’s tethered prototype. A non-consensus product is not automatically a non-consensus security; price is where the alpha either exists or disappears.

**Ro is the single-anchor parallel for category fit: regulatory and capital intensity can constitute the venture moment rather than the defect.** Hop Aero could turn launch approvals, government qualifications, reusable-vehicle reliability, and austere-site operations into the same kind of difficult rail that made Ro’s 50-state prescription infrastructure defensible. Hop Aero does not yet earn the exception because the persona’s revealed sleeves are crypto, DTC, healthcare rails, Wizard-of-Oz services, and unusual asset structures—not defense hardware—and public evidence identifies no moat beyond future flight data and approvals.

**The $1.25 million Air Force contract leads me to predict that within 36 months Hop Aero will have accumulated $10 million–$25 million in U.S. defense prototype and development awards but will still lack both recurring commercial revenue and an operational 250-kilogram, 750-kilometer delivery service.** Hop Aero’s founders have enough domain conviction to keep winning funded technical milestones, while Hop Aero’s absent owned distribution and multi-stage regulatory regatta pull it toward becoming a government-program aerospace specialist rather than a broad logistics network.

**The tethered prototype built in under 30 days is the strongest objection to that forecast because Hop Aero may be compressing hardware iteration faster than the public record reveals.** Ro is the relevant Known Exception: capital intensity and regulation can themselves become the moat, and Hop Aero’s $1.25 million Air Force contract plus Milenovic’s 10-to-100-unit monthly production experience provide a real bull mechanism. Hop Aero still loses that argument today because one customer, one tethered flight, one hot-fire, environmental exposure around launch sites, and no demonstrated full-scale performance leave too many simultaneous tacks. A verified autonomous flight carrying 250 kilograms over at least 500 kilometers, paired with a second named procurement program, would flip my verdict to Invest.
