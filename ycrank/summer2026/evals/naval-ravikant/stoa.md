# Stoa -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The strongest evidence is knowledge acquired through action. Kaan Yigit built production trading, pricing, and data systems at Uniper; Eren Berke Saglam traded interest-rate derivatives at Citi; Berat Celik previously formed two companies. This is not a team discovering markets from a pitch deck. But only Yigit’s experience closely matches the operating machinery, and the dossier does not show that any founder previously traded physical GPUs, inspected hardware, or built dealer liquidity. The founders understand exchanges better than they have yet proved they understand this exchange.

The first-month $300 million in RFQs is unusually strong evidence that the market wants coordination. It is not evidence that the market clears. Stoa does not disclose executed volume, revenue, repeat participation, or even a current H100 index value. A marketplace becomes defensible when participants contribute scarce liquidity and reliable price history to one another. RFQ notional can begin that loop, but bids without settlements are conversations, not a network.

The upside is real because the fragmented offline supply is large and becoming more liquid. AI data-center chip shipments are projected to grow from $123 billion in 2024 to $286 billion in 2030, while Blackwell upgrades release H100 inventory into a secondary market. Stoa can serve dealers today and later provide benchmarks, financing inputs, and transaction infrastructure to neoclouds, data-center operators, OEM channels, liquidators, and GPU-backed lenders. This is genuine room to maneuver, although the missing secondary-market SAM and Compute Exchange’s coverage of physical GPUs, reserved capacity, and forward contracts prevent me from treating that expansion as captured demand.

The central asymmetry is that Stoa contractually owns the resulting bid, offer, volume, and transaction data. If settlements accumulate, copying the interface will not copy the market history. Today that asset remains embryonic: Stoa disclaims inspection, authenticity, delivery, and payment responsibility, while counterparties have enough incentive to bypass it that the terms impose 12 months of non-circumvention. Procurri already combines resale with inspection and logistics. The platform is therefore attempting to own the information layer while leaving the difficult physical trust layer elsewhere. That can produce leverage, or it can leave Stoa as a broker whose relationships walk away.

The strongest bull case is that $300 million of immediate RFQ demand proves the founders have already assembled supply and buyers that public metrics cannot yet reveal. Pre-seed marketplaces often look empty immediately before liquidity becomes self-reinforcing. I make exceptions to missing monetization when an observable network or unusually difficult product already carries the option, but Stoa currently shows neither completed transactions nor a functioning public benchmark. There is no disclosed integrity, morale, or artificial-deadline red flag; the problem is structural evidence, not founder character.

I would not write the check today. My base case is that within 36 months Stoa becomes a specialized GPU brokerage and pricing-data business producing $2 million–$6 million in annual revenue, rather than the dominant exchange its positioning implies. One result would flip the decision: three consecutive months exceeding $25 million in executed GMV, with at least 40% of counterparties repeating and a published index derived from settled trades. That would show that users are creating liquidity and proprietary information for one another—the asset that survives copied code.
