# Touchy -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The important event is not that the founders changed the name from Tokenless to Touchy. It is that they moved from model-selection infrastructure to a consumer assistant in roughly two months. That could be a running back changing direction, or it could be three smart people chasing a newer noun. The dossier never identifies the observation that killed the first route, the blocker they encountered, or what changed after their response. So the pivot earns some credit for speed, but none as proof of being relentlessly resourceful.

The strongest evidence is the artifact. Touchy is a live iOS product combining voice, camera input, memory, lock-screen access, and connections to more than 1,000 tools. This is harder than publishing an agent demo. Rohit has relevant AI research, Andrew previously built educational software used by about 500,000 students, and Kevin contributed to Daft and SkyPilot and has a repository with roughly 2,500 stars. These look like fierce nerds. But the measured part of the proof is missing. The 1,000-tool claim is not accompanied by task-completion rates, latency, retention, or evidence that autonomous actions work reliably. The founders themselves say present models remain inadequate for their intended filtering and personalization.

Who wants the crude product now? “iPhone users” is not an answer. The App Store has too few ratings for an overview, the launch post’s 11,000 views and 63 likes measure spectators rather than users, and a $14.99 Touchy Plus listing is a price rather than revenue. The $18.6 billion market estimate is even less informative. There is no named niche repeatedly completing cross-app tasks, tolerating failures, paying, or pulling the product into an unexpected workflow.

The bull case is that this absence is merely launch timing. Three technically strong Berkeley classmates have already shipped a difficult programmable interface, and their rapid change of route may reflect unusually compressed learning. If users begin appropriating Touchy for workflows the founders did not anticipate, it would fit the attractive pattern of technical users pulling on a new primitive. That case stings because a pre-seed consumer product can look empty immediately before it starts growing.

But Touchy presently has the opposite evidence pattern: a substantial artifact without a demonstrated collision with either a user or an obstacle. There is no founder-originated need in the dossier and no proof that the team has already endured the ugly work of permissions, privacy, failed actions, and app integrations. Apple owns the operating-system surface and announced Siri AI with personal context, camera understanding, and system-level actions in June 2026. Meanwhile, Touchy sends sensitive inputs to several outside model providers and says airtight safety guarantees are still required. The schlep may be real, but identifying it is not the same as having crossed it.

My 36-month forecast is that Touchy pivots back toward agent infrastructure or a narrower workflow product before reaching $1 million ARR as a general consumer assistant. I would pass today. One condition would flip me: a documented iOS or reliability blocker that the founders overcome with a shipped workaround within 14 days, followed by a cohort of 100 users completing at least three cross-app tasks weekly for eight consecutive weeks. That would simultaneously prove founder motion and a narrow well of demand.
