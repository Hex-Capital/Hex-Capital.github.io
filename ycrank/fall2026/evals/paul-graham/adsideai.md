# Adside.ai -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The interesting fact about Adside.ai is that the founders say reusable scraping infrastructure let them ship AdsSpy in days rather than months. They found a way to shorten the work instead of treating the work as fixed. That is useful evidence about founders. But the September 2026 Apify account leaves the hardest part unresolved: what specifically stopped them, what did they personally change, and what happened afterward? I give the shipping speed partial credit. Their previous exit cannot supply the missing sequence.

The resulting product deserves separate credit. Adside has implemented cross-platform research tooling, published Meta scraping components, and collected more than 133,000 ads across 6,055 YC companies. Those are inspectable accomplishments. The technical team has built something beyond a diagram. But collecting advertisements and successfully managing advertising spend are different thresholds. The dossier establishes the research machinery more convincingly than it establishes reliable campaign execution or improved customer results.

There is also a credible reason these founders encountered the problem. Choy and Miara operated paid ads for their previous software business. They shared a coworking space for four years and built and sold Botdog together. This reduces my concern that two strangers assembled an “AI for marketing” application. Coordinating tracking, creatives, accounts, and platform integrations contains enough tedious work to conceal an opportunity. What I cannot credit is adaptation: the dossier documents no rejected hypothesis followed by a product change. Starting another company after an exit does not establish that behavior.

The strongest argument for investing is that Adside already reports 30 companies live in October 2026. Combined with a functioning product and an experienced pair of founders, that could be the beginning of a narrow well. The workshops, attended by more than 40 founders and growth practitioners, also put the team near potential users. But “live” does not tell me who comes back, who pays, or which task they would be upset to lose. The $3,500 monthly minimum for managed service makes the distinction consequential. A few customers buying sustained campaign management would tell me much more than workshop attendance or a $50 demo charge.

The bear case is that the founders become very good at operating an agency whose software competitors can reproduce. Omneky and Madgicx already overlap with parts of the workflow, and Adside itself demonstrates how reusable infrastructure lowers development costs. My preference for programmable tools does not automatically transfer to internal agents: the dossier shows no developers building unexpected workflows on Adside. Nor does it establish a proprietary substrate merely because the research database is large. Still, there is no evidence of waiting for a major partner, premature delegation, or a fragile new cofounder relationship. Competition alone would not make me pass.

I would hold off on writing a check. The working-product exception deserves consideration, but this dossier lacks the unmistakable user pull or independently validated breakthrough needed to override the incomplete founder test. My one flip condition is a documented four-week customer episode: an advertising-platform restriction blocks a named customer, the founders ship a workaround within days, and that customer continues paying and using it. That would connect the promising machinery to the behavior I am actually betting on.
