# Amorphic Labs -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The proposed secret is that a software agent can manufacture a prospect-specific product demonstration more cheaply and reliably than a sales engineer. What would prove it wrong? If repeated operation across changing customer applications still requires human review, workflow-by-workflow configuration, and correction, then Amorphic Labs has automated the video editor but not the scarce labor. The dossier reports no reliability result, repeat usage, customer deployment, or shipped experiment that tests this proposition. Computer-use APIs arrived in 2024–2025; building on them is timing, not yet a secret.

The strongest fact is the founding structure. Dylan Kelly and Frank Li have been best friends for nearly a decade and have competed together in mathematics, debate, pitching, business, and hackathons. Li was an OpenAI Member of Technical Staff and project lead, with prior ML-infrastructure work at NVIDIA; Kelly brings software, quantitative, and published AI-research experience. This is not a convenience marriage assembled for YC, and the core technical work appears internal. But their ownership, compensation, and explicit full-time status are undisclosed, so economic exposure remains unproved.

The weakest fact is market ownership. Amorphic enters against Consensus, Reprise, and Walnut, which have raised approximately $143 million, $82 million, and $56 million. The distinction—operating the underlying application to create a narrated recording rather than assembling captures—is a feature boundary, not a monopoly mechanism. No exclusive data, measured reliability advantage, network effect, dominant beachhead, or tenfold technical result exists in the public evidence. The inferred accumulation of workflow maps and correction traces cannot earn credit before it demonstrably improves across customers. This resembles the fashionable application layer, not the increasingly proprietary AI production stack.

The bull case is serious: an unusually well-aligned team, including a former OpenAI technical lead, may solve arbitrary application operation before incumbent demo platforms reorganize around agents. If the product can reliably seed data and record changing enterprise applications, the initial wedge could expand from videos into the operating system for sales engineering. That would be a genuine technical exception to today’s commodity structure. Yet the dossier supplies no independent result comparable to such an exception: the terms instead warn that agent actions may be inaccurate or incomplete and require vendor review. The product also handles credentials, browser state, prospect data, and unauthenticated bearer links, raising implementation costs precisely where enterprise trust matters.

Distribution and economics remain indefinite. Amorphic names plausible buyers—software founders, sales leaders, and RevOps leaders—and is pursuing founder-led demo bookings and referrals. But there is no disclosed price, conversion rate, sales cycle, customer, revenue, or evidence that producing each video has attractive marginal economics. Subscriptions, metered usage, and negotiated fees are three contemplated charging methods rather than one validated revenue engine. A $6 billion sales-enablement market projected to reach $21.2 billion does not show that this narrow product can capture fund-scale value.

My 36-month base case is that Amorphic Labs pivots from personalized demo videos into broader sales-engineering workflow automation, or is acquihired, before reaching $3 million ARR in its present category. I pass today: strong founders, but slogan rather than proved secret; feature rather than monopoly. One condition flips the decision—three named software vendors each using the system for at least 100 externally delivered demos per month, with over 95% completed without human correction and documented expansion after six months. That would simultaneously test the technical secret, establish a bounded beachhead, and begin converting operating traces into proprietary advantage.
