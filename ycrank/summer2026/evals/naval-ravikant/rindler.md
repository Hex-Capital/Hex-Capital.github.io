# Rindler -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

$12.06 billion in forecast 2026 agent spending gives Rindler a large field, while zero evidence of user-added network effects makes Rindler broad-market infrastructure rather than a compounding platform. That is the signature: high pivot-room, low network value. Rindler can move from ATS workflows to bank exports, registries, procurement, or healthcare, but each customer still appears to receive an independent service. Code scales. Custom mappings scale less. Networks compound; services accumulate.

**Uber** is the high rung on market optionality because black cars were merely the entry point into transportation, logistics, and local commerce. Rindler shares the load-bearing abstraction: “turn any website into an API” can expand across every authenticated workflow still trapped behind a browser. Rindler diverges where Uber became more valuable with every driver and rider; Rindler maps sites individually, lists several workflows as available only on request, and depends on Kernel and Browserbase for execution. A large market is useful only if Rindler can traverse it without rebuilding the road each time.

**Perplexity** failed the network test because one user did not directly improve another user’s product, and Rindler exhibits the same single-sided architecture more starkly. Rindler’s inferred library of verified mappings could become shared infrastructure, but the dossier names no mapping count, community contributions, or mechanism through which a customer automatically adds value for the next customer. The Perplexity exception is the relevant precedent: waive the network requirement when permissionless distribution, specific knowledge, and category timing are overwhelming. Rindler has credible category timing after MCP adoption, but Rindler has not yet earned the other two parts of that waiver.

$100 per month for 100 successful sessions gives Rindler code leverage at delivery but only partial permissionless leverage in distribution. Over 18 months, the hosted MCP endpoint can replicate cheaply, verification data can improve recovery, and the free chat product can create product-led discovery. The opposing mechanism is labor hiding inside software: per-site mappings, enterprise sales calls, credential capture, CAPTCHA infrastructure, and compliance work for encrypted bank sessions. Rindler also lacks the unstated pattern I usually buy—open-source adoption or founder media distribution predating the round. Michael Serrano’s top GitHub repository has two stars; Arthur De Los Santos’s has one. Rindler is code-native, but code-native is not synonymous with permissionless.

Michael Serrano’s MIT physics and computer-science degrees, Roblox ML work, and CSAIL LLM research place Rindler above the generic ex-FAANG-product-manager founder. Arthur De Los Santos adds vision-language navigation research directly adjacent to browser control. Rindler would move into the top tier on specific knowledge if the founders had already maintained a widely used browser-agent project, published durable reliability research, or operated authenticated automation at scale. Without one of those artifacts, Rindler shows trainable technical excellence more clearly than non-trainable obsession.

**Stack Overflow** is the single-anchor parallel for the ten-year-partner test: Atwood and Spolsky had years of public work showing how they thought, shipped, and treated a developer community. Rindler’s founders have known each other since 2022, built production-described ATS, bank, and registry workflows, and clearly pass the intelligence-and-energy screen. Rindler provides little evidence on the integrity leg or on how Serrano and De Los Santos behave through conflict. Silence is not guilt, so I apply near-neutral treatment. Play long-term games with long-term people—but first observe them playing.

Browserbase has raised $67.5 million and already owns adjacent cloud-browser distribution, so my falsifiable 36-month forecast is that Browserbase acquires Rindler for under $50 million by July 2029 after Rindler reaches $1–3 million ARR but fails to establish an independent mapping network. Rindler’s typed actions, authentication, and verifier-gated configurations are valuable complements to Browserbase’s primitives; Rindler’s supplier dependence and absent user-added compounding make integration more probable than category ownership. A useful feature can become an acquisition. A network becomes a destiny.

Rindler’s reported 3x reduction in failures, 4x faster completion, and 6x lower execution cost form the strongest case against that forecast. The bull mechanism is a hidden data network: every failed session teaches Rindler how websites break, every verified mapping becomes reusable supply, and MCP distributes Rindler to agent builders at near-zero marginal cost. That could reproduce the Perplexity exception—single-sided today, defining infrastructure tomorrow. The dossier still supplies only company-reported benchmarks, no independent reliability history, no customer count, and no proof that mappings created for one customer reduce work for the next. I stay neutral and do not write the check today. I would flip to Invest if Rindler verifiably reaches 1,000 reusable mappings with more than half of new paid deployments activating self-serve without founder-assisted configuration.
