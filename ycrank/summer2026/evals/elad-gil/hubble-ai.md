# Hubble -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Aaron Leon built EHR and AI infrastructure serving 1M+ patients at Amazon One Medical, while Hubble discloses no revenue or usage curve. That pairing is Hubble’s signature: unusually credible builders attacking infrastructure they have lived inside, but without evidence that buyers are pulling the product out of them. Hubble is shipping-rich and curve-poor—closer to a compelling pre-seed operating thesis than something already “just working.”

Perplexity is the high rung on founder shipping: Aravind Srinivas repeatedly turned investor conversations into shipped product within a week. Hubble shares the underlying mechanism because Leon built healthcare infrastructure at scale and Prabha Dublish launched telehealth, marketplace, integration, and AI-care products as Grow Therapy’s first product hire. Hubble diverges from Perplexity because 40+ live tools demonstrate construction velocity, not iteration velocity: Hubble provides no named customer feedback loop, release cadence, or evidence that Dublish and Leon ship between conversations.

Lyft is the low-rung warning on revenue velocity: visible activity can still be a false signal when the underlying economics do not establish a winning position. Hubble offers even less evidence on this axis—no revenue, customer count, repeat usage, signed LOI, or month-over-month growth. Hubble’s production deployment across Epic, Cerner, athenahealth, UnitedHealthcare, and Aetna is technically meaningful at pre-seed, but it does not distinguish enterprise-pilot tourism from actual buying behavior.

CMS-0057-F requires affected payers to enhance four FHIR API categories beginning January 1, 2027, giving Hubble a real regulatory catalyst rather than an $8.57 billion TAM slide. Over the next 18 months, compliance deadlines should create integration work, expose gaps between nominal FHIR availability and usable longitudinal records, and increase demand for Hubble’s browser, voice, and API fallbacks. Hubble does not score at the Stripe level because the rule primarily compels payers, while Health Gorilla, Particle Health, and 1upHealth already possess infrastructure and distribution positioned to absorb the same demand.

Particle Health, Health Gorilla, 1upHealth, and Zus Health make Hubble’s index position unresolved. Hubble could become an index on patient-data access if every legal, research, and digital-health workflow routes through its permissioning, provenance, and orchestration layer; today Hubble must instead win share from established networks with $40 million to $80 million-plus already raised. This score flips upward only if Hubble proves its 40+ tools let customers activate materially faster or retrieve materially more complete records than those platforms, turning Hubble from another aggregator into the default control plane above them.

Color Genomics is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow infrastructure on top of commodity sequencing avoided making new science the investment premise. Hubble similarly builds deterministic identity, permissioning, provenance, and audit workflows on top of existing EHRs and payer systems; Hubble does not need new physics or a foundational-model breakthrough. The remaining dependency chain—reliable retrieval across brittle browser and voice channels, defensible consent handling for PHI, and enterprise adoption despite incumbent substitution—is execution-heavy but not a three-miracle science project.

Forty-plus live tools lead me to predict that by July 2029 Hubble reaches $3 million to $7 million in ARR from patient-record retrieval workflows for legal and life-sciences customers, but Hubble does not become the broad healthcare-interoperability default. Hubble’s builders should find a narrow paid wedge, while fragmented coverage and national-network incumbents prevent the Stripe-like index outcome implied by “the intelligence layer for patient information.”

Prabha Dublish’s Grow Therapy experience and Aaron Leon’s 1M-patient infrastructure history create the strongest bull mechanism: CMS reopens the market precisely when two domain-native builders have already assembled 40+ production tools. Hubble could be a Color Genomics-style known exception where workflow ownership above the hard layer matters more than owning the underlying network. The bull case loses today because Hubble names no customer, revenue figure, repeat usage rate, or completeness benchmark, while 1upHealth already serves 75+ enterprises and 35M+ patients. I would not write the check yet; three named enterprise customers producing greater than 20% month-over-month paid-usage growth for three consecutive months would flip Hubble to Invest.
