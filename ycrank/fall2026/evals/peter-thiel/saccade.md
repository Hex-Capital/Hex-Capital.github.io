# Saccade -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Saccade’s proposed secret is that a 15-minute eye-movement assessment can reveal Alzheimer’s pathology up to 15 years before symptoms. That is a falsifiable proposition: blinded testing against confirmed pathology could show that its signal disappears outside the training population or adds nothing to existing assessments. But the dossier identifies neither a prevailing belief that Saccade overturns nor peers who reject its specific proposition. The current homepage explicitly discloses unvalidated diagnostic performance. I give the claim limited credit for specificity; I cannot give it credit for being true.

The strongest argument for investing is that this is already more than a diagnostic concept. UCSB documents custom cameras, illumination, thermal management, and an attachment built for Apple Vision Pro. Shreyas Bhardwaj has relevant medical-device and soft-robotics experience, and the October 2026 launch reports deployment in San Francisco clinics. A cheap, repeatable measurement of otherwise inaccessible pathology could become important infrastructure. This fits my interest in neglected physical technology and in making hidden reality measurable. The decisive distinction is between an engineered instrument and a demonstrated biological measurement. Saccade has evidence of the former.

Even a successful measurement would leave a second question: why does Saccade keep the value? Longevity clinics provide a recognizable beachhead, but ViewMind already offers overlapping eye-movement assessments and preventive-health products. The patent-pending attachment and documented engineering earn some proprietary-technology credit. Neither establishes exclusive diagnostic capability, a tenfold advantage, or ownership of the clinic workflow. Longitudinal pathology-linked data could eventually change that, but an undocumented future dataset is not a present monopoly mechanism. The strongest bull case requires Saccade to discover a signal its existing competitors cannot reproduce.

The founding structure remains an open question. Bhardwaj participated in the hardware’s development, which is more informative than a prestigious affiliation alone. But a team size of two does not establish who the other person is, whether all core founders work full-time, their shared history, or their ownership. I use the stated default for alignment. There is no documented part-time-founder, investor-control, or outsourced-core-engineering red flag here. Equally, the record does not establish the durable operator partnership that would justify exceptional conviction.

Distribution is somewhat more concrete than the economics. Saccade names US longevity clinics as its initial route and reports clinic deployment, earning limited credit beyond a channel label. Yet the launch’s availability claims sit uneasily beside a homepage recruiting future research participants. There are no disclosed assessment volumes, prices, sales cycles, or commercial terms. Without pricing and margins, I cannot construct one revenue engine capable of producing a power-law outcome; the $10.2 billion diagnostics-market estimate supplies no substitute. Moreover, an asymptomatic result does not itself establish treatment eligibility, so clinical usefulness must be demonstrated separately from patient curiosity.

I would pass today. The exceptions that could override unresolved technical and monopoly questions—independent technical proof, persistent customer behavior, or a credible deployment path anchored in demonstrated performance—are not established by unnamed clinic installations. This is also not a documented technical collapse or regulatory violation; the founding claim remains unproved rather than refuted. My single verifiable flip condition is an independently run, blinded, multisite study showing that Saccade detects confirmed amyloid pathology in asymptomatic patients with clinically useful accuracy and meaningful incremental value over existing assessment methods. That would turn the central proposition into evidence worth underwriting.
