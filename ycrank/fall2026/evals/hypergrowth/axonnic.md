# Axonnic -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch

### Company case

**Repeatable direct sales** is Axonnic’s strongest path. The initial buyer is a manufacturer trying to automate a force-sensitive task such as sewing or delicate assembly. Axonnic’s sleeve captures muscle signals that video misses, and its workflow uses those signals to train a policy for the factory’s robot. The job plausibly travels across markets. Axonnic reports a signed sewing LOI with an Italian manufacturer, while its standard Gen 2 preorder terms specify US delivery. Deploying at a second factory still requires adapting the demonstrations to that factory’s robot. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Axonnic reports paid design-partner pilots and sleeve shipments. Those commitments support early buyer fit. The memo documents live control of a Proception robotic hand, while the Italian sewing deployment remains at the LOI stage. The memo gives too little detail to trace an independent buyer from discovery through payment and factory go-live, and it provides no evidence of repeat paid use. If the first factory gets enough value to buy a rollout on another line, that could support account expansion. That is a scenario assumption. The demo and pilot reports leave the decisive step, a repeatable paid production rollout, unobserved. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Each factory could remain a custom engineering project even if buyers want the product. Axonnic reports that interference and hardware problems forced repeat recordings. Wired electronics target one cause of that rework, and the memo gives no before-and-after labor or cost measurements. Robot-specific retargeting and separately negotiated data reuse rights could also limit how much work carries over to the next buyer. The structural downside gate remains open because the evidence establishes rework on early deployments while leaving proportional costs across successive deployments unresolved. (Defensibility, Key Risks)
