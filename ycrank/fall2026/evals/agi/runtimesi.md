# Runtime.si -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A, scarce complements, is the strongest case.** The candidate bottleneck is getting physical robots to handle changed manipulation tasks reliably without hours of new demonstrations and fine-tuning. Runtime’s policy searches for actions in a reconstructed scene and updates the simulation from physical feedback. Cheaper simulation and planning could make that capability valuable to robot builders if it cuts changeover labor while meeting production cycle times. The November policy launch is observed. Runtime claims it matched or exceeded unnamed models on tasks including rope threading and egg grasping, with trial counts, success rates, and live cycle times still unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

RoboSkillHub documents reusable skills and which ones have been verified on physical hardware. That gives limited evidence of accumulated operating knowledge. A Runtime-controlled library of simulation errors could strengthen the advantage across deployments, but that remains an inference. The founders’ control, manipulation, and manufacturing experience and the policy launch support seed-stage execution credit. The memo identifies no buyer commitment to the current policy. (Defensibility, Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under a hypothetical software license, the expansion unit is the next deployed site. Contribution would be license revenue less incremental compute, integration, and ongoing support. The memo gives no inputs to estimate that contribution or the number of reachable sites. Path B remains unproven because the memo identifies neither a newly viable buyer or transaction nor a reason to pay Runtime under model parity. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

With abundant capital and comparable models, rivals could build similar simulation search or solve the same changeover problem through short demonstrations or learned skill recombination. Open physics tools and Simify’s physical rearrangement demonstration make replication credible. If Runtime’s planning latency or simulation errors require frequent intervention, the apparent setup savings could also disappear in production. The memo has no like-for-like deployment comparison to settle either issue. That leaves the Path A outreach gate unmet while a representative field test remains feasible. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent robot deployer commit to paid repeat use after a head-to-head production trial across representative task changes? The trial should compare Runtime with the buyer’s best available alternative at matched success and cycle-time thresholds, counting setup, integration, retries, and human intervention per successful task. A sustained cost advantage traceable to Runtime-controlled calibration data or verified skill coverage, coupled with paid repeat use, would support **Reach out** if the updated score clears the outreach threshold. Equivalent performance and delivery cost from a readily available substitute would move the action to **Pass**.
