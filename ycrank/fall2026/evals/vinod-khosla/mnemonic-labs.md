# Mnemonic Labs -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Mnemonic Labs could discover profitable strategies and still fail my consequence-of-success test. What becomes radically cheaper, and for whom? The dossier supplies a $26.3 billion algorithmic-trading market estimate, but no quantified research-cost baseline, improvement in capital allocation, or expansion of access to financial expertise. Trading profits can support an excellent business. They do not automatically establish a societal resource multiplier. The consequence is unproven; I would not assume it is small.

The strongest argument for investing is the founders’ direct experience. Videh worked at Citadel and Walleye; Kalki worked at Tower Research, where the company attributes $20 million in annual PnL to his work. That is relevant evidence of domain competence, explicitly separate from Mnemonic revenue. Their company-reported training pipeline and architecture, built in under six weeks, make this more substantive than a slogan about AI for finance. But the move from robotics research into markets does not establish evidence-driven belief revision, and the dossier documents no complementary recruiting against a specific weakness. I credit the experience without treating it as proof of those different capabilities.

The central technical question is whether more training produces more *deployable* investment advantage. Over 25TB of tokens describes an input. Mnemonic discloses no scaling curve, leakage-controlled comparison, or returns after transaction costs and market impact. Its own architecture leaves open genuine novelty; the evidence does not establish the red flag of merely replicating a standard model. Equally, it establishes no measured discontinuity against existing quantitative methods. Prodigy’s overlapping foundation-model effort makes the distinction consequential: a working training system alone does not demonstrate a scarce capability.

The next dollar should attack that uncertainty. I would ask for a preregistered comparison across training scales, with untouched forward data, conventional-model baselines, fixed risk limits, and execution costs at stated capital capacity. The dossier contains no such test, budget, threshold, or stop decision. Nor does it disclose training and inference economics or a selected commercial model. These receive the missing-evidence defaults. They are not evidence of reckless spending or permanent subsidy dependence. In particular, cheap predictions would mean little if their usable trading capacity vanished as capital increased.

My execution-heavy fintech investments show that scientific novelty is not an absolute requirement. The strongest exception here would be exceptional practitioners turning an expensive existing workflow into a much broader financial platform. Mnemonic has the practitioners; it has not demonstrated that broader mechanism. The other exceptions require extraordinary quantified consequence or visible breakthrough evidence, neither supplied here. **I pass with my own money.** My verifiable flip condition is an independently audited forward trial showing that Mnemonic delivers comparable or better risk-adjusted net returns at one-tenth the fully loaded research cost of a conventional quantitative process, at a disclosed, economically meaningful capital capacity. That would connect its technical ambition to an actual cost collapse.
