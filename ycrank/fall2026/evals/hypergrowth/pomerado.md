# Pomerado -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Conditional on strong product-market fit, Pomerado’s strongest path is to acquire agent developers through its open-source core and npm, then convert recurring portal workflows to paid cloud usage. The current product packages website tasks as reusable MCP or REST tools. The catalog grew from 46 tools across 23 sites to 584 tools across 447 sites, supporting the inference that developers in multiple markets can use the same core product where they have portal access. Signup is self-serve and Premium pricing is published, while independent checkout and production setup for authenticated workflows remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Recurring workflow calls and maintained integrations could support renewals. The $50 monthly Premium plan caps calls and new integrations, so heavier use within that plan leaves the bill flat. Custom outcome-based Business pricing offers a possible expansion path with unverified terms and uptake. The two-person team attracted 35,000 weekly npm downloads in a November snapshot, and the founders reported several use cases. The memo establishes neither realized sales nor pre-revenue status, so initial traction receives the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

In a 30-day scenario where a Premium account uses all 1,000 daily calls, $50 leaves about $0.0017 per call to cover all attributable costs at break-even. Browser, proxy, and sandbox use, model processing, integration creation and repair, and any human onboarding or support draw on that budget. Reusing integrations and automating repairs could improve contribution. Actual utilization and cost per account remain unknown. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Authenticated portals could pull the business toward hands-on deployment and maintenance. Operators can change interfaces, authentication, or access policies. The founders reported a 2.2% run error rate and said 82% of errors were resolved automatically. The remaining failures could require repeated human work, and sensitive-data handling could narrow access for regulated buyers. The structural gate clears because the memo does not establish that bespoke rollout or uneconomic servicing is unavoidable for authorized developer workflows. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of developers acquired through npm or the open-source repository independently activate authenticated workflows, pay for cloud production, and renew with positive contribution after attributable onboarding, execution, repair, and support costs? A cohort that demonstrates this through its first renewal would move the action to Reach out.
