# Axiomat -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Axiomat could make on-demand pricing analysis economical for teams that currently restrict AI use and reserve manual validation for a narrower set of decisions. The memo records a public launch and a product demo. Axiomat says it shows users its interpretation of a business rule, then uses Lean to verify that the generated query matches the formal definition before execution. It retries or abstains if it cannot prove a match. The memo provides no representative buyer deployment or measured labor savings. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Scenario assumption.** If approved rules can be reused across frequent questions, teams could spread the initial rule setup over many decisions and reduce analyst review per usable answer. The cost comparison must include rule authoring and maintenance, inference, retries, and analyst time spent on exceptions and abstentions. With equally capable models widely available, buyers might still pay for an approved rule library embedded in their workflows if switching requires substantial revalidation. The memo describes rule approval and offers no buyer evidence of switching costs or preference under functional parity. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

A valid proof can certify a query against a mistaken definition. If analysts still have to resolve ambiguous rules or handle frequent abstentions, the workflow may open little new demand. Snowflake’s semantic views and approved SQL examples could also satisfy the same buyer at lower adoption cost. The memo contains no head-to-head buyer result that settles either condition. Both Path B gates remain open, with a concrete test available. (Defensibility, Key Risks)
