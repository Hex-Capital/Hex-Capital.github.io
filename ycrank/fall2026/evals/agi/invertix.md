# Invertix -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path B, New Economies, is Invertix’s strongest path. Solar operations teams manually stitch together alarms, asset records, and repair coordination across portals, spreadsheets, and email. Altenia’s CEO reported that some work went from days to minutes. That supports faster turnaround for an existing operator. The market-opening hypothesis is that smaller portfolio operators could afford to investigate and resolve lower-priority faults they previously left unattended. Testing that hypothesis requires comparing manual and agent-assisted cost per verified resolution, including operator review, retries, escalations, technician time, and post-repair checks. The testimonial leaves both the full cost and the amount of previously skipped work unmeasured. (The Idea; Market & Traction)

With equally capable models widely available, buyers might still pay Invertix to preserve asset histories, approval rules, and maintenance procedures embedded in their workflows. That switching cost is an inference from the product design. TotalEnergies’ solar-team testing shows an independent operator committing time, while paid repeat use for the proposed new workflow remains unverified. Invertix launched Prometheus, and its CTO brings prior autonomy experience. Representative evidence on triage and repair verification remains limited. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Founders & Team)

The expansion unit is a customer portfolio. Invertix quotes by portfolio size and integration scope. A viable rollout would require portfolio fees to cover engineer-led onboarding and ongoing support, with enough reusable integration work to keep the next deployment economical. The memo offers little operating cost data to test that assumption. (The Idea; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Invertix may speed up work that existing operators already perform while leaving the cost of resolving marginal faults largely intact. Customer approvals and field work can still consume labor, and Metris and Areg.AI already offer overlapping workflows. If an equivalent rival can reproduce the asset context and approval workflows at reasonable cost, Invertix’s proposed switching advantage weakens. The memo leaves both Path B gates unresolved. Path A has weaker support because the documented product coordinates customer operations and customers retain approval over plant-control changes. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent small solar portfolio operator renew at a commercially viable price after a trial shows that Invertix can reliably resolve a class of faults the operator previously deferred? Use the operator’s work logs to establish what went untreated and why, measure full cost per verified resolution, and offer a functionally equivalent alternative using models of comparable quality at renewal. Move to Reach out if the economics support recurring use and the buyer renews because Invertix’s embedded workflows provide measurable value. Move to Pass if a representative trial shows that full delivery costs still rule out the new use or that the buyer readily switches under model parity.
