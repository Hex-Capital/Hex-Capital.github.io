# Halmos Labs -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Halmos Labs’ potentially important secret is that checking an experiment’s assumptions can save more laboratory work than optimizing within those assumptions. The strongest evidence is the fermentation collaboration: the system identified a missing nutrient variable, and an unnamed customer confirmed the omission before revising its experimental plan. This suggests a machine might improve the question scientists ask. The proposition would weaken substantially if prospective comparisons showed that competent experimentalists or existing optimization tools routinely found the same omissions. No named peer rejects this proposition in the dossier, so its contrarian status remains unproved.

The technical evidence warrants attention. Halmos reports matching a bioprocessing campaign’s best performance with 30% fewer evaluations across repeated simulations. Its retrospective demonstrations recovered four of six responding mutations among ten candidates from an enriched, single-patient input and placed a validated epitope within twenty predictions for each of five TESLA patients. These are concrete tests, although company-reported and limited in scope. The strongest bull case is that assumption checking transfers across research problems and becomes indispensable wherever laboratory experiments are expensive. The complication is unusually revealing: Halmos repeatedly proposed experiments outside a collaboration’s permitted range. A recommendation creates economic value only when the customer can execute it. Logical consistency alone does not establish experimental usefulness.

Who owns the resulting advantage? Halmos solicits projects across biotech R&D, assay development, process development, and manufacturing. That breadth leaves its first dominatable market undefined. Atinary already provides experiment optimization and announced a Bruker collaboration in August 2026; Synthace supplies experiment-design and automation workflows. These are adjacent competitors, not proof that Halmos is interchangeable with them. But automatic deductive checks and sampling across assumptions have no independently established performance advantage, and the proposed feedback loop carries no demonstrated exclusive data rights. My preference for infrastructure and systems that make uncertain information usable makes this product interesting. It does not establish a monopoly mechanism.

The founding structure remains an open question. Akke’s biochemical research at MIT, AstraZeneca, and Cambridge gives the product a credible scientific origin; Löfgren’s quantitative modeling and LLM-security experience corresponds to its verification ambitions. Neither credential establishes long-standing collaboration, full-time commitment, ownership, or control. Shared attendance at Lund supplies little evidence about those questions. I see no documented convenience pairing, outsourced core engineering, investor-dominated capitalization, or foundational technical retraction. Those red flags cannot be presumed. Equally, the record does not establish the alignment needed for a decades-long company.

Distribution has a plausible starting point: directly scoped projects for identifiable research teams. It has no demonstrated repeatable channel, sales cycle, or implementation economics. The September 2026 terms contemplate paid offerings but disclose no prices; margins and customer expansion are also unknown. I cannot yet connect one revenue engine to a power-law outcome. The exceptions that could overcome these gaps—independent technical proof, persistent transaction demand, or a concrete validated deployment path—are not established by the simulations, unnamed customer confirmation, or university verification funding. I would pass on writing a check today while keeping the scientific question open. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: an independently audited prospective fermentation trial showing that Halmos’ proprietary validation checks achieve the same yield with at least 30% fewer permissible wet-lab experiments than a tuned incumbent workflow.
