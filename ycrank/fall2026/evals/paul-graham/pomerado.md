# Pomerado -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Pomerado has shipped something I would want to try. But the question that would make me invest remains unanswered: what happened when a customer’s integration broke, and what did the founders do next? Websites without APIs are a specific obstacle. The dossier does not document a particular encounter, the workaround, or how quickly it reached a user. I cannot turn a sensible product architecture into evidence of resourcefulness.

The technical choice is good. Use a model to discover and test an operation, then stop paying a model to rediscover it on every execution. Pomerado’s published code and [architecture](https://github.com/Pomerado/pomerado/blob/main/docs/how-it-works.md) make this more substantial than a proposed agent platform. The separate review model is another concrete design decision. That earns meaningful technical credit. The claimed performance gains remain unverified, however, and publishing an implementation does not establish that it survives changing websites and authentication flows.

Akshay’s enterprise-agent work at Scale AI, including Mayo Clinic deployments, gives the problem selection credibility. Alan brings enterprise data and AI experience, and their previous collaboration on SafeSip reduces the concern that they assembled a cofounder relationship for an application. But employment history does not tell me which deployment failure prompted Pomerado. Nor does the dossier show a rejected hypothesis followed by a changed product. Those are unanswered questions, not evidence that the founders are passive or inflexible.

The strongest argument for investing is that these founders have already put a useful primitive into developers’ hands. Pomerado fits my pattern of backing programmable tools before their eventual uses are obvious. Its 1.1k GitHub stars and 100 forks suggest developers find it worth examining. But that pattern becomes compelling when developers build recurring workflows on the primitive. Product Hunt’s October 16 rank of #5 and 181 points measure a launch response. Neither those figures nor forks establish that a particular user needs Pomerado again tomorrow. The published $50 monthly plan establishes an offer, not a purchase.

The opportunity may be in the maintenance work. Browserbase, Browser Use, and Skyvern already overlap with script generation or replay, so generating an integration is unlikely to remain scarce. Keeping an important integration working could be scarce. Pomerado’s terms acknowledge interruptions from website and authentication changes; that is where the founders could learn something competitors miss. The launch channels do not themselves trigger my launch-dependent distribution red flag, but the dossier supplies no individual customer recruitment story either. A working-prototype exception can overcome uncertainty about a newly formed team; it cannot, by itself, establish exceptional founder behavior or unmistakable demand here.

I would pass today. Pomerado has its strongest evidence in the artifact, while my investment gate starts with the people overcoming resistance. There is no documented exception strong enough to replace that missing evidence. One verifiable deployment would change my decision: a customer integration fails, the founders ship a workaround within a week, and that customer subsequently pays and uses it weekly for four weeks. That single case would connect the code to both resourcefulness and a real well of need.
