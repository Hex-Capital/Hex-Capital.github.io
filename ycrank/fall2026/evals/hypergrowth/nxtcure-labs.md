### Company case

**Strongest path: Channel-led or repeatable sales.** GalenOps targets clinical-stage oncology biotechs that need to run trials. The memo documents a launch inviting sponsors to book a demo and a demo built around a synthetic study. Nxtcure says GalenOps automates routine trial work and routes consequential decisions to qualified human reviewers. If sponsors find the service valuable, Nxtcure could sell trial execution directly and reuse the same onboarding and review workflow across sponsors and subsequent programs. The core job plausibly travels across markets, with local validation and oversight requirements still unresolved. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The first unproven link is moving from a demo to a paid live study through a repeatable setup. The memo provides no evidence of sponsor budget or workflow commitments, live onboarding, repeat paid programs, or measured delivery effort. The earlier trial-matching pilots involved a different product. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

GalenOps could function as a high-touch CRO if each protocol requires substantial custom setup and qualified review hours rise in proportion to the number of studies. The synthetic demo leaves that delivery burden unmeasured, and the planned digital-twin modules still require validation. The structural downside gate does not apply because the memo provides no live rollout evidence that proportional delivery costs are unavoidable. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
