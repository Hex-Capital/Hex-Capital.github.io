# ClaimKit -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

ClaimKit’s strongest path is to reuse its evidence-collection software across markets and reserve specialist time for local tax judgment. Finance and engineering teams in the US, UK, and Australia face the same recurring job of connecting project records, code activity, and payroll to an R&D claim. ClaimKit says its Australian product continuously links evidence from more than 50 tools, and the memo documents distinct filing workflows in the three markets. The shared evidence layer supports partial portability, with the work required to add another filing regime still uncertain. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

Direct signup, a US credit calculator, and reusable integrations give buyers a way to start. Quoted fees and specialist review on every claim keep the path to a completed filing high-touch. ClaimKit reports more than 80 clients across three countries seven months after product launch, with a listed team of four. Edexia’s report of lower costs after switching mid-project gives some support to inferred early paid use. The paid share of those clients and the pace of revenue growth remain unknown. Continuous evidence collection gives customers a reason to return for later filings. The Australian terms document per-engagement fees, with repeat billing and same-customer expansion still unverified. (*ClaimKit*, *The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

For an Australian filing, incremental contribution is the allocated upfront software fee plus the tax-agent fee at lodgement, less attributable sales, onboarding, specialist review, AI and infrastructure, and support costs. The agent fee is the greater of a quoted minimum or an agreed share of the tax benefit. The scaling scenario assumes automated evidence matching reduces preparation time enough for each specialist to handle more filings. Fluer’s testimonial supports lower customer effort. ClaimKit’s own review time, capacity needs, and contribution per filing remain unmeasured in the memo. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

ClaimKit could scale like a tax practice, with each additional filing requiring substantial specialist time and each new market requiring dedicated filing expertise. Mandatory review, distinct filing workflows, and incomplete customer records support that risk. The structural downside gate remains open because the evidence-collection workflow is reusable across the markets ClaimKit already serves, and the memo leaves open whether automation can reduce specialist work enough to make higher volume economical. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Does specialist time per paid filing fall as volume rises? Compare fees collected and attributable costs for comparable filings across successive cohorts in the three current markets, including rework and the cost of adding review capacity. Move to Pass if the comparison shows that unavoidable specialist work makes higher volume uneconomic even after the documented automation is in use.
