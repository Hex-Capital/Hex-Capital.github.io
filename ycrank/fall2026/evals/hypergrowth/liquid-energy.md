# Liquid Energy -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** The likely first buyer is an inference platform that repeatedly needs more capacity and faces long waits for conventional supply. If a platform can route paid workloads to NanoDCs through one reusable integration, Liquid Energy could win more volume from that account by bringing additional factory-built units online. In a second geography, the same platform and module could serve the same workload if the site meets local siting requirements and can sustain the required solar, battery, and connectivity performance. That portability is an inference. The memo identifies neither a second-market buyer nor a cross-market test. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The memo documents the November 5 launch. Liquid Energy reports 20 units on hand, four-week deployment, 30-minute activation on arrival, and agreements with unnamed inference platforms. Those are early supply and distribution signals. Paid activation, buyer commitment terms, live workload, and repeat orders remain unverified. The pre-seed growth mechanism is plausible, with current fit still open. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A platform could send Liquid Energy more work while each increment of capacity still requires another company-funded module. Liquid Energy owns the hardware, and its commodity-price scenario models about **$1,500 in annual compute revenue per NanoDC**. At that revenue, each unit has $1,500 a year to cover its share of channel and sales costs, connectivity, site operations, maintenance, and recovery of the capital spent on hardware, shipping, and setup. Realized pricing, sustained billable throughput, and installed cost are unknown. Battery autonomy and satellite reliability could further reduce billable output. Factory repetition could lower assembly cost, but the memo offers no measured savings. This makes contribution the decisive uncertainty. The structural Pass gate requires observed scaling costs, which the memo leaves unresolved. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent inference platform that pays for a first NanoDC deployment expand to a second site through the same integration and generate positive contribution there? A paid expansion order, sustained billable throughput across day and night, and actual incremental sales, deployment, operating, and capital costs would answer it. A successful rollout would move the action to **Reach out**.
