# Mantle Machines -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic sale to an industrial equipment maker. Mantle describes a bundled service that combines machine records, remote diagnosis, and technician dispatch. The founders say flying an in-house engineer to a repair can cost more than $5,000 in travel alone. An OEM could have a reason to own Mantle if the service reliably reduces repair costs and downtime across machine models, and ownership captures more of that benefit than a service contract. The memo records Teradyne’s investment in machine-monitoring vendor MachineMetrics, a signal of adjacent strategic interest. It provides no measured savings or evidence of acquisition interest in Mantle. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Mantle reports multiple live customers and more than 250 field technicians. That gives it a starting point for a paid OEM rollout across several machine models. A useful milestone would measure downtime and cost per resolved incident against the OEM’s current service, while tracking Mantle’s margin after technician labor, parts, and expert support. The memo gives no rollout budget or evidence that the monthly fee covers those costs. Customer contract assignability and rights to transfer machine service records are unverified. The adjacent management-led buyout shows that a maintenance business can transact, with no evidence of a separate route that would pay this holder. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, dilution from 5% to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity. The sale scenarios assume a full cash exit within eight years, an eligible holder selling its entire stake, $5 million in combined net debt and deal costs, and a pro rata waterfall with no senior claims or holdbacks.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Holder cash |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------|
| Modest sale | $45M enterprise value less $5M in adjustments leaves $40M in distributable equity | $1.2M (2.4×) |
| Strong sale | $105M enterprise value less $5M in adjustments leaves $100M in distributable equity | $3.0M (6.0×) |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested, with no distribution or eligible share sale within eight years | $0 |

The sale values are illustrative assumptions. With the modeled adjustments, the hurdle is about $88.3 million in enterprise value. The memo provides no pricing, margins, or transaction value that would support a realizable sale range. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Roboworx already bundles robot field service with telemetry-based predictive maintenance, giving a prospective OEM buyer a reason to contract for overlapping capabilities. Frequent visits or costly parts could also erode Mantle’s flat-fee margins. Together, those risks could leave Mantle operating as a service provider without a buyer willing to clear the proceeds hurdle. The memo leaves the economics of its broader CNC-and-robot offering open, so the evidence supports Watch without triggering the Pass gate. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a paid, multi-model OEM rollout show enough economic benefit to justify an acquisition near the modeled $88.3 million enterprise-value hurdle? Buyer-validated savings against the incumbent service, positive delivery margins, and a credible case for owning the capability would move the action to Reach out. Without that evidence, keep Mantle on Watch.
