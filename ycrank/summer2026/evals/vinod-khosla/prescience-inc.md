# Prescience, Inc. -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The opportunity is consequential: U.S. private-health-insurance spending reached $1.6446 trillion in 2024, and employer family premiums averaged $26,993 in 2025. If Prescience can use medical histories to choose materially better care trajectories, it attacks both waste and poor care rather than merely automating insurance administration. But the dossier never quantifies the reachable population, expected savings, or clinical improvement. A giant cost pool is not itself a 100x consequence.

Time Machine is the entire option. Reinforcement learning that searches possible care trajectories could become a proprietary medical decision system; Crystal, appointment handling, billing assistance, and automated plan filings cannot. Prescience provides no validation study, sensitivity data, outcome comparison, or cost benchmark against Angle Health, Collective Health, brokers, or conventional care management. Competitors already automate overlapping navigation and administration. Until Time Machine produces a measured discontinuity, I must treat the product as an integrated insurance operation with an unverified AI claim—not a demonstrated technical breakthrough.

The team makes the bull case sting. Rishab Jain combines computer science, neuroscience, more than ten ML-and-health papers, and two company-reported acquisitions. Aditya Jain adds a Harvard MD, biomedical engineering, and medical-AI research. They have built together for ten years, obtained California Administrator License #6019366, and assembled partners across HSAs, compliance, provider networks, risk, and administration. This is unusual pre-seed founder-market fit. What is missing is evidence of belief revision or recruiting owners for actuarial, regulatory, security, and clinical-validation risks. Two talented brothers are not yet a risk-tuned organization.

The largest kill risk is clinical-model validity, yet no falsifiable milestone is disclosed. I want the cheapest experiment that can distinguish better care routing from plausible-sounding recommendations: a prespecified cohort, risk-adjusted baseline, clinical safety threshold, savings threshold, cost, and stop condition. The absence is especially important because Prescience processes claims, prescriptions, health records, financial information, and connected Gmail documents without a completed SOC 2 examination. Capital spent on national distribution before resolving validation and security would remove little risk.

The economics are directionally intelligent. Charging no administrative fee and earning money only when employers save aligns Prescience with customers and could undercut incumbents. But no fee percentage, contract minimum, gross margin, savings methodology, or operating-cost model is public. Human-physician escalation may be a necessary safety control, but its frequency determines whether this becomes scalable intelligence or expensive care navigation. Alignment is not yet cost parity.

I pass today. The strongest exception case is a technically credible, unusually relevant founding pair attacking an undeniable trillion-dollar value pool through a regulated operating wedge; that combination can justify buying an option before revenue is visible. It does not override the missing technical discontinuity and risk-removal plan. One result flips my decision: an independently auditable study across at least 10,000 covered lives showing 20% or greater risk-adjusted medical-cost reduction with non-inferior clinical outcomes over twelve months.
