# COACH -- Elad Gil Evaluation

$500,000 ARR after five months is the signature: COACH has a **Harvey-like revenue-velocity signal but a Lyft-like distribution problem**, because 120% month-over-month growth is “just working” while Rilla already occupies the same field-conversation workflow at an estimated $70 million ARR. COACH is selling successfully into a compounding category, but COACH does not yet look like an index on that category; it looks like a fast-moving application fighting an incumbent for share.

**Harvey** is the high rung on revenue velocity because dollars, not pilot enthusiasm, forced the market to keep repricing the outcome. COACH shares that mechanism: $500,000 ARR in five months, 120% monthly growth, 100% pilot conversion, and 0% churn indicate actual buying behavior rather than enterprise-pilot tourism. COACH diverges from Harvey because every metric is founder-reported without customer counts, cohort definitions, or measurement periods; I would still pay up for the curve, but I would verify the invoices before calling it fully “just working.”

**Lyft** is the low-rung failure mechanism on index position: entering a networked market as an excellent number two does not give number-one economics. COACH faces a cleaner version of that concern because Rilla already records, transcribes, analyzes, and coaches in-person sales conversations, with $78.9 million raised and an estimated $70 million ARR. COACH’s customer-specific closer comparisons and shared knowledge base may create switching costs, but COACH currently must displace Rilla account by account rather than becoming infrastructure every field-sales entrant automatically touches.

2026-era transcription models and agent harnesses crossed a useful capability threshold, but COACH is exploiting a technological improvement inside a market Rilla has already validated rather than reopening a market peers still consider dead. Over 18 months, COACH’s catalyst can compound through lower analysis costs, faster deployment, and better team-specific coaching, yet those same model improvements accrue to Rilla and Gong. COACH therefore sits above a steady-state SaaS market but below the Stripe-style re-opening where a new interface changes who can enter and how distribution works.

Mathieu and Thomas Perez previously built Alsace Test to €3.5 million in revenue within 14 months, then worked alongside Réseau ACEH representatives in door-to-door and in-home meetings; Nicolas Sebag contributes ten years of software work and mobile-development experience. COACH’s six pivots and the shutdown of Lucy could be thrashing, but the current $500,000 ARR makes the more useful interpretation rapid iteration toward demand. COACH would move into the top founder-shipping rung if two product releases tied to customer feedback and retention were documented; absent that, prior shipping and current commercial execution are strong without proving the Perplexity-style week-by-week build rhythm.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow infrastructure on top of a validated hard capability is better than underwriting the hard capability itself. COACH assumes transcription and LLM analysis work, then concentrates execution risk in recording procurement, coaching quality, and enterprise distribution; COACH does not require new science. Consent management, security approval, behavior change by representatives, and differentiation from Rilla are multiple execution dependencies, but only the recording-data constraint has the potential to become a separate market miracle.

$500,000 ARR compounding from a small base makes my 36-month forecast that COACH reaches **$12 million–$20 million ARR and is acquired by Gong**, rather than becoming the independent index on field-sales intelligence. COACH can produce that outcome by converting forward-deployed pilots, accumulating customer-specific conversation data, and selling measurable conversion lift, while Gong uses COACH to close its face-to-face coverage gap before Rilla becomes too expensive to buy.

Rilla’s estimated $70 million ARR is the strongest bear case: COACH may be a late clone whose 120% growth, 32% conversion lift, 100% pilot conversion, and 0% churn all dissolve once cohort definitions are exposed, while recording consent and security slow deployment. COACH survives that objection because €3.5 million at Alsace Test, $500,000 ARR in five months, and direct field-selling experience form a coherent execution pattern rather than a pedigree narrative. I would flip to Pass if verified billing cohorts show COACH below 100% net revenue retention or below 15% month-over-month ARR growth for two consecutive quarters.
