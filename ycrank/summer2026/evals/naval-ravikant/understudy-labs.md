# Understudy Labs -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The founders have specific knowledge earned through action. Aamir Poonawalla spent roughly ten years building Instacart’s ads-serving, ML, auction, and experimentation infrastructure; Luis Manrique worked in advertising ML and AI products, then closed approximately $2 million during Gumloop’s first year. They met while building optimization systems at Instacart. This is not résumé adjacency. They have built and sold inside the exact loop Understudy automates: observe production behavior, evaluate alternatives, and allocate traffic.

The product is also more than a landing page. Understudy captures traces, creates task-specific evaluations, trains smaller open-weight models, and promotes them only after held-out tests. Its published benchmark covers four reasoning tasks and seven CRM actions, with ten runs per promoted configuration. The negative result—that generic adapters failed to transfer cleanly across slices—is credible technical evidence because the team exposed a limitation and built fallback routing around it.

But execution is not durability. If Fireworks, Together AI, Baseten, or an upstream model provider copies this workflow, Understudy currently owns no scarce substrate. Customer payloads cannot train or evaluate another customer’s models. Aggregated operational metadata may improve routing and reliability, but no deployment volume shows that it compounds. Customer-specific traces can become valuable to the customer without becoming a moat for Understudy. The code creates savings; it does not yet create ownership.

The unstated-pattern test is weaker still. The MIT toolkit is permissionless in distribution, but 12 GitHub stars and five forks do not demonstrate that one participant improves the product for another. Private-preview customers require engineer-assisted implementation. This is presently a services-assisted optimization product, not a network accumulating value while the company sleeps. It also sits in infrastructure without controlling hardware, a proprietary model, or a physical bottleneck.

The market is large but the wedge-to-platform path remains asserted. ModelOps is projected to grow from $7.73 billion in 2025 to $43.60 billion in 2030, yet no workload-specific SAM or defensible adjacent transaction is identified. Pricing exists through order forms, invoices, and written agreements, but the dossier gives no payer, price, completed transaction, or revenue. A small unnamed design-partner group proves access, not value capture.

The strongest opposing case is that unusually domain-matched founders can turn a copyable first product into the control plane for replacing frontier models. The known-exception analogy is Notion: a demonstrated prototype and capital-efficient builders justified investment before a user-distribution loop existed. The shared mechanism is founders developing a product language before the category settles; the material difference is that Understudy faces several heavily financed platforms already selling adjacent infrastructure, while its benchmark and metadata do not yet establish an asset they cannot reproduce. I see no disclosed integrity, artificial-deadline, or founder-dependence red flag, but absence of a veto is not an invest signal. I remain neutral and do not write the check today. I flip if Understudy reaches $1 million ARR from at least ten customers while demonstrating that permitted cross-deployment metadata measurably improves routing performance.
