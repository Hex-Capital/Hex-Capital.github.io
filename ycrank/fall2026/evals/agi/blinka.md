# Blinka -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Blinka supports neither AGI path. Its USD accounts and cross-border payment routes serve businesses operating internationally. The memo identifies no AI workload that depends on these services and no scarce input that Blinka supplies to AI deployments. The industry-enabled path also scores zero. Blinka says that founders of U.S.-incorporated businesses operating abroad face account rejections and can lose 4–5% to FX. Its disclosed approach addresses that friction through remote account opening with Portage Bank, stablecoin settlement through Mural Pay, and local payout routes. The memo describes no AI-driven reduction in the cost of serving these businesses. (*The Idea, Key Risks*)

For the banking business, the memo reports more than 400 businesses onboarded in the first two months and more than $18 million in payment volume. That supports early demand, with repeat paid use and the share of customers previously denied banking still unverified. The founders shipped the current product after several pivots, and the CTO brings credit-risk modeling experience. Dependable delivery through payout disruptions remains unverified. (*Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks*)

Blinka depends on partners for deposits, card issuance, and stablecoin settlement, while competitors offer overlapping payment and finance workflows. Vendor records and accounting integrations could increase switching costs, but the memo provides no retention or adoption data across products. Disclosed fees and reported payment volume leave the revenue and delivery cost of the next successful payout unknown, so scale economics stays at the absent-evidence default. (*Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Blinka may already be opening a market for U.S.-incorporated businesses that operate abroad. Mercury’s U.S. operations requirement gives that access gap a concrete basis, and Blinka’s reported onboarding and payment volume suggest customers are using its alternative. If manual compliance review makes some of those accounts uneconomic to serve, AI could make the segment viable. The memo does not establish that manual review is the binding constraint or that Blinka uses AI to lower its cost. The zero-path Pass gate applies. (*The Idea, Market & Traction*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can AI-assisted compliance review bring the fully loaded cost of serving previously uneconomic accounts below the revenue those accounts generate? A pilot comparing similar applicants under the same bank eligibility rules could answer this by measuring review costs, exception handling, and repeat paid payouts. If AI review met the bank’s compliance requirements and crossed that economic threshold, I would move to Reach out. Blinka’s partner-backed accounts and payout routes would still be needed to deliver the service. (*The Idea, Key Risks*)
