# Hesper AI -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Hesper’s strongest path is **New Economies**. Automating record collection, provider verification, and evidence reconciliation could make it economical to investigate claims that insurers currently skip. Hesper puts outsourced investigations at $1,500–$2,500 per claim, and a founder reports 82% less average adjuster time per claim over two weeks at one MGA. That result supports the labor-saving mechanism. The memo leaves open which claims were previously skipped, whether investigation quality held up, and how much the all-in cost fell per completed investigation that met the buyer’s quality standard. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Hesper reports paying production customers and pilots with carriers, MGAs, and TPAs. Those are early demand signals for investigations. The memo does not establish repeat payment for newly viable investigations or conversion after the free-claim offer. The team’s prior document-automation and insurance sales experience, plus the rebuild after its fraud-scoring product found no buyers, support its ability to ship and adapt. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, The Idea)

The expansion unit is a claim investigation. In a scenario where an MGA adds investigations for claims it previously skipped, the all-in cost per accepted case must fit what the MGA can justify spending. That cost includes Hesper’s fee and the buyer’s review and rework. Hesper’s own contribution depends on whether that fee covers model calls, external data, and any human exception handling across all attempts. The memo describes usage-based pricing and adjuster sign-off, but gives no realized price, exception rate, or delivery cost. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Hesper may be making an existing claims workflow cheaper while claims platforms absorb the same work. Its integrations and source-linked findings fit existing adjuster workflows, and carrier-specific rules could make switching more costly. Duck Creek’s native agent infrastructure gives buyers a credible alternative. The memo documents neither buyer preference at functional parity nor a network effect or controlled scarce asset. The market-opening and model-parity gates remain unproven. There is no observed parity-driven switching that would close the path, so the action is Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent MGAs repeatedly pay Hesper at sustainable prices to investigate claims they previously skipped, when a functionally comparable option is available? A paid cohort with documented prior triage decisions, buyer-verified investigation quality, all-in costs per accepted case, and renewal after a side-by-side comparison would answer it. Reach out if the cohort establishes both newly viable investigations and a sustained preference for Hesper. Pass if the cohort shows savings confined to claims already investigated or buyers readily switch at parity.
