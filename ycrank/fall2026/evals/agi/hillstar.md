# HillStar -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

New Economies is HillStar’s strongest path. Utility teams manually read technical proposals and reconcile pricing sheets. HillStar reports processing 22 bids totaling about 19,800 pages in nine minutes on an engineering-services RFP. That supports a reduction in first-pass review effort. The memo gives no comparable baseline for staff hours or all-in cost per completed, committee-ready evaluation. A plausible newly viable use would be a municipal utility running a competitive solicitation for a smaller equipment purchase it previously skipped because review took too much staff time. That remains a scenario assumption. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

HillStar’s company-reported municipal deployment, where it flagged a missing signed price certification before shortlisting, shows use in a live procurement decision. Payment, renewal, and demand from previously excluded buyers remain unverified. Behroyan’s utility infrastructure experience and Stone’s record building construction and geothermal software support seed-stage execution. Source-linked findings and committee review provide a way to test whether the system catches mandatory requirements. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

The unit of expansion is one completed solicitation. HillStar reports generating a 19-section solicitation package from a scope of work, suggesting that automation could reduce drafting effort as well as review effort. Contribution at scale depends on the fee per event and the cost of setup, model processing, specialist review, and exceptions. The memo leaves those economics unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

HillStar may make existing RFPs faster while buyers keep procurement in their current systems. Enverus already embeds AI bid analysis in its sourcing suite. HillStar says it retains buyer-specific judgments and award rationale, which could make repeat events easier, but the memo shows no buyer preference at comparable functionality or demonstrated switching cost. HillStar’s documented role is bid evaluation, with no evidence that it controls a scarce input to power or equipment procurement. Feature overlap raises a credible substitution risk. The memo establishes neither actual switching nor a direct test of market opening, so the decision gate is Watch. (Defensibility, Key Risks, The Idea)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent utilities pay HillStar repeatedly for solicitations they previously skipped when an alternative offers comparable bid-analysis quality? Move to Reach out if a paid cohort documents those previously skipped events, a lower all-in cost per committee-ready evaluation at comparable compliance accuracy, and repeat selection of HillStar at a price that covers its delivery costs. Keep Watch if the comparison is inconclusive. Move to Pass if the test shows buyers gain convenience on existing events and readily switch at parity.
