# Florin -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first problem is not underwriting; it is underwriting the round. Florin discloses no post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is a genuinely scarce first check. That earns the financing default, not the benefit of the doubt. The capital plan is equally undefined: “zero underwriters” is a product claim, but there is no named use of proceeds, dated experiment, or pass/fail milestone showing what this round will prove.

The potentially valuable experiment is legible. Commercial robotics, space, energy, and defense companies often fit rate tables badly, and Florin proposes converting ACORD forms, permits, imagery, public records, and social evidence into a bindable quote in under one minute. But the hard question is not whether software can produce a quote. It is whether an insurer can price unusual risks without humans and remain solvent after claims arrive. Florin publishes no policies bound, actuarial validation, loss ratio, reinsurance arrangement, or carrier authority. Simulating tens of thousands of policy years is not evidence about one real policy year.

The team is the strongest part of the case. Shaurya Aggarwal shipped Google TPU work and encryption deployed across more than 100,000 hosts; Amol Pant built perception and compute systems across Orchard Robotics, Tesla, NASA, and Boeing; Aydin Sorensen reports scaling TerraFirma from $500,000 to $30 million in realized revenue while supporting more than 50 teleoperated vehicles. Those are real operating receipts on the physical-technology side of the bridge. The missing side is insurance: no founder has disclosed carrier management, actuarial, claims, regulatory, or reinsurance experience. This is an outsider team with technical access, not yet an outsider-insider translation layer.

The bull case stings because these backgrounds may let Florin distinguish physical risk that legacy class codes flatten, and proprietary policy-and-claims data could eventually become a defensible dark pool. The market is enormous, and established competitors have collectively raised more than $800 million, which confirms that automated commercial underwriting is commercially important. But it also means the public thesis is already crowded: Coterie already offers sub-one-minute binding, while Corgi, Shepherd, and Nirvana bring operating data, capital, and narrower risk specialization. Florin currently owns no disclosed data, workflow, broker channel, or customer relationship that has caused a purchase.

Deleting the next financing makes the structure look worse. A claimed full-stack carrier needs licensing, risk capital, claims operations, and reinsurance, yet Florin discloses none of them and provides no premium, margin, burn, runway, or break-even model. The recent shift from startup banking to insurance further weakens the claim that this round is a focused, truth-revealing experiment. Three equal-status founders without disclosed decision ownership add another unresolved governance question precisely where regulatory and underwriting failures require fast accountability.

I expect Florin to pivot within 36 months from being a full-stack carrier to selling underwriting or submission software to licensed insurers or MGAs. The technical team is credible enough to build the workflow, but the dossier does not establish the institutional access required to hold the risk. I would reverse this pass if Florin disclosed one real cohort of at least 50 paid policies bound under named licensed capacity, including earned premium and an independently reviewable loss-ratio result. That would turn the model from an AI assertion into operating evidence.
