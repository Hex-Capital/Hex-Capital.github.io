# Skymerse -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Damian Szumski is a licensed flight dispatcher who previously owned Flightradar24’s API product, and that makes Skymerse a domain-missionary business without a founder-distribution asset. Skymerse’s highest dimension is asymmetric founder conviction: Szumski has lived across LOT, FLYR, Lufthansa Systems, and Flightradar24 rather than discovering aviation agents during YC. Skymerse’s lowest dimension is founder-as-brand: no public writing, personal audience, or community predates the product. The analytical signature is an execution-heavy aviation asset whose founder can build the machine but cannot personally pull demand through it.

**Drop.io** is the high rung on founder conviction because I built against free file-sharing incumbents before the market supplied an obvious playbook. Skymerse shares that lived-problem mechanism: Szumski’s dispatcher license, aeronautics degree, and operational roles make three million processed NOTAMs look like accumulated obsession rather than a demo-day feature. Skymerse diverges from Drop.io because there is no public writing exposing Szumski’s non-obvious belief, and the dossier does not establish three years spent specifically building Skymerse. The career is missionary evidence; the public intellectual artifact is missing. That places Skymerse near the high rung, but not on it.

**Sublime Security’s** low-rung mechanism on founder-as-brand is enterprise distribution without a creator or community asset; excellent operators can still win, but their names do not sit on the cap table as acquisition channels. Skymerse shows the same absence more sharply: Szumski has no discoverable personal account or public repositories, while Skymerse has only 134 LinkedIn followers. Notamify’s 3,500 registered users belong to a product utility, not a Szumski-centered community. Skymerse therefore gets no John Fish-style distribution premium and must buy every enterprise relationship through integrations, pilots, and trust.

Skymerse’s unknown round price is the central underwriting failure because price compounds through every regatta tack. Aviation operations is not the canonical hot AI-agent deck, but the “AI” tag, Jeppesen’s Airflow launch, and Air Space Intelligence’s $34 million financing create a plausible path from illegible niche to consensus auction within 18 months. Skymerse could earn pricing power if LOT Polish Airlines converts into repeatable deployments; Skymerse could also burn that advantage by raising as generic aviation AI before revenue makes the asset legible. No round terms or visible tier-one passes are disclosed, so I apply the absent-evidence default with only a modest non-consensus premium.

Notamify’s $24.90 monthly Pro plan and custom enterprise contracts make Skymerse more structurally interesting than a blank enterprise SAFE. For the capital-structure score to flip into the top tier, Skymerse would need to show that Notamify’s thousands of daily users fund most product development while airline pilots produce expansion revenue without five successive rounds. Today the three million processed NOTAMs provide a downside cushion, but revenue, burn, retention, and enterprise contract values are undisclosed. Skymerse looks bootstrappable and venture-acceleratable; Skymerse has not yet proved it is default-alive rather than a SaaS cap table financing years of safety certification and integrations.

**Sublime Security** is the useful single-anchor parallel for category fit: unsexy, high-consequence enterprise infrastructure can become attractive before consolidation makes it obvious. Skymerse has that same operational grit through LOT’s production deployment, operator-specific manuals, and a custom aeronautical model. Skymerse does not sit inside my crypto, DTC, creator, or holdco sleeves, but it does touch the Wizard-of-Oz pattern—first solve the narrow NOTAM workflow, then automate the broader operation. The Ro exception is relevant because regulatory complexity can become the venture moment; it is not yet controlling because Skymerse’s terms prohibit using its output as the sole authoritative basis for safety-critical decisions.

Jeppesen/ForeFlight will acquire Skymerse within 36 months for less than $75 million after Skymerse reaches roughly $2 million to $4 million in ARR, primarily from cargo, business-aviation, and regional-airline operators. Skymerse’s founder depth should produce a credible operational decision layer, while Skymerse’s weak owned distribution and dependence on incumbent-controlled integrations make acquisition more likely than an independent platform outcome. The machine becomes valuable; the channel captures it.

LOT Polish Airlines is the strongest objection to that forecast: Skymerse already has a named airline deployment, 3,500 registered users, thousands of daily users, and three million processed NOTAMs before the broader product is mature. The bull mechanism is a Ro-style regulatory moat—each operator manual and correction deepens proprietary workflow data until Jeppesen cannot reproduce Skymerse merely by exposing Airflow APIs. The evidence that still wins is Notamify’s explicit requirement for independent verification, combined with public traction being attributed mainly to Notamify rather than Skymerse’s planning-to-cockpit system. I would not write my own check on undisclosed terms. A verified round at or below a $12 million post-money valuation would flip the verdict to Invest because price would compensate for the channel and authority risks.
