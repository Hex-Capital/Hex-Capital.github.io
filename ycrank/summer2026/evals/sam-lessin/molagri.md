# Molagri -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I first want the missing numbers: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and the size and terms of Julian Capital’s check. YC backing does not establish that capital is scarce, and financing silence earns no presumption that I am buying a cheap, non-consensus belief. Molagri may be an unfashionable industrial-biotech bet, but without terms I cannot distinguish that from a fashionable synthetic-biology company whose technical uncertainty has already been priced as success.

The product contains a legitimate venture experiment, but the round does not name it. Molagri has produced its first virus-like particles and solved a honey-bee receptor structure; the obvious test is whether one formulation can kill a target pest in field-relevant conditions without harming pollinators. Yet there is no dated efficacy threshold, dose, manufacturing-cost target, or regulatory milestone tied to the proceeds. “Hire researchers and advance the platform” would finance activity. It would not tell me which card this check flips.

The founders are the strongest evidence. Zaky Hassan and Min Jin spent six years together studying virus-host interactions, published in serious journals, and converted cryo-EM and protein-biochemistry expertise into test material and a receptor structure during the YC batch. They have shipped scientific work on the molecular side of the bridge. They have not shown comparable operating receipts from agriculture: no field deployment, pesticide registration, fermentation process at commercial yield, or crop-science product launch. This is a powerful one-sided translation team, not yet a team proven on both sides.

The bull case stings because the modular targeting-and-payload architecture could turn today’s missing data into tomorrow’s proprietary dark pool. One signed grower LOI gives Molagri more than academic interest, and competitors have spent hundreds of millions validating biological crop protection as a real category. But the LOI does not show that proprietary receptor data caused a purchase, while Vestaron has treated more than one million acres and GreenLight already has a registered product. The exception to my price gate requires hard external receipts; Molagri currently has neither field efficacy nor paid adoption strong enough to supply that override.

Deleting the next financing makes the problem sharper. There is no revenue, pricing, gross margin, burn, fermentation yield, shelf-life result, or registration budget. In regulated crop protection, those omissions prevent a credible one-and-done model; the company may need several expensive technical and regulatory steps before customers can buy anything. My base case by August 2029 is that Molagri remains below $1 million in annual product revenue and raises—or is seeking—another institutional round to finance field trials and registration rather than operating from product cash flow.

I am passing today. The verifiable flip condition is one paid, independently measured grower field trial that meets prespecified pest-efficacy and pollinator-safety thresholds at a disclosed production cost below the contracted selling price. That single result would connect the founders’ technical receipts to purchase behavior and reveal whether this is a product business rather than a capital-intensive research program.
