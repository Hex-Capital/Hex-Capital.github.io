# OpenRelay -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I first need three numbers the dossier does not provide: post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce outside check. Financing silence earns 6/28, not the benefit of the doubt. OpenRelay sits in the hottest capital market on earth, against RunPod, Together AI, Lambda, and Vast.ai. A cheap check into an unfashionable routing layer could be interesting; an auction-priced check into “AI infrastructure” is simply consensus wearing technical clothing.

The strongest fact is not the market forecast. It is that two people already operate across 22 physical locations, four continents, 11 accelerator SKUs, and more than 100 billion tokens per week. Prashant Patel shipped Amazon Bedrock’s Custom Model Import to multi-million-dollar ARR and later supported 10,000-plus-GPU clusters at Voltage Park. Jaden Wang built a half-megawatt data center, worked at TensorDock and Voltage Park, and deployed virtualization and HPC infrastructure. This is a real outsider-insider translation layer: cloud inference software on one side, fragmented physical capacity on the other. They have shipped on both.

The product also contains the beginning of a proprietary dark pool. Continuous benchmarks across dissimilar accelerators can produce cost, latency, failure, and model-performance data unavailable to a customer using one provider. Production usage and a reported B300 deployment cutting infrastructure cost by more than 25% show customer action rather than follower theater. But OpenRelay has not disclosed provider count, paying-customer count, retention, revenue, or how much of the 100 billion-token volume is external and recurring. An OpenAI-compatible endpoint makes adoption easier while making departure easier too. Without evidence that routing data measurably improves with network density, this may be a useful broker rather than a compounding network.

The bull case stings: two founders have built an operating system at implausible pre-seed scale, while heavily financed competitors remain tied to their own clouds or expose infrastructure choices users must manage. Hardware fragmentation is increasing, and OpenRelay could become the neutral control plane precisely because it does not own a single accelerator thesis. The problem is that the dossier names no finite capital-funded experiment. “Recruit providers, sell enterprise demos, and grow usage” is a business plan, not a venture moment. Dedicated VMs still lack automatic migration, SOC 2 Type II is unfinished, and hosted-inference failover is narrower than the headline. The check must flip one card—perhaps proving automatic cross-provider recovery for a defined enterprise workload by a dated milestone—not finance five simultaneous infrastructure ambitions.

Delete the Series A. The company publishes token and GPU prices but no gross margin, burn, runway, contribution economics, or route to break-even. Capacity arbitrage can look wonderful at the endpoint while leaking cash through idle reservations, provider payments, support, and failure handling. My verifiable flip condition is three consecutive months showing at least five unrelated paying customers, positive contribution margin after provider and failover costs, and over 90% retained token volume, paired with a disclosed sub-$10 million post-money round financing one dated reliability test. Without that package, I am passing despite unusually strong technical receipts: the founders have proved they can build the bridge, but not yet that this financing is scarce, finite, or economically one-and-done.
