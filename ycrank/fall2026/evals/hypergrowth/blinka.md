# Blinka -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** The initial buyer is a U.S.-incorporated business that operates abroad and regularly pays overseas teams or vendors. Blinka documents a remote USD account application through Portage Bank and local payout routes that use Mural Pay for stablecoin settlement. It solicits referrals from accountants, lawyers, and incorporation services. If those partners can repeatedly send eligible buyers through the same application to a first payout, subsequent payment cycles and adoption of cards or invoicing could grow each account. The referral channel is documented. Its conversion rate and the work required to activate a customer are unknown. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Blinka reports more than 400 businesses onboarded in the first two months, more than $18 million in cumulative payment volume by November 4, and mostly organic customer growth. It names customers including Bricksave, Dapta, and Creditop. For a newly launched product, onboarding and reported payment activity are concrete early signals of fit. The memo gives no account-level evidence of repeat use, paid expansion, or which channel brought in the businesses that transacted. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Second-market scenario.** If a cohort of Mexico-based operators has the same U.S. entity structure and meets Portage’s eligibility requirements, Blinka could reuse the USD account and application. The Mexico Tech Week sponsorship shows outreach. It leaves local payout reliability, compliance review, and support effort untested at the cohort level. The contribution test would start with realized revenue from those accounts and subtract referral or sales effort, onboarding and compliance work, partner and settlement charges, and support for payout exceptions. The memo provides fee terms but no realized revenue or incremental cost breakdown. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The CEO says reported account closures at other U.S. platforms prompted Blinka to bring its launch forward. That could explain a burst of urgent account openings without establishing that buyers will route routine payments through Blinka. Cumulative volume could also be concentrated among a few accounts. Disclosed card terms charge 0% for FX and cross-border fees, while every new corridor depends on payout availability and partner execution. If the next cohort requires manual eligibility reviews and frequent settlement support, volume may grow faster than contribution. The memo establishes those dependencies but leaves their marginal cost unresolved, so the structural Pass gate is unmet. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a Mexico-based cohort of U.S.-incorporated businesses sourced by one referral partner activate through the existing application and generate positive incremental contribution from repeat payouts? A cohort record showing who applied, who activated without bespoke founder help, who transacted again on their normal payment cycle, and what revenue remained after acquisition and delivery costs would answer it. A successful cohort would move Blinka to **Reach out**.
