# Polemos Industries -- Paul Graham Evaluation

What have Polemos Industries’ founders done when something refused to work? The dossier cannot answer that. It identifies a three-person team but no founders, prior work, or episode in which an obstacle forced a shipped workaround. That leaves my most important test unobserved. It does not mean the founders are passive. It means I cannot yet distinguish unusually resourceful builders from three people describing something unusually difficult.

The strongest case for Polemos is that affordability might come from choosing a simpler weapon and manufacturing route. Its proposed short-range ballistic missiles, compact launchers, and connections to existing reconnaissance drones describe a more specific approach than “AI for defense.” Using existing production lines could avoid the mistake of having to invent both a product and its factory. But these remain company descriptions. There is no documented working artifact, measured performance, unit cost, production yield, or manufacturing agreement. The technical score therefore stays at its missing-evidence default. A proposed shortcut becomes evidence when someone has actually taken it.

There is also a plausible urgent customer problem: armed forces may locate more targets than they can afford to engage. The March 25, 2026 agreement intended to quadruple PrSM production capacity supports the broader need for more supply. It establishes no pull for Polemos specifically. The October 23 blog coverage and zero advertised jobs tell me nothing about whether an operator wants this crude product badly enough to keep using it. Neither does the broader $44.3 billion market estimate. A named customer repeatedly using an early system would answer a question that those figures cannot.

The manufacturing idea is the objection that most challenges my pass. If the founders have already made an existing production process work for their product, the sparse public record could conceal the decisive accomplishment. But Ares also advertises simplified manufacturing and commercial components, so affordability alone does not establish that accomplishment. My unstated preference for concrete proof before a round is unfulfilled here. So is the preference for a difficult substrate already assembled. The known exceptions do not rescue the case: none of the working-product, unmistakable-demand, or independently validated proof that would justify an override appears in this dossier.

I would not infer the usual red flags from silence. Polemos has not been shown waiting for a partner, delegating the hard work, or dismissing contrary evidence. There is simply no documented product revision, so adaptability receives its default. Manufacturing, integration, and export authorization are plausible sources of neglected work, but there is no founder history connecting this team to those burdens; problem origin also receives its default. I would pass today. One verifiable condition would change that decision: a dated, customer-confirmed deployment record showing a named blocker, the founders’ shipped workaround, and repeat use after the fix. That would connect the people, the product, and the need.
