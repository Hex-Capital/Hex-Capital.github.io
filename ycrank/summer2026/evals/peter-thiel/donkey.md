# Donkey -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Prelude OS acquired 31 paying Chinese factory customers in 39 days, making Donkey a commercially proven operator bet whose strongest dimension is founder pedigree and whose weakest is definite planning. Benjamin Martindale’s Mandarin-language factory work and Minghao Tan’s customs-data engineering are real, adjacent experience. Donkey nevertheless offers no named two-to-five-year milestones, architectural commitments, or destination beyond executing today’s importer-of-record workflow. Donkey has operators who know how to move goods; it has not yet shown that they know precisely what monopoly they intend to build.

**Affirm** is the high rung on founder lineage: Max Levchin had already shipped PayPal to global scale before re-entering financial infrastructure. Donkey shares the re-up mechanism—Martindale and Tan built Prelude together, won 31 customers, and reached cash-flow positivity—but Donkey diverges sharply on magnitude. Prelude was a rapid commercial experiment, not a scaled institution. Donkey sits above the generic first-time YC founder and below the operator-pedigree exception that justified Affirm or Asana; 31 customers demonstrate sales competence, not power-law inevitability.

**Quora** is the low rung on definite planning: exceptional pedigree could not make consumer Q&A’s roadmap definite. Donkey exhibits the same axis failure without Quora’s Facebook CTO override. Donkey can describe the next transaction—identify a factory, quote within 72 hours, inspect, take title, clear customs—but not the next five years. Tariffs changing after November 10, 2026 may reward operational agility, yet agility is not a plan. Definite optimism names the destination; Donkey currently names a workflow.

ImportYeti’s 70 million bills of lading expose Donkey’s partial secret over the next 18 months. Donkey believes shipment data becomes economically valuable only when someone assumes counterparty, inspection, customs, and delivery risk; trading companies preserve 15–30% markups because opacity is their business model, not an accidental defect. That is better than the red-flag phrase “AI-native trading company.” But Donkey names an incumbent class rather than a specific incumbent whose architecture prevents adaptation, while ImportYeti, Sourcify, and Jiga demonstrate that the surrounding insight is already mimetic. The secret compounds only if Donkey’s settled orders reveal supplier reliability that public filings cannot.

Donkey’s dossier identifies no structural barrier, which fixes the monopoly score below the investable range. For Donkey to flip tiers, accumulated pricing, defect, and delivery records would need to make its quotes measurably more accurate while repeat importers become unwilling to switch because Donkey’s first-sale documentation and supplier history are uniquely reliable. Today, inspectors across seven provinces can be hired, public shipment records can be searched, and embedded margins can be undercut. Donkey may become an efficient merchant, but efficiency without exclusion produces competition—and competition is for losers.

**Palantir** is the relevant single-anchor parallel for Donkey’s regulated-sector moat. Palantir converted difficult government integration and institutional trust into years of switching costs; Donkey could convert CBP first-sale documentation, importer-of-record liability, credit insurance, and factory inspection into a similar compliance memory at smaller scale. Donkey is atoms-adjacent rather than atoms-deep: it takes title to physical goods and bears transit and buyer-default exposure, but owns no factory, certification regime, or irreplaceable infrastructure. Regulation creates friction for Donkey; the dossier does not yet show regulation creating monopoly.

Net-45 terms and factory ownership through delivery should let Donkey win meaningful volume while simultaneously limiting its power-law shape. By July 2029, Donkey will process $20–40 million of annualized GMV, but more than half of its gross profit will remain dependent on human inspection and trade operations, leaving Donkey a technology-enabled trading company rather than a software monopoly. Prelude’s sales velocity supports the volume forecast; working capital, customs evidence, quality disputes, and China concentration produce the operational ceiling.

Seven Chinese provinces, 31 prior paying customers, and a founder driving from Vermont to San Francisco form the strongest opposing case: Donkey may be the Asana-style known exception where verified execution overrides a missing definite plan, while proprietary order data quietly creates the moat. Donkey’s bull mechanism is credible—each completed shipment could improve factory attribution, landed-cost accuracy, and underwriting until competitors cannot quote safely. The objection fails today because Donkey discloses no current order volume, repeat rate, prediction accuracy, or evidence that its nine-person operation gains leverage with scale. A cohort of 50 Donkey buyers achieving more than 70% repeat ordering without proportional growth in trade-operations headcount would flip my verdict. Until then: neutral, and no personal check.
