# Rational -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Rational processes roughly 12,000 invoices per cycle for a Fortune Global 500 customer despite having founders with no professional accounting background. That pairing is Rational’s signature: unusually deep schlep exposure attached to an idea that does not appear organic to either founder’s own life. Rational has found unpleasant work, but the founders seem to have found it by searching rather than by tripping over it themselves. Search-derived ideas can work. They simply lack the strange inevitability Dropbox had when Drew Houston kept losing USB sticks.

**Coinbase** (2012) is the high rung for schlep blindness: compliance was not incidental work but the work everyone else avoided. Rational has a similar pile of ugliness in its 300-plus integrations, missing-document follow-ups, approval gates, audit trails, accounting liability, and customer-data isolation. Rational diverges because Brian Armstrong personally ran into bitcoin’s institutional friction, whereas neither Jibril Moinuddin nor Christ Xu practiced accounting. Still, supporting 12,000 invoices per cycle means Rational has entered the machinery rather than merely drawing a picture of it. I score the schlep highly because the boring exceptions will accumulate faster than competitors expect.

**Loopt** (2005) is the low-rung exception: force of personality overrode weak product timing, rather than an organic problem producing the product. Rational shows the same weak origin signal without a documented Sam Altman-level override. Xu’s machine-learning experience explains why Rational can build agents, and Moinuddin’s operating history explains how Rational can sell them, but neither biography says, “I suffered through this workflow and built the tool I needed.” Rational’s broad “AI employees for accounting” framing also resembles a market selected after frontier models improved. The Fortune Global 500 deployment prevents this from being merely an AI-wrapper pitch, but it does not make the idea organic.

Approximately 20,000 students reportedly passed through Moinuddin’s earlier STEMplicity operation, Xu reportedly had a $400,000 prior exit, and Rational already supports about 50 people at Tenace International. Those are animal tracks. Over the next 18 months, Rational’s resourcefulness should compound if the founders personally inspect failed reconciliations, chase missing invoices, and turn every exception into software. It will fail to compound if Rational responds to enterprise friction by hiring implementation managers. The dossier proves that the founders ship and obtain access to serious customers; it does not yet contain an Airbnb-style stunt showing what Rational’s founders do when the obvious route stops working.

Jibril Moinuddin and Christ Xu both attended the University of Michigan, but Rational provides no account of how long they have built together. Rational gets credit for being a complementary pair: Moinuddin has founded and sold, while Xu has engineered at Tencent, Jane Street, and OpenAI. The score would flip upward if the founders had worked together for two years before Rational or could name a pre-Rational artifact they built jointly. It would flip downward if they met specifically to apply to YC. Cofounder chemistry is one of the few things a list of credentials cannot reveal.

**Stripe** (2010) is the useful single parallel for hacker genealogy. Patrick and John Collison’s earlier Auctomatic work proved that they could build difficult infrastructure before Stripe existed. Xu’s algorithm research, OpenAI internship, Jane Street ML work, Tencent engineering role, and reported $400,000 exit give Rational real technical ancestry of that sort. Rational lacks the public GitHub trail, Lisp oddity, or hacker-community artifact that made the Collisons unusually legible, but Xu is not technical-by-credential-only: the prior exit is evidence that code escaped the laboratory.

More than 300 integrations and firm-specific approval histories should push Rational toward a narrower, messier product than the phrase “AI employees” suggests. My 36-month prediction is that by July 2029 Rational reaches $8 million to $15 million in ARR as an independent vendor focused mainly on supervised accounts-payable and close workflows, rather than remaining a general accounting employee. Rational gets there by converting exceptions into workflow memory: every invoice discrepancy and human correction makes Rational harder to remove from a firm. This is less like inventing a new category than burrowing into an old one, which is often what a good schlep looks like.

Basis has raised at least $134 million, Numeric has raised $89 million, and Rational has disclosed no revenue. The strongest bear case is painful: Rational may be a capable pair entering a consensus AI market with no organic founder insight, no public hacker artifact, and no structural barrier preventing Basis or accounting incumbents from reproducing the same approval workflows. But Rational’s 12,000 invoices per cycle for a Fortune Global 500 customer is harder evidence than a generic demo, and Rational’s 300-plus integrations suggest the founders have already accepted work a thin wrapper would avoid. I would invest, cautiously. If Rational cannot convert at least three design partners into paid annual contracts above $100,000 each by July 2027, I would conclude the deployments were demonstrations rather than customer pull and change the verdict to Pass.
