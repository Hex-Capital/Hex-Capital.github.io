# Marble -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers are missing: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. I cannot infer cheapness from “pre-seed,” and YC affiliation does not prove an auction. Marble therefore clears neither side of the price gate. The same problem applies to the capital plan: there is no named use of proceeds, dated milestone, or finite experiment that tells me what new truth this round buys.

The strongest evidence is inside the restaurants. Marble is live with six named groups, including A&W Restaurants and The Kati Roll Company, and one customer reports 40% lower food waste, eliminated stockouts, and more than 30 manager hours saved each week. Computer-vision counts feeding forecasts, orders, and corrections could create proprietary location-level context. But “live” is not the same as paid, retained, or expanding. The $500-or-Oura referral incentive and 106 company followers show manually purchased distribution, not an owned channel already causing purchases.

The founder bridge is credible. Aakar Khanna has operated his family’s multi-unit restaurants and worked around restaurant finance and startup operations; Arjun Chaliha led AI/ML work at Bloomberg and previously built a tutoring marketplace. This is not two résumé tourists discovering food waste through a market report. The CEO/CTO split also provides nominal accountability, although a decade-long co-founder relationship does not answer who has final authority when product, financing, and customer demands conflict.

The bull case is the known AI exception: a functioning operating product can matter more than generic skepticism when customer outcomes are measured. Marble’s deployments, 99% inventory accuracy, 97% forecast accuracy, and reported labor and waste savings resemble that mechanism. The material difference from **Ando** is that Marble has disclosed neither pilot-to-paid conversion nor retention, and its accuracy claims lack sample sizes and error distributions. Meanwhile, Nory already sells autonomous forecasting and ordering, and Restaurant365 reaches more than 50,000 locations with over 100 POS integrations. The next model release does not solve Marble’s integration disadvantage.

Delete the Series A and the underwriting collapses. There is no pricing, revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or contribution metric. There is also no evidence that the round funds one narrow test rather than an ambitious list spanning inventory, purchasing, invoices, prep, scheduling, waste, and menu engineering. A useful inventory product could emerge here, but “autonomous back-of-house” currently packages several simultaneous execution risks into one claim.

My 36-month base case is that Marble narrows to inventory capture and forecasting and reaches roughly $1–3 million ARR, rather than becoming the restaurant operating system. I am passing because the operational signal is real but the security and venture moment are unobservable. One verifiable flip condition would change that: a disclosed sub-$10 million post-money round explicitly funding conversion of at least five of the six current groups into annual paid contracts, with positive contribution margin and 80% or better twelve-month location retention.
