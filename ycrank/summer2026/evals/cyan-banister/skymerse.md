# Skymerse -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Damian Szumski is a licensed flight dispatcher who worked inside LOT, FLYR, Lufthansa Systems, and Flightradar24 before building Skymerse. Skymerse is therefore an operator-born aviation company with its highest signal in lived experience and its lowest signal in command authority: Damian knows why the cockpit is blinking, but the dossier never tells me who has the final switch. The 3,500 registered Notamify users and three million processed NOTAMs give Skymerse real Biz and Tiz. The missing CEO designation leaves Rizz—and the founder structure around it—unobserved.

Anduril is the high rung because Palmer Luckey carried a prior technical obsession into a difficult physical domain that conventional investors disliked. Damian brings Skymerse the same load-bearing mechanism: an aeronautics degree, a dispatcher license, airline-operations experience, and ownership of Flightradar24’s API product before Skymerse existed. Skymerse diverges because Damian’s biography documents professional proximity, not the autobiographical moment when aviation operations became his problem, and because Notamify’s alerts are an adjacent product rather than proof that he has already shipped the full planning-to-cockpit system. He is plainly *of* the place, not visiting it, but the Anduril rung remains above him: 27/32.

HQ Trivia exposed what happens when an irresistible artifact temporarily conceals unresolved founder authority: the product sings until the humans disagree about who conducts. Skymerse names Damian only as “Founder,” lists no CEO, provides no co-founder history, and documents no tiebreaker or conflict protocol. Skymerse may be a single-founder company, which would dissolve much of the concern, but the dossier does not establish that. A hard no versus a rebuttal matters here; this is a rebuttal, so I apply the governance default rather than inventing clean authority: 6/16.

Jeppesen’s 2026 Airflow launch determines whether Skymerse remains magically weird over the next 18 months. Skymerse compounds if three million interpreted NOTAMs and operator-specific manuals produce corrections and operational judgments that Jeppesen cannot reconstruct from aeronautical data alone; Skymerse then becomes the peculiar intelligence layer living between official information and actual dispatcher behavior. Skymerse degrades into a feature if Airflow, Flightkeys, or Air Space Intelligence can supply the same verdicts through integrations they already control. Aviation AI is unsexy and difficult, but no sophisticated investors are documented passing on Skymerse for those reasons, so the round is not yet proven underpriced: 17/24.

Notamify’s terms prohibit using its outputs as the sole authoritative basis for safety-critical decisions, placing Skymerse inside a regulated domain without yet granting Skymerse a regulated moat. The capital-intensive frontier score flips upward if Skymerse earns an approval, certification, or contractual operating role that lets an airline rely on its verdicts without complete manual rechecking; it flips downward if “autopilot” remains a metaphor wrapped around disclaimers. LOT Polish Airlines is meaningful evidence of access, but the deployment covers NOTAM monitoring rather than autonomous dispatch. Skymerse gets credit for ugly, consequential aviation infrastructure, not for hardware or authority it has not built: 11/15.

Niantic is the tactile anchor because I could play Ingress and feel the world change before a spreadsheet explained it. Skymerse has a quieter version of that test: a dispatcher can watch Skymerse ingest a live schedule, reconcile weather and NOTAMs, and issue an operational alert, while thousands reportedly use Notamify daily. Skymerse is still more observable than a generic B2B API, yet a normal human cannot ride, photograph, or safely trust Skymerse within 48 hours; even the operator must verify its answer against official sources. The artifact exists, but it lives behind the deli counter: 8/13.

LOT Polish Airlines using Notamify makes my 36-month forecast concrete: by July 2029, Skymerse will reach $3 million to $7 million in ARR across regional airlines, cargo, and medevac operators, then be acquired by Boeing’s Jeppesen rather than becoming an independent flight-planning incumbent. Skymerse has the founder-domain depth to win the specialized interpretation layer, while Skymerse lacks the embedded distribution and authoritative-data position needed to displace Jeppesen outright. I’m never playing the game I seem to be playing—the product called “autopilot” is probably an acquisition-grade corpus of operator decisions.

Air Space Intelligence has raised $34 million, Jeppesen owns embedded aeronautical distribution, and Skymerse’s public traction is still primarily Notamify traction; that is the bear case that stings. Skymerse could spend years translating dynamic third-party data only to watch an incumbent bundle equivalent interpretation, while the safety disclaimer prevents the labor displacement that supports venture-scale pricing. The evidence that wins today is Damian’s lived operating history plus LOT usage, 3,500 registered users, and three million processed NOTAMs—real behavior, not an AI-agent TAM slide. If Skymerse cannot name a second paying airline using the broader flight-specific verdict product, rather than Notamify monitoring, by July 2027, I flip from Invest to Pass.
