# Vestris -- Paul Graham Evaluation

More than 3,700 county systems make Vestris a high-schlep, low-animal-evidence company. Vestris has chosen work most software founders instinctively avoid: retrieving land records, calculating prorations, drafting title schedules, and waiting for underwriter sign-off. But Vestris gives me no equivalent of the Dropbox demo or Airbnb cereal boxes—no paying customer, repeated usage, or improvised sale proving Aahil Valliani and Joshua Tang will claw through the mess.

Coinbase is the high rung because compliance was not adjacent friction; Armstrong’s willingness to live inside it became the company. Vestris has the same promising mechanism in county-specific records and sensitive closing documents, with initial coverage in Washington, D.C. and Montgomery County. Vestris remains below Coinbase because the website still leaves commitments for underwriter sign-off, and its “decades” of approved and rejected titles have no disclosed ownership, size, or exclusivity. The schlep is real. Vestris has not yet shown it owns the schlep.

Kiko is the low rung’s warning: selling the product on eBay was evidence of cleverness, but not evidence of the ambition and persistence the original company needed. Vestris has shipped a preview, waitlist, demo flow, and two-jurisdiction coverage, so Vestris is not merely a deck. Yet custom enterprise pricing without a named customer, revenue, signed LOI, or unscalable onboarding story leaves the animal test mostly unanswered. Animals leave tracks. Two live jurisdictions are a faint track, not cereal boxes.

Eight to ten hours of processor work per file makes Vestris unusually organic. Valliani and Tang spent about six weeks inside a settlement company and then built Vestris around the documents, portals, searches, and duplicate entry they personally encountered. Over 18 months, that proximity could compound: every closing exposes another exception, county portal, and correction that improves Vestris. It could also decay if six weeks supplied only the obvious workflow and not the tacit knowledge that experienced title processors accumulate over years.

Two supervised-content patents put Valliani above the technical-by-credential-only floor, but Vestris shows no public repositories, competitive-programming history, hacker-community presence, or named nontrivial artifact predating the startup. The score would flip upward if Vestris demonstrated that its browser agents reliably navigate dozens of hostile county systems, or if Valliani’s prior Safe Kids work revealed unusual software craftsmanship rather than merely patent authorship. Until then, Vestris has technical credentials and a product claim, not hacker genealogy.

Reddit is the useful single-anchor parallel on founder configuration: two Michigan founders can be a strong unit, just as a pair of college collaborators can build something they themselves want. Vestris benefits from Valliani’s computer-science background and Tang’s settlement-processing exposure, but Vestris documents only university overlap and approximately six weeks working together. That is enough for a functioning pair, not enough to infer the long-standing trust that lets cofounders survive several years of county integrations and enterprise rejection.

Washington, D.C. and Montgomery County will still define Vestris’s shape 36 months from now: I predict Vestris remains independent, reaches roughly $1 million to $3 million in ARR, and narrows into processor-assistance software across fewer than 30 jurisdictions rather than achieving autonomous nationwide closings. Vestris has found an excellent schlep, but jurisdiction-specific integrations and underwriter approval will turn expansion into repeated manual work. Without stronger evidence that the founders are animals, Vestris is more likely to become a useful narrow business than the default operating system for closings.

Two Michigan students spending six weeks processing files and then building browser agents for two jurisdictions is the strongest case that the animal evidence is merely hidden by the dossier. Airbnb looked absurd before the cereal-box story surfaced, and Vestris could similarly be understating frantic founder-led implementation behind the preview site. The objection stings because Vestris’s organic origin and unpleasant workload are exactly where black swans begin. The evidence that wins today is the absence of a single named customer, paid order, repeat workflow, or founder hustle anecdote. Ten paying title firms and founder-built support for 20 jurisdictions within twelve months would flip my verdict to Invest.
