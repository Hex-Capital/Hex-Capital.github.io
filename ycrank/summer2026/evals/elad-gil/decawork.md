# Decawork -- Elad Gil Evaluation

**Color Genomics** is the high rung for single-miracle architecture: workflow infrastructure built on top of a hard capability that already works. Decawork has the same attractive shape—AI agents already act across enterprise systems, while Decawork externalizes credentials, scopes permissions, and records attributable actions. Decawork is therefore a technically clean, single-miracle control plane whose weakest signal is whether anyone will pay for the current product. The divergence from Color is trust: Decawork becomes a concentrated gateway failure point, lists no security certification, and has disclosed no current-product revenue or usage curve.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism for traction: activity can be a false signal when it does not establish improving economics or a path to category leadership. Decawork’s prior autonomous-IT product ran across multiple enterprises, but Decawork pivoted from resolving human-employee incidents to governing AI agents; usage of the former does not validate the latter. For current-product revenue velocity there is no evidence—applying the absent-evidence default. In the AI cycle, Decawork’s silence matters because something that is “just working” should already produce repeat deployments, expansion, or dollars.

**Eighty-eight percent** of surveyed organizations reporting agent-security incidents, while only 22% treat agents as identities, is a real recent catalyst rather than TAM decoration. Over the next 18 months, every additional production agent compounds credential exposure, approval requirements, and audit volume, making Decawork more urgent without Decawork needing identity security’s forecast $29 billion market to expand. The re-opening is strong, but Okta’s March 2026 agent registration and kill-switch announcement shows incumbents recognized the same window; Decawork is skating for the puck, not skating alone.

**Aman Raj** built Barclays compliance tooling used by 240-plus teams, a fintech serving 52,000 families and processing $1.5 million, and a consumer-AI product with more than 100,000 MAU. Combined with Sarthak Aggarwal’s NVIDIA and Ema experience, Decawork has materially better shipping evidence than credential-only founders. The score would move into the Perplexity rung if Decawork showed that Aman or Sarthak shipped gateway, approval, and recovery features between customer conversations with repeat usage; absent that cadence, Decawork demonstrates prior execution rather than current iterative velocity.

**Stripe** became an index on e-commerce because every growing merchant naturally touched the payments rail. Decawork could become an index on enterprise agents if every agent invocation passes through its gateway, but Decawork currently must persuade IT departments to insert a new credential-holding control point while Okta brings an 8,200-integration catalog. That is a distribution contest against category leaders, not automatic category participation. Decawork’s monitoring and failure-recovery scope is a useful wedge, but no disclosed integration, policy-data, or adoption moat yet makes Decawork the default rail.

**Okta’s** convergence makes my 36-month forecast specific: by July 2029, Decawork will have narrowed from a broad agent-identity control plane into agent-run observability and failure recovery, reaching $2 million to $6 million ARR rather than displacing Okta in identity governance. Decawork’s single-miracle architecture makes a functional product likely, while Decawork’s missing current-product curve and weak index position make a focused adjacent wedge more probable than control-plane dominance.

**Aman Raj’s** 240-team Barclays deployment and Decawork’s prior production footprint form the strongest bull case: like the Anduril exception, Decawork has founders who shipped on the hard workflow before a specific market catalyst made the category urgent. Decawork could use monitoring and recovery to enter accounts, then expand into identity once run data and policy templates create switching costs. The case still loses today because the prior product was publicly presented only one month before the pivot, current-product buying behavior is undisclosed, and Okta, Astrix, Oasis Security, and Token Security already occupy the control layer. Three named enterprises paying Decawork at least $1 million aggregate ARR with greater than 20% monthly growth for three consecutive months would flip my verdict to Invest.
