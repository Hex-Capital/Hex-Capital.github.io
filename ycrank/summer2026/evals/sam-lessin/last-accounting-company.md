# Last Accounting Company -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot underwrite the deal before knowing the deal. Last Accounting Company discloses neither post-money valuation nor competing offers, and YC Summer 2026 does not establish that this is a cheap, genuinely scarce first check. More importantly, the dossier never says what the round will prove. “Hire, launch, and grow” would be a capital factory; a valid venture moment would be something like reaching a dated volume of autonomously closed books with a measured error rate and positive contribution margin.

The strongest evidence is not the AI story. It is the proprietary ledger already sitting inside a live accounting workflow: more than $80 million in transaction value across dozens of Finnish companies, with evidence links, permissions, controls, and human review. That is materially better than 944 LinkedIn followers or a waitlist. Customers are entrusting the system with consequential financial records, and the API and MCP interface can make reviewed ledger data useful outside the month-end process. This is an emerging dark pool of workflow and correction data, although undisclosed revenue prevents me from concluding that it already causes durable purchases rather than subsidized design-partner usage.

The bull case stings. Four founders have assembled complementary financial-data, software, finance, and legal capabilities; Riki Karjalainen and Sami Laakkonen previously built petabyte-scale infrastructure at Token Terminal, while Veikko Pere brings finance and legal exposure. The product also joins two mistrustful systems—automated agents and statutory accounting—through human approval, audit trails, validation rules, and restricted permissions. If the dozens of companies are paying market rates and renewing, this could be a real operating business with AI as an option rather than an AI wrapper searching for one.

But the dossier withholds the cash facts that decide that case: fixed-price service, no published price, no revenue, no gross margin, no burn, and no accounting-labor requirement per customer. Human review can be a liability control or a hidden negative contribution margin. Pilot, Truewind, and Zeni already occupy adjacent positions, and improving general-purpose computer-use models make “our agent is better” a depreciating asset. The durable advantage must therefore be customer workflow ownership and operating economics, not model accuracy or $80 million of gross transaction value.

The team has shipped serious financial-data infrastructure on one side of the bridge and has now deployed an accounting system on the other, but the biographies do not show a prior accounting operation, regulated filing product, or scaled service business. Four childhood co-founders also create a governance question the dossier does not answer: who has final authority over product, accounting liability, and financing when those interests conflict?

My 36-month base case is a credible Nordic accounting firm with proprietary software and roughly €3 million–€8 million ARR, not yet a category-defining global software company. I am passing today because the demonstrated workflow is being offered without the price, capital experiment, or unit economics needed to price its convexity. One verifiable condition flips me: evidence that paying cohorts renew while fully loaded human-review costs decline enough to produce positive contribution margin, coupled with a round priced below a $10 million post-money valuation to fund a dated automation milestone.
