# Ageospatial -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to an insurance analytics platform is the strongest supported route. Moody’s acquisition of CAPE Analytics shows that a platform has bought geospatial property data to complement catastrophe models. Ageospatial says it integrates dated property records and inspection triage into carrier workflows. An acquirer could use those integrations to reach more underwriting teams if the product delivers repeatable savings on inspections or underwriting work. The founder reports signing the company’s largest insurance customer, with contract value undisclosed. AXA and Die Mobiliar appear on the company’s “Trusted by” list, with engagement terms undisclosed. The memo supplies neither measured carrier savings nor a relevant acquisition price. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

A plausible seed milestone is two paid production deployments that quantify carrier savings and track the engineering time and imagery cost required to roll out each installation. The reported customer signing and YC backing give Ageospatial a starting point, while contract economics and available capital remain unknown. A whole-company acquisition could keep the operating entity in place, subject to any change-of-control terms in carrier contracts and imagery licenses. The memo provides no evidence of an independent route that could pay this holder. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The 5× gross cash objective is $2.5 million within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity. The sale cases below are illustrative. They assume the holder is eligible to sell the entire stake, the waterfall distributes equity proceeds pro rata without senior preferences, and all consideration is paid in cash at closing within eight years.

- **Modest sale.** $50 million in enterprise value less $5 million in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $45 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $1.35 million, or 2.7×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value less $10 million in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $2.7 million, or 5.4×. At 2.5% ownership, the same sale pays $2.25 million, or 4.5×.
- **Prolonged private.** If Ageospatial reinvests available cash and no eligible share purchase or distribution occurs, the holder realizes $0 within eight years.

### Countercase

Moody’s already owns CAPE, and Nearmap already sells imagery-based underwriting and renewal workflows. A potential buyer could extend an existing product if Ageospatial’s carrier integrations prove costly to repeat or deliver little incremental savings. Third-party imagery terms could further limit what a buyer can use after closing. These risks leave pricing and transferability unresolved. The memo establishes no structural block or supported sale range that would leave the holder materially short under the strongest credible route, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Could the incremental savings from Ageospatial’s carrier deployments justify a strategic acquisition price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? Carrier-verified savings across two paid production deployments, with rollout costs documented and a plausible acquirer’s valuation rationale grounded in those results, would move the action to Reach out if the implied price clears the hurdle after deal adjustments and dilution. If the strongest credible buyer economics leave proceeds materially short, move to Pass.
