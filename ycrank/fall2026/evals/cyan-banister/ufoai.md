# UFO.ai -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a small business assigning continuing responsibilities to software: reconcile these accounts, maintain this service, remember why we changed suppliers. UFO.ai’s September 21 context-management experiments offer a small leak from that world. Keeping history without repeatedly compressing it into summaries could help agents carry responsibility across time. But remembering, correcting a mistaken belief, and safely acting on it are separate transitions. The September 23 account of an agent returning to an archived repository shows exactly where that chain still breaks. There is something here to investigate; the dossier establishes only part of the journey.

The founders make me take that investigation seriously. Alex Graveley’s record includes creating GitHub Copilot, founding Hackpad through its acquisition, and directing Comet at Perplexity. Those are successive encounters with software creation, shared knowledge, and delegated browsing. Marshall Kiely brings founding AI-engineering experience at Notable Health alongside company-finance experience at Perplexity. This is considerably more informative than two impressive résumés pointed at a fashionable category. Still, the dossier does not establish when their particular obsession with persistent agents began, how they recruited each other, or which customer encounter shaped UFO.ai’s specification. Strong building history earns substantial credit; it does not supply the missing origin story.

What attracts me beneath the workspace interface is the published separation of execution, durable identity, authorization, accounting, and extensions. That fits my pattern of looking for concealed infrastructure beneath a visible application. An Apache-licensed runtime, self-hosting, and customer-supplied model keys also give customers meaningful operating choices. Yet inspectable infrastructure becomes a valuable enabling layer only when other products or businesses depend on it. UFO.ai has not demonstrated that dependency, and competitors can reuse its core. Glean and Dust already combine organizational context with agents; openness must earn its place in the customer’s daily operations.

Why does a team surrender control to a hosted workspace in the first place? UFO.ai’s unlimited members and prepaid task billing suggest an alternative to charging for every person who participates. I like the direction of that agency transfer. But the dossier identifies neither a specific incumbent toll nor the cost of escaping it, and UFO.ai’s own task rates are undisclosed. I cannot yet tell whether this removes an irritating economic restriction or simply changes the meter. Likewise, 99 stars and 13 forks establish attention and experimentation, not repeated delegation of consequential work. Missing revenue is ordinary at this stage; missing observed behavior leaves my product conviction at its starting point.

The strongest argument against my hesitation is that this team could build the necessary plumbing before customers know how to ask for it. I can invest before revenue when a functioning prototype, independently demonstrated commitment, and a concrete future path compensate for absent sales. UFO.ai has meaningful pieces of that exception, especially Graveley’s shipped work, but no documented customer commitment or repeated use completes it. Privacy also matters here: the default local carrier can read the host and lacks kernel-enforced network egress restrictions. Docker and E2B are documented alternatives, so this is not evidence of an unavoidable privacy failure. It does mean that self-hosting alone cannot establish safe custody. The differing founder listings warrant clarification, but do not establish concealed leadership or broken governance.

I am neutral at **51/100**. The team is the strongest signal; demonstrated changes in customer behavior are the weakest. One verifiable milestone would reopen the investment decision: three unrelated businesses completing eight consecutive weeks of recurring, consequential work through UFO.ai, with customer-confirmed time savings and execution records showing enforced isolation and recovery from stale instructions. I want to see someone comfortably leave a responsibility with this product. That would make the imagined future much less imaginary.
