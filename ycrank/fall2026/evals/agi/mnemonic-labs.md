# Mnemonic Labs -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path A, Scarce complements, is the strongest candidate. If cheap frontier intelligence lets quant desks generate more candidate strategies, the scarce input could be a model that reliably turns market activity into executable trades with positive net returns. Mnemonic could control that capability if its own orders, fills, and outcomes improve the model in ways rivals cannot readily reproduce. The memo’s cited market-event study supports the premise that this kind of model can learn order-flow patterns across markets. Mnemonic reports building its training pipeline and architecture in under six weeks and putting more than 25 TB of tokens into its launch training run. Both founders have quant trading experience. The memo documents the launch. It supplies no live results, data rights, or independent buyer commitments. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*)

Under the memo’s inferred proprietary-trading scenario, the expansion unit is additional capital deployed into a strategy. Incremental trading gains would need to cover fees, slippage, market impact, and incremental model and operating costs. The memo supplies no realized inputs for that bridge, so profitable deployment and the capacity to scale it remain open. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Published methods give rivals a route to market-event pretraining without privileged full-order-book access, and competitors report live trading that can generate their own execution feedback. Mnemonic’s training run therefore leaves open whether its model can produce a distinct, durable edge. The memo contains no live comparison that establishes or defeats that possibility, so the scarcity gate remains open and outreach stays on Watch. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Would an independent quant desk commit budget for continued access after a representative live comparison shows that Mnemonic’s reusable execution feedback produces a repeatable edge after trading costs against a credible rival with comparable model quality?
