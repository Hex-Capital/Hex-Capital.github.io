# Ryhla -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** Ryhla’s strongest case is that it can make scarce feeder capacity easier to find. Developers of 1–50 MW projects can pay application fees and wait months to learn whether a site has capacity or needs costly upgrades. If cheaper intelligence makes more small data center projects viable, more developers could compete for the same utility study capacity. That demand increase is a scenario assumption. Ryhla’s potential advantage is earlier access to reliable, territory-specific answers about which sites are worth pursuing. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Ryhla reports that utilities approved 91% of the sites its models recommended in a 2025 cohort. The memo gives neither the cohort size nor outcomes for rejected sites, so the figure leaves accuracy against existing tools and the cost of missed viable sites unresolved. Ryhla also reports a live Midwest solar deployment and a battery developer case with about $160,000 in avoided fees after screening out eight of ten projects. These are early demand signals. Payment and repeat use remain unknown. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Both founders have grid-modeling research experience, and they report deploying models with utilities before serving developers. Ryhla lists coverage across 11 utility territories. Each new territory is a plausible unit of expansion, with economics depending on project volume and pricing relative to the costs of acquiring data, validating models, and handling filings. Those inputs remain unmeasured in the memo. The San Francisco location and Fall 2026 YC batch make this a plausible seed fit. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Utilities control approval and required upgrades. Where public hosting-capacity maps are adequate, developers can use them for initial screening, and utility-side vendors already automate parts of technical review. With cheap models and ample capital, competitors could reproduce Ryhla’s screening and filing workflow if they can obtain comparable feeder data and study outcomes. Ryhla claims proprietary training data and backtesting, but the memo leaves access rights and comparative accuracy open. Substitution is credible, and the evidence leaves Path A’s control gate unresolved. Path B also lacks evidence of lower delivery labor opening a new market and of buyers paying under model parity. A seed-stage field test can resolve the central Path A question, which supports Watch. (Defensibility, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Ryhla use feeder data and utility outcomes it has durable rights to reuse to get paying developers to a verified site decision faster or at lower cost than their best available workflow with access to equally capable models? A prospective paid trial across representative territories should independently check a sample of both recommended and rejected sites and measure time and total cost to a verified decision. A repeatable advantage backed by durable data rights would support Reach out if the updated score and confidence clear the outreach gates. Comparable results from readily available alternatives would move the case to Pass on the current evidence. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
