# River -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

River’s strongest liquidity route is a strategic acquisition by a productivity software vendor. River’s built-in editors and multi-model workspace could give a buyer an integrated way to handle projects spanning documents, spreadsheets, slides, and connected apps. The memo documents a desktop launch and published subscription tiers. River claims 100,000+ creators, while paid conversion, retention, and workflow savings remain unverified. The reported $500,000 YC convertible note provides an early financing signal, with runway and the cost of proving paid adoption still open. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The proceeds bridge uses a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-ons. The 5× gross cash objective requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale scenarios assume an eligible holder sells the full stake for cash within eight years and receives 3% of equity proceeds, with no senior preferences or further holdbacks.

- **Modest sale.** An assumed $40 million enterprise value less $5 million in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million in equity proceeds, yielding $1.05 million to the holder, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** An assumed $100 million enterprise value less $10 million in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million in equity proceeds, yielding $2.7 million, or 5.4×. If the holder can sell only half the stake at closing, realized proceeds fall to $1.35 million, or 2.7×.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested yields $0 in realized proceeds by year eight.

The sale prices are scenario assumptions. The memo leaves buyer-supported pricing, the actual waterfall, transfer and holder eligibility, and any independent liquidity route unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Microsoft already offers cross-file execution and in-place edits under existing tenant controls. Anthropic and Genspark also offer overlapping workflows, and Anthropic supplies models to River. The clearest prospective buyers therefore have readily available substitutes, weakening the case that they would pay for River’s product or user base. The evidence leaves open whether River can build a paid workflow that a buyer would value above the proceeds hurdle. Neither the structural-block gate nor the inadequate-proceeds gate is established. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Do paying consulting teams renew after six months because River measurably reduces the time they spend producing client reports across files and apps? Paid cohort records and timed comparisons against those teams’ existing tools would answer it. Repeat renewals and substantial time savings across multiple teams would move the action to Reach out to test whether a strategic buyer could justify a price that clears the holder’s hurdle.
