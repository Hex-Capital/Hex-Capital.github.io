# Dualizer -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an industrial simulation vendor is Dualizer’s strongest supported route. Emerson’s purchase of its remaining AspenTech shares shows strategic demand for process simulation assets. Dualizer reports that its in-house solver calibrates models against plant data and connects them to engineering workflows. A vendor could gain a faster way to update and calibrate plant models if those capabilities deliver repeatable savings in production. The memo gives no measured buyer benefit or basis for estimating what a buyer would pay for Dualizer. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is to convert pilots into paid production deployments and demonstrate repeatable savings across plants after onboarding and customization costs. The memo reports several active pilots with undisclosed payment terms, a $100,000 prize, and $125,000 in listed seed funding. Those signals support continued testing, with the capital and time needed for repeatable deployments still unknown. Transferability of software and customer rights remains unverified. The memo gives no evidence of an independent route that would pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at full pro rata participation. The sale prices below are sensitivity assumptions. Both sale cases assume closing within eight years, $5 million in debt and deal adjustments, no senior waterfall claims, and an eligible holder selling the entire stake for cash.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $45 million enterprise value less $5 million leaves $40 million for equity | $1.20 million, or 2.4× |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value less $5 million leaves $95 million for equity | $2.85 million, or 5.7×. Selling 80% of the stake would yield $2.28 million, or 4.56×. |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested throughout the eight years, with no funded payout or eligible share purchase | $0 realized |

### Countercase

An incumbent could add conversational automation to existing HYSYS models, as ProcAgent demonstrates, while MaximaLabs already offers a copilot with a deterministic solver and plant calibration. Dualizer’s published calibration demo uses synthetic histories, and earlier tests failed to generalize. If each plant requires substantial custom work, a buyer has less reason to pay for the full platform. These risks leave buyer economics unresolved. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the memo establishes neither a structural block nor a credible sale range that falls short of the proceeds hurdle. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Dualizer demonstrate repeatable net savings across paid production deployments that give a strategic buyer an economic reason to pay a price yielding at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? Customer-verified savings after deployment and support costs, paired with credible buyer economics at that price, would move Watch to Reach out.
