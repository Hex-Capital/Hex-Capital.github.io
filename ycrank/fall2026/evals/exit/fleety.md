# Fleety -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Fleety’s strongest exit path is a strategic acquisition by ServiceTitan. The memo describes Fleety writing assignments directly to ServiceTitan’s dispatch board and validating bookings against call transcripts and equipment records. Fleety reports that at Albert Nahman it handled about 92% of assignments, enabled one dispatcher to manage 30+ technicians, and saved about $60,000 annually in overhead. If that benefit repeats and exceeds what Dispatch Pro can deliver, ServiceTitan could have an economic reason to buy the workflow. The memo provides one quantified customer outcome and leaves the incremental benefit over Dispatch Pro unmeasured. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)

A milestone that would strengthen the acquisition case is sustained, incremental savings across several paying ServiceTitan shops, with measured deployment effort. The reported deployment gives Fleety a starting point. Its reported on-site setup for every customer and the limited evidence on revenue and funding leave the cost and timing of reaching that milestone uncertain. Dispatch-board writeback shows the products can work together today. Continued platform access after a change of control, contract transfer terms, and holder sale rights remain unverified. The memo identifies no independently supported liquidity route. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks; Key Facts)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in gross cash, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% pro rata. Both illustrative sale cases assume $5 million in combined net debt and deal costs, an eligible sale of the holder’s entire stake for cash by year eight, and a pro rata waterfall with no senior preference deductions.

| Scenario | Distributable equity | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale at $50 million enterprise value | $45 million | $1.35 million (2.7×) |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | $95 million | $2.85 million (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private operation with cash reinvested through year eight | No distribution | $0 |

The strong scenario clears the benchmark on those assumptions. The memo supplies no transaction pricing or buyer economics to support either illustrative sale value. That gap and the single quantified customer outcome keep evidence confidence low. (Market & Traction; Key Facts)

### Countercase

ServiceTitan already offers native technician matching by sales potential and skills, and it controls access to the dispatch board Fleety writes to. ServiceTitan could satisfy customers with its existing product or build comparable booking validation itself. The memo leaves both the value of Fleety’s incremental workflow and the durability of platform access unresolved. This supports Watch. A structural closing block and a realizable price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle are both unestablished. (Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Fleety show that buying its workflow would create enough incremental value for ServiceTitan to support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity within eight years? Measured gains over Dispatch Pro across paying shops, paired with a buyer-side build-versus-buy analysis that supports that price after deal adjustments, would move Watch to Reach out.
