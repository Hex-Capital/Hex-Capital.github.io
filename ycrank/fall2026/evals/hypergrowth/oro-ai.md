# ORO AI -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A, product-led adoption,** is the strongest path. The prospective buyer is an AI lab evaluating whether its agents can make purchasing decisions whose consequences take weeks to surface. SMB Bench gives the lab a reusable test against fixed reorder rules and a scripted expert. If labs use it to compare successive model releases, the public report, leaderboard, and run traces could attract new teams, and hosted access could monetize recurring evaluations. That buying cadence and revenue model are inferred. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The memo records 670 dataset downloads, and ORO reports 859 benchmark runs across 16 models and 18 tasks. The downloads show discovery, while the run count leaves buyer identity and repeat use unresolved. Hosted access is granted by email, and the memo identifies no paying lab or buyer-funded trial. GetASAP helped calibrate the simulator. Its role provides evidence about the realism of the buying workflow, while demand from AI labs remains unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Facts)

In a second-market scenario, a lab that accepts the same simulator, reference policies, and grader could use the same hosted workflow. That assumes local buying practices do not require recalibration. The next lab would add provisioning and support, and each workload could add execution and grading costs depending on who runs it. A market that needs different buying rules or data would also add operator recruitment, calibration, and validation. The memo gives no measured rollout or marginal costs. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Labs may download SMB Bench for research but pay only for tests that recreate their own tools, data, and workflows. The current benchmark covers one wholesaler-calibrated purchasing workflow, with other business functions run by fixed rules. If paid demand requires a custom environment for each lab, domain-expert time and validation could rise with each account. That would trigger the Pass gate if buyer or rollout evidence showed proportional delivery effort with no supported path to reuse. The memo leaves that question open, supporting Watch. (Defensibility, Key Risks)
