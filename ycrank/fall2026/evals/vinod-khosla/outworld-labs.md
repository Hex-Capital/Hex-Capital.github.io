# Outworld Labs -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Outworld Labs could remove a physical bottleneck in robot learning: every training attempt that requires hardware, an operator, and a task reset consumes scarce real-world capacity. A simulator that reliably substitutes computation for those attempts could change the economics of teaching robots dexterous work. That consequence interests me. But the dossier supplies neither today’s cost baseline nor the fraction Horizon can eliminate. I cannot turn “indistinguishable from reality” into an orders-of-magnitude consequence merely because robotics is consequential.

The strongest evidence is the product’s technical direction. Outworld describes a custom manipulation physics engine and a specific method: measure hardware behavior, fit the simulation, and report agreement. That earns modest credit beyond an unspecified simulator claim. The decisive question is whether agreement predicts successful behavior on hardware outside the calibration examples. There is no numerical comparison with MuJoCo or Isaac Sim, no transfer-success result, and no calibration-time measurement. A model can fit yesterday’s measurements beautifully and still teach tomorrow’s robot the wrong contact dynamics.

The bull case deserves attention. Gardea brings BAIR robotics research; Grant brings research experience spanning BAIR, Google DeepMind, NASA, and Oxford. This is relevant technical preparation for a difficult physical problem. Outworld also fits my less-public pattern of backing scientific workflows that could become repeatable platforms: calibration could eventually produce reusable knowledge across robots and tasks. But the dossier identifies that learning loop as a hypothesis, not an established asset. The founder-reported September 28, 2026 showcase tie supports taking a demonstration seriously; it does not establish transfer performance. Their credentials also do not document belief revision or complementary recruiting, so I retain the default founder score.

The economic danger is repeated calibration labor. MuJoCo and Isaac Sim are available free; Outworld must beat the total cost of using them, including physical experiments, engineering time, and failed transfer. A paid simulator can readily beat free software on that basis. The dossier simply contains no pricing or operating assumptions to establish it. Likewise, measuring hardware behavior is a sensible development process, but it is not yet a disclosed capital-allocation plan with a largest kill risk, a decisive threshold, and a stop-or-pivot response. Those missing disclosures receive the stated defaults. No revenue, small headcount, and limited public visibility are ordinary pre-seed conditions.

I would pass with my own money today. The known exceptions warrant checking: extraordinary consequence or a documented instigator attacking a giant existing value pool can justify investing before conventional milestones. Outworld has relevant researchers and a plausible enabling technology, but neither exception is established strongly enough here. Conversely, there is no evidence of the advertising, exit-seeking, or generic AI-wrapper red flags; this is an unproven technical option, not an active category mismatch. My one verifiable flip condition is an independent customer trial showing at least **5x lower all-in cost per hardware-successful policy** than that customer’s existing MuJoCo or Isaac Sim workflow, including calibration labor, on held-out manipulation tasks. That result would connect the engine to both physical leverage and customer economics.
