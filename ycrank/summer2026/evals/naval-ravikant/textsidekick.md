# Sidekick -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Justin So has acted inside the problem, but the evidence is stronger on access than mastery. He comes from a third-generation manufacturing family, worked summers on the EDS Manufacturing floor, and has put Sidekick in 20-plus plants serving 600-plus frontline workers ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/textsidekick), Aug 2026; [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/textsidekick), Jul 2026). That is real founder-market contact. It is not yet evidence that he personally built or sold a durable operating system for factories, nor does the dossier show retention, resolved downtime, or customer outcomes.

The wedge is intelligent. SMS removes accounts, app installation, and training from a workforce that already knows how to text. Photos, voice reports, document retrieval, work-order routing, and manager escalation can turn scattered plant knowledge into an operating history ([Sidekick](https://textsidekick.com/), Aug 2026). The same interface can move from plants into warehouses and other industrial facilities, giving Sidekick room inside a connected-worker market estimated at $9.73 billion in 2025. The market is broad enough; the question is whether Sidekick owns anything inside it.

Copy the code and little obviously remains. The plant history is customer-owned, the retrieval and routing loops are unproven, and no proprietary model, exclusive dataset, hardware layer, or cross-customer network is disclosed. MaintainX already has asset histories, work orders, voice memos, manual search, enterprise integrations, and an expected $135 million-plus of 2026 ARR; Autodesk can add SMS without surrendering those accumulated advantages ([MaintainX](https://www.getmaintainx.com/newsroom/maintainx-unveils-advanced-ai-features), Jul 2025; [Autodesk](https://adsknews.autodesk.com/en/pressrelease/autodesk-to-acquire-maintainx-advancing-unified-platform-in-operations/), May 2026). An easier interface is distribution until the incumbent copies it. Then it is a feature.

The strongest bull case is that 600 workers are already generating fixes, workarounds, and answers, eventually making every plant more intelligent. At pre-seed, 20 deployments can matter more than polished revenue. The capital-efficient-product exception represented by Notion is relevant because an early product need not begin with monetization or a visible network. The material difference is that Sidekick enters a legible category against incumbents already holding the workflow data; the dossier does not show an unusual product language or compounding asset that those incumbents lack.

There is no disclosed integrity problem, artificial financing deadline, or founder behavior that triggers a personal veto. The structural problem is leverage. Contributions remain trapped inside each customer, so one plant does not improve acquisition or utility for the next. Sidekick therefore misses the permissionless pattern: every new account still appears to require founder-led demos, integrations, and service. Paid plans are mentioned in the terms, but no payer, price, recurring revenue, or completed transaction establishes direct value capture ([Sidekick Terms](https://www.textsidekick.com/legal-terms/terms-and-conditions), Jan 2026).

I pass with my own money today. Sidekick has credible access, a useful wedge, and unusually broad pre-seed deployment, but its strongest evidence proves demand for the interface rather than ownership of the underlying advantage. One verifiable condition flips me: show that a cohort of plants retained for twelve months has produced an exclusive resolution dataset that measurably improves answer accuracy or downtime outcomes with each deployment—and that customers are paying recurring prices for it. Then the history becomes an asset rather than a database column.
