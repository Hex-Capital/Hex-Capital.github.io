## The Idea

- **Problem:** GPU buyers, sellers, operators, and lenders rely on private quotes, forwarded inventory lists, and partial broker information because supply lacks a shared price-discovery layer ([Stoa](https://www.stoaexchange.com/); [YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/stoa), Aug 2026).
- **Approach:** Buyers post hardware specifications, vetted dealers submit blind firm quotes, and Stoa records RFQs, awards, shipping, inspection, and settlement, with a stated 48-hour RFQ cycle ([YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/stoa), Aug 2026).
- **Differentiation:** Compute Exchange also sources physical and reserved GPUs across more than 100 providers, Procurri combines resale with IT-asset disposition, and Rillor supplies governed GPU datasets, while Stoa concentrates on institutional physical-hardware RFQs and deriving price history from bids and settlements ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/17/compute-exchange-opens-secondary-market-used-nvidia-h100-a100-gpus/), Jul 2026; [Procurri](https://www.procurri.com/products-and-services/buy-and-sell-used-gpus/); [Rillor](https://rillor.com/)).
- **Business Model:** Stoa charges transaction-related fees when an RFQ executes, with rates disclosed during registration or in a separate agreement rather than on a public pricing page ([Stoa Terms](https://www.stoaexchange.com/terms), Jul 2026).
- **TAM/SAM:** Omdia estimates AI data-center chip shipments grew from $123 billion in 2024 toward $286 billion in 2030, implying approximately 15.1% annual growth, while no public SAM estimate was found for secondary enterprise-GPU transactions ([Stoa Research citing Omdia](https://www.stoaexchange.com/research/why-gpu-price-discovery-is-fragmented), 2025).
- **GTM / Distribution:** Stoa solicits buyers, sellers, dealers, neoclouds, data-center operators, OEM channels, liquidators, and GPU-backed lenders through direct inquiries, calls, referrals, and waitlist onboarding ([Stoa](https://www.stoaexchange.com/); [YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/stoa), Aug 2026).

## Defensibility

- **Moat today:** Stoa contractually owns aggregated market data derived from pricing, bids, offers, volumes, and transactions, and reports more than $300 million in RFQs during its first month, although no executed-trade value is disclosed ([Stoa Terms](https://www.stoaexchange.com/terms), Jul 2026; [YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/stoa), Aug 2026).
- **Future moat:** [Inferred]: A larger history of settled trades across SKUs, conditions, regions, and counterparties could improve Stoa’s price benchmarks and dealer liquidity, but the currently unavailable H100 index value leaves this mechanism unverified ([Stoa Index](https://www.stoaexchange.com/stoaindex), Jul 2026).
- **Market structure:** No structural barrier identified at this stage.
- **Commoditization risk:** Compute Exchange already offers identity-verified physical GPUs, reserved capacity, and forward contracts, while Procurri provides used-GPU resale with inspection, logistics, and ITAD operations ([Compute Exchange](https://compute.exchange/); [Procurri](https://www.procurri.com/products-and-services/buy-and-sell-used-gpus/)).

## Key Risks

- **Marketplace liquidity:** Stoa reports $300M+ in RFQs but no executed-trade value, and its H100 composite index is currently unavailable, limiting public evidence of settlement depth ([YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/stoa); [Stoa Index](https://www.stoaexchange.com/stoaindex), Aug 2026).
- **Counterparty and hardware risk:** Stoa’s terms state that it does not inspect or take title to hardware and does not guarantee product existence, authenticity, condition, delivery, or payment ([Stoa Terms](https://www.stoaexchange.com/terms), Jul 2026).
- **Disintermediation:** Stoa imposes a 12-month non-circumvention obligation and claims compensation equal to avoided fees, indicating that counterparties can attempt to transact outside the platform after introductions ([Stoa Terms](https://www.stoaexchange.com/terms), Jul 2026).
- **Export-control exposure:** The terms require compliance with U.S. export and sanctions rules and prohibit marketplace activity involving China, Hong Kong, or Macau, constraining eligible transactions and requiring counterparty screening ([Stoa Terms](https://www.stoaexchange.com/terms), Jul 2026).
- **Name collision:** An unrelated AI-team product uses “Stoa” on Product Hunt, and an unrelated real-estate technology company uses the name on LinkedIn, creating search-result and company-identity confusion ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/stoa-4); [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/better-with-stoa), 2026).
