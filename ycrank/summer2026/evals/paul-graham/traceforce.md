# Traceforce -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The most important missing fact is not revenue. It is what happened when the founders were blocked. Xia Hua and Varun Wadhwa found a real blind spot—gateways cannot see agents touching local files, credentials, MCP servers, and skills—and shipped an endpoint product now running on more than 1,500 devices across five enterprises. But that is the solution to a customer problem, not yet evidence of a founder-built workaround to resistance. The dossier never says what failed, what they changed, or how quickly the change altered a deployment. I can see that they build. I cannot yet see how they behave when the play collapses.

The technical proof is much stronger. Traceforce has deployed software that inventories local AI tools, constructs an activity graph, and can block actions before execution. The Atlas registry covers more than 600 MCPs, MCP X-Ray has 80 GitHub stars and 15 forks, and the company has completed SOC 2 and ISO 27001 work unusually early. This matches an important revealed pattern: technical founders assembling a concrete substrate before asking investors to believe the platform story. The missing false-positive and false-negative measurements matter, however. Security software that blocks the wrong command is not merely inaccurate; it teaches users to disable it.

There is also a plausible narrow well. Enterprise security teams responsible for unmanaged AI activity are specific users with an urgent job, and five companies have tolerated installation on real devices. One deployment covers a company with more than 500 employees, while Fortune 500 proofs of concept supply additional but weaker evidence. This is substantially better than a waitlist. It is still unclear whether these teams repeatedly use Traceforce, expand coverage, or pay for it. The difference between 1,500 installed endpoints and 1,500 protected endpoints that security teams refuse to give up is nearly the whole demand question.

The founders belong near this problem. Xia led cloud-data-protection engineering at Clumio; Varun built distributed backup infrastructure, RAG database systems, and payments software. They have also worked together before. Endpoint deployment, MDM integrations, sensitive telemetry, policy enforcement, and enterprise compliance constitute real schlep. But the dossier does not show that either founder personally encountered this exact failure before forming the company. Nor does it show a rejected hypothesis or evidence-driven route change. Fifty CISO and CIO interviews before development demonstrate diligence, not the running-back behavior I am trying to detect.

The strongest case against my hesitation is that I am demanding a story when the artifacts already reveal the founders. Two people have obtained certifications, built four device-management integrations, catalogued 600-plus MCPs, and reached five enterprises before Demo Day. The known-exception logic nearly applies: a working product plus real-world pull can sometimes replace a missing founder prior. The opposing danger is equally concrete. Zenity already offers endpoint discovery and pre-execution blocking, while WitnessAI and Noma attack adjacent layers with far more capital. If Traceforce responds by leaning on PORT1, MSP partnerships, integrations, and conference sponsorships before learning direct founder-led sales, it will become a feature distributed by other companies rather than the defining security layer.

I would not write the check yet. The evidence clears the technical and user-pull thresholds, but it does not clear my people-first gate because resourcefulness under resistance remains unobserved. One condition would flip me: show a dated enterprise deployment where a specific accuracy, coverage, or procurement blocker appeared, the founders shipped the workaround within two weeks, and that same customer subsequently expanded paid device coverage.
