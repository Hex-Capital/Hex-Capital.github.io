# Tire Swing -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Tire Swing’s reported $500,000 of funding tells me how much capital may have gone in. It tells me nothing about what ownership my check buys, how many investors are competing, or whether capital is actually scarce. Post-money valuation: unknown. Competing term sheets: unknown. I cannot call this an auction because it is a YC company, and I cannot call it cheap because the team has two people. With my own money, that distinction matters.

The product addresses a real operating mess: household documents become income calculations, eligibility determinations, and requests for missing information. But who owns the valuable part of that workflow? Tire Swing reports thousands of deterministic and AI checks; Yardi already owns property-management relationships and offers direct payroll and banking verification through Nova Credit. Document review could complement those systems, but public regulations and a check library do not establish proprietary access. Founder demonstrations using prospect files and attendance at AHF Live establish a selling motion. Neither establishes that access has caused a purchase. The potential dark pool is accumulated property configurations and reviewer decisions; the dossier identifies that as a possibility, not an asset already demonstrated.

The strongest case against passing is that this is precisely where a small team can build a useful business before the market looks exciting. HUD’s January 1, 2027 HOTMA enforcement date for covered housing authorities creates a concrete reason to revisit workflows. Source-linked findings, deterministic checks, and qualified human approval offer a credible way to sell assistance without pretending certification errors disappear. The reported SOC 2 Type II certification also suggests attention to enterprise requirements. This fits my pattern of backing software that translates between difficult operating systems: messy tenant evidence on one side, formal eligibility rules on the other. The catch is that the customer owns the files and approval authority, while an incumbent may already own the relationship. Tire Swing must demonstrate that its position in between is worth paying to preserve.

Witten’s cybersecurity investigations and leadership of a memory-forensics working group provide operating substance beyond a Princeton credential. Irvine’s work on Cloud Custodian provides relevant experience with software rules and controls. That earns some founder credit. Neither biography documents operating access inside affordable housing, however, so the bridge is currently supported more convincingly from the engineering side. The earlier healthcare-compliance product makes that question sharper: what did the founders learn that makes housing the durable business? The transition itself proves neither failure nor privileged insight. Likewise, naming a CEO and CTO supplies no basis for inventing an equal-founder governance problem.

My larger problem is the capital plan. The dossier names no round-funded hypothesis, measurable result, or deadline. The HOTMA deadline belongs to customers; it is not automatically Tire Swing’s venture moment. Subscription language in order forms also does not establish revenue, reviewer-adjusted margins, burn, or a route to break-even. Required human approval is sensible product design, but the economics must include whatever support and exception handling Tire Swing itself supplies. The known exceptions do not rescue the underwriting: there is no documented paid conversion and retention, unusual enterprise adoption or acquisition validation, or prior-exit financing structure that compensates for the missing gates. Those are absent receipts, not evidence of an actively bad business.

I pass at 30/100. There is a plausible useful company here, but I cannot yet connect scarce capital to a proprietary purchase mechanism and then to cash independence. My one verifiable flip condition is a documented financing package at a sub-$10 million post-money that funds a single six-month test: three named housing operators convert to paid recurring use with positive contribution after inference, onboarding, and company-provided review support, within a budget that reaches break-even without another raise. That would connect the deal, the experiment, and the business. Today the dossier connects the product to a problem.
