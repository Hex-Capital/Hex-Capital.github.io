# Olam Labs -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Om Buddhdev built Roblox games with roughly 6 million visits before working on agent infrastructure at Latitude, making Olam Labs a specific-knowledge machine without a proven network. That high-founder-depth, low-network pairing is Olam Labs’s signature. **Stack Overflow** is the high rung: developers with lived knowledge built a product for developers, and the community itself demonstrated the knowledge publicly. Om’s game-building history and Shreshth Sharma’s reinforcement-learning work create the same founder-product-market fit, but Olam Labs diverges where Stack Overflow compounded openly: Olam Labs’s leading repositories have only 28 and 11 stars, and its public arena displayed no ranked players. The founders know the problem unusually well; the market has not yet reflected that knowledge back at them.

**Perplexity** is the low network rung: a single-sided product where the next user does not directly make the prior user’s experience better. Perplexity escaped through exceptional technical knowledge and permissionless product distribution. Olam Labs currently has the same missing mechanism. Its 69,140 poker hands improve a centralized evaluation dataset, but Olam Labs has not shown that an additional human, model, or laboratory automatically attracts or benefits the other sides. Data accumulation is not yet a network effect. A database gets larger; a network gets harder to leave.

Olam Labs recorded 86,729 graded table-talk turns and attracted approximately 32,000 views for its Social Arena launch, giving Olam Labs a credible code-and-media leverage seed. Over the next 18 months, free games can produce traces, benchmarks, research posts, and model comparisons at near-zero replication cost—a useful flywheel. The break in the loop is monetization: custom environments and founder-led sales to frontier laboratories require labor and institutional permission. Olam Labs scores well on leverage because the public arena can distribute itself, but not at the Twitter rung until gameplay—not booked calls—is clearly the dominant acquisition channel.

The model-evaluation market is forecast to grow from $1.35 billion in 2024 to $8.20 billion by 2032, giving Olam Labs room to move from social-game benchmarks into pre-deployment safety testing, dataset licensing, harness analysis, or agent-training environments. Olam Labs would move into the highest market tier if two non-poker environments produced transferable rankings and paying customers bought more than one evaluation category. The opposite counterfactual is equally clear: if Social Poker remains the only working environment, Olam Labs is a benchmark feature inside a crowded evaluation market, not a platform with pivot-room.

Om Buddhdev and Shreshth Sharma have designed games together since middle school and previously ran a charity, which is a better 10-year-partner signal than two impressive résumés assembled for a financing. **Notion** is the relevant single anchor: long-lived product obsession mattered more than early commercial proof. Olam Labs has the same residue of genuine obsession, reinforced by Om’s Latitude work and Shreshth’s reinforcement-learning experience. Olam Labs has not yet supplied the other half of the Notion evidence—a completed venture, a difficult rebuild, or years of public technical work—so intelligence and energy read strongly while integrity remains appropriately unproven rather than presumed.

Olam Labs’s 69,140 poker hands point toward a narrow but valuable outcome: within 36 months, Olam Labs will pivot from a public Social Poker arena into contracted pre-deployment behavioral evaluations for frontier laboratories and reach $3 million to $7 million in ARR, while the public games remain the data-acquisition and media layer. Olam Labs reaches that range because specific knowledge can sell scarce evaluation judgment even without a true network; the founders need a repeatable benchmark suite, not millions of consumer users.

Patronus AI has raised $70 million, Braintrust $80 million, Galileo $68 million, and General Intuition $320 million—the bear case is that Olam Labs mistakes an interesting poker dataset for proprietary infrastructure while better-funded platforms absorb simulation, observability, and distribution. Olam Labs also relies on LLM graders, one public environment, and a Pi-based harness whose configuration can change measured performance. That objection stings because Naval’s unstated pattern prefers organic builder adoption, while Olam Labs currently points toward bespoke enterprise selling. The founders’ specific knowledge and already-recorded interaction corpus keep the exception alive, much as Perplexity’s technical edge overcame its absent network. I flip to Pass if, by July 2027, Olam Labs has neither launched a second live environment nor secured one paying frontier-lab customer.
