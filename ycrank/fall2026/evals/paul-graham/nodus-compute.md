# Nodus Compute -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The question I cannot answer about Nodus Compute is what the founders did when something broke. Kotra and Varshney have relevant technical experience, and they met while interning at Scale AI. But the dossier contains no named obstacle followed by a dated workaround and an observable result. That matters more to me than the list of employers. I want to see how these two behave under resistance. Their resourcefulness is untested in this record, not disproved.

There is something here worth testing. Nodus describes matching containers to compute and restoring saved files on replacement machines so customer code can reload checkpoints. Its public Python SDK and documented checkpoint integrity checks and generation fencing put it beyond a purely verbal proposal ([GitHub](https://github.com/nodus-compute/), [Security](https://www.nodus-compute.ai/security/)). This fits my recurring interest in programmable infrastructure built by technical founders. But implementation detail is weaker evidence than a witnessed recovery. The terms explicitly exclude resumption for ephemeral workloads and make recovery conditional on continuity mode. The useful technical proof would be a customer job surviving an interruption and finishing more cheaply, including recovery overhead.

The strongest argument against passing is that ten startups may already be getting exactly that benefit. Kotra reportedly claimed ten pilots and savings above 30% in October 2026. If those teams repeatedly entrust real workloads to Nodus, I could be looking at a small well of urgent demand concealed by thin public reporting. But the claim reaches this dossier through a mirrored search snippet; neither repeated usage nor the savings methodology is established ([reported post](https://workingww.twstalker.com/viswa_kotra)). Ten pilots deserve some demand credit. They do not establish ten users who would complain if the product disappeared. Nor do they supply the unmistakable pull or independently validated proof needed for a known-exception override of my founder gate.

Nodus’s economic opportunity depends on doing the unpleasant work reliably. An H100 supply price of $2.600 an hour, before Nodus fees, does not establish a cheaper completed job or a profitable service ([Pricing](https://www.nodus-compute.ai/pricing/)). Failed runs and founder support consume the apparent bargain. Modal’s multi-cloud platform and SF Compute’s capacity resale market mean Nodus must find users whose particular execution problem remains unsolved. Varshney’s Google cost-optimization work makes the choice of problem plausible, but the dossier never connects that experience to a specific personal collision that produced Nodus. Likewise, it records no rejected hypothesis followed by an evidence-driven product change. Those two dimensions receive their missing-evidence defaults.

I would pass on this record. The familiar red flags—dependence on a major distribution partner, premature delegation, or refusal to change after contrary evidence—are not documented. The problem is that the affirmative founder evidence is missing where my decision depends on it. My condition for reopening the pass is one inspectable four-week customer case: a named production blocker, the founders’ dated shipped response, and separate customer records showing weekly paid use afterward. That would let me distinguish a team learning its way into an indispensable execution system from a technically plausible GPU broker.
