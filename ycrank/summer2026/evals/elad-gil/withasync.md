# Async -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: frontier models can now execute multi-step administrative work for small service businesses, but Async becomes a large company only if each deployment eliminates enough labor to support outcome pricing while requiring progressively less custom work. The dossier does not establish that controlling ratio. “Thousands of hours” automated across four named law firms proves utility, but no contract value, deployment cost, implementation time, gross margin, renewal rate, or expansion rate shows whether Async is building software economics or a technology-enabled services business.

The strongest evidence is inside the workflow. At Clifton Law, agents handle intake, conflict checks, engagement letters, and task assignment without forcing a system migration. Company memory, approval logic, isolated infrastructure, and integrations across email, documents, calendars, CRMs, and databases give Async operational state and responsibility beyond a prompt wrapper. Better models should improve task completion rather than automatically erase these integrations. SOC 2 Type I also addresses a real adoption barrier in regulated environments, although approval queues and attorney oversight preserve meaningful labor and reliability costs.

There is genuine early pull. Martinez Legal, Clemente Mueller, Joshua Lowenthal, and Clifton Law are named deployments, and customers report automating live procedures rather than merely signing LOIs. That is unusually informative at pre-seed. The evidence remains company-reported, however, and all four published stories are law firms. No disclosed revenue, repeat purchase, retention, or capacity constraint demonstrates that customers value the outcome enough to support durable margins.

The bull case is that the apparent services burden is actually the discovery mechanism for a repeatable legal-operations product. Narrow audits identify common workflows; outcome pricing captures part of the labor savings; each successful deployment creates templates and integrations that reduce the cost of the next one. Four customers and thousands of automated hours could be the beginning of this learning curve. If so, expanding immediately across healthcare, real estate, and childcare obscures Async’s best evidence rather than enlarging its market.

My concern is that the team has moved from AI billing infrastructure in 2025 to legal agents in early 2026 and then to four verticals by August 2026 before publishing non-legal customer proof. Lindy and Relevance AI already provide broad agent infrastructure, while specialized competitors can amortize integrations and reliability work within one domain. Async’s audits, customization, separate expansion agreements, and ongoing maintenance may leave gross margins tied to scarce implementation labor. This is the physical-economics red flag translated into AI services: advanced software attached to an unchanged delivery model does not necessarily create software economics.

The founders show commitment and useful iteration. Ben Smith dropped out at 20 after prior selling experience, and he and Connor Park previously built Skope together. That supports persistence and some second-time learning, but the repeated repositioning has not yet isolated the market insight those iterations produced. Neither rare frontier-model expertise nor a documented technical breakthrough supports the exception granted to research-stage companies without clear market economics.

I would not invest today. My 36-month base case is that Async narrows to legal operations and reaches roughly $3 million to $8 million in ARR by August 2029, but remains constrained by forward-deployed implementation rather than becoming a horizontal agent platform. The single flip condition is verifiable: show at least ten law firms purchasing a second workflow within twelve months while deployment hours per new workflow fall by more than 50% and gross margin exceeds 70%. That would validate both reusable distribution and the economic variable currently missing.
