# Shire Intelligence -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a restaurant where nobody walks the floor just to discover what needs doing. A cleared plate becomes a useful signal; a busser gets the right task; a host stops seating three parties in one server’s section. Shire Intelligence’s existing-camera approach is a plausible beginning. But getting there requires reliable service-event recognition, followed by staff trusting the resulting instructions during a chaotic dinner rush. The dossier establishes neither transition yet. Recognizing an occupied table is a smaller achievement than understanding hospitality. That future is my hypothesis, rather than an evidenced six-year map supplied by Shire.

The founders give me more to hold onto. Alex Tabaku spent four years working in restaurants, and Cameron Kuperman worked alongside him; their relationship goes back to kindergarten. Harshith Guduru brings complementary software and research experience. These people have encountered the room they want to coordinate. That fits my preference for an operating irritation becoming a product specification. What remains missing is the chronology of obsession: experiments before the company, repeated attempts to solve this particular problem, or evidence of recruiting others into an independently developed conviction. The dossier identifies a CEO; it supplies no evidence of a leadership shell game or equal-control deadlock.

Why does a host have to keep reconstructing the dining room in their head? Because the restaurant’s software does not automatically know what just happened on the floor. That is a concrete coordination failure, and removing it could return attention to servers and discretion to hosts. It is weaker evidence of an artificial gatekeeper or extracted rent. Shire’s [Mathews Restaurant testimonial](https://shireintelligence.com/) reports $65,000 in payment-processing savings without a measurement period, but those savings do not establish the economics of camera-driven coordination. At $40 a month, the intelligence product offers an inexpensive entry point; the dossier does not show who captures the resulting value or how much staff control improves.

The strongest case against passing is that restaurant workers may already have built the right thing before publishing the evidence investors want. Shire reports seven-minute faster table turns and 14% more monthly guests served. If repeatable, those are meaningful changes to a dining room. Its founders also report a working prototype that won NexHacks’ general track. I can make a pre-revenue exception for a convincing artifact; revenue is not the missing ingredient here. The gap is behavioral proof: no dated deployment cohort shows servers repeatedly accepting its instructions, and the outcome claims lack sample sizes. The prototype deserves credit, while the claimed operating transformation remains lightly supported.

The camera architecture matters to my decision. Shire’s published policy discusses reservation and staff information without specifying footage retention or training use. That leaves my consent and custody gate unresolved. **Flock Safety** illustrates why I would investigate rather than automatically reject camera-derived information: extracting a useful event from an image can create substantial value. The material difference is that recognizing a license plate presented a specific technical utility; Shire still needs to establish that inferred service needs produce dependable action. Meanwhile, disclosed use of SAM3 and DINOv3 establishes implementation ingredients, not ownership of a scarce enabling layer. PreciTaste’s adjacent vision and workflow capabilities make that distinction commercially relevant.

I pass on Shire Intelligence today. The founders’ restaurant experience is the strongest signal, but I cannot yet connect it to a controlled asset that makes the future restaurant possible. The POS offering could finance that destination, consistent with my interest in ordinary beachheads funding harder infrastructure; the dossier does not establish that connection. My verifiable flip condition is one independently observed, consented 90-day restaurant deployment showing staff repeatedly acting on camera-generated tasks and sustaining faster table turns under a documented footage-handling policy. That would move me from Pass to active diligence.
