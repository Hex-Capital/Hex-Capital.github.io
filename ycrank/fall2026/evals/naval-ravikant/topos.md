# Topos -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The September 2026 prime-progression project is the most useful fact about Topos. Ryan Min June Shin and Sohum Sukhatankar have built mathematical software together; Sukhatankar has also researched seizure-localization models at Boston Children’s Hospital. Those are actions I can examine. Harvard, powerlifting, and a spelling championship tell me much less about this business. The founders have relevant mathematical and biological exposure, but the dossier does not establish that either has built formal verification for biological reasoning. That earns meaningful founder credit, short of demonstrated knowledge of the exact problem.

The decisive question is what verifies the verifier. Topos describes agents formalizing problems and checking one another. Multiple agents can reproduce the same mistaken assumption. An independent checker could establish that a conclusion follows from specified premises; it cannot establish that those premises describe biology. Topos must identify which correctness guarantee it sells. The dossier supplies neither a checker specification nor a demonstrated proprietary asset. I assign defensibility its missing-evidence default. An undisclosed architecture is uncertainty, and “swarm” supplies no additional credit.

The strongest case against passing is that these founders occupy a useful intersection: computational mathematics, clinical machine learning, and an existing working relationship. They could discover a tractable class of biological models where verification saves expensive experimental work, then accumulate reusable formalizations. That would fit my preference for owning a difficult technical bottleneck. I sometimes accept missing distribution when an unusual working prototype demonstrates exceptional insight. Topos’s public mathematical artifact supports the founders’ ability, but it does not demonstrate the proposed biological product. The exception has not yet earned its override. There is no documented integrity or financing-pressure red flag; my hesitation concerns the mechanism.

The commercial path is similarly unresolved. Biology is a field, not an initial buyer. The dossier names no purchasing workflow or demonstrated route from that workflow into adjacent spending. Harmonic, Axiom, and Edison establish nearby technical activity, but their financing does not establish demand for Topos. YC participation supplies no customer evidence either. Nor does communication between agents establish a network effect between customers. My preference for permissionless contribution and customer-created value finds no demonstrated mechanism here. Market expansion and user effects therefore receive their respective defaults.

I would pass with my own money today. No disclosed payer, pricing, or transactions means direct value capture also receives its default; being pre-seed is no separate deduction. The founders are the affirmative signal, while the asset that could compound remains hypothetical. **My flip condition is an independently audited customer deployment showing that Topos’s proprietary, reusable formalization corpus catches experimentally confirmed biological errors that the same underlying models miss without it.** That would connect their specific knowledge to something a competitor cannot acquire merely by copying the code.
