# Terse -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Terse’s founders have exposed their knowledge to reality. They have published an actor runtime with SQLite persistence, generated Python and TypeScript clients, and dated releases. Thomas’s seven years on Siri and Apple Intelligence provide relevant context; Olivier’s backend engineering experience supports execution. The strongest evidence is the artifact developers can inspect and run. That earns substantial founder credit. It does not establish an asset competitors cannot reproduce.

The decisive problem is ownership of the resulting value. Terse’s MIT license permits competitors to reuse its implementation, while Cloudflare and Rivet already supply overlapping actor primitives. Self-hosting and decorated persisted fields give developers choices, but the dossier establishes no exclusive data, proprietary model, or demonstrated switching cost. Even the operational details remain consequential: the October 26 release documents out-of-order socket messages and possible message loss on immediate close. Solving these problems could create useful software. Terse still needs a mechanism that lets the company capture something durable from solving them.

The strongest bull case is that developers adopt the primitive first and subsequently pay its creators to operate it. Terse has an inspectable product, 256 GitHub stars, 19 forks, and a Show HN launch with 53 points and 25 comments. That is enough attention to take the possibility seriously. My exception for backing a demonstrated prototype before a distribution loop deserves consideration here. But the dossier does not establish unusual capital efficiency or differentiation strong enough to override the copying problem. The exceptions for an already-forming network or extraordinary founder recruiting have no corresponding evidence here.

Open source also fits my preference for permissionless contribution. The missing step is whether contributors make Terse more useful for one another. Nineteen forks do not establish contributed improvements, reusable integrations, or referrals that compound. Nor does multiplayer functionality inside a customer’s application demonstrate a network effect for Terse itself. Agent infrastructure plausibly opens into broader stateful applications, but the dossier supplies neither a demonstrated adjacent buyer path nor independent evidence of that adjacent spend. Cloudflare’s company-wide revenue cannot fill that gap.

I would pass with my own money. Pricing, paying customers, and revenue are unverified, so direct value capture receives the missing-evidence default. The earlier workflow-platform description does not prove abandonment, and the dossier supplies no integrity or fundraising-pressure red flag. This is a capable builder bet whose commercial advantage remains unproven. One verifiable condition would reopen it: a paid production cohort demonstrably retaining Terse’s hosted service because of a proprietary reliability advantage unavailable in its self-hosted fork. That would show the founders’ specific knowledge accumulating into an owned asset.
