# Torus -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Marcus Lima has already founded Heimdal, Rahul Thayil has already founded Orion, and Torus is still building a capital-light document workflow product. That pairing is Torus’s signature: unusually strong founder learning capacity attached to an unusually weak required-capital profile. Torus may become valuable because repeat founders learn faster than incumbents, but value is not consequence. The economic mechanism Torus currently breaks is engineering rework, not engineering itself.

**Slash** (2024) is the high rung on learning rate because its documented Kanye West pivot showed a founder changing direction when reality contradicted the plan. Torus sits below Slash but above a linear first-time startup: Lima previously raised $9.7M for Heimdal and managed more than $100M of capital projects, while Thayil raised $3.5M for Orion before moving from threat intelligence into physical engineering. Those are strong adaptation signals. Torus diverges from Slash because the dossier contains no documented mistake, thesis reversal, or reason either founder abandoned the prior venture. Serial founding is evidence of motion; it is not yet evidence of learning.

**DoorDash** (2013) is the low-rung exception on required capital: commodity software and logistics initially required no $50M scientific milestone, yet ambition eventually reshaped consumer-commerce flow. Torus reproduces the low-capital mechanism without yet reproducing the exception. Torus offers managed-cloud or customer-network deployments, drafts RFIs and procurement approvals, and appears fundable through ordinary enterprise-software economics. No plant, fleet, foundation-model training program, or >$50M technical milestone exists. I am over-resourced for Torus unless the ambition expands from checking documents to autonomously engineering physical infrastructure.

The $4.86B AI-in-construction market and Torus’s example of a $500K rework order put consequence above a generic document chatbot but below a category-collapse bet. Over 18 months, Torus could compound firm-specific project graphs, approval histories, and more than 50 consistency checks into lower engineering labor and fewer change orders across an industry short 349,000 workers in 2026. But Torus presently improves the incumbent EPC process rather than making that process disappear. A 20-30% better engineering workflow is useful. It is not **Impossible Foods** replacing animal protein or **OpenAI** attacking a large fraction of $15T in labor.

Torus has no published accuracy benchmark, error rate, proprietary model, or defensibility signal, while Trunk Tools already interprets drawings across full project-document corpora. The technical-risk score flips upward only if Torus proves that cross-document parameter reasoning requires a proprietary engineering model or data architecture that general foundation models cannot reproduce, with accuracy high enough to support approvals without routine human checking. Today Torus routes editable outputs through human review and offers more than 50 check categories. That is prudent product design, but it converts a technical miracle into ordinary enterprise execution.

**Glean** made domain-expert continuity acceptable because Arvind Jain paired deep search expertise with a consequential attack on Fortune 500 information asymmetry. Torus has the continuity without the documented heresy: Lima has capital-project experience, and Thayil has AI and autonomous-systems experience, but neither founder is shown contradicting engineering orthodoxy or publicly defending a position experts reject. Torus therefore sits below Glean on founder defiance. Experts extrapolate the past; Torus currently looks like two capable experts applying the newest tool to the workflow they understand.

Multiple Fortune 500 companies and major EPCs reportedly deploying Torus create a credible path to a useful vertical-software outcome. My falsifiable prediction is that by July 2029 Torus reaches $8M-$15M in ARR and enters acquisition discussions with Autodesk, rather than becoming an independent platform that autonomously designs physical infrastructure. Torus can reach that range through founder-led pilots, expansion across engineering packages, and inexpensive software deployment. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself” does not mean every great company is my company.

Trunk Tools has raised $70M and already overlaps Torus across drawings, specifications, RFIs, revisions, bids, and submittals; the strongest bear case is that Torus becomes a feature before customer-specific project graphs become a moat. Incomplete SOC 2 certification, no disclosed contract values, and no accuracy benchmark make that case sting. Torus survives the bear case because two repeat founders already report deployments with Fortune 500 companies and major EPCs, an unusually informative pre-seed signal, but opacity does not earn a consequence-first check. One milestone would flip my verdict to Invest: $3M of disclosed ARR from at least three named EPC customers that each renew after twelve months.
