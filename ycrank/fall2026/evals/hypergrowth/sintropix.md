# Sintropix -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with multi-entity companies, Sintropix’s most portable workflow is the recurring close. The memo describes a shared ledger that ingests bank, invoice, payroll, and expense data, posts transactions, and consolidates results across currencies. The founders reported 10 ERP customers across Chile, Mexico, Brazil, and the U.S. by September. That is an adoption signal for a two-founder team. The memo leaves paid production, realized revenue, and the ERP’s commercial growth rate unverified. The strongest expansion mechanism is the reported monthly fee tied to entities and integrations. Customers that add either can spend more, although realized expansion and churn remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

The documented acquisition path runs through Launch YC and founder-led demos. Whether buyers can purchase, connect their data, and complete a first close independently remains unclear. The next-account contribution would be its monthly fee less attributable selling, setup, infrastructure, support, and exception-review costs, plus any filing work the deployment requires. Automated matching and posting could reduce labor per close. The memo gives too little evidence on fees and workload to establish positive contribution. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The strongest countercase is that buyers require local filing alongside the close. The memo identifies country-specific filing work in Brazil and human review for ambiguous transactions. If comparable buyers need both, new markets may require substantial compliance work, and new accounts may add recurring service labor. That constrains the portability of the bundled product. The structural downside gate remains open because the shared close and consolidation workflow offers a plausible route for buyers that keep their existing local filing arrangements. Buyer willingness to pay for that configuration remains unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can paying multi-entity customers in a new jurisdiction complete their first close on the existing ERP with standard integrations, limited founder help, and positive incremental contribution while keeping local filing in place? A production rollout across several paying accounts, with setup and review time recorded, would test that path. Repeatable results would support Reach out if rescoring reaches 65 and confidence rises to at least Medium. Evidence that ordinary buyers require substantial jurisdiction-specific rebuilding to adopt the ERP would change the action to Pass.
