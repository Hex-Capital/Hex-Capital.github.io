# OpenScaffold -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

OpenScaffold becomes consequential if deployment expertise stops being a scarce human service and becomes reproducible computation. Generating an agent scaffold from discovery, business objectives, and previous deployments could be a mechanism for that transition. But the dossier supplies neither the labor baseline nor the share of deployment work the product can eliminate. I cannot price an extraordinary outcome from the word “autonomous.” The consequence remains unquantified.

The technical question is whether the scaffold can carry customer requirements through execution without repeatedly handing difficult decisions back to engineers. Auctor already connects discovery to delivery artifacts; Tandem accumulates account context; June implements enterprise systems with reviewed, sandbox-tested changes. OpenScaffold needs a measurable advantage against those constraints. No comparative benchmark, deployment-success methodology, or proprietary learning effect is disclosed. That leaves technical discontinuity unproven. It does not establish that the founders are merely wrapping a model, either; applying that red flag would outrun the evidence.

The strongest argument for writing a small check is the team’s proximity to building real systems. Rushil Knagaram was employee number three at USUL; Param Mehta was a founding engineer there and previously led an AI platform for transaction-error handling at JPMorgan. They could understand where implementation labor hides and which decisions can become software. Execution-heavy enterprise businesses also fit the broader investing pattern beyond scientific breakthroughs. However, these credentials do not document rapid belief revision or recruiting against a specific weakness. The October 26, 2026 positioning change from Sigma’s “Lakehouse-as-a-Service” could reflect learning, but the directory record establishes neither the evidence behind that decision nor its operational timing. I give it no adaptability premium.

OpenScaffold’s largest unresolved risk is reliable execution across customer environments at an economically useful level of human supervision. The dossier contains no costed experiment, pass/fail threshold, or spending sequence aimed at that risk. Nor does it disclose pricing, inference costs, review labor, or support burden. Cheap agent runs can still produce expensive deployments. No public revenue or repeat-use evidence resolves that uncertainty; at this stage, their absence is ordinary rather than an adverse signal. There is also no evidence of premature sales expansion, subsidy dependence, or exit-driven founders. These dimensions receive their missing-evidence defaults.

I pass at **25/100**. The known exceptions to my gates require a documented instigator addressing an enormous existing value pool, or exceptional evidence that the consequence justifies buying the uncertainty. This dossier establishes neither strongly enough to override the missing proof. My one verifiable flip condition is an auditable benchmark across three unrelated paying customers showing at least a 10x reduction in fully loaded deployment cost against their existing process, at equivalent scope and acceptance quality, including all founder intervention and rework. That would make the proposed expertise-cost collapse concrete enough to reopen the investment decision.
