# Quivly AI -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Quivly AI has evidence that someone wants what it makes. Siftly, Octolane, and Hyperspell are named customers; the product reportedly covers more than 10,000 of its customers’ accounts; and the CEO says it generates revenue. Those are three different claims. The account count measures workload, not paying customers, and the revenue amount is undisclosed. But together they establish something more useful than enthusiasm for an AI demo: post-sales teams have entrusted the product with actual work ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/quivly-ai), [CEO statement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/chandrikamaheshwari_since-sharing-were-joining-y-combinator-activity-7507482894622228480-KPRa)).

The missing fact is what happened when these founders got stuck. There is no named obstacle followed by a dated workaround and an observable result. There is also no documented hypothesis they abandoned because customers disproved it. June and July product releases establish continued building, but a release calendar cannot establish resourcefulness or learning. I give both dimensions their missing-evidence defaults. This matters because I am deciding whether to back the people through several changes of product, and the dossier mostly shows their current product.

The reason to keep looking is that the founders encountered the underlying problem before choosing this company. Maheshwari previously built internal customer-intelligence tools, and Agrawal worked on pricing and billing infrastructure. Reconstructing one account across billing, usage, support, and conversations is exactly the sort of tedious work that can conceal an opportunity. Their experience makes the consumption-based wedge credible, though the origin account lacks a dated artifact connecting the earlier tools to Quivly’s creation ([CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/swartz-center-for-entrepreneurship/resources-funding-and-talent/venturebridge/venturebridge-2025-spotlight-quivly.html)). The strongest version of Quivly starts with one customer whose consumption changes require an immediate, specific response.

The product has substance. Documented releases include MCP access and configurable agent actions; approval thresholds and action logs address the consequences of letting software write into customer systems. That earns technical credit beyond architecture claims, although there is no independently measured reliability result ([product updates](https://www.quivly.ai/blog/july-2026-updates), [agent controls](https://www.quivly.ai/platform/ai-agents)). It partially matches my pattern of backing programmable tools that users can adapt. The missing part is evidence that users are inventing workflows the founders did not anticipate. Workshops make that possible; they do not establish that it has happened.

The strongest objection to withholding a check is the customer evidence. Mycroft says its customer-success managers handle up to 60% more ARR, and Quivly reports onboarding falling from 30 days to seven. A small team delivering those improvements could have found a deep well of demand ([customer testimonial](https://www.quivly.ai/), [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/quivly-ai)). But the 4.3× expansion claim describes customers’ revenue, not Quivly’s growth, and its cohort and attribution are unspecified. I checked the exception for a working product with unmistakable pull: these reports justify serious interest, but do not yet supply an override to the missing founder evidence. Nor do they match the independently validated numerical proofs that have justified other unusual bets.

I would hold my money for now. Gainsight already offers overlapping agents and account-based pricing, so Quivly’s advantage will have to emerge through faster learning about a particular user’s problem ([Gainsight](https://www.gainsight.com/press/gainsight-proves-ai-native-services-model-for-retention-as-atlas-manages-over-75m-in-customer-arr/)). Founder-led workshops are encouraging; there is no evidence of dependence on a grand launch or a major partner. Unknown cofounder history is also an unknown, rather than an adverse finding. My one condition for reopening the investment decision is a customer-verified account of a consequential deployment blocker that the founders overcame with a shipped workaround within two weeks, followed by that customer continuing the workflow. That would reveal the people behind the product.
