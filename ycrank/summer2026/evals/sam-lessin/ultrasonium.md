# Ultrasonium -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The proposed post-money valuation is unknown, the competing-term-sheet count is unknown, and YC, Glasswing Ventures, and Blindspot Ventures are already involved. That does not prove an auction, but it does not establish the cheap, scarce first check I need either. With 17 employees and fourteen advertised openings, this is also not obviously a small experiment waiting for one catalytic check. On price—the first gate—I have almost no usable evidence.

The $305,000 NSF Phase I award comes closest to defining a real venture moment. Ultrasonium must show that acoustic fields can shape molten metal with submillimeter accuracy, density above 99%, acceptable surface finish, and progression from low-temperature alloys to aluminum and copper. Those are falsifiable technical results, and success would materially change the value of the patent issued in January 2025. But the dossier gives no deadline and never says the equity round is organized around completing that experiment. “Hire fourteen people and pursue aerospace, defense, energy, compute, machinists, research, dental, and medical” is not one card being flipped.

The team is the strongest part of the case. Chris Carter has worked on additively manufactured steel and NASA-funded melt-pool simulations; Alexander Urbanski founded a DOE-funded energy company and was the first employee at manufacturing-monitoring company Phase3D; Jack Qiu and Hunter Brown bring control physics and national-laboratory hardware experience. This is a credible translation layer between computational field control and physical metallurgy, not four software founders discovering manufacturing from a market report. The unresolved founder issue is equally concrete: four co-founders carry CEO, CSO, engineering, and research titles, but nothing identifies who has final authority when product requirements, scientific purity, and financing conflict.

The commercial evidence does not yet earn an exception to the missing-deal gate. The 15x speed and 75% cost claims lack disclosed test methods, comparison systems, part geometries, or production volumes. TigerLaunch, 248 LinkedIn followers, and an SBIR award demonstrate attention and technical legitimacy, not that Ultrasonium owns a workflow that causes purchases. No machinist, aerospace contractor, or research institution is named as a pilot or buyer. Meanwhile Seurat and VulcanForms have raised roughly $178 million and $575 million, respectively, which is evidence that this category can become a capital factory long before it becomes a cash-generating business.

The bull case stings: a patented, powderless process built by unusually relevant researchers could make incumbent capitalization irrelevant if it really delivers comparable parts fifteen times faster at one-quarter the cost. Physical-world complexity is not itself a problem, and the measurable NSF targets are better than generic hard-tech aspiration. But exception-level underwriting requires external commercial proof or validated operating economics. Ultrasonium discloses no revenue, pricing, gross margin, burn, runway, or credible route to break-even without another round. Fourteen openings point in the opposite direction.

I am passing today. My flip condition is one paid machine order from a named machinist or industrial customer, contingent on independently verified density, accuracy, throughput, and per-part cost results. That single contract would simultaneously show that the technical experiment survived contact with production and that an owned process can cause a purchase; another prize, grant, benchmark claim, or investor logo would not.
