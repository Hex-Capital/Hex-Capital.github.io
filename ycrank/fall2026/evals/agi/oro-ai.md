# ORO AI -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** As AI labs test longer-running agents, they need evaluation environments that reveal failures whose costs surface weeks after a decision. ORO’s produce-buying simulation runs matched 90-day scenarios with and without delayed customer and supplier effects. ORO reports that no model beat the fixed buying rule in every run and that the best model averaged $7,749 in simulated profit versus $10,795 for the scripted expert. The prototype demonstrates a way to measure a specific reliability gap. Whether labs struggle to obtain equivalent evaluations remains open. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Wholesalers helped calibrate the simulation, and ORO controls access to its grader. If frontier-quality models become cheap and widely available, labs could still pay ORO for credible calibration and repeatable grading if those are costly to reproduce. The memo records 670 dataset downloads in a month, an early interest signal with buyer identity, budget, and repeat use unresolved. The founders’ distributed-systems experience and the shipped leaderboard and run traces support seed-stage execution. Hosted licensing is an inferred business model, and the memo gives no cost data for calibrating, running, or maintaining additional workflows. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea)

### Countercase

A lab could use a vendor such as Veris to recreate its own workflows or build a comparable test using SMB Bench’s public methods and dataset. ORO has demonstrated calibration for one buying workflow, and the memo establishes no exclusive data rights. This makes substitution credible, while equivalent performance and cost remain untested. The scarcity and control gate remains open, which supports Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
