# Standard Machines -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The important missing fact is not revenue. It is what broke. Jacob Peake worked on GPU architecture at Apple, so he has been near the problem, but the dossier names no obstacle he encountered, workaround he built, or result that followed. “RL environments for chip design” could describe something a fierce nerd built after fighting an EDA workflow, or something inferred from a fashionable market map. These are very different companies. The public evidence does not tell me which this is.

The technical claim remains a noun rather than a proof. There is no accessible product, benchmark, deployment, taped-out chip, or threshold crossed by an agent trained in Standard Machines’ environments. Cadence Cerebrus and Synopsys DSO.ai already apply reinforcement learning to design optimization. ChipAgents, Cognichip, and Ricursive have collectively raised hundreds of millions of dollars. This does not make the market unattractive, but it raises the proof required: Standard Machines must demonstrate a task or learning loop those systems cannot readily reproduce.

The strongest bull case is Peake himself. An Imperial graduate who subsequently designed GPU architecture at Apple has real contact with both the technical substrate and the ugly constraints around chip development. Training-data access, proprietary design IP, tool integration, and multi-year verification cycles create precisely the sort of schlep that can conceal an opportunity. An environment layer could also become a programmable primitive for other AI-chip systems. But resemblance to a useful primitive is not developer adoption, and exposure to a schlep is not evidence that Peake has yet found a way through it.

There is likewise no narrow well of users. No chip team, AI lab, or EDA vendor is reported using the product; there are no repeated runs, signed LOIs, or customer-controlled datasets. A $3.46 billion market growing 35% annually explains why competitors exist. It does not identify who wants this crude product now. The absence is acceptable at pre-seed, but it cannot satisfy the demand half of the investment gate.

The exception I have made for extremely young technical founders required numerical technical proof plus direct exposure to the problem. Peake has credible exposure but no disclosed proof. Nor does Standard Machines show the pre-round proof spike that distinguishes several of my recent technical investments. I see no affirmative launch-theatre, partnership, premature-hiring, or delegation red flag; the trouble is simpler. Nearly every decisive claim is still untested.

I would pass today. My verifiable flip condition is one named semiconductor team repeatedly using a working Standard Machines environment to train an agent that beats its existing flow on a specified design task and measured benchmark. That single result would reveal the product, the user, and perhaps the founder response to the data-access blocker. Until then, I am being asked to invest in the category and résumé rather than the running back.
