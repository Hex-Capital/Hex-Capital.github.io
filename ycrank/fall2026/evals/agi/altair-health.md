# Altair Health -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass

### Company case

Altair supplies medical billing services. The memo identifies no AI workload that depends on an input Altair supplies, so the building-blocks path scores zero. For the industry-enabled path, independent practices already manage billing across separate systems using spreadsheets and phone calls. Altair says it connects to existing EHRs and clearinghouses, checks proposed claim actions against encoded payer policies, and routes exceptions to in-house billers. That design could reduce routine review labor, but the memo leaves two threshold questions unanswered: which practices or billing uses were previously priced out, and whether Altair lowers the fully loaded cost per successfully reimbursed claim enough to serve them. The industry-enabled path also scores zero, triggering Pass. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Altair reports tracking policies across more than 3,400 payers and reusing payer-specific resolutions. That operating know-how could help it retain customers, though its advantage over competing billing products remains untested. Existing spreadsheet and phone workarounds establish a concrete buyer problem, and the founder-reported clinic use of the earlier co-pilot adds a limited demand signal. The reported conference launch and the founders’ software sales and clinical ML experience support execution credit, with dependable delivery of the current managed service still unverified. Directory pricing and unmeasured exception labor leave scale economics at the default. (Defensibility, The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

If small practices can afford only selective manual review today, automated policy checks could make routine prechecks across their claims viable. The CEO’s report that a billing team prechecked hundreds of claims with the earlier co-pilot makes that scenario plausible. Under model parity, practices could still pay for EHR and clearinghouse integration, policy maintenance, and human exception handling. The reported use leaves the clinic’s prior review coverage, labor savings, and willingness to pay for the current service unknown. It therefore leaves the Pass gate in effect. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

Can the current managed service make routine claim prechecks affordable for an independent practice that previously could afford only selective review? A paid deployment with a documented prior baseline and comparable claim mix would move the action to Reach out if it showed sustained broader precheck coverage at a cost per successfully reimbursed claim the practice can afford, including policy upkeep, human exceptions, and rework.
