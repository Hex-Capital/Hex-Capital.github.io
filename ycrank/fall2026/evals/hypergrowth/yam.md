# Yam -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A, product-led adoption.** Yam targets women who already seek out fanfiction, dating sims, or romance shows and want to shape the story themselves. Creators publish games through shareable links, and players can start free. If the story brings players back, currency packs and subscriptions give Yam two ways to monetize repeat play. The public catalog spans 112 fandoms, and its top listing displays 2.4 million plays. That shows creators can publish games that draw activity. The share of new players who arrive through creator links, reach a first playable story, and convert remains unknown. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)

Yam reports 1 million cumulative beta play hours and more than three hours of average daily playtime per daily active user. That suggests some players commit meaningful time to the experience, an early signal of fit. Cohort returns, paid expansion, and realized revenue remain unknown. Chinese-language coverage is a weak signal of discovery beyond the U.S. A second-market test would check whether the same story engine and sharing flow work with localized dialogue, culturally relevant games, and local moderation and payment requirements. (Market & Traction; Key Facts; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Yam’s strongest visible play count belongs to a fan game that could be removed after a rights-holder request. If original or licensed games draw fewer players, the creator channel may be harder to repeat. Heavy free play could also raise costs faster than revenue. In that scenario, each additional AI-generated turn adds model costs, more players add moderation and support work, and play on free accounts has to be covered by what paying players spend after payment fees. The memo gives no per-turn cost or paid conversion data. A structural Pass gate remains unproven, so a measured cohort is more informative than a conversation today. (Market & Traction; The Idea; Key Facts; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can creator links to original or licensed games produce a returning, paying cohort in a second market using the same self-serve flow? A tracked cohort that reaches first play, returns across multiple sessions, and generates enough pack and subscription revenue to cover incremental acquisition, model usage, moderation, support, and payment costs would move Yam to **Reach out**.
