# twentyfour26 -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest fit. twentyfour26 offers finished CNC parts from its own San Francisco shop. The company describes using software to screen CAD uploads and simulate machining plans for jobs that fit its standardized cell. The scenario to test is whether this workflow saves enough labor for hardware teams to order individual prototype revisions they previously batched or skipped. Cheap models alone cannot deliver a finished part. The potential reason to choose twentyfour26 is reliable delivery of an inspected part at a price and lead time that make those extra orders worthwhile. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The Fall 2026 memo documents a service launch and per-job quote terms. The company reports producing parts for Bay Area hardware teams, with turnaround as fast as about 48 hours. The memo leaves payment, repeat orders, and typical turnaround unverified. The founders bring relevant hardware design and software experience, and the launch shows progress toward a live workflow. Automated loading, cutting, and checking remain in development. That supports a focused seed-stage trial. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

The standardized cell is a plausible unit of expansion. Compare human minutes per successful job with a conventional shop at equivalent tolerances and delivery reliability, counting quote review, setup, interventions, retries, and inspection. Then subtract direct labor, software, machine running costs, materials, scrap, and fulfillment from the realized job price. A second cell would need enough eligible orders at that contribution to cover equipment and shop overhead. Geometry, material, and workholding limits narrow the eligible pool, and the memo gives no measured contribution or utilization. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The strongest countercase is that twentyfour26 makes existing prototype orders faster while leaving the cost of difficult jobs largely intact. Exceptions and rework could absorb the labor savings, and well-capitalized competitors can combine comparable CAM tools with owned production and broader supplier capacity. Protolabs already reports automated toolpath verification, and twentyfour26’s proposed feedback loop from inspection to machining plans is still a development goal. The memo establishes neither a scarce capacity position for Path A nor like-for-like substitution that defeats Path B. Both Path B conditions remain unproven, so Watch applies. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent hardware teams repeatedly pay twentyfour26 for prototype revisions they previously skipped or batched when they can choose a shop with comparable CAM capabilities? A matched paid trial should document buyers’ prior ordering behavior, compare offers at equivalent tolerances and delivery reliability, and track labor through successful delivery, including rejected attempts and rework. Repeat paid orders at positive contribution, with buyers choosing twentyfour26 for its delivered price or lead time under comparable automation, could move the action to Reach out if the reassessed score reaches 65 with at least medium confidence. If the trial shows that savings disappear after exceptions or buyers readily switch to an equivalent offer, move to Pass.
