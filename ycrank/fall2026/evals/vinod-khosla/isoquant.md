# isoquant -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Isoquant’s most consequential number is its reported 91.7% reduction in inference cost on banking intent classification. That is roughly a 12x improvement, alongside 91.8% accuracy versus GPT-5.4 mini’s 74.7%. If reproducible across expensive expert workflows, specialization could change which services are economically deliverable. But this dossier establishes one classification experiment, not that larger consequence. The $21.1 billion inference-software market supplies a spending baseline; it does not quantify the expertise, resources, or population that isoquant could reach by collapsing costs. I see a potentially useful technical mechanism. I cannot yet price the world-changing option.

The technical evidence deserves attention. On GLM-5.3-Flash, isoquant reports 452 ms median time to first token against Together’s 890 ms, Fireworks’ 1,550 ms, and Baseten’s 1,320 ms. Those are approximately 2–3.4x improvements, below my discontinuity threshold, but a customer’s report of twice-as-fast agent inference supplies some supporting evidence. The banking experiment is stronger: a named baseline, 3,079 requests, and simultaneous improvements in accuracy and estimated cost. It earns meaningful technical credit. Its unresolved limitation is transfer: a specialist beating a general model on one bounded task does not establish a repeatable advantage across production workloads.

The strongest argument against passing is that two founders have already turned an internal capability into infrastructure people apparently want. Aniket Deshpande brings model-training and reinforcement-learning infrastructure experience; Mugdhaa Patankar brings distributed serving and developer-tooling experience. Their transition from Agent FM to commercializing its inference optimizations is an observed change in route, although the dossier does not document the evidence that changed their beliefs or difficult recruits won. Current-product claims of 1.2 trillion tokens in a month and 1,000-plus customers are substantial usage signals for this stage, even with no paying-customer breakdown. Agent FM’s 60-plus paying users belong to the earlier product. This team could discover a valuable specialization business before larger providers recognize the opportunity.

That possibility fits my preference for turning a scarce technical workflow into a repeatable platform. It also tests my exceptions for evolving products and founders pursuing large existing value pools before formal milestones. Isoquant has genuine evidence of product evolution, but neither a quantified extraordinary consequence nor a demonstrated compounding technical advantage sufficient to override the remaining gates. Kernel optimization and workload training give this more substance than an interface alone. Conversely, customer-data training requires written consent, so a proprietary learning loop cannot simply be assumed. Advertising measurement establishes neither an advertising-tool business nor dependence on paid distribution. The relevant competitive question is whether isoquant improves faster than Together, Fireworks, and Baseten can reproduce its gains.

The largest kill risk is sustainable economic superiority. A posted price of $0.07 per million input tokens and $0.15 per million output tokens tells me what customers pay, not what isoquant spends. There is no disclosed bridge from GPU utilization, tuning expense, and workload mix to unsubsidized margins. Nor does the dossier specify a costed experiment, numerical threshold, and stop-or-change decision aimed at that risk. I assign the absent-evidence defaults here. Small team size, missing revenue disclosure, and an unfinished assurance process do not establish failure.

I pass with my own money today. The plausible business is specialized workload optimization; the evidence does not yet support ownership of a broadly superior inference cost curve. My one flip condition is a prospective, independently audited 90-day production trial across three unrelated customers demonstrating at least 10x lower fully loaded cost than their named incumbent at equivalent task quality, with positive gross margins after tuning and infrastructure costs. That result would connect the promising experiment to a repeatable economic engine.
