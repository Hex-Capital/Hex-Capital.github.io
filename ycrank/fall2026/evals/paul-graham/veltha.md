# Veltha -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The useful fact about Veltha is that founder outreach initially got no replies, and the founders changed what they did. Edin’s July account reports response rates rising from 0% to 5% and more than 50 conference leads. That is a small, observable response to resistance. It tells me more than the Palantir credentials. But it stops before the important part: which prospect gave them a difficult requirement, what did they build, and did that person then use it? I give the founders credit for learning how to reach buyers, without treating a better conversation as a shipped solution.

The product attacks work that looks sufficiently unpleasant to contain an opportunity. Someone must collect missing records by phone or fax, connect evidence to governing rules, and take responsibility for a determination. Veltha proposes doing that work with licensed adjusters signing the decisions. Operating the service could teach the founders things that selling another claims dashboard would never reveal. Roland’s underwriting-automation experience and Edin’s government AI deployments make this a credible technical team for regulated workflows. They do not establish that either founder personally encountered the workers’ compensation problem that produced this company. That origin remains missing.

The strongest argument for investing is the claimed reduction from six hours per file to ten minutes, alongside four times the caseload per adjuster. If those results survive real claims, the founders may have built something buyers will endure considerable inconvenience to obtain. Source-linked determinations are also a more concrete artifact than a promise to automate insurance. But both performance figures are company-reported, with no disclosed methodology or customer identification. Faster file preparation and higher end-to-end capacity are different measurements. I cannot tell which remaining tasks consume the time, or whether accuracy survives the increased workload. Veltha has some technical proof, but it has not yet supplied the unusually concrete proof spike that would make me overlook weak commercial evidence.

Workers’ compensation adjusters are a recognizable user niche. The dossier does not show them pulling the product back. Fifty leads are worth something; they are not fifty users, and the 451 LinkedIn followers add nothing to this question. Founder-led outreach is encouraging because the team is doing its own selling rather than waiting for a large partner. Still, there is no documented repeated use, payment, or referral. Nor can I award adaptability points merely because Co-Adjuster software became a TPA offering. That could be an excellent discovery about what customers actually buy. Without the observation that caused the change, it is a sequence of descriptions rather than demonstrated learning.

The competitive problem makes that distinction consequential. Sedgwick already combines claims operations with AI, and Reserv already offers human claims handling with explainable automation. A roughly $5 billion workers’ compensation TPA market leaves room for a substantial business, but automation alone does not explain why an employer entrusts Veltha with its claims. The bull case is that these founders personally absorb the operational burden, win a tiny group of employers, and learn faster than established administrators. The working-product and exceptional-user-pull exceptions could justify backing them before their founder history is fully documented. Here, the product evidence remains self-reported and the user pull remains prospective, so neither supplies the override.

I would pass on the evidence available. Veltha has earned more attention than another generic insurance AI pitch, but my people-first gate requires a stronger example of execution under constraint than improved prospecting. The missing revenue history is ordinary at this stage; the unresolved question is what happens after a buyer says something difficult. My flip condition is one documented employer deployment showing a specific claims-workflow blocker, a founder-built fix shipped within weeks, and subsequent repeated paid use with adjuster-verified accuracy and handling time. That would connect the founders’ resourcefulness to a product someone needs.
