# Decawork -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is not yet large enough. Decawork points to an $11.14 billion non-human-identity market and a $308.3 million AI-governance market, but those figures measure spending, not transformation. The product may prevent agent incidents and reduce IT labor, yet the dossier never quantifies today’s losses, administrator hours, or population affected. This is risk-control software, not evidence of a 100x collapse in the cost of operating enterprises.

The technical case is weaker. Per-agent identities, scoped credentials, approval gates, gateway monitoring, and attributed logs are useful, but Okta already registers agents, assigns credentials, governs access, and benefits from an installed integration network. Astrix, Noma, WitnessAI, and Zenity overlap from adjacent directions. Decawork provides no accuracy, deployment-time, incident-rate, or remediation-speed benchmark against any of them. SOC 2 status is necessary trust infrastructure; it is not a technical discontinuity. Routing every tool call through one gateway may even concentrate operational risk before it creates defensibility.

The founders are the real option. Sarthak Aggarwal worked on NVIDIA systems reportedly deployed at OpenAI and Meta and on enterprise AI used by Microsoft and Hitachi. Aman Raj built compliance tooling used by more than 240 Barclays teams. Their previous autonomous-IT product reached multiple enterprises in India and the United States, and they subsequently redirected the company toward agent governance. That is relevant domain exposure and some evidence of tactical flexibility. It is not yet proof of rapid belief revision: the dossier does not explain what evidence triggered the pivot, what assumption was falsified, or which complementary recruit owns security architecture, reliability, or enterprise distribution.

The bull case is that these founders have arrived early at a control point that becomes mandatory when companies operate thousands of autonomous agents. Prior enterprise deployment, reported SOC 2 status by July 2026, and a platform spanning launch through retirement could let Decawork become the system of record rather than another security scanner. The **Block** exception is tempting because both begin with a narrow operational wedge capable of expanding into infrastructure. The material difference is that Decawork has not demonstrated an enormous captured value pool, current-product usage, or founder behavior strong enough to override commodity technology.

Capital is also not being tied to a decisive kill test. No current customer, revenue, usage volume, latency threshold, outage target, or competitive trial is disclosed. The largest risk is straightforward: will enterprises place a startup gateway between agents and production systems when Okta and heavily funded security vendors can bundle overlapping controls? SOC 2 does not answer that question. Neither does the dossier provide pricing or a bottom-up calculation showing that Decawork costs less than an incumbent bundle plus internal IT labor.

I pass. The team may build a respectable enterprise product, but the present evidence describes implementation breadth in a crowded category, not a Black-Swan mechanism. One condition flips my decision: three named enterprises each routing more than one million production tool calls per month through Decawork while demonstrating at least a 5x reduction in mean time to remediate agent incidents versus their prior Okta-native stack. That would establish both technical discontinuity and a credible learning loop.
