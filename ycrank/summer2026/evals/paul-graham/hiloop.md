# hiloop -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Karan Brar led ML infrastructure at DynamoAI, and Thomas Boser built production ML systems at Discord, SoFi, Crosswise, and Reducto. That makes hiloop an organic-idea company whose founders have lived inside the problem, but hiloop has thinner public hacker genealogy than their résumés imply. Nine public repositories for Brar, a seven-star FPGA demo for Boser, and almost no adoption of hiloop’s seven repositories show competent builders, not yet hackers who bent a community around an artifact.

**Stripe** is the high rung for organic ideas: the Collisons spent years wrestling with internet commerce before turning their own pain into a developer primitive. Brar and Boser met while working on ML, post-training, and platform systems at Reducto, so hiloop sits just below that rung; hiloop is plainly adjacent to pain they experienced, but the dossier never says they built hiloop for themselves before anyone asked. That missing sentence matters. It separates an organic idea from an informed market selection, though their DynamoAI and Reducto history keeps the distinction narrow.

**Kiko**’s low-rung failure mechanism was technical competence without a hacker artifact powerful enough to overcome timing; a calendar could be built, but it did not pull the world toward it. Hiloop has more serious machinery—forkable snapshots, experiment lineage, brokered secrets, and 4,188 autonomous experiments—but hiloop’s public SDKs have one star at most. Hacker genealogy is not the same as having engineering jobs. Boser’s seven-star NIPS2017 FPGA repository and Brar’s nine repositories place hiloop above the low rung, but far below the pre-company technical wake left by Stripe or Reddit.

**4,188 experiments on 50 Nvidia B200s in two days** are hiloop’s best evidence for the animal test. Hiloop did not merely describe recursive improvement; hiloop built enough infrastructure to beat a cited autoresearch result and reduce NanoGPT runtime by 12.54%. Over the next 18 months, that resourcefulness compounds only if the founders convert expensive experiments into unscalable customer work—personally wiring evaluations, secrets, and customer-cloud deployments. No revenue, named customer, or cereal-box stunt is public, so the Airbnb exception does not yet apply: the artifact is impressive, but there are no tracks showing what Brar and Boser do after a customer says no.

**Prime Intellect’s 6,000 reported customers** make hiloop’s schlep ambiguous. The unpleasant part is not provisioning GPUs; well-funded clouds already do that. Hiloop earns a higher score because reproducible experiment lineage, brokered customer secrets, statistical verification, and on-premises deployment force hiloop into the grimy boundary between research code and production systems. The score would flip upward if Brar and Boser were manually operating recurring campaigns inside three named customer clouds; it would flip downward if hiloop remained a cleaner interface over rented compute.

**Reddit** had founders who already knew one another as dorm-room hackers, which made disagreement survivable before the product worked. Brar and Boser met at Reducto and worked together on ML, post-training, and platform systems, giving hiloop a credible pair with complementary shared context. Hiloop does not disclose whether that relationship lasted six months or several years, so hiloop falls short of the strongest pair configuration. Still, this avoids the solo-founder and startup-speed-dating red flags, and neither founder presents as an MBA planning to hire the builders later.

**No public revenue is reported for hiloop**, and within 36 months I expect hiloop to reach $1–3 million ARR through customer-specific optimization campaigns and then be acquired by Together AI, because hiloop’s lived workflow knowledge can create an embedded infrastructure wedge while hiloop’s weak public hacker pull makes an independent developer default less likely.

**50 B200 GPUs for two days** supply the stinging bear case: hiloop may be disguising brute-force compute as recursive self-improvement while Prime Intellect, OpenPipe, and Together AI bundle the useful pieces with vastly greater capital and distribution. Hiloop’s 12.54% NanoGPT runtime gain could remain a benchmark curiosity, and premium compute burn is one of my explicit red flags. What keeps hiloop investable is the narrower machinery—forkable snapshots, one-trace observability, brokered secrets, and customer-cloud deployment—which addresses the schlep incumbents tend to expose rather than solve. If hiloop cannot show three paying customers running repeat campaigns by July 2027, I would treat the benchmark as a demo without an animal behind it and flip to Pass.
