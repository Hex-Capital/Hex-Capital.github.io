# Shepherd Robotics -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** Shepherd could expand skilled technician capacity for data center commissioning by taking over repetitive cabling and freeing technicians to troubleshoot. The memo records a public prototype demonstration and a 49.1% year-over-year increase in U.S. private data center construction spending through September 2026. Shepherd reports bench success of 95% for cable routing and 60% for cable insertion. Those results support task-level feasibility. Net technician time saved on a live commissioning job remains unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Cheaper manipulation models could reduce the effort required to adapt robots to new sites. Shepherd’s local adaptation pipeline is an early capability, and the founders bring factory robot deployment and autonomy experience. The memo records no buyer commitment or revenue signal. In a rollout scenario, each additional site would need enough qualified work to cover hardware, integration, and support, with technician hours saved after setup, supervision, and rework. The memo provides no site-level cost or utilization figures. (Defensibility, Founders & Team, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

With abundant capital and comparable models, competing integrators could use open checkpoints and fund their own site adaptation. Watney reports operating robots in customer data centers, and Nines targets fiber patching. Shepherd’s site-specific training could recur as a cost at every deployment. Reuse of technician corrections depends on customer permission and whether those corrections transfer. The memo leaves both durable control and field-level bottleneck relief unproven. That keeps the seed case at Watch because a buyer-run trial can test both. Path B lacks evidence of a newly viable transaction. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
