# Forward -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Forward’s founders have earned the right to attack this problem. Riley Janjis worked on more than $1B of credit diligence at Rixon Capital and Longreach Alternatives; Daniel Yoon worked in private credit at Kain Lawyers. Their reported 200-plus daily cold calls put that knowledge into contact with buyers. This is specific knowledge acquired through work. Their previous tutoring business demonstrates an ability to operate together, but it does not become a private-credit advantage merely because it succeeded.

The investment turns on what those founders can accumulate beyond their own effort. Forward structures borrower information and matches it to lender mandates. If a competitor copied the software tomorrow, the announced Fidelity Bank, Hudson Cove, and iPartners relationships would remain. Those relationships are nonexclusive, however, and the dossier establishes neither proprietary execution data nor a demonstrated matching advantage. Fifty funds onboarded is useful supply. It does not yet show that the fifty-first fund makes existing participants more successful. Founder persistence can start a marketplace; it cannot substitute indefinitely for marketplace liquidity.

The strongest objection to waiting is that Forward may already be crossing that threshold. Investor-reported annualised revenue of $180K within two months is meaningful at this stage. Founder-reported processed volume exceeding $300M suggests access to substantial transactions. But processed volume is not disclosed funded volume, and the revenue report identifies neither payer nor pricing, recurrence, or margins. These figures support early value capture. They do not establish that transactions close more reliably as the network grows. My working expectation is that, by October 2029, Forward will earn most of its revenue from software-assisted origination or transaction services rather than a largely self-service marketplace.

Forward fits my pattern of bringing fragmented offline supply into a searchable system. It has not yet demonstrated the more valuable pattern: participants contributing useful information or distribution without proportional company labor. Private credit offers room to maneuver, but the dossier names no concrete adjacent buyer and expansion path beyond today’s advisor-to-fund matching. Finitive already addresses that function; F2 and Heron occupy nearby workflows. A broad industry gives Forward possibilities. It does not establish which additional transaction the product can win.

The founder-led exception deserves consideration: unusually capable builders can justify investing before network effects appear. Forward has relevant operating experience and early commercial evidence, but no demonstrated proprietary technical asset or exceptional product behavior that resolves the structural uncertainty. There is no documented integrity violation, artificial financing deadline, or founder instability to trigger a veto. Its Australian licensing restrictions constrain permissible activity; they do not establish misconduct. I would keep the relationship open and withhold my check. One verifiable flip condition would change that decision: a matched-cohort analysis showing that increased participation raises funded-deal conversion while reducing staff hours per funded deal. That would show the founders’ knowledge becoming a system that compounds.
