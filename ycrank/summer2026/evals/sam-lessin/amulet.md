# Amulet -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Amulet’s $0.20-per-GiB-month pricing and 115 LinkedIn followers reveal the signature: Amulet is strongest as a granular, usage-metered infrastructure asset and weakest as a founder-distributed business. Software is no longer investible—fine, that is provocative shorthand. The operational point is that Amulet needs a financing structure matched to uncertain storage consumption, not a factory-model SaaS cap table subsidizing customer acquisition until Series C. Amulet has the beginnings of a legible economic unit, but no owned channel supplying that unit with demand.

Marina Mogilko / LinguaTrip is the high rung on capital structure because $1.7M bought exposure to measurable, already-profitable earning power through an instrument designed around the actual asset. Amulet shares the load-bearing mechanism of granular cash-flow pricing: active data is billed while API calls, transfers, snapshots, and forks are free, making Amulet’s consumption unit unusually clear. Amulet diverges because private-beta partners produce no disclosed revenue, retention, gross margin, or downside cushion, while negotiated private deployments remain sales terms rather than a non-standard financing instrument. The meter is venture-shaped; the cap table is not yet proven to be.

Fin’s original consumer-assistant model exposed the low-rung failure mechanism on owned distribution: an impressive Wizard-of-Oz product without a durable customer channel eventually had to tack into workplace analytics. Amulet shows the same distribution vacuum more starkly—115 LinkedIn followers, no public founder audience, no public GitHub repositories, and a work-email waitlist instead of a pre-existing community. Amulet may earn technical adoption, but nothing in the dossier puts Nithik Bala or Varun Puru themselves on the cap table as a distribution asset.

Archil’s $18M financing makes the round counterfactual uncomfortable: Amulet operates in a category already validated by a funded direct competitor, alongside E2B at $32M and Modal at $465.7M. With no Amulet valuation, round size, investor list, or visible pass from a tier-one fund, I apply the absent-evidence default and then discount it for the Summer 2026 agent-infrastructure heat. Amulet would flip this dimension if the entry were materially below the batch median after credible infrastructure funds passed for reasons the founders could rebut. The Robinhood and Pinterest exceptions do not rescue Amulet: there is no evidence of an unusually early personal-network entry or missionary founder signal strong enough to override price discipline.

Nithik Bala’s Rockset and Meta web-infrastructure work and Varun Puru’s Meta and Amazon experience can compound over 18 months into a serious systems advantage: every production workload could improve Amulet’s deduplication, provenance, conflict handling, and operational reliability. Amulet already claims 1.9-ms p99 reads, 8.4 GB/s throughput, 3-ms forks, and sub-100-ms mounts. The counterweight is the recent pivot from customer-success agents to a filesystem, combined with no public writing or repositories showing that Amulet’s founders held this belief before the YC opportunity appeared. Domain competence is present; asymmetric conviction is not yet evidenced.

Sublime Security is the useful single-anchor parallel: deep infrastructure can be a non-consensus entry when founders build a sharp primitive before the next platform consolidation. Amulet has that shape technically—content-addressed commits, copy-on-write forks, conflict-safe publishing, and per-run provenance—but Amulet is pointed directly at the hottest consensus workload in the batch. Amulet does not activate the revealed sleeves in crypto, DTC, prescription rails, creator infrastructure, holdcos, or Wizard-of-Oz services, and the dossier provides no manual operating loop that preceded automation. “Filesystem for agents” risks being “AI is just more internet” rendered as a storage product.

E2B’s $32M capitalization supports my 36-month forecast: E2B acquires Amulet before July 2029 and before Amulet reaches $3M ARR. Amulet’s forkable persistent workspaces are strategically valuable inside a complete agent-sandbox platform, but Amulet’s absent distribution and free API, transfer, metadata, snapshot, and fork operations make standalone customer economics harder than product integration. The regatta likely ends in a platform tuck-in rather than an independent Series A starting line.

Amulet’s strongest bull case is that 1.9-ms p99 reads and 8.4 GB/s per mount become the storage equivalent of Ro’s prescription rails: an apparently capital-intensive implementation burden that compounds into the moat. If Amulet becomes the neutral persistence layer across E2B, Modal, and independent compute environments, the lack of owned distribution matters less because every platform becomes a channel. The Ro exception does not presently win—Amulet has partial POSIX coverage, excludes live same-file multi-writer access, publishes its own benchmarks, and faces primitives that cloud and sandbox vendors can bundle without regulatory barriers. Ten unaffiliated paying customers, at least $1M ARR, and net revenue retention above 120% by July 2027 would verifiably flip my verdict.
