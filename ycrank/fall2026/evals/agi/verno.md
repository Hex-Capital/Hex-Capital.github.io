# Verno -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Verno’s strongest AGI path is making cross-document checks affordable at every piping and instrumentation diagram revision. The memo describes an existing workflow in which engineers send redlines to drafters and manually reconcile downstream files. Verno reports finding three conflicting design pressures for one tank in documents already issued for construction. That finding suggests a check Verno could perform before release. The investment hypothesis is that drafting and reconciliation labor makes this depth of checking impractical at every revision today. Verno’s documented workflow prepares native CAD edits and corresponding updates in downstream files, applies engineering rules, and routes changes for engineer approval. If approval takes substantially less time than manual reconciliation, a buyer could run the check at a cadence it previously could not afford. Even with equivalent models, buyers would still need native file write-back, customer-specific rules, and traceable approvals. The memo provides no measured reduction in fully loaded cost per approved revision. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The building-block path scores zero. The memo identifies chip fabs and power plants as target markets but supplies no evidence that engineering-document reconciliation constrains a specific AI buildout workload or that equivalent supply is slow to add. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Customer templates, connectors, and revision histories could create switching costs, though retention is unverified. Verno reports an active pilot and YC reports live customer sites, with no disclosed buyer payment or repeat use. The team’s prior work in engineering documentation, document extraction, and process engineering supports early execution, while the memo supplies no measured error rates across customer document sets. Undisclosed pricing, setup effort, and review costs leave the economics of the next deployment unverified. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Verno may speed up a check customers already perform at the same cadence. Setup-specific CAD validation, engineer review, and correction of propagated errors could consume the labor savings. Autodesk’s existing in-project P&ID validation also gives it a starting point for extending checks to downstream files. The reported conflict supports the value of checking, while the evidence on cost, cadence, and paid adoption leaves the economic threshold unresolved. That gap warrants Watch. If buyers can use Verno only to accelerate existing checks, both AGI paths score zero and the Pass gate applies. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Will a pilot EPC pay to check every P&ID revision on a project where it previously could not afford that cadence?
