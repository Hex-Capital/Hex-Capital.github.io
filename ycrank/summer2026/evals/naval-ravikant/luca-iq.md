# Luca IQ -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The founders’ advantage is knowledge acquired through action. Angelo Policicchio worked as a CPA, an EY public accountant, and an FBI forensic accountant; Akash Sadashivapeth ran data science at Welligence. Together they converted that domain knowledge into a functioning calculation engine, completed a tax season with customers, passed IRS Assurance Testing, and obtained federal 1040 e-file authorization in July 2026. Credentials explain access. Shipping through a regulated filing cycle proves specific knowledge.

The customer result is unusually concrete for pre-seed: 1,100 hours saved, administrative preparation reduced by 57%, and intake-to-review-ready compressed to 30 minutes. Hundreds of firms reportedly applied for the pilot. This proves pain and execution, but not yet value capture: Luca IQ discloses volume-based pricing without rates, paid-customer count, revenue, retention, or contribution economics. A completed tax season is stronger than an LOI; it is still insufficient to distinguish repeatable demand from a successful assisted pilot.

The central problem is that the founders have built leverage for customers without yet proving leverage for themselves. The deterministic rules engine, source-level audit trail, and e-file authorization are real assets, not a thin AI interface. But April and Column Tax already operate authorized filing infrastructure, while Filed and TaxGPT surround the same workflow. If a competitor copies Luca IQ’s code, the IRS approval and implementation experience remain; no exclusive filing data, proprietary model, accumulated correction advantage, or prohibitive workflow lock-in has been demonstrated. Rules maintenance may even add labor faster than deployment adds defensibility.

The market offers room to maneuver, although the path is only partly drawn. Luca IQ can move from CPA firms into PE-owned accounting groups and fintech developers using the same calculation-and-filing capability, inside a global tax-management-software category estimated at $23.94 billion in 2025. Yet the current product covers federal individual Form 1040 filing, no professional-software SAM is supplied, and April already claims national coverage. A parent-market estimate is not a causal expansion strategy.

The strongest bull case is that regulated infrastructure compounds quietly: each filing season could deepen integrations, accumulate review corrections, and make switching progressively painful. The founders’ exact-domain experience could justify investing before those assets become visible. But Luca IQ currently acquires firms through outbound sales, and nothing shows one customer creating utility or distribution for another. This is not a permissionless system; every new account appears to require another sale and implementation. The known-exception logic does not rescue it because there is neither a self-reinforcing network nor evidence of exceptional capital efficiency.

I would not reject the company; I would wait. The likely outcome from the present evidence is a useful, founder-led tax infrastructure business rather than a nonlinear angel outcome. The single flip condition is a paid-cohort study showing that proprietary corrections from deployed firms measurably improve outcomes for later firms and produce greater than 120% net revenue retention. That would turn rules maintenance from recurring labor into a compounding asset.
