# machine0 -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I need are missing: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and round size. That fixes the deal score at its low default. More importantly, machine0 does not say what this check will prove. “Cloud computers for AI agents” is a product thesis; it is not yet a capital plan with one dated experiment, one pass/fail metric, and a dramatic change in value if the result is positive.

The strongest evidence is Barnaby Malet himself. He has published cloud resource-allocation research, operated DevOps, infrastructure, and security as Upflow’s CTO/CPO, and already built the Director MCP gateway before pivoting. That is a real outsider-insider bridge between cloud operations and agent tooling, not résumé decoration. He has also shipped an unusually complete pre-seed product: root-accessible persistent VMs, static endpoints, credential profiles, snapshots, NixOS and Ubuntu support, and machines scaling to eight H200 GPUs.

The bull case is that the market is moving exactly toward the product. Agent runs now last six to eight hours, laptops are the wrong execution environment, and machine0 has already produced a named customer, a PACIFIC endorsement, and a Show HN launch with 95 points and 36 comments. A solo technical founder has turned that need into self-service, metered infrastructure rather than a mockup. Those are meaningful pre-seed receipts.

But the dark pool is missing. The customer evidence does not show that proprietary data, embedded workflow, or owned distribution caused a paid conversion or repeat usage. The company rents its underlying compute from DigitalOcean while E2B, Daytona, and Modal can reproduce lifecycle management, snapshots, GPUs, and persistent execution. Modal added full-VM sandboxes in June 2026; Daytona already reported $5 million in annualized revenue. If the public insight is simply “agents need persistent computers,” heavily financed clouds can absorb it as a feature.

The economics are equally unresolved. Per-minute pricing—from $0.013 per CPU hour and $0.836 per GPU hour—establishes a comprehensible cash meter, but there is no revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, utilization, or evidence that DigitalOcean resale leaves enough contribution margin to reach profitability without another round. Supplier dependency, a control plane without a guaranteed uptime percentage, and API-retrievable managed SSH keys make this an operationally serious service before it is demonstrably a durable business.

I am passing today, despite liking the founder-product fit. The one verifiable flip condition is three consecutive months in which at least 25 paying teams each run persistent workloads weekly, with greater than 60% gross margin and no financing required to maintain the service. That would show machine0 owns an operating workflow rather than merely presenting a cleaner interface to commodity compute—and it would identify the finite experiment this round should fund.
