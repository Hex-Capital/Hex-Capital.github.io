# Workers IO -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

By 2032, I can imagine software agents writing changes, breaking them inside simulated worlds, and returning with reproducible failures before anyone touches production. Workers IO could supply the place those agents practice. Its open-source coding-agent skills are a small, tangible artifact pointing toward that world. But connecting an agent to a tool does not establish an autonomous verification loop. The dossier supplies neither that demonstrated transition nor the intervening unlocks. I can imagine the future; I cannot count my imagination as company evidence.

The enabling layer is the strongest part of this bet. Workers IO describes deterministic virtual machines, fault models, and exact replay across payments, trading, and infrastructure workloads. Choudhary’s August 2026 technical article discusses seeded execution, intercepted I/O, and virtual clocks—enough substance to make me curious about the machinery. This fits my pattern of looking beneath a fashionable application wave. However, the published Bun implementation does not establish arbitrary unmodified-system coverage, and Antithesis already sells overlapping whole-system testing. Workers IO has an identifiable technical asset; its control over a scarce bottleneck remains unproven.

Choudhary has built things before. His YC biography reports selling The Timelinears at nineteen after reaching three million monthly active viewers, alongside engineering experience at Grit, OnDeck, and Contract Wrangler. That earns more belief than an accelerator badge. What I cannot reconstruct is a pre-company obsession with distributed-system failures: repeated experiments, operating scars, or complementary technical recruiting. A prior audience business demonstrates initiative, but it does not establish command of hypervisor engineering. The solo-founder disclosure identifies an accountable CEO and avoids the specific equal-control concern; it does not tell me whether this team can traverse the technical distance.

Why does debugging steal so much time? Because the failing sequence disappears, and engineers must reconstruct conditions that conventional tests never exercised. Workers IO addresses that concrete frustration. Yet the dossier identifies no institutional restriction, captured rent, or documented transfer of economic control, so my broken-system criterion stays at its missing-evidence default. Likewise, $2,500 monthly pricing is an offer, not evidence that someone reorganized a release process around the product. The skills repository’s 208 stars and 32 forks show attention and some developer effort, but no documented repeated use of the simulation backend. Those are different commitments.

The strongest objection to passing is uncomfortable: infrastructure can become indispensable before customers publicly discuss using it. A founder with a prior sale, an implementation artifact, and transparent pricing could be opening verification to teams that previously could not access it. My pre-revenue exception matters here; revenue is unnecessary when prototype performance, founder depth, and concrete customer commitments establish the path. Workers IO has pieces of that case, but the dossier does not establish that combination. Its encryption, isolated workloads, no-training commitment, and private deployment options also address the privacy concern sufficiently that I would not treat sensitive workload processing as an automatic veto. There is no evidence of the closed-model dependence or dismissive attitude toward incumbent complexity that would turn this into an active thesis mismatch.

I pass on writing a personal check today. The infrastructure position attracts me, but the present evidence does not connect it tightly enough to my primary future-and-founder gates. **My flip condition is one customer-verifiable case showing a named payments or trading team repeatedly using Workers IO on an unmodified production-representative system to reproduce a consequential failure its existing tests missed.** That would establish the user commitment and technical reach currently left to inference.
