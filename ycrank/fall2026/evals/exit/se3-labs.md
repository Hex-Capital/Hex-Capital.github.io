# SE3 Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A robot model developer could buy SE3 to gain ongoing access to standardized physical workcells, partner-site workflows, and human correction data. Ownership could save repeated setup and reset work and shorten the time it takes to collect usable training data across sites. SE3 reports access to automotive, processing, and hospitality sites. The memo leaves the savings, policy gains, and value of ownership to a buyer unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The operating milestone is a funded partner-site test showing that SE3’s correction data improves full-task completion on held-out workflows and reduces the cost or time per successful rollout relative to the buyer’s current approach. The released dataset of 500 episodes across 10 tasks shows that SE3 can collect benchmark data. Its reported zero completions in 100 zero-shot Dream-1 trials provide a baseline for testing improvement. Partner-site sandboxes are still described as a next step. The reported $125,000 seed entry leaves total financing, runway, and the cost to reach the milestone unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Using the shared scenario in place of actual investor terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year target is $2.5 million gross cash before investor taxes. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. At 3%, the hurdle rises to $88.3 million in enterprise value with illustrative net debt of $5 million. Company debt, fees, preferences, and actual dilution are unverified.

- A **modest sale** at $25 million enterprise value in year eight yields $600,000 gross, or 1.2×, after the illustrative debt.
- A **strong sale** at $100 million enterprise value in year eight yields $2.85 million gross, or 5.7×, on the same assumptions. The memo supplies no buyer economics or acquisition pricing that supports this case. (Market & Traction)
- In a **prolonged-private scenario** where growth plateaus, buyer interest fails to emerge, public-market scale stays out of reach, and SE3 reinvests all available cash, the investor realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. A wind-down would distribute only the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo gives no basis to estimate.

The memo leaves the other routes unevidenced. A sponsor buyout or company-funded payout would require demonstrated cash generation, a secondary would require eligible demand for the holder’s shares, and an IPO would require evidence of operating scale and a path to resale. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could commission evaluations under SE3’s project-based terms, use RoboDojo to run benchmarks in-house, or source physical rollouts and recovery data from Enact. Those options weaken the case for paying to own SE3. Raw recording is disabled by default and requires authorization, and reuse depends on contract rights. A buyer’s ability to retain site access and reuse the data after closing is unverified. These constraints support Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural block nor a supported price range that would leave the holder materially short, so the Pass gate does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a robot model developer’s results from a funded partner-site test justify buying SE3 at a price that could deliver $2.5 million to the modeled holder within eight years? Move to Reach out if the test documents repeatable gains on held-out workflows, the buyer’s comparison with in-house testing and contracted evaluations supports a price above the modeled hurdle, and the contracts show that the site and data rights driving those gains can transfer.
