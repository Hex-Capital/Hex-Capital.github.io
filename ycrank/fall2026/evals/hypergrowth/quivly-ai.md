# Quivly AI -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Conditional on strong product-market fit, Quivly’s post-sales workflow could travel across markets. Usage-priced B2B software vendors can use the same core product to turn kickoff conversations into launch plans, then combine usage and support signals to manage adoption and renewals. The workflow uses common software inputs. Connector access and customer data approvals could limit deployment, but the memo identifies no dependency that would require rebuilding the product market by market. The report, workshops, and paid Enterprise pilot give Quivly a repeatable way to engage buyers. The documented motion includes CEO-led demos, and the memo leaves open whether ordinary buyers can purchase, connect their data, and reach first value independently. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The published monthly tiers double from $500 to $1,000 as capacity rises from 50 to 100 accounts and from five to ten agents. Mycroft’s reported use of Quivly to track implementation milestones and billable hours supports recurring use. A growing customer could upgrade as it manages more accounts, though unlimited seats cap one avenue for expansion, and the memo gives no evidence that upgrades offset churn. Quivly separately reports that downstream accounts under management rose from 10,000+ in October to 18,000+ in November. That is an adoption signal for the product launched in May by the company’s four-person team. The memo establishes neither realized revenue nor growth in paying customers, so the commercial traction score uses the default. (Quivly AI, The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

In a full-utilization scenario, either listed tier yields $10 per managed account per month at monthly list prices, or $8 with the annual discount. Incremental contribution depends on inference, connector and data costs, support, and the sales and setup labor attributable to each new buyer. The memo gives no measured inputs for that bridge, so cost economics uses the default. The gaps in paid acquisition, commercial growth, and delivery costs keep confidence low. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The strongest countercase is that even buyers who value Quivly may need hands-on help connecting systems and configuring approval workflows. ChurnZero and Vitally already have access to account data in their own products, which could make their automation easier for existing customers to adopt and limit Quivly’s renewals and upgrades. The documented demos, connector dependencies, and approval controls make that risk plausible. The structural gate stays open because the memo does not establish that bespoke setup is unavoidable or that the required work makes incremental growth uneconomic. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of typical buyers go from discovery through Quivly’s report or workshops to paid production with little staff help? A dated cohort showing independent purchase, first live use through standard connectors, and positive contribution after inference, support, and setup costs would move the action to Reach out if it lifts the score to at least 65 and confidence to Medium. If buyers still need substantial help and the economics remain unclear, keep Watch.
