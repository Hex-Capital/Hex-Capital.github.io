# Nines -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest path. Nines says technicians must match, insert, and verify thousands of fiber connections per GPU rack and proposes automating that work during commissioning. A rack integrator serving multiple operators could have a reason to buy Nines if owning its control software and deployment know-how lets it commission racks faster across sites and retain more of the savings than a service contract would allow. That ownership advantage is an inference. The memo records a public launch, while repeatable production performance and customer savings remain unverified. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*)

A paid production pilot across multiple rack formats would test whether Nines can reduce commissioning labor and time while keeping errors and rework within customer tolerances. The reported $125,000 seed entry and the CTO’s robotics and production inference experience support an early test. The cost, runway, and funding for a multi-site pilot are unknown. IP ownership and transfer terms are also unverified. The memo supplies no independent route to cash for this holder. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

The memo gives no terms for the proposed check, so the proceeds test uses the illustrative $500,000 investment at a $10 million post-money valuation. Initial ownership of 5% falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year target is $2.5 million in gross cash, or 5×. Under simple pro rata allocation, that takes $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale prices below are scenario assumptions. Both sales assume zero net debt, transaction fees, and senior claims, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration and the holder receives 3% pro rata before taxes. All consideration is cash payable at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0.

| Scenario | Timing and assumed outcome | Holder cash | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 at $40 million enterprise value | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 at $100 million enterprise value | $3.0 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged-private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The holder keeps the shares. | $0 | 0× |

The strong sale clears the hurdle at 3% and returns 4× at 2%. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction price evidence to underwrite either sale price. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Operators could procure the service or build overlapping capability internally. The memo records Microsoft Research’s prototype for the related task of transceiver manipulation and identifies rack-specific integration work that could limit reuse across sites. If a service contract delivers comparable savings or in-house teams can replicate the capability cheaply, a buyer has little reason to pay an acquisition price that clears the hurdle. The prototype and integration risks make that countercase credible, but the memo does not establish a production substitute or a price ceiling. The structural Pass override does not apply. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

Move from Watch to Reach out if a prospective acquirer validates commissioning time, labor, and rework against the manual baseline and uses those results to support a price above the applicable hurdle after dilution and deal adjustments.
