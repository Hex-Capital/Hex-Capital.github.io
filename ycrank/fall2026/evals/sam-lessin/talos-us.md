# Talos -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Talos has a plausible reason to exist. I cannot yet identify the investment I am being offered. Post-money valuation: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. Whether my money would be the first scarce check: unknown. YC participation establishes none of those answers. The dossier also names no capital-funded experiment with a deadline and a pass/fail result. Those are missing underwriting inputs, not evidence of bad founders, but they leave my two primary gates at their absent-evidence defaults.

The interesting possibility is that external sensors can turn inaccessible electrical equipment into something operators can diagnose without interrupting service. Transformer replacements taking years makes that worth investigating. Talos fits my pattern of tolerating physical complexity when software changes how infrastructure operates. The strongest bull case starts with Amitav Kohli’s Constellation predictive-maintenance experience and the team’s demonstrated ability to map **304 magnetic readings around a condensate pump**. That is tangible work. Combined with the founders’ waveform and machine-learning research, it earns some credit for bridging sensing and diagnosis. It does not yet establish that the team has shipped reliable transformer diagnostics into an operator’s maintenance workflow.

The commercial hinge is who owns the observations and what those observations cause someone to do. Talos could accumulate equipment histories paired with verified faults; today, that proprietary dataset is explicitly an inference. The founder-routed demo form and 95 LinkedIn followers establish no purchase, retained workflow, or privileged utility access. Meanwhile, Oktogrid already offers magnetic-adhesion sensing, and Camlin supplies established diagnostic methods. Talos needs to demonstrate that multimodal sensing and spatial localization change a maintenance decision enough to justify buying another system. A pump visualization cannot settle that question.

This is where the opposing case has teeth: a small team might establish that result cheaply, and reliable external diagnosis could become valuable well before meaningful revenue appears. I would not require a pre-seed company to have a long sales history. But the known exceptions to my price and capital-plan gates depend on documented conversion, retention, operating economics, or unusual contractual protection. Talos supplies none of those overrides. Nor does the dossier establish the AI red flag of economics that require errors to disappear; diagnostic accuracy remains unresolved. Hardware, installation, support, and false alarms are the costs I would underwrite. With pricing, margins, burn, and runway undisclosed, I cannot credit a route to surviving without another round.

I pass on the evidence available. The founders have built something relevant, but the investable mechanism remains unverified. My single condition for reopening diligence is **a paid transformer pilot whose independently checked results meet pre-agreed detection and false-alarm thresholds while producing positive contribution margin after installation and support**. That would connect technical proof, customer action, and cash economics in one observable result. Until then, the potential dark pool is a research opportunity rather than an owned economic asset.
