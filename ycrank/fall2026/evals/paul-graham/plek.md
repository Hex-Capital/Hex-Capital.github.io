# Plek -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Bouquet has studied the problem Plek is trying to solve. What I cannot yet see is what he did when solving it became difficult. His MIT task-level workforce research is directly relevant, and selling Biot in 2022 makes him more interesting than someone who has merely discovered enterprise AI. But neither fact supplies the episode I most want: a customer’s problem broke the proposed solution, and the founder found a way through. Resourcefulness remains untested here. So does the ability to change routes after contrary evidence.

Plek’s central difficulty is the distance between estimating work and understanding it. The founder reports mapping 250 employees into 735 tasks and identifying 36 FTEs doing tasks where AI is commonly used. That is a concrete output. It does not establish that the employees actually divide their time that way, or that automating those tasks saves the corresponding payroll. The published research and reported assessment earn technical credit; the proprietary ontology claim does not establish accuracy. Employee interviews could close this gap, but the dossier contains no measured comparison between the initial estimate and the corrected map.

The strongest case for investing is that Bouquet understands this gap and is willing to do the tedious work inside it. His documented research predates Plek, so this is grounded in prior work. Founder-led outreach to COOs and CFOs is also the right beginning. If he personally diagnoses workflows and implements useful automation, the service work could teach him what software to build. Workhelix’s assessment offering and Skan’s direct observation make producing another organizational map less interesting. They also make a fast, accurate route from assessment to implementation potentially valuable. This is the bull case that makes passing uncomfortable.

But Plek has not yet shown the customer behavior that would complete that argument. The 13,500-employee example and the 15-company, $50 billion portfolio example are expressly illustrative or historical under its terms. They cannot establish commercial pull. The smaller founder-reported assessment establishes activity, without showing payment, repeat use, or a buyer asking for more. Negotiated statements of work provide a way to charge; they do not demonstrate that customers buy. Plek fits my unstated preference for technical founders producing concrete artifacts, but lacks the independently validated result or unmistakable usage that makes that pattern persuasive. My known exceptions require a working product, exceptional pull, or validated proof strong enough to replace a missing prior. The dossier does not establish that strength.

I would pass today. Plek does not exhibit the launch-dependent or large-partner-dependent distribution red flags. Its conflicting SOC 2 disclosures are a concrete concern, however, especially when free-text employee responses reach AI providers without redaction. Enterprise buyers must trust both the map and its maker. One verifiable result would change my decision: a documented customer deployment in which Bouquet encounters a material mapping error, ships a correction within two weeks, and turns that corrected map into deployed automation that the customer pays to expand. That would connect founder behavior, technical accuracy, and urgent demand. Today those connections remain the investment hypothesis.
