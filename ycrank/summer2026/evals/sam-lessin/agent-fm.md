# Agent FM -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I start with three blanks: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. YC participation does not answer them. Agent FM may be cheap and overlooked, but financing silence is not evidence of non-consensus pricing—especially in an AI developer-tools market where Conductor has already raised a $22 million Series A and OpenAI has shipped overlapping supervision features. At an unknown strike price, I cannot underwrite cheap convexity.

The product is real enough to distinguish this from a slide deck. Two founders have shipped macOS and Windows support, passively observe Claude Code and Codex without wrapping their changing CLIs, and expose session, token, model, and tool-error data. Aniket Deshpande’s Meta engineering experience and the founders’ own parallel-agent workflow provide credible user-side context. What is not established is the second side of the translation layer: neither founder has documented experience operating an observability platform, selling security-sensitive tooling into enterprises, or managing the privacy boundary created when session context reaches Gemini or OpenAI.

The central problem is that Agent FM has built an interface before proving ownership of an economic workflow. Its 809 LinkedIn followers, roughly 9,000 combined founder followers, free tier, and paid-plan waitlists show reach; they do not show that the reach caused a purchase, retained usage, or even a completed trial. Starter at $48 annually and Pro at $144 annually are price cards, not revenue. The inferred future dark pool—cross-session failure and decision data—does not exist as a demonstrated asset until enough users repeatedly contribute data and receive an outcome they will pay to preserve.

The bull case stings because the market is moving quickly: Codex exceeded one million developers, usage doubled over roughly six weeks, and engineers increasingly supervise parallel agents. Agent FM’s passive, cross-vendor architecture could survive where wrappers break, and audio may become the natural interface for supervising work away from the terminal. That could be a genuine wedge. But the known-exception bar for a generic AI application is hard external evidence—viral adoption, enterprise demand, unusually strong conversion, or a rejected acquisition—not follower count and product availability. None is present here.

Nor does the dossier name the venture moment. “Hire, launch, and grow” would be a capital factory; a valid experiment would specify something like: by a fixed date, convert a defined cohort of engineering teams to paid deployment while retaining them and passing enterprise privacy review. Without that metric and deadline, I do not know what truth the pre-seed check buys. Delete the next financing and the economics are equally opaque: there is no revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or controllable cash metric showing that $48–$144 annual pricing can support acquisition, inference, and support costs.

My 36-month base case is that Agent FM pivots from ambient audio and group chat into enterprise cross-agent observability and remains below $1 million ARR. The product can survive while the current category disappears into Codex, Conductor, or another command center. I am passing with one clean flip condition: show at least 20 paying engineering teams with 70% or better 90-day retention, positive contribution margin after narration costs, and a sub-$10 million post-money round funding a dated enterprise-conversion test. That would establish purchase-causing workflow ownership, viable economics, and scarce pricing at once.
