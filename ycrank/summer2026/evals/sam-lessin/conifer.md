# Conifer -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot underwrite Conifer’s round before knowing the proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. The dossier provides none of them. YC and Wall Street Journal attention are not automatically adverse, but they increase the risk that I am being asked to buy an increasingly fashionable local-AI story without evidence that the price still compensates me for being wrong.

The technical story is more credible than the financing story. Conifer has built a proprietary Rust inference engine that reportedly decodes up to 60% faster than llama.cpp on Apple Silicon, and Charles Muehlberger previously researched edge-device multimodal inference and built edge-AI hardware. Michael Jeffords has also shipped applied computer-vision work and helped expand HeartCheck screenings to more than 10,000 people. This is real construction, not three people prompting a hosted model, but the team has demonstrated the local-compute side more clearly than the cloud-routing, enterprise-procurement, or AI-infrastructure operating side.

The proposed venture moment should be unusually clean: prove that difficulty-based routing can reduce a customer’s token bill by more than 80% without materially degrading task completion. Yet Conifer has not attached the round to a dated experiment, routing-accuracy threshold, customer cohort, or pass/fail result. “Build the product and find teams with high token spending” is a plan; it is not a truth-revealing use of scarce capital. The company-reported savings claim therefore remains an engineering hypothesis rather than an investable result.

The strongest bull case is that the founders already possess the beginning of a proprietary workflow. A local-first gateway could observe prompts, model choices, failures, escalations, and successful outcomes, eventually creating a routing dataset unavailable to generic hosted providers. The 248-star repository and prior Sage launch to more than 100 waitlist developers suggest some developer pull. But stars, followers, and waitlists have not yet caused a documented purchase, retained workload, or referral. The supposed dark pool is inferred, and no routing volume or outcome-data scale is disclosed.

Deleting the next financing makes the problem sharper. Local requests produce no API revenue, while cloud requests are consolidated into pay-as-you-go billing; there is no disclosed take rate, gross margin, burn, runway, or paying usage from which to derive a route to break-even. Meanwhile, Ollama can add automatic routing, OpenRouter already has an estimated $50 million in annualized revenue, and both funded companies occupy one side of Conifer’s bridge. This is the AI red flag in economic form: the differentiation may be erased by a runtime or gateway incumbent before Conifer owns enough workflow data to defend it.

The known-exception bar would require hard external evidence strong enough to override missing terms and application-layer commoditization—paid enterprise demand, measured routing accuracy, or a rejected strategic offer. Conifer has none yet. I am passing, but one verifiable condition would flip me: a cohort of at least ten named teams paying for three consecutive months while independently measured task success remains within five percentage points of frontier-only routing and total model spend falls by at least 60%. That would show the workflow causes purchases, produces proprietary outcome data, and supports economics beyond the next model release.
