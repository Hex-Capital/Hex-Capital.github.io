# Verdun -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Verdun’s strongest liquidity route. The memo records Motorola Solutions’ $79 million acquisition of Blue Eye, net of cash acquired, with plans to integrate live intervention into its incident-management workflow. Verdun offers a related capability through site-specific alarm triage and staffed response on customers’ existing cameras. A security platform could buy Verdun to add that service across its installed base. The memo provides evidence of a buyer paying for an adjacent capability. Verdun’s incremental value to a buyer remains an inference. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Verdun says its AI closed about 85% of alerts in a 14-day sample. That supports testing whether automation reduces operator costs, while threat recall and savings across paid deployments remain unverified. Verdun’s parallel trial gives it a way to measure both against incumbent monitoring and test whether customers convert to paid service. The memo confirms YC backing and gives differing funding figures, leaving runway and the capital needed for a larger trial uncertain. Existing-camera deployment makes integration plausible. Dealer-held contracts and site-specific video permissions leave assignment, change-of-control consent, and continued data access open. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at full pro rata participation.

| Scenario | Gross cash to the holder |
|---|---|
| Modest sale | At an assumed $79 million of distributable equity, 3% yields $2.37 million, or 4.74×. Blue Eye’s reported price is a reference point for this sensitivity. Its reported price metric does not establish distributable equity for Verdun. |
| Strong sale | At an illustrative $100 million enterprise value, $10 million of net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million of equity. A full cash sale with equal pro rata participation yields $2.7 million, or 5.4×. A senior preference that diverts proceeds, partial holder participation, or deferred cash paid after year eight could erase the $200,000 cushion. The memo leaves the actual waterfall and holder eligibility open. |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested yields zero realized proceeds. The memo gives no evidence of an eligible secondary or a funded payout. |

### Countercase

Motorola already owns Blue Eye, and Deep Sentinel also combines automated detection with live intervention. A strategic buyer can obtain similar functionality elsewhere, and Verdun’s short automation sample leaves the tradeoff between operator savings and missed threats unresolved. The closest disclosed transaction also falls below the holder’s hurdle if its reported price is modeled as distributable equity. Together these facts support Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural barrier to a sale nor a ceiling on what the strongest credible route could pay. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Verdun show repeatable operator-cost savings at comparable threat recall across paid sites, at a scale that gives a strategic buyer an economic reason to pay enough to clear $83.3 million in distributable equity? Side-by-side results and a buyer valuation range tied to those economics would move the action to Reach out. A supported range that leaves the holder materially short across credible deal terms would move it to Pass.
