# Plek AI -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Plek AI has identified $30 million of potential customer EBITDA, but I cannot price my ownership of that opportunity. Proposed post-money, competing offers, and the capital required to reach a self-supporting business are undisclosed. The conflicting reports of $125,000 versus $500,000 from YC establish none of those things. I am passing at 31/100. This is an ununderwritten deal with a credible technical founder; there is no evidence that it is an auction, either.

The interesting mechanism is employee-level reconciliation. Mapping a census against 50,000 jobs and 250,000 tasks, then avoiding double-counted savings across compensation, location, organization, and AI could replace a meaningful piece of consulting work. Plek reports matching a six-week advisory assessment at a 3,600-person company. That is useful preliminary evidence of analytical capability. It does not establish that the customer paid, implemented the recommendations, or returned. The $30 million belongs in the customer’s opportunity model, not Plek’s revenue column.

Bouquet is the strongest part of the underwriting. A reported 2022 Biot exit and published task-estimation research provide operating and technical receipts beyond an MIT credential. Plek fits my pattern of connecting systems that struggle to communicate: academic workforce models and PE operating decisions. But the dossier does not establish Bouquet’s operating access inside PE firms. A November 2026 operating-partners summit presentation offers an introduction; it does not demonstrate owned distribution. Related research code and data are public, exclusive rights are undocumented, and permission to aggregate customer information is not an accumulated proprietary dataset.

The bull case has teeth: one PE sponsor could deploy Plek repeatedly across its portfolio, turning an expensive assessment into a recurring workflow and generating benchmarks competitors cannot easily obtain. That could make the first assessment unusually valuable. My exceptions permit hard conversion evidence to outweigh missing financing gates, and a prior exit makes that possibility worth taking seriously. Here, however, negotiated paid-engagement terms show how Plek *could* charge, without establishing a paid customer or renewal. Orgvue already offers overlapping classification capabilities. The AI application red flag therefore remains unresolved: who owns the customer relationship and differentiated information after the analytical capability becomes commonplace? Neither fashionable fundraising nor dysfunctional founder governance is established by this dossier.

Finally, what truth does my check buy? Plek supplies no funded experiment with a metric and deadline, so I cannot credit a venture moment I have invented for it. There is also no margin, burn, or runway evidence supporting a path without another financing. Those are missing underwriting inputs, not misconduct or failures to meet mature-company standards. My one verifiable condition for reopening the decision is a PE sponsor renewing for a second portfolio-company deployment with documented cash contribution sufficient to cover delivery and Plek’s operating burn. That would demonstrate repeat access becoming a self-supporting business—the evidence currently missing between a promising assessment and my personal check.
