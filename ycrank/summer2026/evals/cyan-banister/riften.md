# Riften -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. Every serious company should own a small ecology of models trained around its recurring work, with frontier systems reserved for genuinely difficult requests. Riften supplies a plausible path: replace one endpoint, route production traffic by cost and quality, capture outcomes, train private open-weight models, then let those models earn more traffic. DeepSeek-R1’s January 2025 release is a leak from that future. But the dossier does not show that Riften itself has crossed the threshold from story to artifact: no accessible website, routing benchmark, trained model, or production request exists in the public evidence.

The economic irritation is real. Companies may be paying OpenAI or Anthropic prices for work cheaper models can perform, while receiving no compounding company-specific intelligence in return. Riften could transfer control over inference spending, training evidence, and eventually model ownership back to the customer. Yet the rent is not measured: there is no observed customer bill, savings percentage, quality threshold, or account showing that private-model training beats ordinary caching, prompting, or routing.

I like the stack position more than the proof. This is infrastructure beneath the visible application wave, and an OpenAI-compatible environment-variable migration is exactly the kind of small doorway through which a deeper system can enter. The sting is that Not Diamond already trains custom routers from customer evaluations, OpenRouter has raised $153 million, and open-source LLMRouter includes more than 16 routing methods. Without controlled data, superior evaluation machinery, or a private model demonstrably taking traffic from frontier models, Riften is presently an interface sitting on the bottleneck rather than an owner of it.

The missing founder record matters disproportionately. There is no named founder, prior experiment, repository, customer wound, or evidence that this team independently obsessed over inference economics before the category became crowded. I do not treat that silence as opportunism, but I cannot award Rizz, Tizz, or Biz from a YC category label. The pre-revenue exception for CosmicBrain AI depended on a functioning prototype, signed industrial proofs of concept, and a founder’s dated robotics history; Riften’s dossier supplies none of those counterweights.

The strongest bull case is that public evidence is simply lagging a very young company. If Riften already has a working drop-in gateway, each routed request could create the evaluation data needed to improve routing and train customer-specific models, turning an apparently commoditized gateway into a compounding control plane. But that loop is still hypothetical, and it processes sensitive application requests and outcomes. The dossier provides no consent, custody, retention, or upstream-provider architecture, while adversarial suffixes can steer routers toward expensive models and unified evaluations show sophisticated routing systems failing to beat simple baselines.

I pass today. This is a good six-year shape with no mundane artifact proving Riften is the team making it arrive early. One verifiable condition flips me: two named customers each routing at least 10 million monthly requests, with a Riften-trained private model handling at least 30% of their traffic at equivalent measured quality and 50% lower cost under an auditable data-custody policy.
