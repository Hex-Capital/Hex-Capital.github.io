# Gradon -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

The strongest path is repeatable direct sales to civil engineering firms. Gradon advertises demos and compensated referrals to firm principals, followed by a paid pilot on a live project and an unlimited-use named-seat subscription. Its Windows agent uses firm-specific standards inside tools such as Civil 3D and sends the work to engineers for review. If early customers see enough value to convert, Gradon could reuse the same pilot and rollout process across firms that regularly produce plan sets. The job and software make that plausible across markets where firms can use the same workflow and permit external AI processing. Paid adoption in a second market is unreported. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The CEO reports that the first civil engineering customer used Gradon for plan sets, CAD, and reports, that new firms were onboarding weekly, and that contracted ARR reached the mid-six figures in four weeks. Gradon also reports one project taking 180 hours against a 750-hour manual estimate. These are specific early signals of buyer commitment and potential time savings. The memo provides no account-level record connecting a demo or referral to paid production, or showing recurring paid use or seat additions across projects. Given the recent launch, a repeatable deployment handoff would be the first meaningful test. (Market & Traction)

### Countercase

Firm-specific setup could make each win a custom implementation. The memo describes at least a week of on-site work per deployment, giving a lower bound on rollout time while leaving labor cost and scope for reuse uncertain. Unlimited-use seats also leave open how inference and support costs change as usage rises. If each new firm requires comparable hands-on effort, deployment capacity could limit growth even with strong demand. The structural downside gate remains untriggered because the memo provides no comparable deployments showing that the work must stay proportional to sales. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can someone other than a founder take two independent civil engineering firms from a live-project pilot to paid production using the same rollout process? Account-level records showing paid conversion, accepted plan-set work, and bounded implementation hours with reusable setup would move Gradon to **Reach out**. If successive deployments still require extensive bespoke work and show no credible route to reuse or handoff, move to **Pass**.
