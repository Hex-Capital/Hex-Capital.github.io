# Constor -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Constor could make smaller or lower-probability HVAC and insulation bids worth pursuing by automating PDF takeoffs. A2C Isolation reports completing takeoffs in minutes instead of hours and bidding on and winning more jobs. The investment scenario assumes some of those bids would previously have been skipped on cost grounds and that the savings persist at equivalent bid accuracy after estimator review, corrections, and retries. The memo leaves both assumptions untested. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Constor reports five paying design partners, 12 paid preorders, and about 15 contractors bidding with the product. Overlap, repeat paid use, and willingness to pay without early discounts remain unclear. For a seed team, Louis’s HVAC estimating experience, Elio’s delivery of the initial blueprint analysis, and Dany’s first sale show relevant execution. The registered office is in France, leaving geographic fit with the primarily U.S. strategy open. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Founders & Team)

Subscriptions and credits give Constor a way to charge per contractor or by usage. Contribution at scale depends on revenue per contractor after processing, onboarding, and support. The memo gives signup pricing terms and a founder claim of launch profitability, with realized pricing and delivery costs undisclosed. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Constor may simply speed up bids contractors already planned to submit. Its terms require customers to verify quantities, and incomplete or illegible drawings can increase review and rework. With cheap, capable models and abundant capital, buyers could get comparable quantities from bundled tools. Beam AI already offers overlapping mechanical takeoffs, and Countfire links automated counts to pricing. Constor’s editable HVAC quantities, 3D routing, and reported access to partner drawings and feedback could matter, but their effect on accuracy or buyer preference under parity remains unmeasured. The memo identifies no separately controlled scarce complement for Path A. Both Path B conditions remain open, so the outreach gate is unmet and a seed trial can still change the decision. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent HVAC or insulation contractors repeatedly pay Constor’s undiscounted price to submit bids they would otherwise skip when a comparably capable alternative is available? A paid trial on real bids should record which jobs contractors would have skipped and compare total estimator time and bid-ready accuracy across their existing workflow, Constor, and the alternative. Repeat paid use with material all-in savings and a demonstrated preference for Constor under parity would support Reach out if the revised score clears the threshold and mandate fit is plausible. If the savings disappear after review or buyers readily switch under parity, Pass.
