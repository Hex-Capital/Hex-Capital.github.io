# Perit.AI -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Perit.AI reports six major AI-lab customers and multiple millions in ARR. That earns attention. It does not tell me what I am buying. The recorded $500,000 accelerator investment establishes neither the proposed post-money valuation nor investor competition. Both remain unknown. I cannot call this auctioned capital, but I cannot underwrite a cheap, non-consensus entry either. The dossier also names no experiment this round funds, with a deadline and a measurable result. Those are my two largest criteria; commercial activity cannot silently substitute for either.

The interesting asset is the production workflow connecting sector practitioners to labs with specific speech failures. Perit reports 4,000-plus delivered audio hours across nine locales, supported by Foundry and a 650-person annotation bench. Combined with the reported customer relationships, that is meaningful evidence that the team can translate expert labor into something buyers want. It fits my preference for businesses joining systems that struggle to work together. But who owns the scarce access? Contributors are not documented as exclusive, and non-exclusive data licensing is the default. Mercor and Turing could recruit and calibrate competing teams. The 350 monthly dataset downloads demonstrate exposure; they do not establish a proprietary customer funnel.

The strongest argument against passing is that Perit may already have built the functioning business most AI pitches merely promise. Multiple millions in reported ARR is substantial evidence at this stage. Kshitij previously co-founded ZeoAuto, reported as profitable at $2 million ARR; Poorva has co-founded SalaryBook and brings engineering experience from Google, Atlassian, and Uber. Those are useful operating receipts, although direct experience selling training data to frontier labs is less established. My exception for Ando is relevant: both businesses have evidence of enterprise demand around AI. The material difference is that Ando supplied measured conversion and retention; Perit's unnamed customers and unspecified ARR methodology leave recurring purchasing behavior unresolved.

Delete the next financing from Perit's model. What survives? Custom quotes separately itemize contributor compensation, but the dossier supplies no gross margin, reviewer cost, burn, or runway. With a reported team of 47, impressive sales could support either a profitable specialist or a labor-intensive operation consuming cash. Revenue earns some economic credit; ZeoAuto's profitability belongs to the founder's record and cannot establish Perit's margins. I see no evidence that the product requires agent errors to disappear or that the founders are manufacturing metrics for another round. The unresolved issue is whether calibrated human work produces durable cash contribution as competitors enter.

I pass on the investment as documented. The other known exceptions do not resolve the gaps: there is no rejected acquisition establishing an external value floor, or person-level financing structure with an explicit capital plan. Perit's strongest evidence establishes customer demand, while its weakest evidence concerns what my capital buys and proves. One verifiable flip condition would be a renewed lab-contract cohort whose collected cash, after fully loaded delivery costs, covers company operating burn. That would demonstrate enough independent business value to reconsider the missing capital-plan evidence. Today, I have a credible supplier and an ununderwritten investment.
