## The Idea

- **Problem:** Dydema identifies insufficient brain recordings as a bottleneck to developing consumer interfaces that translate brain activity into commands otherwise entered through keyboards, mice, or controllers ([Dydema LinkedIn, accessed Oct 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/company/dydema)).
- **Approach:** Its current Wizard API fits and evaluates licensed pretrained models and classical methods on labeled EEG trials, hosts the selected classifier, and returns labels and probabilities, alongside the website’s custom data-recording offering ([API documentation](https://developers.dydema.com/docs), [Dydema website](https://dydema.com/), Oct 2026).
- **Differentiation:** Dydema offers hosted task classification and commissioned recordings, whereas Neurosity supplies Crown hardware and an SDK, Neurable integrates EEG into headphones, and EMOTIV combines headsets, APIs, and brain-data infrastructure ([Dydema](https://dydema.com/), [Neurosity](https://neurosity.co/developers), [Neurable](https://www.neurable.com/), [EMOTIV](https://www.emotiv.com/about), Oct 2026).
- **Business Model:** Wizard charges $1 per model creation, $1 per model monthly hosting, and $0.003 per prediction window, with $5 initial credit and prepaid billing ([Dydema pricing, effective Sep 23, 2026](https://developers.dydema.com/pricing)).
- **TAM/SAM:** No public TAM/SAM data found for this specific segment; the broader global BCI market is estimated at $2.77B in 2026 with 15.13% CAGR through 2034, encompassing medical applications beyond Dydema’s offering ([Straits Research, Oct 2026](https://straitsresearch.com/report/brain-computer-interfaces-market)).
- **GTM / Distribution:** Public entry points include self-service API keys, developer documentation, data-request inquiries, and consumer-control demonstrations on social channels ([Developer quickstart](https://developers.dydema.com/docs/quickstart), [website](https://dydema.com/), [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/dydema), Oct 2026).

## Defensibility

- **Moat today:** Dydema says it records brain data for model training, but corpus size, exclusive ownership, and measured advantages attributable to proprietary recordings have no public data found ([Dydema website, Oct 2026](https://dydema.com/)).
- **Future moat:** [Inferred]: Repeated commissioned studies could accumulate differentiated training data if reuse rights and recording diversity support generalization; public evidence does not establish that advantage.
- **Market structure:** [Inferred]: No structural barrier identified at this stage, because EMOTIV already combines EEG hardware, developer tools, data, and foundation-model infrastructure ([EMOTIV, Oct 2026](https://www.emotiv.com/about)).
- **Commoditization risk:** [Inferred]: Existing EEG vendors and developers could assemble competing classification services from available models, although matching data collection and validation requires additional work ([REVE project](https://brain-bzh.github.io/reve/), [Wizard documentation](https://developers.dydema.com/docs), Oct 2026).

## Key Risks

- **Technical generalization:** Wizard reports zero-shot imagined left-versus-right movement accuracy of 52% across nine datasets against 50% chance and does not offer that zero-shot task; labeled-trial calibration remains available ([Wizard documentation, Oct 2026](https://developers.dydema.com/docs/task-models)).
- **Consumer usefulness:** [Inferred]: Slow command throughput could constrain adoption for everyday input; Dydema describes a demonstration’s theoretical ceiling as approximately 1.3 bits/second, with filtering and errors reducing actual throughput ([Dydema LinkedIn, Oct 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/company/dydema)).
- **Neural-data privacy:** Dydema says embeddings can re-identify participants across sessions, creating confidentiality exposure; its terms require consent, identifier removal, and treatment of embeddings as personal data ([Dydema terms, Sep 2026](https://developers.dydema.com/terms)).
- **Third-party licensing:** Wizard’s pretrained-model licenses restrict profiling and high-stakes uses, limiting permissible applications; the service refuses clinical and diagnostic tasks ([Wizard documentation](https://developers.dydema.com/docs/task-models)).
