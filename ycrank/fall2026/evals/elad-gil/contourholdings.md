# Contour -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Contour sits at the intersection of improving document automation and a dated institutional push toward digital permitting; environmental consulting practices are its acquisition targets, infrastructure developers are the underlying buyers, and the controlling variable is retained profit per compliant deliverable. Automating report production creates equity value only if Contour preserves pricing and reduces total delivery costs after expert review, engineering, and liability. If customers pay for hours, faster drafting could also reduce the invoice. The dossier establishes the proposed mechanism but supplies no measured baseline or demonstrated improvement. I would pass today.

The market timing deserves some credit. The April 2025 federal modernization memorandum and CEQ’s April 2026 call for workflow automation are concrete institutional developments. They do not establish that Cortex can accelerate regulatory decisions or change consulting margins. The cited $27 billion environmental consulting market includes substantial work outside Contour’s permitting focus, and its projected 3% annual growth offers little automatic lift. Paces and Transect already address permitting information and environmental assessment. Contour’s opportunity therefore depends on changing delivery economics within acquired firms, rather than simply entering a large category with AI.

The strongest bull case is ownership. Acquiring practices could give Contour authority to redesign operations and access to established customer relationships, avoiding the adoption friction of selling another tool to consultants. Embedding engineers alongside practitioners is a concrete implementation approach. This fits my pattern of backing software that changes a traditional industry’s economics. But the website describes an acquisition strategy; the dossier documents no completed acquisition or deployed integration. I cannot award workflow ownership for intended ownership. Better models would plausibly improve Cortex’s drafting, while any durable advantage would have to come from operational integration, accumulated project state, and responsibility for completed work.

The founders bring relevant adjacent experience: Agrawal built enterprise search at Rezolve AI, and Lubar worked in investment banking at Lazard. Those facts make the proposed division of work plausible, but they establish neither prior startup learning nor costly personal commitment. Likewise, Fall 2026 YC participation and zero YC job postings provide no evidence of customer pull. Missing revenue is ordinary at this stage; it still leaves demand unvalidated. My exceptions for frontier research, scarce model-building talent, or demonstrated transformation of physical-business economics do not apply on the available evidence. There is also no documented fundraising-status or founder-liquidity red flag. The unresolved red flag is underwriting service operations as a technology business before establishing different margins.

I would revisit Contour after one verifiable result: a six-month operating cohort at an acquired practice showing at least a ten-percentage-point contribution-margin improvement from Cortex, including embedded engineering and expert-review costs, with comparable project mix and unchanged acceptance and rework rates. That is my proposed underwriting threshold, not a result the company has reported. It would distinguish a promising acquisition thesis from a repeatable economic engine. Until then, the institutional catalyst is the strongest evidence, while product-market pull and the central margin mechanism remain unproven.
