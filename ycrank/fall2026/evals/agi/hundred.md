# Hundred -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Hundred’s AGI path is making inspection of every incoming precision part viable for electronics factories that currently sample trays. The memo describes manual microscope checks and incoming-part sampling. Hundred claims its controlled-light station scans whole trays, calibrates from one known-good sample, and detects and grades defects smaller than 100 µm. If inspection labor is what limits coverage, scanning trays with little operator review could make every-part inspection affordable at normal receiving volumes. The memo gives no production measurements to establish that threshold. Even with comparable vision models available, factories would still need controlled image capture, tray handling, and a way to route rejects. The memo identifies no AI workload whose deployment is constrained by access to this kind of inspection, so the building-block path earns zero. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Per-part photos could make the station harder to replace if factories rely on those records in supplier failure investigations, though the memo offers no evidence of that use. Manual microscope checks are a concrete buyer workaround. The public launch shows Hundred is soliciting buyers, while the memo provides no buyer trials, commitments, or revenue. The founders bring relevant experience resolving hardware supplier problems and shipping production software, with factory qualification still unverified. (Defensibility, The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team)

Assuming a station sale or lease, the economics depend on whether avoided inspection labor, scrap, and assembly interruptions outweigh the station, handling, review, and false-reject costs at production volume. The memo supplies no pricing or operating data to establish that bridge. Watch reflects a concrete use with a decisive gap that a production trial can test. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Reflective finishes and acceptable part variation could drive enough manual review and false rejects to erase the labor savings or slow receiving. Factories with installed cameras also have a retrofit option that could deliver comparable coverage with less new equipment. The memo identifies both risks and gives no production comparison. If representative testing shows Hundred only makes existing sampling more efficient, the enabled-industry path falls to zero and the decision becomes Pass. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a factory that currently samples incoming trays use Hundred to inspect every part at normal receiving volume and lower its total cost per incoming part? A paid pilot on representative lots should compare Hundred with the factory’s current process, tracking operator time, throughput, false rejects, missed defects, and downstream costs. Sustained full coverage at lower total cost and within the factory’s defect tolerance would move the action to Reach out. (The Idea, Key Risks)
