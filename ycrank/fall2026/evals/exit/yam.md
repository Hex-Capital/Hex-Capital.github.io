# Yam -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Yam’s strongest path. A romance-game publisher such as Dorian could acquire Yam’s persistent-cast simulation engine and creator platform to add generated romance games and bring in an established audience. Yam’s public catalog spans 112 fandoms, and its top listing displays 2.4 million plays. Yam separately reports 1 million beta play hours and more than three hours of daily playtime per active user. The buyer rationale is an inference. Ownership could beat building or licensing if the deal brings a retained paying audience and creator distribution that the buyer would struggle to reproduce. The memo supplies no measured buyer benefit or relevant acquisition price. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A seed-stage milestone would be successive 90-day cohorts of paying users who return to rights-cleared original games, with positive contribution margins after model and moderation costs. Yam already ships creator publishing and offers subscriptions and currency packs. Realized revenue, cohort retention, burn, and runway are undisclosed. The recorded $125,000 seed investment and an angel check of undisclosed size leave the capital needed to reach that milestone uncertain. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

**Illustrative proceeds.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× gross cash target within eight years is $2.5 million. A simple pro rata allocation requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. At 3% ownership and an illustrative $5 million in net debt, the hurdle is $88.3 million in enterprise value before any fees or senior claims. At year eight, a $25 million sale yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), while a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million gross (5.7×), assuming cash at close. Yam’s actual holder terms, debt, and realizable sale price are undisclosed. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public-market scale remains out of reach, and Yam reinvests all available cash for eight years, the holder realizes $0 and retains the shares. The memo shows primary investment, with no evidence of demand for eligible existing shares. It also leaves sponsor financing, IPO readiness, and cash available for dividends or buybacks unevidenced. Any wind-down recovery would be limited to residual value after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could build a similar experience using commercially available models and its own creator tools. Yam’s highest-played listing uses third-party fandom IP, and rights-holder takedowns could remove catalog content that a buyer would want to acquire. Yam has announced plans for original IP, but the memo gives no original-title retention or revenue data to show that demand would carry over. This threatens transfer of part of the catalog. The Pass gate remains untriggered because a sale of the software and a rights-cleared audience is still plausible, and the memo establishes no proceeds ceiling. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Yam show that rights-cleared original games retain enough paying users to support an acquisition price that clears the holder’s eight-year cash target? Successive 90-day paid cohorts, positive contribution margins after model and moderation costs, and a buyer-specific estimate of incremental profit from ownership would answer it. Move to Reach out if those results support a credible sale range that clears the hurdle under plausible dilution and deal terms.
