# Agency Tool Company -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is too small as presently defined. Agency Tool Company may make robot software updates less painful, but the dossier does not connect that improvement to an order-of-magnitude change in robotic labor, fleet utilization, or operating cost. The disclosed market is $613.79 million in 2024, projected to $1.91 billion by 2032. That is a respectable software category, not yet a societal cost pool large enough to make a low-probability outcome compelling.

The strongest evidence is technical. Byte-level deltas, compression, interrupted-transfer resumption, device-specific artifacts, rollback, and targeting form a credible mechanism for the reported 20x improvement over Docker pulls or SCP. But this is a company-controlled transfer-speed benchmark, not evidence that a fleet can operate 10x more robots per engineer. Balena, Mender, Formant, Foxglove, and Memfault already own adjacent surfaces and could reproduce delivery features. Supporting standard OCI containers and arbitrary files helps adoption while weakening lock-in.

The bull case is the founders. Morrison and Foster built and deployed hundreds of autonomous mowers at Scythe, stayed together through its 2026 acquisition by ASI, and are now productizing infrastructure they personally lacked. Three beta partners—Burro, Tempo Works, and Gather AI—span agriculture, construction, and logistics, suggesting a recurring problem rather than one former employer’s peculiarity. This is the execution-heavy platform exception I would take seriously. It does not yet override the pass because the dossier identifies neither an enormous value pool nor a proprietary learning loop: deployment histories and approvals are only inferred future switching costs.

Capital now needs to attack one kill risk: whether faster artifact delivery materially lowers the human cost or failure rate of operating heterogeneous fleets. The private beta is a useful venue, but Agency Tool Company discloses no pass/fail threshold, experiment cost, timing, or stop branch. Measuring transfer speed while leaving fleet economics unmeasured risks polishing the tool before proving the company.

The founders’ record establishes domain depth, not the specific learning evidence I require. There is no documented belief revision, difficult complementary recruit, or hiring plan mapped to security, embedded systems, or enterprise adoption risk. Economics are similarly blank: no pricing, revenue, gross-margin model, or comparison against the cost of internal deployment engineers. Software does not need a physical Chindia-price curve, but it must still beat the customer’s DIY alternative without depending on implementation labor.

My 36-month base case is that Agency Tool Company remains below $2 million ARR and is acquired by a broader robotics-operations vendor such as Formant rather than becoming an independent infrastructure standard. One result would flip me: an independent fleet operator demonstrating that ATC lets each deployment engineer manage at least 10x more production robots. That would turn a 20x file-transfer feature into a consequential labor multiplier. Until then, I would pass with my own money.
