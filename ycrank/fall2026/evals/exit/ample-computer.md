# Ample Computer -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a **strategic acquisition by a developer hosting platform**. Ample’s deployment planner reads app source code and provisions the services it needs. A hosting platform could buy and integrate the planner if doing so brought more complex apps into production and kept them on its infrastructure. The memo documents a verified Claude connector and 2,144 published deployment recipes. Ample reports more than 4,000 users in 13 days and cites a customer whose live store received 55 order or checkout submissions in 18 hours. Those signals support production use. The gains in deployment success, paid workload retention, and support costs that could justify ownership remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A paid production pilot against a hosting platform’s existing agent deployment workflow is a plausible seed milestone. Early usage and the founders’ developer infrastructure experience support a test. The memo lists a $125,000 YC seed entry and leaves runway and pilot funding open. The different operator names in Ample’s legal and company pages leave IP ownership and customer-contract assignment unverified. The memo supplies no independent evidence of secondary demand or of cash generation that could fund a buyout or shareholder payout. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The illustrative investor puts in **$500,000 at a $10 million post-money valuation**, takes 5% initial ownership, and holds 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in holder cash, or about **$83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds** at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios below assume zero net debt or other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, no senior preferences, and pro rata allocation before investor taxes. All equity consideration is cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and transaction-related compensation are each $0. The prices and dates are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no usable acquisition price evidence. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Railway and Render already expose infrastructure tools to external coding agents. A hosting platform could extend its existing tools or partner with Ample and capture enough of the benefit at a lower cost. The published recipes show breadth, while the memo provides no comparative deployment success rates to establish a performance advantage. This supports Watch because the ownership advantage and achievable price remain open. The evidence establishes neither a price ceiling below the hurdle nor a probable eight-year outcome with no shareholder cash, so the Pass override does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production pilot show that owning Ample would create enough incremental value for a hosting platform to support the acquisition hurdle? Move to **Reach out** if the pilot measures gains over the platform’s existing workflow in retained paid workloads or support costs, and the platform’s rollout economics show a credible path to roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership.
