# Native -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Native’s founders have earned knowledge of this problem. They built Wepost together, reached a reported NOK80,000 MRR in September 2025, and started Native in January 2026. Bekken produced content for actual businesses; Hauga operated a marketing company; Gråsberg built the predecessor’s technology. This is stronger evidence than three impressive biographies. They have encountered the distance between producing content and getting a business owner to pay for it. Winding down Wepost does not establish either abandonment or integrity failure; the dossier supplies no evidence of unfair treatment.

The question is what their learning becomes. Native uses frontier models for generation and Ayrshare for publishing. A competitor can purchase those inputs. Norn is the potentially valuable part: choosing what to generate from brand context, performance, and approval or rejection feedback. Native’s terms document a mechanism for collecting training signals. That earns some defensibility credit. They do not establish that these records produce a predictive advantage competitors cannot reproduce. The observational research selects retained accounts and acknowledges survivorship bias. A feedback database becomes an asset when its contents improve decisions.

The strongest argument against hesitation is substantial: Native reports $2M ARR within six months, 2,200 paying customers, and a $79 monthly Pro plan. Businesses are buying the product before the predictive thesis is settled. My willingness to back an unusual, capital-efficient prototype before a distribution loop develops is relevant here; waiting for every property to mature can mean waiting away the opportunity. But these figures establish direct value capture. They do not establish customer-created distribution, improving contribution economics, or that one brand’s feedback makes another brand’s results better. The reported 40,000 monthly posts measure output; they cannot answer those questions.

Native fits my preference for tools that let individuals turn knowledge into scalable output. It does not yet demonstrate the stronger pattern of participants contributing value for one another without proportional company effort. Agencies and enterprise customers provide plausible expansion beyond small businesses, and the proposed Norn API could carry prediction into other marketing products. Yet the $36.4B social-media-management estimate describes a parent category, not Native’s reachable opportunity. Blaze already offers strategy, creation, publishing, and learning. Expansion needs a reason buyers choose Native beyond obtaining the same workflow from another supplier.

I would keep Native close, but I would not write a check on this evidence. The founders and monetization are convincing; the compounding mechanism remains unproven. There is no documented integrity, financing-pressure, or founder-behavior red flag driving that decision. My flip condition is a controlled cross-brand test showing that adding other customers’ feedback materially improves held-out brands’ marketing outcomes over the same system trained only on each brand’s own history. That would turn Native’s growing customer base into evidence for a proprietary learning system. Today, it is evidence for a product people will pay for.
