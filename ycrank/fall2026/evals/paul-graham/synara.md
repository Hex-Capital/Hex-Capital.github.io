# Synara -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Boris Ploix has already spent years working on the problem Synara attacks. He co-founded Calipsa as CTO in 2016; Motorola acquired it in April 2022. That is a substantial reason to take him seriously. But I want the next detail: what broke at Synara, what did he do about it, and how quickly did it work? The dossier contains no such episode. His previous company establishes direct exposure to video monitoring. It cannot supply an undocumented instance of resourcefulness at this one.

The interesting part of Synara is taking responsibility after detection. Filtering camera alerts leaves someone else holding the problem. Synara proposes to handle verification, voice intervention, and police escalation too. There is real schlep here: integrating existing cameras, managing operators, and making a response happen. Ploix’s prior work makes this a credible place for him to look. It does not establish that he personally encountered the specific coverage gap that produced Synara, so I give the origin criterion substantial but incomplete credit.

The strongest case for investing is that this founder knows where the previous layer stopped being useful. Selling the complete service through integrators, under their brands, could let Synara reach installed cameras without replacing them. Human verification could also let the team serve customers before automating every difficult incident. These are sensible mechanisms. But the dossier does not show an operating deployment or measured response performance; its operational displays are illustrative. Nor does it identify a customer repeatedly using or paying for the service. The October 2026 YC launch and Menlo Times coverage establish that Synara announced itself. They do not establish that anyone needs it badly.

Competition makes that distinction consequential. Deep Sentinel already combines existing cameras with live guards, while Alarm.com acquired a majority stake in CHeKT in February 2025. Synara therefore has to discover something more specific than demand for AI monitoring. Which integrator has a customer whose incidents the existing services cannot handle well? What does Synara do differently for that customer? Wholesale prices are undisclosed, so I also cannot tell whether assuming responsibility produces attractive recurring revenue or simply transfers operator costs onto a two-person company. The integrator channel is not itself the red flag of depending on one grand partnership; no such dependency is documented.

I would pass on the evidence available. Synara fits my preference for founders working inside an ugly problem they know, but not yet the pattern of an inspectable technical result or users pulling a crude product into service. There is also no documented rejected hypothesis and subsequent product change; that is missing evidence, not obstinacy. I checked the exception case: a working product, unmistakable pull, or independently validated proof could outweigh an incomplete founder record. None is established here. My flip condition is one independently corroborated customer deployment showing a named obstacle, Ploix’s dated workaround, and the resulting service being used repeatedly and paid for. That single case could establish both the founder behavior and the demand this dossier leaves unresolved.
