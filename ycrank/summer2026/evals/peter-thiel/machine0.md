# machine0 -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Barnaby Malet previously built Upflow as CTO/CPO before its $15M Series A, but machine0 now sells CPU time from $0.013 per hour on rented DigitalOcean infrastructure. That pairing is machine0’s signature: unusually strong operator pedigree attached to an unusually weak atoms moat. machine0 may be a good founder discovering a useful feature; power-law returns require a founder discovering a monopoly.

**Affirm** is the high rung on lineage because Max Levchin had already shipped PayPal to global scale before attacking credit. machine0 shares the relevant mechanism: Barnaby Malet has cloud-resource-allocation research, prior founder experience, and a 1.1K-star repository rather than the median YC résumé. machine0 diverges where the analogy becomes load-bearing—Upflow raised $15M, but the dossier shows neither a scaled exit nor evidence that Malet previously controlled infrastructure with monopoly economics. Strong lineage earns attention; it cannot manufacture a secret.

**Asana** is the low rung on the atoms axis: pure workflow software without a regulatory, capex, or physical barrier, rescued only by Dustin Moskovitz’s Facebook-scale pedigree. machine0 reproduces the weakness more starkly because machine0’s KVM/QEMU machines ultimately run on DigitalOcean; the scarce asset belongs to the supplier. NixOS images with 13 GitHub stars are reproducible configuration, not proprietary atoms. Malet’s Upflow experience is meaningful, but it is not the Moskovitz exception.

**E2B** exposes the strongest version of machine0’s secret: agents running for six to eight hours need persistent, suspendable computers rather than ephemeral sandboxes. Over 18 months, machine0 can compound that insight through snapshots, static IPs, and reusable environments—but Daytona already sells “composable computers,” while VPS providers and hyperscalers can add persistence without changing their architecture. machine0 has named an incumbent emphasis, not an incumbent incapacity. A secret that competitors can accept next quarter is timing, not truth.

**DigitalOcean** supplies machine0’s capacity, while E2B, Daytona, Modal, RunPod, and self-hosted KVM stacks supply substitutes. For machine0’s monopoly score to flip, machine0 would need to become the default state layer for long-running agents: portable snapshots, identity, workload history, and integrations whose accumulated data make migration irrational. The dossier instead identifies no structural barrier and discloses no customer usage. At present, pricing power belongs below machine0 and competition surrounds machine0.

**SpaceX** committed to a multi-year reusability roadmap whose intermediate failures still advanced a definite destination. machine0 pivoted from the Director MCP-governance gateway to commercializing its VM CLI, but machine0 discloses no two-to-five-year architectural commitment beyond persistent cloud computers. The pivot may be intelligent; it is still evidence of discovery mode. A definite plan would name the infrastructure machine0 must own, the control layer it will standardize, and the milestone at which DigitalOcean becomes dispensable.

**$0.013 per CPU hour** points toward a commodity wedge rather than monopoly pricing. By July 2029, machine0 will have pivoted from general-purpose persistent VMs into an enterprise agent-security and workspace-control product and will remain below $5M ARR, rather than becoming an independent agent cloud. machine0’s capable founder can follow customers into a better control-plane problem, but machine0’s rented compute and undifferentiated VM economics will force that move.

**Show HN’s 96 points and 37 comments** form the strongest opposing case: machine0 may be catching the exact moment when autonomous agents stop being sessions and become persistent workers. Barnaby Malet’s infrastructure research and Upflow operating history could create an Asana-style pedigree override, with NixOS reproducibility becoming the initial niche from which machine0 expands. The objection stings because pre-seed infrastructure platforms often look like wrappers before usage compounds. It does not yet win: machine0 has 13 stars on its principal image repository, no disclosed customers or revenue, and a well-funded near-direct competitor in Daytona. A disclosed $1M ARR run rate from at least 100 customers with gross margins above 70% would flip my verdict by demonstrating that machine0 owns more than resale economics.
