# Terse -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Terse has published something developers can run. What I cannot identify is the deal: proposed post-money, competing offers, or what scarce capital would prove. Articulate and YC appear as investors, but their presence establishes neither an auction nor an attractive entry price. The dossier also supplies no use-of-proceeds experiment. I would pass on writing a personal check today. Working software establishes that these founders can build; it does not establish why this round should exist.

The product has a coherent mechanism. Named actors serialize requests, own SQLite storage, and expose generated clients and WebSockets. Self-hosting gives developers deployment control that Cloudflare’s managed service does not. But Rivet also offers self-hosted actors, so deployment control alone does not create scarce access. Terse’s MIT license can help adoption while letting others reuse the implementation. Its potential dark pool would be production workflows embedded around actor identities and persisted state. The dossier documents the ingredients for that dependence, without evidence that a customer has developed it.

The strongest objection to passing is the team. Thomas Karatzas spent seven years on Siri and Apple Intelligence and previously founded a company that was acquired. Olivier Simard-Morissette brings backend experience from Pigeon, Squarespace, and The New York Times; the team has shipped the runtime and releases. That is a credible bridge between assistant development and persistent application infrastructure, consistent with my preference for founders who connect operating systems they understand. Yet the documented exit and shipped code are stronger evidence than the job titles: the dossier does not establish prior scaled operation across both sides of this particular bridge.

Terse could become the infrastructure developers adopt before procurement notices. Its 256 stars, 19 forks, and October 27 Show HN tally of 53 points and 25 comments give that bull case an actual starting point. They do not show production retention, purchases, or control over distribution. My exceptions to price and capital-plan gates require unusually hard external validation—paid conversion, durable economics, acquisition interest, or a financing structure that removes repeated fundraising. None is documented here. Nor would I call Terse a generic model wrapper: persistent state solves a real systems problem. The concern is who captures its value when overlapping primitives are already available.

Delete the next financing and the economic model remains unspecified: hosting and support are inferred possibilities, with no verified pricing, margin, or burn. That earns the missing-evidence default, rather than an assumption that the business is uneconomic. The October 26 release’s socket ordering and dropped-message limitations nevertheless identify what production buyers must underwrite; support costs cannot simply be assumed away. My flip condition is a signed financing plan below $10 million post-money that funds a dated test of five paying production customers retained for six months, with contribution economics sufficient to reach break-even on that capital. That would connect the software, the purchase, and the purpose of my check.
