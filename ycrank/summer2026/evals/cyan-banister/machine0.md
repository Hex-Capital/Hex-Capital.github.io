# machine0 -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032: autonomous agents work for days, maintain durable identities, operate fleets of machines, and continue while their owners sleep. The laptop becomes a viewing window, not the workplace. machine0 has found a real leak from that future: coding runs already last six to eight hours, and `machine0 new mybox` produces an always-on, root-accessible computer with credentials, snapshots, and a static endpoint. The causal chain is plausible, but incomplete. The dossier does not show why agents will require machine0’s particular control plane rather than the persistent runtimes that every cloud platform is rapidly adding.

Barnaby Malet is the strongest part of this company. Cloud resource-allocation research, responsibility for Upflow’s infrastructure and security, a prior MCP gateway, and a 1,100-star GitHub project establish technical work that predates this product. This is not a tourist discovering infrastructure during an AI boom. He has Tizz and credible Biz; the unanswered piece is Rizz. A solo founder can move beautifully fast, but there is no evidence yet that he has recruited exceptional people around an independently derived technical advantage.

The present system has genuine irritations: laptops close, local credentials leak, raw VPS administration consumes attention, and Modal’s ordinary sandboxes expire after 24 hours. machine0 returns control through persistent root access and a tiny CLI surface. But this is workflow repair, not yet the dismantling of an artificial rent. No quantified delay, fee, institutional gatekeeper, or transfer of economic agency appears in the dossier. Convenience can be valuable; it is simply less asymmetric than removing a rule that should never have existed.

The product is working. PACIFIC appears under “Trusted by,” Geoffrey Néagiar is identified as a customer, an independent reviewer exercised the system, and the Show HN launch earned 95 points and 36 comments. Those are useful artifacts, but they do not reveal costly or weird behavior. I cannot see agents accumulating months of state, customers entrusting production credentials, or operators paying repeatedly for fleets. Per-minute billing proves that machine0 can charge; it does not prove that anyone is paying enough, often enough, to make persistence a habit.

The bull case stings because a domain-fluent founder, a functioning prototype, and early customer contact can justify investing before revenue. That combination has overridden missing traction for me before, as with CosmicBrain AI. The material difference is that CosmicBrain paired its prototype with signed industrial proofs of concept and controlled robotics technology. machine0 runs entirely on DigitalOcean while E2B, Daytona, and Modal already possess capital, runtimes, snapshots, and overlapping agent-control features. The concealed-infrastructure pattern initially attracts me, then collapses under inspection: machine0 does not yet control the scarce layer beneath the wave. It also injects credentials and exposes retrievable managed SSH keys through its API, making custody architecture part of the product rather than an implementation detail.

I would not write the check today. My 36-month base case is that machine0 either pivots toward higher-level agent-fleet management or remains below $1 million ARR as persistent VMs become a standard cloud feature. The sole-founder structure avoids the governance ambiguity I fear, and the terms say proxied traffic is neither stored nor inspected, so this is not a fatal privacy rejection. It is a missing-control rejection. One verifiable condition flips me: ten named customers each running retained agent fleets for at least 90 days on a machine0-controlled, multi-cloud execution layer, demonstrating that the durable asset is the agent environment rather than resold DigitalOcean compute.
