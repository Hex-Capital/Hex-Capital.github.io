# Last Accounting Company -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The contested truth is that accounting becomes agent-native only when the agent owns the ledger and the service, rather than operating somebody else’s software. What would prove this false? If human review remains the economic bottleneck, or if Pilot, Truewind, and Zeni can reproduce the same automation without replacing their existing systems, Last Accounting Company has built an integrated feature set rather than a new category. The dossier gives the secret substance—Björn collects documents, reconciles transactions, prepares filings, and maintains books—but does not identify a rejected consensus or disclose accuracy, correction-rate, or labor-efficiency results. It is a plausible secret under test, not yet a demonstrated zero-to-one result.

The strongest evidence is organizational. The four founders have known one another since childhood; Riki Karjalainen and Sami Laakkonen also built petabyte-scale financial-data infrastructure together at Token Terminal. Veikko Pere adds finance and legal training, while Pietari Virkkunen brings technology and investment experience. This is not a team assembled around a fashionable YC application. All four publicly identify with LAC, and the core ledger appears to have been built in-house. Founder ownership, compensation, and explicit full-time status remain undisclosed, so alignment is strongly indicated but not completely proved.

The Finnish beachhead is appropriately narrow, and the product has passed through more than $80 million in transaction value for dozens of companies. That is considerably more informative than a waitlist. Owning the ledger, evidence links, permissions, controls, MCP interface, and API may also let LAC improve from reviewed corrections while incumbents remain trapped above third-party systems. But processed volume is not market share, and proprietary architecture is not yet a monopoly mechanism. No disclosed evaluation shows a tenfold accuracy or cost advantage; no exclusive data right prevents imitation; and the company itself limits training to selected design partners. In a market already occupied by three funded AI-accounting providers, competition has not yet been escaped.

The bear case is therefore sharper than “AI accounting is crowded.” Human review may preserve statutory trust while destroying software margins. The service accesses bank, payroll, tax, Gmail, and Drive data, so each new jurisdiction adds security, liability, integration, and regulatory burdens. Fixed pricing could compound the problem if exception-handling labor grows with customer complexity. Europe’s $174.2 billion accounting-services market supports a large outcome, but no published price, revenue, gross margin, or accountant-to-customer ratio establishes that one engine can produce power-law economics.

The opposing case stings: dozens of companies have already entrusted the system with consequential records, and $80 million of transaction value suggests that customers are not merely experimenting with a chatbot. A proprietary Nordic ledger paired with human-reviewed output could become a data and workflow system that generic model providers cannot absorb. The childhood team and Token Terminal history also fit the pattern of an operator network forming around a difficult infrastructure thesis. Yet neither customer count nor transaction value reveals paid retention, automation depth, or value capture. The same evidence supports a valuable regional accounting firm and a dominant software company; those are radically different investments.

I would investigate rather than invest today. The verifiable flip condition is a twelve-month customer cohort showing at least 80% gross retention while more than 90% of routine transactions are completed without accountant correction and service gross margin exceeds 70%. That would connect the proposed secret to both monopoly economics and distribution. Without it, Last Accounting Company is founder-grade but not yet last-mover-grade: a strong team has built a real system in a real beachhead, while the evidence still cannot distinguish monopoly from a technologically sophisticated commodity.
