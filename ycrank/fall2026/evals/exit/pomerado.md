# Pomerado -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest private-company path. A cloud browser or agent workflow platform could buy Pomerado’s hosted integration catalog and repair capability to cover more legacy portals and reduce the cost of keeping those workflows running. Ownership could give the buyer control over coverage, repair priorities, and service reliability. That advantage matters only if it creates incremental value beyond building or licensing comparable tools. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

The public catalog grew from 46 tools to 584 in just over two weeks. That supports Pomerado’s ability to publish integrations quickly. The founders reported a 2.2% run error rate and automatic resolution of 82% of errors. The company’s separate benchmark used prebuilt integrations and one attempt per task, leaving reliability and maintenance costs under repeated production use open. A paid deployment that measures completion rates and repair costs through real portal changes is a plausible next milestone. The memo reports a $500,000 YC note, with runway and the cost of reaching that milestone unknown. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

The proposed investor’s terms are unavailable, so the proceeds bridge uses the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation. The initial 5% stake dilutes to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year, 5× objective requires $2.5 million in gross cash, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% ownership and $62.5 million at 4%.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing and cash to the investor |
|---|---|
| Modest acquisition | Year 5 at $40 million enterprise value yields $40 million in distributable equity and $1.2 million to the investor, or 2.4×. |
| Strong acquisition | Year 7 at $100 million enterprise value yields $100 million in distributable equity and $3 million to the investor, or 6×. At 2% ownership, the same sale yields $2 million, or 4×. |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. The investor retains the shares and realizes $0, or 0×. |

Both sale scenarios assume an all-cash closing, zero net debt adjustment, zero transaction fees, and simple pro rata allocation with preferences assumed to have no effect. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and separate compensation each contribute $0 to the model. These scenarios illustrate the hurdle. The memo supplies no buyer economics or valuation evidence to support an acquisition price, no sale-specific evidence about transfer rights or consents, and no independently supported fallback that pays this holder. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

A platform could use the MIT-licensed core, license the hosted product, or build a comparable capability. Skyvern has announced a similar workflow compilation and repair approach. Even a buyer that values the catalog would depend on customers maintaining authorization as portal operators change access policies or authentication. These facts weaken the ownership case. Whether those substitutes can match Pomerado’s production performance and cap a buyer’s price below the hurdle remains open, so the structural Pass override does not apply. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a potential platform buyer use results from a paid production deployment to show that owning Pomerado’s maintained integrations creates enough incremental value over building or licensing to support the modeled $83.3 million cash-sale hurdle? A buyer-validated comparison of completion rates and maintenance costs through real portal changes, with economics that support that price, would move the action to Reach out.
