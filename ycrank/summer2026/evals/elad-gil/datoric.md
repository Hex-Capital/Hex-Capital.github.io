# Datoric -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Nearly seven figures of revenue in 30 days makes Datoric a high-velocity operator whose weakest feature is its index position: Datoric is already selling into the multimodal-data wave, but Scale AI, Turing, Sama, and Toloka can all fight for the same projects. That pairing matters. Revenue says Datoric may be “just working”; weak structural distribution says Datoric may still be a fast-growing services business rather than an index on AI training.

**Harvey** is the high rung on revenue velocity because its valuation moved from $3B to $5B to $8B as buying behavior kept confirming the curve. Datoric shares the load-bearing mechanism: nearly seven figures from the current product in the preceding 30 days, not pipeline, LOIs, or recycled Arzule revenue. Datoric diverges from Harvey because one reported month is a snapshot, with no customer names, revenue concentration, renewals, or month-over-month series. I would pay for the Datoric curve before I paid for a story, but I would not yet extrapolate it.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism on index position: competing for share as the second player in a market whose network economics favor someone else. Datoric is not trapped in a pure consumer network effect, but Datoric must win projects against Scale AI’s $1.5B annualized run rate and established networks at Turing, Sama, and Toloka. Sample-level provenance, project isolation, and 300,000 reported active contributors lift Datoric above a thin labeling shop; those controls still look replicable rather than like a rail every multimodal developer must traverse.

Reasoning and multimodal models have shifted spending toward created human data, reportedly reaching roughly $1B annually at several AI companies in 2025. Over the next 18 months, that catalyst should compound Datoric’s demand for computer-use traces, 30-plus-language voice data, and egocentric video across 80 task categories. Datoric is skating for the puck in a market re-opened by model capability and the exhaustion of open-web data, although the dossier describes that catalyst as inferred rather than demonstrating a discrete regulatory or technical break owned by Datoric.

Nikhil Reddy and Jeffrey Lin took Arzule to more than $16K in monthly revenue after five weeks, pivoted, and then reported nearly seven figures from Datoric in one month. That is strong circumstantial evidence that Datoric’s founders ship and sell, not merely promote; Reddy’s 2023 automation of 15,000-plus annotation hours also reflects intimate knowledge of the fraud surface. The score would flip into the top rung if Datoric showed two or three dated product iterations driven by customer feedback, because rapid pivots plus revenue demonstrate cadence but not yet the Perplexity pattern of building specific requested artifacts between conversations.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow and rights infrastructure on top of a hard underlying capability can be more investable than betting on the capability itself. Datoric does not need a new model architecture or new physics; Datoric needs contributor verification, consent, QA, and licensing to remain reliable at 300,000 contributors. The single miracle is turning that operational system into durable trust at scale. Residential recordings, fraud control, privacy compliance, and repeatable margins create execution risks, but they are coupled parts of one operating problem rather than independent scientific miracles.

Nearly seven figures in July 2026 leads me to forecast that by July 2029 Datoric reaches $40–70M in annualized revenue, with most revenue still coming from build-to-spec collection, and is acquired by Turing rather than becoming an independent data infrastructure platform. Datoric’s visible buying velocity can produce a meaningful business quickly; Datoric’s replicable controls and project-based distribution make consolidation more likely than Stripe-like category ownership.

Scale AI’s $1.5B run rate is the strongest bear mechanism: incumbents can copy provenance features, subsidize collection, and turn Datoric’s nearly seven-figure month into a temporary, concentrated procurement event. Datoric’s 300,000 contributors may mean registered supply rather than productive supply, while residential-video privacy could raise costs faster than revenue. The evidence that wins today is actual current-product revenue plus a catalog containing 250,000 computer-use traces, not a science-project promise. I would flip to Pass if two consecutive quarters show less than 50% repeat-customer revenue or gross margin below 35%; I would flip the acquisition forecast toward an independent platform if rights-and-provenance software becomes a majority of Datoric’s revenue.
