# Lyon -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Training on 28 billion transactions is the fact that makes me take Lyon seriously. Noya’s NVIDIA and finance affiliations suggest proximity to the problem; building a model for a fintech serving tens of millions demonstrates action. The company-reported **4× precision improvement** in identifying premium-card converters is meaningful evidence of execution, although the unnamed customer and undisclosed benchmark methodology limit confidence ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/lyon)). I give the founders substantial credit for work in the exact domain. Their degrees add little to that evidence.

The ownership question is harder: what does Lyon accumulate when the customer gets better predictions? Its models are customer-owned and remain inside the customer’s VPC. That architecture may help procurement, but the dossier establishes neither exclusive data rights nor a Lyon-owned asset that compounds across deployments ([Lyon](https://lyon.so)). A competitor copying the code would still need to reproduce the training and operating competence. The evidence does not establish how difficult that would be. I therefore give defensibility the missing-evidence default rather than converting technical accomplishment into an assumed moat.

There is room to maneuver. Premium-card conversion provides an initial application; credit deployment underway and insurer engagement identify adjacent demand. Customer embeddings and task-specific adapters offer a concrete mechanism for addressing multiple predictions with shared infrastructure. That earns market credit without inventing a TAM. But Fundamental and SAP-owned Prior Labs give buyers alternatives, and Lyon has not established why customer-specific event training wins economically against pretrained approaches. Each additional application could expand the platform—or require another substantial implementation.

The strongest argument for investing now is that two founders have already reached an unusually large production-data environment. Lyon fits my pattern of backing infrastructure bottlenecks, and its current work is more informative than a polished demonstration. An early exception could be justified before revenue disclosure if the technical advantage were sufficiently durable. What is missing is the compounding mechanism: no observed customer-to-customer contribution loop, proprietary distribution, or retained model advantage. Monetization and network effects each receive their stated defaults. Neither silence proves a broken business. The dossier also supplies no affirmative evidence of integrity violations, artificial financing pressure, or reassurance dependence; uncertainty about a benchmark is not evidence of dishonesty.

I am **Neutral**. Lyon has demonstrated founder capability more clearly than enduring ownership of the resulting value. My money needs both. One verifiable flip condition would be a customer reference confirming a paid renewal because replacing Lyon requires rebuilding embedded models and adapters at a documented, material cost. That would establish direct value capture and a concrete reason the relationship survives a competitor reproducing the software.
