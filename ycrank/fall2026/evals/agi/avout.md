# Avout -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** Avout’s strongest case is that credit for hard-to-underwrite equipment becomes a bottleneck as cheaper intelligence increases demand for physical AI infrastructure. Avout says Series A and B hardware founders use venture equity for equipment and inventory when banks struggle to underwrite the assets. Its proposed financing workflow uses borrower operating data to help outside lenders assess collateral and repayment. The memo documents a GPU-backed facility supported by a customer contract, establishing a financing precedent. The credit gap among Avout’s target borrowers rests on company reports. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Avout says it helped arrange a $1.5 million term sheet for a data-center company. That is an early signal of lender interest, with borrower acceptance, closing, and disbursement unverified. The founders’ prior work in real-asset investing and regulated financial infrastructure supports early execution, while repeat origination remains unproven. Under the memo’s inferred placement-fee model, contribution per funded loan would depend on the fee and the cost of underwriting and placing both successful and unsuccessful applications. The memo gives no fee, conversion, or cost data for that calculation. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

With abundant competing capital, direct lenders and GPU specialists could serve the same borrowers. GPU Capital already uses server appraisals and customer contracts to size loans. With cheap frontier intelligence, competitors could also perform similar underwriting analysis. Borrowers would still need a lender willing to fund their assets, and Avout relies on outside lenders for that decision. The memo leaves open whether Avout has protected funding access or whether its operating data improves credit decisions. That blocks Reach out, but the existing alternatives do not establish equivalent terms for Avout’s target borrowers, so the Path A override is not triggered. Path B remains unsupported because the memo provides no evidence that automation makes a previously uneconomic financing transaction viable. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks, The Idea)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Avout repeatedly close loans for target hardware borrowers who lack comparable direct financing, under enforceable lender terms that protect Avout’s origination economics? Verified disbursements to multiple independent borrowers, documented comparisons with their direct financing options, and a signed lender agreement would answer it. That evidence would move Avout to Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 with at least medium confidence.
