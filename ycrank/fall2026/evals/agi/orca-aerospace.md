# Orca Aerospace -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest seed-stage case. Scheduled ground contacts can delay fault response. Orca proposes moving bounded decisions onboard, where a language model suggests actions and a deterministic planner and safety layer check commands before execution. The scenario is that fleet operators could routinely buy fault recovery during contact gaps if Orca can meet the required safety and response standards while reducing total operator labor per successful recovery. Orca plans human telemetry review before expanding command authority. The memo gives no Orca flight result or like-for-like labor comparison that counts review, retries, and exceptions. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

The signed Orbital letter of intent, worth up to $20 million for a planned fleet, and Lyra’s joint concept study show buyer interest. The LOI value depends on fleet deployment, and payments are undisclosed. The founders’ prior work on autonomous fault recovery and onboard collision avoidance is relevant to the next milestone. Orca still has to demonstrate integration on flight-representative equipment within spacecraft memory and power limits. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*)

The proposed scale unit is a satellite on an already integrated flight-software stack. In that scenario, the integration fee offsets stack-specific engineering and qualification, while each satellite license must cover incremental deployment, validation, and support. Reuse across Orbital’s fleet is planned, and the memo gives neither fee amounts nor delivery costs. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Shield AI and Sedaro demonstrated onboard AI with independent command checking, and AIKO demonstrated onboard replanning. With comparable models widely available, Orbital could choose another supplier if it can qualify equivalent software on its spacecraft. Orca may earn a switching advantage if replacing its software requires requalification, but the Reflex integration is still planned and the memo shows no buyer preference under functional parity. The rival demonstrations make substitution credible without establishing equivalence for Orbital. Both Path B gates remain unproven. Path A has no separate evidence that Orca controls scarce qualified capacity. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Orca turn the proposed Pathfinder test into a paid buyer qualification for routine fault response during contact gaps? A buyer-verified result would show that Orca meets agreed safety and response thresholds with sufficiently fewer operator hours per successful recovery than the ground workflow, including review, retries, and exceptions, and that the buyer commits to a paid deployment after testing a functionally comparable alternative. That result would support Reach out if the updated case clears the score and confidence gates. A result showing that review erases the labor savings or that the buyer readily switches at parity would move the action to Pass.
