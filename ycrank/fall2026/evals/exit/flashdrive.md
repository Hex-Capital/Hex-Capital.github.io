# FlashDrive -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by Dropbox is the strongest supported route. Dropbox has launched an agent infrastructure offering centered on object storage and plans to add filesystem capabilities. FlashDrive claims that its mounts and MCP access let agents share files across environments and merge simultaneous Markdown and CSV edits. If those merges reliably reduce lost work across local machines and cloud sandboxes, Dropbox could gain a reason to buy the capability to accelerate its filesystem roadmap. The memo provides no measured buyer benefit or acquisition interest. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The launch and its 28,040 post views establish early visibility. The next milestone is a production pilot that measures reductions in conflict-related rework against existing storage across local and sandbox environments. The released product makes the test plausible, while production adoption, pilot funding, and runway remain unverified. The memo supplies no transaction terms, evidence of transferability, or independent route to holder liquidity. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. Assume an eligible holder sells the entire stake for cash within eight years and receives 3% of equity proceeds after debt, deal costs, and any senior claims. With zero deductions, a **modest $50 million enterprise-value sale** pays $1.5 million (3×), and a **strong $100 million sale** pays $3 million (6×). The strong case can absorb about $16.7 million in deductions under that assumed waterfall and still meet the objective. **Prolonged private operation** with cash reinvested and no eligible share purchase yields zero realized proceeds. These are illustrative prices. The memo supplies no buyer economics or comparable sale price to support either. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

Dropbox could build enough filesystem functionality in-house, while AWS and Daytona already bundle shared storage into their environments. FlashDrive’s reported merge behavior covers Markdown and CSV, and the memo leaves reliability under interruptions and other file formats unverified. If those limits erase the advantage in production, a buyer has little reason to pay for the product. The substitutes weaken the acquisition case, but the memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a price ceiling that would trigger Pass. Watch fits the unresolved buyer economics. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can FlashDrive show a strategic buyer enough production value from cross-environment conflict handling to justify a price that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? A buyer-backed pilot showing repeatable reductions in conflict-related rework, with a credible price range that supports at least $83.3 million in distributable equity value under the benchmark, would move the action to Reach out.
