# Agency Tool Company -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Jack Morrison and Davis Foster worked together for eight years at Scythe Robotics before starting Agency Tool Company. Agency Tool Company is a practiced founder pair building the tool they wished they had, but the dossier shows more accumulated competence than fresh animal resourcefulness. That is the signature: unusually strong cofounder configuration, with the weakest evidence on what Morrison and Foster did specifically to force Agency Tool Company into existence before funding. I would still invest. Experience can make founders cautious, but these founders used it to identify a sharp, unpleasant problem.

**Stripe** (2010) is the high rung for cofounder configuration: the Collisons had years of shared trust, complementary hacking ability, and commerce experience predating Stripe. Morrison and Foster have the same load-bearing mechanism at Agency Tool Company after eight years together building and deploying hundreds of Scythe robots. Agency Tool Company diverges because the dossier provides no equivalent of the Collisons’ sibling shorthand or Auctomatic-era public hacker trail. But eight years operating physical machines together is a stronger test of founder compatibility than nearly any interview.

**Kiko** (2005) exposed the low-rung failure mechanism for resourcefulness: selling on eBay could have been an acquihire-shaped stopping point instead of evidence of frightening ambition. Scythe’s 2026 acquisition by ASI initially raises the same red flag, but Agency Tool Company weakens it by launching Deploy with Burro, Tempo Works, and Gather AI instead of taking comfortable jobs after the sale. That resembles the **Kiko** founders returning for **Twitch** more than founders cashing out. Agency Tool Company nevertheless has no cereal-box stunt, rejection-rescuing prototype, or other new track left by an animal, so I will not award full credit for inherited accomplishments.

Scythe Robotics deployed hundreds of autonomous machines across more than 30 states, and Agency Tool Company is the missing deployment platform Morrison and Foster wanted during that work. This is an organic idea in the strongest sense: Agency Tool Company starts with a scar, not a market report. Over the next 18 months that advantage should compound because every failed transfer, hardware variant, rollback, and approval chain can become product behavior competitors must rediscover through customer support. Three launch partners across agriculture, construction, and logistics already widen that learning loop beyond Scythe’s lawn-mowing context.

ATC Deploy moves containers, models, configurations, and operating-system images onto intermittently connected physical robots, where a targeting error can stop an operating fleet. Agency Tool Company would fall a tier on the schlep dimension if the claimed 20-times-faster delta transfer proved to be merely a cleaner Docker pull for robots with reliable broadband. The evidence points the other way: resumable byte-level delivery, selective targeting, approval chains, and rollback force Agency Tool Company to own precisely the tedious reliability work software-only founders unconsciously avoid.

**Dropbox** (2007) turned a working technical artifact into a more persuasive credential than a résumé. Agency Tool Company has a similar artifact signal because Deploy is already used by Burro, Tempo Works, and Gather AI, not merely demonstrated in a launch video. Agency Tool Company lacks public GitHub repositories for either Morrison or Foster, which cuts against my unstated hacker-genealogy pattern. Foster’s RIT dropout history, Thiel Fellowship, engineering leadership, and record of shipping hundreds of robots compensate substantially: deployed machines are a harder artifact to fake than an active GitHub graph.

Burro’s partner fleet exceeds 750 machines, and my 36-month forecast is that Agency Tool Company remains independent, serves at least 40 commercial robot fleets, and reaches $6 million to $12 million in ARR from deployment and hardware-backed CI. Agency Tool Company can reach that range by becoming the trusted release path beneath several robotics categories rather than competing with customers’ robot applications. The mechanism is schlep accumulation: each ugly edge case makes Agency Tool Company safer to keep and harder to replace with scripts.

InOrbit already supports remote software and configuration updates, Formant has raised $45.25 million, Balena covers general Linux fleets, and Agency Tool Company’s Build product is still “coming soon.” The strongest bear case is that Agency Tool Company has mistaken an internal Scythe tool for a standalone business: three friendly launch partners remain unpaid, internal platform engineers keep their SSH and Docker scripts, and better-funded vendors absorb delta deployment as a feature. Agency Tool Company wins my check because three named operators are using Deploy across distinct physical settings, while Morrison and Foster have already lived through the failure modes incumbents must learn secondhand. I would flip to Pass if none of Burro, Tempo Works, or Gather AI converts to a recurring paid deployment within 12 months.
