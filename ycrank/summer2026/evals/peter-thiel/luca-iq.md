# Luca IQ -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Luca IQ’s contested truth is that professional tax software should cease to be a closed application and become an API-accessible calculation-and-filing infrastructure layer. The falsification test is straightforward: if CPA firms use Luca IQ merely to automate data entry while retaining incumbents as the authoritative calculation system, the company has built an accessory, not a new foundation. The first tax season offers meaningful evidence for the stronger thesis—one customer reports 1,100 hours saved, a 57% reduction in administrative preparation, and 30 minutes from intake to review-ready—but it does not reveal whether the incumbent engine was actually displaced.

The product is more substantial than another fashionable tax copilot. Luca IQ passed IRS Assurance Testing, obtained federal 1040 e-file authorization, and built a deterministic engine connecting source documents, tax rules, return lines, diagnostics, and MeF transmission. This makes an uncertain input legible through an auditable system; AI interprets documents, but deterministic computation controls the legally consequential output. Yet authorization is qualification, not monopoly. April and Column Tax already offer API-based filing infrastructure, while Luca IQ presently lacks April’s reported national coverage and has not completed SOC 2 Type II certification.

The proposed beachhead—CPA firms handling professional 1040 preparation—is bounded and real. Hundreds of firms reportedly applied for the 2026 pilot, and customers completed a tax season before federal authorization arrived. But no dominant share, exclusive data, measured retention, or tenfold technical advantage is documented. Filing histories and review corrections may eventually create switching costs, but a future data flywheel is not a present moat. Luca IQ is therefore caught between two business forms: a proprietary system of record capable of replacing legacy vendors, or one more workflow component that those vendors and better-funded API competitors can bundle.

The founding structure is the most important unresolved gate. Angelo Policicchio has unusually direct domain knowledge—a CPA with EY and FBI forensic-accounting experience—while Akash Sadashivapeth brings engineering and data-science leadership. Their skills are complementary, and they participated together in the 2026 Rock Summer Fellows program and HBS New Venture Competition. But the dossier establishes neither a long working history nor founder ownership, economic exposure, or the full-time status of every core founder. Two 2026 Harvard MBAs building together may become a durable founding relationship; it may also be a recently assembled professional partnership. Missing evidence is not refutation, but it cannot establish a sound foundation.

The revenue engine is coherent—fixed fees based on return volume—but its power-law arithmetic is absent. Luca IQ discloses neither rates, paid-customer count, revenue, gross margins, nor expansion per firm. The $23.94 billion global tax-software TAM cannot substitute for this arithmetic. Distribution is similarly half-solved: the company names CPA firms, accounting-practice acquirers, and fintech developers, but these are three routes with different contract values and sales motions. Hiring an intern to cold-call and book demonstrations is evidence of activity, not a repeatable channel. The completed tax season matters more, although the conversion of hundreds of applicants into paying, retained firms remains undisclosed.

The strongest bull case is that the market has already supplied the proof normally missing at pre-seed: working software, a regulated authorization, repeat production use, and a customer reporting four-digit labor-hour savings. Those facts could mean the calculation engine—not the AI interface—is the overlooked infrastructure asset. They do not yet amount to the exceptional zero-to-one technical proof that would override commodity competition and uncertain founding alignment. My base case is that Luca IQ reaches $3–8 million ARR by August 2029 but remains an integration layer rather than controlling the professional-tax calculation market. I would reconsider upon one verifiable result: at least 50 paying firms processing 50,000 annual returns through Luca IQ as their authoritative calculation engine, with over 90% firm retention after a second tax season. Until then, this is a promising product inside a competitive category, not yet a last mover. Neutral.
