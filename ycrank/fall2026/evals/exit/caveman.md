# Caveman -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Caveman’s strongest path. An enterprise AI gateway or coding-agent platform could buy Caveman to embed its proprietary coding harness and Cloud’s workload replay and rollback controls across its customer base. Ownership could justify a premium if control of the harness and rollout system lets the buyer capture durable savings beyond what it could achieve by building or licensing comparable tools. Palo Alto Networks’ acquisition of Portkey shows a strategic buyer integrating an adjacent gateway capability. The memo gives no purchase price for that deal. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The original repository showed about 121,000 stars, and Caveman shipped a local proxy, Cloud, and Code within 2026. Those are observable distribution and execution signals. In company-run tests, Code cost 21% less per solved task than Codex, with both solving 90 of 192 runs. The next operating milestone is a buyer-relevant production test that shows repeatable savings after billing effects, with task success maintained. The launch cadence and reported YC funding make that test plausible at seed. Its cost and Caveman’s remaining runway are unknown. IP assignment and the rights to transfer customer contracts or workload data are also unverified. The memo supplies no independent fallback that could pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The proceeds bridge uses the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-on checks, and a $2.5 million gross cash target within eight years. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt and transaction fees, a simple pro rata allocation with no preference adjustment, and full cash payment at closing before investor taxes. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. Prices and timing are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no usable valuation evidence.

| Modest acquisition | Year 5 | $50M | $50M | $1.5M | 3.0× |

At 2% ownership, even the modeled $100 million sale returns $2 million, or 4.0×. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and Caveman reinvests available cash. The investor still holds the shares at year eight.

### Countercase

A buyer could use gateway compression such as Edgee’s or license Caveman’s tools if either option delivers comparable savings. The memo identifies no structural barrier to those substitutes. It also reports that an independent test of Caveman’s original skill used 8.5% fewer output tokens while increasing the total bill after a run crossed a long-context pricing tier. That test covered the original skill and shows why the current product needs production evidence on actual billed cost per successful task. The substitution risk and unsupported purchase price warrant Watch. The memo establishes neither a substitute that matches the full production benefit nor a binding ceiling on what a buyer could pay, so the Pass override does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Caveman demonstrate enough incremental value from owning its harness and rollout controls to support an acquisition that distributes about $83.3 million to equity holders? A prospective buyer’s production test should compare actual billed cost per successful task at comparable quality against its best build or licensing option. A buyer-side estimate showing that the measured advantage at its workload scale could justify that price would move the action to Reach out.
