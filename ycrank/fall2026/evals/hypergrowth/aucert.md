# Aucert -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with mobile engineering and QA teams, Aucert’s core release-verification workflow looks portable. Teams can submit a build or app-store link, and Aucert describes agents that explore the app and return findings for release review. The same workflow could serve teams across markets through common mobile distribution channels. The free link-based scan and sample report also give buyers a concrete way to evaluate the product. Whether buyers can purchase and set up private-build verification independently remains unclear. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Each release creates a reason to run another scan. Aucert says its organization-scoped knowledge graph retains what agents learn about each app, and customer-approved expectations could make later scans more useful. Draft monthly terms specify limits on scans, devices, seats, and storage. Those limits suggest ways to charge for heavier use, while recurring payments and paid expansion remain unverified. The October public launch and company-reported November private beta establish the product’s early stage. Paying-customer counts, realized revenue, and commercial growth remain unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under a paid-subscription scenario, incremental contribution would be the subscription fee less AI processing, device runtime, storage, and any sales, setup, and support time attributable to the account. Aucert says AI providers receive captured screenshots and UI hierarchies. The draft plan limits identify some usage drivers, but pricing and delivery costs at representative workloads remain unresolved. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

A free scan of a public app may be easy to try while production verification remains labor-intensive. Agents can miss unvisited flows, and buyers may need to approve expectations and review findings before relying on scans for releases. Aucert also says it sends screen data to AI providers as captured, with redaction planned. If typical buyers require hands-on setup or extensive manual review on every release, adoption and contribution could both suffer. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo leaves the amount of required work and its cost unresolved. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of mobile QA teams go from a free link-based scan to paid, recurring verification of private builds with minimal founder help and positive incremental contribution? Billing records, scan logs across successive releases, and measured delivery costs would answer it. A repeatable result that supports a score of at least 65 at Medium or High confidence would move Aucert to Reach out.
