# Limnion -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Todd Persen has earned the right to attempt this. Co-founding InfluxData and Era Software, then serving as Hydrolix CTO, represents repeated action inside databases, logs, and streaming analytics. Era’s 2022 acquisition by ServiceNow supplies an external outcome. Limnion has unusually strong evidence of founder-specific knowledge. The investment question is whether that knowledge becomes an asset that compounds outside the founder’s head.

Siglake’s architecture makes that difficult to establish today. OpenTelemetry ingestion, WALs, distributed SQL, and indexed Parquet files represent substantial engineering. But Apache-2.0 licensing lets competitors inherit the implementation, and externally readable Iceberg tables deliberately preserve customer portability. Those choices can make the product attractive while leaving ownership of the resulting economics contested. The dossier identifies no exclusive data, proprietary model, or accumulated operational advantage that survives copying the code. That affirmative replicability puts defensibility below my missing-evidence default.

The strongest objection is that Persen could turn operational difficulty into a business before competitors turn available code into a reliable service. Customers buy someone who answers when storage fails. His experience makes that credible, and my exceptions permit backing demonstrated builders before monetization or distribution becomes visible. However, the managed offering remains in development, the documented 72-hour soak attempt failed, and tenant isolation and multi-replica failover remain unqualified. Limnion has yet to demonstrate the operational asset that would justify that exception. Publishing these limitations is useful candor; the dossier contains no documented integrity or artificial-deadline red flag.

The market offers room, but the expansion argument remains broad. Engineering teams replacing proprietary telemetry storage are identifiable buyers. The cited $11.91 billion observability market includes capabilities Siglake delegates to Grafana, Jaeger, and external alerting systems; it cannot all be credited to this wedge. Limnion does fit my pattern of enabling outsiders through permissionless tools. Its 577 GitHub stars and three forks, observed November 3, establish attention, while leaving customer-created utility unproven. There is no demonstrated contribution or referral loop. With no published pricing or revenue, direct value capture also receives exactly the absent-evidence default.

I would keep Limnion close and hold my check. The founder signal is strong enough to prevent a dismissive pass; the current asset structure keeps me neutral. My single verifiable flip condition is a production study showing that each additional deployment contributes exclusively reusable optimization data that measurably lowers costs for existing customers. That would connect Persen’s specific knowledge to customer-created value that compounds across deployments.
