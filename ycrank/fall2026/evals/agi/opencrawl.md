# OpenCrawl -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

OpenCrawl scores zero on **Building blocks of superintelligence**. Its shared board and searchable transcripts address a specific workload, coordinating coding agents across tools and machines. More parallel sessions could increase the need for coordination, but Agent Relay already offers cross-tool messaging and searchable sessions, and Claude Code provides native messaging and shared tasks. The memo establishes workflow friction and available substitutes, with no evidence that scarce coordination capacity limits AI deployment. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

OpenCrawl scores zero on **Industry enabled by superintelligence**. The company describes engineering teams manually relaying context and agents repeating investigations or making conflicting changes. Automatic capture and shared updates could reduce that work. The memo documents an efficiency opportunity for teams already using coding agents and gives no evidence of a buyer or workflow that becomes economically viable at a previously impractical frequency. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The strongest commercial case is hosted coordination for teams using multiple agent clients. Accumulated decision history could raise switching costs, but that remains an inference given feature overlap and the absence of retention or migration evidence. The public launch and repository activity establish that the team shipped, and the founders’ data infrastructure and agent experience support execution credit. The CTO’s report of onboarding and agent-to-agent messaging is an initial use signal. Repeat paid use and dependable delivery across clients remain unverified. Reported overage pricing provides a potential revenue line, while ingestion, storage, retrieval, and support costs are unknown. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

If manual handoffs and conflicting edits make frequent parallel work uneconomic for mixed-client teams, shared history and live coordination could open a genuinely new workflow. That outcome could still require a distinct service under model parity because the service connects sessions across tools and machines. The CTO-reported messaging event shows an initial interaction, with no measured savings or evidence that a team could sustain a previously impractical workflow. Both AGI paths score zero, triggering the Pass gate. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a mixed-client engineering team use OpenCrawl to sustain a previously uneconomic cadence of parallel coding-agent work? A paid pilot that holds agent capability constant, compares the team’s existing workflow, and measures cost per successfully merged change including coordination, review, and rework would answer it. Repeated paid use at the newly viable cadence, with the improvement attributable to cross-client coordination, would change the action to Reach out.
