# The Agentic Data Co. -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest path in Fall 2026. A speech-model lab could buy YapRoom’s recording pipeline, rights to private data, and audio simulation tooling to control what gets collected and how models practice interruptions and tool use. That ownership rationale is an inference. It depends on the lab getting a durable production benefit that it cannot reproduce economically through licensing or an internal build. (The Idea, Defensibility)

**Observed evidence.** OpenYap Turn placed second on end-of-turn detection, a concrete signal for conversational timing. YapRoom produced 1,602 conversations from 239 speakers with synchronized tracks and participant consent, giving the team an operating base for a production test. **Company claim.** Unnamed AI labs buy its data. The memo gives no contract economics or production results for the combined data and simulation offering. A paid lab pilot measuring turn-taking and tool-task completion against licensed alternatives is a plausible seed milestone. Reported funding figures differ, and the budget and runway for that test are unknown. The memo documents participant consent but leaves assignment and change-of-control rights unverified. A buyout, secondary, IPO resale, or company-funded payout has no separately supported path to cash for this holder. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Facts, Key Risks)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** Use a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3%, with no follow-on checks. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume all-cash consideration, zero net debt and other price adjustments, and a simple pro rata waterfall with no preference deductions. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. The memo supplies no acquisition price evidence or holder terms, so these are sensitivities before investor taxes.

### Countercase

A lab can start with the free commercial-use corpus, license the company’s private data, or buy consented full-duplex audio from oto, whose published entry price is $10 per audio hour. YapRoom’s participant payouts alone are $20 per usable two-person hour before review, adding pressure to the economics of owning collection. The benchmark measures conversational timing and leaves tool-task performance unverified. These facts weaken the acquisition rationale, but they do not establish that licensed alternatives can match the combined offering or cap a strong outcome below the hurdle. The Pass override does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a paid speech-lab production pilot show that owning the collection pipeline, transferable private-data rights, and simulation tooling creates durable value over ordinary licensing or an internal build? Move to Reach out if the pilot measures a sustained production gain and the lab’s economics plausibly support the base-case proceeds hurdle. Move to Pass if licensing delivers equivalent results and the resulting price ceiling leaves holder cash materially short.
