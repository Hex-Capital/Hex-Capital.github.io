# Composal -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

By 2032, I can imagine a small team asking software to build, explore, and repair a product while people decide what deserves to exist. Composal is working on an awkward step along that path: who checks the work when the programmer is also a machine? Its October customer research supplies a small leak from that future—teams still manually walk user journeys after automated tests pass. Getting from there to autonomous releases requires reliable exploration of unfamiliar applications, then evidence trustworthy enough to authorize shipping. That is a concrete path, but Composal has demonstrated only part of it.

Why does the human still have to click through everything? Because passing a test and satisfying a user are different events, especially when a coding agent helps produce both the feature and its tests. Composal proposes an independent browser observer that returns recordings, logs, and reproduction steps. This could return time and release confidence to small engineering teams. The dossier identifies duplicated labor, however, rather than a gatekeeper extracting a quantified rent. The [$500 monthly subscription](https://composal.com/docs/verify) gives me a price to investigate, not evidence of customer savings or revenue.

The most revealing number is the one false success in Composal’s [166-attempt local benchmark](https://composal.com/reflog/openai-decisions-browser-agents). I appreciate that the team explicitly limits what its 157 passes establish. A verifier that confidently misses a failure can preserve the very human checking it promises to remove. Meanwhile, half of ten recent setup calls stalled on authentication or network restrictions. People spending time trying to connect the product is some evidence of interest; it does not establish repeated dependence. I want to inhabit the product, then watch someone else trust it when a release matters.

The founders make this more interesting than an interchangeable testing pitch. Zembashis has worked on coding agents, sandboxes, and repository infrastructure; Davies brings product engineering and prior company-building experience; Moehlmann has built commercial functions at AI companies. Their overlapping employment also gives the team some shared operating history. But those credentials do not yet establish the dated, independently pursued obsession I look for. Composal resembles my pre-revenue **CosmicBrain AI** exception in offering enabling software for autonomous work; the material difference is that the dossier lacks comparable signed deployment commitments and a documented lifelong building record. Missing revenue would not stop me. The evidence supporting that exception would need to be present elsewhere.

Composal also fits my less obvious preference for infrastructure concealed beneath a fashionable application wave. Its public CLI, MCP, and repository integrations could put verification inside the development loop, and persistent journey maps could become useful accumulated knowledge. The strongest bull case is that every increase in generated code makes independent verification more valuable. Nine interviewed teams building their own QA agents support the existence of that need. They also show that customers can attempt the solution themselves, while mabl’s October announcement shows an incumbent approaching the same dependency. Composal controls a harness and product context; the dossier does not establish control over a scarce bottleneck. Credentials and recordings reaching external AI providers also require clear custody boundaries. Disposable accounts and documented controls are constructive, but they do not settle that question.

I am **Neutral** with my own money. The future is legible; evidence that users have begun living in it is thin. I see no documented leadership shell game or basis to assume dependence on one closed model supplier, so neither earns a penalty. My one flip condition is a verifiable deployment cohort: three independent teams paying the listed subscription for three months, repeatedly using Composal in release decisions, with auditable missed-bug results and documented limits on credential and recording exposure. That would connect the attractive system story to behavior someone has something to lose by changing.
