# Tarifflo Inc. -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I need are missing: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and what this round specifically buys. Summer 2026 YC membership does not prove an auction, but it does not prove scarce capital either. Tarifflo therefore gets no benefit of the doubt on price. More importantly, “grow the platform” is not a venture moment. The company must name the finite experiment—probably moving its ABI product from private beta into production—and the dated result that would make the business materially more valuable.

The operating evidence is much better than the financing evidence. Tarifflo has audited more than 1 million products, links over 10 million shipment records, and reports recovering $1.3 million for Orly after reviewing 10,000-plus items. Public companies had begun signing onto the earlier classification product by February 2025. Those are customer actions tied to a painful workflow, not LinkedIn theater. The direct CBP connection, filer code, licensed-broker review, and brokerage-partner channel form a credible translation layer between importers who mistrust compliance software and customs infrastructure that resists casual automation.

Bryce Judy is unusually legible for this problem. He has performed customs-classification work at KPMG, holds a CBP broker license, published HTS benchmarking research, and has now shipped a product into the same regulated workflow. This is not a résumé assembled around a fashionable tariff story. The active-founder ambiguity is also limited: YC lists Judy alone, so I know who is accountable even though the company previously described Tanner Helms as a co-founder.

The dark pool is plausible but not yet proven durable. Tarifflo may possess reviewed classifications, correction outcomes, and linked shipment histories, but the dossier does not establish that it owns those records exclusively or that they measurably improve decisions. Zonos already supplies CBP-selected infrastructure; KlearNow has customs connections across five countries; Altana sells network intelligence to enterprises and government agencies. If Tarifflo’s advantage is simply current tariff volatility plus another AI classifier, the next well-funded vendor can copy the public thesis. The proprietary asset must be the closed loop from entry data to accepted correction to collected recovery.

The strongest bull case is that the contingency model has already converted obscure compliance knowledge into measurable customer cash while requiring no upfront purchase. That is precisely the kind of neglected, physical-world operating system where difficult integration can matter more than elegant software. But $1.3 million recovered is not $1.3 million earned. Tarifflo discloses neither its contingency percentage nor collected revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, refund cycle, or broker-review cost. A business paid only after refunds—sometimes behind 300-day correction windows—can produce spectacular customer ROI while starving itself of cash.

I am neutral at the current evidence. No known-exception override rescues undocumented price and capital use: Tarifflo has meaningful deployment, but not the extraordinary adoption, retention, acquisition interest, or cash economics that would justify ignoring both gates. One verifiable flip condition would change my decision: show financing documents below a $10 million post-money valuation that dedicate this check to reaching production ABI filing by a fixed date, with ten unaffiliated importers completing filings and at least $500,000 of collected, positive-contribution-margin contingency revenue. Then the round would purchase a truth rather than another fundraising narrative.
