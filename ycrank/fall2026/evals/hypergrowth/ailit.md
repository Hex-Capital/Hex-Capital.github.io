# Ailit -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Usage-based expansion is Ailit’s strongest path. Freight railroads have a recurring traction-power need. Ailit proposes supplying it through swappable battery tenders on existing diesel-electric locomotives, with diesel available as backup. Ailit would own the packs and swap sites and charge per kWh at a proposed price about 20% below diesel-equivalent cost. The job plausibly travels across markets, although each rollout depends on locomotive compatibility, suitable swap stops, and adequate utility connections. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The memo documents a full-scale prototype positioned behind a locomotive in Richmond ahead of commissioning. A first customer deployment is planned for 2027. The memo identifies a supplier-directory listing and gives no evidence of a buyer-funded test, a repeatable route from discovery to paid activation, repeat billed use, or measured incremental delivery costs. The first unresolved link is getting a railroad into paid, recurring loaded service with swaps that fit its schedule. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

If a first corridor proves value, a railroad could expand by shifting more scheduled runs to battery power. In a favorable scenario, those runs use spare pack and charging capacity at existing stops. Incremental billed revenue could then grow faster than delivery costs if it covers electricity, battery wear, swap labor, and any added capacity. The memo leaves those costs and the available capacity unmeasured. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Each additional corridor could require its own utility connection, swap sites, and backup pack inventory. If those costs rise roughly in proportion to billed kWh, and lower diesel prices compress the tariff, paid volume could grow without improving contribution. Loaded-haul performance and repeatable swap times also remain unproven. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo contains no operating evidence showing that proportional costs are unavoidable. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can the first railroad customer sustain paid battery use on scheduled loaded runs and add more runs through the same swap sites with positive incremental contribution? Move to Reach out if billing and operating records show repeat paid kWh at the railroad’s normal cadence, swaps that fit its schedule, and revenue from the added runs that covers their measured delivery and capacity costs.
