# Osseus -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Osseus links CAD, electronics, software, tests, and supplier constraints while naming two co-founders and no CEO. Osseus is an unsexy robotics-infrastructure bet whose strongest dimension is proximity to difficult physical engineering, while its weakest is the absence of a human tiebreaker. I can imagine the permissioned graph becoming useful; I cannot yet identify who makes the irreversible decision when Anish Mariathasan and Ayush Patel disagree. Magically weird machinery needs a driver.

SpaceX is the high rung for capital-intensive frontier hardware: the rocket itself forced technical truth into the open. Osseus shares the instinct to enter a hard physical domain where mechanical, electrical, and software decisions collide, and customer-cloud or VPC deployment makes Osseus less toy-like than another browser AI wrapper. The divergence is load-bearing: Osseus does not build the robot, own the test rig, or absorb hardware risk, and Osseus explicitly warns that its AI findings can be wrong. It sells intelligence around frontier artifacts rather than a frontier artifact.

GameCrush is the low-rung governance failure: multiple founders, compromised product authority, and no reliable tiebreaker when pressure arrived. Osseus names Anish and Ayush only as “Co-Founder,” documents a shared Imperial College history from 2022–2026, and supplies no CEO, equity split, operating authority, vesting protocol, or conflict mechanism. Osseus therefore reproduces the structural question before earning the HQ Trivia exception; invited workspaces are not tactile virality powerful enough to override founder ambiguity.

CoLab’s $72 million Series C makes the next 18 months a compounding race rather than a blank canvas. If Osseus forward-deploys deeply, every linked revision, supplier constraint, failure, and approved finding can improve Osseus’s customer-specific graph and create switching costs. If connector maintenance consumes the founders, CoLab, Duro, and Bild can absorb the visible features while Osseus becomes a services shop with an elegant ontology. “AI-native PLM” is already a category sentence, so Osseus sits closer to consensus infrastructure than to Crusoe stapling Bitcoin miners onto gas flares.

Anish Mariathasan brings biomedical engineering, compliance AI, computer-vision research, and an abandoned Oxford AI-for-health PhD; Ayush Patel brings Apple agentic-QA work and a Raspberry Pi self-driving robot. Osseus therefore has adjacent technical exposure, but the dossier never shows either founder living inside a robotics release process, chasing a supplier-induced failure, or shipping a multidisciplinary robot at scale. Osseus would flip upward if Anish or Ayush could identify a specific pre-company hardware failure caused by the disconnected systems Osseus now joins. Until then, they look like talented visitors to the well pad, not people of the place.

Niantic was observable before it was legible: I could play Ingress and feel the product changing physical behavior. Osseus can anchor findings to a connector, net, part, or dimension, but Osseus itself remains an invitation-only B2B workspace available through a scoped demo. The underlying robot is tactile; the value of Osseus is still an abstraction validated primarily by founder testimony. No public deployment, repeat usage, or named robotics customer supplies the Flock Safety camera—or even the Postmates delivery—I can watch happen.

Duro is my forecasted endpoint: by July 2029, Osseus will be acquired by Duro for less than $20 million after failing to exceed $2 million ARR. Osseus is attacking a real, ugly integration problem, but custom connectors across CAD, electronics, code, simulation, tests, suppliers, and issue systems create endless prioritization disputes. Without a named CEO, Osseus is unusually exposed to the moment when one founder wants to become a broad PLM platform and the other wants to remain a robotics-specific intelligence layer.

DeepMind supplies the strongest bull case: Anish’s biomedical, compliance, and vision-language work plus Ayush’s Apple QA and self-driving-robot work could be polymath compounding rather than shallow adjacency. Osseus also has a thoughtful security architecture—mirrored permissions, default-deny memory promotion, and VPC deployment—that incumbents may struggle to retrofit. The DeepMind exception stings, but Osseus has no public repository, named pilot, revenue, or artifact demonstrating exceptional technical discontinuity, while CoLab and Duro already sell adjacent AI workflows. A named robotics customer renewing Osseus at $250,000 or more annually after twelve months would flip my verdict; without that proof, I keep my own money in my pocket.
