# Raven -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a handset maker is Raven’s strongest path. The demonstrated prototype shows a workflow layer that can take a request, act across apps, and surface a result or draft for approval. An OEM could buy that layer if integrating it across its handset lineup would bring reliable workflows to market faster than building or partnering. That benefit is an inference. Buyer interest, the size of the benefit, and transfer rights for Raven’s software and third-party integrations remain unverified. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

Raven reports shipping early test handsets. The commercial milestone is a production cohort that keeps paying after the initial three months and repeatedly completes cross-app tasks. The $199 pre-order is refundable, and the projected $99–$199 monthly price still needs validation. Raven is working with hardware partners, and the funding needed for manufacturing and fulfillment remains unknown. The prototype’s $50,000 award was in API credits. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

**Proceeds scenario.** Assume a $500,000 check at $10 million post-money, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target requires $2.5 million in gross cash before investor taxes, or $83.3 million of distributable equity. With $5 million of illustrative net debt, that means about $88.3 million in enterprise value. On the same assumptions, a $25 million sale yields $600,000 (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). At 2% ownership, that same $100 million sale yields $1.9 million (3.8×). These prices are scenarios. Raven’s realizable sale price, actual debt, fees, and senior claims remain unknown. (*Key Facts*)

If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and Raven reinvests available cash in hardware and operations, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. The memo provides no independent basis to underwrite a sponsor buyout, eligible secondary, IPO resale, or funded payout. Wind-down proceeds would be limited to any residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantifiable. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Samsung already offers supported multi-step tasks on the Galaxy S26, and Phone Harness lets agents act on existing phones. A handset maker could extend its own software or partner for similar capabilities. Raven’s reported early deliveries leave open whether its integrated workflow earns sustained usage that an OEM would pay to own. These substitutes weaken the acquisition case and support Watch. A Pass gate would require evidence that the alternatives eliminate the reason to buy or that the strongest credible sale leaves the holder materially short. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Raven’s production usage support an OEM acquisition price that clears the holder’s cash target? Move to Reach out when a funded cohort shows recurring successful workflows and paid retention beyond the initial three months, and a buyer-side build-versus-buy analysis tied to those results supports a price around or above the modeled $88.3 million hurdle, adjusted for actual ownership and claims. Otherwise, keep watching.
