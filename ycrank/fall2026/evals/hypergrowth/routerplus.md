# RouterPlus -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales with account expansion.** Production AI teams have a recurring need to manage inference cost, latency, and peak capacity. A consumer app reported 25% lower P95 latency after switching models, and an AI assistant company reported that RouterPlus sourced capacity for peaks of 15× average traffic and arranged a backup. These testimonials show a concrete reason to change a production workflow. The company-reported 3 million daily agent conversations at its largest customer is a volume snapshot. Paid status, retention, and workload expansion remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The growth scenario assumes RouterPlus can turn developer signups and workload consultations into paid capacity contracts using the same API, benchmarking, bidding, and failover process, then add workloads within each account. The memo documents those entry points and one company-reported pooled provider deal. Conversion through either entry point and reuse across geographies remain unverified. A second-market rollout would test whether eligible providers can meet local latency and data-handling requirements through the same setup. The published data policy describes prompt and response retention on shared endpoints, with opt-out and dedicated endpoint options that could change the rollout. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

In that scenario, incremental contribution would come from the reserved-capacity quote after provider and backup costs RouterPlus bears, gateway compute, and the labor needed to benchmark, onboard, and support the account. Any capacity RouterPlus commits to ahead of customer demand would add exposure to idle capacity and working capital. Published token rates carry no RouterPlus markup, and bring-your-own-key traffic carries no RouterPlus inference fee. The memo gives neither realized reserved-capacity revenue nor rollout costs. That leaves the economics of the growth mechanism untested. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

RouterPlus could be a capacity broker whose work grows with every account. Individual quotes, workload-specific model tests, peak validation, and backup procurement could each require fresh sales and engineering effort. Existing alternatives already offer routing and failover, which may limit what customers will pay for those features. The pooled deal gives RouterPlus a plausible way to reuse procurement work, but the memo gives no cost comparison across deployments. The structural Pass gate remains untriggered because proportional costs at scale are unproven. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Move to **Reach out** if one deployment shows paid usage through a normal billing cycle, meets peak and data-handling requirements with the existing setup, and records positive contribution after provider, backup, compute, and staff costs without a bespoke rebuild.
