# Caution -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Caution’s contested truth is precise: hardware attestation is insufficient because a binary is not trustworthy unless an outsider can reproduce it from reviewed source and match it to the running workload. What would prove this wrong? Either independent rebuilds routinely fail, or customers decide that AWS Nitro’s existing assurances are sufficient. Caution has built the relevant experiment—a git-based workflow covering source, kernel, configuration, and toolchain—and deployed it with customers. But the dossier does not show that named peers reject this proposition; competitors addressing attestation and confidential computing suggest an acknowledged problem, not an undiscovered truth. This is a credible technical thesis, but only a partially contrarian one.

The founders are the strongest organizational evidence. Anton Livaja and Lance Vick have worked together since co-founding Distrust in 2021, Ksenia Lesko operated alongside them there, and their experience spans Ledn, BitGo, Turnkey, HSMs, supply-chain security, and security consulting. Caution was formed by an existing team around work they already understood, not by strangers pursuing a fashionable category. Yet the dossier does not establish that every founder is full-time, that core work is entirely in-house, or that the founders retain meaningful ownership. Long alignment is demonstrated; complete economic alignment is not.

The decisive weakness is monopoly. Caution describes itself as general-purpose while supporting only AWS Nitro, serves customers across five disparate workload categories rather than dominating one bounded beachhead, and distributes the complete platform under AGPLv3. Cloud vendors control the trust roots, while OPAQUE, Edgeless Systems, and enclaive already overlap with deployment, orchestration, and attestation. Reproducibility is a useful feature, but no patent, exclusive data, network effect, measured technical advantage, or customer-imposed standard prevents replication. Caution has found a technical secret without yet finding the business secret that lets it retain the value created.

The commercial structure is similarly indefinite. Engineer-led outreach and private-beta onboarding have produced live deployments, including the publicly identified TrustedStake relationship, so distribution is not merely a slide. But Caution discloses no price, sales cycle, conversion rate, contract value, revenue, or renewal behavior. Commercial licensing, managed customer-cloud deployments, fully managed hosting, and support are four possible monetization modes rather than one proven revenue engine. A $153.8 billion market forecast cannot supply the missing arithmetic.

The bull case is real: 84.5% of surveyed organizations identify attestation-chain validation as a barrier, Caution already has production workloads, and the team has unusually direct experience with the problem. A genuine zero-to-one technical event can sometimes override an uncertain monopoly, as OpenAI did, but the material difference is validation: Caution has not shown an independently measured breakthrough or customer behavior powerful enough to remove substitution risk. Its users demonstrate usefulness, not inevitability.

My 36-month forecast is that Caution becomes a sub-$1 million ARR managed security and compliance consultancy focused on crypto and private-AI enclaves, rather than a general-purpose hosting platform. I pass because the weakest primary gate—value capture—is contradicted rather than merely undocumented. I would reverse that judgment if three unrelated customers each renewed contracts above $100,000 annually because their auditors required Caution’s source-to-runtime baseline across AWS Nitro and at least one independent hardware trust root. That would turn a reproducible tool into an emerging standard.
