# Invertix -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a large solar operator is Invertix’s strongest supported route. Its agents triage alarms, coordinate repairs, and verify production through existing tools. Altenia’s CEO reported that some work went from days to minutes. That suggests a buyer could gain by deploying the software across its fleet if owning it offers a durable economic advantage over licensing it. The testimonial establishes a workflow benefit, while annual savings and acquisition interest remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The next milestone is a paid production rollout that reproduces those gains across multiple sites and measures integration cost. The €1.7 million pre-seed and reported testing with TotalEnergies’ solar teams support pursuing that milestone. Invertix reports roughly 2 GW of agent coverage. Engineer-led integration leaves rollout time, runway, and additional capital needs uncertain. Contract transfer, data rights, and holder eligibility remain open. The memo gives no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. Both sale scenarios below assume the holder is eligible to sell the full stake, receives cash by year eight, and shares pro rata after debt and deal adjustments, with no preference diversion or holdbacks. The prices are illustrative assumptions. Buyer willingness to pay remains unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Cash proceeds to the holder |
|----------|-----------------------------|
| Modest sale | $40 million enterprise value less $5 million in assumed debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $1.05 million, or 2.1×. |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value less $10 million in assumed debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $2.7 million, or 5.4×. At the same price, selling half the stake yields $1.35 million, or 2.7×. |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or funded payout yields zero realized proceeds within eight years. |

### Countercase

A buyer could capture much of the workflow benefit through licensing or a competing product. Metris and Areg.AI already offer overlapping agent workflows, and Invertix’s engineer-led deployments could make fleet-wide integration expensive. That weakens the case for paying an acquisition price that clears the holder’s hurdle. The memo leaves both the availability of a ready substitute and the achievable sale price unresolved, so neither Pass gate is established. (Defensibility, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a paid, multi-site rollout produce enough savings after integration costs to justify a strategic purchase that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? Move to Reach out if measured fleet-level savings and a buyer-side comparison of ownership and licensing support roughly $83.3 million or more in distributable equity under the benchmark. Evidence that the strongest credible route leaves the holder materially short would change the action to Pass.
