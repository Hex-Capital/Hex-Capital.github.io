# hiloop -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The contested truth is that recursive improvement will come less from inventing another training algorithm than from giving agents reproducible control over the entire experimental loop. What would prove this wrong? If hiloop’s 4,188-trial result remains confined to NanoGPT-style benchmarks and human researchers cannot reproduce comparable gains on proprietary objectives, then “infrastructure for recursive self-improvement” is a slogan rather than a secret. The initial evidence is unusually concrete: 50 B200 GPUs over two days, 25 paired confirmation runs with interleaved controls, and median validation performance improving from the cited 0.9109 result to 0.9016 bits per byte. But the dossier never identifies which prevailing technical belief this overturns. It demonstrates a method before it demonstrates a disputed truth.

The strongest fact is the founding structure. Karan Brar and Thomas Boser worked together at Reducto on ML, post-training, and platform systems before forming hiloop; both now publicly identify with the company, and the difficult core system appears to have been built in-house. Their 4,188-experiment campaign is evidence of execution, not résumé theater. Missing ownership, salary, and the duration of their working relationship prevent a complete judgment, but this is a team assembled around an operating problem it had already encountered—not two strangers assembled around the fashion of autonomous research.

Can this become a monopoly rather than an orchestration feature? Not yet. Forkable compute, snapshottable runs, and unified experiment lineage form a coherent product, but Prime Intellect already reports more than 10,000 beta training jobs, while Runloop and OpenPipe cover adjacent combinations of sandboxes, evaluations, and reinforcement learning. Model vendors and internal platform teams can also absorb these capabilities. The inferred moat—accumulated failed paths, verified outcomes, and researcher decisions—could become proprietary data, but one public campaign and no retention evidence cannot establish switching costs. The company has technical substance inside a market that is visibly converging.

The bear case is therefore severe: hiloop may be an excellent two-person research team building a layer that better-capitalized compute platforms give away. Its published result depends on external models and scarce B200 capacity; request-time secret delivery remains unfinished; and its claimed breadth stretches from post-training through prompts, tools, data, harnesses, and classical ML. Broad applicability is not a beachhead. The opposing case is that the complete AI production stack is becoming valuable in its own right, and the founders have already shown that they can coordinate thousands of experiments and extract a statistically confirmed improvement. But this is not an OpenAI-like exception where a zero-to-one technical event overrides uncertain monopoly economics: hiloop improved a benchmark; it did not create a new technological category.

Distribution and economics remain the weakest link. Founder-led campaigns aimed at teams with measurable optimization objectives are more definite than saying “enterprise sales,” and customer-cloud deployment fits buyers unwilling to surrender code or data. Yet Erdos Miller’s unexplained logo, seven YC Launch votes, no published pricing, and no revenue leave the sales cycle, implementation burden, margins, and repeat behavior unknown. Managed campaigns, hosted compute, and enterprise deployments are three possible businesses, not one proven revenue engine. My 36-month base case is that hiloop narrows into coding-agent experimentation or is absorbed by a compute platform before exceeding $3 million ARR.

I would investigate, not invest today. The verifiable flip condition is three unaffiliated customers each paying at least $100,000 annually and expanding into a second campaign within six months while retaining their experiment history in hiloop. That would simultaneously identify the beachhead, validate distribution economics, and show that lineage is becoming a monopoly mechanism rather than a feature. Until then: capable founders, real technical proof, uncertain secret, commodity danger.
