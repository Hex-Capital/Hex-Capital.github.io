# Altair Health -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** Altair targets independent practices that repeatedly work through eligibility, authorizations, claims, and denials. The memo documents a direct demo funnel and separate onboarding guides for new practices and those switching billers. Altair describes connecting to the practice’s existing EHR and clearinghouse, checking claim actions against payer rules, and sending exceptions to in-house billers. The growth scenario assumes that the same sales and setup process can get each practice live and that practices can then route more billing work through Altair. Paid activation and account expansion remain unverified. (The Idea)

The clearest early signal is from the predecessor co-pilot. The CEO reported that a billing team at an unnamed Arkansas behavioral-health clinic prechecked hundreds of claims and attributed millions of dollars in avoided receivables delays to those checks. That reported use is an early signal of workflow fit. The benefit estimate is unverified, and payment for the current managed-billing product and continued use across billing cycles are unknown. (Market & Traction; Key Facts)

If practices in another reimbursement market have the same recurring job, Altair could reuse its claim-checking engine. That scenario would require validating a different set of payer rules, any local EHR and claims interfaces, and patient-data requirements. Altair reports tracking 3,400+ payers and reusing payer-specific resolutions. The economic upside depends on that reuse reducing exception work as claim volume grows. The relevant measure is fees collected after incremental sales effort, integration and policy work, and biller time. The memo leaves those costs and the realized fee basis unknown. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Altair could scale like a labor-intensive billing service. Each new practice may require substantial integration work, each new reimbursement market may require rebuilding payer coverage, and each additional batch of claims may add biller hours as exceptions arise. The product’s dependence on payer-specific rules and human exception handling makes this a concrete risk. The Pass gate remains open because the memo leaves actual rollout effort, exception costs, and cross-market portability unmeasured. A single paid rollout can materially change the case at this stage. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent practice outside the U.S. come through Altair’s standard demo funnel, pay for the current product, and run a live billing cycle using the same core workflow? A buyer-funded rollout that records setup and policy work and shows positive contribution on live claims after biller time would move the action to **Reach out**. Evidence that each market requires a core rebuild, or that biller hours rise proportionally with claim volume without a supported way to reduce them, would move it to **Pass**. An inconclusive rollout would leave it at **Watch**.
