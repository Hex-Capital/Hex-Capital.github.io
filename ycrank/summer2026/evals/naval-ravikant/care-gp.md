# Care GP -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

400+ clinic operators make Care GP a code-leveraged workflow engine with organic pull, but not a network: 3 million processed documents validate utility while each clinic remains economically independent of the next. Care GP’s signature is high permissionless-code potential paired with low compounding user-added value. Code scales. Deployments, compliance, and integrations do not. That architecture can produce an excellent healthcare-software business without producing the 10,000x geometry I seek.

Twitter is the high rung because every new post distributed the product, enriched the network, and replicated at zero marginal cost. Care GP shares part of that mechanism: Care GP reports minimal outbound selling, 175% monthly usage growth for five months, and software agents capable of processing documents without proportional labor. Care GP diverges where the analogy becomes load-bearing: onboarding requests a Best Practice Premier site ID, plans are contact-priced, and every clinic carries integration, privacy, and accuracy obligations. Organic demand raises Care GP above ordinary enterprise SaaS, but gatekeepers remain inside the machine.

Clearview AI’s low-rung network mechanism was a technically rare product whose next customer did little to improve the prior customer’s experience. Care GP has the same structural weakness without Clearview’s extreme technical rarity: clinic 401 does not bring clinic 400 more referrals, counterparties, or workflow utility. Care GP may learn from corrections, but the dossier does not establish exclusive data, and a 5%–20% human edit rate suggests the learning loop remains operational rather than defensible. Perplexity is the relevant Known Exception, but Care GP does not yet combine Perplexity-level founder technical depth, permissionless global distribution, and pre-consensus category timing. The network waiver does not apply.

Melvin Chen interviewed more than 150 healthcare providers after working as a healthcare-technology investor, operator, and commercial lead. Over 18 months, that specific knowledge can compound inside Care GP through workflow selection: documents lead to calls, billing, and adjacent administrative agents, while 3 million processed documents reveal where clinics actually fail. The limitation is that Care GP’s founder evidence remains learned proximity rather than non-trainable obsession—no clinical-practice history, public repository, or previously scaled healthcare product is named. Care GP has founder-market fit; Care GP has not yet shown founder-product inevitability.

$187.7 billion of global AI-healthcare spending is not Care GP’s market; Australian GP administration is. Care GP earns pivot-room through Samantha, Veronica, billing, pathology, referrals, and specialist communications, but regulation and practice-management integrations make each geographic expansion a partial rebuild. Care GP would move up a tier if the same agent infrastructure could enter hospitals, pharmacies, and international primary care through two or more practice systems without proportional compliance and implementation headcount. Until then, Care GP occupies a large problem inside a narrower operating corridor.

AngelList is the single-anchor parallel for the founder test: long-term partnership confidence came from demonstrated building, clean counterparties, and a founder using the product’s underlying system personally. Melvin Chen brings intelligence and energy—Oxford valedictorian, two prior co-founder roles, 18,000 LinkedIn followers, and Care GP’s reported profitability—but Care GP provides no outcomes for Avigo or StarLake, no co-founder history, and little evidence on how Chen treated prior partners. Silence on integrity is neutral. Care GP earns evidence for shipping, not yet evidence for a ten-year relationship.

3 million processed documents and 85% pilot conversion point to a specific 36-month outcome: by July 2029, Care GP reaches A$10–20 million ARR and is acquired by Best Practice Software rather than becoming a global healthcare network. Care GP’s code leverage should expand gross output faster than headcount, while Care GP’s Best Practice dependency and Australian compliance assets make vertical integration more probable than permissionless global escape. A valuable tool can compound cash flows without compounding users.

85% pilot conversion, zero reported churn, profitability, and 20% weekly growth form the strongest case that my forecast is too optimistic about durability: Heidi, Lyrebird, or Best Practice could bundle overlapping automation, while Care GP’s contact-priced enterprise motion, 5%–20% edit rate, and platform dependency turn software leverage back into sales, review, and regulatory labor. Care GP nevertheless matches one important unstated pattern—organic distribution before a large outbound organization—and 400+ operators show demand beyond a controlled pilot. Care GP misses the builder-product and network patterns, and the red flags of incumbent permission and pedigree without a public shipped artifact still bite. I would flip from Neutral to Invest if Care GP demonstrates that more than 30% of new clinics arrive through self-serve or customer referrals while pooled learning drives the human edit rate below 2% across at least two independent practice-management systems.
