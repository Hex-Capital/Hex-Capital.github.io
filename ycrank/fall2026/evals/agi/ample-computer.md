# Ample Computer -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Ample could make complex web apps economical for small teams that previously went without them because configuring and maintaining cloud services took too much engineering time. In Ample’s own salon-booking test, the conventional setup required nearly 10 services. Ample says it can infer dependencies from source code, provision those services, and return a live URL. The test identifies a concrete source of labor, but the memo gives no before-and-after measure of labor or full cost per app that launches and stays operational, including failed deployments, manual fixes, and ongoing support. The market opening remains an inference. (*The Idea*)

Two customer testimonials report real activity on deployed apps, including 55 order or checkout submissions for SA Collection and 30 registrations for a business fair. These are signals of use. The memo does not establish that the builders would otherwise have gone without the apps or that they paid or deployed again. Ample’s launch report of more than 4,000 users adds an adoption signal. The founders’ prior work on Shopify App Store infrastructure and developer tooling, together with Ample’s live product and verified Claude connector, supports their ability to ship and put the product in builders’ hands. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *The Idea*)

The expansion unit is an additional live app. **Scenario assumption:** If a Pro subscriber uses all 10 app slots, the $20 monthly subscription yields $2 per app before compute, storage, email, and support costs. The memo gives no realized utilization or delivery costs, so contribution at that workload is unknown. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Ample may make deployment easier for builders who could already ship. With equivalent coding models, buyers would still need reliable provisioning and hosting, but the memo gives little reason they would pay Ample specifically. Railway and Render already expose infrastructure tools to external agents. Ample’s 2,144 published deployment recipes show workload coverage, while comparative success rates and buyer preference under functional parity remain untested. The memo establishes neither equivalent end-to-end substitutes nor a durable Ample advantage. Both Path B gates remain open for testing, which supports Watch. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Ample win paid repeat deployments from independent small teams that previously shelved comparable apps because deployment took too much engineering time? In a seed-stage trial, give those teams the same coding model on Ample and the closest available alternative. Measure hands-on labor and full delivery cost through stable operation. Move to Reach out if the savings make previously shelved apps viable, teams choose Ample again at a price that covers delivery, and the comparison shows what they pay Ample for. If the savings fail to open the use case or teams get the same outcome and readily switch, close Path B and Pass.
