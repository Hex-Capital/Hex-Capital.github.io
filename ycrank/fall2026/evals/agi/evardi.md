# Evardi -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path A, Scarce complements, is the strongest fit. **Scenario assumption:** If AI-driven electricity demand strains distribution feeders, utilities could pay for a qualified way to identify transformer headroom and approve safe load transfers. Evardi combines utility data into a transformer-level model and checks proposed switching steps against local operating limits. In company-reported simulations on public data, it caught 60% of errors that passed manual review and reduced unsafe switching actions by 42% in storm drills. Those results support the checking mechanism. The amount of usable capacity it could unlock in the field remains unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Under model parity, Evardi’s potential advantage is a validated utility-specific network model and switching workflow that takes time to qualify. The memo establishes no exclusive control or demonstrated switching costs. Manual checks show an existing workaround, and Evardi reports deployments at utilities serving 10M+ customers, with payment, buyer commitments, and repeat use unverified. The founders’ utility and energy operations experience, earlier mini-grid pilots, and current simulation work support seed-stage execution. Each utility deployment would need data integration, mapping validation, and operator sign-off. Repeat deployment cost and operator workload remain unmeasured. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

SMUD reports deploying meter-based transformer mapping and load monitoring alongside an AspenTech integration for coordinated switching and restoration. That is a credible route around Evardi. The memo contains no comparison showing whether the incumbent setup meets the same operating requirements at comparable cost and effort. The substitution gate remains unresolved, which supports Watch. (Defensibility, Key Risks)
