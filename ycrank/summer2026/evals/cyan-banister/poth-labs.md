# Poth Labs -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Matthew Wong spent his Palantir years integrating enterprise data, while Poth Labs now resolves identities across CRM, support, call, and behavioral systems; Poth Labs is founder-adjacent to a lived problem but trapped inside its lowest-scoring form: an abstract, crowded AI-SaaS product no ordinary person can touch. That pairing makes Poth Labs a capable deployment founder’s knowledge-graph wedge, not yet a magically weird object I would chase down a hallway to fund.

Niantic is the high rung for founder-problem continuity: John Hanke spent a decade building location systems before Niantic made the world playable. Wong’s Palantir deployment work and Mojmír Horváth’s Tietoevry data-science experience place Poth Labs above the credential-only AI-agent archetype; they have actually handled the integration terrain. Poth Labs diverges because the dossier supplies no specific customer-data failure Wong personally suffered, no prior product he scaled in this domain, and no evidence that Poth Labs existed in his bones before it existed in a market map. This is adjacency, not autobiography: 20/32.

The consensus YC AI-agent darling is the low rung because the product is software abstraction whose value exists mainly in a founder’s testimony. That failure mechanism appears in Poth Labs: a normal human cannot ride a customer-specific knowledge graph, photograph entity resolution, or feel a cited contradiction the way I could play Niantic or watch a Flock Safety camera work. Five named users—Inkle, Sol, Composio, UnCircuit, and Illume—make Poth Labs more observable than vaporware, but they do not make Poth Labs tactile. The HQ Trivia exception does not apply because no explosive consumer behavior overrides the abstraction: 4/13.

Poth Labs names Matthew Wong as CEO and Mojmír Horváth as CTO, which is better than shared titles, but the dossier provides no founder-history, equity split, operating agreement, or documented tiebreaker. Over 18 months, integration disputes will compound because Poth Labs must simultaneously choose which connectors to build, how aggressively to infer identities, and whether interviews or the knowledge graph owns the roadmap. Poth Labs has already pivoted after customers used the graph more than the survey layer; one clear CEO could turn that responsiveness into speed, while an implicit 50/50 partnership could turn the next signal into a referendum. I apply most of the governance default: 8/16.

Enterpret has raised $25.1 million, Dovetail $71 million, Chattermill $34.8 million, and Unwrap $12 million, so Poth Labs is entering a funded category rather than an aesthetically rejected one. Poth Labs would flip my non-consensus score into invest territory if credible investors were passing because adaptive interviews felt invasive, identity resolution appeared technically impossible, or the round remained conspicuously underpriced despite repeat usage. The dossier instead shows several YC peers adopting Poth Labs and a familiar AI-plus-enterprise-data story. There may be a rebuttal here, but I have not heard the weird objection yet: 10/24.

Crusoe needed physical infrastructure, commodity exposure, and social license on North Dakota well pads; Poth Labs needs Supabase, PostHog, Netlify, Calendly, and permission to consolidate sensitive customer records. Poth Labs does touch an unsexy seam—data governance and erroneous entity resolution can poison causal conclusions—but its privacy policy says customer data is neither sold nor used for model training, and nothing indicates a regulatory license, hardware moat, or capital-intensive technical barrier. Poth Labs is enterprise plumbing with procurement friction, not three uninvestable things stapled together: 4/15.

Five named users and the graph-over-survey pivot lead me to predict that within 36 months Poth Labs will abandon broad “why users behave” research, specialize in customer-identity resolution for B2B SaaS, and reach $1 million–$3 million ARR without becoming the category leader. Poth Labs has already shown the mechanism: customers pulled hardest on the underlying graph, while Enterpret, Dovetail, Chattermill, and Unwrap can copy the interview layer. The useful future is narrower than the story—the infrastructure hidden inside the consumer-research wrapper.

Three agreed PothAI pilots, five named Poth Labs users, and several Summer 2026 YC adopters form the strongest bull case: customers—not a memo—caused Poth Labs to expose the knowledge graph, and that is the kind of environmental signal I trust. DeepMind is the Known Exception that stings here: Horváth’s 4.5-billion-parameter multimodal research and Wong’s geophysics-plus-Palantir path could reveal polymaths whose cross-domain compounding matters more than outsider biography. Poth Labs still loses today because no revenue, repeat-usage frequency, founder tiebreaker, or proprietary cross-customer learning establishes that the graph compounds faster than funded competitors can reproduce it. Ten paying customers retaining above 90% after twelve months, with documented improvement in identity-resolution accuracy across deployments, would flip my verdict to Invest.
