# TryNearby -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Local-discovery feeds now give restaurants a new acquisition channel, restaurants buy recurring creator coverage at $399 per month, and the controlling variable is whether TryNearby can preserve restaurant retention as it recreates marketplace density city by city. The current evidence clears that first gate: 120+ paying restaurants after ten months, 37% month-over-month growth, over 90% monthly retention, and roughly half of new customers arriving through owner referrals. Those numbers indicate product-market pull, not merely creator enthusiasm. The unresolved issue is whether retention reflects attributable restaurant economics or an unusually strong Southern California launch network.

The technology shift is real but incompletely measured. TikTok’s February 2026 Local Feed and Instagram’s August 2025 Map make geographically relevant creator content more valuable. TryNearby’s agents then coordinate onboarding, invitations, bookings, reminders, rescheduling, and restaurant updates across more than 10,000 monthly texts. This can replace owner labor and agency coordination, but the dossier does not quantify contribution margin, creator compensation, operational intervention, or customer-acquisition cost. A $399 subscription only produces attractive software economics if automation absorbs most campaign complexity.

The strongest evidence is demand before polish. Restaurants are paying approximately $48,000 in aggregate monthly subscription revenue, creators have produced 2,500+ videos and roughly 35 million views, and restaurant owners reportedly refer about half of new customers. The reported revenue increases—nearly 100% for one restaurant and 50% for another—are promising but company-reported and lack an independent attribution method. Retention and referrals carry more weight than those testimonials because they represent customers repeatedly choosing the product with their own money.

This is also more than a thin content generator. The product retains creator preferences, travel constraints, availability, and rejection history while taking responsibility for recurring operational actions. Better models should improve coordination rather than erase the workflow. The concern is reproducibility: Hyper and Tablevert already advertise automated restaurant-creator campaigns, and no structural barrier prevents a funded competitor from rebuilding the software. TryNearby’s defensibility therefore depends on local liquidity and booking reliability becoming meaningfully better with density—not on calling interaction data proprietary.

The team has unusually relevant second-time learning. Yousef Abdelfattah helped build FaZe Clan from age 14 through its public listing; Obaida Albaroudi reportedly helped grow a prior consumer application from $360,000 to $2 million ARR; and Albaroudi and Ahmad Ibrahim previously built creator-campaign infrastructure together, including systems tracking more than 10 billion views. This is credible founder-market fit, although the dossier documents experience rather than costly commitment or desperation.

The bear case is that TryNearby is a geographically fragmented, service-heavy marketplace exposed to TikTok and Instagram, with low subscription revenue per restaurant, uncertain attribution, and no demonstrated software-like margin expansion. None of the known talent, frontier-research, or physical-economics exceptions rescues that structure. I still invest because paying retention, referral-driven distribution, and an already-operated workflow provide stronger pre-seed evidence than the market-size narrative. I would flip to pass if a second-city cohort of at least 50 restaurants cannot sustain 85% monthly logo retention for six consecutive months without founder-led selling.
