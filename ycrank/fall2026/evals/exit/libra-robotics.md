# Libra Robotics -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a solar equipment supplier is Libra’s strongest supported exit route. Libra gave a live demonstration at FTC Solar’s Robo Day and reports compatibility with its 1P Pioneer tracker. A supplier such as FTC Solar could have a reason to own Libra’s installation technology if it consistently lowers installation costs or speeds up projects across customer sites. The demonstration establishes a point of contact. The economics of ownership remain an inference. (Market & Traction, Defensibility)

Libra reports signed solar installation agreements and pilots booked through March 2027. That gives it a plausible window to demonstrate repeat paid installations across sites, with customer-verified costs and throughput and measured crew and equipment requirements. Libra reports a 10× reduction in task-to-deployment time, with independent cross-site performance, agreement values, and capital needs still unverified. Ownership of the relevant IP and any customer consents required for a sale are also unverified. The memo supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity. The acquisition cases below are illustrative price sensitivities. Both assume a full cash sale within eight years, $5 million in combined net debt and closing adjustments, and an eligible holder receiving 3% of the remaining equity proceeds pro rata with no senior preference ahead of it.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M leaves $45M in distributable equity. | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $5M leaves $95M in distributable equity. At 2% ownership after further dilution, the holder would receive $1.90M (3.8×). | $2.85M (5.7×) at 3% |
| Prolonged private | The company reinvests available cash through year eight, with no funded payout or eligible share purchase. | $0 (0×) |

The reported $56 million in potential deployment value comes from letters of intent. The memo provides no realized revenue, measured customer savings, or acquisition pricing that would support the strong sale case. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A supplier could obtain installation automation through a commercial partnership. Sunstall has worked with both Libra and Cosmic, and competitors offer overlapping capabilities. If each new site still requires substantial engineering, ownership could add field costs without giving the buyer a durable advantage. That is a credible reason for a buyer to decline or pay less, but the memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a price ceiling that forces a Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would the savings from repeat paid solar installations give a tracker supplier enough reason to buy Libra at a price that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Customer-verified results across multiple sites, paired with a supplier’s quantified case for owning the technology, would move the action to Reach out if they support roughly $88.3 million or more in enterprise value under the modeled deductions and ownership.
