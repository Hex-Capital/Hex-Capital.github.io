# The Subvocal Company -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path A, product-led adoption, is the strongest fit. Frequent dictation users have a recurring reason to switch to typing when speaking aloud would disturb others. The memo documents a prototype showcase and a direct U.S. reservation flow. The company reported roughly 750 reservations backed by about $150,000 in refundable deposits. That is an early signal of willingness to put money down. Full-price conversion, activation, and recurring use remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

If production glasses reliably save time in everyday dictation, demos and wearer referrals could bring in the next thousand buyers through the same web checkout. Those buyers would need to get useful results without founder help. Frequent users could then pay for the planned unlimited tier. A second English-speaking market is a plausible test of the same product and buyer workflow. It would still require checking recognition across new wearers and accents, local device and privacy requirements, shipping, and returns. The memo documents U.S. reservations only. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

At the stated $499 early-bird price, each additional sale would need to cover hardware, fulfillment, expected returns, acquisition, and any hands-on setup or support. Inventory also ties up cash ahead of shipment. More dictation adds cloud decoding costs, which the planned roughly $20 monthly tier could help cover if frequent users subscribe. The memo provides neither measured per-unit costs nor paid usage economics. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

A compelling demo may drive refundable reservations even if the shipping product saves too little time to displace typing. The company reported 85–92% showcase accuracy and was still working on calibration across wearers, sessions, and devices. Production changes can alter the signal the model receives. If those issues turn setup into hands-on calibration and everyday use into repeated correction, support costs could rise with each sale while usage fades. That risk warrants Watch. The structural Pass gate would require evidence that these costs persist at scale without a supported way to reduce them. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of frequent dictation users who buy production glasses at the stated early-bird price set them up without founder help and keep using them for routine work over their first month? Cohort records showing completed purchases, unassisted setup, and recurring use would move the action to Reach out. If use fades or setup still requires substantial help, keep watching for a production revision that changes that result.
