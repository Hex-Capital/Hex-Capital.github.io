# Induction Labs -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Photon-1 is a specific-knowledge machine without a network. Induction Labs has compressed 575 million training frames into a 106B-A5B model, yet Induction Labs has no mechanism by which the next developer automatically improves the product for the previous developer. The highest signal is Jonathan Li’s Cohere reasoning and reinforcement-learning work; the lowest is user-added compounding. This is a frontier model laboratory, not yet a platform. I would invest, but the check buys technical optionality rather than an established flywheel.

**Replit** is the high rung for specific knowledge: Amjad Masad spent years turning a personal obsession with code environments into infrastructure used by other builders. Induction Labs approaches that rung because Jonathan Li published first-author ACL research, worked on synthetic data and RL infrastructure at Cohere, and then built a 2-billion-video index rather than a deck about building one. Induction Labs diverges because David Li is still a Waterloo student and Photon-1’s reported advantage over Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite remains internally measured. Specific knowledge is visible; repeatable superiority is not yet independently visible.

**Perplexity** fails the network test in the narrow sense: one searcher does not automatically make the next searcher’s product more valuable. Induction Labs has the same missing mechanism without Perplexity’s demonstrated product distribution. An API could gather reinforcement-learning rollouts, but Induction Labs has no public access program, customer feedback loop, or production deployment showing that usage becomes proprietary learning. The Perplexity known exception applies partially because deep AI knowledge and code-native delivery can waive the network requirement. It does not erase the requirement; it merely prevents an automatic pass.

Photon-1 can replicate at software margins, but training it required roughly 30,000 H200 GPU-hours. Over the next 18 months, Induction Labs compounds if the 100-times compression encoder turns every new screen-recording domain into a cheap model extension and developers can adopt Photon-1 through an open API. Induction Labs decays into capital leverage if every improvement requires another private corpus, larger cluster, and researcher hiring cycle. The unstated builder-tool pattern is present—Induction Labs is building for agent developers—but the dossier’s absence of open-source adoption, pricing, or public sign-up keeps code leverage in the product rather than the distribution.

The AI-agents market is projected to grow from $8 billion in 2025 to $48.3 billion by 2030, giving Induction Labs room to move from computer use into robotics observation, simulation, or general action models. Induction Labs would fall a full tier on market pivot-room if Photon-1’s encoder only worked on desktop screen recordings and could not transfer beyond that narrow corpus. The single pretraining run already models desktops, checkers, and billiard physics, so the evidence points toward a general observational-learning substrate rather than vertical computer automation.

Jonathan Li’s prior Cohere work and the brothers’ claim that they are “building a company together again” place Induction Labs nearer **Stack Overflow** than the pedigree-only founder pattern. Stack Overflow was built by developers who had already developed public judgment about developer problems; Induction Labs has an ACL 2023 paper and a shipped 106B-A5B artifact, but little public evidence about how Jonathan and David Li handle disagreement, employees, or adversity. Induction Labs passes intelligence and energy. Integrity remains at the absent-evidence default: quiet is not guilt, but a ten-year partnership cannot be inferred from 812 LinkedIn followers.

Photon-1’s 575-million-frame corpus points to a falsifiable outcome: by July 2029, Induction Labs will have pivoted from a standalone “imagination model” into consumption-priced computer-use model infrastructure and reached $10 million to $30 million in ARR. Induction Labs gets there by selling compressed observational pretraining and action fine-tuning to agent developers who cannot reproduce the 2-billion-video indexing pipeline. The model is not the enduring product. The learning engine is.

World Labs has raised $1.23 billion, General Intuition $454 million, and Runway $860 million—the bear mechanism is capitalized commoditization. Induction Labs could be a clever research result trapped between hyperscalers with proprietary interaction data and startups able to outspend Induction Labs on compute and talent; internal Gemini comparisons, undisclosed video licensing, and the unexplained shift from Axiom 1 to Photon-1 sharpen that red flag. The evidence that wins today is a working sparse model reportedly trained with 30-times less compute and served at three-times lower cost, built by a founder with direct RL experience. Asymmetric math tolerates technical risk at pre-seed; it does not tolerate permanent laboratory status. If Induction Labs has no external developer running Photon-1 in production by July 2027, I flip to Pass.
