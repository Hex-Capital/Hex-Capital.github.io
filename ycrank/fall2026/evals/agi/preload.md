# Preload -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Preload has a plausible building-blocks path in training data for contact-sensitive robot manipulation, including gripping and knot tying. Its demonstrated rig synchronizes first-person video, glove contact readings, and forearm EMG. Preload reports that EMG distinguished light from heavy handling in a preliminary test. If effort labels materially improve policy success on physical robots, wider deployment of dexterous robots could increase demand for calibrated demonstrations across tasks and operators. Glove fit and calibration could slow the supply of usable recordings. Scarcity remains speculative because the memo gives no buyer evidence of difficulty sourcing equivalent data and no controlled policy comparison on physical robots. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The industry-enabled path scores zero. The memo describes a training-data service and identifies no previously uneconomic use that AI makes affordable for a distinct customer segment. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Preload’s EMG and sensor synchronization know-how provides an early basis for value capture. Its capture prototype, the ProHand demonstration, and the founders’ biosignal and recording-hardware experience support seed-stage execution. Customer demand remains at the default because the memo shows outreach and a demonstration but no buyer trial, purchase commitment, or paid deployment. Scale economics also remains at the default because collection price, usable recording yield, and integration cost are unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Video has already transferred to robot hardware in the research described in the memo, and competing capture stacks already offer synchronized touch or force data. A policy trained on those inputs could match Preload’s results at lower collection cost if EMG adds little across operators. That unresolved comparison keeps the building-blocks path at Watch. Evidence that readily available inputs meet the same requirements would trigger Pass. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

At the same data-collection budget, does adding Preload’s EMG channel to video and tactile data let a buyer’s policy clear its deployment threshold on a contact-sensitive task that the baseline misses? A buyer-run comparison on physical robots using demonstrations from multiple operators would answer it. A repeatable threshold-crossing gain would move the action to Reach out. Equivalent performance from the baseline would move it to Pass. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
