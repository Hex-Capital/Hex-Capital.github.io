# OpenHack -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with engineering and application-security teams that can grant repository access, OpenHack has a credible route to global demand. Reviewing pull requests and prioritizing findings as software ships are recurring jobs that the same repository-based product and CLI could serve across markets. Private-source approval can narrow access for some buyers. The memo identifies no need to rebuild the core workflow by country. *(The Idea, Key Risks)*

The listed self-serve signup and open-source CLI give developers a way to discover and try OpenHack independently. The published entry tier is free and Enterprise pricing is custom, with founder-led demos and onboarding. A self-serve path from trial to paid managed use remains unverified. Repeated pull-request reviews and customer-specific application maps could support continued use. Current pricing leaves it unclear whether retained accounts pay more as they add repositories or scan more often. *(The Idea, Defensibility, Key Facts)*

OpenHack named six users at its November launch and reported that Papermark fixed a finding. The scanner’s 444 GitHub stars document developer interest. The memo gives no realized revenue, paying-customer count, or confirmation of pre-revenue status, so initial traction uses the rubric default. Cost economics also uses the default. For a paid project, the contribution bridge would subtract model inference, scan infrastructure, and attributable onboarding and support labor from the project’s revenue. The free allowance brings in $0 directly, and the memo gives neither paid rates nor workload costs to estimate contribution on a representative paid project. *(Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Facts)*

### Countercase

The CLI could attract developers while paid managed scanning remains a founder-led enterprise sale. Buyers must approve temporary access to private source code, and the company’s own benchmark found 277 of 761 planted vulnerabilities. If buyers require hands-on onboarding or substantial manual review to trust the results, labor could rise with each paid deployment while recurring scans add inference cost. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo does not establish that those costs or human interventions are unavoidable for ordinary paid buyers. *(The Idea, Key Risks)*

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of ordinary developer teams move from self-serve signup to recurring paid managed scans with positive incremental contribution and minimal human help? Cohort records showing independent activation, repeat billing, and positive contribution after scan infrastructure, inference, onboarding, and support would move the action to Reach out.
