# Verdun -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Verdun identifies a concrete failure: nuisance alarms can leave a real threat waiting more than 20 minutes for human review. What I want next is the incident where Tai and Hoang encountered that failure, built around it, and changed the result. The dossier supplies an architecture, but no dated sequence connecting an obstacle to a shipped workaround. That leaves my most important founder test unanswered. Experience tells me where to look for resourcefulness; it cannot substitute for seeing it.

These founders have unusually good reasons to understand the problem. Tai built Actuate, and Hoang built its first gun-detection model. They have worked together before. Actuate’s reported 110,000 cameras across more than 15,000 sites establish exposure to physical security, though they establish no adoption for Verdun ([leadership](https://verdun.ai/leadership)). This is the strongest part of the case. Site protocols, unreliable footage, operator escalation, and video-ingestion costs are precisely the unpleasant details that can conceal a useful business. Their prior work makes this opportunity more credible than an idea discovered by browsing market reports.

The technical description earns some credit. A cascade of cheaper filters, multimodal reasoning, temporal analysis, and site context is a specific approach, and Tai recognizes that inference costs can destroy the economics ([engineering role](https://verdun.ai/careers/evals-engineer), [October 2026 essay](https://verdun.ai/essays/traditional-video-monitoring-is-badly-broken)). But a described system receives less credit than a demonstrated one. There is no measured detection performance, review latency, or operating cost. Hakimo already offers contextual verification and response, so those features alone do not establish an advantage. My pattern of backing concrete technical proof or a difficult proprietary substrate does not yet supply affirmative evidence here.

The strongest argument against passing is that this team may know exactly which customers the existing services disappoint. Prior operating experience and an established working relationship could let Verdun recruit a small group of desperate property operators and install the service personally. That would be a good beginning. The dossier, however, identifies no returning users, paying customers, or customer-directed product changes. YC admission, two vacancies, and Charlie Warren’s referral solicitation do not answer who needs the crude product now. The referral effort also does not establish the red flag of dependence on a large partner. Likewise, discontinuing Tai’s consumer project shows a change of direction, but supplies no evidence of a stable goal and a route revised after learning.

I would pass with my own money on this evidence. This is missing proof, rather than evidence that the founders are passive or the product unwanted. The known exceptions to my usual screens depend on a working prototype, unmistakable user pull, or validated technical results; none is established for Verdun. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: a dated deployment record showing a named property operator’s specific monitoring failure, the founders’ shipped correction, and that operator repeatedly using the corrected service. That would connect their considerable experience to the behavior I am actually trying to fund.
