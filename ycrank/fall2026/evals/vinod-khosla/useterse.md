# Terse -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Terse could become infrastructure on which agents retain state and coordinate work. The consequence worth financing would be making reliable autonomous work dramatically cheaper. The dossier establishes a working infrastructure primitive, but it does not quantify that consequence: no spending baseline, affected workload population, or mechanism connecting adoption to an order-of-magnitude improvement. Cloudflare’s $2,167.9 million of company-wide revenue is not the addressable spending pool for durable actors. I cannot price the upside of this option by borrowing the incumbent’s entire business.

The strongest argument for Terse is the team’s ability to build and reach developers. Thomas Karatzas spent seven years on Siri and Apple Intelligence, previously built an acquired company, and published RustGPT, which has 3.2K stars. Olivier Simard-Morissette brings backend engineering experience from Pigeon, Squarespace, and The New York Times. Together they have released a self-hostable runtime with SQLite-backed actors and generated Python and TypeScript clients. That is substantive execution for two people. The product’s 256 stars, 19 forks, and 53-point Show HN launch provide initial developer attention. They do not yet establish the committed technical community that can turn an open-source wedge into a platform. MIT licensing itself is no objection; repeat dependence on the product is the evidence I want.

The technical question is whether Terse removes a binding constraint that competing systems cannot remove economically. Serial request handling and actor-owned storage address a real coordination problem. But Cloudflare, Rivet, and Restate already supply overlapping primitives. Self-hosting distinguishes Terse from Cloudflare; Rivet also supports it. Decorated persistent fields distinguish the programming interface, but the dossier supplies no measured throughput, reliability, or cost discontinuity. I give the missing-benchmark default rather than assume the implementation lacks undisclosed technical depth. Equally, I will not award breakthrough credit for resemblance to infrastructure that might become important.

Terse’s October 26 release makes the next engineering question concrete: messages can arrive out of order, and sending immediately before closing can drop a message. The documented persistence rules also leave nested mutations and interleaving behavior for developers to manage. These limitations are not automatically fatal; their importance depends on the guarantees applications require. The missing evidence is a decisive test of those guarantees, with a threshold, cost, and response to failure. Publishing limitations is useful transparency, but it does not establish risk removed per dollar. Likewise, the earlier workflow-platform description establishes a product change without establishing what evidence caused the founders to revise their beliefs or whom they recruited to close a weakness.

I pass on the current evidence. Terse fits the broader pattern of a capable software team building an execution-driven platform, which deserves consideration even without scientific novelty. But the exceptions require something stronger: an independently established enormous value pool, exceptional evidence of a transformative trajectory, or a quantified mission that justifies buying extreme uncertainty. None is demonstrated here. The prior acquisition does not imply an exit-driven agenda, and there is no basis to call this a transformer wrapper. The objection is specific: neither extraordinary consequence nor extraordinary capability is established. Hosting revenue remains an inference, and no operating-cost model establishes economic superiority. One verifiable result would reopen my investment decision: an independently reproducible study across three paying production deployments showing at least 10x lower total operating cost than the best applicable Cloudflare or Rivet configuration, at matched durability and delivery guarantees.
