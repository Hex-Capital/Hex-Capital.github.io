# Nines -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition by a rack integrator or data center infrastructure supplier. Nines says it is developing robots to patch and verify fiber during GPU rack commissioning. If the robots reliably reduce technician hours and shorten commissioning across rack formats, an integrator could have a reason to own the technology and deploy it across customer sites. The memo documents a public launch and reports a $125,000 YC seed entry. Nines identifies its website inspection sequence as a simulation, and the memo gives no paid deployment, measured savings, or buyer valuation evidence. The acquisition rationale is plausible, but the price a buyer could justify remains unknown. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

A paid production pilot across multiple rack layouts is a concrete next milestone. The CTO’s robotics and production inference experience provides some support for reaching it. The memo leaves the pilot budget, runway, and integration effort unquantified. It also leaves transaction rights, consents, and holder eligibility unverified and establishes no independent liquidity route. (*Founders & Team*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale cases below are illustrative. Both assume $5 million in combined net debt and deal adjustments, an all-cash closing within eight years, and an eligible holder selling the full stake with a pro rata payout and no senior preference reducing it. The memo supplies no transaction terms to verify those assumptions. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

- **Modest sale.** $45 million in enterprise value leaves $40 million in distributable equity and pays the holder $1.2 million in cash, or 2.4×.
- **Strong sale.** $105 million in enterprise value leaves $100 million in distributable equity and pays the holder $3 million in cash, or 6×. At 2% ownership after dilution, the same sale pays $2 million, or 4×.
- **Prolonged private.** If Nines keeps operating and reinvests available cash, with no funded payout or eligible share purchase, the holder realizes $0 within eight years.

### Countercase

A strategic buyer could build the capability internally or source it from another vendor, especially if Nines needs substantial custom integration at each site. Microsoft Research’s transceiver manipulation prototype shows overlapping in-house capability, and Watney reports hyperscaler deployments. The memo documents no moat or field performance advantage for Nines. This weakens a buyer’s reason to pay an acquisition premium, but the memo establishes neither a ready substitute for Nines’ commissioning workflow nor a supported sale range that falls materially short. The Pass gate is therefore untriggered. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production pilot across multiple rack layouts demonstrate savings that would justify a strategic acquisition above the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Operator-verified labor hours, time to commissioning, failed connections, and integration costs would answer it if the net savings, scaled across a plausible buyer’s deployments, support an acquisition range clearing the hurdle after deal adjustments and dilution. That result would move the action to Reach out.
