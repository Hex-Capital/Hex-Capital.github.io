# Letter -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**Building blocks of superintelligence.** Letterpress-1 uses models to turn search and visitor signals into proposed edits for Git-based marketing sites. The memo identifies no scarce input that limits an AI workload, and no mechanism by which Letter would expand supply of that input. The adjacent AI-search market forecast speaks to demand for marketing tools. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

**Industry enabled by superintelligence.** Letter describes a workflow that combines signal monitoring, sandboxed edits, and pull requests with live previews. This could reduce the engineering time spent turning a finding from an SEO tool into a reviewed site change. The memo identifies existing tasks such as sitemap fixes and landing pages, but establishes no buyer or update cadence that was previously uneconomic. Letter’s reported 100-plus customers and thousands of pull requests leave open how many changes shipped and whether any represented work buyers previously could not justify. Both paths score zero, triggering Pass. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Commercial case.** The observable beta launch and earlier product releases support the team’s ability to ship. Letter’s reported brand evaluations and integrated sandbox offer a specific workflow advantage, with switching costs and retention still unverified. The reported customer count supports early interest, while realized revenue and repeat paid usage remain unknown. The published 20% markup on model cost leaves about 17% of the model charge before sandbox, support, and rework costs. The memo does not establish contribution per successfully shipped change. The launch and product design are clearer than the evidence on buyer behavior and delivery economics. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A small team with a Git-based site might be able to ship recurring customer-evidence pages and long-tail fixes that previously sat in its engineering queue. That scenario assumes Letter reduces drafting, integration, and review time enough to make those changes worthwhile at its subscription price. The sandbox, previews, and brand checks give buyers a reason to consider a dedicated product even with access to comparable models, though the memo identifies overlap with general-purpose coding agents and content tools. The workflow makes the scenario plausible, but the evidence does not clear the seed-stage threshold for a newly viable use. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paying team use Letter to repeatedly ship a class of site updates it previously left undone because of cost? A buyer-confirmed before-and-after comparison should count shipped changes and include subscription and usage fees, staff review time, and rework. Repeat paid use at a cost the buyer can sustain, with a meaningful delivery advantage over its SEO tools and a comparably capable general-purpose coding agent, would change the action to Reach out.
