# Shire Intelligence -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Shire Intelligence advertises intelligence starting at $40 a month alongside free POS installation. That makes the capital question concrete: what does my check purchase that restaurant receipts cannot finance? The dossier reports $500,000 from YC in September 2026, but supplies neither the proposed post-money valuation nor competing offers. I cannot establish whether the belief is cheap. It also supplies no proceeds-funded experiment with a metric and deadline. Those are missing underwriting inputs, not evidence that the founders are running an auction or a financing factory.

The founders provide the strongest reason to investigate. Kuperman and Tabaku worked in restaurants together; Tabaku has four years of restaurant experience. Their team also built a camera prototype that reportedly won NexHacks’ general track. That is a modest but concrete bridge between restaurant operations and shipped technical work, beyond degrees and internship titles. It fits my preference for people who can translate between physical operations and software. It does not yet establish that they can deploy and support the system during a chaotic dinner service.

The strongest objection to passing is that Shire may already have the customer relationship through which this product becomes economically useful. Mathews Restaurant provides a named testimonial reporting $65,000 in payment-processing savings. Shire separately claims 14% more monthly guests and seven-minute faster table turns. If an existing POS relationship gives the team trusted access to restaurant workflows, cameras could become an inexpensive extension of a business owners already value. Existing CCTV also offers a plausible way to avoid installing another hardware system. That is a substantially better starting position than an AI demo looking for a buyer.

But the evidence does not yet connect those pieces. Processing savings do not establish that camera intelligence caused a purchase, and the operating improvements lack sample sizes or a documented measurement method. Restaurant footage could become proprietary data, but training rights and a deployed correction loop are undocumented. The disclosed SAM3 and DINOv3 components demonstrate implementation; they do not establish exclusive access. PreciTaste already operates across vision, POS integrations, and staff guidance, so the defensible asset would have to emerge from Shire’s customer relationships and operational learning. A model release alone will not create it.

At $40 a month, the relevant cash metric is contribution per retained restaurant after inference, onboarding, support, and any installation subsidy. Shire supplies no margin, burn, runway, or break-even plan, so I cannot establish whether this round reaches a self-funding business. I am not assuming the economics fail. Nor does the dossier establish equal-founder paralysis: there is a named CEO and no disclosed ownership split. Before making an exception to my financing gates, however, I would want the kind of documented conversion, retention, or cash economics that can outweigh missing terms. The testimonial and prototype do not yet provide that override.

I pass on the evidence available. My one verifiable condition for reopening is a signed, sub-$10-million post-money financing plan funding a dated deployment test whose pass condition is positive contribution from retained, paying camera customers after all deployment and support costs. That would connect the price, the experiment, and the operating business. Today, the founders’ bridge between restaurants and engineering is more convincing than the case for investing my money in this particular round.
