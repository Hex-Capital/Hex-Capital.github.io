# Hubble -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Prabha Dublish launched healthcare products at Grow Therapy, and Aaron Leon built Amazon One Medical infrastructure serving 1M+ patients. Hubble is a domain-lived founder bet wrapped in an almost invisible product: strongest where biography meets the problem, weakest where I want to touch the artifact myself. Hubble feels less like a shiny AI company than plumbing installed by people who have already crawled beneath the hospital floorboards. I would invest, but the lack of tactile pull and the oversubscribed round keep this from becoming an automatic bubbe check.

Niantic is the high rung because John Hanke spent a decade inside maps and location before Pokémon Go materialized; the product was downstream of the biography. Hubble shares that mechanism: Dublish was Grow Therapy’s first product hire, while Leon built EHR and AI infrastructure at One Medical and led 50+ engineers. Hubble diverges because neither founder has yet demonstrated Hanke-like years of singular obsession or a comparable product shipped independently at scale. They are of the place, not visiting it—but I have seen their résumés under fluorescent light, not their Wi-Fi name at the Four Seasons.

DeepMind’s abstraction would normally fail my low-rung physical-world test; polymath founder compounding supplied the exceptional override. Hubble offers no equivalent override yet. Hubble’s API, browser automation, voice retrieval, and no-code studio can produce an observable patient record, but a normal person cannot install the product or feel its pull within 48 hours. Forty-plus integrations are real artifacts for an operator, not a rocket, camera, ride, or game in my hands.

Hubble’s oversubscribed pre-seed financing is the dangerous signal on magical weirdness. Over the next 18 months, CMS-0057-F can make Hubble’s regulated workflow knowledge compound as payers prepare for January 1, 2027, but Particle Health, Health Gorilla, 1upHealth, and Zus Health make the category legible rather than strange. Hubble could become magically weird through the retrieval method—APIs plus browser plus voice under one auditable permission model—but today the round sounds warmer than the idea sounds misunderstood. My unstated sourcing pattern is also absent: Hubble arrived as an oversubscribed YC deal, not as something peculiar I discovered operating in my own environment.

Prabha Dublish is explicitly Hubble’s CEO, while Aaron Leon is explicitly CTO. That clears the first governance gate, but Hubble provides no equity split, operating agreement, vesting protocol, or documented tiebreaker beyond titles. The counterfactual is simple: evidence that Dublish has final product, financing, and personnel authority would move Hubble toward the high rung; a 50/50 structure would trigger the GameCrush and HQ Trivia red flag immediately. Titles are a rebuttal to my concern, not yet a hard no.

Vigil Labs is the useful parallel: regulated software becomes defensible when every permission, audit event, and exception teaches the system something competitors cannot casually reproduce. Hubble processes protected health information with deterministic identifiers, provenance, permissioning, and per-call audit logs, placing Hubble squarely in my ugly, regulated, unsexy lane. The analogy stops short because Hubble is software infrastructure rather than frontier hardware, while established networks already possess structural advantages. Still, regulation is not merely friction here; handled well, it is wet concrete around the product.

Forty-plus live tools across Epic, Cerner, athenahealth, UnitedHealthcare, and Aetna support a specific forecast: by July 2029, Hubble will reach $8M–$15M ARR and at least 150 supported payer/EHR workflows while remaining largely invisible to patients. Hubble gets there through founders who know where healthcare systems actually break, then turns each ugly retrieval exception into reusable workflow infrastructure. I’m never playing the game I seem to be playing: Hubble is not really selling record retrieval—it is trying to become the permissioned operating layer between fragmented healthcare institutions.

Health Gorilla’s national network, 1upHealth’s 75+ enterprises and 35M+ patients, and Particle Health’s $49.3M raised form the strongest bear case: Hubble’s 40 tools may be a fragile collection of browser and voice workarounds that incumbents can copy while Hubble inherits the security liability. That case stings, especially because an oversubscribed round undermines the underpriced non-consensus pattern and no Known Exception supplies HQ Trivia-level product love or DeepMind-level polymath force. Hubble’s answer is founder specificity plus production deployment across both EHRs and payers—evidence that the retrieval surface is already more than a demo. If Hubble cannot show three paying enterprise customers with repeat production retrieval and audit-ready consent records by July 2027, I flip from Invest to Pass.
