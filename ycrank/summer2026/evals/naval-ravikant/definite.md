# Definite -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The investable fact is not three Waterloo résumés. It is that Gurshabd encountered manual attestations and approval controls at BitGo, while Mazin watched regulatory numbers get rechecked at Optiver and built financial-analysis agents at Boosted.ai. That is specific knowledge acquired inside the problem. Farhan adds production machine-learning experience from Meta, but the dossier does not show that this team has shipped Definite into a finance organization, sold it, or sustained repeated usage. The founders understand the work; they have not yet proved the company.

The product makes the right technical separation. Language models interpret messy documents, while a deterministic engine performs calculations, thresholds, and validation, producing evidence-linked workpapers for human approval. Definite also reports that this engine paired with DeepSeek V3 beat GPT-5 on FinBalance at roughly half the cost. That is credible evidence of execution, but not yet durability: the result is company-reported, no independent replication was found, and a benchmark advantage can disappear with the next model release.

What survives if Numeric, Stacks, or Basis copies the architecture? Today, very little that is demonstrated. Customer-specific mappings, policies, evidence relationships, and approval logic could compound into switching costs, but the dossier labels that mechanism as inferred and provides no deployments or retention data. Definite is selling automation to each institution through founder-led enterprise sales; customers do not contribute supply, distribution, or utility to one another. This is proprietary workflow software, not yet a permissionless network or a scarce-data system.

The bull case is that regulated finance rewards correctness, provenance, and trust more than novelty. Definite’s founders have lived the failures, its deterministic verification layer is more appropriate than an unconstrained agent, and advance-paid private order forms could support high-value contracts. A demonstrated prototype can occasionally justify investing before a user loop appears. But terms permitting advance payment do not prove that anyone has paid, and 170 LinkedIn followers plus 11 Launch YC votes do not show adoption. The absence of revenue is normal at pre-seed; the absence of evidence that deployments create a durable asset is the problem.

The stated $928.9 million financial-close market is real but not enormous for a venture outcome, and the path from reconciliation into broader regulatory reporting, audit, insurance, or banking controls remains asserted rather than demonstrated. The succession from Ledgix to Bylaw, then bank reporting, then broad reconciliation may reflect useful search rather than weak conviction. It is still a red flag against believing the wedge is settled. I see no evidence of an integrity problem, artificial financing pressure, or founders demanding reassurance; the structural concern is simpler: a capable team may build a services-heavy enterprise product in a crowded market without creating leverage that compounds while it sleeps.

My 36-month base case is Definite reaches roughly $1 million to $3 million in ARR from regulated-finance deployments but remains a narrow, sales-led control platform rather than a defining network. I pass today. One condition flips the decision: five regulated institutions renew after twelve months and show that accumulated mappings and evidence relationships cut each subsequent deployment’s implementation work by at least 50%. That would turn the inferred moat into a measured compounding asset.
