# Spacesheep -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Michael Makarov has worked on the problem he is now selling. Building internal deployment and the first CC Artifacts version at Anthropic is specific knowledge acquired through shipping; his maintained sessionpipe project adds a visible artifact beyond the résumé ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/founders/michael-makarov), [maintainer registry](https://sessionpipe.org/governance/)). That earns Spacesheep substantial founder credit. It does not establish that knowledge became an asset the company owns. A founder can understand a problem exceptionally well while competitors capture its economics.

Spacesheep’s decisive question is what survives replication of its publishing, comments, versioning, and semantic search. The dossier establishes a functioning product but no proprietary model, exclusive data, or demonstrated switching cost. Collaboration history might eventually become expensive to replace; that remains a hypothesis. Meanwhile, Notion already supports external agents and interactive HTML blocks, and Anthropic hosts shareable interactive applications. Agent independence gives users a reason to try Spacesheep. The evidence does not yet show a reason they must stay.

There is room to maneuver. The initial buyer is a developer trying to review agent output; the adjacent buyer is a team preserving plans, explanations, dashboards, and decisions. The shared capability is turning generated output into persistent material that other people can inspect and revise. Gamma’s reported revenue above $100 million and Notion’s estimated $865 million ARR demonstrate spending around these activities, although neither measures Spacesheep’s addressable demand. Published prices—$4.50 Pro, $9 Max, and $10 per Team member monthly—identify a direct payer and transaction structure. They establish an offer, with no disclosed completed payments or delivery margins ([plans](https://spacesheep.dev/plans)).

The strongest objection to withholding a check is that waiting for a mature network could miss the founder who invents the workspace. Notion justified an early exception through a working prototype and documented capital efficiency before an observable distribution loop; Spacesheep shares the prototype mechanism, but exceptional capital efficiency is unestablished here. James Briggs’s named use for coding plans and reviews is more informative than the launch-day Product Hunt rank of #4. Still, 22 Chrome extension users and the available testimonials do not establish repeated collaborative behavior. Public sharing and connectors fit my preference for permissionless distribution, but no measured user-to-user benefit appears. The dossier supplies no evidence of integrity violations, financing pressure, or dependence on investor reassurance; the founder’s response to launch-video criticism is modest evidence of receptiveness.

I am **Neutral**. Spacesheep has a founder worth taking seriously and a plausible expansion path, with little demonstrated compounding beyond his own output. My one verifiable flip condition is a team-cohort result showing that additional active collaborators cause existing users to return and reuse shared artifacts more frequently, sustained across three months. That would turn collaboration from a feature description into evidence of accumulating value.
