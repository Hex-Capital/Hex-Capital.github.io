# Central Blue -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Central Blue’s proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and need for a first scarce check are all unknown. I cannot call this an auction because it is in YC, and I cannot call it cheap because it is early. The more consequential gap is what my money would settle. A nearly completed terrestrial prototype, a planned Wave Motion demonstration, and a 2027 CORE demonstration are three different milestones. The dossier does not connect round proceeds to one measurable experiment whose success changes the business’s value.

The distinction matters because Central Blue sells delivered power, while the Blackwing Space demonstration tests recovery software. Successful autonomy would be useful without establishing that another spacecraft can receive economically valuable energy. HELION’s advertised 300 kWh storage and 10 kW delivery for 4–5 hours are targets, not achieved performance. A useful venture moment would establish usable energy delivered through the complete system, within a customer’s pointing, thermal, and availability constraints. The disclosed plans lack a funded pass/fail threshold for that test.

The strongest argument against passing is the team. Brian Wu’s Perseverance visual-navigation work and robotics experience, alongside Jeewoo Choi’s Boeing thermal and environmental testing, give Central Blue credible contact with autonomy and aerospace hardware. Their prior planetary-robotics collaboration adds substance beyond two Stanford résumés. This fits my pattern of backing people who connect software with difficult physical operations. But the documented work does not yet establish that they have shipped both the power infrastructure and the customer integration being joined. Star Catcher’s reported October 1, 2026 demonstration-payload launch makes Central Blue’s incomplete terrestrial prototype a meaningful execution gap; its competitor’s $88 million raised says nothing about Central Blue’s own financing terms.

Dedicated receivers could eventually embed Central Blue in spacecraft designs. Today, the contact form recruits design partners; it does not document a purchase, signed pilot, or retained workflow. The prospective proprietary access remains prospective. There is also no disclosed pricing, margin, burn, or seed-funded break-even plan. Those omissions receive my missing-evidence defaults, rather than penalties for being pre-seed. Still, deleting the next financing from the model leaves no documented route to a self-supporting business. Receiver integration may create switching costs, but it also creates adoption work against a competitor pursuing existing-array compatibility.

I pass at 30. The physical-infrastructure pattern is real; the operating receipts needed to override my financing gates are absent. My known exceptions depend on demonstrated conversion, retention, external demand, or an explicit financing structure that avoids repeated raises. Planned demonstrations provide none of those overrides. Nor does the dossier establish an auction, coercive deadline, or dependence on another round, so I will not invent those red flags. One verifiable condition would reopen underwriting: a signed, customer-funded end-to-end demonstration contract specifying usable delivered energy, acceptance limits, and a deadline within the seed budget. That would turn Central Blue’s engineering agenda into a commercial experiment. I would still need the price before writing my own check.
