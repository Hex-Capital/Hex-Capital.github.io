# Repovive -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Repovive has found a buyer for scarce reasoning work. The question is whether it can turn that work into an asset. Its reported $100K in contracts across two formalization RL environments is meaningful at pre-seed. But selling another environment and making every existing environment more valuable are different businesses. I see evidence for the first; the second would earn my check.

Shayan’s Lean dataset work and Amir’s model post-training experience put the founders close to the problem. The earlier programming extension independently establishes that the team shipped something people used: 879 installs and 11 ratings averaging 4.91/5. That is useful evidence of action, although competitive-programming assistance is adjacent to industrial verification. The exact-domain research and experience remain substantially founder-reported, and the claimed 8.3-percentage-point improvement awaits benchmark publication. I give the founders credit for relevant execution without treating their biographies as independently demonstrated technical superiority.

The reported community of 12,000 medalists is Repovive’s most interesting potential asset. Copying the code would not instantly reproduce those relationships. This fits my preference for outsiders contributing scarce knowledge to a system. But recruitment reach becomes a network effect only when participants improve the product for one another. The dossier establishes neither that loop nor exclusive contribution rights. Repovive could own a compounding formalization corpus; it could also repeatedly recruit experts whose work becomes the customer’s property. The second arrangement sells skilled labor with software assistance.

The strongest case against passing is that Repovive already has commercial commitments where many research startups have demonstrations. Two contracted environments suggest buyers value the work, and the separate $200K coding-data LOI identifies an adjacent purchase using related capabilities. That gives the founders room to maneuver beyond Lean theorem solving. Still, the LOI is not revenue, contract values do not establish cash collection, and neither establishes margins. Harmonic’s expansion into C/Verilog verification makes broader applications concrete, but its financing does not establish Repovive’s addressable spending. The path into production chips and critical software still requires evidence that correct proofs address correct specifications.

I sometimes accept missing network effects when an exceptional prototype and unusually efficient execution supply another route to compounding. Repovive’s unpublished benchmark does not yet support that exception. Nor does its audience establish the self-reinforcing adoption that would let me overlook uncertain economics. There is no disclosed integrity problem, artificial financing deadline, or founder behavior that triggers a personal veto. My objection is structural: lab procurement could turn a promising expert community into a sequence of bespoke deliverables, while customers retain the accumulated learning.

I would pass today. My one flip condition is a documented paid renewal showing that a second lab buys access to the same Repovive-controlled formalization corpus, with substantially less incremental expert work than the first deployment. That would demonstrate reusable ownership and economic compounding. The founders have found valuable work. I need evidence that completing it makes the next sale structurally better.
