# Jinko -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**Path A, product-led adoption.** Jinko’s first buyer is a developer building an AI travel agent that repeatedly needs to turn a flexible request, such as finding a trip within budget, into a live booking and handle later changes or refunds. The memo records live flight and hotel search in Exa Connect and documents self-serve API keys and quickstarts. A startup founder reported reaching live bookings in four hours. That is an early activation signal, with the acquisition source, founder assistance, and payment still unknown. If developers can discover Jinko through Exa or agent plugins and launch through the same quickstart, retained integrations could generate more booking fees as their own users book more trips. The memo shows no repeat booking or account expansion data. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A second-market test could use the same API with a U.S.-based developer. Jinko’s reported inventory and IATA and ARC accreditations give it a starting point for supplying that integration. Portability would depend on whether the buyer can get suitable fares, take payment, and service bookings through the same setup. The memo does not establish buyer demand across geographies. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

### Countercase

The easiest part to adopt may be free discovery, while paid checkout and servicing carry the operational load. Etraveli already offers overlapping flight search, booking, and servicing through MCP, so Jinko’s budget-led discovery needs to translate into bookings to support paid adoption. At the published 1% fee, an illustrative $500 flight yields $5 in booking-fee revenue before costs, assuming standard pricing. The economics turn on the cost of searches before conversion, payment and settlement where Jinko bears them, and support for changes and refunds. Excess-search charges offer a possible offset, while fare-markup restrictions limit another source of revenue. The memo gives no realized revenue or incremental cost data. These risks support Watch. The structural Pass gate requires evidence of a ceiling or proportional scaling costs. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent cohort of AI travel developers in a second market come through Exa or an agent plugin, go live through the standard quickstart, and generate repeat paid bookings with positive incremental contribution? A cohort record showing acquisition source, onboarding hours, bookings across successive periods, and revenue after search, payment, and servicing costs would move the action to **Reach out** if it confirms that pattern. One-off bookings or incomplete records would keep Jinko on **Watch**.
