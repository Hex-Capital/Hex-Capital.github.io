# Hickory & Company -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Hickory & Company has identified a real institutional asymmetry: sponsor banks are accountable for fintech partners whose daily operations they do not control. That could support an important company. But what does Hickory believe about solving this problem that its competitors reject? Source-linked research, agents working against acceptance criteria, and human approval describe a product architecture; they do not establish a secret. The dossier supplies neither a disputed proposition nor an experiment that tests one. That is missing evidence, rather than a disproved thesis.

The monopoly question produces a more adverse answer. Sponsor-bank oversight is a recognizable beachhead, but [Parcha already offers configurable compliance agents and audit logs](https://blog.parcha.ai/agent-hub/), while Bretton AI and Hummingbird address overlapping investigations and review workflows. These products need not be identical for competition to constrain Hickory’s economics. Its published differentiation rests on workflow expertise, with no demonstrated exclusive data, proprietary technical advantage, or dominant position. Institution-specific policies might eventually create switching costs, but implementation work alone does not establish who captures the value. Hickory could make compliance substantially better while customers retain several credible suppliers.

The strongest objection to passing is the team’s proximity to the problem. Joshua Howland previously built banking products at Seis; Scott Grow worked with him there and brings more than 20 years of compliance experience. Their shared operating history provides affirmative evidence of alignment, although founder ownership, full-time commitments, and control of core engineering remain undocumented. This also resembles two patterns I favor: financial infrastructure and companies assembled through existing operator relationships. But the dossier does not establish that Hickory repairs the specific operating failure that closed Seis. The prior company’s reported 500,000 accounts and $10 million revenue demonstrate relevant experience; they establish neither Hickory’s demand nor its monopoly.

The [claimed live-bank integration](https://hickory.co/for/sponsor-banks) is the most consequential product signal because deployment inside a bank can expose constraints that a demonstration conceals. It remains unnamed, however, with no documented usage, accuracy, or customer outcome. My exceptions to competitive or execution concerns require evidence that directly removes the concern: a verified technical breakthrough or persistent customer behavior. This integration claim does not yet do that. Nor do Hickory’s terms establish a regulatory violation: requiring qualified human review is sensible. The unresolved economic question is how much review remains after automation, because that determines whether the institution buys meaningful labor savings or another supervisory obligation.

Distribution has progressed beyond an aspiration. [Humanfold describes prospect lists, LinkedIn outreach, a sales pipeline, and 22 launch videos](https://humanfold.ai/work/hickory). That earns some credit for an actual acquisition effort, but no conversion rate or sales cycle establishes repeatability. Separately, institution-level software contracts remain an inferred revenue model: there is no published pricing or gross-margin structure from which to construct a power-law outcome. I do not require pre-seed revenue. I require a reason that one coherent revenue engine can become enormous; the adjacent $2.3 billion AML estimate does not supply that arithmetic.

I would pass on Hickory & Company today. The decisive issue is demonstrated competitive overlap without an observable mechanism for escaping it; the missing secret and economic evidence leave that issue unresolved. There is no evidence of the founding red flags—part-time leadership, convenience matching, or investor-majority ownership—that would justify condemning the team. One verifiable result would reopen my decision: a named sponsor bank’s paid renewal following a head-to-head trial showing that a proprietary Hickory capability produces regulator-accepted work at materially lower total cost than Parcha, including human review. That would begin to convert domain familiarity into evidence of a business competitors cannot readily substitute.
