# Vorelios -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to an engineering simulation platform is Vorelios’s strongest supported route. The memo documents Siemens’s collaboration with PhysicsX on an aerodynamics model, which shows a platform investing in this workflow. Vorelios reports subsecond predictions from clean CAD and autonomous searches over thousands of candidates, with predictions within a few percent of an independent solver in initial runs. A platform could have a reason to acquire Vorelios if its multi-physics design search cuts end-to-end engineering time enough to justify buying the capability. The memo contains no customer-measured savings or acquisition pricing evidence. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

A plausible seed-stage milestone is a paid pilot on an unfamiliar aerospace design family that measures solver agreement and total workflow time, including CAD preparation. Vorelios’s reported prototype and the memo’s reported $125,000 in financing support an initial test. The memo gives no pilot commitment, budget, or runway to reach that milestone. The company reports building its own training pipeline, while ownership and transfer rights for the models, data, and software remain unverified. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, dilution from 5% to 3%, and zero follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale scenarios below are illustrative. Both assume a closing within eight years, an eligible holder selling its entire stake for cash, and pro rata distribution of the residual after debt and deal adjustments, with zero additional senior claims or preference diversions.

- **Modest sale.** $40 million in enterprise value less $5 million in debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million for equity and $1.05 million for the holder, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value less $10 million in debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million for equity and $2.7 million for the holder, or 5.4×. At 2% ownership, the same sale yields $1.8 million, or 3.6×.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested and zero eligible share purchases or distributions yields zero realized proceeds within eight years.

### Countercase

Siemens already has an aerodynamics collaboration with PhysicsX, and NVIDIA’s open-source PhysicsNeMo gives potential buyers tools to build their own surrogates. Vorelios also reports that its previous architecture failed on unfamiliar design families. If that weakness persists or CAD preparation absorbs the reported speed advantage, a platform could meet its needs through an existing partner or an internal build. The memo establishes competitive pressure and a concrete validation gap. Evidence of a sufficient substitute, a structural closing block, or proceeds below the hurdle under the strongest credible route is still missing, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid pilot sponsored by a simulation platform show reliable results on an unfamiliar aerospace design family and enough end-to-end savings to support a plausible acquisition price above the $83.3 million distributable-equity hurdle? Independent solver checks, measured workflow savings including CAD preparation, and the sponsor’s quantified comparison of buying versus building would answer it. A result that supports the hurdle after deal adjustments would move the action to Reach out.
