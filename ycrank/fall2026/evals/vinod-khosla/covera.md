# Covera -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Covera could make insurance brokerage substantially more productive. The investment question is how much of insurance’s cost structure that changes. Ten hours saved per user weekly is useful; it does not establish an orders-of-magnitude reduction in the cost of obtaining appropriate coverage. The dossier identifies a $4.69 billion agency-software market, but supplies no bridge from administrative automation to radically cheaper insurance expertise. That leaves the consequence-of-success case unproven.

There is more substance here than an assistant demo. Hello Risk recorded 155 tasks in two weeks and 90-second median quote preparation. Persistent account context and execution across carrier portals address limitations of standalone ChatGPT. But preparation time is only one component of completed work. The reported four-hours-to-ten-minutes improvement comes from an unnamed testimonial; the named case study estimates savings using assigned manual durations. These establish a working product, without establishing a reproducible 5–10x technical advantage. FurtherAI already offers overlapping workflows, and connector limitations leave some work outside Covera’s execution boundary.

The strongest argument against passing is the founders. Venet and Regerat met at Pretto, built Simio together, and sold it in 2024. They have brokerage exposure, experience shipping CRM software, and company-reported adoption across 50+ agencies. My actual investing pattern includes execution-heavy financial businesses; scientific novelty is not mandatory. Covera could use administrative work as its entry into a much broader brokerage platform. That possibility deserves attention. However, the dossier does not establish the giant value pool Covera could capture beyond software, or the mechanism for reaching it. The exceptions I make for an initially modest product require that larger case. The founders’ previous acquisition is execution evidence; it supplies no evidence that they are optimizing Covera for an early exit. Their record also leaves belief revision and recruiting against specific weaknesses unobserved.

The next dollar should test whether automation survives responsibility for the completed submission. Hello Risk recorded two corrected values and retained human approval. Those corrections are not a measured error rate: tasks and extracted fields have different denominators. Covera needs to measure total labor, including checking, corrections, portal failures, and servicing consequences. The dossier supplies no costed experiment, pass/fail threshold, or stop condition against that uncertainty. A founding account-executive opening does not prove premature scaling, but it earns no credit for removing the technical risk. Likewise, proprietary carrier mappings could eventually compound; today that learning advantage remains inferred. The red flag is competition through integrations and sales before differentiated capability is demonstrated, although the evidence does not establish that Covera has already chosen that path.

I pass at **28/100**. Missing public revenue is ordinary at this stage. Missing unit economics means I cannot tell whether Covera eliminates labor or relocates it into implementation, review, and support. Custom pricing, a free trial, and a claimed 12x customer ROI do not resolve that question. My flip condition is one independently audited production cohort showing at least **10x lower total labor per completed, accuracy-matched submission**, including review and exceptions, at a customer price below the displaced labor cost and positive contribution margin after inference, onboarding, and support. That result would connect the working product to a consequential cost curve.
