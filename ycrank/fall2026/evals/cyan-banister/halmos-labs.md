# Halmos Labs -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I want a biologist to spend scarce laboratory time resolving uncertainty that actually matters. Halmos Labs offers a small glimpse of that world: its computational demonstration placed four of six known responding mutations among its top ten candidates, with labels withheld during ranking. But the input was enriched and came from one patient. Getting from that artifact to dependable experimental decisions requires two transitions: predictions must survive prospective laboratory testing, and recommendations must respect the constraints of the laboratory doing the work. The dossier establishes an early computational step along that path.

The founders give me a reason to keep looking. Mattias Akke’s biochemical machine-learning and drug-discovery research at MIT, AstraZeneca, and Cambridge connects directly to choosing useful experiments. Linus von Ekensteen Löfgren brings quantitative modeling and LLM-security research; founding engineer Linus Särud adds offensive-security experience. That combination makes sense for a system whose agents must interrogate one another’s assumptions. It establishes relevant technical depth and a complementary team. It supplies much less evidence of independently pursued, pre-company obsession or the founders’ command of selling research outcomes.

The most interesting customer action is wonderfully mundane: someone changed an experimental plan. Halmos reports finding an overlooked fermentation nutrient variable that a customer confirmed before revising its experiments. A scientist spent attention checking the suggestion and allowed it to affect real work. That matters. The reported 30% reduction in evaluations across repeated bioprocessing simulations adds support, although simulated savings cannot establish laboratory savings. Both results remain company-reported, without enough customer or cohort detail to establish repeated dependence.

Why does the scientist lose months today? Exhaustive screens consume laboratory capacity, but the dossier does not identify a gatekeeper collecting an avoidable rent or a rule that withholds control from researchers. Halmos could save substantial time without changing who controls the research process. Its fit with my less glamorous workflow-repair pattern is clearer than its fit with returning economic agency. Likewise, automatic deductive checks and exploration across assumptions could become enabling infrastructure across biological applications, but the dossier establishes neither exclusive feedback data nor a scarce technical asset that customers must obtain from Halmos. Atinary’s August 2026 Bruker collaboration illustrates the competing route: experiment optimization already connected to instruments.

The strongest argument against waiting is that demanding revenue would miss precisely this kind of company. **CosmicBrain AI** supplies a relevant exception: a functioning technical prototype can justify an early check before sales. The material difference was its founder’s lifelong building record and signed industrial proofs of concept. Halmos has relevant researchers, computational artifacts, and summer 2026 verification funding from LU Innovation, so I take that exception seriously. However, its repeated recommendations outside a collaboration’s permitted experimental range expose the gap between checking reasoning and producing executable science. The enabling-layer pattern attracts me; that operating gap prevents it from carrying the investment decision.

I am neutral on Halmos Labs and would hold my own money today. The dossier does not establish the severe privacy or misleading-leadership red flags that would make this an active rejection. My hesitation concerns whether useful research assistance becomes dependable infrastructure. One result would change my decision: an independently documented prospective campaign at a named industry partner showing at least 25% fewer wet-lab experiments to reach a predefined performance target, with recommendations remaining inside agreed experimental constraints. That would connect the little computational leak to the future I want to inhabit.
