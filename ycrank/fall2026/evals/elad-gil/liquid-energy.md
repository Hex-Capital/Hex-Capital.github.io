# Liquid Energy -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Grid delays are creating buyers for inference capacity that arrives with its own power. Liquid Energy’s investment case turns on one variable: fully loaded cost per delivered token at the availability customers require. Avoiding a grid connection matters only if solar intermittency, storage, cooling and GPU utilization leave that number competitive. The dossier establishes the bottleneck but supplies no operating measurements or customer price threshold that establish whether NanoDC clears it. I would pass on the current evidence.

The strongest argument against passing is the magnitude of the timing advantage. JLL’s January 2026 outlook puts average grid-connection waits above four years in primary data-center markets; Liquid Energy advertises first-token delivery within eight weeks of signing. Bring-your-own-power mandates create an institutional opening alongside the technical one. If factory integration makes that delivery promise repeatable, customers could rationally pay a premium for capacity available years earlier. That is a meaningful market change. However, eight weeks is a company claim, and its terms say specifications remain in development and advertised capabilities may never be offered. I credit the opening without treating the proposed deployment advantage as demonstrated economics.

Liquid Energy also enters a market where capital and competing supply are already forming. Rune announced a $40 million Series A in September 2026 for compute attached to existing solar generation. Crusoe reports over $100 million in contracted inference ARR, although that is neither recognized revenue nor Liquid Energy’s traction. Carrying an unfolding solar array could expand site selection, but it also places generation equipment inside the capital and operating budget. My physical-infrastructure investments require technology to change the margin structure; the dossier does not yet show that change. Integrating compute, power, cooling and networking creates a plausible product boundary, but there is no documented operating loop, fleet data or customer integration establishing durable ownership. Better models alone do not establish better returns on this hardware.

The founders provide some affirmative company-building evidence. Sajan Khosa previously founded AJMER and signed three brokerages; Sid Ravikumar was a founding engineer at Jelly.fm. Those experiences deserve modest credit for prior startup exposure, without implying proven solar manufacturing or fleet-operation expertise. Liquid Energy’s YC participation, Velocity affiliation since 2024 and NVIDIA Inception affiliation do not establish customer pull. There is no disclosed paid deployment, repeat usage or named capacity buyer, so demand receives the missing-evidence default. The research-stage exception does not resolve this: the dossier supplies neither dated frontier results nor a documented financing path that could substitute for product pull.

The bull case fits my pattern of investing across a technology discontinuity, including physical infrastructure when engineering changes its economics. The unresolved red flag is whether Liquid Energy inherits hardware capital intensity while selling inference into an increasingly competitive market. Missing data does not prove commodity margins, but it cannot justify assuming superior ones. My single flip condition is an independently verified, paying NanoDC deployment that meets the buyer’s contracted availability and token price at a positive fully loaded margin, including storage, site costs and GPU depreciation. That would connect the real market opening to an investable business.
