# IMPACT Drones -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The cost asymmetry is real: infrastructure operators may face drones costing under $50,000 while legacy effectors cost more than $1 million. IMPACT Drones proposes an expendable autonomous interceptor, launched from a sealed cell and visually guided despite GPS jamming. That could improve defensive economics. But the dossier quantifies a $6.6 billion counter-UAS market, not a 100x societal consequence. It does not show how many sites remain undefended, the losses avoided, or whether its subscription changes protection costs by an order of magnitude.

The technical case is plausible but unproven. HUNTER and turbine-powered SABLE address different threat classes, yet IMPACT publishes no interceptor cost, range, speed, success rate, false-positive rate, or debris envelope. Fortem reports 5,500 successful captures, Origin Robotics is deployed by four European NATO states, and Cambridge Aerospace advertises 30-kilometer range and 700-kilometer-per-hour performance. Against those named incumbents, “ramming plus visual navigation” is an architecture, not a demonstrated 5–10x discontinuity.

The strongest bull case is the team. Jasper Lortije has already built GNSS-denied navigation for long-range ISR drones and helped secure the 2025 NATO Summit; Alessandro Fiume previously led sales at the same company. This is a technically relevant founder paired with a commercial counterpart, attacking an undeniable and rapidly expanding threat. Q3 2026 pilots in the Middle East could produce the operational learning loop that public materials do not yet show. A small team can move faster than incumbent defense procurement organizations.

But pilots are useful only when designed to kill the thesis. The dossier supplies no numerical pass threshold, test cost, accountable owner, or if/then response for the largest risks: autonomous classification, interception reliability, collateral debris, and civilian deployment authority. U.S. law does not clearly authorize private infrastructure owners to conduct kinetic mitigation. Spending on site deployments before resolving who may legally authorize an engagement risks proving a product that customers cannot operate.

The subscription model could hide attractive economics, but today it hides all economics. There is no price, reload cost, airframe cost, utilization assumption, or comparison with Fortem’s reusable net capture and competing interceptors. Cheap hostile drones do not imply cheap IMPACT protection. Two airframes, sealed launch cells, sensors, monitoring, reloads, and liability may recreate the cost structure the company claims to disrupt.

I pass today. The verifiable flip condition is a named civilian-infrastructure pilot showing at least 90% successful interceptions across representative jammed and cluttered conditions, with a legally approved operating framework and fully loaded cost per defended engagement below $50,000. That single result would address technical discontinuity, deployment authority, and economic parity. Without it, this is a capable aerospace team with a differentiated configuration—not yet a Black-Swan defense platform.
