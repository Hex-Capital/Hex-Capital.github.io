# Invertix -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Invertix’s candidate secret is that renewable-energy operations can be automated by encoding how an organization actually works, rather than merely detecting faults in its equipment. That is a testable proposition: compare completed maintenance workflows, engineering hours, and error rates against existing tools at the same plants. But the dossier neither identifies a prevailing belief that rejects this proposition nor reports that comparison. Delfos is already developing overlapping automation. “Energy superintelligence” names an ambition; it does not yet establish a secret.

The strongest argument for Invertix is that customers appear to be buying before the technical argument has been settled publicly. Company-reported “multiple six figures” in ARR by June 2026 is meaningful at this stage. Vireo’s May report of more than 1.8 GW managed also places the product beyond a demonstration. These figures could describe the beginnings of an essential operating layer. They could also describe successful delivery of customized engineering. The distinction matters because customer-specific ontologies establish deployment context, while exclusive data, demonstrated switching costs, and a transferable technical advantage remain unproved. Customer-controlled data rights make automatic cross-customer compounding particularly unsafe to assume.

The founding relationship is more encouraging than a team assembled for an accelerator application. Perrotta and Durmaz worked together at Brightdale in early 2025 and became roommates before building Invertix. That is affirmative evidence of shared history. Their commercial and applied-AI backgrounds suggest complementary responsibilities, although credentials do not establish economic alignment. Full-time status, ownership, compensation, and control of essential engineering remain undisclosed. I see no documented part-time-founder or investor-majority red flag; I also cannot treat those founding questions as answered.

Distribution is the most concrete part of the business. Invertix sells through demos, embeds engineers, and has confirmed access to TotalEnergies On. Vireo describes approximately 60 days from contract to deployment: a real delivery process, although not evidence of a repeatable sales cycle. This approach may fit valuable enterprise contracts. Without contract values or implementation margins, however, the same embedded engineers could become the constraint on expansion. Reported ARR supports demand; it supplies neither customer-level economics nor the arithmetic for a multibillion-dollar outcome. The adjacent $3.07 billion software-market estimate cannot supply that arithmetic either.

Invertix fits my broader interest in technology entering physical infrastructure, and its reported deployments weaken the objection that everything must work simultaneously before customers receive value. The strongest bull case is that embedded engineering discovers operational knowledge incumbents cannot easily reproduce, eventually converting labor into proprietary software. I would take that possibility seriously. But the known exceptions do not yet justify a check: there is no independently demonstrated technical discontinuity, and early purchasing does not establish the persistent customer behavior that would resolve the monopoly question. Raptor Maps, Delfos, and enSights already occupy relevant workflows. Their presence does not prove Invertix is interchangeable; it makes ownership of the resulting value the central unanswered question.

I pass today. My 36-month expectation is a specialized renewable-operations vendor reaching roughly €2–6 million in ARR while still relying materially on embedded delivery, rather than becoming the category’s dominant operating layer. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: an independently audited, multi-customer deployment study showing that Invertix completes the same operational workflows at one-tenth the total delivery cost of the best incumbent alternative, with equivalent reliability. That would turn the proposed ontology advantage into demonstrated proprietary technology.
