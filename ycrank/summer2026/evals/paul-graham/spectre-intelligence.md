# Spectre Intelligence -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The most important fact is that Spectre Intelligence has two AI pods trading real semiconductor and biotech capital. The least useful fact is that its founders attended Harvard. Shipping a live system before anyone grants permission is real evidence. But the dossier never names what broke, what Michelle Li or Joshua Zyzak did next, or how quickly their workaround changed performance. I can see the machine running; I cannot yet see whether its builders are relentlessly resourceful.

The technical proof is therefore intermediate rather than high. The system records decisions, reasoning traces, trades, and ground-truth P&L, and Spectre says it is already profitable. Zyzak has worked on reinforcement-learning robotics and attended Optiver’s trading program; Li combines biology research with personal trading experience. But “profitable” without AUM, returns, drawdown, benchmark, or even a dollar figure is not a measured result. Spectre also says LLM traders remain unreliable and identifies memory, learning, and long-running robustness as unresolved. Real capital makes this more than a diagram. Missing numbers keep it from being convincing proof.

There is no separate user-pull signal. In proprietary trading, P&L can substitute for customers, but only when the return series is visible. The compensated expert network supplies inputs rather than demand, and no outside investor, repeat allocation, management fee, or performance fee is disclosed. The $5.6 trillion hedge-fund market does not help answer the simple question: does this particular system produce durable alpha? Abundance, Nof1, Numerai, and established quantitative funds can use similar models and market data, while Spectre itself acknowledges that successful strategies will be copied.

The founders have plausible contact with the wedge. Li’s biology work maps to the biotech pod, while Zyzak’s AI work and quant exposure map to the trading system. Recruiting specialists, operating live strategies, and potentially handling adviser obligations are genuine schleps. But the dossier does not document a founder-originated collision with a trading problem, nor a dated change of route after contrary evidence. One product version with no described setback is missing evidence, not evidence of rigidity.

The strongest bull case is that two young technical founders are already running profitable strategies and accumulating a proprietary labeled dataset before raising institutional-scale capital. That could compound quickly. The relevant known-exception pattern is youth overridden by numerical technical and real-world proof. Spectre has real-world deployment but withholds the numbers, so the exception does not clear. Its pre-round proof spike is asserted rather than inspectable, and a business exposed to alpha decay cannot rely on assertion.

I would pass today. The age and competition are not decisive; the missing causal sequence is. A working trading system is valuable, but I do not yet know how these founders react when a strategy stops working, and that is nearly the whole company. One condition would flip me: a broker-verified six-month record beating appropriate sector-neutral benchmarks net of costs, accompanied by a dated example of a model failure that the founders diagnosed and corrected within days, restoring performance.
