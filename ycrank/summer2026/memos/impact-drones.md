## The Idea

- **Problem:** Data centers, energy infrastructure, airports, and bases face low-cost hostile drones while legacy effectors can cost $1M+ against threats costing under $50,000 ([company website](https://impact-drones.com/), Aug 2026).
- **Approach:** A sealed on-site cell detects a threat, launches an autonomous interceptor through a frangible lid, and uses onboard visual navigation to ram the target despite GPS jamming ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/impact-drones), Aug 2026).
- **Differentiation:** IMPACT uses two ramming airframes—HUNTER for close-range threats and turbine-powered SABLE for faster targets—versus Fortem’s tethered-net capture, Origin Robotics’ radar-guided BLAZE, and Cambridge Aerospace’s 30-km Skyhammer interceptor ([IMPACT](https://impact-drones.com/), [Fortem](https://fortemtech.com/products/dronehunter-f700/), [Origin Robotics](https://alpineeagle.com/alpine-eagle-partners-with-origin-robotics/), [UK Ministry of Defence](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/uk-start-up-to-supply-interceptor-missiles-to-uk-military-and-gulf-partners), 2026).
- **Business Model:** Sites subscribe for protection sized to the site, with hardware, reloads, and monitoring included, but no public price is disclosed ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/impact-drones), Aug 2026).
- **TAM/SAM:** The global C-UAS market was estimated at $6.6B in 2025 and projected to reach $20.3B by 2030 at a 25.1% CAGR, while no public SAM was found for civilian fixed-site kinetic interception ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/ResearchInsight/counter-cuas-systems-outlook-2030.asp), 2026).
- **GTM / Distribution:** The company solicits briefings for partnerships, pilots, and procurement, names data-center, airport, and energy-security buyers, and schedules pilot deployments for Q3 2026 ([company website](https://impact-drones.com/), [Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/impact-drones), Aug 2026).

## Key Risks

- **Civilian deployment authority:** U.S. law authorizes trained government and law-enforcement bodies to mitigate credible drone threats but does not expressly give private infrastructure owners equivalent authority, creating a dependency on government authorization or partnership ([6 U.S.C. §124n](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title6-section124n), 2026).
- **Technical validation:** Public materials schedule pilots for Q3 2026 but provide no intercept success rate, environmental-test results, or false-positive rate; the company’s disclosed mitigation is separate HUNTER and SABLE airframes for different threat classes ([company website](https://impact-drones.com/), Aug 2026).
- **Collateral and classification risk:** Ramming over airports, grids, or populated campuses can create debris and mistaken-engagement hazards, while European airport operators report that safety concerns and differing regulations constrain kinetic responses ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/1fcc78547151369f756f5c9f677681de), Aug 2026).
- **Deployed competition:** Origin’s BLAZE is deployed by four European NATO states and Fortem reports more than 5,500 successful captures, while IMPACT’s public deployments remain prospective ([Latvian investment agency](https://www.liaa.gov.lv/en/article/latvian-defence-technology-gains-international-recognition-france-selects-latvian-made-interceptor-drone-system), [Fortem](https://fortemtech.com/press-releases/2025-06-17-fortem-technologies-opens-new-advanced-manufacturing-headquarters-to-meet-global-demand-for-counter-drone-defense/), 2026).
