# Vyra -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The most useful fact about Vyra is that its browser connections broke and the founders rebuilt the system. On October 25 they reported shipping a headless server architecture to address disconnections and project-concurrency limits. That is a named obstacle followed by a concrete response. It tells me more about Caleb Pong and Sulan Zhang than their audience numbers do. But the date of the fix does not establish how quickly they solved the problem, and the announcement does not establish that it stayed solved. I give the founders meaningful credit for acting, with less certainty about the result ([Vyra MCP announcement](https://www.usevyra.com/blog/vyra-mcp-v2)).

These founders encountered the problem through actual work. Caleb spent seven years editing videos; Sulan is a creator with an audience YC lists at over 80,000. They began building together in winter 2025. That makes Vyra an organic idea with a plausible source of early users. The neglected work is making editing reliable enough that a creator can hand over footage and stop supervising. Buffer’s July test establishes that the software worked, but processing an 81-second clip took roughly 20 minutes and omitted music and B-roll. That is functioning software with substantial unfinished work. Their editing experience supports the problem choice; it does not itself prove that they have mastered this operational burden ([Vyra About](https://www.usevyra.com/about), [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/vyra), [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/ai-video-tools/)).

The strongest case for investing is that this team can both build the tool and put it in front of creators. Vyra shipped an iPhone app on October 15 and the headless MCP version ten days later. The company claims 93,984 creators, and Caleb reports collecting 10,000 email signups in 24 hours. There could be a small, intensely interested group inside those numbers. But I cannot identify it from the dossier. Four App Store ratings, promotional views, and an undefined creator count do not tell me who comes back with their next video. Plans priced from $24 to $129 per month create a way to charge; they do not establish that anyone pays. The decisive distinction is between people who want to try automatic editing and people who entrust their weekly output to it ([mobile announcement](https://www.usevyra.com/blog/vyra-mobile), [Vyra](https://www.usevyra.com/), [pricing](https://www.usevyra.com/pricing)).

Vyra’s MCP interface fits my preference for tools that technical users can compose into larger systems. The missing part of that pattern is evidence of users actually inventing workflows around it. Descript already offers MCP editing, so exposing tools to an assistant supplies no automatic advantage. Vyra could win by learning one creator workflow unusually well. The server rebuild shows willingness to change implementation, but it supplies no separate evidence that the founders rejected a product hypothesis after observing users. I would not count the same rebuild twice. Nor would I infer the usual launch or partnership red flags: the dossier shows continued shipping and founder demonstrations, rather than dependence on one ceremonial launch or an unnamed large partner ([Descript](https://www.descript.com/mcp), [Vyra](https://www.usevyra.com/)).

I would hold off on writing a check. The working-product exception deserves consideration: an unusually convincing prototype can justify backing founders before retention is established. Here, independent testing confirms functionality while exposing a serious speed limitation; the later reliability improvements remain company-reported. That does not yet override the incomplete resourcefulness evidence. My one verifiable flip condition would be a documented four-week cohort of ten independent shortform creators who pay and return weekly to publish through the rebuilt product, with project logs confirming successful exports. That would connect the founders’ response to resistance with a narrow group that actually needs what they built.
