# Fire Wallet -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Fire Wallet has shipped an app. What I cannot find is the episode that would make me want to back its founders: something broke, they found a way around it, and a customer’s situation changed. Joshua Goldbard’s experience founding MobileCoin makes him worth meeting. It does not answer that question. Neither does the company-reported $100M+ raised by his previous company. The missing episode leaves resourcefulness unproven; it does not establish that the founders lack it.

The iPhone release is Fire Wallet’s strongest evidence. Someone has built and distributed software, which is a meaningful step beyond describing a wallet. But the evidence supports a narrower claim than the advertised product. The App Store specifies Ethereum transfers and recovery phrases; the website describes multichain access and email/PIN recovery; the terms describe distributed HSM key management. These might describe different versions. Until reconciled, they cannot establish that Fire has solved the difficult combination of privacy, recovery, and self-custody. Three five-star ratings establish very little about that combination ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fire-wallet-digital-assets/id6792120467), [terms](https://fire.co/terms/)).

The strongest argument for investing is the enterprise prospect that reportedly introduced ten other companies. A prospective customer recruiting other prospective customers is more interesting than a waitlist. Together with the named Trustboop LOI, it suggests a possible narrow well: businesses already moving substantial sums that dislike their existing payment tools. But the prospect’s approximately $1.5M monthly volume and the roughly $100M annual opportunity belong to prospective customers. Fire has not disclosed processing those payments. Nor does the dossier establish repeat use or payment to Fire. Transaction fees are permitted but unspecified, so even eventual payment volume would need to become retained fee revenue ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/fire-wallet)).

Goldbard’s domain experience and his prior work with Manuel make this a stronger bull case than three strangers assembling a fashionable crypto pitch. Payments also contain plenty of unpleasant work worth doing. Yet the dossier does not identify the particular problem these founders encountered personally, or a customer observation that caused them to change their route. Both remain at my missing-evidence defaults. My pattern of backing useful technical primitives would become relevant if businesses repeatedly built workflows around Fire’s checkout or sub-wallets. Today those capabilities are descriptions. Seeking integration partners does not itself trigger my partnership red flag, and the existing Josh–Manuel relationship partly answers my concern about newly assembled teams. Neither supplies the missing execution evidence.

I would pass at present. I checked the working-product and validated-proof exceptions: the published app makes the former worth considering, but there is no documented rapid correction, independently validated technical result, or unmistakable user pull strong enough to override the resourcefulness gate. The flip condition is one documented enterprise deployment in which a named payment blocker leads to a founder-shipped fix within weeks, followed by four consecutive weeks of customer payments through Fire. That would connect the software to the founders’ behavior and the customer’s need. The current dossier leaves those connections open.
