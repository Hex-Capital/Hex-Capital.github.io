# Vestris -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Aahil Valliani and Joshua Tang spent six weeks processing settlement files before building Vestris, giving Vestris real founder-problem contact but no compounding user network. That is the signature: specific knowledge above the pre-seed baseline, leverage architecture below the angel-return bar. Vestris may turn scarce workflow knowledge into useful code, but each title firm remains an island. Software scales. Distribution does not.

**Stack Overflow** is the high rung for specific knowledge: developers serving developers after years of public building and domain immersion. Vestris shares the founder-as-user mechanism because Valliani and Tang personally encountered the reported 8–10 hours of processing per file, and Valliani adds computer-science training plus two software patents. Vestris diverges where the analogy matters most: six weeks inside a settlement company is exposure, not obsession. No public repository, technical writing, or multi-year title history shows that Vestris possesses knowledge another capable YC pair could not acquire.

**Perplexity** exposed the low-network pattern: a single-sided product can still win when permissionless distribution, rare technical knowledge, and category timing overwhelm the missing network. Vestris also lacks user-added compounding—one title firm’s orders do not automatically improve another firm’s experience, and custom enterprise pricing reinforces customer isolation. But Vestris has neither Perplexity’s consumer distribution nor Aravind Srinivas-level retrieval expertise. Firm-specific corrections might accumulate into shared workflow intelligence, yet Vestris documents no usage scale, exclusive data rights, or mechanism making the next customer increase value for prior customers.

Vestris sells through a waitlist, booked demos, direct outreach, and custom pricing by order volume and jurisdiction. Over 18 months, that mechanism compounds labor: every new county requires integration work, every enterprise requires trust and onboarding, and more than 3,700 county systems multiply exceptions faster than code removes them. Vestris’s browser agents are code leverage inside the product, but demo-led enterprise sales and underwriter sign-off remain permissioned gates around the product. The unstated portfolio pattern is missing: no open-source adoption, organic technical audience, creator channel, or free product loop predates the round.

The $18.5 billion title-premium market gives Vestris room in dollars but not yet room in architecture. Vestris could flip this dimension into a high score if the county agents became horizontal infrastructure for tax records, liens, permitting, insurance, and mortgage servicing—several pivots from one reusable data layer. The dossier instead shows coverage only in Washington, D.C. and Montgomery County, while Qualia, Snapdocs, Endpoint, and Dono already occupy adjacent surfaces. A large industry is not the same as a large maneuvering space. Geography can become a thousand niches wearing one TAM.

**Notion** is the useful parallel for the 10-year-partner test: Ivan Zhao kept rebuilding because the product was a long-running personal pursuit, not a market memo. Vestris offers partial evidence in Valliani and Tang’s prior working relationship, Valliani’s patents, and their willingness to leave Michigan, which reads as intelligence and energy. Vestris offers little legible evidence on the integrity leg or how the founders handle long-term partners, so silence earns the neutral default rather than suspicion. No founder red flag appears, but no decade-grade proof appears either.

Washington, D.C. and Montgomery County coverage predicts Vestris’s next 36 months: by July 2029, Vestris will pivot from an autonomous contract-to-closing platform into a narrower county-record and Schedule B review copilot sold through incumbent title systems, reaching $1–3 million ARR without becoming an independent closing network. Vestris’s specific workflow knowledge should locate a valuable wedge; Vestris’s weak distribution and absent network should prevent the wedge from becoming a permissionless platform. Useful SaaS is a plausible outcome. A 10,000x architecture is not yet visible.

Vestris’s strongest bull case is that browser-operating agents finally turn 3,700 fragmented county systems from a burden into a proprietary data flywheel, while decades of approved and rejected titles make Schedule B automation difficult to copy. **Perplexity** proves I can waive the network requirement when specific knowledge, code distribution, and category timing are exceptional. The case fails today because Vestris does not disclose ownership or exclusivity of that training set, still requires enterprise demos and underwriter approval, and has no customers or repeat usage demonstrating cross-jurisdiction learning. I would flip to Invest if Vestris verifiably deployed across at least 50 jurisdictions and acquired a majority of new title firms through inbound referrals or self-serve adoption rather than direct sales.
