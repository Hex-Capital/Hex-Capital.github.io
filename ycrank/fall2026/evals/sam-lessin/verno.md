# Verno -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Verno could save an EPC from ordering the wrong equipment after a drawing changes. That is an understandable reason to buy software. But what am I buying as an investor? The dossier supplies no proposed post-money, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is the first scarce check. I cannot call the round an auction merely because Verno is in YC. I also cannot underwrite cheap optionality without a price. Those are missing deal facts, and my money stays in my pocket.

The strongest case starts with the people connecting engineering operations to document automation. Oliver Wesche built ML systems at Blue Origin and generative AI at Asurion; Ujjawal Madan built document-extraction and voice systems at Asurion and Promise. Rohit Shinde adds more than a decade in petrochemicals and EPC consulting ([team background](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Wil-verno-ai-that-edits-and-checks-the-engineering-documents-behind-every-industrial-plant)). This fits my translation-layer pattern: people with operating experience connecting systems that do not communicate reliably. The founders have built relevant technology, and the team has industrial context. The remaining gap is evidence that they have already operated this particular bridge at scale.

There is also customer action here. A July 2026 recruiting post described an EPC design partnership processing actual project drawings; November launch material reports live customer deployments. Those facts matter more than 618 LinkedIn followers or a pitch competition. Editing native CAD files and propagating accepted changes could place Verno inside a consequential workflow. That is the bull case that makes passing uncomfortable: customers may be granting access to precisely the messy, private engineering context that a generic model cannot obtain. But access to customer drawings does not establish ownership of a proprietary data advantage, repeat usage, or paid conversion. The evidence supports an early foothold.

The economic question is whether that foothold produces cash after the checking work. Verno requires engineer acceptance, which is sensible in an environment where one incorrect edit can contaminate connected deliverables ([product FAQ](https://www.verno-ai.com/faq)). It also means automation savings must be measured after review and correction. Autodesk already offers P&ID authoring and configurable validation; Verno needs to earn its separate invoice through the complete change workflow. There is no disclosed revenue, margin, burn, or break-even plan. I see neither proof of uneconomic operation nor evidence for overriding my financing gates. My exceptions for demonstrated conversion, retention, or unusually strong operating receipts do not apply on this record.

I pass at **37/100**. Verno has credible builders and an industrial deployment foothold, but no disclosed capital plan names the hypothesis, deadline, and pass/fail result this round purchases. I will not invent that venture moment for them. One verifiable condition would reopen diligence: the existing EPC partner signs a paid renewal after a full project cycle with documented positive contribution after onboarding, engineering support, and correction costs. That would show that embedded access is becoming a business. Price and the purpose of the round would still need underwriting before I wrote a check.
