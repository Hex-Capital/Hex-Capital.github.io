# hardware intelligence -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Athreya Anand built agentic workflows at Google while Rishov Sarkar built AMD simulators and Siemens EDA compiler flows. That pairing makes **hardware intelligence** a high-consequence bottleneck bet trapped inside a low-capital software profile. At maximum ambition, **hardware intelligence** could replace substantial verification labor and shorten the feedback loop governing every advanced chip. The economic mechanism that breaks is expert-hours-per-design iteration. Yet Wave currently addresses waveform debugging inside a $17.53B EDA market, not semiconductor design as a whole. The consequence is potentially large, but the declared wedge remains an optimization play.

**OpenAI** (2019) is the high rung on consequence because automating cognition alters a civilizational variable, not merely a workflow. **hardware intelligence** shares the important mechanism: Wave’s agent traces faulty signals, proposes code fixes, evaluates power-performance trade-offs, and reruns simulations—work performed today by scarce verification engineers after simulations lasting hours or days. But **hardware intelligence** diverges where the analogy matters most. OpenAI aimed at general intelligence; Wave launches as a terminal-native debugger competing with Verdi. A $42.85B projected EDA market in 2035 is meaningful, but a better interface to one portion of EDA does not yet make an incumbent system disappear. Consequence over probability only works when the consequence is unmistakable.

**DoorDash** (2013) exposed the low-capital mismatch in my framework: a company can become enormous without requiring science-scale checks, leaving my capital depth irrelevant at entry. **hardware intelligence** shows the same mismatch without DoorDash’s demonstrated network-effect mechanism. Pilot onboarding, booked demos, and two software founders imply conventional enterprise-software economics; **hardware intelligence** names no fabrication facility, proprietary model-training program, or deployment milestone requiring more than $50M. This is not Commonwealth Fusion’s $115M Series A or Genesis AI’s $105M seed. DoorDash became a known exception because consumer-commerce flow compounded into a $72B outcome. Wave has no comparable distribution flywheel yet.

Synopsys introduced an LLM-based Copilot in 2023, while ChipAgents has raised $74M and reports collaborations with NVIDIA and Ambiq. Over the next 18 months, **hardware intelligence** must make verification reliability compound into proprietary advantage: each simulation, root-cause trace, accepted fix, and waveform comparison must improve later diagnoses without leaking customer RTL. Instead, customer-data restrictions may prevent retention, and incumbent integrations may keep the useful data inside Synopsys, Cadence, or Siemens. **hardware intelligence** has genuine technical work—multi-gigabyte waveform reasoning, simulator orchestration, and safe resimulation—but not a named scientific miracle. If customers cannot permit cross-design learning, Wave remains an agentic interface built on commodity models.

Rishov Sarkar is a Georgia Tech ECE Ph.D. who developed AMD AI Engine simulators, Siemens EDA LLVM flows, and LightningSim. That is exceptional founder-market fit, but it is expert continuity—the precise red flag my TED argument identifies when experts extrapolate their existing field. **hardware intelligence** would flip this dimension if Sarkar published evidence that incumbent waveform-centric verification is fundamentally wrong, or if Anand documented a rejected thesis that led **hardware intelligence** from generic agents to a technically distinct verification architecture. No such defiance appears in the dossier. Credentials are useful; controversial insight is investable. McKinsey forecast fewer than one million U.S. cellphones by 2000. The actual number was 109 million.

Wave launched in July 2026 with pilot onboarding open, but no prior pivot or thesis update is documented. **Slash** earned an exception in my portfolio because the Kanye West pivot exposed learning rate under pressure; the pivot, not fintech fashion, carried the signal. **hardware intelligence** provides no equivalent evidence—no “we were wrong,” abandoned architecture, customer-driven repositioning, or prior exit—so I apply the absent-evidence default rather than penalizing a Summer 2026 company for being young. Anand and Sarkar’s decade-long relationship reduces founder-fracture risk, but **hardware intelligence** has not yet demonstrated that its learning rate exceeds its technical résumé.

ChipAgents’ $74M financing validates demand while making the likely 36-month outcome more specific: **hardware intelligence** reaches $3M-$7M ARR through verification pilots and is acquired by Siemens EDA for under $150M, with Wave absorbed into an incumbent debugging suite rather than becoming an independent EDA platform. **hardware intelligence** has a credible wedge, and Sarkar’s Siemens tooling history makes integration legible. The same mechanism caps the outcome: customers want agentic debugging inside trusted simulation environments, while the incumbents own licenses, repositories, and deployment channels. Useful product. Plausible acquisition. Not yet a category collapse.

Synopsys’ six-figure-per-seat workflows create the strongest opposing case: **hardware intelligence** could be the **Glean** exception, where a domain expert uses a narrow enterprise-search wedge to attack a much larger information bottleneck. The bull mechanism is terminal-native adoption from individual verification engineers, expanding from waveform diagnosis into autonomous RTL repair while expensive incumbents remain trapped in GUI and license-server architecture. ChipAgents’ $74M raise proves investors believe agentic chip design can support a large platform, and Anand plus Sarkar fit my unstated preference for founders with recognized technical reputations. The evidence that wins today is harsher: **hardware intelligence** reports no revenue, named pilot, benchmark, moat, accumulated debugging dataset, or technical architecture that Synopsys cannot bundle. I would flip to Invest if **hardware intelligence** publishes an independent benchmark showing at least a 5x reduction in verification time with no missed injected defects across three named semiconductor customers.
