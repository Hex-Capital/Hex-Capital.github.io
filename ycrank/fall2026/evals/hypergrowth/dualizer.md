# Dualizer -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** The first buyer is a process engineering team that repeatedly reconciles plant drawings, operating data, and simulations to investigate bottlenecks. Dualizer solicits introductions and offers to model a prospect’s process in a demo. The memo reports several active industry pilots with undisclosed buyers and payment terms. The pilots are an early signal that buyers will test the workflow. The memo provides no paid conversion, repeat use, or evidence that the pilots came through the advertised sales route. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Facts)

**Scenario assumption.** If one calibrated model becomes part of a plant’s recurring bottleneck analysis, the account could grow through additional units or sites. The same engineering job could recur abroad. Portability would depend on whether Dualizer can reuse its modeling and approval workflow while adapting to each plant’s drawings, operating data, and equipment models. The memo gives no second-market buyer evidence or rollout plan. At this stage, a paid rollout that measures the work required to get live would be more informative than a conversation. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Each sale could become a custom engineering project. Earlier EntroPINN tests failed to generalize, Dualizer’s published calibration demo uses synthetic histories, and the company is hiring a forward-deployed engineer to handle site adaptations. If each new plant requires fresh data mapping, custom unit modeling, and hands-on validation, deployment and support labor could rise in proportion to subscription revenue. The memo gives no deployment cost breakdown or measured reuse across sites, so the structural-cost Pass gate remains unresolved. (Key Risks; Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent plant in a second geography buy through the same sales process, go live on real plant data using the same deployment workflow, and return to the model for recurring bottleneck decisions? A paid rollout with usage records and a breakdown of engineering hours, support, and compute costs against subscription revenue would answer it.
