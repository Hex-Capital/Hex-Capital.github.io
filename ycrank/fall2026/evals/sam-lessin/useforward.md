# Forward -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Forward reports more than $2 million raised, but I cannot price the check: post-money valuation, competing offers, and remaining capital need are undisclosed. That is missing deal evidence, not proof of an auction. The $100,000 equity-free grant makes the next question sharper: what uncertainty requires my equity capital? The dossier identifies no funded experiment with a deadline and a measurable result. A functioning credit business can still be an investment I decline at an unknown price.

The interesting asset is access to capital providers. Forward sits between debt advisors holding borrower information and funds holding mandates and undeployed capital. That fits my preference for businesses translating between fragmented systems. Fidelity Bank, Hudson Cove Capital Management, and iPartners are credible named relationships; 50+ onboarded funds suggest the founders can get through doors. But neither announced partnerships nor onboarding establishes that Forward owns access that causes a purchase. The prospective dark pool—knowledge of which lender actually closes which deal—becomes valuable through repeated execution. Its existence remains unproven here. Finitive already operates in private-credit matching, so assembling a marketplace is not itself the scarce insight.

The strongest argument against passing is the investor-reported $180,000 annualised revenue within two months. Combined with founder-reported $300 million in processed deals, this could mean Forward has found paying demand unusually quickly. I take that seriously at pre-seed. But “processed” does not establish funded volume, annualised revenue does not establish recurring revenue, and the unspecified 500% weekly growth adds nothing underwritable. The documented exceptions to my gates depend on demonstrated conversion, retention, or unusually strong external validation. Forward supplies an encouraging revenue observation without that supporting chain. It earns some economics credit; it does not establish a path to profitability without another round.

The founders strengthen the case more than their credentials alone would. Riley Janjis worked on $1 billion-plus of credit diligence, and the separate tutoring business reports six-figure annual revenue and 100+ clients. Those are operating receipts. Daniel Yoon’s private-credit experience adds relevant context, while Darryl Luo’s former CTO role supplies technical background without documented deployment results. This team has partial evidence of the outsider-insider bridge I want. Still, the software must improve execution enough to retain the relationship: document automation faces adjacent competition from F2 and Heron, and Forward’s own terms acknowledge incomplete lender coverage and input-dependent matching. Nothing establishes that AI errors disappear or that regulatory boundaries are being breached; neither assumption belongs in the underwriting.

I pass with my own money today. Forward may become a useful, profitable credit intermediary, but the dossier does not connect its early activity to a capital-funded truth or durable cash generation. My single verifiable reopening condition is a written round proposal below a $10 million post-money that funds one dated experiment: demonstrate repeat paid placements with positive contribution profit within 90 days, with the existing capital sufficient to finish the test. That would make the price and the purpose of my check assessable.
