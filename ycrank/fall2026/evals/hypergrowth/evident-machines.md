# Evident Machines -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Repeatable direct sales is the strongest plausible path. Frontier AI labs could buy a standard evaluation package to rerun electrical-engineering tasks as their models change. The company reports that models averaged a 29.3% pass rate on 10 physical tasks they passed at 100% in simulation. That result illustrates what a physical benchmark could reveal. The memo provides no independent buyer validation. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The job plausibly travels across markets. The growth scenario assumes Evident Machines can run the same task library from its existing lab and deliver results that buyers can use without substantial changes to equipment or evaluation workflows. The benchmark is newly launched after a pivot. The first unproven link is whether an independent lab will pay to adopt the existing package. The public contributor call recruits task authors, and the memo records no buyer acquisition, paid onboarding, repeat use, or budget commitment. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Even with buyer interest, each lab could require custom experiments and recurring bench labor. The company describes operators handling wiring and probe placement, and the memo documents Anthropic working with Diode on overlapping evaluations. If buyers prefer tailored work, Evident Machines could face both a credible substitute and delivery costs that rise with each account. The memo gives no measured labor or rollout costs to establish that pattern, so the structural downside gate does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
