# Streamline Systems -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a drone manufacturer is the strongest supported path. Streamline’s asset is a retrofit navigation module for existing drones. A manufacturer could pay to own the module if controlling its integration and supply across multiple platforms were worth more than buying modules under a supply agreement. That ownership premium is an inference. Streamline reports an autonomous flight demonstration without GPS and compatibility with existing platforms. The memo identifies no independent flight-test report as of November 1, 2026, and provides no buyer-side estimate of the value of ownership. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The operating milestone is independent production qualification on a customer platform followed by a paid repeat order. Streamline reports more than $80,000 in contracted revenue and deposits reserving more than $87,000 worth of modules. The deposit amount and overlap are undisclosed, so the figures cannot be added. The memo records $500,000 in financing and a company target of Q1 2027 for availability. These support a plausible seed-stage path to qualification, with the test budget, burn, shipments, and cash collection still unknown. The memo also leaves ownership and transferability of the core IP and any required consents unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The illustrative holder invests $500,000 at a $10 million post-money valuation, starts at 5% ownership, dilutes to 3%, and makes no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership. At 2% ownership the hurdle rises to $125 million, and at 4% it falls to $62.5 million. With $5 million of illustrative net debt, the 3% hurdle is about $88.3 million in enterprise value before any fees or senior claims. A $25 million sale in year eight yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), while a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million gross (5.7×). The memo supplies neither actual holder terms nor buyer economics or transaction evidence to support either price.

If growth plateaus, buyers stay absent, public scale remains out of reach, and the company reinvests all available cash, eight years of private operation yield $0 realized while the holder retains the shares. Wind-down proceeds would be limited to the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo gives no basis to estimate. The listed financing does not establish an eligible secondary, and the memo supplies no evidence of sponsor-buyout cash flow, IPO resale readiness, or funded dividends or buybacks. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Even if the module qualifies, manufacturers could keep purchasing it or source from established navigation suppliers such as ANELLO Photonics or Advanced Navigation. The disclosed reservations are concentrated among companies competing for one procurement program, so delayed or lost awards could weaken repeat demand before owning the technology becomes attractive. The memo establishes credible sourcing alternatives but no confirmed drop-in substitute, structural closing barrier, or supported sale-price ceiling that would trigger Pass. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

After independent production qualification and a paid repeat order, what would a drone manufacturer pay to own Streamline instead of continuing to buy its modules? A buyer-side estimate of the incremental value of ownership that supports a sale price clearing the holder’s cash hurdle within eight years would move the action to Reach out. Evidence of demand for modules alone would keep it at Watch.
