# Phone Harness -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Phone Harness makes it easier for developers to reach applications that have no usable API. That is useful. What I cannot establish is why my money is scarce here: the dossier supplies no proposed post-money, competing-offer count, or evidence that this is the first otherwise-unavailable check. I will not infer an auction from YC membership. But I cannot underwrite a cheap entry without a price, and there is no stated capital plan identifying the experiment this round would fund.

Shawn Pana is the strongest reason to keep looking. He previously led growth at Browser Use, including product launches and developer partnerships, and has now shipped a mobile-control product with a publicly available cloud beta. This fits the translation-layer pattern I like: connecting agent developers to phone workflows through practical infrastructure. The iPhone Mirroring approach removes Xcode and phone-side installation from personal-device setup. Those are concrete execution receipts, although they do not yet establish that Pana can operate a reliable commercial device fleet at scale.

The serious bull case is that removing setup friction wins the developer relationship before anyone realizes that relationship is the valuable asset. Approximately 4,000 GitHub stars and 403 forks give that argument substance. Free personal use could lead developers into paid hosted execution, where persistent logins and dedicated configurations could make recurring workflows expensive to move. If that happens, Phone Harness could own the operating relationship while competing models supply interchangeable intelligence. Dismissing this solely because revenue is undisclosed at pre-seed would miss the mechanism.

But the dossier has not demonstrated that conversion. Stars and forks do not establish paid workloads, retained workflows, or proprietary access. MIT licensing lets developers take the control layer elsewhere, and Droidrun/Mobilerun already offers competing infrastructure. The reported September 16 telemetry issue—covering scripts, OCR output, and unredacted typing arguments—also matters specifically to this thesis: customers must trust the service before entrusting it with persistent authenticated workflows. Current remediation is unverified. The exceptional cases that could override my missing financing gates involved paid enterprise demand, measured conversion and retention, or a concrete alternative financing structure. Phone Harness supplies none of those receipts yet.

At $0.05 per cloud phone-minute, the company has a posted price, not demonstrated unit economics. Device costs, utilization, support burden, gross margin, and burn are undisclosed, so I cannot establish a route to a business that survives without another round. Nor should I assume the economics fail: supplying controls to external agents does not itself require perfect autonomous task completion. The relevant constraint is which workflows customers repeatedly pay to complete despite unsupported camera, Face ID, and multitouch flows. A financing plan could test that question, but no funded milestone, metric, or deadline is documented.

I pass on the current evidence. My verifiable condition for reopening is a cohort of five unrelated customers renewing dedicated-phone workloads after 90 days at positive contribution margin, including support and human intervention costs. That would show that the developer attention becomes an embedded commercial relationship. I would then have something concrete to price; today, the founder has shipped more convincingly than the investment has been defined.
