# Alkera AI -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a data engineer telling an agent to change a revenue pipeline, then going home without wondering whether tomorrow’s dashboard will quietly redefine revenue. Alkera AI is working on something that future needs: understanding what a change will break. But getting there requires both reliable dependency checks and organizations trusting agents with recurring production decisions. The dossier establishes an approach to the first transition. It does not yet show the second beginning. That distinction determines whether I am buying an enabling company or another useful developer tool.

The most compelling detail is Alkera’s combination of column-level lineage, graph-based planning, and sandboxed change testing. A model can write valid code that produces the wrong business answer; knowing the consequences is a separate engineering problem. This fits my pattern of looking beneath a fashionable application for the machinery it needs. However, Altimate publishes deterministic validation and overlapping context capabilities, while Hex offers governed analytics and inspectable lineage. Alkera controls an implementation, but the dossier does not establish control over a scarce dependency. Apache-2.0 distribution could help adoption; it does not establish ownership of the bottleneck.

Gao, Li, and Tran have more relevant preparation than their Yale credentials alone suggest. Financial ingestion pipelines, distributed-compute tooling, production agent frameworks, and computational research put them close to the actual failure modes. Their shared university and laboratory history also gives the partnership some substance. What I cannot reconstruct is the obsession before the company: repeated independent experiments, a dated sequence of builds, or recruiting that demonstrates someone else followed an unusually specific conviction. I give the founders credit for relevant work without turning their employers into evidence of lifelong determination.

Why does a person still have to babysit a transformation that executes successfully? Because execution is a poor substitute for preserving meaning. Alkera identifies that operating failure concretely, along with the friction of coordinating remote experiments through SSH. Returning that attention to engineers matters. Yet the dossier identifies no quantified toll, artificial restriction, or gatekeeper whose economics Alkera overturns. The $50 Plus and $200 Pro subscriptions tell me what access costs, not what customers have recovered. Likewise, Product Hunt’s October 7 fourth-place finish and the repository’s 13 stars establish discovery, not repeated dependence. A working artifact exists; costly user behavior remains unestablished.

The strongest objection to passing is that reliability infrastructure can become essential before its market looks impressive. Alkera reports an 83.28% benchmark pass rate against Claude Code’s 68.49%, averaged over five runs. If that advantage survives customer-specific definitions and production changes, the team could earn a place underneath many workflows. My pre-revenue exceptions leave room for exactly that possibility, but they require compensating evidence of unusual founder obsession or committed real-world trials. This dossier supplies neither. The external model gateway also makes consent and data custody a concrete diligence issue, although it does not establish a severe privacy violation or dependence on one closed supplier. Gao is identified as CEO; there is no affirmative evidence of a leadership shell game or control deadlock.

I pass with my own money today. Alkera AI has a credible engineering direction, but my six-year conviction would currently come mostly from my imagination. One verifiable result would change that decision: a named customer documenting 90 consecutive days of paid production use in which Alkera safely handles recurring pipeline changes that previously required engineers to supervise a general coding agent. That would demonstrate the organizational transition this bet needs.
