# Egoist Machines -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Erin McGurk’s audience of more than 1 million makes **Egoist Machines** a creator-distributed asset trapped inside a consensus AI startup category: founder-owned distribution is the highest signal, while round mispricing is the lowest. **John Fish / Bookshelved** is the high rung because the audience predated the product and made the person part of the cap table; **Egoist Machines** shares that load-bearing mechanism through McGurk’s following and 14,059 LinkedIn followers. The divergence is commercial legibility: Bookshelved attached a niche audience to a recognizable transaction, while Egoist Machines has a waitlist, 268 company followers, and no evidence that McGurk’s educational audience wants an AI Passport. The asset may be the person, but Egoist Machines has not yet shown that the asset transfers.

**Pinterest** is the low-rung warning on round consensus: the missionary founder and personal-network entry overrode a category whose pricing was already resolving. **Egoist Machines** has no disclosed valuation, round size, visible tier-1 pass, or non-standard instrument, so I apply the 9/30 absent-evidence default. YC plus “user-owned memory across every AI app” sounds perilously close to the hot 2026 deck, with Mem0 already having raised $24 million and incumbents bundling memory. The Pinterest exception does not rescue Egoist Machines yet: McGurk’s audience could substitute for network-sourced edge, but there is no evidence of a discounted entry price or a round inaccessible to the consensus crowd.

The consumer Passport being free for life leaves **Egoist Machines** with a structurally awkward regatta: acquire consumers through McGurk, persuade developers to integrate, then discover what developers will pay. That is at least three financing tacks before the Series A starting line, and **Egoist Machines** discloses neither developer pricing nor a live pilot. The capital-structure score flips into Invest territory if the free passport generates a proprietary consent graph and two named developers pay for access before another round; absent that, this looks like a bootstrappable privacy utility being force-fit into a generic SAFE. Creator distribution supplies a modest downside cushion, but a revenue-share or creator-holdco instrument would fit the present asset better than conventional software equity.

David Khachaturov’s Cambridge PhD, OpenAI PaperBench work, BetterJoy repository with 4.7K stars, and 178 LodeDB commits give **Egoist Machines** more pre-pitch conviction than a sanitized agent wrapper. The single-anchor parallel is **Drop.io**: a technical primitive built against free incumbents can work when the founder keeps turning the crank and makes simplicity the product. Egoist Machines has that technical persistence in Khachaturov and a clear CEO in McGurk, avoiding the unresolved-authority red flag. Egoist Machines still lacks three years of category writing or evidence that AI Passport itself—not adjacent LodeDB—has been the founders’ sustained obsession, so the missionary signal is real but incomplete.

Mem0’s $24 million Series A shows how memory infrastructure compounds over 18 months: integrations produce usage, usage improves the memory layer, and the installed layer attracts more integrations. **Egoist Machines** currently stands outside that loop with no named developer customer, no retention count, and only an asserted 800-plus connector sources. Egoist Machines can use McGurk’s audience to seed one side, but OpenAI and Google control the surfaces where portable memory must be consumed. My unstated creator-infrastructure pattern helps; my explicit “AI is just more internet” aversion hurts more. Without live interoperability, receipts and revocable passes are product features, not a sleeve-defining economic network.

The July 13, 2026 waitlist launch points to a specific 36-month outcome: by July 2029, **Egoist Machines** will have pivoted from a free universal consumer passport into B2B consent and identity infrastructure for AI applications, reaching $1 million–$3 million ARR rather than becoming the default cross-assistant consumer identity. Egoist Machines can cheaply recruit early users through McGurk, but developer monetization and sensitive-data compliance will pull the business toward enterprise APIs. The free passport becomes the Wizard-of-Oz data-acquisition wedge; the paid asset becomes permissioning, audit receipts, and regulated context exchange.

Erin McGurk’s 1 million-plus reach and Khachaturov’s AI-security background form the strongest case against that constrained forecast: **Egoist Machines** could become the neutral identity layer precisely because OpenAI and Google cannot credibly govern data moving between competitors. Category-scoped access, revocation, read receipts, and no storage of Google user data give Egoist Machines a trust architecture incumbents may struggle to copy politically, while the creator channel solves consumer cold start. That bull case stings, but no named integration, switching-cost evidence, Passport retention, or developer pricing shows the neutrality thesis clearing market structure rather than merely describing it. Three live assistant integrations plus 100,000 activated passports with 30% 90-day retention by July 2027 would flip my verdict to Invest; without those, I will not pay a consensus AI price for an unproven protocol.
