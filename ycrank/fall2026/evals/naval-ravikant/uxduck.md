# UXDuck -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Ben Butterworth has acquired specific knowledge by building the things UXDuck now sells the promise of simplifying: mission-planning software, MAVLink integrations, controller interfaces and command-and-control applications. His public runtime makes that experience inspectable. The prior employer’s 1,000-plus drones are not UXDuck deployments, but his work there matters as founder evidence. This is a credible builder working inside his accumulated knowledge. Nothing in the dossier establishes an integrity or artificial-deadline red flag.

The difficulty is what remains after another capable builder copies the product. UXDuck’s Apache-2.0 runtime connects vehicles over serial, UDP and TCP, supports ArduPilot/PX4 simulation, and exposes MCP and plugin interfaces. Useful engineering. Available engineering. QGroundControl and Mission Planner already cover substantial parts of the workflow, with extensibility of their own. Connecting to hardware does not give UXDuck ownership of a hardware advantage. The dossier identifies no proprietary model, exclusive operating data or established integration lock-in.

The strongest argument against passing is that Butterworth could make UXDuck the place where drone developers contribute reusable capabilities. A plugin written for one operator could eliminate integration work for another. That fits my preference for permissionless contribution and tools that turn individual knowledge into scalable output. The browser simulation trial gives developers somewhere to begin; the November 14 hackathon gives them a reason to build. But those are mechanisms for starting an ecosystem. Five repository stars, zero forks and a scheduled event do not establish that one participant has yet created value for another.

I can make an exception for an exceptional builder with a working prototype before revenue or a visible network exists. UXDuck deserves that consideration. The unresolved question is whether this founder’s scarce knowledge becomes a shared asset or remains work he performs separately for each customer. The market does offer room: developers could lead into professional mission operations using the same vehicle connections. Auterion’s reported approximately $115 million annual revenue demonstrates spending around integrated drone software, but neither that figure nor the broader $11.33 billion market estimate establishes UXDuck’s reachable adjacent demand. Pricing and realized revenue remain unknown, earning the pre-seed default rather than an additional penalty.

I pass today. My concern is that UXDuck becomes a useful engineering tool whose support obligations grow faster than its accumulated advantage. The founder makes that outcome far from inevitable; the current product does not yet escape it. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: three unrelated paying operators repeatedly reusing the same independently authored plugin, with deployment records showing at least a 50% reduction in integration time without Butterworth doing the integration. That would show other people’s work beginning to compound inside UXDuck.
