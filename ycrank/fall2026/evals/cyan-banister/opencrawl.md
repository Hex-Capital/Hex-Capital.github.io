# OpenCrawl -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine an agent saying, “Keep this corner of the world searchable for me,” and getting fresh, permission-aware information without a human maintaining five separate systems. OpenCrawl proposes much of that machinery: source discovery, hosted crawlers, processing and maintained indexes. But imagining that future is my contribution. The dossier supplies no observed transition showing developers handing this responsibility to agents. MCP makes the connection easier; it does not establish the changed behavior that would make me reach for my checkbook.

The founders are the strongest reason to keep looking. Shalin Patel built transcript-ingestion infrastructure at Pocus; Deep Utkarsh worked on a query compiler at Sigma, including work associated with a 2022 VLDB paper, and built GTM agents at Pocus. They have encountered the plumbing from both ends. Their shared workplace and shipped projects give me more than impressive employers. What I cannot establish is a sustained, independently pursued obsession with this particular problem or evidence that they can recruit others into it. The apparent change from OnePatch to OpenCrawl is a question about what they learned, not proof that they are chasing fashion.

The uncomfortable bull case is that their previous product already forced them to pay some operating tuition. The founders report **32 TB of telemetry ingested and more than 600 incidents detected or investigated** at OnePatch. That could make this team unusually alert to the failures hidden behind a tidy retrieval API. Those figures belong to their engineering history, however, not OpenCrawl adoption. My pre-revenue investment in **CosmicBrain AI** shows why missing revenue need not stop me: the shared mechanism is technically experienced founders building enabling software. The material difference is its functioning prototype and signed industrial proofs of concept. OpenCrawl has no comparable documented customer commitment here.

Why does a developer need another search provider? The promising answer is that developers want ownership of a particular information universe without becoming its maintenance staff. That would return time and control. Yet the dossier does not identify a concrete access restriction, fee or institutional gatekeeper that OpenCrawl removes. Its **$25 starter grant and $10 monthly allowance** make experimentation accessible; they establish neither customer savings nor willingness to pay. I want to see what someone stops doing once this product works.

OpenCrawl does fit my pattern of looking beneath the visible application wave. Crawlers and maintained indexes are useful infrastructure, and this is more substantial than merely putting a chat box on a model. But Firecrawl’s **September 2026 Alexandria release** already combines source discovery, connectors and indexes. Bespoke pipelines could become valuable customer assets; the dossier does not demonstrate control of a scarce dependency. Nor does it show repeated OpenCrawl usage. On my privacy gate, the company’s stated workspace and source-access checks are encouraging intent, but insufficient evidence to clear custody and permission enforcement. There is no documented severe violation, founder-control conflict or dependence on a single closed model supplier to justify treating those red flags as established.

I pass with my own money today. The founders’ relevant building history is stronger than the evidence that OpenCrawl opens a distinctive future or changes a user’s behavior. My one verifiable flip condition is a referenceable customer using a permission-preserving OpenCrawl index in production for 90 consecutive days after retiring its own crawler-and-index maintenance workflow. That single deployment would let me inspect the artifact, the agency returned and the reason this infrastructure deserves to exist.
