# Async -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

$7.6B of global AI-agent spend growing at 49.6% makes Async a large-pivot-room business with almost no network compounding. Async can move among legal, healthcare, real estate, billing, and fundraising workflows, but Clifton Law does not make Async more valuable to the Law Office of Joshua Lowenthal automatically. The market is broad. The architecture is narrow. Async has optionality in where it sells, not yet leverage in how value accumulates.

Uber is the high rung for pivot-room: black cars became UberX because the underlying market was transportation, not limousines. Async shares the useful part of that mechanism. Ben Smith and Connor Park have already moved from nonprofit fundraising agents to Skope billing, then legal agents, then Async across service industries. Async diverges because Uber’s adjacency was validated by marketplace liquidity, while Async’s only named deployments remain two law firms and the cited $7.6B TAM describes an entire category rather than Async’s reachable market. Async sits above a vertical SaaS niche, but below a platform whose expansion path is already encoded in the product.

Perplexity’s single-sided product is the low rung that survived without a marketplace because Aravind Srinivas brought exceptional retrieval knowledge and permissionless product distribution. Async also lacks direct user-added value, but Async lacks Perplexity’s compensating signals: every workflow begins with an audit, each expansion needs a new agreement, and no retention, proprietary-data, or deployment-volume evidence shows that one customer improves the next. Async may accumulate customer-specific memory and action history, but private switching costs are not a network. A database becomes leverage only when learning escapes the account boundary.

Async’s contact-form-to-audit-to-custom-deployment motion compounds labor faster than code over the next 18 months. Each Async engagement requires scoping, integrations, approval logic, maintenance, and outcome ownership; meanwhile Gumloop, Relevance AI, and Anthropic can spread reusable capabilities across their entire installed bases. Code hidden behind consulting is still labor leverage at the distribution layer. Async can reverse that geometry only if audits become templates, templates become self-serve products, and customers arrive without founder-mediated selling. No open-source adoption, organic technical media, or product-led channel is visible today.

Connor Park’s computer-science degree and one-star `founders-by-university` repository do not establish non-trainable specific knowledge, while Ben Smith’s resale background establishes commercial energy more clearly than domain depth. Async’s repeated shipping—from fundraising agents through Skope to deployed legal workflows—keeps Async above the absent-evidence floor, because iteration is real evidence. The score flips into invest territory if Park has built a proprietary long-horizon execution system that measurably outperforms Claude’s ready-to-run workflows, or if Smith’s operating history inside law firms and clinics proves deeper than the public dossier shows. Today, the artifacts show hustle. They do not yet show knowledge that cannot be hired.

Twitter’s founder signal combined Jack Dorsey shipping with Ev Williams having completed Blogger cleanly; intelligence, energy, and integrity were visible through prior behavior. Async shows energy through multiple pivots and two live law-firm deployments, but Async offers little public evidence for the ten-year-partner test. The homepage’s claim of SOC 2 Type I certification conflicts with Async’s security page saying certification remains in progress. That may be stale copy, but security claims are promises, and promises are where integrity becomes observable. Ben Smith and Connor Park have no disclosed contested history, so the read is unresolved rather than adverse.

Two named law-firm customers anchor my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Async will have pivoted into a legal-focused managed-automation product producing $1M-$3M ARR, rather than becoming a cross-industry agent platform. Async’s broad market gives Async room to search, while its low-network, consultative architecture rewards narrowing into repeatable intake, conflict-check, engagement-letter, drafting, and review workflows. Verticalization improves delivery economics; it does not create the 10,000x geometry I need from a personal angel check.

$2.23B of U.S. AI-agent spend growing at 46.9% supports the strongest bull case: Async has crossed the capability threshold at precisely the right moment, two named customers prove real-world execution, outcome pricing aligns incentives, and repeated pivots show unusual founder velocity. Perplexity proves that a missing network can be waived when permissionless distribution and specific knowledge are overwhelming. The objection fails because Async currently has neither compensating feature: Async sells through audits and new agreements, publishes no technical moat, and competes with Anthropic’s 15 ready-to-run SMB workflows. I would flip to Invest if a verifiable cohort showed that more than half of new Async deployments launch from reusable templates without founder or implementation labor.
