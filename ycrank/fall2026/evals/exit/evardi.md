# Evardi -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an established distribution or outage-management software vendor is Evardi’s strongest supported route. Evardi combines utility topology, meter data, and operating limits to flag unsafe steps before operators approve switching orders. Evardi reports that its software caught 60% of errors that passed manual review in simulation. A vendor could acquire the software to add those checks to its existing operator workflow. The result gives a buyer a reason to test the product, while the memo gives no evidence of buyer interest or willingness to pay. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The next milestone is a paid production pilot that measures the reduction in unsafe switching steps against the utility’s existing workflow and records the cost of integration. Evardi reports deployments at utilities serving more than 10 million customers, but the memo gives no paid contract or revenue evidence, and the published studies used simulations on public data. The pilot would require utility data access, integration staff, operator approval, and security review. The memo leaves the pilot budget, funding, and timeline unverified. A software acquisition is plausible, with IP ownership and the assignment of utility contracts and data rights still to verify. The memo supports no independent payout route. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, The Idea, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The memo supplies no acquisition pricing evidence, so the following figures are sensitivities. Both sale cases assume $10 million in net debt and other deal deductions, no preference claims ahead of the holder, eligibility to sell the full stake, and cash paid at closing within eight years.

| Scenario | Holder proceeds |
|---|---|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value yields $40M in distributable equity and $1.2M in cash to the holder, or 2.4×. |
| Strong sale | $110M enterprise value yields $100M in distributable equity and $3.0M in cash to the holder, or 6.0×. If only 80% of the stake is sold for cash within eight years, realized proceeds fall to $2.4M, or 4.8×. |
| Prolonged private | Continued operations with cash reinvested and no eligible share purchase or funded payout yield $0 in realized proceeds within eight years. |

### Countercase

SMUD reports using meter-based transformer monitoring alongside AspenTech outage management for coordinated switching and restoration. That gives a prospective buyer a credible alternative to acquiring Evardi. The simulated results leave open whether Evardi adds enough safety or operational value over an incumbent stack to justify a purchase. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported price range that falls short under the strongest credible route, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would the safety improvement measured in a paid production pilot justify an acquisition price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A utility-validated comparison against its incumbent workflow, coupled with a strategic buyer’s quantified buy-versus-build assessment, would answer it. Move to Reach out if that assessment supports a path to $2.5 million in holder cash within eight years after deal deductions and payout terms. Stay at Watch while the price remains unsupported, and move to Pass if the strongest credible price and terms leave the holder materially short.
