# GatherHQ -- Paul Graham Evaluation

GatherHQ’s most revealing detail is the outbound engine Mayank built: roughly 15 meetings or opportunities in its first two weeks, according to his May 2026 interview. He is doing the selling himself and building tools to do it better. That is encouraging. But what had stopped working, and what did he change to get around it? The dossier does not establish that sequence. Meetings demonstrate activity; they do not yet demonstrate resourcefulness under resistance. I would leave that criterion at its missing-evidence default rather than substitute his previous exits for an answer.

The product has substantially better evidence. Cover Genius independently credits Gather with research involving 1,392 consumers across seven countries. That is a delivered result involving real operational work: recruiting people, interviewing them, and producing something a customer published. It earns substantial technical-proof credit. Separately, Gather reports more than 65 enterprise marketing teams, including Fortinet, Envoy, and CoStar. This identifies a plausible narrow well: marketers who need buyer evidence for campaigns. It earns demand credit, though the dossier does not show whether those teams return for another study. The reported 10× growth has no defined denominator. A “six-figure day” has no defined accounting meaning. Neither can answer that question.

I am less persuaded by the simulations than by the interviews. Gather itself warns against treating simulations as statistical predictions without supporting evaluation. Its 300,000 reported interviews fit my unstated preference for companies that assemble a difficult underlying resource before announcing a platform. But an accumulated corpus is not automatically a reusable proprietary resource. Customer permissions matter, and the dossier supplies no independent accuracy result for the buyer models. Slack and agent integrations suggest a programmable interface; they do not yet show developers repeatedly building unexpected things with it.

The strongest argument for investing is that these founders may already know considerably more than this public record reveals. Mayank and Anand built companies together, Jay sold their previous product, and their experience includes buyer research and enterprise sales. The usual red flags of newly assembled cofounders, delegated early selling, and dependence on a large distribution partner are not established here. Gather also moved from restaurant and retail feedback toward market research by May 2026. But the observation that caused that change is missing. I cannot distinguish an evidence-driven correction from a change of market description. Likewise, relevant prior employment does not establish the particular frustration that caused them to start Gather. Those two dimensions retain their defaults.

Competition makes the unanswered repeat-use question consequential. Outset already offers interview-grounded digital twins, and Salesforce’s September 2026 agreement to acquire Listen Labs could put overlapping capabilities into existing customer relationships. Gather can still win by learning one marketing workflow more deeply and doing the awkward work customers need. Its published credit prices establish a way to charge, not evidence that customers repeatedly choose it. The decisive distinction is whether customers keep buying fresh research because Gather changes their decisions, or buy an occasional impressive report that another supplier could produce.

I would not write a check yet. The known exceptions in my framework require a working product or validated pull strong enough to replace a missing founder prior. Gather’s independently credited research makes that possibility credible, but it does not yet establish compelling repeat demand or exceptional founder behavior under constraint. My one flip condition is a documented customer episode in which a founder encounters a specific research or integration blocker, ships a workaround within two weeks, and the customer subsequently purchases a second study. That would supply the missing founder evidence and a separate observable demand outcome. This is a neutral judgment with a concrete unresolved question, not evidence that the founders have failed it.
