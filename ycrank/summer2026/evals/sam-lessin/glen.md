# Glen -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Nikos Dritsakos’s $500,000 SalesBop exit and agent-tooling experience at Composio make Glen a founder-conviction bet trapped inside my least-favored sleeve. Glen’s highest dimension is asymmetric founder conviction; Glen’s lowest is category fit because horizontal AI memory is precisely where model platforms, agent vendors, and enterprise-search incumbents can compress a startup into a feature. That pairing describes a capable operator likely to find a business, but not necessarily by preserving the current product or cap table.

**Drop.io** is the high rung on founder conviction: I spent years pushing a file-sharing primitive against free incumbents because I had lived the behavior and would not be talked out of it. Glen has a credible version of that mechanism—Dritsakos previously built AI sales software, managed AI products, and worked on agent tooling at Composio before starting Glen. Glen diverges from the high rung because there is no public writing, GitHub work, or opinionated category artifact demonstrating that organizational memory is a missionary obsession rather than the next adjacent opportunity. The prior exit and domain continuity move Glen materially above the absent-evidence default, but not into overwhelming-conviction territory.

**OpenAI** represents the low-rung failure mechanism on category fit: foundation-model economics make the surrounding seed wrappers commodity exposure unless the wrapper owns distribution, proprietary workflow, or a genuinely illegible asset. AI memory is not investible. That is overstated, but Glen is a horizontal MCP context layer facing Glean at $300 million ARR, Dust with more than $60 million raised, and Unblocked with $30 million raised, while Glen has no demonstrated structural barrier. Glen’s read/write organizational timeline is more thoughtful than a generic chatbot shell, yet it remains software-on-top-of-the-AI-stack rather than pre-consensus crypto, Wizard-of-Oz service automation, creator infrastructure, or another sleeve where I already possess an underwriting edge.

No public round size, valuation, investor roster, or visible tier-1 pass exists for Glen—no evidence, so I am applying the absent-evidence default. Glen is a Summer 2026 YC company using the consensus vocabulary of agents, MCP, shared context, and enterprise productivity; a work-email waitlist does not establish that the SAFE is mispriced. The counterfactual that changes the score is concrete: Glen would need a below-batch-median valuation and evidence that established enterprise-AI investors passed because they considered organizational memory too narrow or too difficult to secure. Without those facts, I cannot manufacture value-seed alpha from an unknown price.

A one-person organization with production deployments, founder-led calls, and zero active job listings gives Glen an initially efficient regatta. Over the next 18 months, Glen’s shared memory can compound through accumulated decisions and tool-use patterns, but enterprise permissions, integrations, retrieval evaluation, and security reviews can compound headcount and sales costs faster than revenue; Glen currently shares learned information organization-wide unless private mode blocks writes. No disclosed pricing or revenue demonstrates a downside cushion, so the likely financing path remains a conventional enterprise-SaaS cap table rather than a bootstrappable business hitting one discrete venture moment.

**John Fish / Bookshelved** had distribution on the cap table before Slow supplied capital; the creator and the acquisition channel were the same asset. Glen has only a weak parallel: Dritsakos has 5,000 LinkedIn followers and more than 500 connections, but Glen has no named newsletter, community, channel, or measurable organic audience. Glen may convert the Composio and AI-operator network into founder-led design partners, yet professional reach is not owned distribution—the company must still earn organizational adoption account by account.

Glean’s $300 million ARR and the adjacent funding of Dust and Unblocked drive my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Glen will have pivoted from horizontal organizational memory into agent permissioning and audit infrastructure for engineering organizations, reaching roughly $1 million–$3 million ARR rather than becoming the general organizational context layer. Glen’s current weakness—everything learned being broadly visible inside an organization—is also the sharper pain with a budget, while Glen’s strongest asset is Dritsakos’s ability to tack from direct experience in agent tooling and SOC 2 work.

MCP support from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and AWS is the bull case that stings: Glen could become the neutral memory layer precisely because every agent client needs shared state, and Dritsakos’s prior exit plus live production onboarding could make Glen a **Pinterest**-style known exception where missionary temperament overrides imperfect category and distribution fit. The dossier does not yet support that override: Glen provides no customer count, repeat-usage measure, revenue, retrieval benchmark, or source-permission parity, while funded incumbents already own enterprise access. I would flip to Invest if Glen showed a cohort of at least 10 paying organizations retained for six months at an entry valuation below $10 million post-money; that would turn the current AI wrapper into a demonstrably mispriced workflow asset.
