# LATO -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

If LATO works, a weeks-long, $300,000 commercial study could become a continuously updated research system. That is a real cost pool, but the dossier never quantifies the resulting cost, the number of studies affected, or a path to a 10x—let alone 100x—change. The consequence case is therefore bounded: better investment diligence, not yet radically cheaper expertise at global scale.

The product combines sourced experts, multilingual voice interviews, fund data, and simulations. The 30-recruiter study proves that LATO can execute an interview workflow. It does not prove that the simulation predicts market behavior. No forecast history, confidence interval, blind comparison, or measured cost and speed advantage exists. AlphaSense already has AI-led calls, 280,000-plus transcripts, and more than $600 million in ARR. Against that incumbent, “fresh interviews plus simulation” is a hypothesis, not a technical discontinuity.

The founders are the strongest part of this option. Tymek Staniszewski spent six years inside the buyer workflow at Verdane; Tien Chu brings eight years of software and AI experience plus published neural-network research. Their 11-year relationship and complementary backgrounds are better than a fashionable pairing of generic AI résumés. Moving from an Excel model checker in April 2026 to research studies by July also shows tactical flexibility, although the dossier does not establish what evidence caused the change or show difficult recruits attracted to own compliance and simulation science.

The bull case is uncomfortable enough to take seriously. Blume Equity and FoodLabs are named customers, research already represents 70% of use cases, and normal product operation could accumulate proprietary interviews and fund-specific knowledge. The relevant exception is Block: an incremental workflow wedge can expand into a much larger infrastructure platform. The material difference is that LATO has not quantified an American Express-scale value pool or shown that its learning loop improves with use; today, the incumbent owns the larger corpus and overlapping workflow.

The largest kill risk is prediction validity, followed closely by expert-call compliance. Yet LATO reports no pass/fail experiment for either: the recruiter demonstration tests interview collection, while SOC 2 and ISO 27001 remain in progress and no MNPI screening process is described. My 36-month base case is that LATO drops “market simulation” as its central claim and becomes an AI-managed expert-research workflow producing $2 million–$8 million ARR. That could be a good company. It is not presently a Black-Swan investment.

I pass today. The one condition that flips me is a prospective, independently auditable benchmark across at least ten investment studies showing that LATO predicts measured market outcomes at equal or better accuracy than conventional research, at no more than one-tenth of the cited $300,000 cost, under a documented expert-compliance process. That single result would convert the simulation from marketing language into a cost-curve discontinuity.
