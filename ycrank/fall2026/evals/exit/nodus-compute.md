# Nodus Compute -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to a GPU infrastructure provider such as Runpod is the strongest supported route. The memo documents owner-first routing and a feature that lets operators lend eligible idle GPUs. A buyer could use that combination to add sellable capacity without reserving every GPU itself. Nodus reports a 7.65× cold-start speedup in a verifier run on one A100, an early signal that faster recovery could make some interrupted workloads more economical. The buyer benefit remains an inference, and the memo provides no evidence of buyer interest. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A paid production pilot across multiple fleets is a plausible next milestone. The founders report that more than 200 teams joined after launch, and the company lists named users. That gives Nodus a starting point for recruiting pilot customers. Paid usage, pilot funding, and the capital and time needed to reach production scale remain unverified. A sale of the orchestration software is conceivable, but the memo leaves IP ownership, contract assignment, and any required operator consents unverified. It identifies no independent route to holder liquidity. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. Published hourly rates establish what Nodus offers to charge. The memo gives no paid volume, margins, or transaction evidence to support an exit price. (The Idea, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Assumed proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $40 million enterprise value less $10 million in debt and deal adjustments leaves $30 million in distributable equity | $900,000 (1.8×) |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value less the same $10 million leaves $90 million in distributable equity | $2.7 million (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Nodus continues operating and reinvests available cash, with no distribution or eligible share purchase within eight years | $0 |

Both sale cases assume an all-cash close within eight years, an eligible holder selling the full 3% stake, and a pro rata waterfall with no additional senior claims, escrow, or earnout. The prices and deductions are illustrative. In the strong case, another $10 million in deductions lowers the holder’s proceeds to $2.4 million (4.8×), below the objective.

### Countercase

Runpod or another GPU provider could assemble enough of the functionality from existing scheduling and pooling tools. Nodus identifies no structural barrier, and its pooling benchmark used synthetic tenants on a single node. Owners can reclaim lent hosts, and community hosts can inspect job data. Those constraints narrow the workloads a buyer could serve and could erase the savings that would justify an acquisition premium. The memo establishes neither a ready substitute for the combined product nor a supported sale-price ceiling below the proceeds hurdle. The Pass gate is therefore unproven. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production pilot across multiple independent fleets show repeatable savings per completed job versus an operator’s existing scheduler, after accounting for host reclaims, retries, and integration costs? Move to Reach out if measured savings across enough eligible workload support a plausible buyer valuation that clears the $83.3 million distributable-equity hurdle after deal deductions. Keep Watch if the pilot leaves those economics unresolved.
