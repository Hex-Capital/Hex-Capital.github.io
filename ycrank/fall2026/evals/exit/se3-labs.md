# SE3 Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A robotics model developer could pay to own SE3’s workcell operations, partner-site access, and rights to reuse correction data if that package materially lowers the cost of getting robot policies to work at new sites. Ownership would matter if it secured reliable access and reuse rights the buyer could only obtain by acquiring SE3. Those economics and rights remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The memo records 500 released teleoperation episodes across 10 benchmark tasks. SE3 reports access to three types of U.S. sites and describes partner-site sandboxes as its next step. Its own Dream-1 evaluation reported zero full-task completions in 100 zero-shot episodes. The test demonstrated that SE3 could capture a failure baseline. The next milestone is a paid partner-site trial showing repeatable improvement on held-out task variations and measuring the cost of achieving it. Total financing, runway, and the trial budget are unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** The memo supplies no investment terms or usable acquisition pricing evidence. The illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3%, with no follow-on checks. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the investor and $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The equity hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

- **Modest sale, year 5.** At an assumed $40 million enterprise value, the holder receives $1.2 million at close, or 2.4× the check.
- **Strong sale, year 7.** At an assumed $100 million enterprise value, the holder receives $3 million at close, or 6×. At 2% ownership, the same sale pays $2 million, or 4×.
- **Prolonged private, through year 8.** Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public-market scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. The investor realizes $0, or 0×, and retains the shares.

Both sale scenarios assume zero net debt and zero other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. The assumed waterfall is pro rata with zero preference deductions. All consideration is cash at close, with zero stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, or compensation. Returns are before investor taxes. The scenarios illustrate the hurdle, and the memo supplies no evidence for an independent route to holder cash. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A robotics developer could build its own benchmarks using RoboDojo, contract with SE3 by project, or buy physical rollouts and recovery data from Enact. If those options deliver comparable results, ownership could command too little to clear the hurdle. Raw recording requires authorization, and reuse depends on contract rights and customer permission. Those constraints also threaten the buyer’s ability to use the asset after closing. The memo leaves substitute performance, transferability, and the price ceiling unresolved, so the structural Pass override does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid partner-site trial show that owning SE3 would generate enough value for a robotics model developer to support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds? A buyer-validated trial would move the action to **Reach out** if it shows repeatable gains on held-out workflows, quantifies the economics against both in-house testing and a service contract, and documents that the site and data rights behind those gains can transfer.
