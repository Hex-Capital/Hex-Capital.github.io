The secret receives the absent-evidence default.

The decisive investment question is whether solving its particular vision problem would create a monopoly. No initial customer segment, exclusive dataset, or measured technical advantage is documented. Roboflow and TwelveLabs establish that substantial adjacent capabilities exist; they do not establish that Perceptual Labs competes directly with either. The two-person team could be organized around an unusually definite technical insight. It could also be exploring a category. Founder identities, shared working history, employment status, ownership, and responsibility for core engineering are all unavailable. None of my founding-structure red flags—part-time commitment, convenience matching, outsourced engineering, or investor-majority ownership—is established. I assign the stated default rather than invent either alignment or dysfunction. Likewise, without a product, price, margins, or expansion path, there is no revenue engine whose eventual magnitude I can underwrite. Missing revenue at pre-seed is ordinary; missing business definition leaves the power-law question unanswered. No buyer, acquisition channel, or delivery economics establishes distribution either.

The strongest objection to passing is the research failure itself: both the website and YC profile were inaccessible after retries on October 29, 2026. Almost the entire apparent emptiness could come from inaccessible sources. Perceptual Labs’ infrastructure classification is compatible with my unstated pattern of investing across the AI production stack, although a category label earns no affirmative credit. My known exceptions would matter if there were an independently verified technical discontinuity, persistent customer transactions, or a concrete deployment path through a difficult coordination problem. This dossier documents none. It also establishes no regulatory shutdown or collapsed technical claim. The proper conclusion is unresolved, not refuted.

I pass on writing a personal check today, while leaving the company open for investigation. One verifiable condition would change that decision: an independently evaluated, paid deployment in a named customer segment that demonstrates a substantial advantage over the customer’s best available alternative and establishes Perceptual Labs’ exclusive rights to the asset producing that advantage. That would provide a concrete reason this particular company should exist—and a starting point for testing whether it can retain the value it creates.
