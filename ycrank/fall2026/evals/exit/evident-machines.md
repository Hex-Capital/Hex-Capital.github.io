# Evident Machines -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a frontier AI lab is the strongest path. A lab could buy Evident Machines for its physical test environments, instrument readings, and expert workflows. The company reported that models passed all 10 benchmark tasks in simulation but averaged a 29.3% pass rate on physical hardware. The memo records the benchmark launch, and the standalone results were unavailable for verification. The reported gap suggests the benchmark can surface failures that simulation misses. The case for ownership would strengthen if a lab showed that ongoing access to the data and workflows materially improves performance on unseen hardware and that licensing would capture less of that benefit. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The launch shows that the team can run a small physical benchmark. A paid lab pilot measuring gains on unseen hardware across repeated runs is a plausible next milestone. Manual wiring and probe placement leave throughput and pilot costs uncertain. The memo’s $125,000 funding entry does not establish runway, and the contributor arrangements leave ownership and transfer rights unverified. The memo also gives no evidence of cash generation for a sponsor buyout or company-funded payout, eligible secondary demand, or operating scale for an IPO. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Under the shared scenario, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target requires $2.5 million in gross cash before investor taxes, or $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership. The equity hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. Using illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% case requires $88.3 million in enterprise value. A $25 million sale in year eight yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), while a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). The memo provides no company-specific sale price, debt, fees, or senior claims to substantiate those outcomes. If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested with no secondary or payout, the holder realizes $0 and retains the shares. Any wind-down recovery would come from the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo does not quantify. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Anthropic and Diode already built overlapping circuit-board training tasks and an internal benchmark. That collaboration gives a frontier lab a credible way to obtain related capabilities through partnership, and Evident’s reported results show a measurement gap without establishing a training gain or a reason to acquire the company. The overlap falls short of establishing a ready substitute for Evident’s physical experiment workflows. The evidence supports Watch because neither a structural block nor a sale-price ceiling that would trigger Pass has been established. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid frontier-lab pilot show repeatable gains on unseen hardware and quantify an advantage from owning the transferable data and workflows over licensing them? Buyer-validated gains and ownership economics that support a sale range around or above the modeled $88.3 million enterprise-value hurdle would move the action to Reach out.
