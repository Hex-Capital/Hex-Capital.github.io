# Bryel -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Bryel appears to have built models for customers. What I cannot find is the incident that tells me how these founders behave when something breaks. A customer’s data was unusable; a training run failed; an incumbent could not solve the task. What did Kevin Miao and Frankie Li do next, and how long did it take? The dossier supplies outcomes without that sequence. Miao’s Apple engineering and BAIR research establish relevant technical experience. Li’s AI-product background adds useful context. Neither establishes resourcefulness under resistance, which is the thing I most need to know before investing my own money.

The strongest evidence is the [October 2026 YC announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/y-combinator_bryel-yc-f26-is-on-a-mission-to-help-train-activity-7513631895663042560-50op): delivered models with classification running 20× faster, search costing 5× less, and agents matching frontier performance at 5–8× lower cost. These are specific claims about useful artifacts. They deserve provisional technical credit. But without tasks, baselines, or measurement methods, I cannot tell whether Bryel solved a difficult problem or selected a favorable comparison. This partially matches the pattern I like of technical founders producing concrete proof before raising money. It does not yet establish the unusually strong proof that would override missing founder evidence.

The best argument for Bryel is its willingness to embed a training team. Building datasets, reward environments, and customer integrations is precisely the tedious work that can conceal an opportunity. Manual delivery could teach the founders what Foundry must automate. The difficulty is that no named user niche emerges from those engagements. Who keeps needing another model, and why? Delivered models establish technical activity; they do not independently establish repeat usage or payment. Revenue is undisclosed, and the proposed service-and-software business model is an inference. Meanwhile, Fireworks already offers specialized-model training and customer ownership. Bryel needs its customer work to uncover a narrower, more urgent problem than that description captures.

I would take the working-product exception seriously. **Dropbox** showed how a compelling prototype could outweigh uncertainty about a newly formed founding team. Bryel shares the possibility that the artifact reveals more than the founders’ public histories. The material difference is that Bryel’s strongest performance evidence remains an uninspectable report. There is also no documented customer pull strong enough to supply the missing conviction. Conversely, I would not invent red flags: soliciting partners does not establish dependence on one, and an undisclosed cofounder history does not mean the relationship was assembled for an application. There is simply no evidence of an evidence-driven route change or a personal collision with this problem, so both receive their missing-evidence defaults.

I would pass today. Bryel has enough technical substance to merit a conversation, but it falls below my founder-action gate, and the product evidence does not justify an exception. One verifiable flip condition would be a dated, customer-confirmed account of a failed deployment that Miao and Li rescued within weeks through a shipped workaround, with a reproducible performance improvement and continued production use. That single account would connect the promising numbers to the founders’ behavior and to someone who actually needs what they built.
