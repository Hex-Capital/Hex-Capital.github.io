# Bryel -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Bryel proposes to leave the customer owning both a specialized model and the software for improving it. That makes the capital-allocation question unusually sharp: what valuable asset does Bryel retain after doing the work? The dossier gives me neither a proposed post-money valuation nor competing-term-sheet data. Both are unknown; YC membership does not establish an auction. It also supplies no round-funded experiment with a measurable result and deadline. I cannot underwrite cheap uncertainty without knowing the price or what the check proves.

The strongest argument for this team is that it has apparently crossed from research into customer delivery. [YC’s October 2026 announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/y-combinator_bryel-yc-f26-is-on-a-mission-to-help-train-activity-7513631895663042560-50op) reports delivered models: classification running 20 times faster, search costing five times less, and agents matching frontier performance at five to eight times lower cost. If those improvements survive production workloads, an embedded training team could turn expensive API consumption into customer-owned infrastructure. That fits my preference for customers owning their workflows. It also creates the uncomfortable possibility that Bryel delivers substantial customer value while capturing only a consulting fee.

Foundry could become the continuing connection between customer feedback, training data, and deployment. That is the translation-layer pattern I look for: technical specialists making an otherwise inaccessible capability usable inside an operating business. But the proprietary data belongs to the customer, and the dossier does not establish durable access, renewals, referrals, or purchases caused by an embedded workflow. A business-email funnel and 186 company followers establish no such mechanism. [Fireworks already offers specialized-model training, ownership, and continued improvement](https://fireworks.ai/news/fireworks-raises-1-5-billion-series-d). Bryel therefore needs evidence that its delivery process compounds into something customers keep buying. Ownership alone does not distinguish it.

Kevin Miao’s Apple engineering experience, BAIR work, and September 2026 research publication establish relevant technical substance. Frankie Li’s product leadership at Fundamental Research Labs, including Shortcut, adds experience translating AI into products. I give the team modest credit above the biography default, but the dossier does not document a scaled enterprise operation or a prior commercial outcome connecting both sides of this particular bridge. The reported customer-model improvements are the stronger objection to passing: these founders may already be solving difficult problems that infrastructure vendors leave unfinished. Nevertheless, the known exceptions to my financing and AI reservations required observed conversion, retention, cash economics, or unusually strong external validation. Unspecified deployments and benchmarks without methodology do not establish that override. Nor does the dossier justify assuming an auction, dysfunctional founder governance, or dependence on another financing.

I pass at **29/100**. This is a judgment about the investment evidence, not a penalty for having two people or little publicity. There is no disclosed revenue, margin, burn, or seed-funded break-even plan, so economics receives the prescribed default; financing and use of proceeds do too. The specific risk is that Bryel’s customer-owned architecture transfers the durable asset outward while leaving the team selling repeated implementation work against well-funded substitutes. My verifiable flip condition is a paid customer renewal that remains contribution-positive after fully charging training compute and delivery labor. That would demonstrate recurring economic capture and reopen underwriting. Round terms would still determine whether I write the check.
