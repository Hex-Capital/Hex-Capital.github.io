# Shielded -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The strongest fact is not the MIT degree or the commodities résumé. It is that Shielded turned messy ERP data into a live product for a restaurant chain covering 200 SKUs within 48 hours, while its other reported deployments finished in under 72 hours. The ordinary process takes weeks. This is a shipped workaround to a real obstacle, but it is still one step short of proving James Simon relentlessly resourceful: the dossier does not identify what broke during an installation, what he personally improvised, or whether the customer kept using the result.

There is plainly a working artifact. Shielded has combined ERP exports, bills of materials, supplier records, market prices, and external events for defense, CPG, restaurant, and manufacturing deployments. That is more than a diagram. But the hard technical claim is not ingestion speed. It is whether the system calculates margin exposure accurately enough to justify an operational change or hedge. There is no back-test, realized outcome, or measured accuracy result. The product has crossed the “works at all” threshold, not the “customers can trust it with money” threshold.

The bull case is that these deployments reveal a narrow well before revenue becomes public. Supply-chain teams currently take weeks to answer questions that tariffs can make urgent in hours, and Simon encountered the underlying propagation problem through commodities work at Balyasny. The integrations are also genuine schlep: inconsistent ERP records, supplier origins, BOMs, volumes, and price data. Competitors with between $74 million and $322 million raised validate the need, while Shielded’s company-specific margin calculation may be a sharper wedge than broad risk monitoring.

But deployments are not yet pull. None of the users is named, no payment or repeat usage is reported, and “completed in under 72 hours” could describe four founder-assisted demos rather than a product that became part of anyone’s workflow. The $1,000 referral offer is an acquisition tactic, not demand evidence. Nor is there evidence that Simon changed the route after encountering contrary data. A founder with the right background can still build an impressive answer to a question customers admire but do not revisit.

The competitive market makes this distinction decisive. Interos, Altana, Prewave, and Everstream already own overlapping data, mapping, and monitoring positions. Shielded’s potential proprietary substrate remains inferred, and recommending hedges without validated outcomes creates a trust problem larger than merely connecting another data source. The unstated pattern I would want—an unusually concrete proof spike before the round—is incomplete. The known exceptions do not rescue it: there is a working product, but neither independently measured technical proof nor unmistakable user pull strong enough to replace the missing founder-response evidence.

I would not write the check yet. Shielded looks like a domain-native founder who has found an ugly wedge and ships quickly, but I cannot yet tell whether he is a running back or merely fast on the first route. My flip condition is one named customer paying for the product and using it for two separate risk events, with a documented forecast-to-realized-margin comparison that caused an operational or hedging decision. That single result would test technical truth, repeat demand, and whether the 48-hour installation creates lasting value.
