# Gradon -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** The strongest initial buyer is a civil engineering firm principal looking to free up engineer time on recurring plan sets, CAD work, and reports. Gradon advertises demos and compensated referrals to principals, a paid pilot on a live project, and unlimited-use named seats. Its Windows agent works in existing tools such as Civil 3D and Revit, using an index of the firm’s past projects and standards. This gives Gradon a concrete route from a first project to more paid seats if other engineers adopt the same workflows. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The CEO reports weekly firm onboarding, mid-six-figure contracted ARR in four weeks, and an unnamed first civil engineering customer using Gradon for plan sets, CAD, and reports. Gradon also reports one project taking 180 hours against a 750-hour manual estimate. These are early signals of buyer commitment and potential value. The memo provides no collections data, referral-to-paid conversion records, or evidence of repeat projects or paid seat expansion. Confidence is low because the rollout mechanism that would drive sustained growth remains untested in the memo. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Second-market scenario.** If civil engineering firms elsewhere use the same desktop tools for similar production work, Gradon could reuse the agent and pilot workflow. The next deployment would still need to incorporate the new firm’s drafting standards and project files, fit its licensed review process, and meet its data-handling requirements. The memo gives no buyer-level evidence from a second geography. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Gradon could sell a valuable pilot while operating a labor-intensive implementation business. The memo describes firm-specific configuration and flags at least a week of on-site work per deployment. Each new firm may add travel, setup, and support. Each additional workload may add AI processing costs for Gradon and licensed review time for the buyer. Under unlimited-use seat pricing, more tasks on an existing seat can raise delivery costs without raising subscription revenue. Reusable setup across firms could improve the economics, but the memo contains no measured rollout costs or contribution data. The Pass gate remains open because the evidence has yet to establish that these costs scale proportionally without a credible improvement path. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a referral-sourced civil engineering firm in a second geography convert a live-project pilot to paid seats and use Gradon on its next project through substantially the same rollout? A paid invoice, repeat-project usage, and a comparison with earlier deployments showing limited localization and lower setup effort after accounting for travel, support, and AI processing would move the action to **Reach out**. A rollout with unresolved repeat use or costs would keep it at **Watch**. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
