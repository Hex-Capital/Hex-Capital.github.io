# Verno -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Verno’s strongest evidence is that its founders have put software against actual engineering drawings. The July 2026 EPC partnership and reported November customer deployments matter more than the pitch competition or university affiliations. Wesche has built ML systems at Blue Origin and Asurion; Madan has built document-extraction systems; Shinde brings a decade-plus in petrochemicals and EPC consulting. There is relevant knowledge here, expressed through work. The remaining uncertainty is how much of that knowledge the founders have already converted into reliable engineering automation.

The decisive question is what Verno owns after another team reproduces the editing capability. Native CAD edits, downstream document checks, and engineer acceptance form a useful workflow. They do not yet establish a durable asset. Project connectors and engineering rules could accumulate into one, but the dossier demonstrates neither exclusive rights nor switching costs. Autodesk already supplies P&ID authoring and configurable validation. Amazon Bedrock processing does not by itself make Verno a thin wrapper; equally, integration complexity does not establish proprietary technology. I assign the absent-evidence prior to defensibility.

The strongest argument against passing is that working inside a live industrial project teaches things a competing coding agent cannot obtain from a prompt. Customer drawings contain exceptions, conventions, and consequences. Verno could turn accepted corrections into a reusable engineering system, with each deployment reducing the work required for the next. That fits my preference for converting individual knowledge into scalable output. But customer-specific learning can also remain customer-specific labor. The dossier supplies no observed transfer between customers, no user contribution loop, and no evidence that Verno may reuse those corrections. My exceptions for unusually capable founders and compelling early prototypes deserve consideration here; the current evidence does not establish exceptional capital efficiency or enough differentiated technical proof to override the missing asset.

The wedge has economic room: EPCs create the documents, and industrial operators must maintain consistency as plants change. The shared capability is propagating an accepted change across connected deliverables. That gives Verno a credible direction beyond a single drafting task, although adjacent spending and expansion demand remain unquantified. Demonstrations on prospects’ drawings are a sensible way to sell this capability. They are not proprietary distribution. Likewise, live deployments establish use, while undisclosed pricing and revenue leave direct value capture at the default. I will not turn a customer site into an assumed paying contract.

I would pass with my own money today. Verno has credible builders addressing expensive mistakes, but its strongest evidence concerns execution while its weakest concerns value compounding across users. Its industrial setting fits my interest in infrastructure bottlenecks; the disclosed advantage remains software and integration. Nothing in the dossier triggers an integrity, artificial-deadline, or founder-behavior veto. The objection is structural. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: a controlled deployment showing that corrections Verno has contractual rights to reuse from earlier customers materially improve accuracy for a new customer without customer-specific engineering. That would demonstrate an asset accumulating beneath the application.
