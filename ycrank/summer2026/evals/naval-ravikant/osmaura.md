# Osmaura -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Yeabkal Abeje’s MIT policy-NLP work and Jity Woldemichael’s decision to leave MIT make Osmaura strongest on founder intelligence and energy; Osmaura is weakest where each law firm remains an isolated customer rather than a node that improves every other customer. Smart founders can build valuable software. Networks build themselves. Osmaura currently looks like capable founders pushing a single-sided legal sales tool uphill.

**Stack Overflow** paired practicing developers with a product they had already lived, shipped, and earned trust around. That made the founders legible as long-term partners before the business was legible. Osmaura shares the shipping energy: customers reportedly log in daily, and Osmaura’s founders abandoned a major-law-firm negotiation after two months rather than let one procurement process consume the company. But internships at AWS and Meta, a Jane Street fellowship, and months of customer interviews are observations, not a ten-year behavioral record. Intelligence and energy read high. Integrity reads clean but largely untested.

**Perplexity’s** weak network mechanics were survivable because exceptional retrieval expertise and permissionless product distribution supplied the asymmetry elsewhere. Osmaura has the same single-sided weakness without the same override: one firm adding its practice groups, prior deals, and private relationships does not make Osmaura better for another firm. Confidentiality may prevent cross-customer learning altogether. Osmaura could accumulate a proprietary conversion dataset, but no outcome volume is disclosed. A possible future data loop is not a network.

Osmaura’s “Book a call” funnel turns code leverage into labor leverage at the distribution boundary. Over the next 18 months, every custom demo, security review, CRM integration, and partner-training cycle adds human work roughly in proportion to revenue; the withdrawn major-firm negotiation already reveals the slope. Osmaura can replicate software at zero marginal cost, but Osmaura cannot yet replicate trust or distribution without permission. Code inside the product does not make the business permissionless.

Abeje’s argument-extraction and policy-prediction research gives Osmaura real technical relevance, while Woldemichael’s infrastructure background can help make noisy public signals reliable. The score flips into Invest territory only if Osmaura demonstrates non-trainable legal-growth knowledge—such as a proprietary signal taxonomy built from repeated matter-origination outcomes that incumbent CRM vendors cannot reproduce. Osmaura currently has months of interviews, no founder history inside a law firm, and GitHub projects with one to three stars. Specific knowledge is obsession made legible. The dossier shows aptitude more clearly than obsession.

**Uber** began with a narrow black-car wedge but sat inside transportation, delivery, logistics, and local commerce—multiple pivots inside one enormous architecture. Osmaura sits inside a $3.8 billion global law-firm CRM proxy, with an implied $1.46 billion North American segment and Nexl, Lawmatics, and Intapp already occupying adjacent workflows. Osmaura could expand into accounting, consulting, or investment banking, but that would require rebuilding customer context and distribution rather than merely turning the same product toward a neighboring demand pool. A niche is acceptable when it is a wedge. Osmaura has not yet shown the platform behind the wedge.

**Intapp DealCloud** already owns relationships, pursuits, and professional-services workflows, so my falsifiable 36-month prediction is that Osmaura reaches roughly $1 million to $3 million ARR and is acquired by Intapp for under $50 million by July 2029. Osmaura’s founders can produce a useful signal-detection feature, but Osmaura’s low network compounding, enterprise sales exposure, and incumbent overlap make a feature acquisition more probable than an independent 100x platform. The smart and leveraged get richer; the smart but permissioned often get bought.

Customers reportedly using Osmaura daily are the strongest objection to that forecast. The bull case is that daily behavior creates proprietary conversion labels, the legal niche supplies high willingness to pay, and 41% law-firm GenAI adoption removes deployment friction—an early Perplexity-like application before the market recognizes the new category. Osmaura could then become the system of intelligence above every legal CRM. The exception does not yet apply: Perplexity combined elite specific knowledge with permissionless distribution, while Osmaura combines founder-led sales, confidential customer silos, and two-month procurement cycles. I would flip to Invest if Osmaura reaches 100 law firms primarily through self-service or customer referrals while proving that anonymized conversion feedback improves recommendations across firms.
