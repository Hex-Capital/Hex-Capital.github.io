# Markov -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: public computer-use models made GUI automation technically plausible in 2024–2025, AI labs now need reliable training and evaluation environments, and Markov becomes large only if those labs repeatedly buy application-specific trajectories and evaluators rather than generate sufficient synthetic data themselves. That is the controlling belief. The dossier establishes the bottleneck but not the commercial threshold: 150K+ cumulative Hugging Face downloads and 17,814 in the preceding month demonstrate attention, while a 2025 system obtaining results from 312 human trajectories plus synthetic actions directly challenges the quantity-based thesis.

The technology shift is real. Anthropic released computer use in October 2024, OpenAI followed with Operator in January 2025, and both systems remained error-prone. Markov has built unusually substantive pre-seed assets against that opening: 48,478 videos, roughly 12,300 hours of professional-software activity, synchronized input events, and an AutoCAD environment retaining files, screenshots, and model turns. This is more than a data-labeling pitch. It is early technical proof that the team can construct the environments needed to train and audit agents.

The strongest case for Markov is that the public dataset is distribution rather than the final product. Downloads create access to agent developers; contracted environments, evaluators, rubrics, and gold outputs could then become the paid layer. If that loop works, stronger models increase the number of workflows labs attempt and therefore increase demand for reliable evaluation infrastructure. But this remains a proposed loop. There are no named customers, paid projects, renewals, or usage constraints, and AutoCAD Bench currently has only 50 tasks with an evaluator that can accept visually similar but semantically incorrect geometry.

The bear case is substantial. Scale AI, Mercor, Turing, and Invisible already possess buyer relationships, expert networks, and more than $100 million of funding each. Labs can also collect their own trajectories or synthesize them. Markov’s flagship corpus is public, internet-sourced, and lacks recording-level chain-of-title documentation, so 12,300 hours may represent a diligence burden rather than a proprietary asset. This market may reward owned evaluation infrastructure, but raw recording volume is likely to commoditize.

Mandal’s work at Sarvam AI, DevRev, and Telekinesis and Ashok’s robotics-assistant experience are directionally relevant. Building this corpus with two people also shows execution speed. The dossier does not, however, document a prior startup outcome, costly personal commitment, or the kind of second-time learning that earns affirmative founder credit under my framework; prestige and youth are not substitutes for that evidence.

My 36-month base case is that Markov shifts from selling “data for computer-use AI” toward application-specific evaluation environments and RL gyms, reaching roughly $2–8 million ARR rather than becoming a scaled raw-dataset vendor. I would monitor, not invest today. The verifiable flip condition is one named frontier lab or major agent company paying for an application environment and renewing or expanding the contract after using its evaluator in production training.
