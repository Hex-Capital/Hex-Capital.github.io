# Dydema -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Conditional on strong product-market fit, Dydema’s clearest path is a headset for a narrow, recurring hands-free task such as confirming an agent prompt. The October 30 demo used a research cap and three imagined movements. The prototype maps decoded commands to actions such as keystrokes, a reusable interface that could serve the same workflow across markets. That portability is plausible if the intended consumer sensors can deliver comparable performance. Dydema reports brief calibration for previously unseen users, and its planned online ordering gives it a plausible direct sales route. Independent purchase and setup on the consumer headset remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

A calibrated command could become part of a user’s daily workflow if it stays reliable across sessions. That supports a repeat-use hypothesis, while recurring consumer billing and expansion economics remain unknown. The earlier Wizard research API has self-serve keys and metered pricing. Those mechanisms are documented for the research product. The two-person team demonstrated a prototype in October, and the memo leaves realized consumer revenue and paid adoption unknown. (Dydema, The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The consumer contribution bridge starts with net revenue per headset and subtracts hardware, fulfillment, expected returns, acquisition, and setup support, plus any ongoing decoding cost Dydema bears. Cheaper sensors could improve contribution if they preserve reliability. The memo provides neither a consumer price nor representative costs to establish incremental contribution. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Even if a niche of users values hands-free control, the research-cap demo may overstate how easily ordinary buyers can get reliable results from cheaper sensors. The limited command set and calibration could make the headset a supplement to existing inputs, with too little repeat use to sustain demand. If reliable setup also requires substantial hands-on support, each additional buyer could be expensive to activate. The structural gate stays open because the memo leaves the required support and its cost unresolved. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can ordinary buyers in two markets buy the same consumer headset, calibrate it unaided, and still use it for agent confirmations a month later at positive per-buyer contribution? A paid rollout that tracks setup completion, repeat use, and attributable costs would answer this. Move to Reach out if the rollout supports a score of at least 65 with Medium or High confidence. Move to Pass if reliable setup requires per-buyer work that makes scaling uneconomic and the rollout reveals no credible way to reduce it.
