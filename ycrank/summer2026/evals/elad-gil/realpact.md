# RealPact -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: multimodal models now let brokerages turn an address or voice command into a populated contract, but RealPact becomes a large company only if brokerages route a substantial share of recurring transactions through it rather than treating contract generation as an occasional convenience. The dossier quantifies the feature-level gain—30–40 minutes reduced to under five and an estimated 200 hours saved per agent annually—but does not yet identify the usage or retention threshold that produces durable economics. That missing controlling variable is the main limitation.

The technology shift is real. Language models structure government records, vision models read deeds and tax cards, and voice initiates the workflow; together they convert a previously manual process into an address-to-contract system. This is more than a text-generation demo because RealPact retrieves records, cross-references data, populates state-specific forms, and returns a signature-ready agreement. The economic evidence remains one-sided, however: delivery time improves roughly eightfold, but there is no pricing, gross-margin, error-rate, or transaction-volume evidence showing how that improvement changes brokerage purchasing behavior.

The strongest bull case is the product pull. More than 10 brokerages reportedly run RealPact, named users include Four Seasons Sotheby’s, NextHome Modern Realty, and Beacon Realty Group, and Four Seasons has advanced to a firm-wide pilot. Current-product sales are also reported. At pre-seed, that is meaningful evidence that an unfinished product addresses an actual operational burden, even without disclosed revenue. The unresolved question is whether “running” means repeated production usage or lightweight pilots; neither retention nor contracts completed per active agent is provided.

Workflow ownership helps, but market structure cuts against it. RealPact can accumulate templates, integrations, permissions, and transaction state, yet no present moat is documented. SkySlope already reaches 900,000 professionals and processes 3 million transactions annually while offering AI-enhanced transaction management. If stronger models make record extraction and form completion broadly available, the incumbent can distribute the feature through an existing system of record. RealPact’s nationwide plan also multiplies state-specific forms, MLS agreements, compliance obligations, and accuracy exposure before New Hampshire usage has demonstrated defensibility.

The founders deserve credit for second-time learning. Ranvir Deshmukh built the acquired CourseMe product to 4,000 monthly users, one million page hits, and adoption by 90% of Dartmouth students; Erik Peterson built it with him, and the pair iterated from SignPact into RealPact. This shows shipping and product adaptation, though not documented real-estate expertise or the costly commitment that would justify a top founder score.

I would monitor rather than invest today. None of the known exceptions applies: this is not frontier research led by an unusually scarce technical team, nor does it reconstruct physical-market economics. The red flag is specific rather than generic—the product could become an incumbent feature before its workflow state creates switching costs. My flip condition is verifiable: Four Seasons converts to a paid deployment and processes at least 70% of eligible purchase agreements through RealPact for six consecutive months, with expansion into a second state and disclosed accuracy above 99%. That would identify the controlling variable as recurring transaction penetration rather than demo-level time savings.
