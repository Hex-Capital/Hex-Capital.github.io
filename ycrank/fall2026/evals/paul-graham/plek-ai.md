# Plek AI -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Plek AI’s most interesting claim is that it reproduced a six-week advisory assessment at a 3,600-person company and identified $30 million in annual EBITDA opportunities. I want to know what happened when the employee data failed to fit the model. What did Bouquet change, and how quickly? That episode would tell me more about the founders than the size of the estimated savings. The dossier supplies the result they advertise, but no named obstacle followed by a dated, shipped workaround. Resourcefulness remains unproven.

There is real substance behind the product. Bouquet’s task-level workforce research directly overlaps Plek’s mechanism, and the related research publishes code and data. Mapping employees to 50,000 jobs and 250,000 tasks, then reconciling overlapping interventions, is a specific technical approach. Avoiding double-counting matters: you cannot eliminate a role twice. This partly matches my unstated preference for founders who build a difficult substrate before calling it a platform. But the commercial benchmark is company-reported. Agreement with a consultant’s estimate does not establish that either estimate predicts achievable savings. I give the technical work intermediate credit, with a substantial certainty discount.

The strongest argument for investing is that Bouquet may have found the commercial expression of work he already understands unusually well. His MIT research and reported 2022 exit from Biot make this more interesting than an interchangeable workforce chatbot. A PE operating partner could use employee-level reconciliation repeatedly across portfolio companies. Negotiated engagements could also let the founders perform the messy work manually while discovering what to automate. Yet the dossier establishes research proximity more clearly than an experienced personal need or an ugly barrier the founders have actually crossed. And it does not show one buyer returning with a second company. The $30 million is potential customer benefit, not Plek revenue; the November 2026 summit appearance establishes access, not demand.

Orgvue’s existing workforce-design capabilities make that distinction consequential. Plek could produce impressive assessments while remaining an occasional consulting purchase. Repeat use would reveal whether its reconciliation solves a persistent job that existing tools leave unfinished. There is also no documented rejected hypothesis and subsequent product revision, so I assign the specified default for adaptability rather than infer either flexibility or stubbornness. I see no evidence of dependence on one large partner, premature sales delegation, or a hastily assembled cofounder relationship. Missing history does not establish those red flags.

I would pass today. I checked the exceptions for working products and independently validated technical proof: Plek’s research foundation and reported assessment are meaningful, but neither supplies unmistakable user pull or validation strong enough to replace the missing founder evidence. My flip condition is one referenceable PE customer’s paid repeat deployment, with a dated record showing how the founders overcame a concrete data or workflow blocker in the first deployment. That single customer history would connect the technical work to both founder behavior and actual need.
