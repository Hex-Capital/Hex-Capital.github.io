# Florin -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Florin’s contested truth is precise: commercial risks in robotics, space, advanced energy, and defense can be priced more accurately by software using external evidence and policy-year simulations than by human underwriters using inherited class codes. What would prove it wrong? If policies quoted in under one minute cannot be bound without human exceptions—or produce unacceptable losses—the “zero underwriters” carrier is a slogan, not a secret. Florin reports neither bound policies nor actuarial results, so its falsifiable proposition remains an unsupported founder assertion rather than a demonstrated zero-to-one result.

The founding structure is the strongest evidence. Shaurya Aggarwal and Amol Pant have been friends since middle school; Amol and Aydin Sorensen were college roommates; all three later lived together for two years. This is a company formed from durable relationships, not a team assembled for YC. Their work spans Google TPUs, Tesla Autopilot compute, robotic perception, encryption across 100,000 hosts, and fleet operations. But technical prestige cannot substitute for insurance competence: the dossier identifies no actuarial, regulatory, reinsurance, or carrier-management experience, and it provides no ownership, compensation, or explicit full-time-status evidence.

The purported beachhead is not narrow enough. “Robotics, space, advanced energy, and defense” combines several industries with different hazards, policy forms, regulatory questions, and loss distributions. Florin is also entering a market where Corgi, Shepherd, Nirvana, and Coterie collectively disclose more than $800 million in funding and already automate underwriting or sub-one-minute binding. There is no proprietary dataset, measured accuracy advantage, dominant niche, or structural barrier today. A future claims-data flywheel is possible, but resemblance to a monopoly mechanism is not ownership of one.

The bull case is that these founders understand the machines being insured better than traditional carriers do. Declined physical-technology companies may constitute an unusually motivated customer base, and retaining premium economics as a carrier could create a much larger business than selling underwriting software. If Florin’s simulations can safely price risks that class-code insurers cannot understand, every bound policy improves its proprietary dataset. That would connect a natural secret to compounding economics.

But the coordination stack is presently adverse. Florin must validate its pricing model, obtain or disclose carrier authority and capacity, satisfy state AI-governance requirements, finance risk, acquire customers, and survive claims before one element independently proves the thesis. Its site discloses neither a carrier nor a capacity provider, while its proposed use of social-media evidence adds discrimination and governance exposure. A banking-to-insurance pivot apparently occurred shortly before launch, further weakening the claim that this company was organized around a long-developed insurance secret. None of the known-exception patterns applies: there is no independently verified technical breakthrough, persistent transaction demand, or deployed pilot that overrides these risks.

Distribution is currently a work-email form, founder email, and Calendly aimed at businesses facing declinations or high quotes. This identifies an urgent buyer condition, but not a repeatable route, contract economics, sales cycle, broker strategy, or implementation burden. I would pass today. The decision would flip with one verifiable result: a capacity-backed cohort of at least 100 paid policies bound without human underwriting, followed for twelve months with a disclosed loss ratio consistent with sustainable carrier economics. Until then, Florin has a serious team and an exact claim, but competition has evidence while Florin has assertion.
