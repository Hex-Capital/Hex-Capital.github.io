# Plek -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Plek has a founder worth listening to and a round I cannot yet underwrite. The reported $500,000 YC investment tells me neither the proposed post-money valuation nor how many investors are competing for the next check. I will not infer an auction from YC membership. I also cannot call this scarce capital at an attractive price. More fundamentally, the dossier never identifies what one additional check proves, by when, or against what pass/fail threshold. Those are missing investment gates, even for a credible pre-seed company.

Pierre Bouquet supplies the strongest affirmative evidence. His MIT task-level workforce research directly matches the product, and the reported sale of Biot in 2022 establishes experience beyond publishing a method. Plek fits my pattern of backing people who translate between systems: here, workforce research and executive capital allocation. But the bridge is only partly demonstrated. The dossier establishes research depth and a prior exit; it does not establish that Bouquet has deployed enterprise automation and owned the resulting operating outcome. That earns substantial founder credit without treating academic authority as enterprise execution.

The strongest bull case is that Plek starts upstream of the automation vendor. An executive who cannot identify where labor dollars go cannot sensibly decide what to automate. Starting with organizational records, validating through employee interviews, and then implementing changes could give this team access to both the purchasing decision and the workflow itself. The founder-reported assessment of 250 employees, 735 tasks, and 36 FTEs on AI-addressable tasks makes that proposition concrete. It does not establish that proprietary access caused a purchase, or that those 36 FTEs represent realizable savings.

That distinction decides the investment for me. Plek’s displayed cases covering 13,500-plus employees and 15 companies within a $50 billion portfolio look commercially meaningful until its terms describe case studies as illustrative and based on example or historical data. I cannot underwrite them as customer receipts. Nor does permission to reuse de-identified data establish a proprietary data advantage. Workhelix already offers overlapping task-level assessment, while Skan observes actual workflows. Plek needs evidence that its initial estimates lead to a buyer action competitors cannot readily capture. The conflicting SOC 2 disclosures and unredacted survey-text handling also create specific obstacles to earning the access this strategy requires.

Implementation could produce a useful, cash-generating business early. Negotiated statements of work provide a possible collection mechanism, but no disclosed revenue, delivery cost, margin, or burn establishes that outcome. The important counterfactual is whether Plek survives and compounds if another financing never arrives. Nothing here proves it cannot; nothing yet funds that conclusion either. I checked the exception case: measured AI performance with paid conversion and retention could outweigh my skepticism, and a prior exit could support an unconventional financing with an explicit capital plan. Plek documents the exit, but neither operating receipts nor that plan. The research pedigree alone does not override the gates.

I pass on the current evidence. My one verifiable flip condition is a completed, seed-funded customer deployment that converts the assessment into paid implementation and produces positive contribution cash after delivery costs. That would establish that Plek can turn its research access into an economic transaction. Today, the dossier proves the founder understands the measurement problem more clearly than it proves an investable business.
