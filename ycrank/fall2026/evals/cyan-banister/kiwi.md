# Kiwi -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032: a sales operator tells agents what outcome she wants, and every action carries a receipt showing which records justified it. Getting there requires dependable source data, reproducible decisions, and permission to stop when the evidence is stale. Kiwi’s versioned lake, SQL traces, and historical replay address pieces of that transition. But that future is my hypothesis. The dossier does not yet show agents or operators organizing their work around this architecture. I can see a useful component; I cannot yet see the small, unmistakable behavior that tells me the larger world has started arriving.

The founders make me take the component seriously. Melanie Chen has worked on data infrastructure at Plaid and led post-sales at Chalk; Bill Qin built Chalk SQL. Those are relevant operating experiences, not merely proximity to AI. Their shared workplace also gives this team a plausible technical foundation. What remains undocumented is the independently pursued obsession: earlier experiments, repeated builds, or people recruited into a difficult idea before it became fashionable. I give Chen and Qin meaningful credit for knowing the machinery, without inventing a lifelong mission from their résumés.

Why did Chen spend **20 hours in one week correcting agent work**? That is the irritating question worth following. If unreliable business context makes an operator supervise every step, automation has quietly created another job. Kiwi’s proposed transfer of control is concrete: inspect the query, recover the relevant snapshot, and stop a workflow rather than clean up afterward. This is an operating failure with a measurable labor cost, although the dossier identifies no gatekeeper collecting a rent or artificial restriction being removed. And the more than **100 customer-discovery conversations** establish investigation, not adoption. Order-form language establishes how Kiwi could charge, not that anyone has paid.

The strongest argument against waiting is that dependable data infrastructure can become essential before its importance is obvious. Kiwi fits my pattern of finding useful plumbing beneath a fashionable application. Its architecture is more substantial than a conversational interface, and the founders have unusually relevant experience building the underlying systems. Still, Clay already combines company context with agents that learn from outcomes. Kiwi has not demonstrated that its particular infrastructure controls a scarce dependency customers cannot readily replace. I can accept pre-revenue when a working prototype, exceptional founder history, and concrete commitments supply the missing conviction; this dossier does not establish that combination.

I would keep my own money in my pocket today, while making time to use Kiwi. The privacy question matters because it concentrates CRM records, meeting information, and email metadata, then supplies context to model providers. Encryption and training restrictions are affirmative protections; they do not fully establish consent and custody boundaries. That is an unresolved question, not evidence of a severe violation. Likewise, Qin’s inconsistent founder designation does not establish a leadership shell game: Chen is clearly identified as CEO. My one verifiable flip condition is a production customer demonstrating, over eight consecutive weeks, that operators repeatedly trust Kiwi’s snapshot-and-replay workflow enough to reduce measured correction time by at least 80%. That would turn a sensible architecture into evidence that people actually surrender the babysitting.
