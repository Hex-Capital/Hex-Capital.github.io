# Stratum Industries -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Raj Jagiasi’s 220% average quota attainment and Vihaan Jagiasi’s founding-engineer role at acquired Cadastral make founder execution Stratum Industries’s strongest dimension; Stratum Industries’s weakest dimension is category fit, because rubric-driven AI adjudication is consensus AI software wearing a GovTech jacket. Put differently: Stratum Industries is an execution-machine sibling pair trapped inside an asset class I do not want to own at a normal YC price. The founders may have rizz and tizz, but the financing still has to survive the regatta.

**Drop.io** is the high rung for asymmetric conviction: I kept building a file-sharing primitive against free incumbents because the operating belief predated the pitch. Stratum Industries shares some operator intensity—Raj ranked among Deel’s top five of more than 1,000 representatives, while Vihaan was Cadastral’s third founding engineer—but Stratum Industries diverges where conviction becomes missionary evidence. Neither founder has disclosed government-procurement experience, years inside adjudication, or opinionated public writing about agency backlogs. Strong executors can sell an opportunity; the dossier does not yet show that Stratum Industries would exist without the 2026 AI-agent opportunity.

**Fin’s** workplace-analytics pivot exposed the low-rung failure mechanism for category fit: a compelling Wizard-of-Oz origin can collapse into ordinary enterprise software once automation becomes the product rather than the learning method. Stratum Industries begins even farther down that path. Stratum Industries describes agents that read applications, route work among models, and generate cited summaries, while ClearOS already advertises nearly the same adjudication workflow and Salesforce already offers FedRAMP-authorized public-sector agents. “AI is just more internet” is deliberately overstated, but the operational point lands here: Stratum Industries has supplied no manual-service wedge, proprietary workflow data, or unusual structure that makes the AI exposure scarce.

No round size, valuation, investor list, or financing instrument is disclosed for Stratum Industries: **no evidence — applying absent-evidence default**. Stratum Industries moves above that default only if the round is demonstrably below the Summer 2026 batch median and at least one tier-one fund passed because government adjudication looked too slow or illegible. Without those facts, the counterfactual runs the other way: YC, “AI agents,” and a market where GovWell has raised $34.5M and PermitFlow $90.5M look like a recognizable financing narrative, not value-seed mispricing. I cannot underwrite alpha from an unspecified SAFE.

Salesforce’s FedRAMP-High and DoD IL5 authorization shows how Stratum Industries’s capital structure can deteriorate over the next 18 months. Stratum Industries must fund security work, procurement cycles, integrations, model validation, and consequential-decision review before disclosed revenue exists; human approval and cited summaries reduce product risk but do not shorten authorization queues. Each unpaid agency pilot becomes another regatta tack, and each tack makes Stratum Industries more dependent on follow-on equity before default-alive economics appear. There is no evidence of unusual financing terms or revenue cushion, so the generic SaaS-cap-table default is compounded by a capital-intensive buyer.

**John Fish / Bookshelved** had an asset on the cap table before the startup existed: a creator identity with owned distribution and measurable audience attachment. Stratum Industries has weaker but nonzero founder reach—Raj has roughly 3,000 LinkedIn followers, Vihaan roughly 2,000, and Vihaan generated more than 1,000 Perplexity Pro sign-ups at Waterloo within 48 hours. Stratum Industries nevertheless needs adjudication leaders, not student sign-ups or general professional followers. No agency community, practitioner newsletter, or pre-existing network of reviewers is disclosed, so Stratum Industries demonstrates distribution ability without owning the distribution required by the business.

GovWell’s 130-plus government customers across 34 states make my 36-month forecast concrete: by July 2029, Stratum Industries will abandon horizontal federal-state-local adjudication, narrow to one state or municipal licensing workflow, remain below $3M ARR, and sell to GovWell for less than $30M. Stratum Industries has enough founder execution to reach a usable product and a few deployments, but not enough category differentiation or capital insulation to win a horizontal platform race against GovWell, ClearOS, and Salesforce. The likely outcome is a successful regatta tack for the founders, not a venture-scale outcome for my personal check.

**Ro’s** known exception is the strongest bull case: regulatory complexity can convert expensive operating work into a moat, and Stratum Industries has the sales-engineering pairing to clear that work. Raj’s enterprise-sales performance, Vihaan’s acquisition experience, millions of backlogged applications, and the FDA’s December 2025 agentic-AI deployment could let Stratum Industries become the specialized trust layer that Salesforce cannot credibly supply. The objection stings because government authorization could be Stratum Industries’s venture moment. The dossier still loses that argument today: Stratum Industries has no disclosed customer, pilot, certification, adjudication dataset, or government-domain founder history, while ClearOS already claims the same wedge. I pass; one signed, paid production contract with a named agency covering at least 10,000 adjudications annually would flip the verdict.
