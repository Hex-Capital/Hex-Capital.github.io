# Avoca Systems -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032 and I can see patients arranging scans without learning the bureaucracy of radiology: the system interprets the referral, applies the correct protocol and site rules, gives preparation instructions, books the appointment, and follows the patient across the episode. Avoca Systems has one artifact leaking from that world today—live deployments across networks operating more than 150 clinics—but it has not mapped the intervening transitions or shown why it becomes the operating system rather than a very effective scheduling product. The future is plausible; the chain is still mostly mine, not theirs.

The present-day wound is wonderfully concrete. More than 30% of calls are abandoned because a booking must reconcile clinical protocols, referrals, preparation instructions, local rules, and RIS workflows. Avoca’s clinic-specific knowledge bases, human escalation, and real-time write-back attack that operating knot rather than merely placing a cheerful voice on top of a calendar. The missing piece is rent: the dossier quantifies failed access but does not identify who economically benefits from the current fragmentation or how much staff cost, scanner idle time, and lost revenue the product recaptures.

Users are already trusting the artifact with consequential work. Several radiology networks are live; one customer reports 86% call resolution and 4.6/5 satisfaction, another converted 30 bookings in half a day, and deployments report a 90% reduction in abandonment. These are company-reported figures without revenue or cohort retention, but they describe completed patient actions inside functioning clinical workflows—not a waitlist, LOI, or narrated demo. This is Avoca’s strongest evidence.

Max Shand brings real company-building tuition: first employee at Afterpay, founder of Serenade, and a 2024 acquisition. Campbell Mercer-Butcher has led engineering across insurance and property software and previously founded Epex. That combination supplies commercial command and technical delivery, but the dossier does not show a pre-company radiology obsession, a dated operating wound, or how these two founders learned to trust one another. I see Biz and shipped-company proof; I cannot yet see the independently derived Tizz specific to this problem. I also need an explicit governance answer because the public record does not identify decision control or a tiebreaker.

The bull case is that radiology specialization compounds. Every clinic rule, resolved exception, and RIS integration could make Avoca more accurate and harder to replace, while SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, HIPAA compliance, and named integrations raise the cost of entry for generic agents. The bear case stings more: openDoctor and AbbaDox already occupy radiology workflows, Assort Health and Prosper AI are heavily funded, and Avoca depends on third-party systems whose installed vendors can absorb these functions. Its infrastructure is still described as Australia-only while it begins selling into the United States. That is not a controlled lower-stack bottleneck yet; it is an integration-heavy application positioned between several powerful suppliers and competitors.

I am neutral and would not write the personal check today. Avoca has built a useful, trusted machine, but the dossier proves present execution more strongly than a six-year asymmetry. My flip condition is verifiable: show a U.S.-hosted deployment across a named multi-site radiology network, with twelve-month retention and documented expansion revenue, while demonstrating that Avoca’s accumulated rules and exception data—not merely access to the same voice models—produce materially better booking accuracy than an incumbent RIS workflow.
