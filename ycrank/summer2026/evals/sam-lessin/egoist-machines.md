# Egoist Machines -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and status of this check are undisclosed. I cannot infer scarcity from YC admission or press coverage, so the deal fails my first gate at the evidence level: I may like the idea, but I do not know whether I am buying a neglected belief or paying consensus pricing for “portable AI memory.” The dossier also names no use of proceeds, dated milestone, or pass/fail experiment. “Build the passport and recruit developers” is a direction, not a venture moment.

The sharp experiment is available if Egoist chooses it: prove that context portability causes a user to employ a second AI application more frequently or pay for it. The product already has the right instrumentation—field-level permission requests, revocable access, and receipts recording what each application reads. But the company has not connected that mechanism to an integration, repeat usage, pilot, or purchase. A waitlist opened July 13, 268 company followers, and founder-led promotion are activity around the product, not evidence that the workflow changes economic behavior.

The bull case stings. Erin McGurk has built distribution reaching more than one million people, including reported audiences of 515,000 on Instagram, 205,000 on TikTok, and 55,000 on YouTube. David Khachaturov brings AI-security work at FrontierOne, OpenAI PaperBench experience, and a shipped open-source project with 4,700 GitHub stars. This is a plausible outsider-insider translation team: consumer trust and education on one side, AI infrastructure and security on the other. At pre-seed, that combination can matter more than revenue.

But Egoist does not yet own a dark pool merely because McGurk owns attention. The dossier shows no number of followers joining the waitlist, no application integrations, and no approved context moving between two products. Meanwhile OpenAI controls native memory, Anthropic controls relevant APIs, and Mem0, Supermemory, and Letta already sell or develop model-agnostic infrastructure. The red flag is not simply competition; it is that Egoist needs mistrustful platforms to cooperate while offering consumers a free-for-life product and disclosing no developer price. The known-exception override for an expensive or uncertain AI seed requires hard conversion, retention, or revenue evidence. None appears here.

Delete the next financing and the economic model disappears. There is no developer pricing, gross margin, burn, runway, or controllable cash metric, while consent review may itself become recurring user friction. My 36-month base case is that Egoist pivots from a consumer passport into a developer-facing consent or identity SDK and remains below $1 million ARR. I would flip if three independent AI applications integrate the Passport and at least 1,000 users transfer context between two of them, with 25% repeating that cross-app action within 30 days. Until that causal proof exists, I am passing—not on the team, but on an unpriced round funding an unnamed experiment around an access layer nobody has yet demonstrated they will use.
