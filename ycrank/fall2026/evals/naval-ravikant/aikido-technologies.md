# Aikido Technologies -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Aikido Technologies has earned the right to attempt this. Kanner and Yu worked together at Principle Power in offshore R&D and hydrodynamics. More importantly, ABS witnessed a quarter-scale upending demonstration and issued a statement of maturity in February 2025. That is specific knowledge exposed to physical reality. The founders’ strongest evidence concerns floating wind, however. Their datacenter expansion was announced in March 2026; operating marine compute remains unproven.

Copying the software would leave a competitor with most of the hard work remaining. Aikido owns floating-wind patent US12571372B2, granted in March 2026. That is an identifiable asset, independently distinct from the team’s experience. But the patent covers floating-wind systems, not an established monopoly over offshore computing. Existing marine suppliers can support competing integrations. Hardware clears my software-commoditization filter; it does not automatically establish defensibility. The unanswered question is whether Aikido’s design creates a repeatable cost advantage after installation, backup power, and servicing.

The expansion has a coherent mechanism. The same platform that supports generation could supply power, cooling, and space to compute customers constrained on land. Aikido identifies AI inference customers and a UK site targeting 2028 operations. Independent research estimates floating datacenters growing from $450 million in 2025 to $2.445 billion in 2032. That supports adjacent demand, but it does not establish how much Aikido can economically serve. A 15MW+ turbine’s nameplate capacity is not continuous compute capacity. Four-plus hours of batteries, grid connections, and diesel backup make the operating economics central to the thesis.

The strongest case for writing a check now is that this team has already crossed an engineering threshold that most AI infrastructure entrants cannot even approach. Its $4 million 2024 financing supported the original floating-wind development; the compute application could monetize that work through a more urgent buyer. My exceptions permit investing before revenue when exceptional founder behavior, a compelling capital-efficient prototype, or self-reinforcing adoption carries the uncertainty. Here, the independently witnessed proof concerns platform upending, while the integrated datacenter demonstration and cooling performance remain prospective. The exception is plausible, but the evidence has not earned it.

Aikido fits my shift toward physical infrastructure bottlenecks. It fits less well with my preference for systems where participants create value for one another: the dossier establishes no customer contribution or distribution loop. Nor does it establish a payer, price, completed transaction, or realized revenue; financing and NVIDIA Inception membership cannot substitute for those. There is no documented integrity violation or artificial financing deadline to veto the founders. I would nevertheless keep my money uncommitted today. My flip condition is one independently verified, paid offshore compute pilot demonstrating contracted availability and positive contribution economics after backup power and marine servicing. That would connect the team’s demonstrated knowledge to a business that can compound.
