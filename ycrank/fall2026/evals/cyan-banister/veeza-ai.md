# Veeza AI -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Why should the color of your passport determine how many evenings you spend fighting a website? Six years forward, I want a traveler to state a destination and have a trusted agent assemble the application, explain the constraints, and handle the administrative work. Veeza AI is working on a recognizable piece of that world: navigating portals, checking documents, completing forms, and booking appointments. But that future is my extrapolation. The dossier does not yet establish the intervening steps from tourist-visa assistance to a durable mobility agent.

The present-day wound is much clearer. Egyptian travelers face scarce appointments, repeated paperwork, and a choice between doing everything themselves and paying intermediaries. Veeza charges EGP 1,500 for several Schengen destinations and EGP 4,000 for UK or US applications, separately from government charges. Those fees could buy back meaningful time and control. The limit matters: software can remove clerical friction, but it cannot manufacture embassy appointments or change admission rules. I see a specific institutional bottleneck and a beneficiary; I do not yet see evidence quantifying the broker rent displaced or the hours returned.

The little artifact that gets my attention is the WhatsApp community. Approximately 400 people producing more than 10,000 feedback messages, reported in October 2026, suggests travelers are spending effort helping this thing work. Paying customers and the April 19 closed beta followed by the June 7 public beta give that activity substance. The reported increase from 1,000 to 7,000-plus users adds breadth, although it does not tell me how many completed applications or returned. I would put more weight on those conversations than on 253,000 launch-post views. People discussing their paperwork are closer to the operating truth than people watching a launch.

Aly Moursy has personally experienced Egyptian-passport travel, co-founded Dlvvr, and led product at Efreshli. That is useful operating context, and it fits my pattern of looking for a lived irritation that becomes a product specification. It is thinner evidence for an obsession that predates the company: the dossier supplies no dated sequence of visa experiments or independently developed technical work. Moursy is clearly identified as CEO; there is no affirmative evidence of a leadership shell game or equal-control deadlock. I can make exceptions for absent revenue when a prototype, founder history, and a worked-backward future reinforce one another. Veeza already has payment evidence, but the other two legs remain less developed.

The strongest case against my hesitation is that the helpful consumer service could quietly become the infrastructure. Arabic and English assistance, accumulated portal exceptions, and travel-agency distribution could make Veeza the operating layer other businesses use to serve MENA travelers. That would fit my tendency to find concealed infrastructure beneath an ordinary beachhead. Today, however, route-specific learning is an inferred advantage, and reusable document storage does not establish control of a scarce asset. Atlys and iVisa/HelloGov already combine overlapping automation and assistance. The dossier shows useful work being performed; it does not yet show why more usage gives Veeza an advantage those competitors cannot readily acquire.

Passport and financial-document custody also deserves a direct answer. Veeza states that it encrypts stored data, restricts access, and accepts deletion requests; applicants can inspect prepared forms before submission. Those are concrete safeguards, so I would not invent a severe privacy violation. They also do not establish how consent and document access work throughout the automation chain. My decision is **Neutral**: the agency returned is compelling, while the future mechanism and controlled enabling asset remain unproven. One verifiable flip condition would be a route-level study showing repeat customers completing applications across multiple destinations while human handling time falls materially through reusable workflows. That would demonstrate that each application is building something beyond the next service fee.
