# Financial Datasets -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I can underwrite the product more readily than I can underwrite the check. The proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and financing history are absent. For Financial Datasets, that is not clerical missing data: price determines whether I am buying an overlooked two-year data asset or paying consensus prices for “MCP for finance.” Without terms, I assume neither scarcity nor cheap convexity.

The product has real technical receipts. Virat Singh manually mapped more than 17,000 issuer-specific XBRL tags, built an audit cycle comparing 20,000 data points across 1,000 companies and 75 sectors, and ships filings, fundamentals, ownership, prices, and operating KPIs through APIs and MCP. His investment-banking experience, engineering work at Airbnb, Acorns, Faire, and others, and authorship of the 26,600-star Dexter repository put him on both sides of the bridge: he understands financial research workflows and has shipped the agent software consuming them.

The bull case is that the workflow is already becoming a proprietary distribution pool. Financial Datasets reports one million daily API requests and thousands of agents; Julius and OpenBB provide concrete channels; the company has 14,000 X followers; and engineers and researchers at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Jane Street, and Citadel reportedly use the data. Those are unusually strong pre-seed usage signals. But they do not yet establish the causal fact I care about: how many users pay, retain, expand, or choose this dataset when a free SEC pipeline or overlapping API is available. Followers, integrations, named users, and requests can all become metric theater when the purchase event remains hidden.

The economics are similarly unresolved. Pricing is intelligible—$20 per 1,000 prepaid requests and $200 monthly for 100,000—but there is no disclosed revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, paid conversion, or licensed-price-data cost. One million daily calls could be a wonderful cash engine or an expensive free workload. Delete the next financing and the dossier cannot yet tell me which. The maintenance burden from restatements, ticker changes, splits, and acquisitions also grows with coverage, while Massive, Intrinio, Alpha Vantage, Finnhub, and internal ingestion teams constrain pricing power.

Most importantly, no named venture moment connects the round to a finite truth-revealing experiment. “Hire, integrate, and grow” would be a capital-factory plan; the investable version would specify what scarce capital proves that current usage cannot prove organically. The strongest opposing case is that two years of mapping work plus massive request volume already constitutes the exception: technical adoption may precede monetization, and a solo founder with an owned developer audience could convert cheaply. It stings, but I cannot treat API traffic as purchase behavior or infer favorable terms from YC admission.

I am passing at the evidence presently available, not declaring the business bad. The one flip condition is a financing memo documenting a sub-$10 million post-money first institutional check and committing its proceeds to a dated paid-conversion test—for example, 100 retained Build-or-higher customers at positive gross margin within twelve months. That would turn an impressive developer asset into a cheap, finite venture experiment.
