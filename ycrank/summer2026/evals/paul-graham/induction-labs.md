# Induction Labs -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Photon-1 is a 106B-A5B model built by two brothers, yet neither Jonathan nor David Li is documented as having personally needed the product. That pairing is Induction Labs’s signature: unusually strong cofounder configuration attached to a weakly evidenced organic idea. Induction Labs looks like two formidable hackers searching for the problem their artifact solves. That can work, but it reverses the usual Dropbox sequence: pain first, program second.

**Stripe** is the high rung for cofounder configuration: two brothers with years of shared commerce hacking and complementary technical force. Jonathan identifies David as his brother and says they are building together again, so Induction Labs has more evidence of durable chemistry than the ordinary YC pair assembled for an application. The divergence is that the Collisons had already turned Auctomatic into a $5 million outcome, while Induction Labs provides no comparable evidence about the Lis’ previous company. Induction Labs has the promising genealogy, but not yet the proof that the genealogy produces commercial animals.

**Loopt** is the low rung for organicity: weak product timing meant Sam Altman’s force of personality had to override the absence of a naturally usable product. Induction Labs has Jonathan’s Cohere work in reasoning and reinforcement-learning infrastructure, but the dossier never says Jonathan or David needed a video-pretrained computer-use model in their own workflow. Induction Labs may have discovered a new learning method, but discovery is not the same thing as an organic startup idea. No evidence ties Photon-1 to personal pain, so I apply the absent-evidence penalty.

Photon-1’s 575 million training frames, 30,000 H200 GPU-hours, and 106B-A5B architecture are tracks left by animals. Over the next 18 months, resourcefulness compounds if Induction Labs can turn the internal two-billion-video index into faster experiments, better rollouts, and a product outsiders can use. It decays if Induction Labs keeps publishing increasingly impressive models without finding users. A 30-times compute claim is a technical stunt; it is not yet the Airbnb cereal-box stunt, because nobody has paid for it.

The two-billion-video index puts Induction Labs deep inside a genuine schlep: filtering roughly two million screen recordings, compressing state representations by more than 100 times, and eventually untangling licensing and consent. Induction Labs would move into the top tier on this dimension if the Lis secured defensible data rights and produced verified reinforcement-learning rollouts from paying deployments. It would fall sharply if provenance objections force the corpus offline, because then the apparent moat was merely borrowed data.

**Dropbox** earned conviction with a working demo before the market analysis existed. Photon-1 is Induction Labs’s equivalent artifact, and it is technically far beyond a glossy AI wrapper: Jonathan’s ACL 2023 research, Cohere reinforcement-learning work, and a functioning 106B-A5B model establish real hacker genealogy. Induction Labs falls short of the strongest hacker rung because Jonathan’s largest cited GitHub repository has only 17 stars and David has no public repositories. The artifact is stronger than the public trail, but artifacts count more.

Axiom-1 appeared in August 2025 and Photon-1 replaced the public narrative in July 2026 without an account of what happened between them. By July 2029, I expect Induction Labs to have abandoned the attempt to sell a general computer-use foundation model and pivoted to enterprise computer-use training infrastructure, reaching roughly $2 million to $6 million in ARR. Induction Labs has the brother-pair and technical resourcefulness to keep iterating, but weak evidence of organic founder pain makes a narrower problem discovered through users more likely than the original research thesis becoming the business.

Photon-1 reportedly beats Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite with 30 times less pretraining compute and three times lower serving cost, but the strongest bear mechanism is research-project drift: internal benchmarks, no public pricing, no customers, undisclosed data rights, and an unexplained jump from Axiom-1 to Photon-1. Induction Labs could become two excellent researchers serially replacing models before anyone depends on them. The two-billion-video index and shipped Photon-1 artifact keep me on the invest side because they show the Lis can do work most applicants merely describe. I would flip to Pass if an independent benchmark failed to reproduce the Gemini advantage or if Induction Labs could not name three repeat external users within twelve months.
