# Native -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Reach out  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Native’s strongest path is selling a monthly marketing subscription to small businesses without a marketing team. Assuming strong product-market fit, the same workflow can serve buyers across markets. Native reads a business’s website, generates branded creative, and publishes organic posts and Meta ads. Native reports paying customers in more than 20 countries, which supports portability. Local language, creative conventions, and channel preferences can still limit where the current product works well. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

Paid ads bring prospects to a free website-to-post preview, giving owners a way to try the product independently. The memo leaves checkout and first live publishing unverified. Recurring content work and accumulated approval and performance history give customers reasons to renew. The $79, $249, and $499 monthly tiers give multi-brand customers room to expand, though a typical single-brand buyer may stay at $79. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

Native launched publicly in March 2026. By November, the CEO reported more than $2 million in ARR, and Native listed more than 2,200 paying customers. That is a steep reported time to scale of roughly eight months, with seven registered employees in September. The ARR calculation and month-by-month growth remain unverified. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*)

At the $79 Pro list price, incremental contribution depends on the cost of website processing, creative generation, publishing, and support. That contribution must cover paid acquisition and any onboarding labor over the customer’s paid lifetime. The CEO reported that acquisition cost fell from roughly NOK 1,800 to NOK 800 per new customer. That improvement supports the scaling case, with payback still uncertain. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Paid acquisition may be buying short-lived subscriptions. Native’s research measures reach and engagement while leaving sales lift unmeasured. If owners cannot connect the product to sales, single-brand churn could erode recurring revenue and leave too few paid months to recover acquisition costs. Meta’s overlapping creative tools add pressure on the same platform Native uses for paid publishing. The structural gate clears because the current evidence supports a portable workflow and leaves acquisition payback as a testable uncertainty. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Do recent paid-acquired Pro cohorts generate enough cumulative contribution to recover acquisition and onboarding costs at current ad volumes? Monthly collections, cancellations, and attributable serving costs by cohort would answer it. Consistent payback would sustain Reach out. Repeated failure to recover those costs would move Native to Watch.
