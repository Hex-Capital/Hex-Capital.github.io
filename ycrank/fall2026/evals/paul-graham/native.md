# Native -- Paul Graham Evaluation

What happened at Wepost? The founders had reached NOK80,000 MRR by September 2025, then wound it down to start Native in January 2026. That is the part I would ask them about first. They abandoned something that was earning money. Perhaps they discovered a much better way to solve the same problem. But the dossier does not identify the observation that forced the decision, the obstacle they encountered, or the workaround they shipped. I cannot turn a change of company into evidence of resourcefulness without knowing what happened between those two events ([Wepost reporting](https://www.shifter.no/nyheter/gikk-to-maneder-uten-lonn-for-snuoperasjon-na-far-de-flere-stjernegrundere-med-pa-eiersiden-etter-lansering/417633), [founder biography](https://native.no/about-us/benjamin-bekken)).

The strongest argument for investing is that Native has already persuaded a lot of people to pay. The company reports $2M ARR within six months and more than 2,200 paying customers. Those figures are substantial evidence at this stage. Taken approximately, they imply about $76 a month per customer, close to the $79 Pro price. This looks like selling a useful product repeatedly to small businesses. The important question is whether those businesses keep paying once the novelty wears off. Neither follower counts nor the size of the marketing market would improve that answer ([YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Vdd-native-artificial-intuition-for-marketing), [About](https://native.no/about-us), [Pricing](https://native.no/pricing)).

There is also functioning software. Native reports publishing more than 40,000 posts in a month, which gives its execution more substance than an architecture diagram. But I would separate the working publishing system from the claim that Norn knows which ideas will succeed. Native uses frontier models to generate assets and Ayrshare to publish them. Its distinctive claim lives in the selection step. The reported 5.5% engagement for Briggs on Homes, against 2.9% for its own posts, is encouraging. It does not isolate that selection step, and Native's research acknowledges survivorship and selection biases. The team has shipped a product; it has not yet established the special predictive ability described by “artificial intuition” ([Native](https://native.no/), [Research](https://native.no/research/the-taste-layer), [Terms](https://native.no/terms-and-conditions)).

The bear case is that customers are buying inexpensive content production while Native believes they are buying judgment. That distinction matters because Blaze already offers strategy, creation, publishing, and learning. If customers cannot tell whether Norn chooses better ideas, accumulated feedback may produce little protection against switching. Native fits my preference for concrete proof before grand platform claims through its paying customer base. Its proposed programmable infrastructure is still an API waitlist. I would give the existing business credit and give the future platform none yet ([Blaze](https://www.blaze.ai/), [Norn](https://native.no/blog/introducing-norn)).

These founders have more relevant experience than a team that merely noticed an AI trend. Bekken produced content, Hauga founded a marketing company, and Gråsberg worked in AI/ML. They also built Wepost together, so the newly assembled cofounder red flag does not apply. The dossier supplies no evidence of waiting for a major partner, outsourcing founder responsibility, or substituting launch publicity for customers. But professional proximity alone does not establish the founder-originated need or neglected operational burden I look for. The known exceptions involving a prototype overcoming cofounder uncertainty, validated science overcoming youth, or extraordinary nonprofit demand address different objections. Native's substantial commercial pull deserves consideration as an override, but it does not establish how these particular founders respond when blocked.

I would remain interested and hold my check. That is a neutral judgment about the evidence, with unusually strong customer pull on one side and an unanswered founder test on the other. My single flip condition is a verifiable account of one recent customer blocker: what failed, what the founders personally shipped within days or weeks, and evidence that the affected customers resumed use or renewed because of that change. Native already has enough customers to supply such a case. It would connect the impressive result to the founder behavior I am actually trying to buy.
