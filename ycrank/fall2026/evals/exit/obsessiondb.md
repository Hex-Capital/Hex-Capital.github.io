# ObsessionDB -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a managed database vendor is the strongest path. A buyer could integrate ObsessionDB’s ClickHouse-compatible storage engine and distributed cache into its own service. Ownership could justify a premium if control over storage, routing, and deployment lets the buyer improve margins across its fleet or ship a differentiated service faster than it could build one. EnterpriseDB’s acquisition of Altinity establishes that a database vendor has bought an adjacent ClickHouse business. The memo supplies no deal price or evidence of interest in ObsessionDB. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Osmosis reported that annual operating costs fell from about $60,000 to $30,000 after migrating from Tinybird, with 13 million API requests in 30 days. ObsessionDB separately reports paying production customers and a 50 TB deployment. Those are seed-stage signals that the team could test whether the advantage repeats across workloads. The operating milestone is lower fully loaded cost at comparable latency across multiple production workloads, including cold-cache traffic. The memo leaves the test budget and runway unknown. The architecture originated in work the founders did at Numia, which makes chain of title for the storage engine a transaction diligence item. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

The illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. A 5× cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% ownership and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, excess cash, and deal fees, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. They assume fully pro rata cash at closing, zero preference impact, and zero stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, or compensation. Actual investment terms and the waterfall are unknown.

| Scenario | Timing | Gross cash to holder at 3% |
|-----------|--------|----------------------------|
| Modest sale at $50 million enterprise value | Year 5 | $1.5 million, 3× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | Year 7 | $3 million, 6× |
| Prolonged private ownership | Through year 8 | $0, 0×, with shares retained |

The sale prices are scenario assumptions. At 2% ownership, even the strong sale returns $2 million, or 4×. Osmosis’s savings support customer value, while buyer economics and transaction pricing remain unquantified. In the prolonged-private case, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and the company reinvests available cash. The memo supplies no independently supported fallback that would pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer may already have enough of the capability or may be able to license it. ClickHouse Cloud offers isolated compute over shared storage and a managed agent service. Altinity has released a shared-object-storage extension, and ObsessionDB already sells a customer-cloud license. The hot-run benchmark leaves open whether ObsessionDB’s advantage survives cache misses. If existing products or a license deliver comparable economics, ownership could command too little to clear the proceeds hurdle. The evidence leaves that question open, so it supports Watch. It establishes neither a structural price cap nor that the prolonged-private outcome is probable. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a managed database vendor justify paying enough to clear the holder’s cash hurdle for ownership of ObsessionDB’s storage engine? A buyer-validated comparison of ownership, internal development, and licensing, grounded in repeatable production cost and latency results, would move the action to Reach out if it supported a cash price above the ownership-adjusted hurdle. A credible substitute that caps the price materially below that hurdle would move the action to Pass.
