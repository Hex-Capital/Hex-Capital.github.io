# Proto -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a designer changing an interface and its behavior together: the empty state, the failed payment, the awkward loading screen, all surviving the trip into production. Proto wants to close that distance. But the six-year path is still my hypothesis. Generating prototypes from requirements is a starting point; the dossier does not establish how that becomes a persistent system connecting design decisions to running software. I want to find the little piece of that future already escaping into everyday work.

Maayan Albert is the strongest reason to keep looking. [Stamper existed in 2020](https://www.design.cmu.edu/news/design-alumni-develop-creative-coding-tool), years before this round, and put programming inside an artboard-oriented environment. That is a wonderfully specific breadcrumb. Combined with launching Browserbase’s Director and working as a founding engineer at Avenue, it shows repeated movement between visual creation and executable software. This is more substantial than a résumé decorated with recognizable employers. It establishes independent building and relevant experience, although the dossier offers less evidence of recruiting first believers or commanding the commercial side.

Why must a designer separately remember every error, loading, and empty state? That is real labor, and Proto’s automatic state construction addresses a concrete source of it. Returning that attention to the designer fits my preference for repairing irritating systems. But who controls the remaining bottleneck? Albert says engineers still complete the connections after Proto submits pull requests. The product therefore restores some creative control without yet demonstrating that it removes the handoff delay. There is no quantified cost or institutional rent here to justify a stronger systems-disruption score.

The best objection to passing is that Albert may understand this boundary well enough to make it disappear gradually. State-restoring comments could preserve the context that gets lost between a beautiful prototype and a working feature. That fits my pattern of finding consequential workflow repair beneath an attractive creative product. My pre-revenue exceptions also leave room for a founder with a long technical obsession and a compelling artifact. Revenue is not the missing permission slip. The missing evidence is someone repeatedly reorganizing work around Proto: the dossier contains demonstrations and alpha access through DMs, but no documented repeat team behavior. Its 65 company-account followers and October 31 coverage do not answer that question.

The enabling-layer case is weaker still. Figma opened its canvas to agents in March 2026; Magic Patterns introduced overlapping contextual canvas and coding-agent handoff capabilities in October. Those facts do not prove Proto is easy to reproduce. They do mean that generating interfaces and handing off code cannot, by themselves, establish control of a scarce dependency. No controlled technical bottleneck is demonstrated. I also cannot infer the red flags of dependence on one closed model supplier, severe privacy problems, or conflicted founder control from silence. The concern supported here is narrower: Proto has not shown why other products will need its layer.

I pass with my own money today, while taking Albert’s history seriously. My one verifiable flip condition is three independent teams using Proto weekly for eight consecutive weeks to carry stateful designs through merged production pull requests, with observable evidence that preserved context reduces their handoff work. That would show the creative environment becoming part of how people actually build. Today, I have credible evidence of the builder and a plausible repair; I do not yet have evidence of that behavioral transition.
