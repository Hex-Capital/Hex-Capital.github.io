# HABU -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B is the strongest fit.** HABU targets institutional investors and family offices that repeatedly allocate capital to ground-up apartments. If its underwriting workflow reduces the effort to screen and diligence sites, the same investor or co-GP channel could fund successive projects and generate additional development fees. The memo records one Phoenix site under contract and $1.126 million in corporate securities sold. HABU reports a working screening module, and its CEO reports prospective project checks of $100,000 to $1 million. The memo establishes corporate fundraising and a site contract. Project-investor conversion, paid development work, and repeat allocations remain unverified. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

**Second-market scenario.** Assume the same buyer segment will fund a deal in another city and HABU can reuse its screening software. HABU would still need to collect local zoning and approval knowledge, source a site, and arrange project financing. Start the contribution analysis with development fees collected, assume zero promote until profits are realized, and deduct incremental deal sourcing, investor acquisition, diligence, and delivery oversight. Track HABU’s co-investment separately as capital required per project. The fee-only option could reduce that capital requirement if buyers take it up. The memo shows a plausible way to save screening labor, while the cost and capital required for each additional paid project remain unmeasured. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

The case for passing is that HABU scales like a local developer even if screening becomes much faster. Its Phoenix workflow relies on manually gathered approval knowledge, and every sponsored project needs its own construction loan and equity. HABU’s targets of five times as many projects per manager and up to 20% faster delivery lack a completed-project comparison. These are concrete constraints, but the memo leaves open how much sponsor labor and capital each additional project requires. The structural Pass gate remains unproven. (*Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can HABU activate a paying project investor in a second city through the same channel and core workflow, with positive fee contribution after measured local setup, acquisition, and delivery costs and a credible limit on HABU’s own capital per deal? A funded project, a collected fee, and a rollout cost breakdown that meet those conditions would move the action to **Reach out**. Evidence that each new market requires a fresh local operation and proportionate sponsor capital, with no supported way to reduce either, would move it to **Pass**.
