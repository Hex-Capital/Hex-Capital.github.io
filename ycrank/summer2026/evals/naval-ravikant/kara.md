# Kara -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The strongest asset is specific knowledge acquired through action. Aarin Jhaveri is a fifth-generation diamantaire who has sold more than $5 million of natural and lab-grown diamonds across the value chain; Anuveer Chadha worked on diamond quantum-sensing systems at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. Kara also claims to grow, process, and characterize single-crystal diamond in its own reactors. This is materially stronger than three credentialed founders discovering advanced materials through a market report. But selling diamonds and using diamond sensors are not yet the same as repeatedly manufacturing semiconductor-grade material at commercial yield. The knowledge is real; its transfer into production remains partially proven.

Code commoditization is not Kara’s problem. A competitor can copy its website and software without copying reactor recipes, impurity control, isotope control, surface finishing, or accumulated yield learning. Diamond’s reported 2,400 W/m·K thermal conductivity and 10 MV/cm breakdown field create genuine physical leverage. The problem is that Kara has not disclosed the evidence that would turn technical difficulty into its advantage: wafer dimensions, defect density, throughput, yields, patents, or customer-qualified specifications. Element Six and Diamond Foundry already sell overlapping products, and more than a dozen firms are developing single-crystal diamond wafers. Difficult manufacturing is a barrier to everyone; it becomes a moat only when Kara is demonstrably better at it.

The market has an authentic forcing function. NVIDIA describes Blackwell racks requiring up to 120 kW, while Akash Systems announced commercially available diamond-cooled AMD MI350X servers in March 2026. The same material capability could serve power electronics, RF, aerospace, quantum systems, and high-performance computing. That is real room to maneuver. Still, the published numbers expose the gap between possibility and present demand: a $2.12 billion synthetic single-crystal-diamond market sits above a substrate segment estimated at only $10.2 million in 2025. A broad parent category is not a causal expansion path. Kara must show that one qualified process can cross these markets without restarting years of customer qualification each time.

The sharp bull case is that public evidence systematically understates an NDA-driven materials company. Specifications are gated, qualification cycles are long, and a three-person team capable of operating its own reactors may have more technical progress than its 427 LinkedIn followers reveal. Kara also fits the shift toward embodied infrastructure bottlenecks: atoms, process knowledge, and production experience survive when software becomes abundant. But it does not fit the permissionless pattern. Each custom specification, sample, qualification, and fabrication engagement appears to require proportional company labor; no customer presently creates utility or distribution for another customer.

There is no disclosed evidence of unfair founder conduct, artificial financing pressure, reassurance-seeking, or other integrity and temperament red flags. Absence is not affirmative credit. The known-exception case for overlooking revenue and distribution would require an unusually convincing technical artifact or user behavior. Kara’s in-house-production claim is promising, but without measured yield, qualification results, or shipments, it remains a claim about capability rather than proof of a compounding asset. No public customer, pilot, production shipment, payer, price, or recurring revenue was found.

My base case is that within 36 months Kara becomes a qualified specialty supplier producing $1 million to $5 million in annualized revenue, not yet a wafer-scale platform. That can be a good company without yet being the nonlinear option I want. I would not write the check today. One verifiable event flips the decision: a named semiconductor or computing customer placing a repeat production order for Kara material with disclosed wafer dimensions and production yield. That single event would connect specific knowledge, manufacturing advantage, qualification, and direct value capture.
