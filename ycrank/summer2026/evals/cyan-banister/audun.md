# Audun -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. Debt negotiation may be continuous, personalized, and conducted across whatever channel a debtor actually answers; routine plans settle automatically while disputes and hardship reach humans. That future is plausible. Audun has not yet mapped the road to it. The dossier offers sub-second conversational AI as “why now,” but no sequence of institutional changes, no dated future behavior, and no artifact showing this team has pulled that world forward. A walkthrough button and a debtor portal marked “coming soon” are invitations, not leaks from the future.

The present system is more legible. Creditors use manual processes; debtors receive scripted demands that ignore disputes, vulnerability, and affordable schedules. Audun would let a debtor choose the channel, negotiate a plan, and reach a human when automation becomes unsafe. That returns some agency, but the economic mechanism remains incomplete: the dossier does not quantify collection costs, delays, or recovery losses, and the creditor—not the debtor—is the buyer. I need to know whether the system optimizes for sustainable repayment or merely makes pressure cheaper.

The four-person team can plausibly build and sell the product. Peter Flo brings Midjourney and data-science experience; Jacob Hambro and Magnus Njålstad Vonen bring operating and McKinsey backgrounds; Henrik Brun Fevang has data-science work at Oda. But those are credentials, not evidence of a pre-company obsession with collections. No founder is shown handling delinquent accounts, navigating debtor hardship, obtaining a collection licence, or building an earlier artifact in this domain. I see Rizz and technical capacity; I do not yet see the independently derived Tizz.

The sharp bull case is that regulation becomes the moat. Norway replaced its Debt Collection Act in May 2026, GDPR constrains solely automated material decisions, and Audun already advertises human escalation plus interaction histories. A technically strong team could turn those constraints into a compliant operating system spanning reminders, negotiation, payment plans, disputes, and payouts. That would be more valuable than another voice agent. But Audun’s own licence status is unverified, its case volume is undisclosed, and three funded competitors—AgentCollect, Altur, and Domu—already automate overlapping channels and negotiations. Resembling compliance infrastructure is not the same as controlling it.

There is also no costly user behavior yet. No creditor is named, no recovered payment is reported, and no debtor has demonstrably completed or maintained a negotiated plan. The self-service portal is unfinished. Without repeated cases, the proposed data loop around timing, channels, terms, and outcomes remains hypothetical; without that loop, Audun sits above third-party AI capabilities as a workflow application with no disclosed technical, distribution, or data bottleneck.

I pass today. My verifiable flip condition is one licensed Norwegian creditor running at least 1,000 completed cases through Audun, with repeat usage, documented repayment outcomes, and an independently reviewable human-escalation and consent architecture. That would show both the strange behavior I care about—people trusting software with a financially and emotionally costly negotiation—and the controlled compliance asset that could separate Audun from the crowded present wave.
