# Orgo -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Orgo has early support for an AI-enabled service. An agency operator reported that an agent running on Orgo saved a client about 12 hours preparing a Squarespace shop. The seed-stage hypothesis is that agencies could offer recurring work in graphical apps to small merchants whose jobs previously generated too little revenue to cover manual delivery. The agent reduces hands-on labor, while Orgo supplies a persistent desktop that retains files and lets a person take over when needed. Customers with access to equally capable models would still need that execution environment. The testimonial shows time saved on one job. The cost per successful job and the price at which previously excluded merchants would buy remain open. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The building-blocks path scores zero. The specific input is persistent desktop capacity for agents that use graphical software. Orgo says building the stack in-house can take months, but the memo documents overlapping hosted products and gives no evidence of capacity shortages or procurement delays as agent usage grows. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Orgo says it operates its own U.S. infrastructure. Saved machine state and custom templates could make switching harder, though retention and pricing power remain unmeasured. Orgo reports more than 1,000 teams and thousands of production desktops. The agency testimonial adds evidence of actual use, with repeat paid demand still unverified. The shipped product and a founder’s prior commercial computer-use agent work support execution credit. Orgo’s status page reported 99.760% compute-fleet uptime over 30 days, leaving dependable always-on delivery an open question. Published prices establish what Orgo charges, while fleet cost and utilization are missing, so scale economics stays at the default. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The shop example may be a faster way to deliver work the client already bought. If that pattern holds, the economics would support a productivity tool for existing buyers, and the industry path would fall to zero, triggering Pass. AWS and Daytona also offer overlapping desktop capabilities, so even if the use becomes viable, buyers may be able to run it on another provider. The single testimonial leaves the affordability question open, which supports Watch. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an agency repeatedly sell an Orgo-backed desktop workflow to merchants who previously declined the same work at manual prices? A paid pilot with repeat orders would move the action to Reach out if the price fits those merchants’ budgets and covers VM time, human review, and failed attempts. If representative trials show only time savings on work those buyers already purchased, move to Pass.
