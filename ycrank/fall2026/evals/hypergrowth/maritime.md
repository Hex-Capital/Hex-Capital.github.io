# Maritime -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Usage-based expansion** is Maritime’s strongest path. The first buyer is a startup that runs a separate, stateful AI agent for each customer. Maritime hosts each agent in an isolated VM, saves its state while idle, and wakes it when work arrives. If the first deployment works, the startup can add paid agent boxes as it serves more customers. The same job can plausibly travel across markets where buyers can use Maritime’s hosting and integrations. The memo gives no buyer geography or evidence on what a rollout would require where buyers have data-residency requirements. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Keiki documents running its background agent harness on Maritime microVMs. That integration shows an early workflow commitment while leaving payment and realized value open. Maritime lists a self-serve trial and a startup program with credits and migration help. The memo leaves Keiki’s acquisition route, setup effort, and trial-to-paid conversion unknown. Maritime’s reported snapshot of 1,107 agents, 29 awake concurrently, shows fleet activity at one moment. It leaves account-level retention, paid expansion, and typical awake time unknown. The first unresolved link is whether an initial deployment turns into recurring paid fleet growth. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Illustrative scenario.** If a customer adds ten persistent boxes at the listed rate of $1 per 2 GB per month, they consume $10 more in monthly credits. That adds revenue only if the extra usage increases what the customer pays. Incremental contribution then depends on awake compute, persistent storage, and any added support or migration work. Maritime’s sleep-and-resume design gives it a plausible way to limit compute costs for intermittent workloads. The memo contains no measured marginal costs. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Credit-backed trials could produce large fleets with little paid expansion. Keiki’s joint promotion leaves open whether unaffiliated startups will adopt and pay through the same route. For fleets that do expand, higher awake time could raise compute costs under flat box pricing, and simultaneous wakeups could hit plan limits. The structural downside gate remains open because the memo leaves these constraints testable and provides no evidence that they block every credible path. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can independent agent startups go from a first paid deployment to a larger paying fleet with positive incremental contribution? Move to **Reach out** if billing and usage records from a small cohort show that customers add persistent boxes as they onboard end users, increase paid spend beyond credits, and generate incremental revenue above the added compute, storage, and support costs across successive billing cycles.
