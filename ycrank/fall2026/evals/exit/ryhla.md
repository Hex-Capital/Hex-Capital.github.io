# Ryhla -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Ryhla’s strongest path. A grid software vendor could buy its territory-specific feeder models and developer filing workflow to add early site screening to an existing interconnection product. The memo records E.ON’s majority acquisition of envelio, an adjacent grid-modeling business. Ryhla reports that screening out eight of ten planned battery projects saved one developer about $160,000 in fees. That result suggests the screening can deliver customer value. A reason to own the models would emerge if exclusive access to transferable data and utility outcomes gave a buyer an advantage it could sustain over building or licensing the capability. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is repeat paid deployments across multiple utility territories, with utility outcomes used to check recommended and screened-out sites and with the cost of adding each territory measured. Ryhla reports a live Midwest solar deployment, models across 11 utility territories, and 91% approval among recommended sites in one cohort. The disclosed cohort omits its sample size and outcomes for screened-out sites. YC backing is confirmed, while the amount available for expansion and the cost of acquiring data and validating new territories remain unknown. The memo also leaves model and data transfer rights unverified. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Under the illustrative seed scenario, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5% initially, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× gross cash target is $2.5 million. A pro rata sale would require $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. Assuming $5 million of net debt for illustration, a $25 million enterprise sale in year eight returns $600,000 gross (1.2×) at 3%, while a $100 million sale returns $2.85 million (5.7×). The latter returns $1.9 million (3.8×) at 2% ownership. The memo supplies no Ryhla-specific sale price range, debt balance, fees, or preference terms. The adjacent deal establishes a transaction precedent and has no disclosed price in the memo. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A sponsor buyout lacks evidence of predictable cash flow and financing capacity. A secondary lacks evidence of buyer demand and holder eligibility. An IPO lacks evidence of public-market scale and a resale path. A dividend or buyback lacks evidence of excess cash and a payout commitment. If territory expansion stalls, acquisition interest fails to emerge, public scale stays out of reach, and Ryhla reinvests all available cash without a secondary or payout, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. Any wind-down recovery would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A grid software buyer may already have enough capability through existing integrations. PowerClerk and envelio already combine utility-side modeling with automated technical review, and public hosting-capacity maps can handle initial screening where coverage is adequate. Those substitutes strengthen the case for building, licensing, or partnering, especially if each new territory requires fresh data and backtesting. This supports Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a supported sale-price ceiling that would trigger Pass. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Could owning Ryhla’s transferable models and filing workflow create enough incremental value over licensing or building them to support an acquisition that pays the modeled holder $2.5 million within eight years? Repeat paid deployments across utility territories, documented transfer rights, and a potential buyer’s quantified comparison of owning, licensing, and building could establish a price range that clears the hurdle. A supported range at that level would move the action to Reach out.
