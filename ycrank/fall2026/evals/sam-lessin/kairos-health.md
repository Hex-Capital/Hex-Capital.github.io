# Kairos Health -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Kairos Health has customers; I still cannot price the check. Post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and the proposed use of proceeds are undisclosed. YC participation does not establish an auction, and competition prizes do not establish cheap equity. Those distinctions matter. I give the financing questions their missing-evidence defaults because neither the price nor the experiment my money would fund is documented.

The strongest case for Kairos is the bridge its founders have actually worked across. Tippur staffed her mother’s dental front desk; Chintala worked on healthcare patient-call routing. That connects operational knowledge of interrupted receptionists with engineering experience in moving patient calls through a system. Clinic-specific chair constraints, scheduling rules, and PMS write-back address the awkward details between a conversation and a usable appointment. This fits my unstated preference for founders translating between systems. The team has more relevant operating evidence than its university credentials convey.

There is also a functioning business underneath the AI claim. The founder reports 13 paying practices and $4K+ MRR; the October 2026 launch reports 16 live locations. These populations need not be identical, but payment is materially better evidence than demos or conference attendance. Pecan Dental’s reported 1,200-plus monthly calls suggests the product participates in a real workflow. The limitation is ownership: practice configurations may make Kairos useful without giving it proprietary access to the next buyer. Four demos from cold calling and four from a podcast demonstrate selling activity, not an owned distribution engine. Arini and Dentina already overlap with the core functionality.

The objection to passing is substantial: a team with direct workflow experience has persuaded practices to pay before it has accumulated a long operating history. That could become the base business on which AI supplies additional upside. **Ando** provides a relevant exception to my AI skepticism because enterprise workflow adoption supported the technology bet; the material difference is that its documented pilot conversion and retention established repeatability. Kairos has not supplied those measurements. Nor does Pecan Dental’s $21K-plus monthly production establish incremental collections attributable to Kairos. The evidence supports customer utility, but does not yet justify overriding the missing financing gates.

Deleting the next financing leaves $4K+ MRR with undisclosed inference costs, implementation labor, support expense, and burn. Revenue earns some economic credit; it does not establish a route to profitability. Human handoffs are a sensible control, but their labor cost belongs in the underwriting. I am passing at **42/100**: credible founders and paid workflow adoption, with neither a demonstrated proprietary acquisition mechanism nor an underwritable capital plan. My verifiable flip condition is a documented seed offer below $10 million post-money whose budget funds one dated test: reaching operating cash break-even within 12 months, including implementation and human-handoff costs, without another raise. That would connect the product evidence to a check I could actually evaluate.
