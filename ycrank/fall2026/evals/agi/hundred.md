# Hundred -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

New Economies is Hundred’s strongest path. The memo describes electronics factories using inspectors to check incoming parts under microscopes and sampling shipments. Hundred claims its controlled-light station can scan whole trays, calibrate from one known-good part, and detect defects smaller than 100 µm. The potentially new use is routine inspection of every incoming part at receiving stations that currently sample because of inspector time. The memo gives no baseline labor cost for full manual coverage or measured reduction in cost per correctly screened part. At model parity, buyers could still pay for reliable tray handling, lighting, and calibration if those features deliver factory-qualified results. That advantage remains untested. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The public launch and the founders’ experience in supplier hardware, production software, and applied machine learning support execution at seed. The memo records no buyer commitments or paid deployments. A station is a plausible unit of expansion, but sustained throughput, utilization, hardware and service costs, and labor spent reviewing rejects remain unknown. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Hundred could make an existing inspection workflow more efficient while factories obtain comparable results from cameras they already own. Elementary offers a retrofit path, and Overview AI sells configurable inspection cameras. Hundred’s per-part photo history could support failure investigations, but the memo gives no evidence that factories rely on it or that it affects renewal. Reflective finishes, acceptable part variation, and quarantine delays could also erase the labor savings at receiving. The memo shows credible alternatives and unresolved operating risks, with no direct evidence that a substitute matches Hundred’s whole-tray workflow. The New Economies path stays open on Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent factory that samples because full manual inspection costs too much pay to keep Hundred after a production trial? Move to Reach out if the factory commits to continued paid use after Hundred sustains full coverage at receiving volume, meets the factory’s limits for misses and false rejects, and beats manual inspection and the best available camera retrofit on cost per correctly screened part, including hardware, support, and exception labor.
