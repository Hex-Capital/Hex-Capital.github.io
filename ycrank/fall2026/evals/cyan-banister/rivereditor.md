# River -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine opening a research project and finding a computer that already understands its documents, browser sessions, and unfinished work. Getting there requires persistent context, dependable actions, and permissions people can understand and revoke. River has built an artifact pointing in that direction: a workspace that reads related material and edits across applications. But that future is my extrapolation. The dossier does not establish the intervening unlocks or explain what River is accumulating today that makes it essential six years from now. I would pass with my own money on this evidence.

Why does a researcher have to explain the same project to a chatbot repeatedly? Because context lives in one place and work happens somewhere else. River attacks that particular tax on attention, and returning control over a working day interests me. This fits my pattern of backing unglamorous workflow repair. Yet the dossier identifies neither a quantified burden nor an institutional gatekeeper whose economics River overturns. Its $14, $36, and $100 monthly plans sell convenience alongside metered model usage. That can support a useful business; the evidence for a deeper transfer of economic control remains modest.

The strongest argument against passing is that people appear to be doing things with the product already. The founder reports 50,000-plus users, 130,000-plus agents, and 200,000-plus files around the AI Desktop launch; the current homepage claims 100,000-plus creators. Creating files is more informative than joining a waitlist. A small team may have found a way to make delegating computer work feel natural, and habitual delegation could become a powerful distribution advantage. But these are company-reported aggregates without defined activity, repeat cohorts, or payment behavior. I cannot tell whether people entrusted River with their Tuesday afternoon or tried it once on Sunday.

Supple’s Roboflow engineering experience and prior Outwork venture, together with Joseff’s research background and founding-team experience at Progressly before its acquisition by Box, provide relevant building and operating experience. Ten months working together in a garage is a concrete commitment. The biography still supplies limited evidence of an independently pursued obsession predating River, or a specific operating wound that became its specification. My pre-revenue exceptions depend on a working artifact accompanied by unusually strong founder and technical proof; the dossier does not supply that combination. Separately, both founders being listed as CEO raises my accountability flag. It establishes neither equal control nor a leadership shell game, but I would need to understand who actually makes the final decision.

The concealed-infrastructure pattern also remains unproven. Local-file access and a signed-in browser give River reach; the dossier identifies no scarce technical asset or controlled bottleneck beneath that reach. Superhuman Docs already combines documents, AI, and external connections. River’s multiple external models avoid establishing the specific single-supplier dependency I reject, while leaving its durable advantage unanswered. And broad access creates a trust obligation: edit review and approval before consequential actions are useful controls, but they do not establish data custody or consent boundaries. The healthcare tool’s instruction to remove patient identifiers without an organizational agreement reinforces the need to inspect those boundaries. I see an unresolved privacy question, not evidence of a severe violation.

One verifiable result would make me reconsider: a cohort of 30 paying research teams completing substantive cross-application projects in River every week for eight consecutive weeks, with usage records showing repeated delegation. That would demonstrate users reorganizing real work around the product. Today, River’s strongest signal is its functioning product and reported creation activity; its weakest primary signal is the evidenced path from that activity to a distinctive future. I am interested enough to try it, but the dossier has not earned my check.
