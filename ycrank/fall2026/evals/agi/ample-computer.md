# Ample Computer -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Ample could make small, multi-service web apps economical for builders who would otherwise abandon deployment or hire someone to do it. Ample says it reads the app’s source, provisions the required services, and returns a live URL. Its salon-booking test reportedly required nearly ten separate services before consolidation. The memo also records a verified Claude connector. This supports a plausible way to reduce setup labor, but the memo gives no like-for-like measure of human time through deployment, retries, and recovery, or evidence that the savings brought previously excluded builders into the market. (The Idea)

Ample publishes 2,144 deployment recipes, evidence of integration breadth. If cheap models can generate equivalent infrastructure plans, builders could still pay Ample to provision and operate the app, provided its hosting and recovery deliver an advantage they value. The memo gives no comparative deployment success rates or evidence of paid preference under those conditions. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

Customer testimonials report 55 order or checkout submissions for a shop and about 30 registrations for a business fair. These are early signs of live use. The launch count reached a reported 5,000 users, but paid conversion and repeat deployment are unknown. The founders’ prior work on Shopify App Store product and infrastructure is relevant to shipping this platform. The memo gives less evidence of their ability to keep hands-on support low across varied production apps. Ample’s San Francisco location and YC seed entry make it a plausible stage and geography fit. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team, memo header)

At full plan utilization, the listed Pro and Scale prices yield $2.00 and $1.98 per app per month, respectively. That scenario assumes customers use every included slot. The economics of adding a customer depend on what their apps consume in compute, storage, bandwidth, and email, plus the human cost of setup and exceptions. The memo supplies prices, while utilization and delivery costs remain unknown. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Ample may make an existing hosting purchase easier while leaving the same buyers and transactions in place. Given abundant capital and equally capable models, Railway and Render already have a route to automate provisioning inside the same coding assistants. Ample relies on Hetzner for bare metal, and the memo identifies no scarce capacity or access rights Ample controls that would support Path A. The competitors’ agent tools make substitution credible, while equivalent end-to-end performance and actual switching remain untested. Neither Path B condition clears the outreach gate, and the evidence leaves both open to a test. (Defensibility, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will builders who previously abandoned comparable multi-service apps pay to deploy again on Ample after trying an alternative with access to the same coding agent? A comparative paid pilot should measure human time per successful deployment at comparable reliability, including retries and recovery, and record why builders choose where to deploy their next app. Material labor savings that make deployment viable for those builders, followed by repeat paid use that they attribute to Ample’s hosting or operations, would move the case toward Reach out if the updated score clears the threshold. If the pilot shows that the savings fail to open demand or that builders readily switch to an equivalent alternative, move to Pass.
