# Parasma -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Cortical Labs’ 2026 API access made living-neuron systems programmable by independent teams; the prospective buyer is a biological-computing platform or AI developer, and the controlling variable is whether trained neurons deliver useful computation per dollar and joule before biological degradation destroys the advantage. Parasma has not yet measured that variable. There is no disclosed accuracy, latency, energy, cost, or substrate-lifetime benchmark, so the central market belief remains falsifiable but untested.

The technical work is real. Sean Cole connected approximately 200,000 cultured neurons to Doom, and Scientific American reported that the hybrid system outperformed the learning algorithm alone. Parasma also controls a complete experimental loop—electrical encoding, neuronal response decoding, and reward feedback—rather than merely formatting another model’s output. That is the strongest part of the company: better training methods could compound into stimulation policies, response data, and evaluation infrastructure.

The bull case is that biological computing is still pre-crystallization. Cole has direct spiking-neural-network experience, built a working system before forming the company, attracted 255 GitHub stars and 29 forks, and has technical support from Cortical Labs and University of Sussex researchers. A research-stage frontier exception could justify investing before commercial pull. It does not yet apply, however: the dossier shows one MSc-trained founder rather than a demonstrably rare research team, no published economic step change, and no credible capital path through a category where funded competitors already control hardware and cloud access.

The market structure may squeeze Parasma’s current position. Cortical Labs has raised at least $10 million and offers hardware, APIs, and deployments; The Biological Computing Company has raised $25 million for neuron-derived model adapters; Intactis Bio is building rack-mounted systems. Parasma’s principal public implementation is GPL-licensed, it does not own the biological substrate, and its prospective proprietary dataset is inferred rather than documented. Covering an emerging discontinuity can be rational, but an algorithm layer without exclusive workflow state or customer ownership may accrue learning while the integrated platforms capture the economics.

Cole’s founder-market fit is unusually direct, but the biography provides no evidence of prior startup learning or costly personal commitment beyond building the experiment. The advertised $1 million salary for a founding scientist is attached to genuine bottlenecks—noise, non-stationarity, observability, and stimulation policy—but it is difficult to reconcile with a pre-seed financing plan. It suggests Parasma may need frontier-lab capital before proving whether it is funding a prototype or scaling something that works.

I pass today. My 36-month base case is that Parasma becomes a biological-training or evaluation supplier to an integrated hardware company, with less than $1 million ARR, rather than a standalone replacement for silicon AI compute. One result would flip the decision: a paid, independently reproducible 90-day deployment showing at least a 10× energy-efficiency advantage over a silicon baseline at comparable task accuracy while maintaining stable culture performance. That would validate the controlling variable, establish product pull, and show that the physical substrate changes the economics rather than merely producing an impressive demonstration.
