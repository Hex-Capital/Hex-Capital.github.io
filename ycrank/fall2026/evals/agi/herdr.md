# herdr -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B, new economies, is the strongest path.** Herdr ships a runtime that keeps terminal processes alive across client disconnects and surfaces agents waiting for input across user-controlled hosts. As coding agents get cheaper, the plausible new use is for small engineering teams to run more unattended jobs than they could justify supervising manually. The workflow is shipped. The memo has no like-for-like measurement of human time per successfully completed job, including approvals, retries, and recovery, or evidence that teams expanded the work they ran because supervision got cheaper. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The original TUI had 51.6K GitHub stars and 2.16M company-reported cumulative installs. These show interest in the free tool, while repeat paid use by newly served teams remains untested. Herdr’s listed plugin and mod libraries give users concrete ways to customize the product. Those customizations could make switching costly, but there is no measured retention, network benefit, or paid preference under functional parity. Can Celik built the original tool for his own workflow and shipped across desktop and mobile as sole founder. Reliable managed delivery remains the next execution test. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

**Scale scenario.** Assume Herdr Cloud charges per team for connecting its own hosts and those hosts continue to bear agent compute costs. Contribution from each added team would be the subscription price less coordination infrastructure, support, and incident recovery. The waitlist has no disclosed price, and the memo gives no paid conversion or per-team cost data. The reported seed round fits the target stage. Herdr’s Ankara location leaves geographic fit uncertain under the primarily U.S. strategy. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Herdr may make existing coding-agent work more convenient while leaving the economics of delegation unchanged. Host reboots stop agent processes, so recovery labor could absorb the time saved on routine monitoring. At model parity, single-provider teams already have overlapping native supervision, cmux ships agent-status notifications, and competitors can build on Herdr’s Apache-licensed core. With abundant capital, the memo identifies no scarce input or access right Herdr controls for a separate Path A case. The overlap leaves both Path B conditions unproven. There is no like-for-like evidence that teams would switch away, so the override does not close the path. (Key Risks, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Will small engineering teams that previously limited parallel agent work run more jobs and renew a paid Herdr Cloud pilot when an alternative offers equivalent supervision with the same models? A representative trial should show lower human time per successfully completed job at comparable quality, including approvals, retries, and recovery, followed by repeat paid use at a price that covers delivery costs. A positive result could move the action to Reach out if the updated score clears 65 and mandate fit is established. A credible trial showing no newly viable work or that teams switch away under parity would move the action to Pass.
