# Spine -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

New Economies is Spine’s strongest path. Founders and operations leads already spend time resolving payroll exceptions, coordinating with carriers, and meeting filing deadlines. Spine says its specialists take over that work in the customer’s existing HR systems, review AI-drafted responses, and fund the service through carrier commissions at $0 in employer fees. The plausible new use is fully outsourced HR operations for small startups that previously did the work in-house because outsourcing was too expensive. The memo establishes the prior workflow, but gives no baseline labor cost or comparison of cost per correctly completed task. The reported 15% reduction in healthcare costs measures a different outcome. (The Idea, Key Risks, Market & Traction)

The founders report adoption by more than 30 startups, and the memo reports roughly 4.4× commission revenue growth over six months from an undisclosed base. Those are early demand signals. The memo leaves open whether customers were previously priced out of outsourcing and whether they renew. The founders previously built Feed together, and they trace Spine to HR work they handled at earlier startups. Cokar’s legal training and Spine’s launch support seed-stage execution credit, while handling exceptions reliably across customers remains untested in the memo. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

The expansion unit is one employer account. In a viable scenario, carrier commissions per account cover specialist time, exception handling, and software and inference costs. The memo provides neither commissions per account nor the labor and exception data needed to estimate contribution as Spine adds employers. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Spine may be offering an existing service at a lower sticker price by funding it with brokerage commissions. The memo documents overlap with Rippling’s HR support and Bennie’s tech-enabled brokerage. Under model parity, customers could still value having a specialist own the work in their existing systems, and the broker-of-record appointment gives Spine a way to earn revenue from that relationship. The memo shows no evidence that employers prefer Spine when a comparable broker offers the same service, or that Spine controls a scarce input. The competing offers make substitution credible. Whether buyers actually switch at parity and whether lower delivery costs bring new buyers into the market remain open, so the decision is Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Spine retain a cohort of startups that previously handled HR in-house because outsourcing was too expensive, while earning positive contribution through their first benefits renewal? Compare their prior labor per correctly completed payroll or compliance task with Spine’s delivery labor, including review and rework. Check that commissions cover delivery costs and that employers renew after considering comparable offers. Move to Reach out if the cohort shows a material labor reduction, positive contribution, and retention under comparable alternatives. Move to Pass if comparable providers readily win those accounts.
