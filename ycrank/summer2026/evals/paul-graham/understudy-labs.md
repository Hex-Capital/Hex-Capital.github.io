# Understudy Labs -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Luis Manrique closed $1.9 million in ARR at Gumloop, while Aamir Poonawalla spent ten years building Instacart’s ads, auctions, experiments, and logistics infrastructure. Understudy Labs is therefore a strong-pair, weak-schlep company: Understudy Labs has unusually credible cofounder configuration, but the dossier contains little evidence that the founders have chosen work other smart founders find viscerally unpleasant. They look capable. I do not yet know whether they are animals.

**Stripe** is the high rung for cofounder configuration: two brothers who had been hacking on commerce together long before Stripe existed. Understudy Labs has a similar, though weaker, mechanism. Manrique calls Poonawalla his longtime partner; they overlapped at Instacart and co-invented an advertising-campaign pacing patent. That is substantially better than founders paired for an application. Understudy Labs diverges from Stripe because “longtime partner” does not establish years of building together outside an employer, and the public artifacts do not yet reveal the Collisons’ almost alarming technical intensity.

**Kiko** represents the low schlep rung: a neat software product whose failure did not reveal a difficult operational or regulatory problem that became more valuable as the founders endured it. Understudy Labs risks the same software-shaped trap. Capturing traces, constructing evals, tuning weights, and routing models are real work, but Fireworks, Baseten, Together AI, and internal ML teams can assemble overlapping workflows. Understudy Labs has no named compliance burden, physical operation, or integration ordeal that competitors are routing around. Private design-partner migrations may become a schlep, but the dossier has not shown that yet.

Luis Manrique’s $1.9 million ARR record can compound into resourcefulness if Understudy Labs turns each private-preview engagement into a manual migration playbook, then turns that playbook into software over the next 18 months. Understudy Labs has already shipped an MIT-licensed agent toolkit and recruited a small group of design partners, which are tracks rather than slides. But 9 GitHub stars, 5 forks, and no disclosed customer or revenue are not an Airbnb cereal-box moment. The evidence says Manrique can sell and Poonawalla can build; it does not yet show what either will do after the obvious route fails.

Aamir Poonawalla’s decade inside Instacart makes the idea plausibly organic, but Understudy Labs never says that Poonawalla repeatedly suffered through frontier-model replacement himself and built Understudy Labs first as his own tool. That distinction matters. Founder-market fit is not the same as an organic idea. To move this score into the top tier, Understudy Labs would need to show that a concrete Instacart or Gumloop workload produced the original trace-to-eval-to-routing system before customers requested it. Without that origin, I see informed opportunity selection rather than Dropbox’s forgotten USB stick.

**Dropbox** is the useful hacker-genealogy parallel because Drew Houston’s demo established technical taste before market evidence existed. Understudy Labs has a credible artifact in its public agent-tools repository, and Poonawalla’s ads-serving, auction, experimentation, and logistics work suggests he has built systems that cannot merely look correct in a demo. Understudy Labs nevertheless has a thin public trail: Manrique’s `moraine` has 0 stars, Poonawalla’s `eathquake` has 0 stars, and the company toolkit has only 9. The patent and production history put Understudy Labs above credential-only engineering, but below the unmistakable hacker signal that made Dropbox legible early.

Fireworks AI’s more than $1 billion in annualized revenue makes the next 36 months unusually unforgiving. By July 2029, I expect Understudy Labs to have pivoted from “move to open-weight models” into an enterprise evaluation-and-routing control plane and reached roughly $1 million to $3 million in ARR, without becoming the default model-migration layer. Understudy Labs has the pair required to build a useful business, but absent a proprietary schlep, the better-capitalized inference platforms can absorb the obvious product surface.

Luis Manrique’s $1.9 million ARR history is the strongest objection to that forecast. The bull case is that Understudy Labs has paired a proven seller with a ten-year infrastructure builder, obtained design partners before launch, and deliberately gives customers portable weights instead of trapping them. **Loopt** is the relevant exception: weak timing can be overcome when the founder’s force is sufficiently unusual. But Understudy Labs currently supplies no equivalent animal evidence, no named paying customer, and no migration result competitors cannot reproduce. I would flip to Invest if Understudy Labs converts its preview partners into at least $500,000 ARR by July 2027 while documenting two production workloads moved to open weights without degrading held-out quality.
