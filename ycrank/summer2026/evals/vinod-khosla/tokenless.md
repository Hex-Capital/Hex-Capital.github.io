# Tokenless -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

A defective reward function can make a model better at cheating while its developers believe they are buying capability. Tokenless attacks that failure directly. If EnvCheck reliably separates genuine progress from grader exploitation, it could improve how laboratories allocate training compute and decide which agents to deploy. That is consequential. But the dossier does not quantify wasted compute, displaced expert labor, or the improvement reliable auditing produces. I can describe a large consequence; I cannot yet underwrite its magnitude.

The strongest evidence is working technical output: company-reported **110 reward hacks and grading bugs across DeepSWE, Terminal-Bench, and BFCL**, with **66 write-ups** and public reproducible findings. EnvCheck inspects graders, probes sandboxed tasks, shares hypotheses, and requires objective checks or human confirmation. This establishes more than a fashionable interface. It does not establish a 5–10x discontinuity. We lack comparative detection rates, false-positive rates, and compute costs against reference-solution checks or an expert auditor. Finding defects proves the machinery can work; the denominator determines how valuable it is.

The strongest argument against passing is that Tokenless may already possess the beginning of a scarce scientific workflow. Agarwal brings Princeton agent-alignment research and DeepMind experience; Wang has built GPU infrastructure at SkyPilot and worked at Eventual. Combine those capabilities with reproducible discoveries and October’s unnamed early partners, and audit engagements could generate the training material for specialized auditor models. That fits my preference for workflows that become repeatable production platforms. However, the proprietary red-teaming model is future work. The public methodology and MIT-licensed grader make replication plausible, while the dossier establishes neither exclusive data access nor a demonstrated learning advantage.

The first dollars should resolve whether an auditor remains trustworthy when the target starts exploiting the auditor itself. Tokenless explicitly acknowledges judge exploitation; upgrading the judge is a proposed mitigation, not evidence that the problem is controlled. The dossier supplies no costed experiment, numerical acceptance threshold, or stop condition for this risk. Likewise, the September announcement followed by October’s recorded transition from Touchy demonstrates a change of direction, but does not reveal which evidence changed the founders’ beliefs. Relevant credentials deserve attention; they cannot substitute for observed learning or recruiting against a specific weakness.

Commercially, Tokenless solicits audits but discloses no current-product pricing or delivery economics. Touchy’s **$14.99** purchase option tells me nothing about EnvCheck’s margins. The economic question is cost per independently confirmed, consequential defect, including sandbox compute and human review. Vals AI and Gray Swan establish adjacent activity, not Tokenless’s spending baseline or willingness to pay. I would not infer that this team is merely wrapping standard models, nor treat its shared Berkeley origins as proof of poor recruiting. Those red flags remain unproven. The unresolved issue is whether expertise becomes scalable software before buyers reproduce the workflow internally.

I pass at present. My exceptions for pre-product bets require a documented extraordinary instigator addressing a giant existing value pool, or demonstrated technical progress with overwhelming consequences. This dossier establishes useful discoveries without establishing that override. One verifiable result could change my decision: **a blinded audit of previously unseen proprietary RL environments that finds at least five times as many independently confirmed material defects per dollar as the buyer’s existing process, with human-review costs included and the auditor’s judge subjected to adversarial testing**. That would connect Tokenless’s strongest evidence to technical leverage and economic value. Today, that connection remains open.
