# Open Actuation -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a **strategic acquisition by an established propulsion supplier** seeking to add quick-turn custom motor production. The memo records early heavy-lift motor samples for prospective customers. Open Actuation says it built a custom shaft motor in five weeks after the OEM received a 13-week quote with a $50,000 minimum from its existing supplier. The company also says it is fulfilling volume orders for an unnamed drone OEM and that its software and tooling are proprietary. A buyer could gain control of production scheduling and reuse the software, tooling, and test data across its own customers. That ownership advantage remains an inference from one reported production ramp. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is repeat volume production across multiple OEM designs, with measured lead times, yields, reliability, and unit costs. The reported factory and initial order ramp give Open Actuation a plausible starting point. Qualification costs, runway, and reliable input supply remain unresolved. The memo also leaves ownership and transfer rights for the software and tooling unverified. The central evidence for repeatability and buyer willingness to pay is limited, which keeps confidence Low. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** Using the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership dilutes 40% relative to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash. Under a simple pro rata waterfall, that requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The memo supplies no usable acquisition valuation or buyer economics. Catalog list prices provide no basis for estimating an acquisition price. (The Idea, Key Facts)

The sale scenarios below assume no applicable net debt, other price adjustments, or senior claims, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. All consideration is cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. Prices and timing are illustrative.

| Scenario | Timing and assumed outcome | Gross holder cash and multiple |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | Year 5, $40 million enterprise value and distributable equity | $1.2 million, 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7, $100 million enterprise value and distributable equity | $3 million, 6.0× at 3% ownership. At 2%, $2 million, 4.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested | $0, 0.0× realized, with shares still held |

The memo supports no independent fallback that could deliver cash to this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An acquirer could place ordinary supply orders, use KDE Direct’s existing custom-build offering, or build the capability internally. Open Actuation’s reported five-week build suggests an advantage over one supplier quote. The memo gives no comparable lead-time, cost, or qualification data for KDE Direct, and identifies no structural barrier to competing. If a supply agreement delivers comparable results, ownership could command too little to clear the proceeds hurdle. The available evidence leaves that outcome unresolved, so it supports Watch. Neither the modeled $0 outcome nor the available substitute establishes a Pass override. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could a propulsion supplier justify for owning Open Actuation’s production process after repeat volume runs across multiple OEM designs? Measured lead times, yields, and unit costs, paired with a credible buyer’s comparison of acquiring the capability, building it, or contracting for supply, would answer it. Move to Reach out if that comparison supports a price that could deliver at least $2.5 million to this holder within eight years under plausible dilution and deal terms.
