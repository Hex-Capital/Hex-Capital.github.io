# Blinka -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Blinka’s strongest route is a strategic sale to a business banking platform seeking U.S.-incorporated customers that operate in Latin America. Blinka’s documented product combines remote USD account opening with local payouts. Mercury’s requirement for existing or planned U.S. operations illustrates the customer gap. The acquisition rationale is that buying a retained customer base and its payout workflows could be faster than building both. Buyer interest and the value of that shortcut remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The memo records more than 400 businesses onboarded in the first two months and rising reported payment volume. The CEO reported more than $18 million in cumulative volume by November 4. A plausible next milestone is two consecutive quarters of repeat payouts from the same customer cohorts at positive contribution after partner costs. Early usage supports testing that milestone, while the budget and runway for the current product are unknown. The prior financing dates to the real-estate product era. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The 5× objective is $2.5 million in cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale prices and deductions below are illustrative assumptions. Both sale cases assume a full cash sale within eight years, holder eligibility for the full 3% pro rata share, and no preference or other waterfall deductions beyond the stated adjustments.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Cash to holder |
|-----------|-----------------------------|----------------|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments = $35M distributable | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments = $90M distributable | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or payout | $0 realized |

At the illustrative strong price, net debt, deal adjustments, and any senior claims above roughly $16.7 million combined would put the holder below the objective. The memo documents fee terms, while realized margins and evidence supporting an acquisition price remain unavailable. It supplies no evidence of an independent route to holder liquidity. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Slash and Jeeves already offer overlapping capabilities, and Blinka relies on Portage Bank, Third National, and Mural Pay for core services. A buyer could build comparable features or use its existing infrastructure if Blinka’s customers prove easy to win directly. An acquisition could preserve the operating relationships, but change-of-control terms and any required consents are unverified. These risks weaken the case for paying the required price. The memo establishes neither a closing block nor a supported price ceiling below the proceeds hurdle, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Could a strategic buyer justify at least $83.3 million in distributable equity value for Blinka’s retained customer base? Cohort data on repeat usage and contribution after partner costs would let a buyer compare acquisition with building the same capability. A credible bridge from those economics to $2.5 million in holder cash within eight years would move the action to Reach out. A supported price ceiling materially below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
