# Petrichor -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** Petrichor targets the time it takes frontier AI biology teams to turn physical experiments into training-ready records. Petrichor says in-house labs take years to build and outsourced results can arrive weeks after benchwork. It proposes running experiments in parallel and returning step-by-step records. The memo documents a public launch and a $300 million commitment to the Virtual Biology Initiative, a signal of category spending. Petrichor’s turnaround, reproducibility, and buyer commitments remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Assume cheaper models let buyers propose more experiments and abundant capital adds competing bench capacity. The constraint that could still bind is qualified capacity to deliver reproducible results in a form buyers can train on. Petrichor could capture value if buyers qualify its workflows and switching requires meaningful requalification. Customer ownership of the records makes workflow qualification the more plausible source of switching costs. (The Idea, Defensibility)

For a plausible U.S. seed entry, the founders’ published scientific-measurement benchmark shows relevant delivery experience, while repeat wet-lab execution remains unverified. Under an assumed per-batch pricing model, contribution depends on price per accepted batch less bench labor, consumables, quality review, and reruns. Expansion also depends on equipment utilization and the cost of adding capacity. The memo leaves these economics unmeasured. (Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Incumbents may already supply enough of what buyers need. Emerald captures structured experimental metadata, and Ginkgo reports that OpenAI uses its cloud lab for data generation. That overlap puts pressure on Petrichor’s ability to retain value, especially if staff-run execution limits throughput. Equivalent turnaround and reproducibility for Petrichor’s target work remain untested, so Path A stays open. Path B remains unproven because the memo describes staff-run delivery and leaves automation-driven labor savings and newly viable purchases unmeasured. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks, The Idea)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent frontier AI buyer place a paid repeat order after a head-to-head qualification run? The decisive result is a reorder at a price that covers measured delivery costs after Petrichor demonstrates faster delivery of equally reproducible, training-ready records and the buyer confirms that switching would require material requalification of a workflow Petrichor controls. That result would support **Reach out** if rescoring clears 65. A like-for-like result showing ready substitution with negligible switching costs would move the action to **Pass**. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
