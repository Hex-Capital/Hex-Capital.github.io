# Vorflux -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest path. A developer-tools platform could buy Vorflux to integrate recurring engineering work, approval routing, and browser verification into its own product. Ownership could give the buyer control over that workflow and its roadmap and shorten the time to deploy it across customers. That rationale depends on Vorflux proving a production advantage that a buyer would struggle to reproduce or license. The memo reports that Cursor joined SpaceX, which establishes a transaction in the category. The memo supplies no price or buyer economics applicable to Vorflux. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The memo records a $15 million seed announcement and the November AutoBots launch. Vorflux says AutoBots can take on recurring responsibilities and select tasks proactively. Separately, TrashLab reports shipping a feature from a Slack request, and Supermemory reports fixes across six repositories and a backfill that closed about ten issues. Those accounts show concrete uses of the platform. Sustained paid AutoBots usage, measurable savings, and retention remain unverified. The seed financing makes a production test plausible, with runway and test costs unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

**Proceeds bridge.** The memo supplies no terms for the proposed check. The illustrative case uses a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The 5× gross cash objective within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or $83.3 million of distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

| Illustrative scenario | Timing from check | Enterprise value | Distributable equity | Holder cash at 3% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest acquisition | Year 4 | $40M | $40M | $1.2M (2.4×) |
| Strong acquisition | Year 7 | $100M | $100M | $3.0M (6.0×) |
| Prolonged private ownership | Through year 8 | No sale | No distribution | $0 (0×), shares retained |

The sale prices are scenario assumptions. Both assume zero net debt, no transaction fees or senior claims, and a simple pro rata waterfall. All consideration is cash at close. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. At 2% ownership, even the strong sale yields only $2 million (4×). In the prolonged-private case, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. The memo provides no evidence of an independent route to holder cash. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Devin already offers event-triggered work, and Cursor already offers browser testing with video evidence. A prospective buyer could extend existing tools or license Vorflux and capture enough of the benefit to make an acquisition premium uneconomic. The memo identifies no structural barrier to replication. Customer-controlled deployment and reliance on third-party model access also leave open what a buyer could transfer and operate after closing. The overlap is concrete, while equivalent performance on recurring production workflows remains untested in the memo. The evidence supports Watch. A structural Pass would require evidence that a substitute caps the price below the hurdle or that another binding barrier prevents meaningful holder proceeds. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Vorflux demonstrate a repeatable advantage on recurring work in paid enterprise production that makes owning the workflow economically preferable to building or licensing it? A customer-verified comparison showing sustained reductions in engineering coordination and review time at comparable quality, with savings or time to market that could plausibly support the roughly $83 million equity hurdle for a buyer, would move the action to Reach out. Comparable results from readily available tools would move it to Pass if their cost puts the acquisition price below the hurdle.
