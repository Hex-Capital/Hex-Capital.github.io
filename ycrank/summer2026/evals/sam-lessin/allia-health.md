# Allia Health -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first problem is not clinical; it is capital. Allia Health has already raised $2 million from Draper Associates and Stanford Healthcare Innovation Lab angels, but the dossier gives no post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is a genuinely scarce first check. I cannot tell whether I am underwriting a discarded belief or buying into a YC auction after the uncertainty has been marked up. For my money, that silence is a gate, not a rounding error.

The operating evidence is far better. Allia reports daily use by 8,000 clinicians across 1,000 practices, touching 300,000 patients and nearly one million monthly visits. Six hundred clinicians have joined its clinical group, and signed national payer contracts cover $50 million in annualized care capacity. That is not follower-count theater: the company appears to own a workflow that has induced practices to join a new contracting structure. The translation layer between fragmented clinicians and national payers is the investable asset, while the AI features are merely optionality.

But capacity is not cash. Contracted care does not begin until September 2026, so Allia has not yet shown that claims get paid, clinicians remain activated, or value-based contracts produce positive contribution economics. The obvious experiment is converting those signed contracts into collected revenue and retained clinical participation. Yet the dossier never says the $2 million finances that single test, what result constitutes success, or what happens to enterprise value if it works. A launch date is not a capital plan.

The strongest bull case is that very few pre-seed companies have anything resembling 8,000 daily clinician users, 600 activated providers, and signed payer contracts. Those facts could override missing terms if they translated into the sort of paid conversion and retention that makes financing details secondary. They do not—yet. The free EHR for practices under 20 clinicians produces no disclosed revenue, while gross margin, burn, runway, and payer reimbursement economics are absent. Headway already offers a free AI-assisted behavioral-health EHR backed by $327.5 million, so software adoption alone does not establish a defensible or self-financing business.

The team has shipped the current system at meaningful scale, and the combination of Amie Leighton’s healthcare work, Saroosh Khan’s technical execution, and clinical co-founders supplies access to both product and care delivery. What is missing is an operating receipt from the harder side: no prior payer-risk business, multi-state clinical group, or revenue-cycle operation is documented. That matters when Allia is simultaneously coordinating prescribing, billing, outcomes, national contracts, 32-state compliance, and protected health information while SOC 2 and HITRUST certification remain in progress.

I pass today. My 36-month base case is that Allia retreats from owning a national clinical group and becomes a paid revenue-cycle or enterprise operating-system vendor at roughly $3 million–$8 million ARR, because the workflow is stronger than the disclosed care economics. The verifiable flip condition is at least $5 million in annualized collected net revenue from the activated clinical group, with positive contribution margin and over 90% twelve-month clinician retention. That would prove the contracts are a business rather than capacity dressed as traction.
