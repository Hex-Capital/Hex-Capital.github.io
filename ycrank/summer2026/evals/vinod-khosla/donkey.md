# Donkey -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Prelude OS acquired 31 paying Chinese factory customers in 39 days, while Donkey now takes title to goods and delivers duty-paid to American importers. Donkey’s signature is high founder learning rate paired with low technical risk: an adaptive trading company, not a miracle company. Donkey may remove 15–30% intermediary markups, but no economic regime breaks. One intermediary replaces another more efficiently. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself” does not mean make procurement somewhat cheaper.

**Slash** is the high rung on learning rate because its documented pivot revealed founders who changed their thesis when reality contradicted it. Benjamin Martindale and Minghao Tan share that mechanism: Prelude OS reached cash-flow positivity, then Donkey moved from factory software to becoming the US counterparty and importer of record. Donkey diverges from Slash because the dossier never explains what Prelude taught the founders, what failed, or why taking inventory and credit risk was the necessary thesis update. Donkey demonstrates a pivot; it does not yet demonstrate the reasoning quality behind the pivot.

**DoorDash** sits on the low rung for technical or capital risk: no scientific miracle, no research breakthrough, and no reason a generalist could not fund it. Donkey shows the same absence. Donkey’s customs parser, photo-based factory identification, and inspectors across seven provinces are execution assets, but ImportYeti already searches 70 million bills of lading and Sourcify already reports more than 3,000 factories. Working capital, net-45 credit, and first-sale customs documentation create financial and regulatory risk. They do not create the falsifiable technical risk I backed in QuantumScape or Commonwealth Fusion.

The reported 15–30% trading-company markup is Donkey’s consequence mechanism, but Donkey compounds only if settlement data turns procurement into a superior underwriting and supplier-selection system. Over 18 months, every Donkey order could improve factory attribution, defect prediction, landed-cost quoting, and buyer credit decisions. That is useful compounding. It still optimizes an existing China-to-US supply chain instead of collapsing one: Donkey becomes a more transparent trading company in a $7.43 billion procurement-services market. The red flag is precise—20–30% improvement dressed as transformation.

Benjamin Martindale speaks Mandarin, advised Sany and Sinoboom, and drove from Vermont to San Francisco to visit wholesale distributors. Donkey’s founders are unusually close to the problem, but Donkey provides no evidence that either founder has contradicted documented expert consensus. To flip this dimension into invest territory, Donkey would need to prove a disputed claim—for example, that shipment records plus transaction data can identify the true factory and underwrite delivered pricing materially better than experienced importers across thousands of SKUs. Domain experience alone is expert continuity. My TED warning was that experts extrapolate the past; Donkey has not yet shown the instigator who invalidates it.

**Commonwealth Fusion** required $115 million at Series A because the first commercial milestone could not exist without capital-hostile science. Donkey takes title, carries goods through delivery, and offers credit-insured net-45 terms, so Donkey could consume substantial working capital as gross merchandise volume grows. The parallel ends there. Donkey’s capital buys inventory and receivables rather than a technical milestone unavailable to lightly funded competitors. That makes Donkey financeable, but it makes my check size a balance-sheet convenience rather than a unique strategic fit.

Donkey will, within 36 months, either pivot into a software-enabled trade-finance and importer-of-record platform or remain below $5 million in annualized net revenue; Donkey will not establish a defensible AI-native factory-identification network in that period. The mechanism is the dimensional signature: Martindale and Tan have already shown enough learning velocity to abandon one product, while Donkey’s lowest-scoring axis offers no technical breakthrough to protect the present model from ImportYeti, Sourcify, Jiga, or ordinary trading companies.

Donkey’s strongest bull case is the **DoorDash–Slash exception**: Donkey could combine gritty offline execution with a founder pivot, accumulate proprietary settlement data, and turn a mundane intermediary into the default transaction network. Donkey’s inspectors across seven provinces, Prelude’s 31 customers in 39 days, and Martindale’s importer visits make that case sting. It loses today because Donkey discloses no current orders, repeat usage, quote accuracy, defect advantage, or structural barrier, while first-sale valuation and net-45 exposure can scale losses before data becomes a moat. I would flip to Invest if Donkey reaches 100 repeat importers with independently verifiable gross-margin retention and materially lower defect-adjusted landed costs than incumbent trading companies.
