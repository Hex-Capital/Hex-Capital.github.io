# Lantern AI -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to an applicant tracking system vendor is Lantern’s strongest supported route. Lantern says its agents share employer-approved hiring criteria and feedback across sourcing, screening, and interviews while working on top of an existing ATS. Ashby’s acquisition of AI interviewer technology shows that an ATS vendor has bought an adjacent capability. A buyer could have a reason to acquire Lantern if that shared hiring history produces measurable gains across workflows. The memo gives no comparative performance data or evidence of buyer interest. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Lantern reports $1M in enterprise annual recurring contract revenue. It also reports one account expanding from about $11K to $92K annually, and a customer describes screening more than 1,000 candidates and hiring one engineer. These signals support a seed-stage milestone over the next 12–18 months: show repeatable paid expansion across multiple workflows and measure recruiter time saved against native ATS tools. Existing deployments and YC backing make the test plausible. Runway and the capital needed to complete it remain unknown. Customer contract assignment and data-use rights also remain open, and the memo supports no independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500K check at a $10M post-money valuation, with 5% ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5M gross cash objective within eight years. That requires about $83.3M in distributable equity value. Both sale scenarios assume a full cash sale within eight years, an eligible holder selling their entire stake, and a pro rata waterfall with no senior preference deductions. The ARR, multiples, and deal deductions below are illustrative assumptions.

- **Modest sale.** At $8M ARR and 5× ARR, $40M of enterprise value less $5M in net debt and other deal deductions leaves $35M for equity holders. The holder receives $1.05M, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** At $15M ARR and 6× ARR, $90M of enterprise value less $5M in deductions leaves $85M for equity holders. The holder receives $2.55M, or 5.1×. Another $5M in deductions reduces the payout to $2.4M, below the objective. The memo supplies no transaction pricing to support the assumed multiple. (Market & Traction, Defensibility)
- **Prolonged private operation.** If Lantern reinvests available cash and the holder has no eligible share sale or funded payout, realized proceeds are $0 within eight years.

### Countercase

Ashby already offers overlapping automation and has acquired interviewer technology, while Workday is embedding candidate grading in its recruiting workflow. Buyers could cover enough of Lantern’s functionality inside their own products, particularly if Lantern’s integrations limit access to customer records. That weakens the case for paying a price that clears the holder’s hurdle. The memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a credible price ceiling below the hurdle, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would an ATS buyer value Lantern’s measured gains across recruiting workflows at a price that leaves at least $83.3M in distributable equity? A buyer-side valuation range tied to repeatable customer gains and clearing that hurdle after deal deductions would move the action from Watch to Reach out.
