# InfraOptimal -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a small team running its own models while software continuously rearranges the machinery underneath: propose a change, verify it, measure it on the actual device, keep what works. InfraOptimal is building pieces of that loop. But the six-year picture is my extrapolation. The dossier does not establish how today’s dedicated endpoint becomes that future, which transitions must happen first, or what accumulates along the way. I want to know whether every optimization makes the next one easier—or whether the team starts over with every customer.

The September 6 compiler experiment gives me something concrete to examine. Approximately 3–5.6% lower decoder time on an Apple M2 Pro is actual engineering evidence. The warning that the same transformation could regress on microcontrollers and DSPs is equally useful: the machine gets a vote. InfraOptimal’s scoped Z3 proof establishes an integer identity, not whole-program correctness, and the experiment involved agent assistance rather than a fully autonomous product run. This fits my pattern of looking beneath a fashionable application for enabling machinery. It earns infrastructure credit, but it does not yet demonstrate control over a scarce bottleneck or a reusable optimization advantage ([research](https://infraoptimal.com/news/compiler-as-research-system.html)).

Cassidy’s processor-validation work, including 600-plus RISC-V certification tests, and Kocheta’s robotics and research background make this a credible team to investigate that machinery. Their complementary specialties and decision to leave school together are meaningful commitments. I cannot turn those facts into a lifelong obsession story: the dossier lacks the dated sequence of independent experiments and first believers that would establish it. The distinction matters because serving endpoints adds capacity procurement, reliability, and customer operations to compiler research. Being friends since fourth grade tells me something about their relationship; it does not answer who ultimately decides when those priorities collide ([About](https://infraoptimal.com/about); [YC founder profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/founders/james-cassidy)).

Why does the customer’s GPU sit underused, and who gets paid while it waits? Manual performance engineering is a specific labor bottleneck, so automating it could return control to small teams. Yet InfraOptimal bills for reserved capacity by the minute, including idle time. That arrangement can be perfectly reasonable, but improved utilization does not automatically become a lower customer bill. I need the connection demonstrated. The self-service console and coding-agent integration provide ways to reach users; neither establishes repeated paid behavior. Missing revenue is ordinary at this stage. Missing evidence of anyone repeatedly entrusting a workload to this loop leaves the product-immersion test unanswered ([website](https://infraoptimal.com/); [Terms](https://infraoptimal.com/terms)).

The strongest argument against my pass is that a technically capable pair has already produced measurable optimization work, and an early enabling layer can deserve a check before customers arrive. My pre-revenue exception is relevant here. It does not fully carry: the dossier establishes neither the unusually deep founder history nor the external prototype commitments that would compensate for the missing behavioral evidence. Meanwhile, AWS already offers kernel-writing, debugging, and profiling agents, and Together performs compiler and kernel optimization. The risk is that InfraOptimal discovers useful transformations while larger platforms capture their commercial value. There is no documented severe privacy issue, founder-control defect, or dependence on one closed model supplier; I would not invent one. I pass because the independent layer’s advantage remains unproven. My single flip condition is a documented 90-day paid deployment showing repeated autonomous optimizations that preserve correctness and reduce the customer’s total bill against a named incumbent baseline.
