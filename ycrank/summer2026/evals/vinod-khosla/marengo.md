# Marengo -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Marengo is attacking a real cost pool: $21.93 billion in annual data-center design services, embedded in an infrastructure buildout where five technology companies spent more than $400 billion in 2025. But a large pool is not a large consequence. The disclosed outcome—twice the design speed at half the cost—could create a valuable engineering business; it does not yet show how power capacity, construction cost, or deployment time changes by an order of magnitude. This is a 2x workflow claim, not yet a 100x societal option.

The technical claim is similarly underproved. Combining generation, physics simulation, comparison, and stress testing is more interesting than wrapping a language model, particularly in a workflow constrained by 99.999% reliability. Yet Marengo provides no benchmark methodology, completed-project case study, or accuracy result against human engineering teams, Entangl, Augmenta, or incumbent BIM workflows. SWAPP already integrates with Revit and ArchiCAD. Until Marengo proves that it can produce materially better validated designs—not merely concepts faster—the binding constraint remains trust, not generation speed.

The founders are the strongest part of the case. Emil Ares brings physics, control systems, UK Space Agency work, and an earlier six-figure business; Gad Marconi brings aerospace engineering and an orbital-dynamics library. Their move from space-mission design in April 2026 to data centers by August shows tactical flexibility, and unnamed work with large builders may mean customer evidence drove the change. It may also mean they abandoned one demonstration market for a fashionable capital-spending wave. The dossier does not distinguish learning from opportunism, nor show the complementary recruiting required to own electrical, thermal, civil, and reliability risks.

The bull case stings: a three-person technical team has moved from aerospace simulation into an enormous, expertise-constrained workflow just as data-center electricity consumption grew 17%, and it claims contact with some of the world’s largest builders. Ordinary project work could generate proprietary designs, simulation outcomes, approval feedback, and construction data. That is the relevant unstated pattern—a scarce engineering workflow becoming a repeatable production platform. If usage actually produces that closed learning loop, today’s consulting-shaped wedge could become important infrastructure.

But no such loop is demonstrated. There is no disclosed dataset, revenue, repeat usage, integration, certification, or validation corpus. Nor is there a milestone plan naming the cheapest decisive test against the largest kill risk: for example, independently reproducing a completed data-center design within a specified error tolerance, at a stated cost, by a fixed date. Capital spent on sales or design services before that test would scale uncertainty rather than remove it.

I expect Marengo can become a modest engineering-automation business, perhaps reaching $2–5 million ARR within 36 months, but the present evidence does not support a defining technical platform. I pass with my own money. One result flips the decision: an independent comparison showing that Marengo produces a construction-ready data-center design at least 5x faster or cheaper than a named incumbent workflow while meeting identical reliability and code-compliance thresholds.
