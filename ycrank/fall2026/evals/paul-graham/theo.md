# Theo -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Miles Pines has shipped something. What I cannot find is the episode that would make me invest in him: a specific obstacle, what he did about it, and how quickly it worked. Harvard and Citadel do not answer that question. Bootstrapping Endgrate to a reported $100,000-plus annual revenue makes him worth meeting, but the dossier does not describe the resistance behind that result. For Theo, founder resourcefulness remains unmeasured. That is the main reason I would keep my money for now.

The strongest evidence is the product. Theo reports more than 10,000 sites built by September 28, 2026. Its offering extends beyond generating a first draft: hosting, analytics connections, and weekly proposed changes form a functioning service around a continuing job. That earns substantial technical credit. It does not establish 10,000 businesses that need Theo. A generated website is cheap enough that people can create one and forget it. The important transition is from “make me a website” to “keep doing this because it brings me customers.”

There is evidence of that transition. Buy My Lunch reportedly went from 131 Google clicks to 929 in the displayed comparison; 118 118 Money reportedly grew from 50,000 to 118,000 monthly organic visits. Those are more interesting than the site count. They are also company-selected outcomes, without enough information to establish attribution, payment, or retention across a particular customer niche. At $69.99 a month for the first ongoing-SEO tier, Theo could replace work a small business would otherwise neglect. But requiring a replacement website introduces a substantial obstacle before that recurring benefit begins. I want to know which businesses tolerate migration because they need the result badly enough.

The strongest argument against waiting is that Pines encountered this problem himself. He reports generating thousands of organic clicks for Endgrate, then doing similar work for business-owning friends. That gives Theo a credible origin, although the account is retrospective. The tedious work of connecting domains, analytics, and publishing could become its advantage if Pines personally gets customers through it. Durable already offers website creation with SEO/GEO, so the distinction must emerge from better customer results and learning. My preference for founders who assemble something substantial before claiming a platform supports the shipped product here; my pattern of backing programmable tools with developer pull supplies little additional evidence. Requests for introductions to banks and chambers are not yet evidence of the partnership-dependency red flag. Neither do they establish distribution.

A working product and unmistakable user pull can justify an exception to missing founder evidence. Theo has the former; these selected traffic results do not yet supply the latter strongly enough to override the gate. Solo founding is not my objection, and the dossier contains no evidence of rigidity. It simply documents no rejected hypothesis followed by a changed route. My single flip condition is a documented cohort of ten businesses whose domain or analytics setup initially blocked activation, which Pines personally unblocks within two weeks and which then renew through three paid months. That would connect the founder’s response to resistance with customers choosing to stay.
