# Parasma -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Sean Cole implemented closed-loop Doom training on roughly 200,000 neurons, but independent replication could not establish that the neurons—rather than the surrounding software—actually learned. Parasma is therefore an unusually fast-shipping founder wrapped around an unusually multi-miracle science project: Parasma’s strongest signal is Cole’s ability to turn an exotic substrate into a public artifact, while Parasma’s weakest is the number of unvalidated assumptions separating that artifact from durable compute economics.

**Perplexity** is the high rung for founder shipping: Aravind repeatedly returned a week later with the discussed feature built, converting iteration speed into an investable operating cadence. Parasma shares the important mechanism—Cole reportedly implemented the CL1 Doom experiment in approximately one week, published `doom-neuron`, attracted 250 GitHub stars, and subsequently demonstrated token prediction and reinforcement learning. Parasma diverges because Cole has shown one impressive build cycle, not Perplexity’s repeated build-measure-return loop with users; the dossier contains no evidence that Parasma has iterated between customer or investor conversations.

**Lyft** Series C is the low rung for the single-miracle test because the outcome required both market structure to become oligopolistic and Lyft to overcome the stronger network—two probabilities multiplying against the investor. Parasma requires more: living neurons must be proven to perform the learning, biological noise and non-stationarity must become manageable, workloads must beat silicon economically, Cortical Labs must remain a dependable substrate, and customers must accept unresolved bioethics governance. Parasma is not workflow infrastructure on top of a solved hard thing in the **Color Genomics** pattern; Parasma still needs the hard thing itself to work. That is the “science risk + capitalization risk + market risk” configuration I largely avoid.

Cortical Labs’ commercial CL1 and Python-accessible API are a real 2026 catalyst: an independent developer could access biological compute and build a Doom experiment in roughly one week. Over the next 18 months, that catalyst could compound for Parasma if additional substrate vendors expose compatible interfaces, making Parasma the portable training layer; it fails to compound if Cortical Labs, the $25 million-funded Biological Computing Co., and Intactis each vertically integrate their own stimulation stack. Parasma is skating for the puck in a newly accessible market, but the catalyst has opened experimentation—not yet commercial demand.

Parasma reported no product and no revenue on May 17, 2026, and the dossier provides no repeat usage, paid pilot, LOI, or workload-cost benchmark. The counterfactual is straightforward: if Parasma showed three independent laboratories repeatedly training useful tasks, with biological learning isolated through ablations and at least one paying hardware partner, the revenue-velocity score would move an entire tier even before meaningful ARR. Without that evidence, Parasma is a public demonstration rather than something “just working,” and in AI infrastructure the distinction between a false signal and buying behavior matters more than press from *The Guardian* or *Scientific American*.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel for index position: Color captured workflow and data value above commodity sequencing rather than betting on new sequencing chemistry. Parasma wants the analogous position above biological hardware, licensing training algorithms across substrates as biological compute grows. Parasma does not yet own that position: Cole’s principal code is open source, no proprietary dataset or patent portfolio is disclosed, and Parasma depends on Cortical Labs’ CL1 while Cortical Labs and the $25 million-funded Biological Computing Co. can build competing software themselves. Today Parasma is a complement to one supplier, not an index on biological compute.

Cortical Labs’ reported $11 million of funding and ownership of Parasma’s current hardware substrate shape my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Parasma will not have a scaled biological-compute product and will instead be acquired by Cortical Labs for less than $25 million as a training-and-benchmarking software acquihire. Parasma’s shipping strength can create valuable tooling, but Parasma’s multi-miracle architecture and weak independent distribution make a standalone infrastructure outcome unlikely.

The Biological Computing Co.’s $25 million financing supplies the strongest case against that forecast: serious capital is entering the category just as CL1 APIs remove the wet-lab barrier, and Cole has exactly the substrate-specific shipping history that let **Color Genomics** turn commodity sequencing into workflow infrastructure. In that bull case, Parasma becomes the universal training layer while hardware vendors compete underneath it—the biological-compute equivalent of Stripe’s developer layer. The dossier still loses the argument because biological learning has not been independently isolated, the central implementation is public, and Parasma has no paying integration proving hardware vendors will outsource the control plane. Two independent substrate vendors each paying Parasma at least $100,000 ARR for the same portable training stack would flip my verdict.
