# Outworld Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a robotics simulation platform operator such as NVIDIA. The memo records Horizon’s public launch and demo. Outworld says Horizon combines dexterous-manipulation physics, calibration against hardware measurements, scene reconstruction, and policy evaluation. A platform operator could have a reason to buy Horizon if that integrated workflow reduces physical testing and operator time enough to justify buying it over building equivalent capabilities. The memo gives no measured customer savings, buyer interest, or comparable acquisitions. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

A paid production pilot that measures physical testing time and hardware policy performance against an existing toolchain across two robot setups is a plausible next milestone given the demo and the founders’ robotics experience. Pilot funding, runway, and the calibration effort required for each setup remain unknown. The memo leaves ownership and transfer rights for the software and calibration data unverified and gives no evidence of an independent route to holder liquidity. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. Receiving $2.5 million in cash, or 5×, within eight years requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale cases below assume the holder is eligible to sell the entire stake, receives 3% of equity proceeds pro rata with no senior preference reducing that share, and is paid in cash within eight years. The prices and deductions are scenario assumptions.

- **Modest sale.** $40 million in enterprise value less $5 million in debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million in equity value and $1.05 million in holder cash, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value less $10 million in debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million in equity value and $2.7 million in holder cash, or 5.4×. If only 80% of that stake can be sold on the same terms within eight years, realized cash drops to $2.16 million, or 4.32×.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with reinvestment and no eligible share purchase or funded payout yields zero realized cash within eight years.

The strong case clears the hurdle on those assumptions. The memo leaves achievable pricing, sale eligibility, and the payout waterfall unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could extend its own tools or combine existing components. NVIDIA already offers contact-rich simulation in Isaac, MuJoCo Playground fits dynamics to logged hardware trajectories, and SuperDex supplies open-source tactile contact physics. If Horizon also requires substantial fresh calibration for each deployment, the labor savings and the rationale for acquisition could both disappear. The overlap is documented, but equivalence in production is unverified. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible price ceiling below the holder hurdle, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Could the production savings from owning Horizon justify a strategic purchase that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A paid pilot showing repeatable reductions in physical testing and operator time across two robot setups at comparable hardware policy performance, together with a credible buyer-side valuation tied to those savings, would move the action to Reach out if the resulting cash bridge clears the hurdle. Evidence that the buyer can match those results with its existing stack, or that the strongest credible sale leaves the holder materially short, would move the action to Pass.
