# twentyfour26 -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The useful fact about twentyfour26 is that the founders are running production and inspection themselves. They are exposing their software to metal, tolerances, and rejected parts. That is how specific knowledge develops. Mahendru’s aerospace MS and industrial affiliations provide context; operating the shop supplies stronger evidence. Mathew’s October 2 interview reports that manufacturing orders have begun. This earns meaningful founder credit, although the dossier does not yet identify delivered parts or operating results that establish exceptional machining depth.

The investment turns on what those jobs leave behind. twentyfour26 checks STEP geometry, selects stock and tooling, simulates machining, and prepares plans for its own CNC. Owning that entire feedback path could let the founders improve decisions that software vendors cannot directly observe. The strongest bull case is that each manufactured part teaches the system how to manufacture the next one with less intervention. But proprietary process learning is explicitly unverified. Today I can credit an integrated operating capability; I cannot price an accumulated learning advantage into the check.

Copy the software and buy a comparable machine: what remains difficult to reproduce? The dossier does not establish an answer through measured reliability, exclusive data, or a proprietary model. CloudNC’s deployment across more than 1,000 shops by September 2026 makes overlapping programming automation available to competitors. Physical infrastructure fits my recent interest in real operating bottlenecks, but buying hardware alone does not establish defensibility. The founders currently perform production and inspection while developing autonomy. The economic question is whether their attention eventually stops scaling with orders.

There is substantial demand around the wedge. Hardware teams already buy prototypes, and Xometry’s $686.6 million in FY2025 revenue establishes meaningful spending on outsourced manufacturing. However, the quoted $132.9 billion precision-machining market includes work twentyfour26’s current cell cannot necessarily serve. Stocked 6061-T6 aluminum provides a concrete starting envelope. The dossier supplies less evidence for which adjacent buyers and jobs that envelope unlocks. Its individual quotes establish a direct payer relationship, while undisclosed order quantities, receipts, and margins leave value capture only partly demonstrated.

twentyfour26 also lacks the participant mechanism I look for in fragmented markets. Customers submit CAD files to a company-operated shop; there is no demonstrated supplier network, referral loop, or reusable customer contribution that improves another customer’s experience. Process learning could eventually improve everyone’s parts, but that remains a hypothesis. I can make exceptions for an early prototype without distribution when founder insight and product differentiation are unusually strong. Here, the operating prototype makes that exception worth considering, while the unproven advantage keeps it from carrying the investment. The earlier Sutra pivot supplies no evidence of dishonest conduct or reflexive abandonment; the dossier establishes no integrity or financing-pressure veto.

I would hold off on a personal check today. The founders have begun acquiring the right knowledge, but twentyfour26 currently offers stronger evidence of an emerging manufacturing business than of compounding returns to its technology. My single flip condition is a documented cohort of repeat paid jobs showing declining human intervention per part while maintaining tolerance compliance and positive contribution margins. That would connect their costly operating experience to scalable economic value.
