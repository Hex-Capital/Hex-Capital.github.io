# EveryApp -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

EveryApp gives customers ownership of their applications and infrastructure. Good. What does EveryApp retain that customers will keep paying for? Deploying into the customer’s Cloudflare account, with shared authentication, permissions and credentials, could make replacing proprietary subscriptions practical. It also puts the durable workflow on the customer’s side. I like that direction of travel. I need evidence that the company facilitating it captures an economic asset.

Ben Senescu has actually built across the relevant boundary. OpenSEO is a shipped application, and EveryApp supplies the infrastructure beneath it. That earns substantial founder credit beyond his former senior-engineering title at Klaviyo. The strongest bull case is that he has used his own application to discover which deployment problems recur, then built the common layer. This fits my preference for founders translating between systems they understand through operating experience. But using the infrastructure yourself proves functionality; it does not establish that independent customers need the platform enough to pay.

The distribution evidence makes that distinction consequential. OpenSEO has 25.3K GitHub stars and claims 3,000-plus entrepreneurs. EveryApp had 351 stars following its October 26 launch, with 128,561 launch-post views recorded the next day. There is attention available to redirect toward the platform. The dossier does not document that redirection causing paid deployments or retained multi-app usage. I cannot turn OpenSEO’s popularity into EveryApp’s proprietary distribution by assertion. Nor does MIT licensing automatically kill the business: support, reliability and integration can be valuable. But Cloudron and Coolify already compete for deployment work, and EveryApp explicitly declines to promise backward compatibility. Dependable operation is precisely what a managed customer would be buying.

Now the deal. Post-money valuation, competing offers and whether this is the first scarce check are all undisclosed. So is a capital-funded experiment with a metric and deadline. Those receive my missing-evidence defaults; YC membership establishes neither an auction nor an attractive price. The announced managed service starts at $10 monthly, while self-hosting is free and customers pay Cloudflare separately. That architecture might support an efficient business, but pricing is not collected revenue, and customer-paid infrastructure does not establish EveryApp’s support costs, margins or runway. Delete the next financing: the dossier supplies no cash model to underwrite.

I pass on the evidence presented. This is not an objection to a two-person pre-seed company lacking a financial history. My strongest reasons to override missing financing gates would be demonstrated conversion, retained enterprise demand or operating economics; none is documented here. The exceptional-case logic therefore does not rescue the investment, although the shipped application makes the bull case credible. My verifiable condition for reopening is ten independent customers renewing paid EveryApp multi-app deployments for three consecutive months at positive contribution margin after support costs. That would show that the translation layer captures recurring value. Today, the founder has demonstrated the ability to build the bridge; the investment still depends on whether anyone pays to keep crossing it.
