# Moving Atoms -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what happened when the founders were blocked. Moving Atoms identifies a real obstacle—robot developers may wait days for physical tests—but the dossier does not show Shehryar or Ahmad encountering a specific failure, improvising a workaround, and changing the outcome within days or weeks. Building Atom 1 proves technical initiative, but it does not prove the people-first test by itself. I cannot infer resourcefulness from Oxford, TikTok, or a polished account of an industry problem.

The strongest evidence is Atom Harness. It completed 198 Physics-IQ Verified cases and scored 56.6, ranking second overall and first among the listed single-sample entries ([GitHub PR, Aug 20, 2026](https://github.com/google-deepmind/physics-IQ-benchmark/pull/71)). That is a measured artifact built before permission, not a diagram or funding-dependent research plan. But the proof is narrower than Moving Atoms suggests. The pull request remains open, the maintainer questioned whether the entry is a model or a composite harness, and the system combines Seedance 2.5 with Claude Opus 5. More importantly, Physics-IQ tests video prediction, not whether evaluating a checkpoint this way predicts success on a physical robot. It is good fierce-nerd evidence, but not yet robot evidence.

Who wants the crude product now? The website is recruiting a limited design-partner cohort through founder-led outreach, which is the right motion, but no named robot developer is reported to have supplied a checkpoint, returned for another evaluation, paid, or changed a policy because of Atom 1. The 41,000 launch-post views, 189 X followers, and Dealroom coverage are attention rather than pull. No pricing or revenue is public. This matters because Moving Atoms is entering a field containing Skild AI, Physical Intelligence, Genesis AI, World Labs, NVIDIA, and Google. Capital is not the decisive advantage, but actual episodes flowing back from desperate users could be.

The product history also needs an explanation. Moving Atoms previously offered hosted MolmoAct2 access with ten million free tokens per day and now presents Atom 1 as a world-model evaluation system. That may be a quick running-back change, but the dossier names neither the observation that falsified the old route nor the speed and result of the revision. Nor is there evidence that either brother personally suffered the robotics-evaluation problem. Shehryar’s video-recommendation experience helps explain the technical route, and accepting customer checkpoints, inference endpoints, and example episodes creates real integration schlep. It does not yet establish a founder-originated need.

The bull case is sharp: two mathematical founders shipped a public, numerical proof in a technically difficult market, are selling personally, and may use design-partner integrations to accumulate the paired rollout and failure data that becomes the moat. This resembles the unstated pattern of concrete proof spikes before a round. It also supplies the best argument for overlooking Ahmad’s youth and incomplete degree. But the relevant exception requires validation that reaches the claimed domain. An open composite video benchmark with known rotation and reflection errors does not yet override the absence of robot-policy results or user pull.

I would pass today. The operational gate fails because there is no documented blocker-to-workaround sequence, while the benchmark alone cannot substitute for both resourcefulness and demand. My one verifiable flip condition is this: show that within six months at least three named robotics teams each ran two or more policy checkpoints through Atom 1, and that its predicted failures matched subsequent physical-robot failures often enough that at least two teams paid to continue. That would simultaneously turn the benchmark into robot evidence and the design-partner page into a narrow well of users.
