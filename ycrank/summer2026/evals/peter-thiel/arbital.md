# Arbital -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Arbital’s implied secret is that serious traders value unified cross-venue execution more than they value remaining inside the exchange that owns their liquidity. What would prove this wrong? If its $377 million of monthly volume is incentive-seeking flow concentrated in one venue or strategy, rather than retained traders using Arbital across markets. The dossier never states the prevailing belief this proposition contradicts, and the shift from a market-making engine to a social, mobile, multi-asset application makes the thesis less falsifiable, not more. This is presently an ambitious product bundle, not yet a demonstrated secret.

The strongest fact is the founding structure. Karon Pangestu, Steven Mulyawan, and Vincent Owen Toniwan previously worked together at Aevo across growth, distribution, backend execution, and market making. Pangestu helped scale cumulative volume from $1 billion toward $100 billion; Mulyawan onboarded more than 10,000 traders; Toniwan operated infrastructure below two-millisecond latency at 100,000 events per second. This is not a team assembled around a fashionable pitch. It is a complementary group rebuilding a system they understand from the inside. Yet the dossier does not establish full-time status, founder ownership, compensation, or whether the core technology is entirely in-house, so alignment is promising rather than complete.

The live behavior is unusually informative for pre-seed: 2,600 traders and $1.3 billion of cumulative volume in seven months, including $377 million in the current month. But volume is not revenue, and revenue is not monopoly. Arbital depends on third-party venues whose liquidity, APIs, fees, and incentive programs it does not control. Axiom already reports more than $100 million in cumulative revenue, while Hyperliquid owns the underlying exchange and reported $844 million of 2025 revenue. Arbital is positioned between terminals and venues without yet demonstrating why either side cannot absorb its functionality.

The product breadth compounds this problem. Perpetuals, memecoins, tokenized stocks, market making, social features, and mobile distribution may describe expansion, or they may conceal the absence of a narrow market Arbital can dominate. Multi-venue integrations and execution expertise are real capabilities, but neither is presently a monopoly mechanism: there is no exclusive order flow, proprietary liquidity, dominant beachhead share, measured network retention, or data advantage. A trader can use the terminal today and leave when another venue offers better incentives tomorrow.

Distribution is more credible than defensibility. The founders already possess KOL, agency, and high-net-worth-trader relationships; the company has approximately 5,000 X followers; and 2,600 traders have actually used the product. The bear case must concede that this team may convert relationships into enough routed flow to obtain preferential venue economics and eventually turn liquidity aggregation into a compounding advantage. Persistent transaction demand can override regulatory uncertainty. It cannot, however, override commodity economics when the company neither owns the venue nor proves that traders remain after incentives disappear. Arbital’s disclosed inventory controls and circuit breakers mitigate trading losses, but undisclosed licensing scope across perpetuals and tokenized equities remains an additional execution risk.

My base case is that Arbital becomes a useful cross-venue execution business rather than the last mover in consumer trading: capable founders, substantial flow, but no demonstrated secret or value-capture mechanism. I would investigate rather than invest today. One condition would flip the decision: audited cohorts showing that a majority of traders remain active for six months, trade across multiple venues without incentive subsidies, and generate a disclosed positive take rate. That would convert the current story from borrowed volume into proprietary distribution.
