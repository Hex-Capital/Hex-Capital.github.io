# Avoca Systems -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

**250+ live clinics and only 269 LinkedIn followers make Avoca Systems a healthcare-rail execution machine without an owned-distribution asset.** Avoca Systems has already translated an illegible mess—referrals, modality rules, rosters, contrast protocols, faxes, and RIS write-backs—into software that cuts call abandonment from roughly 30% to 2%. The analytical signature is unusually strong category fit paired with unusually weak founder-as-brand leverage: the workflow may compound, but Campbell Mercer-Butcher and Max Shand do not yet bring an audience onto the cap table.

**Ro is the high rung because regulatory and operational complexity made capital intensity the moat rather than the failure mode.** Avoca Systems shares the load-bearing mechanism: HIPAA, SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, six named healthcare-system integrations, and site-specific clinical rules make radiology access harder than “AI scheduling” implies. Avoca Systems diverges because Ro owned more of the prescription transaction across 50 states, while Avoca Systems sits inside third-party RIS interfaces, reports no revenue, and addresses a published scheduling market of only $780 million. The healthcare-DTC and prescription-rail sleeve is real, but Avoca Systems is nearer Hinge Health than Ro on the category ladder: operationally embedded, not yet a system of record.

**Fin is the low-rung warning: operational cleverness without owned distribution can become an endless service layer rather than a compounding asset.** Avoca Systems shows that pattern in muted form. Max Shand has roughly 6,000 LinkedIn followers, but Avoca Systems has only 269 company followers, no disclosed newsletter, channel, community, or organic customer engine, and enterprise sales targets CEOs and CIOs directly. Avoca Systems is not a creator dressed as a startup; Avoca Systems is a sales-and-implementation business dressed as an operating system. That can still work, but the Marina Mogilko structure is unavailable because no person-owned cash-flow asset is visible.

**No public round price exists, so Avoca Systems receives the absent-evidence price default.** The counterfactual is clean: if Avoca Systems is raising below the Summer 2026 batch median because investors read radiology scheduling as narrow, integration-heavy, and boring, the score jumps toward Sublime Security’s pre-consensus profile. If Avoca Systems is priced as “the AI operating system for healthcare” on the strength of 250 clinics, the round becomes the hot vertical-agent deck with better compliance paperwork. Without SAFE terms, investor participation, or a visible tier-one pass, I cannot claim mispricing merely because the market is specialized.

**250+ clinics create a potentially excellent capital structure over the next 18 months if each new deployment reuses Avoca Systems’s accumulated rules rather than restarting professional services.** Avoca Systems can compound through lower implementation cost, denser RIS coverage, and expansion across 150-location networks; eight employees supporting that footprint suggests real operating leverage. The failure mechanism is five regatta tacks of integration labor before default-alive: no revenue signal, negotiated enterprise deployments, and customer-specific clinical logic could turn every sale into bespoke work. Avoca Systems is venture-shaped only if the integration library becomes reusable infrastructure, not founder-drainage disguised as ARR.

**Venmo took many turns of the crank because Andrew Kortina’s conviction survived the product changes; Avoca Systems has weaker public evidence of that missionary arc.** Max Shand was Afterpay’s first employee and previously built Serenade through an acquisition after serving 347 artists, which gives Avoca Systems credible non-linear operating temperament. Campbell Mercer-Butcher brings principal-engineer and technical-lead experience, but neither founder has documented radiology employment, category writing, or a public belief formed three years before Avoca Systems. The prior exit moves Avoca Systems above the absent-evidence default; the lack of radiology obsession keeps it well below Drop.io-style asymmetric conviction.

**Six named integrations point to Avoca Systems reaching $8 million to $15 million of ARR within 36 months and then selling to Visage Imaging rather than becoming an independent horizontal platform.** Avoca Systems can win a valuable, narrow access layer by converting its reported 86% call-resolution rate and greater-than-80% eligible-call booking conversion into network-wide contracts. The same high-category-fit, low-owned-distribution signature makes acquisition more probable than breakout independence: Visage gains scheduling intelligence and customer-specific operational rules, while Avoca Systems gains the installed distribution it does not own.

**AbbaDox already markets VoiceAI, FaxAI, scheduling, results sharing, and referrer communications, which is the strongest case that Avoca Systems gets commoditized before that forecast arrives.** OpenDoctor also offers exam-mapping logic, no proprietary-data ownership is established, and Avoca Systems depends on third-party interfaces—classic vertical-AI red flags inside a sleeve I otherwise like. The bear case stings because Avoca Systems may have built an implementation lead, not a moat. The evidence preventing a pass is 250+ live clinics, a reduction in abandonment from roughly 30% to 2%, six integrations, and serious compliance work: customers are using Avoca Systems in clinical operations, not testing a demo. A disclosed SAFE cap below $12 million would flip my verdict to Invest because the remaining risk would finally be priced as an illegible healthcare asset rather than a consensus AI round.
