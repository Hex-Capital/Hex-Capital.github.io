# Autoresearch, Inc. -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Autoresearch, Inc. needs to establish one important truth: can automated research campaigns produce reliable, economically valuable improvements that competent internal ML teams systematically leave undiscovered? That is the potential secret I would investigate; the dossier does not establish which prevailing belief the founders reject. A prospective test would compare improvements on previously unseen production tasks against an internal team operating under the same compute budget. If the advantage disappears outside the supplied evaluation, the proposed secret fails.

The strongest evidence favoring Autoresearch is its attention to verification. The team reports 4,188 experiments over two days using 50 B200 GPUs, then publishes a recipe with median validation bits-per-byte of 0.9016 across 25 confirmation runs. Interleaved testing addresses a real problem: an autonomous researcher can otherwise industrialize measurement error. This makes the technical work worth examining. But the dossier supplies neither the comparative improvement nor the campaign cost needed to establish an economic breakthrough. The absolute benchmark result cannot answer those questions.

Who keeps the value if this works? Customers supply the task, baseline, and evaluation, and own their research artifacts. Enterprise data restrictions also constrain the proposed cross-customer learning advantage. Autoresearch could become exceptionally useful while customers retain the most valuable discoveries and internalize the workflow. Karpathy’s open-source loop already supplies a substitute starting point; orchestration, lineage, and verification require additional engineering, but the dossier establishes no exclusive technology or bounded customer segment where that engineering produces durable market ownership. This leaves the monopoly mechanism unproved rather than affirmatively disproved.

The founders provide a more concrete positive signal. Karan Brar and Thomas Boser met at Reducto, giving two founders an actual working relationship before this company. Their ML infrastructure backgrounds, complemented by Jad Ghalayini’s formal-verification specialty, make the published work credible. The shared history earns some founding-structure credit; the biographies alone do not establish economic alignment. Jad’s relationship with the other founders, everyone’s full-time status, and ownership remain unknown. There is no documented part-time founder, outsourced core team, or investor-dominated capitalization that would trigger a founding red flag.

The commercial proposition is coherent: a team brings a measurable problem and purchases a research campaign. Founder-led outreach and the YC launch give Autoresearch an identifiable route to those teams. Paid-plan terms and enterprise MSAs establish purchasing arrangements, but disclose no realized sales, prices, compute margins, or repeat purchasing. Consequently, I cannot yet derive a power-law outcome from one revenue engine. The relevant distribution question is whether a campaign’s contract value pays for both its compute and the technical sales and delivery work it requires.

The strongest objection to passing is that verification and experiment infrastructure could become the essential control layer for automated research. That fits my unstated preference for infrastructure serving the AI production stack, and the released recipe gives the thesis substance beyond investor logos. My OpenAI exception likewise accepted uncertain market ownership after a demonstrated technical discontinuity; Autoresearch has yet to establish that magnitude of capability change. No persistent customer behavior currently supplies an alternative override. I would pass on a personal check today, while leaving the technical question open. One verifiable flip condition would be a paid, independently audited deployment in a precisely defined customer segment that demonstrates a proprietary, reproducible performance-per-dollar advantage over the customer’s internal workflow and leads to renewal at positive campaign gross margin.
