# Native -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Native’s founders have earned specific knowledge. They built Wepost together, reached a reported NOK80,000 MRR by September 2025, and started Native in January 2026. Bekken produced content for actual brands; Hauga operated a marketing business; Gråsberg built the earlier product as CTO. These are encounters with the problem, not adjacent credentials. I give the team substantial credit for that history. Winding down Wepost shows a change in direction; without evidence about stakeholder treatment, it establishes neither an integrity problem nor an integrity premium.

The investment turns on what Native learns that a competitor cannot cheaply acquire. Norn collects approval, rejection, editing, and performance signals, then ranks ideas before frontier models generate the assets. That is a credible place to build an advantage. The [terms document training on aggregated signals](https://native.no/terms-and-conditions), but neither exclusive access nor demonstrated predictive superiority. Customer opt-outs further limit assumptions about accumulation. Copy the application tomorrow: Native retains its collected records, but the dossier does not establish how much economic advantage those records preserve. That earns some credit, not a proprietary-model valuation.

The strongest objection to waiting is that Native already has customers financing its learning. The company reports [$2M ARR within six months](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Vdd-native-artificial-intuition-for-marketing), 2,200 paying customers, and a $79 monthly Pro plan. Those are unusually informative signs of direct value capture at this stage. They do not establish contribution margins, but this is already more than willingness to try a free tool. Meanwhile, the SMB publishing wedge has identifiable expansion routes: agencies managing multiple brands, enterprise marketing teams, and marketing products consuming Norn through an API. The same ranking capability could serve each buyer, within a broader social-media-management market estimated at $36.4B. The API remains a waitlist, so that expansion deserves intermediate credit.

Native also fits my preference for turning individual knowledge into scalable output: a small business can publish without hiring its own marketing department. The unresolved question is whether one customer makes another customer’s product better. Forty thousand published posts demonstrate activity; they do not demonstrate that loop. The reported 5.5% engagement against 2.9% for one customer’s own posts does not isolate the contribution of pooled learning. Native’s research acknowledges selection and survivorship biases. Blaze already offers a comparable strategy-through-publishing workflow. Native could grow a useful subscription business while competitors continually reproduce the visible functionality.

I can make exceptions for exceptional founders and a distinctive prototype before a user network appears. Native has the operating history and a shipped product, but the dossier does not establish unusual capital efficiency or a predictive breakthrough sufficient to make that override compelling. Likewise, unresolved monetization can be acceptable when a communication network is visibly forming; Native has monetization, while the self-reinforcing mechanism remains unmeasured. There is no documented unfair dealing, artificial financing pressure, or dependence on investor reassurance. My hesitation concerns the asset being built, not an invented founder red flag.

I am **Neutral** and would keep my money uncommitted today. Native has demonstrated selling ability more clearly than compounding advantage. One result would change my decision: a prospective, controlled ablation showing that other customers’ consented feedback gives new brands a sustained, commercially meaningful performance lift over the same system without pooled feedback. That would demonstrate customers creating value for one another—the mechanism that could turn Native’s early commercial success into a compounding investment.
