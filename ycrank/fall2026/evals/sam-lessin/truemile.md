# TrueMile -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

TrueMile gives me a trucking operator to underwrite, but no deal to price. Post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital are all undisclosed. YC participation establishes none of those things. I also cannot identify the experiment this round buys: seeking two dedicated-lane partners is a commercial objective, but there is no funded hypothesis, deadline, or measurable pass/fail result. Those two missing gates matter more to my check than the size of the freight market.

Harpreet Dhaliwal is the strongest reason to keep reading. He started managing his family’s fleet at 19 and reports generating more than $7 million in that operation. That is an operating receipt, although it is neither verified TrueMile revenue nor evidence of profitability. His operational-agent development and reported data-science training give him a credible bridge between dispatch and software. I give that combination meaningful founder credit, while stopping short of assuming the software has already improved live fleet results.

The owned-fleet model activates two patterns I like: connecting ecosystems through an operator who understands both, and using software to reorganize difficult physical operations. TrueMile can capture the economic benefit of better dispatch directly through freight charges. Human approval of loads and rate confirmations also means the plan does not explicitly require agent errors to disappear. The strongest bull case is that Dhaliwal already knows which decisions lose money, can test improvements inside a fleet he controls, and needs only a few recurring lanes to build a functioning business. That is a substantially better starting point than selling generic automation to an industry the founder has never operated in.

But owning the feedback loop has not yet established that the loop produces an advantage. TrueMile discloses no measured reduction in empty miles, repeat shipper purchases, or proprietary dataset advantage. Its inquiry form and search for two lane partners establish solicitation, not conversion; 94 LinkedIn followers add nothing to that question. Meanwhile, aifleet already combines fleet operations with AI planning. The differing descriptions of TrueMile as a carrier and an external fleet-software platform leave the economic asset unclear: which workflow does it own, and who has paid because it owns it?

Delete the next equity round and the underwriting remains unfinished. There are no disclosed margins, burn, runway, or cash break-even plan. Industry operating costs of $2.336 per mile in 2025 identify expenses the optimization must overcome; they do not establish TrueMile’s economics. I would not infer a financing treadmill from competitors’ fundraising, or punish this team for being small and early. Equally, the exceptions that could override my missing gates—documented paid conversion and retention, exceptional external demand, or a prior-exit-backed financing structure—are absent here. Founder experience is the affirmative evidence; it cannot do every other dimension’s work.

I pass on the evidence available. My verifiable condition for reopening diligence is **eight consecutive weeks of positive operating cash flow from two signed recurring-lane customers, including fleet costs and human dispatch labor**. That would establish a functioning base business beneath the AI option and directly test the strongest bull case. I would still need the round terms before committing my own money.
