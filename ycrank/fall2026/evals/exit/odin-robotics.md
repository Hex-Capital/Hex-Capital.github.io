# Odin Robotics -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by an industrial automation integrator. Odin demonstrated CNC machine tending and conveyor packing, and describes a retrofit approach that lets operators teach new motions on existing lines. An integrator could buy that capability to serve more short-run factories if Odin can show that it reduces setup and changeover labor across installations. The demonstrations establish that the cells can perform the tasks shown. The buyer savings remain an inference. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The next milestone is repeatable paid CNC deployments that sustain customer cycle-time and quality targets through full shifts and part changes, with measured installation and support costs. Odin quotes satisfaction-contingent pilots and the founders report on-site factory work. The memo cites $500,000 in funding, while realized revenue, pilot conversion, and the capital needed to repeat installations remain unknown. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Investor terms are undisclosed. The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale scenarios assume a close within eight years, eligibility to sell the entire stake for cash at closing, and pro rata distribution with no further reduction from the preference waterfall. The prices and deductions are illustrative assumptions.

- **Modest sale.** $50 million in enterprise value less $5 million of assumed debt and deal adjustments leaves $45 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $1.35 million, or 2.7×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value less $10 million of assumed debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $2.7 million, or 5.4×. At 2.5% ownership, the same sale pays $2.25 million, or 4.5×.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested yields zero realized proceeds over eight years. The memo identifies no independent liquidity route available to an Odin investor. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Odin-specific evidence for an acquisition price, the holder’s waterfall, and the rights and consents needed to close remains absent. The scenarios show what the acquisition would need to deliver.

### Countercase

Formic already installs automation for overlapping tasks and is working with Physical Intelligence on adapting robot models for manufacturing. Odin’s machine-specific controls, tooling, and guarding could keep each deployment labor-intensive, weakening an integrator’s reason to buy. That supports Watch. The memo leaves open both the possibility of a buyer paying enough and the holder’s ability to participate, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could a strategic integrator justify from Odin’s results across repeat paid CNC deployments? Customer records showing full-shift performance, changeover labor saved, and installation and support costs would give a buyer a basis for that estimate. Move to Reach out if the resulting buyer economics support a credible price range that clears the $83.3 million distributable-equity hurdle under plausible deal adjustments and dilution.
