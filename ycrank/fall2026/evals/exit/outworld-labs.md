# Outworld Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a robotics simulation platform is the strongest supported route. Outworld publicly launched Horizon with a demo in November 2026. The company describes a dexterous-manipulation engine that fits simulated behavior to hardware measurements and combines it with scene reconstruction and policy evaluation. A platform such as NVIDIA could have a reason to buy Horizon if it measurably reduces physical testing and operator time compared with the platform’s existing tools. That buyer benefit is an inference. The memo provides a demo as an operating signal and leaves production savings and buyer interest unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

A paid, head-to-head pilot across multiple robot setups could measure operator hours and physical resets, including calibration time, at a matched on-hardware policy success rate. The demo and the founders’ robotics research make that a plausible seed-stage milestone. Pilot funding, hardware access, and the time and capital needed to complete it remain unverified. The memo is silent on IP transfer rights, holder participation, and independent liquidity routes. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

Using the benchmark terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale cases below are illustrative sensitivities. Each assumes $10 million in combined debt and deal deductions, a pro rata waterfall with no senior preference claims, and an eligible holder selling its entire 3% stake for cash at closing within eight years.

- **Modest sale.** $40 million in enterprise value yields $30 million in distributable equity and $900,000 to the holder, or 1.8×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value yields $90 million in distributable equity and $2.7 million to the holder, or 5.4×. At that price, total deductions above about $16.7 million would put the holder below the objective.
- **Prolonged private operation.** If the company reinvests available cash and no eligible share purchase or funded payout occurs within eight years, the holder realizes $0.

The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction evidence to support either sale price. The strong case shows what would clear the hurdle under the assumed terms. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

NVIDIA already offers contact-rich simulation and integrated scene workflows. MuJoCo Playground supplies hardware-trajectory fitting, and Meta’s SuperDex supplies open-source tactile contact physics. A prospective buyer could extend its own stack or combine available components. Horizon’s savings could also shrink if calibration has to be repeated for each robot or task. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the memo leaves both the adequacy of those substitutes and the realistic sale-price ceiling unresolved. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Horizon create enough value over a strategic buyer’s existing stack to justify a sale yielding at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? A paid pilot showing repeatable net savings across robot setups, paired with a buyer-side valuation range tied to those savings, would move the action to **Reach out** if the range supports the benchmark under plausible deal terms. A supported price ceiling materially below the hurdle would trigger **Pass** absent an independent route.
