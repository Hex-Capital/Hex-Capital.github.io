# Induction Labs -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Induction Labs proposes a precise secret: computer-use intelligence can be learned more efficiently by predicting future screen states from unlabeled video than by reconstructing actions from application-specific labels. The falsifying event is equally precise: Photon-1 fails to outperform action-labeled models under an independent evaluation controlling for compute and serving cost. This is not merely “AI agents are growing.” The team has trained a 106B-A5B model on 575 million frames and reports 30-times lower pretraining compute and three-times lower serving cost than Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite. But the benchmark is internal and the compute comparison partly estimated. The secret has been tested by its authors, not yet exposed to a hostile judge.

There is real technical substance beneath the claim. An index of two billion public videos, a filtered corpus of roughly two million screen recordings, a differential latent encoder compressing frames by more than 100 times, and custom fused kernels constitute more than a feature wrapper. Yet proprietary technique is not automatically monopoly. H Company already offers an inference API and open-weight Holo3.1 variants, while reporting 78.85% on OSWorld-Verified. Induction Labs has neither a defined beachhead nor evidence that developers cannot substitute another model. The company may have discovered a cheaper path through the mountain without owning the valley on the other side.

The founders are unusually credible for such a young team. Jonathan Li published first-author ACL work while still young and subsequently built reasoning and reinforcement-learning infrastructure at Cohere; David Li brings software and machine-learning experience from Waterloo, Shopify, and Sunnybrook. They are brothers who say they are building together “again,” which is stronger evidence of durable alignment than two strangers assembled for YC. But the dossier does not establish founder ownership, compensation, or that David—whose profile still describes a 2023–2028 degree—is unequivocally full-time. Missing evidence is not a red flag, but founding structure is too fundamental to infer from technical output.

The strongest bull case is that Photon-1 is already a genuine technical event and that commercial details are simply lagging indicators at pre-seed. OpenAI is the relevant exception: a demonstrated zero-to-one result can justify investment even before durable monopoly is clear. The shared mechanism would be an observable capability jump; the material difference is that Photon-1’s jump exists only in company-run tests, while an open-weight competitor already has public benchmark evidence. Eighty thousand announcement views show curiosity, not customer dependence. No disclosed buyer, pricing, API access, revenue, sales cycle, or repeated use connects the model to an economic system.

The unstated pattern toward the complete AI production stack supports taking the architecture seriously, but it also sharpens the problem: this is a crowded and heavily financed layer containing H Company, Adept, Adaption Labs, and adjacent world-model builders. The danger is not that the technology is unimportant. It is that several important technologies compete away their value. The possible licensing exposure around two million public screen recordings adds a second unresolved dependency, though no enforcement action or demonstrated illegality makes it a decisive red flag today.

I would not pass, because the technical secret and sibling founding structure are too strong for that. I would not invest yet, because internal superiority without a bounded market, independent proof, or distribution remains a research result rather than a last-mover position. One verifiable condition flips the decision: Photon-1 must beat Holo3.1 on OSWorld-Verified in an independently administered evaluation while using less than half its measured serving compute. That would convert a company assertion into the kind of technical discontinuity capable of overriding the still-unproven market structure.
