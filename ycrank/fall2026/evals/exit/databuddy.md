# Databuddy -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. An analytics platform such as Mixpanel could buy Databuddy to add a cookieless tracker and a workflow that investigates conversion changes across traffic, errors, Web Vitals, and connected revenue. Databuddy says Databunny exposes the underlying queries and pushes scheduled investigations to Slack. Ownership could give a buyer control over the tracker and investigation workflow, permitting deeper integration and faster rollout if customers demonstrably benefit from using them together. The memo leaves that incremental benefit and any buyer valuation unverified. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*)

A proposed milestone is a 90-day test showing that paying teams repeatedly use the combined workflow and resolve conversion incidents faster than with their existing tools. The memo records 38 active subscriptions and $3,345 in company-wide MRR. Those figures establish early paid adoption. The funding announcement’s claim of more than 3,000 businesses served leaves paid Databunny usage and retention unresolved. Self-serve onboarding makes a cohort test plausible, while the reported raise of more than $560,000 leaves current runway and the capital needed to reach the milestone open. Transferability of the code and customer agreements, including permissions for customer data, remains unverified. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

Under the shared seed scenario, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-ons. The eight-year target is $2.5 million in gross cash before investor taxes. A pro rata distribution requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. At 3%, the illustrative $5 million of net debt raises the enterprise-value hurdle to $88.3 million. With that assumed debt and no modeled fees or senior claims, a $25 million sale in year eight yields $600,000 (1.2×), while a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). Actual shareholder terms, debt, and a realizable sale price remain unknown.

The other routes lack independent support. The memo establishes neither post-reinvestment cash flow for a sponsor buyout or company-funded payout, eligible demand for a secondary, nor a path to public scale and holder resale. In a prolonged-private scenario where revenue plateaus near current levels, buyer interest fails to emerge, public scale stays out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. A wind-down recovery would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantified. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Mixpanel already monitors metrics proactively, and PostHog exposes analytics to AI agents using its existing customer data. A buyer could extend its own product or partner for the remaining telemetry. That puts the burden on Databuddy to show that owning the integrated workflow is worth paying for. The overlap weakens the acquisition rationale, but the memo establishes neither a complete substitute nor a supported sale price that leaves the holder materially short. The Pass gate is untriggered. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could an analytics platform justify for owning Databuddy once paying teams demonstrate repeat use and faster incident diagnosis? A buyer-side build-versus-buy assessment grounded in measured cohort results would answer it. A supported price range that clears the holder’s $2.5 million target under plausible dilution and deal terms would move the action to Reach out.
