# Coasty -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

82.81% on OSWorld Verified makes **Coasty** a fast-learning, capital-light automation company: strongest on demonstrated thesis updating, weakest on requiring capital that only I can supply. **Coasty** could replace brittle RPA and portions of back-office labor, but the present product looks more like infrastructure for an existing automation market than a civilizational redirection. A reliable computer-use agent matters. A metered API priced at $0.03-$0.10 per action does not yet break an economic mechanism at **OpenAI** scale.

**Slash** (2024) is the high rung for learning rate because a documented product pivot revealed founders updating faster than the market, and that learning mechanism also appears in **Coasty**. Between March and July 2026, **Coasty** moved from isolated “AI employee” VMs for consumers and SMBs to developer-controlled automation for legacy back-office software. The divergence is evidentiary: Slash had a publicly explained pivot and subsequent commercial validation, while **Coasty** cannot yet show that its thousands of paying users belong to the current product rather than the prior positioning. The direction changed quickly; whether the underlying thesis improved remains unproven.

**DoorDash** (2013) is the low rung on required-capital fit because capital-light logistics failed my usual test even though founder ambition ultimately overrode it. The same capital-fit failure mechanism appears more starkly in **Coasty**: prepaid usage, BYOK model calls consuming zero platform credits, and VM pricing as low as $0.05 per hour imply that $5-$10 million could fund substantial progress. **Coasty** has no disclosed foundation-model training program, hardware fleet, or $50 million commercial milestone. Any competent generalist can finance this roadmap. I am over-resourced for the deal.

$54.8 billion of projected computer-agent spending understates **Coasty**’s possible consequence because the real prize is labor displacement across accounting, insurance, healthcare, tax, and compliance. Over the next 18 months, **Coasty** compounds consequence only if reliable execution converts human-operated workflows into unattended software labor: more completed workflows create more recovery traces, which improve reliability, which unlock more consequential workflows. Today that loop stops at an 82.81% benchmark, mock healthcare portals, and no quantified production labor removed. **Coasty** may collapse selector-based RPA; it has not shown that it collapses the back office itself. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself,” but greatness requires more than being 20 points better than UiPath on one benchmark.

82.81% completion also defines the technical counterfactual for **Coasty**. The score moves up a tier if **Coasty** demonstrates near-deterministic execution on payments, patient records, and regulatory filings while recovering from interface changes without human intervention—and if those recovery systems improve from proprietary production data rather than commodity multimodal models. Without that evidence, 17% unfinished tasks are not a benchmark footnote; they are the product boundary. UiPath has $1.43 billion in FY2025 revenue and Adept has raised $415 million. Beating both requires a stack-deep reliability miracle, not an orchestration layer with replay logs.

**Physical Intelligence** (2024) provides the clean founder parallel: technically ambitious agents built by researchers capable of questioning what existing models can do. Prateek Jannu left Stanford, Nitish Kovuru taught graduate AI as a Purdue undergraduate, and **Coasty** began building when the founders judged summer-2025 models inadequate. Those are useful acts of defiance. **Coasty** still lacks the Levine-Finn signal—a research reputation, named technical doctrine, or public contradiction of expert consensus. Leaving Stanford is commitment. Publishing an 82.81% result is execution. Neither yet proves that the founders see a truth the field rejects.

UiPath’s $1.43 billion revenue base makes my falsifiable 36-month forecast straightforward: by July 2029, **Coasty** will have narrowed into regulated desktop-agent infrastructure, reached $8-$15 million ARR, and been acquired by UiPath for less than $300 million. **Coasty**’s rapid repositioning supports the narrowing, while its low capital needs and weak structural moat favor acquisition over an independent foundation-model-scale outcome. The likely product can become valuable faster than it becomes fundamental.

Thousands of active users and paying customers across four continents make the strongest opposing case sting: **Coasty** may be the **DoorDash** or **Slash** exception, where rapid learning, YC distribution, and usage outrun my preference for capital-hostile science. An independently confirmed 82.81% score could be the first signal that **Coasty** owns the reliability layer every foundation-model vendor needs. The bear evidence still wins today: earlier-product usage is not confirmed as current-product usage, regulated demonstrations used mock portals, no proprietary-data scale is quantified, and commercially available models remain central. I would reverse the verdict if **Coasty** exceeds $25 million ARR from production regulated workflows while independently documenting at least 99.9% task completion and recovery without human intervention.
