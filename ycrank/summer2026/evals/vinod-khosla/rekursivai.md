# rekursiv.ai -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Joshua Dillon’s 13 years at Google Research and DeepMind make **rekursiv.ai** a consequence-heavy, consensus-expert company: extraordinary potential attached to founders trained inside the prevailing system. If **rekursiv.ai** scales AI scientists whose discoveries improve their successors, the economic mechanism that breaks is the scarcity of human research iteration. Scientific progress stops moving at the speed of graduate students and begins compounding at machine speed. That world is civilizationally distinct. The question is whether two accomplished model builders can invent a new research institution—or merely automate today’s benchmark culture.

**OpenAI** (2019) is the high rung on consequence because recursive intelligence improvement justified my $50M check before revenue or a clear moat existed. **rekursiv.ai** shares the load-bearing mechanism: AI performs economically valuable cognitive work and uses the output to improve the next cycle. The 235 ARC experiments and 684 Sudoku experiments demonstrate a primitive recursive loop, not a slide. But **rekursiv.ai** diverges from OpenAI because its disclosed success remains bounded by ML benchmarks: 17.5% on ARC-AGI-2 and 100% on Sudoku do not yet establish that autonomous researchers generate transferable knowledge. Consequence is enormous; proof of general consequence is not.

**Glean’s** low-rung weakness on founder defiance was expert continuity—an ex-Google search founder extending a system he already understood—and **rekursiv.ai** carries the same uncomfortable pattern. Dillon spent 13 years at Google Research and DeepMind; Dan Kondratyuk spent five years at Google Research before leading Luma’s World Models group. **rekursiv.ai** is not being built by outsiders contradicting a field that rejected them. The mitigation is output: eight system attempts before sustaining 984 experiments over 30 days, plus an algorithm Dillon says he would not have invented. Experts normally extrapolate the past. Here, expert credentials are producing some evidence of machinery that escapes their own intuitions, but not yet a documented repudiation of consensus.

The 48-hour ARC experiments expose the technical mechanism that will compound—or strangle **rekursiv.ai**—over the next 18 months. Every improvement in experiment selection, early termination, evidence tracing, and cross-task memory increases useful research per GPU-hour; that produces more failed hypotheses, which improves future selection, which creates a data flywheel competitors cannot download from GitHub. But literal transfer of Hypothesis-Pinning Search failed on ARC after succeeding on Sudoku. **rekursiv.ai** therefore needs at least three miracles: reliable hypothesis generation, cross-domain transfer, and economically superior experiment allocation. Apache-2.0 components and Sakana’s open-source AI Scientist weaken code as a moat, so the accumulated experimental judgment must become the moat.

Dan Kondratyuk’s eight-system-attempt history is not yet the documented pivot required to move **rekursiv.ai** into the top learning-rate tier. For the score to flip, **rekursiv.ai** would need to publish a specific thesis reversal—perhaps abandoning role-specialized fleets for a different architecture after customer experiments disproved the original design—and show that the revised system transferred across ARC, model training, and one external research domain. Without that evidence, I apply the absent-evidence default. Iterating an implementation is competence; changing a cherished thesis when the evidence rejects it is learning rate.

**Commonwealth Fusion** required $115M at Series A because the capital itself crossed a barrier consensus investors would not touch. **rekursiv.ai** has raised $5M and faces Sakana at $412M, Periodic Labs at $300M, and Lila Sciences at $550M, so compute and talent could support a substantial concentrated check. Yet **rekursiv.ai** has not shown that more than $50M is required before a commercial milestone; software experiments may still follow ordinary consumption-SaaS economics. I am not over-resourced if **rekursiv.ai** becomes the research-compute layer for every frontier laboratory. I am over-resourced if it becomes an elegant research cockpit sold by the seat.

The 71.4–75.5% public ARC-AGI-1 result leads to a falsifiable forecast: by July 2029, **rekursiv.ai** will reach $20M–$35M in ARR from autonomous ML-research deployments and will remain independent, but most revenue will come from optimizing model training rather than autonomous discovery across physical sciences. **rekursiv.ai** can sell shortened experiment cycles before solving general scientific invention, while the high consequence keeps pulling the roadmap toward recursive research. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself”—but the first money will come from labor displacement inside AI laboratories.

Sakana’s $412M capitalization is the strongest bear case: **rekursiv.ai** may be two Google-trained experts wrapping commodity models around public benchmarks while better-funded competitors open-source the same loop. The 14 GitHub followers, 25-star sagent repository, absent revenue, failed literal Sudoku-to-ARC transfer, and no identified moat make that objection sting. **rekursiv.ai** survives the objection because 984 experiments over 30 days, evidence-traced collaboration, and independently inspectable algorithms are stack-deeper than a GPT wrapper; the unstated-pattern check also favors founders with public intellectual reputations, while the Glean exception proves expert continuity can be overridden by consequence. I would invest, but if **rekursiv.ai** cannot show two paying research organizations with independently reproduced improvements on proprietary workloads by July 2027, the benchmark-to-platform bridge has failed and I would flip to Pass.
