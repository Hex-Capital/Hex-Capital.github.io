# Letter -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Letter’s strongest supported route is a strategic acquisition by an AI-search measurement platform such as Peec AI. Letterpress-1 turns site and search signals into Git pull requests with live previews. Owning that workflow could let a measurement platform sell customers a way to act on the issues it identifies. That is an inferred buyer rationale. The memo provides no buyer interest, measured buyer benefit, or transaction pricing. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Letter reports more than 100 customers and thousands of pull requests following its November beta launch. The memo records a $500,000 YC convertible note and lists paid plans starting at $250 per month. The reported usage makes a test of repeat paid production use plausible. The memo leaves paying-customer count, retention, runway, and the capital needed to reach that milestone open. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Using the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity. Both sale scenarios below assume $5 million in net debt and deal adjustments, no senior claims on the remaining equity, and an eligible holder selling its entire stake for cash by year eight. The memo leaves note conversion, payout priority, and transfer permissions unverified.

- **Modest sale.** $40 million in enterprise value leaves $35 million in distributable equity and pays the holder $1.05 million, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value leaves $95 million in distributable equity and pays the holder $2.85 million, or 5.7×. If the holder can sell only 80% of its stake at that price, gross cash falls to $2.28 million, or 4.56×.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested yields zero realized proceeds within eight years. The memo provides no evidence of an eligible secondary, funded payout, or other independent route.

The sale prices are illustrative. Published subscription prices and reported usage do not establish a realizable acquisition range. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could build enough of the workflow itself. General-purpose coding agents cover part of the workflow, and AirOps already offers overlapping content optimization. Letter’s reported pull-request volume leaves open how often customers merge changes and keep paying for the workflow. The overlap weakens the case for paying an acquisition premium, but the memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a credible sale-price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle. The Pass gate is therefore unmet. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a prospective measurement-platform buyer justify paying roughly $88.3 million or more in enterprise value for Letter under the illustrated deductions? A buyer’s build-versus-buy analysis grounded in repeat paid production use and measured savings from merged site changes would move the action to Reach out if it supports that price and a credible path to $2.5 million in holder cash. Until then, Watch.
