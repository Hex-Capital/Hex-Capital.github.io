# Invertix -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Invertix’s strongest founder evidence is already inside operating solar assets. By May 2026, its investors reported more than 1.8 GW managed through its AI workers. Perrotta and Durmaz have gone beyond describing an energy problem and persuaded operators to deploy a solution. That earns more weight than their university affiliations or follower counts. It establishes some specific knowledge acquired through action, although the dossier does not establish either founder’s personal contribution to the underlying energy technology.

The investment question is what those deployments leave behind. Customer-specific equipment mappings, operational histories, and procedures could become expensive to reconstruct. They could also remain engineering work that must be repeated at every account. The reported roughly 60-day contract-to-deployment cycle makes that distinction consequential. Invertix has a plausible mechanism for accumulating context, but no demonstrated switching cost, proprietary-model advantage, or comparative performance result. I also discount the deployment detail attached to a YC update carrying a December 17, 2026 heading, beyond this dossier’s research date.

The strongest argument against my hesitation is that customers are already paying to resolve this uncertainty. Company-reported “multiple six figures” ARR, disclosed in June, is meaningful pre-seed evidence of direct value capture. TotalEnergies On participation separately provides access to operating sites and potential pilots. Embedded engineers may be buying the learning required to build a reusable operating model. But the reported ARR does not disclose pricing, customer concentration, or how much implementation labor supports it. Revenue validates demand; the economics of repeat deployment remain open.

Invertix touches my unstated preference for infrastructure bottlenecks, but its ownership of a durable bottleneck remains unproven. Nor does it yet exhibit the customer-created value I favor: another operator brings another integration, with no demonstrated improvement for existing users. Customer-directed data processing leaves cross-customer learning rights unresolved. The $3.07 billion renewable asset-management software estimate establishes relevant spending, but the dossier does not map a convincing expansion from solar operators into named adjacent buyers. Delfos’s overlapping diagnostics and workflow automation makes that expansion contested from the outset.

I find no documented integrity problem, artificial financing deadline, or founder dependence on investor reassurance. Customer supervision requirements are sensible for critical infrastructure. My known exceptions also matter: an exceptional founder can justify an unclear wedge, and an unusually efficient prototype can precede a distribution loop. Invertix has shipped, but the dossier does not establish the extraordinary founder evidence or capital efficiency needed for those overrides. Its current evidence supports a credible operating-software business more strongly than a system whose advantage compounds without proportional labor.

I am neutral at this stage and would keep my own money uncommitted. The founders have earned attention through deployment and reported revenue; the unresolved issue is what becomes harder for competitors as Invertix grows. My single flip condition is a customer-authorized, held-out deployment study showing that accumulated operational knowledge produces materially better results than the same agent stack without that knowledge. That would demonstrate that implementation work is creating a reusable asset.
