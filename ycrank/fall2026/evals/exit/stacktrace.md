# Stacktrace -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an enterprise agent-security vendor is the strongest route. Stacktrace describes Fleet as a local sidecar that applies a common policy across coding agents and Agent Gateway as a way to hold consequential actions for customer consent. A vendor such as Zenity could infer value from adding both capabilities to its existing coverage if customers pay for them and buying accelerates deployment. Buyer interest and the economic benefit of acquiring Stacktrace remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The memo records Fleet’s launch and reports $500,000 in YC financing. A founder reports unnamed design partners running local or unattended cloud agents. Those signals make a paid production deployment across multiple agent platforms a plausible seed milestone. Paid conversion, runway, and the capital needed to reach that milestone remain unknown. IP and customer contract transfer terms are unverified, and the memo supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The reported convertible note’s terms and its effect on the waterfall are unknown. The sale scenarios assume a close by year eight, $5 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, no further senior claims, and cash paid at closing on the holder’s entire eligible stake. These are sensitivities. The memo supplies no acquisition pricing evidence. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Gross cash to the holder |
|----------|--------------------------|
| Modest sale | $35 million enterprise value leaves $30 million in distributable equity and pays $900,000, or 1.8×. |
| Strong sale | $105 million enterprise value leaves $100 million in distributable equity and pays $3 million, or 6×. If only 80% of the stake is eligible for purchase, cash falls to $2.4 million, or 4.8×. |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with reinvestment and no funded payout or eligible share purchase yields $0 in realized cash by year eight. |

### Countercase

Anthropic already offers managed permissions and hooks that cover some Claude Code oversight needs, and Zenity already covers coding agents alongside SaaS copilots and cloud agents. A buyer could extend its existing controls if Stacktrace’s cross-agent policies and consent workflows add little that customers will pay for. The memo leaves the value of that differentiation unresolved. It establishes neither a structural barrier nor a credible sale price that would trigger the proceeds gate. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production deployment across multiple agent platforms demonstrate enough value over native controls to justify an acquisition that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Measured customer gains and corroborating build-versus-buy economics from a plausible acquirer would move the action to Reach out if they support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity under the benchmark assumptions.
