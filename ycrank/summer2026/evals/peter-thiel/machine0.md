# machine0 -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The contested truth is that autonomous agents need persistent computers, not disposable sandboxes. It would be disproved if long-running agents reliably complete their work through increasingly durable E2B, Daytona, or Modal environments without users demanding root access, static endpoints, or machine continuity. machine0 has made this proposition concrete: one command provisions an SSH-ready machine with snapshots, credential profiles, up to eight H200 GPUs, and support for runs that now last six to eight hours. But a shipped expression of a secret is not yet proof of one. The dossier shows no comparative experiment demonstrating that persistence changes completion rates, operating cost, or customer retention.

The decisive problem is value capture. machine0 runs on DigitalOcean while E2B, Daytona, and Modal already supply overlapping isolation, snapshots, GPUs, and programmable runtimes. Modal introduced full-VM sandboxes in June 2026; Daytona already had persistence and $5 million in annualized revenue. Static IPs, NixOS, and unlimited duration are useful differences, but they are features available to funded competitors and upstream clouds, not a proprietary technology, network effect, scale economy, or brand. The narrow beachhead—operators of persistent agent fleets—is plausible, yet machine0 exhibits no dominance inside it. This is a commodity position wearing the shape of a monopoly.

Barnaby Malet is the strongest part of the company. His cloud resource-allocation research, infrastructure and security responsibility at Upflow, prior CTO/CPO experience, and earlier Director gateway show that machine0 was built by someone who has lived inside the problem. The product also appears to be built in-house by a sole active founder, which avoids newly assembled-team risk. But the dossier does not establish his ownership, salary, full-time status explicitly, or the organizational capacity required to operate a credential-sensitive compute service alone. A strong founder can discover the right wedge; he cannot make an imitable control plane proprietary by force of commitment.

The bull case is more substantial than the revenue evidence suggests. machine0 has at least one identified customer, PACIFIC displayed as trusted, a Show HN launch with 95 points and 36 comments, an independent review, transparent per-minute pricing, and a self-service curl installation. If persistent machines become the default unit of agent infrastructure, the company could turn profiles, snapshots, and fleet history into switching costs before larger platforms focus on that workflow. This resembles the exception made for a technically important breakthrough despite uncertain monopoly—but no such override is present here. A customer logo and Hacker News attention do not establish a zero-to-one event, retained fleet behavior, or proprietary performance.

The economics remain equally indefinite. CPU pricing begins at $0.013 per hour and GPU pricing at $0.836 per hour, so machine0 has one comprehensible revenue engine rather than several unrelated plans. Yet there is no disclosed revenue, gross margin, workload duration, expansion pattern, or evidence that a reseller dependent on DigitalOcean can preserve enough value to produce a power-law outcome. Distribution is better specified than the moat: self-service installation, founder onboarding, Hacker News, YC, and direct outreach all fit technical buyers. What is missing is evidence that these channels repeat beyond an initial launch or that customer value exceeds support and infrastructure costs.

I would pass today. My 36-month base case is that machine0 pivots from reselling persistent VMs into a narrower agent-fleet control plane and remains below $3 million ARR; otherwise its distinctive features are absorbed by larger sandbox and cloud vendors. One condition would flip the decision: demonstrate 20 paying agent-fleet operators spending at least $2,000 monthly, with greater than 120% net revenue retention attributable to proprietary fleet state or workflow history that competitors cannot reproduce through an ordinary VM migration. Until then, the evidence says capable founder, real need, weak ownership of the resulting market—an infrastructure product, not yet a last mover.
