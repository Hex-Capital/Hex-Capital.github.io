# Rasyn -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Tool-using language models can now coordinate fragmented chemistry software; research labs will buy when an agent reliably removes expert labor, and the controlling variable is the percentage of real workflows completed without manual correction. Rasyn has not measured that variable. Fifty-four integrations describe surface area, while the reported 30.3% retrosynthesis Top-1 mismatch rate suggests that correctness—not tool availability—remains the binding constraint.

Marigold is more substantial than a chemistry chatbot. It reads literature and files, selects tools, executes across 40 user-connected and 14 hosted endpoints, checks intermediate results, and can stop when a step fails. That is an owned operational loop, and better models should improve routing rather than automatically erase the product. But much of the underlying software is third-party or open source, Rowan already offers more than 15 computational workflows, and Rasyn has shown neither proprietary workflow state nor a reproduction cost that would deter a well-funded competitor.

The demand evidence is suggestive but not yet product-market pull. A prior Rasyn product reportedly reached seven medicinal-chemistry teams, yet the source is now a 404 search snippet, with no dated revenue, retention, or usage frequency. Marigold itself launched only in August 2026. Pricing from $89 per researcher to $2,000 per institution establishes a monetization path, but the dossier does not show anyone paying it. More importantly, the institutional tier runs into explicit limits: outputs require experimental verification, and the system is not validated for GLP, GMP, 21 CFR Part 11, or EU Annex 11 workflows.

The strongest bull case is that the product transition reflects learning rather than drift: a computational-chemistry founder, seven claimed early teams, and 54 working integrations could mean Rasyn discovered that orchestration is a larger wedge than standalone retrosynthesis. If those teams repeatedly delegated complete jobs, the company could accumulate workflow knowledge even without training on customer data. The research-stage exception does not apply yet, however. The dossier shows neither a rare frontier-model team nor dated scientific results strong enough to override missing market validation, and the rapid shift from Larmor and Marcus to Marigold leaves the central use case unsettled.

Daood Hashmi’s Caltech chemistry work provides credible domain exposure. Nothing public establishes prior startup learning, costly commitment, or the desperation associated with unusually persistent founders, while Ayush Chauhan’s relevant history is unavailable. That is missing evidence rather than a negative, but founder potential cannot substitute for proving that chemistry organizations will trust the agent with consequential work.

I pass today. My 36-month base case is that Rasyn narrows into a specific computational-chemistry workflow and remains below $1 million ARR rather than becoming a general intelligence layer for chemistry. I would flip if, by August 2027, at least 10 independent labs had each paid for the same defined workflow for three consecutive months while at least 90% of jobs completed without expert correction.
