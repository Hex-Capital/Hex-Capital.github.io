# CoArena -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is not yet large enough. CoArena addresses a real bottleneck: fixed benchmarks such as OSWorld become contaminated and fail to measure changing computer workflows. But the dossier quantifies only a $980 million evaluation-and-simulation market and a $304 million benchmarking segment. It does not quantify the labor, failure cost, or expert-service pool that better computer-use agents could eliminate. This may become infrastructure for autonomous digital work, but “may become” is not a 100x consequence case.

The strongest argument is execution. CoArena produced $60,000 of revenue within two weeks, users doubled weekly, its hackathon attracted 2,550 participants, and 14 agents already appear on the leaderboard. Those are unusually informative pre-seed signals. The founders also built an agent reporting 82.81% on OSWorld before launching the arena. A two-person team with direct computer-use experience has found both supply and initial willingness to pay.

But commercial velocity is outrunning technical proof. Blind votes over paired trajectories could produce a valuable proprietary learning loop, yet the dataset page reports neither task-category data nor collected annotation fields. CoArena publishes 57 measures, but supplies no evidence that its rankings predict production reliability 5–10x better than OSWorld, production traces, or simulated environments. LMArena has already accumulated more than three million votes, while Patronus AI and Braintrust can approach the same budget from simulations and customer traces. Today this is a promising data-acquisition mechanism, not a demonstrated discontinuity.

The largest kill risk is trust, not leaderboard breadth. Coasty operates the ostensibly neutral benchmark while developing its own computer-use agent; excluding that agent from new matches reduces the immediate conflict but does not remove the governance problem. Raw prompts and unmasked screenshots reach model providers without SOC 2, ISO 27001, or an external audit. Yet the dossier provides no cheapest decisive experiment, numerical trust threshold, security milestone, cost, or stop condition. More battles accumulated before resolving neutrality and sensitive-data handling could create volume without creating a licensable asset.

The founders deserve credit for commitment and domain fit: Prateek Jannu abandoned Stanford graduate admission, both founders built Coasty first, and their backgrounds cover machine learning, vision, and enterprise agents. What is missing is evidence of belief revision or risk-tuned recruiting. Two technically similar founders have not yet recruited an independent evaluation, privacy, or security owner for the risks most capable of killing the data business. The likely 36-month outcome is therefore a pivot toward enterprise agent evaluation and trace tooling, with less than $3 million ARR, rather than ownership of the neutral computer-use benchmark.

I pass today. The execution-heavy software pattern and early revenue are genuine exception candidates, but the existing value pool is not yet large enough to override weak technical separation, immature data, and undisclosed unit economics for acquiring human judgments and running paired agents. One condition flips the decision: a named frontier lab signs a contract worth at least $1 million annually after independently verifying that CoArena predicts production computer-use failures at least 5x better than OSWorld. That single event would establish consequence, discontinuity, trust, and economic value with evidence rather than ambition.
