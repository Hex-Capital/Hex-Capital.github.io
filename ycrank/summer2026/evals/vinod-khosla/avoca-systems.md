# Avoca Systems -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

**Max Shand’s prior exit from Serenade and Avoca Systems’ deployment across 250-plus clinics** define the signature: unusually strong founder learning capacity paired with a capital profile too small for my advantage. Avoca Systems looks like an effective vertical-software company, not a consequence-first bet. Avoca Systems may automate a painful workflow, but the economic mechanism that breaks is a radiology call center—not radiology itself.

**Slash (2024)** is the high rung on learning rate because a documented pivot revealed how quickly the founders could abandon their original map when reality disagreed. Avoca Systems has a weaker version of that signal: Shand was Afterpay’s first employee, built Serenade to 347 artists, and exited it, while Mercer-Butcher moved through insurance, property, and workflow software. Avoca Systems diverges from Slash because the dossier documents no specific pivot, rejected thesis, or “we were wrong” moment. A prior exit proves endurance. It does not prove learning rate under contradiction.

**DoorDash (2013)** sits at the low rung on required capital: its weakness for my framework was not business quality but that no $50 million scientific or infrastructure risk required my particular check. Avoca Systems has the same portfolio-fit failure more starkly. Avoca Systems integrates with Epic, Oracle Health, Visage, Karisma, Comrad, and eClinicalWorks, but the dossier names no foundation-model training program, hardware fleet, clinical trial, or deployment roadmap requiring more than $50 million. Any competent healthcare-software investor can fund integrations and enterprise sales. I am over-resourced for that problem.

**The $780 million global radiology-scheduling-software market** constrains Avoca Systems’ consequence even if every implementation succeeds. Over the next 18 months, Avoca Systems can compound operational knowledge: every protocol, contrast rule, roster constraint, and RIS write-back makes the next deployment easier. But that mechanism optimizes an incumbent workflow rather than collapsing one. Moving call abandonment from roughly 30% to 2% is excellent execution; replacing scheduling labor across one sub-billion-dollar software category is not the **OpenAI** or **Impossible Foods** world-state. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself” does not mean every efficient company is too important not to do.

**AbbaDox already markets VoiceAI, FaxAI, scheduling, results sharing, and referrer communications**, so the founder-defiance score flips only if Avoca Systems demonstrates a position radiology incumbents consider wrong—not merely difficult to implement. Avoca Systems would need to argue, and prove, that autonomous operational reasoning can replace most human coordination across modalities rather than reduce unanswered calls. Neither Shand nor Mercer-Butcher has documented radiology employment or a public challenge to clinical-software orthodoxy. Experts extrapolate the past, but Avoca Systems currently appears to be executing the industry’s accepted AI roadmap faster.

**Glean** became interesting because enterprise integrations were the substrate for a broader information layer, not the product’s final boundary. Avoca Systems has six named healthcare-system integrations, SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, and HIPAA compliance, yet Avoca Systems shows no comparable proprietary-learning mechanism: no exclusive data rights, demonstrated model improvement, or technical breakthrough competitors cannot reproduce. Clinical-booking errors and protected-health-data exposure create execution risk, but risk is not automatically a moat. Required miracles: zero scientific miracles and perhaps one market miracle—turning customer-specific implementation work into a scalable operational intelligence layer.

**More than 250 live clinics** lead me to a falsifiable forecast: by July 2029, Avoca Systems will be an independent $8 million–$18 million ARR radiology-workflow vendor that has expanded into referral authorization and follow-up, but it will not have displaced the broader radiology operating stack. Avoca Systems’ repeat-founder learning signal supports commercial survival and adjacent expansion; Avoca Systems’ low-capital, integration-dependent architecture supports a good vertical SaaS outcome rather than a civilizational one.

**The reduction in call abandonment from roughly 30% to 2%** makes the strongest bull mechanism workflow-density compounding: Avoca Systems could turn 250-plus clinics, networks exceeding 150 locations, and encoded clinical rules into a data advantage before AbbaDox or OpenDoctor can catch up. Avoca Systems could become a **DoorDash**-style known exception—capital-light, operationally obsessive, and much larger than the initial category suggests. The objection stings because deployment, not research prestige, often determines the winner. The dossier still gives the deciding evidence against it: a $780 million category, direct feature overlap, no proprietary-data rights, and no measured learning advantage. I would flip to Invest if Avoca Systems verifies at least $25 million ARR across 1,000 clinics while autonomously completing over 70% of end-to-end referral, authorization, scheduling, and follow-up work.
