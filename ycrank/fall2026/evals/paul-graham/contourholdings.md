# Contour -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The first thing I want to know is what Contour’s founders have already done inside an environmental consulting practice. Which report got stuck? What did they build to unstick it? How long did that take? The dossier describes acquiring firms and embedding engineers, but gives no instance of a blocker followed by a shipped workaround. That leaves my most important founder test unanswered. It does not establish that these founders wait for permission; it gives me no basis for concluding they overcome resistance.

There is a good idea inside the acquisition model. Owning a practice could put engineers beside the people doing the work, with access to actual documents, customer relationships, and the consequences of mistakes. Contour would collect service fees while learning which tasks to automate. That is a stronger bull case than simply offering another document generator. Aarush Agrawal’s enterprise-search work at Rezolve AI and Zack Lubar’s Lazard background give the team relevant starting skills for software and acquisitions. But the crucial loop—own the workflow, improve it, retain the customer, repeat—has no documented completed turn. Buying revenue would establish demand for the acquired practice; it would still take separate evidence to establish that Cortex improves its economics.

Cortex is described as producing reports, studies, and permit applications. There is no inspected output, measured reduction in expert work, or completed application establishing what it can do. The technical founders I favor turn claims into things one can test. Contour’s description does not yet supply that proof. Nor does the dossier identify a consulting team repeatedly using Cortex or a customer paying for work it enabled. Fall 2026 YC participation is evidence of selection, not customer pull. These are untested dimensions, rather than evidence that the product failed.

The founders have chosen work with real unpleasantness: regulatory interpretation, professional review, acquisitions, and on-site implementation. That could conceal an opportunity. Yet the dossier does not connect the choice to a founder’s own encounter with permitting, and it records no customer observation that caused a product revision. Neither résumé relevance nor willingness expressed on a website fills those gaps. The broader $27 billion environmental consulting market, growing at 3% annually, establishes substantial existing spending. Whether Contour can grow much faster depends on increasing productive capacity without increasing expert labor proportionally. Paces and Transect make regulatory software an existing competitive reality; ownership must produce a measurable advantage.

I would pass on the evidence available. Contour has the outline of an operationally difficult business I could like, but it lacks the concrete proof spikes and technical-user adoption that recur in my investments. The known exceptions do not change that decision: none supplies an override here without a working prototype, validated technical result, or unmistakable user behavior. Conversely, acquisition dependence does not prove the founders are waiting for funding, and their incomplete relationship history does not prove a hastily assembled team. My flip condition is one documented live deployment in which the founders encounter a specific permitting obstacle, ship a correction within weeks, and show the practice repeatedly using the corrected system on paid work with measured time savings and preserved review quality. That would connect founder behavior, technical proof, and demand in a way the current plan cannot.
