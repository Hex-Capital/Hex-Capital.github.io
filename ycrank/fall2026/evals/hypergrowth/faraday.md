# Faraday -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Channel-led or repeatable sales, through direct sales.** Manufacturers and distributors have a recurring job reconciling orders, schedules, and exceptions across disconnected systems. Faraday’s referral request and offer to demo on prospects’ own records give it a concrete route to buyers and a way to demonstrate value. The cross-market case assumes the same job recurs at similar buyers and the shared runtime can carry over, with changes bounded to integrations, workflow rules, and permissions. That is plausible, though the scope of those changes remains unmeasured. (The Idea; Key Risks)

Faraday’s founder reports one unnamed customer deployment where switching to SuperServe’s sandbox resolved a legacy-system blocker. That gives the two-person team an early signal that it can work around a real integration problem. The memo leaves the route from lead to deployment unverified and gives no buyer-confirmed evidence of budget, workflow change, or recurring use. Even assuming strong fit, the first link likely to break is repeatable onboarding for the next buyer. (Market & Traction; Founders & Team; Key Facts)

### Countercase

Faraday could end up doing custom integration on every sale. The reported deployment stalled on legacy-system access, Faraday says reliability must be evaluated against each customer’s workflows, and write-enabled deployments require separate authorization and agreed controls. Its reported benchmark gain supports technical promise, while live reliability, repeat use, and the labor and support costs of successive rollouts remain unmeasured. The structural downside gate stays open because one resolved blocker leaves reuse testable on the next deployment. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a second independent buyer reached through the referral-and-demo motion go live on the same recurring workflow in a paid rollout using the existing runtime, integration pattern, and validation steps? A buyer-confirmed go-live with recorded implementation hours and a clear account of what carried over would move the action to Reach out if it showed bounded customization and a credible onboarding handoff.
