# Antropi Robotics -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition is Antropi’s strongest supported route. Factory OS combines machining-program generation, robotic loading, and inspection feedback in Antropi’s own cells. An established manufacturing provider could buy the system to deploy that workflow across its facilities if it improves yield or lowers the cost per accepted part. The memo records MISUMI’s agreement to acquire Fictiv, evidence of strategic interest in adjacent manufacturing capabilities. The deal gives limited pricing guidance because Fictiv operates a supplier network and the memo supplies no transaction price. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

Antropi reports repeat orders from a space customer and a U.S. factory alongside its Bengaluru pilot. Agarwal also reports that a customer helped fund the first U.S. machine. These are early signals that Antropi can replicate a cell. The milestone is repeat production at both sites with a measurable cost or yield advantage after accounting for machine, staffing, and inspection costs. The memo leaves utilization and the capital needed for further sites unverified. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3%, with no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity. The scenarios below assume a full cash sale within that horizon, holder eligibility, and pro rata distribution with no preference claims. The acquisition prices and deductions are illustrative. Agarwal’s reported $100,000 in October revenue establishes early commercial activity, while the durability of that revenue and the economics a buyer could capture remain unverified. The memo gives no holder terms or evidence of a separate liquidity route. (*Key Facts*, *Market & Traction*)

- **Modest sale.** At $60 million in enterprise value, $5 million in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $55 million for equity. Selling the full 3% stake yields $1.65 million in cash, or 3.3×.
- **Strong sale.** At $110 million in enterprise value with the same deductions, $105 million goes to equity. The holder receives $3.15 million, or 6.3×. Further dilution to 2% lowers that payout to $2.10 million, or 4.2×.
- **Prolonged private operation.** If Antropi reinvests available cash throughout the eight years and there is no funded payout or eligible share purchase, the holder realizes $0.

### Countercase

A buyer could build much of the workflow itself. Protolabs already offers one-day milling on eligible jobs, and Vention sells configurable CNC machine tending. Antropi’s inspection feedback could still justify an acquisition, but engineer approvals may limit throughput and each new site adds costs ahead of proven utilization. These facts weaken the case for paying enough to clear the holder’s hurdle. The Pass gate remains open because the memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a credible ceiling on sale proceeds. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Factory OS deliver enough repeatable savings at an established manufacturer’s facilities to justify an acquisition that pays this holder $2.5 million within eight years? Move to Reach out if matched results from Bengaluru and the U.S. show a durable advantage after capital and operating costs, and a prospective buyer can validate economics that support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity under the benchmark ownership and waterfall.
