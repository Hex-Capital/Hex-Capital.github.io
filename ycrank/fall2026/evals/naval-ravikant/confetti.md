# Confetti -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Confetti’s strongest founder evidence is a completed research artifact: Gu and Yuan coauthored EngineLab, accepted at the 2026 ICML NExT-Game workshop. They have built something together and exposed it to outside evaluation. That earns more credit than their university affiliations. Yuan’s reported Penn Electric Racing participation adds some proximity to physical systems. But neither establishes that these founders have repeatedly taken novice hardware projects from design through working assembly. I see technical ability; the specific knowledge required by this product remains largely unproven.

The decisive question is what Confetti owns after someone copies its software. Helping people build hardware does not itself give Confetti a hardware moat. Its advertised workflow spans design editing, component selection, printed-part ordering, and remixing, but the dossier establishes no proprietary model, exclusive manufacturing relationship, or accumulated compatibility asset. Zoo already offers conversational CAD with geometry inspection and debugging; Flux combines electronics design with sourcing and manufacturing outputs. These competitors do not prove Confetti is interchangeable. They establish that generating designs is becoming available infrastructure. Confetti must capture something durable beyond that capability.

The strongest opposing case is that the complete beginner experience creates the asset. A successfully assembled project could become reusable supply for the next builder. Every remix could lower the cost of making something useful, opening hardware creation to people who cannot operate CAD software. That fits my preference for permissionless creation and users producing value for other users. The integration may matter more than any individual design tool. But Confetti currently advertises this loop; the dossier does not observe it. Its 58 LinkedIn followers establish neither completed builds nor contributions that help another customer. A potential community earns no network-effect premium.

The economic path is similarly plausible but unestablished. Printed-part ordering could put Confetti directly inside a transaction, yet transaction fees are explicitly an inference: no verified price, completed orders, or revenue appears. I assign the missing-evidence default rather than assume bad economics. The initial buyer is a first-time hardware builder, but the dossier does not identify adjacent buyers and demonstrate how the same capability reaches their spending. “Hardware” is a large category; it is not an evidenced expansion path. Manufacturing and shipping also leave open whether each additional project creates reusable knowledge or another obligation for the team.

I would pass with my own money today. My exceptions for prelaunch products depend on a demonstrated prototype or unusually revealing founder behavior; exceptions for uncertain monetization depend on an observable network already forming. Neither override is established here. The possible transition from Asteria does not establish repeated abandonment, and there is no documented integrity or financing-pressure red flag. This is a pass on the evidence, not an adverse judgment of the founders. One verifiable result would reopen it: a cohort of 20 independent beginners paying to manufacture and successfully assemble remixes of an existing community project without founder design repairs. That would begin turning Confetti’s central promise into demonstrated customer-created value.
