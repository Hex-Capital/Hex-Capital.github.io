# Reific -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Reific attacks a real constraint: an agent that answers correctly after seeing the entire history may fail when it must act before the next record arrives. Removing that constraint could make scientific and industrial expertise substantially more useful. But the dossier does not quantify the labor, spending, or resource bottleneck displaced. “Superhuman reasoning over time” names an ambition; it does not establish the consequence of success. I can lose my entire check on an improbable breakthrough. First I need evidence of what that breakthrough buys.

The strongest evidence is the product’s construction. Reific releases real records on a clock, checks answers against recorded outcomes, and combines model judges with numeric checks. That is a concrete experimental mechanism. The November 2 results—31.6% reward for personal agents, 43.1% for industry, and 29.7% for science/compute—expose substantial remaining difficulty. They do not measure improvement produced by training with Reific. A difficult examination can identify ignorance without teaching anything transferable. Neither Prime Intellect nor Patronus is compared against Reific in a controlled training experiment, so I cannot credit a demonstrated technical discontinuity.

The bull case deserves attention. Antanas Žilinskas’s first-authored EVEREST research concerns temporal forecasting, giving him relevant technical grounding for this particular problem. Recorded longitudinal data could also support my preferred pattern: turn a scarce scientific workflow into a repeatable production platform. Reific’s outcome verification is a credible starting point. However, purchasing records and constructing answer keys does not yet establish a proprietary learning loop. Nonexclusive licenses leave competing suppliers able to obtain overlapping inputs. The founder’s research credentials support his ability to investigate the problem; they supply no observed evidence of belief revision or complementary recruiting, which remain unproven rather than adverse.

The next dollar should test transfer. Running 64 simultaneous sandboxes without failure establishes useful infrastructure reliability, but it leaves the central investment uncertainty untouched: does training here improve decisions on genuinely unseen temporal systems? No disclosed experiment connects spending to a transfer threshold and an explicit stop-or-change decision. **My flip condition is an independently reproduced frontier-lab trial showing at least twice the improvement per all-in training dollar versus its existing environment curriculum on previously unseen temporal tasks, scored against outcomes unavailable during training.** That is a proposed investment test, not a result Reific has reported. Include record licensing, answer-key construction, judging, and compute in the denominator.

I would pass with my own money today. Reific is not shown to be a generic model wrapper, and there is no evidence of premature sales expansion, subsidy dependence, or founder resistance to contrary data. Those red flags cannot be invented. But the exceptions that let me invest before conventional validation require unusually compelling evidence of exponential progress, a quantified enormous consequence, or a documented instigator attacking a giant existing value pool. This dossier establishes none of those overrides. Prime Intellect’s broader business exceeding $100 million in annualized revenue supports spending in the surrounding infrastructure market; it does not establish demand for Reific’s specific product. With no disclosed pricing or production-cost model, unsubsidized economics also remain unknown. The interesting option here is a temporal learning engine. The evidence currently establishes an environment-building capability.
