# Particularity -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with its initial consumer protection agencies, Particularity has a recurring job to automate. The company describes browser agents that test changing checkout and cancellation flows, timestamp and hash captures, match them to complaints, and link findings to statutory elements for staff review. The browser testing and capture steps look reusable across jurisdictions. The full investigator workflow depends on mapping those findings to local law and meeting each agency’s requirements for usable evidence. New York City’s Click-to-Cancel rule illustrates how the same cancellation flow can give rise to a jurisdiction-specific enforcement task. The structural downside gate applies because transferring the current agency product would likely require substantial jurisdiction-specific implementation, and the memo establishes no credible near-term path to bounded rollout work. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The observable acquisition path starts with agency outreach and call booking. That points to human-led selling, and the memo supplies no repeatable path from a booked call to a paid deployment or first use. Continued monitoring and an accumulating case history could support renewals if agencies adopt the workflow. Agency contracts are an inferred business model, and the memo gives no pricing or billable unit that would let retained accounts spend more. The October 15 public launch establishes timing. Paid use and revenue status are unknown, so initial traction receives the default. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under the agency-contract scenario, incremental contribution would depend on the contract price after attributable selling and setup, browser runs, evidence storage, and support. Staff review is part of the described agency workflow. Any review or remediation Particularity must perform would also affect contribution. The memo provides too little operating evidence to estimate those costs or show that they fall with repeat deployments, so cost economics receives the default. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The strongest growth case is that agencies can reuse the same browser checks, timestamped captures, and complaint-matching workflow, with local legal rules handled through configuration and agency staff retaining responsibility for review. The memo’s description supports a reusable technical core, and the EU enforcement sweeps show a comparable investigative job abroad. Configuration-only transfer remains a scenario assumption. The memo does not establish that a foreign agency can use the resulting evidence in its existing workflow with bounded setup, so the structural gate still applies. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a consumer protection agency in a materially different legal jurisdiction use Particularity’s current product in a paid deployment and accept its evidence for routine investigations with configuration and standard onboarding? A deployment record showing agency acceptance, limited implementation time, and the product changes required would answer it. Bounded rollout work would clear the portability gate and move the action to Watch while repeatable paid adoption is tested.
