# Kanopy -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Kanopy’s opportunity is to make utility-grade inspection possible with cameras customers already own. Utilities and contractors would buy if video reconstruction reduces the all-in cost per mile of an accepted inspection, including capture, processing, and human review. That is the controlling variable. The claimed 2–4-inch accuracy gives the technical proposition a measurable target, but the dossier does not establish whether that accuracy survives ordinary field conditions or how much review each mile requires. A cheaper sensor only changes the market if it produces a cheaper usable result.

Eliminating specialized LiDAR hardware could expand who collects inspection data and how frequently networks are surveyed. Public reconstruction models such as VGGT, published in 2025, provide a credible technical discontinuity. They also lower competitors’ development costs. Kanopy has a plausible mechanism for changing inspection economics, but no documented comparison of cost per mile, processing time, or delivery margins. The $31.95 billion vegetation-management estimate includes physical treatment; it does not establish the revenue available to this software layer.

The strongest argument for investing is that customers are already engaging with an unfinished product. Antler reported six utilities and contractors signing LOIs or MSAs covering 270,000 miles in April 2026. Kanopy subsequently reported 3,093 mapped miles, including more than 300 across four states in two weeks. Those are meaningful pre-seed signals. I give the operational mileage more weight than the announced scope: an MSA can establish purchasing terms without committing purchases, and the dossier does not connect mapped miles to paid orders, repeat surveys, or customer acceptance. Still, this is enough activity to make dismissing the company as a reconstruction demo premature.

The product also extends beyond generating a 3D scene. Clearance measurements, hazard flags, exportable work records, and release-note corrections to pole associations and wire fitting show work on the operational result. Better vision models could improve that result while Kanopy retains asset history and customer workflow. However, longitudinal records remain a proposed moat, and documented work-order integrations, exclusive data rights, and customer correction loops are absent. My pattern of backing software around physical-industry transitions fits the opportunity. My red flag about unchanged service economics remains unresolved: field collection and manual correction could absorb the hardware savings. Schneider’s announced AiDASH acquisition also means an adjacent competitor could gain distribution through existing grid software relationships.

Yann’s utility advisory experience and Nicolas’s computer-vision leadership make this a credible team to investigate the problem. They do not establish founder desperation or second-time startup learning; Aerate participation alone is insufficient evidence of either. I also do not see a known-exception override. The dossier lacks the documented frontier research and capital path that would justify overlooking commercial proof, while the physical-industry exception requires demonstrated economic improvement. Domain credentials cannot substitute for that measurement.

I would pass on a check today, while keeping Kanopy under observation. The evidence supports real product activity more strongly than it supports an economic step change, leaving the combined market gate unmet. **One verifiable flip condition:** a utility renews a paid deployment after a matched field comparison demonstrates accepted clearance measurements within its specified tolerance at materially lower all-in cost per mile than its existing process, including human review. That result would connect the technical claim, purchasing behavior, and margin mechanism in one customer decision.
