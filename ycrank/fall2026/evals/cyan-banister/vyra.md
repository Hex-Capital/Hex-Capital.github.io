# Vyra -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a creator dropping footage into a personal editor that already understands her taste, remembers what she rejected, and quietly finishes the tedious work. She spends her afternoon making something worth recording. Vyra’s October 25 headless MCP release is an interesting object from that direction: editing can happen through an external assistant without keeping a browser alive. But the dossier does not establish the intervening steps—persistent taste, reliable unattended execution, and a learning advantage from completed projects. I can imagine that future. Vyra has not yet supplied the evidence connecting its current product to it.

The founders are the strongest reason to keep looking. Caleb brings seven years of freelance editing and public software projects; Sulan combines computer science, visual arts, and an 80K-plus creator audience. They started building together in winter 2025 and shipped across web, mobile, and external-assistant interfaces. These are people who have made videos and recruited attention themselves. That gives their diagnosis weight. The record supports complementary builders with genuine operating familiarity, although it does not yet show the long sequence of independently motivated experiments that would make me unusually confident in their obsession.

Why does editing consume hours? Some of that work is mechanical, and some is deciding what deserves to survive the cut. Vyra can return meaningful creative control if it removes the mechanical burden while preserving the creator’s judgment. Its $24 monthly external-AI entry plan makes that experiment accessible. But the dossier identifies a labor-intensive workflow rather than a specific artificial restriction or gatekeeper collecting a quantified rent. I give the team credit for attacking a real time tax; I cannot yet tell how much of that tax the product actually removes.

The strongest objection to passing is that these founders may possess exactly the combination this market needs: editing fluency, technical ability, and a creator who can demonstrate the product to her own audience. Their response to browser disconnections—moving execution server-side—also shows contact with an unpleasant operating reality. This fits my pattern of finding useful workflow repair inside a founder’s own frustration. Yet the behavioral evidence remains thin. “93,984 creators” has no active-user definition, and 17 million promotional views measure attention. Buffer’s July test took approximately 20 minutes to process an 81-second clip without adding music or B-roll. The October rebuild could improve that experience, but its announcement does not establish that users now entrust recurring work to Vyra.

The headless architecture creates another possibility: Vyra could become the editing machinery underneath many assistants. That would interest me more than another conversational editing surface. Descript already offers MCP, however, and Mirage exposes overlapping agent tools. There is no demonstrated scarce asset, proprietary preference learning, or dependency that makes Vyra necessary underneath those interfaces. External-assistant support reduces my concern about dependence on one closed assistant provider; it does not establish infrastructure ownership. Nor does the dossier demonstrate severe privacy misconduct, deceptive leadership, or founders dismissing incumbent complexity. My hesitation comes from the investment mechanism, rather than an established fatal red flag.

I would pass with my own money today. My willingness to back pre-revenue prototypes does not resolve this: Vyra already has a product, and the missing conviction concerns what repeated use uniquely builds. **My flip condition is a verifiable cohort of 20 independent paying creators who use Vyra weekly for eight consecutive weeks, with project records showing that retained preferences let them publish progressively more edits without corrective intervention.** That would turn a plausible personal editor into evidence of a relationship that compounds. These founders have earned another look when that artifact exists.
