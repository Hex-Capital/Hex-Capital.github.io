# Synara -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest path. Synara’s Fall 2026 launch and San Francisco base make it a plausible seed candidate. Its advertised service uses existing cameras to flag incidents for human verification, then supports live voice intervention, verified alarms, and incident documentation. If filtering reduces operator labor at equivalent detection and response quality, integrators could sell daytime response to construction yards and dealerships that currently leave daytime incidents to site staff. The memo records the launch and partner program. The under-30-second alert-to-action figure is a company claim, and the memo provides no measured labor savings, paid pilots, or repeat use by those sites. (*The Idea*; *Market & Traction*; *Key Risks*; *Key Facts*)

Ploix previously led development of false-alarm filtering for monitoring centers. That experience supports his ability to build the filtering layer. Synara’s ability to deliver the full response service at a viable cost remains unverified. In a scenario with recurring wholesale fees, the expansion unit is one additional site on an integrator’s installed camera base. Its contribution would be the site fee less inference, operator coverage, verification, follow-up, and site setup and support. The memo gives no prices, operator workloads, or deployment costs to validate that bridge. (*Founders & Team*; *The Idea*; *Key Risks*; *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Cheaper detection could lower costs for existing monitoring centers while leaving daytime response too labor-intensive to open a new market. Immix lets centers add AI filtering to existing workflows, and Becklar already sells remote guarding through dealers. With comparable models, an integrator would need a reason to keep paying Synara for the executed response. Site rules, escalation contacts, and incident history could create switching costs, but the memo shows no retention or buyer preference and identifies no scarce response capacity Synara controls. The alternatives weigh against durable value capture. They leave both Path B conditions unproven, and the memo offers no independent Path A case. (*Defensibility*; *Market & Traction*; *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent integrators renew paid daytime response for sites that previously relied on staff, at a price that leaves Synara positive site contribution, when a credible alternative has comparable detection? A paid side-by-side trial should measure operator time through verification, exceptions, and follow-up at equivalent incident coverage and response quality, then record which service integrators choose at renewal. Repeat paid preference for Synara with measured labor savings and positive contribution would support rescoring and a move to Reach out if the score reaches 65 with at least medium confidence. If the trial shows that labor savings disappear at equivalent quality or that integrators readily switch at parity, move to Pass.
