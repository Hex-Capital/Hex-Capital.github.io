# Clean -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Clean sits at the intersection of cheaper AI research and an existing sales-intelligence budget: sellers buy better account selection, and the controlling variable is incremental sales profit per researched account after data and software costs. Customs shipments, permits, loans and contracts could identify demand before it appears in conventional intent signals. But an operational event must predict demand for a particular seller’s product. Evidence that a company is expanding does not establish that it is ready to buy what you sell.

The strongest argument for investing is that customers already appear to find this useful. Clean reports **$240K ARR before YC**, and named customer Vivameda reports **$60K in contracts within two weeks**, with reply rates rising from roughly **4% to 19%**. Those are meaningful pre-seed signals. I discount them because the ARR is founder-reported and the testimonial is company-hosted, but I would not dismiss either. The limitation is attribution: replacing a list where half the contacts bounced or changed roles could improve results substantially without validating buying-window prediction. Vivameda’s contracts are customer outcomes, not Clean’s revenue; its additional $120K pipeline is prospective.

That distinction determines whether Clean has a company-sized opening in a category whose market structure is already crystallizing. Clay announced context-aware research agents and signal monitoring in September 2026; Apollo combines research, contacts and outreach. Their estimated revenues of approximately **$200M and $265M**, respectively, demonstrate existing budgets and distribution. Clean’s CEO attributes the summer 2026 launch to newly available AI capabilities, but the dossier supplies no extraction-cost, accuracy or delivery-speed improvement that establishes a new economic advantage. My pattern of backing multiple layers of a technology wave does not resolve this problem: an established application category requires a specific reason the entrant captures value.

There is more product here than a prompt. Source-linked company timelines, verified contacts, a LinkedIn connector and optional email connections provide useful workflow context. Better models could improve record interpretation while Clean retains that context. However, the dossier does not establish a purchase-outcome feedback loop, deep action integrations or responsibility for the completed sales workflow. The claimed proprietary datasets therefore trigger my data-moat question: what would reproducing their coverage, freshness and entity resolution cost a well-funded competitor? No estimate is available. That leaves modest architectural credit, rather than evidence that model progress necessarily strengthens Clean’s position.

The founders’ summer pivot from developer infrastructure to sales intelligence is relevant operating history, and Pavan’s Cisco sales experience gives the team customer context. Neither establishes the costly commitment or demonstrated second-time learning my founder criterion rewards. I also would not transfer the old MCP product’s 60,000 interactions into evidence of demand for this product. Checking the exceptions, the dossier establishes neither scarce frontier-model research nor a scientific or physical-economics breakthrough that would justify overriding the market gates.

I would hold off on investing my own money. Clean’s strongest evidence is current customer pull; its weakest is the economic discontinuity that would let it outgrow incumbent distribution. The bull case is substantial: customers may already be paying for a predictive advantage that public evidence has not isolated. My single flip condition is a **90-day matched-cohort study across three paying customers showing at least twice the gross profit per outbound dollar, after Clean’s fees, versus their best existing Clay or Apollo workflow**, with contact freshness held comparable. That would directly test the mechanism on which I would underwrite this business.
