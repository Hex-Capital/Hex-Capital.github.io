# Repovive -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path B, repeatable sales with account expansion, is the strongest fit. The initial buyer is an AI lab’s post-training team that needs fresh, private Lean tasks with machine-checked reference proofs for training and evaluation. The memo documents a founder-led route through lab introductions and sample requests. Repovive reports $100K in signed contracts across two formalization environments and a separate $200K coding-data letter of intent. The contracts are an early budget signal. The memo leaves buyer count, live usage, and collected revenue unverified. It also leaves open whether the sample route produced either contract. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The growth scenario assumes the same sample and integration process can activate labs in other markets, then turn recurring training and evaluation into paid task refreshes or additional workloads. Reusing the environment could spread integration effort across accounts. Each refresh could still require fresh task authoring, reference-proof work, and quality checks, while each new lab could add sales, integration, and support time. A second-market rollout would show whether the same package can meet another lab’s integration, procurement, and data-handling requirements. Cross-market demand, repeat use, and incremental delivery effort remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

### Countercase

The strongest countercase is that labs buy a one-off formalization project and handle subsequent work internally or with open tools. The memo documents open Lean training resources and in-house lab research, both of which make that outcome plausible. If labs pay only for custom tasks and integrations, each sale could require a fresh round of specialist work. The memo provides no rollout or cost records that establish proportional effort or a structural ceiling on demand, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent lab in a second geography go from the standard Lean sample to a live paid deployment and then pay for a task refresh without a bespoke rebuild? Usage and payment records, together with time spent on sales, integration, proof production, and support, would answer it. A rollout that shows paid repeat use and limited incremental specialist work would move the action to Reach out.
