# Hesper AI -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The useful fact about Hesper AI is that the founders tried selling fraud scores and failed. They then rebuilt around doing the investigation. That is a substantial change: a score gives an already busy investigator another thing to examine; a completed case file might remove work. The sequence starts with fraud-scoring software in April 2026 and ends with a reported production deployment. This is evidence of resourcefulness, though the [founder’s account](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nitish-badu-529b28165_five-months-ago-i-quit-my-job-to-build-something-activity-7505656789254823936-8xCr) does not establish how quickly the rebuild happened. I give this episode credit for overcoming resistance. It does not independently establish a repeated ability to revise product hypotheses.

There appears to be something working. Hesper reports an MGA deployment that reduced adjuster time per claim by 82% over two weeks. Its agents gather records, reconstruct timelines and produce evidence-backed files for review. That is a more useful technical artifact than a diagram of an autonomous insurance company. But the measurement comes from the founders, the customer is unnamed, and there is no independent examination of the files. Identifying $1.2 million in potential fraud does not establish that the fraud was confirmed. The handling-time result is the more informative claim.

The strongest argument for investing is that customers may already be pulling this product into their operations. The founders report [10,000-plus claims investigated](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nitish-badu-529b28165_insurancefraud-claims-insuranceai-activity-7503120272028327936-aFWX). At pre-seed, that deserves attention. The narrow user is identifiable: an MGA investigation team with more flagged files than people to examine them. Yet aggregate processing volume does not tell me whether customers keep submitting claims after an evaluation ends. Hesper offers its first 200 claims free and waives platform fees for 90 days. That is a reasonable way to recruit users, but it makes continued paid use particularly informative. The $1.5 million financing supplies no answer to that question.

Dhariwal and Badu have relevant experience building and selling document automation at Nanonets. They also worked together before Hesper, which reduces my concern about a cofounder relationship assembled for an application. Their prior employers’ revenue establishes experience, not Hesper’s demand. What remains missing is the origin of this particular problem: which investigation did they encounter themselves, and what did existing software prevent someone from doing? The database licences, permissible-purpose requirements and sensitive records described in the [MSA](https://gethesperai.com/legal/msa/) are genuine schlep. Their existence alone does not prove these founders have learned to get customers through it.

Hesper fits my pattern of preferring concrete proof before grand claims, but the proof is still mostly founder-reported. The bear case is that the apparent product improvement depends on human cleanup and customer data access that vary enormously between deployments. Shift and FRISS already have insurance relationships and overlapping products. If each customer requires a different investigation service, processed claims could grow without a repeatable business emerging. Founder-led selling and rebuilding after rejection argue against the red flags of waiting for a partner or delegating early customer learning. A working-product exception can justify overlooking limited insurance tenure; it cannot substitute for evidence that customers repeatedly need the result.

I am **Neutral**. Hesper has earned a serious conversation through its response to failed sales and its reported deployment. It has not yet convinced me to write a personal check. My flip condition is one referenceable MGA renewing a paid deployment after the introductory period, with claim records showing sustained time savings after including human review and correction. That would connect the founders’ resourcefulness to a customer who wants the product back.
