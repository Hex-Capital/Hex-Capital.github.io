# Resin Technologies -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Product-led adoption is Resin’s strongest path. Electrical engineers on hardware teams have a recurring job designing and revising printed circuit boards. The memo documents browser signup, a YC launch promotion, and advertised paid tiers. Resin describes a workflow that turns requirements into editable schematics, routes boards, and exchanges editable files with KiCad and Altium. Those integrations make the same workflow plausible for teams in a second market, assuming their component sourcing and security requirements can be met. If teams adopt Resin on a first board, reusable workspace component libraries could bring them back for later projects and give them a reason to add paid seats. (The Idea, Key Risks, Defensibility)

The first link likely to break is self-serve activation into paid use. The founder reports that unnamed teams shipped boards in hours instead of weeks. The memo leaves their route from signup to first value, the help they received, and their payment status unresolved. The account is an early value signal, with too little detail to establish buyer commitment or repeatable onboarding. Return use, paid expansion, and the incremental cost of compute and support also remain unmeasured. (Market & Traction, The Idea)

### Countercase

Resin’s beta autorouter can introduce wiring, component, or clearance errors, and its terms require engineering review before fabrication. If ordinary review repeatedly turns into extensive rework or Resin staff intervention, teams could try the free product and keep their recurring work in incumbent tools that already offer automated routing. That would weaken paid conversion and raise the cost of each successful activation. The memo establishes the risk and leaves its frequency and cost open, so the structural downside gate does not apply. (Key Risks, Defensibility)

### Decisive diligence question

Can multiple independent hardware teams go from browser signup to a first engineer-approved board and a paid subscription through the same onboarding process? Session, support, and billing records showing that sequence with routine engineering review and bounded Resin staff time would move the action to Reach out. Repeated failures to convert or evidence that each successful team requires substantial custom help would move it to Pass.
