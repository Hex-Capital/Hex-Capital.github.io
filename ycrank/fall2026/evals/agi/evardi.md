# Evardi -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path A, scarce complements, is the strongest fit for this San Francisco seed candidate. The bottleneck is distribution utilities’ ability to verify safe switching on feeders where operators have limited transformer-level visibility and rely on manual checks. Evardi combines utility data into a power-flow model and checks proposed switching steps against operating limits before operator approval. In a public-data simulation, Evardi reports catching 60% of the errors that passed manual review. If cheaper intelligence reduces the engineering work needed to build and maintain these models, Evardi could expand utilities’ capacity to verify switching plans. The scarce capability would be a model qualified on the utility’s own network. Evardi’s ability to supply that capability reliably in the field remains unproven. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Evardi reports deployments at utilities serving more than 10 million electricity customers. That figure describes the utilities’ footprint and leaves paid use, buyer commitments, and renewal behavior unknown. Shah’s grid control-room and integration experience and Borele’s energy operations and engineering experience support seed-stage execution. Each additional utility is a plausible unit of expansion, but contract value and the costs of integration, qualification, and ongoing support are unknown. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

SMUD deployed meter-based transformer mapping and load monitoring alongside an AspenTech integration for switching and restoration. That deployment shows that a utility can obtain overlapping capabilities through its existing stack. With ample capital and cheap models, competitors could build similar integrations, and utilities could retain control of the topology and switching histories that make them useful. Those histories could create switching costs for Evardi, but the memo establishes neither durable control nor a performance advantage over the incumbent workflow. The incumbent evidence leaves Path A open at seed while blocking outreach today. Path B has no separate supported case because the memo identifies no newly viable buyer or transaction. (Defensibility, Key Risks, The Idea)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent utility qualify Evardi for a paid rollout after a head-to-head shadow trial on its own feeders? To qualify, Evardi would need to meet the utility’s safety thresholds, materially reduce operator review time against its current workflow, and earn a commitment tied to a validated capability the utility cannot readily reproduce with its existing stack. A positive result would warrant rescoring for Reach out if it clears the score and confidence gates. Comparable incumbent performance at comparable effort would move the case to Pass. (Defensibility, Key Risks)
