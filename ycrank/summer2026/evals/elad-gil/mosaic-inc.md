# Mosaic -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: coding agents became a substantial software category after Claude Code’s May 2025 launch and $1 billion run-rate milestone, engineering teams now operate several agents concurrently, and Mosaic becomes large only if shared agent state produces sustained team-level adoption rather than remaining an IDE feature. The controlling variable is the number of teams whose agents and developers repeatedly hand work across Mosaic sessions. The dossier supplies no threshold, retention cohort, or paid deployment bearing directly on that variable, so the central market gate remains unproven.

The technology shift is real. Anthropic’s 2026 trend report identifies multi-agent coordination, and Mosaic already connects Claude Code, Codex, and OpenCode while preserving sessions across people and devices. This can reduce reconstruction work as agent concurrency rises. But the $29.47 billion AI-code-tools estimate and 45.3% multi-agent CAGR do not establish Mosaic’s economics. Conductor, Superset, and Pentagon can converge on the same collaboration layer, while upstream coding platforms can incorporate shared state directly.

The strongest bull case is that real-time shared terminal control is the first product in a broader system of record for agentic engineering. Persistent histories, permissions, actions, and organizational context could deepen the workflow, and $200 per seat per month creates meaningful revenue if teams adopt several seats. The July 2026 coverage from The Neuron and Oflight shows that the demo is legible and differentiated enough to attract attention. It does not show that users return, pay, or tolerate an unfinished product, however. Publicity is not product-market pull.

Dorsa Rohani’s distributed-inference work at Tenstorrent and ML research at NVIDIA, Vector Institute, and Deep Genomics are relevant technical preparation; Shubham Patil’s developer-collaboration interface experience covers the product surface. Neither biography documents prior startup learning, costly commitment, or the desperation signal I look for. More importantly, technical fit cannot override an unsettled market structure when the scarce asset is not frontier research and the product distinction is already narrowing. None of the known-exception mechanisms applies.

My base case is that within 36 months Mosaic pivots from a multiplayer coding environment into secure agent-session, permissions, and audit infrastructure rather than exceeding $3 million ARR in its current form. This is where the red flags point: shared terminals enlarge the security boundary, Anthropic’s credential-exfiltration test succeeded in 24 of 25 attempts, Mosaic publishes no security architecture, and dependence on three upstream CLIs creates platform risk. A stronger model benefits Mosaic only if it increases managed collaboration faster than the model vendors absorb Mosaic’s coordination features.

I pass today. The verifiable flip condition is ten paying teams, each using at least three $200 seats, with 90-day weekly retention and recurring handoffs between multiple developers and agents. That would validate the controlling variable, demonstrate pull before polish, and show that persistent workflow state—not multiplayer novelty—is the product.
