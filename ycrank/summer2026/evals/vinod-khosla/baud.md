# Baud -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

A 50M-parameter model running at 1,000+ tokens per second on one 125 MHz U200 FPGA makes **Baud** a capital-hostile technical bet whose weakest dimension is not engineering but documented founder instigation. **Baud** is attempting to break the economic mechanism beneath AI—multiplication-heavy computation—not merely capture part of the $63.22B accelerator market. The signature is unusual: formidable required miracles, but no public evidence that Sarang Zambare or Eric Taylor has explicitly challenged expert consensus or articulated why the field is wrong.

**Commonwealth Fusion** is the high rung on technical and capital risk because fusion demanded multiple falsifiable miracles before commercial validation. **Baud** shares that mechanism: multiplier-free forward and backward passes, a new model representation, an ASIC, a PyTorch compiler, and distributed training must work together. Consensus capital does not welcome that dependency chain. **Baud** diverges because Commonwealth had research-grade physics and a $115M Series A behind its milestones, while Baud has a 50M-parameter FPGA demonstration and an untaped GlobalFoundries 12nm design. The technical risk is real enough to attract me; the validation is not yet deep enough to deserve the highest rung.

**Glean’s** low-rung founder-defiance pattern was domain-expert continuity: an ex-Google search founder applying established expertise, rescued by consequence and enterprise scale. **Baud** shows the same vulnerability more sharply. Eric Taylor has four tape-outs and roles at NVIDIA, NXP, Arteris, and Enfabrica; Sarang Zambare led ML at Peloton and Caper. Those credentials explain why **Baud** might execute, but experts often extrapolate the past. Changing neural arithmetic is contrarian; the dossier does not show either founder publicly defending that position against documented field consensus. A contrarian product is not automatically a contrarian founder.

The reported 6,144-H100 Nemotron run consuming roughly 4.3 MW identifies the consequence mechanism for **Baud**. Over 18 months, successful multiplier-free training would compound through lower silicon area, reduced power, compressed weights, denser clusters, and cheaper experimentation; cheaper experiments then increase model demand, strengthening **Baud’s** own economic rationale. The prize is larger than a faster chip: it is severing frontier AI progress from NVIDIA-scale power and capital requirements. That approaches the OpenAI consequence shape, but retraining every model in Baud’s representation prevents today’s installed model base from moving automatically. **Baud** could alter AI’s cost curve without yet collapsing the GPU regime.

SuperCraft’s 20K+ users and 300K+ designs provide meaningful but incomplete evidence of learning rate because the same Cerelyze entity moved from an AI design product to **Baud**. The score flips upward if Sarang Zambare can document what SuperCraft falsified, why those users did not justify continuation, and how that learning produced the multiplier-free thesis. The score flips downward if SuperCraft was merely abandoned when chips became fashionable. **Baud** has evidence of a pivot, but not yet evidence of the reasoning quality behind it—the distinction between changing direction and learning.

**Genesis AI** is the single-anchor parallel for required capital: a full-stack technical ambition that cannot be proven with commodity SaaS financing. **Baud** will need tape-outs, packaging, compiler engineering, cluster deployment, and model-scale validation while Cerebras, Groq, Etched, and d-Matrix operate with $450M to $3.9B raised. That makes **Baud** appropriately large for concentrated capital, even though the dossier provides no explicit financing plan. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself” does not mean ignore capital adequacy; Range Fuels demonstrated what happens when the roadmap and the capital plan disagree.

The GlobalFoundries 12nm ASIC scheduled for tape-out by year-end 2026 leads to my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, **Baud** will have production silicon and $8M-$20M in annualized revenue from specialized training, post-training, and inference capacity, but **Baud** will remain a greenfield alternative rather than broadly displacing NVIDIA. The mechanism is straightforward. Multiplier-free arithmetic can produce a genuine cost advantage, while mandatory retraining confines early adoption to customers building new models rather than migrating existing weights.

**Etched’s** working chip, more than $1B in customer contracts, and $1.1B raised form the bear case that should sting: **Baud** is an untaped 12nm design confronting better-capitalized competitors while asking customers to abandon existing weights, and “bit-exact in most cases” is not a production compatibility guarantee. That combination can kill **Baud** before engineering elegance becomes economic consequence. The counterweight is unusually concrete for pre-seed: Baud already integrates the representation, FPGA hardware, compiler, and distributed stack, and its founders combine four prior tape-outs with deployed ML across 100,000+ Peloton devices. I invest because those artifacts make the miracles falsifiable. If first silicon fails to demonstrate at least a 5× end-to-end training-cost improvement on a model above one billion parameters, I flip the verdict to Pass.
