# Most Robotic -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The proposed post-money valuation is unknown, the competing-term-sheet count is unknown, and I cannot establish whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. That earns exactly the financing default, not a presumption that Most Robotic is cheap. More importantly, the dossier never names the card this capital flips: there is no customer, robot embodiment, technical mechanism, dated experiment, or measurable result. “Research lab for physical AI” describes a field, not a venture moment.

The strongest evidence is the team. Two MIT computer scientists collectively report robotics research and experience at NASA JPL, SpaceX, and AWS. Those institutions plausibly put the founders close to both advanced software and difficult physical systems. But the credentials are not individually attributable, and the dossier identifies no shipped robot, deployed model, operating result, or customer workflow. Friends since middle school may have trust; it does not tell me who makes the final product and financing decisions when they disagree.

The bull case is that a tiny, technically credible team can use NVIDIA’s open Cosmos 3 stack to compress training cycles from months to days and discover an application before larger organizations can move. That could become a real outsider-insider translation layer. But open infrastructure also makes an unspecified physical-AI lab easier to reproduce. Genesis AI has raised $105 million and disclosed a model, data engine, and robotic hand; FieldAI has raised $405 million and reports deployments across hundreds of industrial environments; Skild AI has raised $2.215 billion and reportedly produced roughly $30 million of 2025 live revenue. Most Robotic has disclosed neither a narrow wedge nor proprietary access that makes those capital disparities irrelevant.

There is also no dark pool yet. No owned dataset, embedded workflow, pilot, repeat prototype use, signed LOI, or purchase can be traced to the founders’ access. The only public distribution mechanism is founders@mostrobotic.com. This is where the exceptional bull case fails: unusual technical talent can override missing economics, but an exception requires hard evidence such as a working product pulling users or enterprise buyers through the funnel. The dossier supplies none.

Delete the next financing and the business disappears from view. There is no pricing, revenue, gross margin, burn, controllable cash metric, or even a defined buyer from which to derive a profitable path. Physical data collection and real-world testing are expensive, while the named competitors have hundreds of millions or billions of dollars. That combination risks making Most Robotic a capital factory built around general research progress rather than a seed-funded company that can become self-sustaining.

I am passing. My 36-month base case is that Most Robotic either shuts down or pivots from a general physical-AI lab into a narrower robotics-data or evaluation tool, remaining below $1 million ARR. The verifiable flip condition is one paid deployment in a named industrial workflow, accompanied by a proprietary data loop and a seed plan that specifies the performance threshold and deadline the check will test. That single receipt would establish the product, the dark pool, and the venture moment; today all three are missing.
