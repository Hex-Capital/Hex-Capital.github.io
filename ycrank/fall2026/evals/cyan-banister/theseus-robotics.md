# Theseus Robotics -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a supermarket where the same machine picks your grocery order during the day and replenishes shelves after closing. Theseus Robotics names those two jobs. The little detail that catches me is the CTO testing displaced cartons and slipping containers: the future has to survive someone putting the cereal in the wrong place. Reliable manipulation, recovery without human rescue, and affordable servicing are the intervening steps. The reported laboratory tests are an early artifact of that path, though they do not yet establish how much distance remains.

The founders give me more confidence than the humanoid label does. Floch led 120 students across six robotics teams and has learning-control research and an open-source motion-tracking application behind him. Weinhardt-Kovács reports deploying a sorting system achieving 800 picks per hour. Malhotra brings hardware experience and reports growing Parts World USA from under $100,000 to $3 million in revenue over four years. Those are separate pieces of technical, recruiting, and commercial evidence; the prior company's revenue belongs to the founder assessment alone. I see people who have already paid some operating tuition, although the dossier does not establish a long, independently pursued obsession with supermarket automation.

Why does a person still have to move every carton onto a shelf? Theseus identifies physical work that software has largely left untouched, but the CEO's unsupported $70 billion spending estimate does not explain an artificial restriction or who collects a rent. Automating necessary labor could improve a store's economics; whether it returns meaningful control to workers or independent retailers remains unshown. The no-capex service offer makes adoption easier in principle while moving equipment financing and maintenance onto Theseus. Someone still pays for the midnight rescue visit.

The strongest case for investing before customer proof is that this team can build its way through those problems. My pre-revenue **CosmicBrain AI** investment followed that mechanism: physical-AI conviction supported by founder depth, a functioning prototype, and signed industrial proofs of concept. Theseus has relevant founder experience and reported laboratory work, but its advertised Zürich and San Francisco previews establish no completed retailer behavior. Neither 814 LinkedIn followers nor a preview-request form tells me someone has rearranged a store around this robot. That difference keeps the exception from carrying the check.

Theseus also resembles my pattern of finding concealed infrastructure beneath a visible application: store manipulation software could become the valuable layer beneath the robot. Resemblance earns no infrastructure premium by itself. The dossier establishes neither control of a scarce technical asset nor a deployment-data advantage, while Telexistence already has retail deployments and a Physical Intelligence partnership targeting autonomous recovery. I find no documented governance, privacy, or single-supplier red flag, but also no demonstrated reason Theseus owns the dependency others will need. I remain interested, with my money in my pocket. One result would change that: a retailer-verified, paid 30-night restocking trial showing positive contribution margin after hardware allocation, maintenance, and every human intervention. That would connect the founders' capabilities to a business that survives the cereal aisle.
