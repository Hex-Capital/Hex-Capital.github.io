# Fire Wallet -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Stablecoin infrastructure is expanding merchant acceptance; Fire Wallet wants cross-border and ecommerce businesses to buy cheaper, more private payments. The controlling variable is whether Fire can retain a transaction fee while delivering a lower all-in payment cost than competing routes. That includes conversion, settlement, compliance, and support. The dossier identifies least-cost routing as a proposition, but supplies neither a fee schedule nor a completed-payment cost comparison. I can formulate the market test. I cannot yet underwrite its answer.

There is a real adoption change underneath the product. Stripe’s June 2025 Shopify announcement covered merchants across 34 countries, and the cited stablecoin payments research reported a $72B annualized run rate with 88% growth. Those facts support an expanding payment channel. They establish much less about the economics available to a new wallet. Fire excludes spreads and payment for order flow, which narrows its monetization options to disclosed interface fees. Meanwhile, Rain and Fireblocks already supply substantial parts of the infrastructure. Better rails could expand Fire’s opportunity while simultaneously compressing what it can charge.

The strongest case for investing is that Joshua Goldbard has already built in this market through MobileCoin, and the team may possess a customer channel before its public metrics become informative. Trustboop’s named LOI and an enterprise prospect introducing 10 additional companies are more useful than anonymous registrations. But the approximately $1.5M/month prospect volume and $100M/year opportunity remain customer pipeline. Neither measures Fire-processed payments, repeat usage, or willingness to pay its fee. I give the founder meaningful credit for prior startup experience; the claimed acquisition, with buyer and terms undisclosed, does not establish the favorable small-exit pattern. The commercial evidence remains in the low demand band.

Fire’s potential durability comes from becoming embedded in checkout, invoicing, reconciliation, and payment permissions. Those connections could turn the first product into reusable distribution. The dossier does not document that operational loop today. Routing uses 0x, fiat services involve MoonPay, and the deployed custody architecture is unclear across the app, homepage, and terms. Distributed threshold-HSM key management is a technical claim worth examining, but it does not establish ownership of merchant workflow. I retain the missing-evidence default here rather than treating specification discrepancies as proof that the technology fails.

My pattern of backing experienced operators across a technology transition favors taking this team seriously. It does not override the missing market economics. The research-stage and scarce model-building talent exceptions do not apply to the disclosed payment product, and there is no demonstrated margin reconstruction to supply another override. The relevant red flag is commoditization: Fire could build useful payment access while infrastructure providers and merchants capture most of the value. I would pass with my own money today. One verifiable flip condition would be a 90-day paid merchant cohort demonstrating repeat settled payments at a positive contribution margin while delivering a documented all-in cost advantage over each merchant’s previous provider.
