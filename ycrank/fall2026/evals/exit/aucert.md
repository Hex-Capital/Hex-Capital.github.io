# Aucert -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is Aucert’s strongest route. A mobile QA or release tooling vendor could buy its verification agents to add design, accessibility, and app-store policy checks to an existing regression-testing product. Aucert describes an organization-scoped knowledge graph that retains app flows and customer-approved expectations. A buyer could gain a head start if those capabilities improve release decisions across repeated builds and can be transferred with the product. That ownership advantage is an inference. Buyer interest, measured buyer benefit, and transfer rights for the code and customer-specific data remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The seed-stage milestone is repeat paid production use with measured reductions in manual review time and evidence that the agents catch release-blocking defects missed by customers’ existing checks. The public launch and sample scan establish that Aucert has something to test, and the founders’ mobile QA and testing experience makes a focused trial plausible. Aucert’s listings differ on which capabilities are live or in beta. Paid deployments, trial costs, and runway remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

**Illustrative proceeds.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash target within eight years. On a simple pro rata basis before investor taxes, the target requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. At 3% with $5 million of illustrative net debt, the enterprise-value hurdle is about $88.3 million. The memo reports a $500,000 convertible note and a separate $125,000 financing entry, with conversion terms and outstanding debt unverified. Actual dilution, fees, and senior claims remain open. The memo provides no buyer economics or adjacent purchase prices to support a realizable sale range. (Market & Traction)

- **Modest sale in year eight.** At $25 million of enterprise value less $5 million of assumed net debt, the holder receives $600,000 gross, or 1.2×.
- **Strong sale in year eight.** At $100 million of enterprise value on the same terms, the holder receives $2.85 million gross, or 5.7×.
- **Prolonged private operation.** If paid adoption plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash goes back into the product, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. Any wind-down recovery would come from the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo does not quantify.

The other routes lack independent support. Draft subscription terms leave recurring revenue and cash generation unverified for a sponsor buyout or a company-funded payout. The financing reports provide no evidence of demand for eligible existing shares, and the memo provides no operating scale or resale evidence for an IPO. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An incumbent may be able to build or license the incremental checks. QA Wolf already maps mobile workflows and runs and repairs tests, Momentic offers exploratory QA, and Google Play covers some Android accessibility and stability checks. Aucert’s strongest answer is that its combined checks and app-specific expectations could catch important defects those tools miss. The memo provides no production comparison demonstrating that advantage. The documented overlap weakens the case for paying an acquisition premium, but it establishes neither a structural block nor a supported sale price that leaves the holder materially short. The Pass gate stays open. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a mobile QA vendor justify paying roughly $88 million in enterprise value for Aucert after seeing repeat paid production use? A buyer-side build-versus-buy analysis grounded in measured reviewer time saved and release-blocking defects caught beyond existing checks would answer it. A supported price range that clears the holder’s target within eight years under defensible ownership and debt assumptions would move the action to Reach out.
