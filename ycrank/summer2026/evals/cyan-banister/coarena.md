# CoArena -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. Computer agents operate ordinary business software, interfaces change continuously, and a fixed benchmark becomes stale the moment it is published. The useful institution is a living reliability map built from real workflows. CoArena has one credible leak from that future: 14 agents executing identical tasks in the same sandbox, producing paired trajectories, blind votes, and 57 published measures. What is missing is the causal bridge from an entertaining arena to the trusted evaluation standard labs use for deployment and training.

The lower-stack opportunity is real. CoArena can collect the failures, actions, observations, and preferences that application builders need but cannot obtain from static tests. The $60,000 generated within two weeks suggests somebody will pay for that layer. Yet the controlled asset remains immature: the dataset page shows no task-category data or annotation fields, and no structural barrier prevents LMArena, Patronus AI, or Braintrust from moving toward the same workflows. Today this is promising infrastructure, not scarce infrastructure.

The strongest evidence is behavioral. Users doubled weekly during the first two weeks, the hackathon listed 2,550 participants, and customers signed consumption-priced contracts while the arena remained free. That is more informative than a waitlist. But I cannot tell whether the $60,000 came from one experimental buyer or several independent labs, how many real tasks users completed, or whether anyone returned after the novelty wore off. The behavior is costly; its repetition is not yet visible.

Prateek Jannu and Nitish Kovuru paid meaningful technical tuition before this launch. They built Coasty, reported an 82.81% OSWorld result, and brought machine-learning, vision, and enterprise-agent experience into CoArena. Dropping a Stanford admission to build and shipping a repository that earned 142 stars are first-believer acts. I still lack a dated pre-market obsession or recruiting story strong enough for the top founder band, and Coasty’s ownership of both the benchmark and a competing computer-use agent creates an uncomfortable conflict even if that agent is excluded from current matches.

The broken rule is benchmark contamination: fixed tasks enter training data and stop measuring performance on changing workflows. CoArena identifies that failure precisely, but it does not yet identify the rent. Who currently profits from unreliable evaluation? What quantified delay or cost disappears? Whose agency is returned? This matters because the company could become a fast-growing evaluation vendor without becoming the institution that changes how agents are trusted. My 36-month base case is $5 million to $12 million in ARR from evaluation and trajectory licensing, but not category-standard status.

The bull case stings: $60,000 in two weeks, a working multi-agent arena, relevant founders, and thousands of hackathon participants may be exactly what an early data flywheel looks like before the corpus becomes legible. I cannot write the check today because the flywheel ingests raw task prompts and unmasked screenshots, sends them to model providers, and lacks SOC 2, ISO 27001, or an independent audit. That is not ordinary pre-seed incompleteness; it is a consent-and-custody gate inside the product’s core loop. I am Neutral at 64. One verifiable flip condition would change the decision: an independent security audit certifying that raw prompts and screenshots remain inside a user-controlled execution boundary inaccessible to Coasty and external model providers.
