# Quivly AI -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Quivly’s strongest exit path is a strategic sale to a customer-success platform seeking faster implementation for usage-priced software customers. Mycroft uses Quivly to track implementation milestones and billable hours and reports handling 3–4× more accounts per field engineer. Octolane reports onboarding more than 100 customers without a dedicated customer engineering hire. Quivly separately claims it reduced time to go live from 30 to 7 days and managed more than 18,000 downstream accounts. The customer accounts and company claims suggest an onboarding capability a buyer could sell across its installed base. They leave Quivly’s recurring revenue and the buyer’s incremental economics open. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

The operating milestone is to convert a cohort of paid enterprise pilots into retained annual contracts with measured implementation savings. Quivly already offers a 30-day paid pilot and has named deployments, so it can test this through its existing sales process. The memo leaves pilot conversion, runway, and the cost of supporting a larger cohort open. A sale of the software business appears structurally plausible. Customer contract assignability, continued connector access, and data-use permissions would need verification. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route for this holder. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

**Proceeds bridge.** The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The following are illustrative sensitivities. The memo supplies no basis for a realizable sale price. Both sale cases assume an eligible holder sells the full stake, receives cash at closing within eight years, and participates pro rata with no additional preference or senior-claim deductions.

- **Modest sale.** $40 million in enterprise value less $5 million in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million for equity and pays the holder $1.05 million, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value with the same deductions leaves $95 million for equity and pays the holder $2.85 million, or 5.7×. If 20% of that payout slips beyond year eight, cash inside the window falls to $2.28 million, or 4.56×.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or distribution yields zero realized proceeds within eight years.

### Countercase

Incumbents could build enough of the workflow to erase the reason to buy. ChurnZero already runs agents inside its Plays, Vitally can write tasks and notes into its workspace, and Gainsight offers AI-assisted renewals. Those overlaps weaken buyer urgency and make repeatable onboarding savings the key evidence gap. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible sale-price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a plausible platform buyer show that buying Quivly’s onboarding capability creates enough incremental value over building it to support a price above the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Buyer-validated economics tied to repeatable savings and retained paid deployments, with a price range that clears the hurdle after deal adjustments and the payout waterfall, would move the action to Reach out.
