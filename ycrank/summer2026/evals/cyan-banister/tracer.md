# Tracer -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032: software agents continuously decide how much intelligence each task deserves, and most applications no longer call a single giant model by default. Tracer could become the orchestration layer making that world economical. But the dossier does not work backward through the required transitions or show that Echo owns the first leak. The strongest artifact—the prior TRACER router with 1,000 GitHub stars, 70 forks, and an arXiv paper—belongs to a classification-routing project, while Echo is a newer coordinated-model product. OpenRouter already offers routing and fusion across more than 400 models. This is a plausible future attached to an ambiguous starting position, not yet a six-year map.

Adam Rida is the reason I keep looking. Applied mathematics, explainable-AI research, inference work, a published routing method, an open-source community, and approximately €100,000 ARR at DeepRecall in under three months constitute first-believer proof. This is not a founder who discovered inference optimization after reading a market report. He has built both technical and commercial artifacts before Tracer. What remains unproven is Rizz: Tracer is a one-person company, and the dossier shows no evidence that Adam has recruited complementary talent around a system that must span research, infrastructure reliability, benchmarking, and enterprise sales.

The present economics are legible but not yet broken open. Echo charges serving cost plus 25%, with observed input pricing of roughly $0.15–$1.25 and output pricing of $0.75–$12 per million tokens. That is clean pricing. It does not identify who captures an artificial rent today, quantify the waste caused by manual routing, or show whose agency changes after adoption. A buyer can already choose among OpenRouter, Martian, and Not Diamond. Lowering a cloud bill is useful; exposing a hidden institutional rule and returning control is different.

The strongest bull case is the exception I made for pre-revenue **CosmicBrain AI**: a working technical artifact plus unusual founder preparation can matter more than early revenue. The shared mechanism is a founder who built toward the problem before the market became obvious. The material difference is that CosmicBrain had signed industrial proofs of concept and a sharply defined physical bottleneck; Echo has public guest messages and private-alpha access but no disclosed customer, repeated production workload, or workload-specific dataset. The 1,000 stars cannot be quietly reassigned to the current product.

Tracer does fit two of my unstated patterns: an open-source artifact reveals the founder before the pitch, and inference coordination sits beneath the visible application wave. But the adverse pattern is stronger today. This is a crowded AI-infrastructure round whose product overlaps directly with a competitor estimated at approximately $50 million in annualized revenue, while Tracer controls neither the underlying models nor a proprietary workload corpus. The opt-in trace policy is the correct privacy posture, and a solo founder avoids the 50/50 governance trap, but the absence of severe red flags is not a moat.

My 36-month base case is that Tracer pivots from a generic coordinated-model endpoint into enterprise inference benchmarking and workload optimization and remains below $1 million ARR by August 2029. Adam looks capable of finding a technically valuable adjacent product; Echo has not shown why production teams will entrust it with the critical path. I would not write the check today. One condition flips me: three named customers route at least half of their production inference through Echo for 90 days while documenting at least a 30% cost reduction at fixed evaluation quality.
