# Archal -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Aidan Tiruvan’s Scale AI agent-red-teaming history makes Archal a lived-domain technical bet whose weakest organ is founder authority: Archal has two people publicly called “Founder” and nobody called CEO. The founders have touched the failure mode, especially Aidan, and Archal’s stateful GitHub, Slack, Stripe, and Linear clones are more than an AI-evaluation wrapper. But Biz, Tiz, and Rizz needs a tiebreaker. Archal shows me Tiz; Archal has not shown me who owns the final decision when two smart CU Boulder friends disagree.

**Niantic** is the high rung on founder origin because John Hanke spent a decade inside location systems before Pokémon Go existed. Aidan’s agent red-teaming at Scale AI gives Archal the same kind of problem adjacency: he encountered the limitations of synthetic tests before Archal built a clone runtime preserving state, referential integrity, and service error shapes. Archal diverges because Aidan’s résumé establishes employment in the domain, not an obsessive autobiographical trail, while Noah Song’s quantum chemistry and geometric-deep-learning work does not connect directly to agent operations. Archal is *of* the technical neighborhood, but the dossier does not yet prove Archal was born from a scar.

**HQ Trivia** is the low rung on governance because tactile product love temporarily concealed the Yusupov-Kroll authority fracture, and the fracture eventually collected its debt. Archal presents the same structural silhouette: Noah and Aidan are both “Founder,” no public source names a CEO, no operating split is described, and even the origin of their CU Boulder relationship is undocumented. Archal may already have a private tiebreaker, but I invest my own money, not my imagination. For Archal, “multiple founders” is not a hard no; it is a rebuttal that must be answered before the check clears.

**Braintrust’s $125 million and LangChain’s $160 million** make Archal’s weirdness a race against absorption. Over 18 months, Archal’s stateful clones could compound through accumulated production failures, higher-fidelity error behavior, and regression suites that become painful to remove; every reproduced Stripe refund or GitHub permission failure would make Archal more useful to the next agent. The opposite loop is brutal: Braintrust or LangSmith adds service simulation, Archal’s 59 deprecated-CLI downloads fail to seed a proprietary scenario corpus, and Archal becomes a feature wearing a company costume. “The improvement loop for AI agents” is clever, but AI evaluation infrastructure is crowded consensus territory rather than Crusoe-level magical weirdness.

**Archal’s controlled DNS/TLS routing and custom-CA requirements** create real technical difficulty without creating the kind of capital-intensive or regulated moat I normally chase. For Archal to flip this dimension into invest territory, the clones would need to become certified execution infrastructure for regulated financial or healthcare agents, where reproducing permissions, payments, and audit state is a compliance requirement rather than developer convenience. Archal currently publishes telemetry-off defaults and request-isolation controls, but no regulator, certification, or named regulated customer appears in the dossier. Until Archal crosses that boundary, Archal remains software-only infrastructure in the hottest category of the batch.

**Flock Safety** let me see the artifact in a parking lot; Archal lets a developer run `npx archal` and watch an agent alter a behavioral copy of GitHub, Slack, Stripe, or Linear. That gives Archal more physical-world verifiability than a pure model-evaluation API: the before-and-after state, trace, reproduced failure, and fix PR are observable artifacts. Archal still lives behind a terminal, however, and the 3 weekly downloads for `@archal/vitest` show no equivalent of a camera appearing on every street corner. I can use Archal, but I cannot yet encounter Archal accidentally in the world.

**Archal’s 59 weekly downloads on a deprecated CLI** point to a narrow 36-month outcome: by July 2029, Archal will have one remaining founder, will have narrowed from an autonomous “improvement loop” to stateful service-clone testing, and will remain below $3 million ARR. Archal’s domain-adjacent technical depth can produce a durable tool, but Archal’s unclear authority and the gravity of Braintrust and LangSmith make a broad independent platform improbable. The technology survives; the original shape does not.

**Archal’s 30 CLI releases** support the strongest bull case: relentless shipping plus a uniquely faithful clone runtime could make Archal the default pre-production environment just as explosive product pull let HQ Trivia override my governance rule. Archal can reproduce unsafe cross-tool actions, preserve referential integrity, rerun a failed scenario, and open a fix PR—an unusually complete loop for two pre-seed founders. The bull case still loses today because 59 downloads on the deprecated package and 3 Vitest downloads are not HQ-level pull, while no revenue, named customer, or unambiguous CEO offsets the funded competitors. One condition flips my verdict: three named paying customers publicly confirming that Archal runs in their CI every week.
