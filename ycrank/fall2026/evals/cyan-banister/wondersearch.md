# Wondersearch -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine an agent searching a newly changed document as casually as opening a file. Getting there requires retrieval that tolerates constant change, predictable costs under repeated queries, and relevance developers can trust without tending another pipeline. Wondersearch attacks the preparation step. That interests me. But “without embeddings” describes an implementation choice; the dossier does not yet establish which new behavior becomes possible because that choice exists. I can imagine the future more clearly than I can trace Wondersearch’s path into it.

Why must a developer prepare the whole bookshelf before asking about one page? There is a real operating irritation here: document preparation, embedding generation, and retrieval maintenance consume engineering time before the application does anything useful. Removing those jobs returns control to developers. The limitation is that this is an engineering burden with competing remedies, without a demonstrated gatekeeper extracting a quantified rent. Pinecone already offers integrated embeddings. Wondersearch needs to remove a consequential constraint that survives that convenience, and its displayed $0.001/query plus $0.33/GB/month does not establish comparative savings.

The strongest argument for writing a check is underneath the interface. Wondersearch reports its own search model, hosted sparse retrieval, and API, SDK, CLI, and MCP access. That fits my recurring attraction to unglamorous infrastructure beneath a more visible wave: document applications and agents could both depend on it. The company’s October 4 benchmark reports 80.06 nDCG@10 on SciFact for production Large. That is a concrete technical artifact. It is still an internally measured relevance result, without independent replication or evidence that the controlled model resolves a scarce production bottleneck. I give the enabling layer substantial credit, while leaving its indispensability unproven.

Dale and Dalton have done more than discover an attractive category. Their prior work includes Master, Polygres, and the parent company’s pgGraph extension; Dalton brings DSO research and technical presales experience, while Dale previously grew Swig Energy to 5,000 customers. These are useful signs of building and commercial competence. The 1,074 pgGraph stars belong to that earlier work, and the enterprise POC belongs to Polygres. They support the founders’ record; they do not establish demand for Wondersearch. The biography shows repeated initiative, but does not yet document a long, independently pursued obsession with this particular retrieval problem.

The missing piece is somebody reorganizing real work around the product. The September 28 launch attracted 128,943 X views by October 12; that measures attention, not repeated expenditure of money or labor. My pre-revenue exceptions matter here: I can back a technical prototype before customers pay when unusually deep founder obsession and signed commitments establish the bridge to use. Wondersearch supplies some technical proof, but not that complete bridge. Being twins establishes neither equal voting control nor a governance defect; CEO and CTO responsibilities are named, while contractual accountability remains unverified. Likewise, the trust center and unfinished SOC 2 audits do not establish a severe privacy violation. Sensitive document custody deserves examination, without turning an unanswered question into adverse evidence.

I would pass with my own money today. The strongest bull case is credible: experienced builders could make retrieval sufficiently effortless that developers stop treating it as a separate project. What holds me back is the distance between a useful technical improvement and an enabling dependency that compounds as agents become more capable. One verifiable result would change my decision: a customer-run, 90-day production comparison showing three independent teams repeatedly choosing Wondersearch over integrated-embedding alternatives because it makes continuously changing corpora economical to search. That would give me an observable piece of the future to underwrite.
