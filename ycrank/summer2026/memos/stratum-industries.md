## The Idea

- **Problem:** Government adjudicators review permits, licenses, patents, drug approvals, grants, and benefits claims using pre-AI software, leaving millions of applications in queues lasting months or years ([Y Combinator, 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/stratum-industries)).
- **Approach:** A reviewer uploads an application and rubric, after which agents read and evaluate the file, route steps among models, request missing information, and produce a cited decision summary for human approval ([Y Combinator, 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/stratum-industries)).
- **Differentiation:** [Inferred]: Unlike GovWell’s broader local-government operating system, PermitFlow’s applicant-side construction-permitting workflow, and Salesforce Agentforce’s configurable public-sector platform, Stratum is positioning around parallel, rubric-based adjudication across multiple government application categories.
- **Business Model:** [Inferred]: The likely monetization path is agency contracts priced by deployment, workflow, seat, or processed case because the product is integrated into government adjudication operations, but no pricing was publicly displayed.
- **TAM/SAM:** No public TAM/SAM data found for this specific segment.
- **GTM / Distribution:** The founders explicitly solicit introductions to adjudication leaders at federal, state, and local agencies, indicating founder-led direct sales into government organizations ([Y Combinator, 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/stratum-industries)).

## Key Risks

- **Decision accuracy:** Government adjudication can affect benefits, licenses, and permits, while NIST identifies confident false outputs as an inherent generative-AI risk in consequential decision-making; Stratum’s disclosed mitigation is human approval and a cited audit trail ([NIST, 2024](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=958388); [Y Combinator, 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/stratum-industries)).
- **Sensitive-data security:** Applications may contain health, financial, or identity data, and GAO found that federal AI guidance did not fully address ten assessed privacy challenges; no Stratum security certifications or deployment architecture were disclosed ([GAO, Mar 2026](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107681)).
- **Incumbent substitution:** Salesforce’s FedRAMP-High and DoD IL5-authorized Government Cloud already supports public-sector agents, giving agencies an overlapping option within existing Salesforce deployments ([Salesforce, 2026](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=ind.government_cloud_service_cloud.htm&language=en_US&type=5)).
- **Competitive overlap:** ClearOS publicly describes nearly the same reviewer-side intake and adjudication wedge, while GovWell reports more than 130 government customers across 34 states ([ClearOS](https://www.getclearos.com/); [GovWell, May 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/govwell-raises-25m-series-a-led-by-insight-partners-to-build-the-ai-operating-system-for-modern-government-302770627.html)).
- **Name disambiguation:** Search results include unrelated companies named Stratum AI, Stratum Biosciences, and ClearOS the Linux distribution, which can obscure company-specific funding and social data.
