# ProvenMetal -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032: American robotics, defense, and medical-device teams revise hardware continuously, and board procurement behaves like cloud infrastructure—visible inside the design tool, domestically routable, and measured rather than chased through email. ProvenMetal has two leaks from that world: a KiCad plugin that checks live inventory in roughly one minute and policy pressure toward domestic electronics capacity. But the dossier does not map the intervening steps from managed orders to that future. It names no 2032 behavior, capacity buildout, or mechanism that turns today’s service into infrastructure.

The founders are the strongest part of this story. Johnny Doyle and Will Carkner built Syncra together before ProvenMetal; its 17-day office pilot collected more than 20 million datapoints and reportedly reduced phantom energy use by 95%. Carkner also built PCB-testing systems at Lumindt. That is real operating tuition: they have designed boards, deployed hardware, measured physical systems, and stayed together through a pivot. It is not yet lifelong manufacturing obsession or proof of commercial command, but it is substantially better than two people discovering “reshoring” from a market report.

The broken system is recognizable but incompletely decoded. Hardware teams wait weeks for overseas boards and surrender control over quality and scheduling. ProvenMetal returns some agency through fixed ship dates, managed procurement, inspection records, and an EDA-native ordering path. Yet I cannot see who captures the economic rent, how much a delayed revision costs, or which specific institutional restriction creates the delay. Even “American made” bends under inspection: assembly is domestic, while fabrication may remain overseas unless the customer selects a fully domestic route.

The working artifact is the KiCad v0.2.0 plugin, but the dossier shows availability rather than behavior. There are no installation counts, paid orders, repeat cohorts, or engineers rearranging their workflow around it. Five LOIs after more than 30 conversations are useful, except June reporting described an AI-enabled X-ray product while August materials describe turnkey PCBA through Network PCB; those LOIs cannot be confidently assigned to the current offering. The closest pre-revenue exception is **CosmicBrain AI**, where a functioning prototype and signed industrial proofs of concept combined with a founder’s long robotics record. ProvenMetal has pieces of that mechanism, but not evidence that customers have tested this particular service configuration.

The sharpest bull case is that fabrication itself need not be the moat. The founders could make factories interchangeable while owning the engineer relationship, revision history, sourcing data, inspection record, and one-hour quote. Five-day delivery may then be less important than removing five days of emails and uncertainty. But today Network PCB performs the assembly, sells overlapping services directly, and advertises a faster three-day turn; MacroFab already routes work across more than 100 North American factories. ProvenMetal sits above the scarce production layer rather than controlling it. My 36-month base case is that it pivots toward multi-fab PCB procurement and quality-management software and remains below $2 million ARR by August 2029.

I do not see a governance shell game—Doyle is identified as CEO and Carkner as CTO—and there is no evident privacy gate. I still would not write the check today. This is a capable hardware team with an actual tool and a real operating wound, but the strangely compelling user behavior and controlled bottleneck are both missing. One verifiable condition flips me to Invest: ten customers placing paid second orders through the KiCad workflow, with those repeat orders fulfilled across at least two independent fabrication partners.
