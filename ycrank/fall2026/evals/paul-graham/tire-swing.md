# Tire Swing -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Tire Swing has changed the problem it works on. I want to know what made the founders change it. In February 2026, [Fondo described healthcare regulatory compliance](https://fondo.com/blog/tire-swing-launches); by October, the product addressed affordable housing. Perhaps a customer showed them a much more urgent problem. Perhaps healthcare was difficult to sell into. Those imply different things about the founders. The dossier establishes the change, but gives neither the observation behind it nor an obstacle they overcame. I cannot yet distinguish resourceful adaptation from another attempt.

Witten’s memory-forensics work and Irvine’s engineering at Instacart and Capital One make the product technically plausible. Tire Swing describes document classification, income extraction, program rules, and source-linked findings, backed by a company-reported library of thousands of checks. That is more concrete than “AI for compliance.” But the [product descriptions](https://www.gettireswing.com/platform) establish what the system claims to do, without showing how accurately it handles difficult files. My preference for founders who assemble a difficult technical substrate applies here provisionally. The credentials support their ability to build; they do not establish what happened when building became hard.

The strongest case for investing is that Tire Swing has chosen work other programmers would find tedious. Affordable-housing certification involves irregular documents, changing rules, and staff who must defend their decisions. The January 1, 2027 enforcement date in [HUD’s HOTMA notice](https://www.hud.gov/sites/default/files/hudclips/documents/PIH-2026-15.pdf) supplies urgency for covered housing authorities. Founder demonstrations using a prospect’s anonymized file are a sensible way to discover the actual problem. Witten’s AHF Live attendance also shows willingness to meet operators personally. But neither establishes that an operator returned with the next file. The neglected work is visible; the founders’ own prior collision with it and the narrow well of returning users remain unestablished.

The decisive question is whether Tire Swing makes a reviewer sufficiently more effective that the reviewer insists on keeping it. Its [terms require qualified human approval](https://www.gettireswing.com/terms_of_service), so producing plausible findings is only part of the job. Findings must be easier to verify than doing the review from scratch. Yardi already supplies direct income and asset verification, making that remaining advantage consequential. Subscription terms show a way to charge, while the reported $500,000 financing says nothing about willingness to pay. I find no evidence of the red flags around waiting for a major partner, premature sales hiring, or passive founders. I also find no demonstrated user pull or independently validated technical result strong enough to justify an exception to my founder-evidence gate.

I would pass on Tire Swing today. This is a plausible product built by technically credible people, but my check depends on evidence about how those people behave under resistance. One verifiable episode could change my decision: a named housing operator encounters a blocking problem on a live certification, the founders ship a workaround within two weeks, and that operator subsequently uses it for another certification. That would connect founder action, a functioning artifact, and returning demand. The current dossier leaves those connections open.
