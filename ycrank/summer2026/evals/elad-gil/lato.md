# LATO -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: autonomous voice agents let investment firms replace weeks-long, $300,000 commercial studies with continuous primary research, but LATO becomes large only if its interviews produce compliance-safe decisions that customers repeatedly trust—not merely cheaper transcripts. The dossier identifies this causal variable, yet supplies no accuracy threshold, forecast history, confidence interval, study-cycle reduction, or customer economics. The controlling belief is therefore plausible but unvalidated.

Demand is the strongest evidence. Blume Equity and FoodLabs are named customers, research represents 70% of reported use cases, and LATO completed a 30-recruiter study in July 2026. For a two-person pre-seed company, that is real product contact rather than a waitlist. The limitation is that no revenue, retention, repeat-study volume, expansion, or distinction between users of the earlier Excel assistant and the current research product is disclosed. Customer pull exists, but its intensity remains unclear.

The technology change is real but already competitive. Autonomous interviewers can expand expert coverage across languages and reduce the marginal cost of collecting primary evidence. LATO then combines interviews, public documents, and fund-owned data into a broader diligence workflow. But AlphaSense launched autonomous AI interviews in August 2025 and now pairs them with more than 280,000 transcripts and over $600 million of ARR. LATO has not quantified a cost or speed advantage against that incumbent, so the discontinuity expands the category without yet showing why this company captures the expansion.

The simulation layer is the central weakness. No benchmark demonstrates that 30 interviews predict market behavior, and no disclosed integrations, evaluation loop, reusable corpus, or proprietary workflow state compounds across studies. A stronger voice model benefits interview execution, but it also makes the feature easier for AlphaSense, Arbolus, Rogo, or Hebbia to reproduce. The claim that accumulated interviews become a moat is especially weak without corpus size, customer permissions, portability constraints, or an estimate of reproduction cost. Expert-call compliance compounds this problem: SOC 2 and ISO 27001 are still in progress, while no MNPI screening or post-call review process is described.

The founders fit the workflow well. Staniszewski spent six years at Verdane and understands the buyer; Chu brings eight years of AI and software experience plus published deep-learning research; their 11-year relationship reduces team-formation risk. That is strong domain coverage, but it is not evidence of prior startup learning, costly personal commitment, or the scarce frontier-model capability required for a talent override. The more persuasive bull case is that domain-native founders, two named customers, and an end-to-end interview workflow can compound faster than the incumbent reacts. It stings, but the current evidence still points toward an AI-enabled diligence product rather than a defensible simulation platform.

My 36-month base case is that LATO reaches roughly $2 million to $6 million in ARR by settling into a services-assisted private-equity research workflow, while the market-simulation claim remains secondary or is abandoned. I pass today because the primary market gate and technology-economics gate remain below threshold. One verifiable result would flip me: three investment firms each renewing at least $250,000 annually after using LATO on ten or more studies, with independently auditable evidence that its forecasts materially outperform conventional commercial diligence.
