# Telamont -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Avoiding $1.4 million of construction rework would matter. Recovering $2–3 million on subcontract packages would matter to the contractor, but recovery alone does not establish that construction became cheaper. Telamont reports both outcomes for unnamed clients; neither comes with project denominators or an independently established counterfactual. I see a potentially valuable intervention in an expensive physical workflow. I cannot yet calculate an orders-of-magnitude consequence. The $1.83 billion construction risk-software market estimates software spending, while the investment question is how much wasted construction expenditure this product can eliminate.

The interesting technical mechanism is continuous reconciliation: catch a changed drawing’s conflict with a specification or contractual obligation before somebody builds the mistake. Telamont’s advertised revision monitoring addresses a specific limitation in Document Crunch’s documented text-only, query-driven drawing analysis. That earns some credit. It does not establish a 5–10x discontinuity. There is no comparative test of detection accuracy, missed critical conflicts, or engineer-hours consumed. Meanwhile, Document Crunch already offers project-level risk workflows, and Trunk Tools offers proactive issue detection. The red flag is convergence toward interchangeable document agents. External model usage does not establish that Telamont is merely a wrapper, but construction playbooks and expert feedback have not yet demonstrated a proprietary learning advantage.

The strongest argument against passing is the combination of relevant builders and apparent customer access. Goulet brings construction exposure and engineering experience at 1Password; Gagnon brings industrial AI experience from Poka. Recruiting Poka co-founder Antoine Bisson as an advisor adds relevant expertise. Customer logos including Canmec and TCI+, alongside reported coverage of billions of dollars in projects, give this team opportunities to discover consequential failures in real documents. My broader investing pattern includes execution businesses that become infrastructure; scientific novelty is not compulsory. But the exceptional cases require a documented instigator attacking a quantified giant value pool, or evidence of an extraordinary capability trajectory. Telamont has credible domain proximity, without documented belief revision, difficult operating recruits, or evidence sufficient to activate that override. Its 220+ updates show activity; they do not establish learning quality.

The next dollar should resolve whether continuous monitoring catches expensive conflicts reliably enough to reduce total human work. Telamont acknowledges inaccuracies and requires human approval. Citations and adversarial testing are sensible mitigations, but the dossier provides no decisive experiment with a threshold, budget, or stop condition. That receives the missing-evidence default. Likewise, private subscription terms reveal nothing about inference expense, onboarding, expert checking, or customer review time. Human approval can coexist with excellent economics. It can also consume the entire saving. There is no disclosed cost model that settles which applies, so economics receives its default too. Public sales recruitment alone does not prove premature scaling.

I pass at 32/100. Telamont could build a useful commercial business, but my own money needs evidence that success changes the underlying cost curve substantially. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: an independently audited, prospective comparison across three customer projects showing at least a 10x reduction in total review cost against the existing workflow, including software and human checking, while matching or exceeding expert detection of critical conflicts. That would turn the central technical and economic claim into something I could underwrite.
