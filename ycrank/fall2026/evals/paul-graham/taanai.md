# Taan.ai -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Taan.ai has shipped a product, but the dossier leaves my first question unanswered: what happened when the founders got stuck? The June 11 beta establishes that they built something. It does not establish a named obstacle, the workaround they invented, or how quickly it changed the situation. Nor does the earlier Aalap venture establish an evidence-driven change of route. These are missing observations, not evidence of passive founders. But I cannot substitute music credentials and signal-processing experience for the behavior I would be betting on.

The strongest evidence is the product’s attempt to solve a precise problem: making a musical event happen when a scene changes. Taan reports **96.1% timing-instruction success**, against **28.9% for ElevenLabs v2**, and exposes its product through an API and MCP integration. That is substantially more interesting than another promise to generate music. But the measurement comes from the company, with independent human annotation still in progress. Its reported audio-distance score is also worse: **4.8 against competitors’ 2.6–3.5**, where lower is better. The technical question becomes a user question: does accurate timing save enough editing work that someone accepts the fidelity tradeoff? ([Benchmark](https://taan.ai/research/control-benchmarks/); [TimingBench](https://taan.ai/research/rushing-or-dragging/))

That is the strongest case for investing. A musician and a signal-processing researcher have built a controllable tool around a concrete limitation in competing products. Taan.ai fits my pattern of backing programmable interfaces, and its benchmark could be the beginning of an unusually concrete proof spike. But an available interface is not yet a primitive developers depend on. The dossier identifies no returning editor, paying agency, or application repeatedly calling the API. The **$15 Creator and $45 Agency plans** establish an offer; the founder-reported **$1 million raised** establishes financing. Neither answers who wants this badly enough to tolerate its rough edges. Aalap’s reported $1,000 MRR belongs to the previous venture. ([Product and pricing](https://taan.ai/); [Founder background](https://ad1thya.com/))

The founders’ backgrounds make this worth investigating, but they do not document the personal collision that produced the idea or a neglected operational burden they have already undertaken. Meanwhile, ElevenLabs already offers video-to-music generation. Taan needs to learn which editors care disproportionately about control before competitors improve theirs. Its four-minute duration ceiling and MCP exports that omit original audio are concrete places where that learning might happen. I would not infer rigidity from those limitations, or infer that an unknown cofounder history means an artificially assembled team. The dossier establishes neither red flag. ([ElevenLabs endpoint](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/music/video-to-music); [Taan MCP documentation](https://taan.ai/docs/mcp/))

I would pass today. I considered the working-prototype exception seriously: Taan has an artifact, and the claimed timing advantage is large. But the unvalidated benchmark and absent user behavior do not supply enough evidence to override the missing resourcefulness test. My flip condition is one documented pilot: the founders fix a named editing blocker within two weeks, after which five agencies pay and return weekly for a month. That would connect founder action, product improvement, and real need—the causal chain currently missing.
