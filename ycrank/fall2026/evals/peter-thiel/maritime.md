# Maritime -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Maritime has built an answer to a real infrastructure problem. What important truth makes that answer a company? The strongest candidate is that persistent, per-user computers will become the basic unit of agent infrastructure, displacing disposable sandboxes. That is my reconstruction of the opportunity; the dossier does not establish a disputed proposition the founders have tested. If developers can preserve agent state outside interchangeable sandboxes with comparable reliability and less expense, the proposed secret fails. Hosting hundreds of research agents at MIT establishes firsthand experience with the problem, but does not settle that question.

The founding relationship is the strongest evidence here. Maria Gorskikh and Aryaman Shrivastava both worked on NANDA agent infrastructure and encountered the hosting burden before building Maritime. This gives their collaboration more substance than a team assembled around a fashionable fundraising category. Their prior work supports some credit for alignment. Full-time status, ownership, compensation, and the duration of their working relationship remain unestablished; I would neither assume exemplary founding economics nor invent a red flag.

The decisive difficulty is market ownership. Persistent storage, encrypted secrets, and snapshot-based sleep/wake make a usable product, but the dossier demonstrates no proprietary technology or measured advantage that prevents substitution. E2B supplies sandbox execution; Daytona offers snapshot and fork workflows; Docker entered managed cloud sandboxes on September 24, 2026. Maritime’s initial customers—startups deploying agents for their own users—are identifiable, but already contested. Customer files and integrations might create migration work. An inferred inconvenience is insufficient evidence of a monopoly mechanism. This is affirmative competitive pressure, rather than merely missing moat information, and it fails a primary investment gate.

The strongest objection to passing is that these alternatives may be organized around the wrong abstraction. Maritime sells an enduring computer for an agent, with sleep and wake managed automatically. A startup could prefer that complete operating model even when individual infrastructure features are available elsewhere. The founders have shipped it, and Gorskikh’s Open Instinct project has attracted 416 GitHub stars. Maritime also fits my observed interest in the infrastructure beneath AI applications. But founder-operated software demonstrates feasibility; its stars do not establish independent production dependence. Neither the technical-breakthrough exception nor the exception for persistent customer transactions is supported strongly enough to override the competitive gate.

There is one coherent revenue engine, with actual prices: $100 monthly for 100 agents and $5,000 for 5,000 on Dedicated. That is better evidence than a collection of hypothetical businesses. Yet $100 million in annual subscription revenue would require approximately 1,667 customers continuously buying the $5,000 plan, before considering discounts or add-ons. The dossier supplies no compute-margin evidence supporting that scale. Separately, self-service deployment, two Product Hunt launches, developer events, and startup credits constitute a concrete acquisition route suited to small initial contracts. The 253 Product Hunt followers establish some visibility, not conversion or repeatable acquisition economics.

I pass on Maritime today. The objection is the absence of a demonstrated way to retain value in a market already supplying overlapping infrastructure, rather than its pre-seed revenue history. No documented founder, regulatory, or technical-integrity red flag drives this judgment. My single flip condition is an independently reproduced production benchmark showing Maritime runs equivalent persistent-agent workloads at one-tenth the fully loaded cost of the best competing implementation. That would turn its packaging distinction into evidence of proprietary advantage and justify reopening the investment.
