# Proto -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Proto’s post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and proposed use of proceeds are all undisclosed. I cannot establish either that my check buys an unpopular belief cheaply or that it finances a specific experiment. YC affiliation establishes neither an auction nor capital scarcity. The product question is concrete: can automatically handling error, loading, and empty states make a prototype workspace valuable enough for designers to move their daily work? But the dossier supplies no funded milestone, deadline, or pass/fail threshold. An interesting product hypothesis does not yet tell me what my money proves.

Albert is the strongest reason to take that question seriously. Co-creating Stamper, an artboard-oriented programming environment, and launching Browserbase’s Director are shipped receipts across visual creation and developer tooling. That fits my preference for founders who can translate between ecosystems. Proto’s state-restoring comments and decomposition of prototypes into pull requests address the actual boundary between design intent and engineering execution. The strongest bull case is that Albert understands this boundary well enough to build a working environment that neither a canvas company nor a coding editor naturally gets right.

The economic question is who owns that environment once the capabilities become common. Figma opened its canvas to agents in March 2026; Magic Patterns introduced overlapping design-context and coding-agent handoff capabilities in October. Proto could accumulate valuable state histories and design decisions, but the dossier labels that switching-cost argument as inferred. No observed team behavior establishes it. Alpha access through founder DMs creates an opportunity to build proprietary relationships; approximately 2,000 founder followers and 65 company followers do not establish that those relationships cause purchases or retained usage. My preference for owned access is therefore unproven here.

The bull case could still win: automatic interface states might remove enough recurring work that teams adopt Proto before incumbents reproduce the complete experience. But engineers still complete the remaining connections after prototype pull requests, so code export alone does not prove the workflow saves money. There is also no published pricing, revenue, margin, or burn information with which to delete the next financing from the model. Those are unknowns, not evidence of bad economics. My exceptions to price and AI skepticism require demonstrated conversion, retention, cash economics, or unusually hard external validation; none is documented for Proto. Nor does an unidentified second team member establish an equal-founder governance problem. The relevant red flag is competitive convergence without demonstrated ownership of the customer workflow.

I pass at **33/100**. The founder’s shipped work earns substantial credit; it cannot also stand in for favorable terms, customer conversion, and a capital plan. One verifiable condition would flip my decision: a documented sub-$10 million post-money round budgeted to fund a single 90-day experiment, with a precommitted success threshold of ten independent teams paying for Proto and repeatedly using its state-to-pull-request workflow. That would establish a cheap entry and a finite venture moment. It is the experiment I would propose, not a plan the dossier says Proto already has.
