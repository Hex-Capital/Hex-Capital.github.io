# Touchmark -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Roman Yanushevskyi’s IOI gold medal, Citadel Securities quantitative research, and Lovable AI work make **Touchmark** intellectually credible, but credibility is not consequence. **Touchmark** is a potentially consequential market-design idea trapped inside a commodity-software capital profile: its strongest dimension is the possibility of replacing token pricing with quality-adjusted pricing, while its weakest is a roadmap that appears to require neither my capital nor capital-hostile invention. The economic mechanism that could break is payment for computation divorced from value. Today, however, Touchmark looks more like a billing feature than a new economic system.

**OpenAI** (2019) is the high rung because changing the cost and supply of intelligence redirects civilization. **Touchmark** shares a narrower version of that mechanism: pricing outputs by evaluated quality could change what AI vendors optimize, how customers allocate budgets, and which models survive. That analogy matters. Incentives determine products. But **Touchmark** has named no labor market, incumbent regime, or tens-of-billions consequence variable; Metronome, Orb, and Valmi can already process usage or outcome events. OpenAI created a new capability. Touchmark may merely change the invoice attached to one.

**DoorDash** (2013) exposed the low-rung capital mechanism: a capital-light business can still become enormous, but only when distribution and ambition compensate for the absence of technical capital risk. **Touchmark** currently has neither a disclosed capital-intensive roadmap nor DoorDash-like evidence of a commerce network compounding. A two-person software startup with a “Coming soon” website and no published customers makes my check structurally unnecessary. The DoorDash exception does not rescue **Touchmark** yet because no proprietary distribution loop, transaction network, or customer behavior dataset has been demonstrated.

Metronome’s **$128M** in funding defines the next 18 months of technical risk for **Touchmark**. If Touchmark deploys its SDK across many vendors, every scored output, billing adjustment, and customer dispute could form a calibration dataset that improves pricing accuracy and makes adoption compound. If deployments do not arrive quickly, Metronome, Orb, Stripe, or Valmi can accept a quality score as another metered event and erase Touchmark’s distinction. **Touchmark** has published no evaluation methodology, accuracy result, dispute process, or dataset size, so the hard problem is real but the proprietary solution is not.

Ilia Bolgov’s product work at Revolut and Roman Yanushevskyi’s Citadel and Lovable internships show expert continuity, not expert defiance. **Touchmark** would move up a full tier if Bolgov or Yanushevskyi demonstrated that automated quality evaluation can settle prices more accurately than negotiated outcomes—and published evidence contradicting billing-industry orthodoxy. **Touchmark** has instead entered a visible 2025–2026 movement toward outcome pricing, including Zendesk’s verified-resolution charges. Experts extrapolate the past. So far, Touchmark is extrapolating the present.

**Slash** (2024) earned an exception because its documented Kanye-driven pivot revealed learning rate under pressure. **Touchmark** has no documented pivot, abandoned thesis, prior exit, or “we were wrong” moment from either Bolgov or Yanushevskyi. I will not confuse intelligence with learning rate; the UC Berkeley study involved 28,000 expert predictions and found credentials only slightly better than chance. **Touchmark** receives the absent-evidence default here because Summer 2026 is early, but early-stage silence is not evidence of the Slash mechanism.

Touchmark’s “Coming soon” website and the ease with which Orb can meter another event drive a falsifiable forecast: within 36 months, **Touchmark** will pivot from standalone quality-adjusted billing into AI evaluation or observability software and remain below **$3M ARR**. Quality evaluation is a real problem, but billing ownership sits with incumbents and customer-perceived value is not reducible to output quality when customer behavior and external events determine outcomes. **Touchmark** will discover that measuring quality is sellable more often than adjudicating price.

Roman Yanushevskyi’s IOI gold medal is the strongest objection to that forecast. The bull case is that **Touchmark** has unusually quantitative founders, Bolgov understands financial products from Revolut, and the first neutral settlement layer connecting output quality to price could accumulate a dataset competitors cannot reconstruct. Glean shows that an expert-continuity founder can still win when information asymmetry becomes consequential; DoorDash shows that capital-light infrastructure can outrun my science bias. The objection stings because **Touchmark** may be designing the economic protocol for an AI market, not a billing feature. The dossier still shows no customers, documentation, evaluation performance, or moat. I would flip to Invest if Touchmark disclosed three named AI vendors processing at least **$100M in annualized billing volume** through independently validated quality scores with a dispute rate below **1%**.
