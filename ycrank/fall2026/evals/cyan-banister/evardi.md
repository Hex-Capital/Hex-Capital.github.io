# Evardi -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. A neighborhood adds heat pumps, batteries and electric cars, and its utility can see which transformer needs attention before somebody’s lights go out. Evardi offers a concrete beginning: reconstruct the distribution network from existing records and estimate loads where physical meters are missing. The next transitions would be trustworthy estimates, then recommendations engineers routinely act on. Those transitions are my hypothesis. The dossier establishes a technical approach, but its published simulations do not yet establish that journey. Still, a virtual meter is a more interesting artifact from that future than another chatbot answering questions about electricity.

Why is the utility blind in the first place? Evardi identifies a specific institutional failure: SCADA visibility stops short of the transformers, while the company attributes 94% of outage minutes to distribution infrastructure. Recovering visibility could give engineers more control over inspections and maintenance, and customers fewer interruptions. But nobody has yet shown me the economics of that transfer. The dossier does not quantify inspection costs, avoided outages or capacity recovered. This earns credit for finding the broken mechanism; it does not establish an artificial gatekeeper or a rent Evardi can remove.

Diva Shah has actually been inside the machinery she wants to change: Kenya Power grid operations, Autodesk utility integrations and Deloitte energy consulting. Aarya Borele brings an earlier offshore-wind venture and energy research. Their relationship dates to freshman year, and Berkeley documented the mini-grid demand-prediction effort in January 2026. That gives the current product a history beyond an accelerator launch. I see relevant operating tuition and repeated interest in energy. I have less evidence of independently built technical artifacts, exceptional recruiting or the commercial command needed to turn a utility experiment into a durable business. Shah’s reported earlier acquisitions remain self-reported.

The strongest argument for investing is beneath the word “agents.” Evardi describes utility-specific power-flow models checked against feeder-head SCADA, with recommendations reviewed before operator approval. That fits my attraction to concealed infrastructure and unglamorous workflow repair: a trustworthy network model could support inspection, maintenance and switching decisions. But the enabling asset must become dependable and difficult to replace. Exclusive access or technical scarcity is unestablished, and Neara and Bidgely/Grid4C already occupy adjacent territory. Evardi could build something useful while leaving much of the bargaining power with utilities and established vendors.

Here is where my enthusiasm catches: what have operators actually rearranged their working lives to do? The claimed footprint covers utilities serving 10M+ customers and operating 180,000+ line miles; those are the utilities’ scale, not evidence of repeated Evardi usage. The reported 61% inspection hit rate versus 6% from billing flags, and 42% fewer unsafe storm-drill switching actions, come from simulations on public data. The deployment claim merits some credit, but I cannot turn simulated outcomes into customer behavior. My exception for pre-revenue physical technology requires a working artifact and concrete industrial commitment; missing revenue is acceptable, while that commitment remains unestablished here.

I am **Neutral** on writing my own check. Evardi has enough domain grounding and a plausible enabling layer to deserve a serious product session. Its disclosed approval checks, isolated workspaces and access controls also provide useful architectural answers; an unfinished SOC 2 audit does not itself establish a severe privacy problem. Shah is identified as CEO, and a historical co-founder’s uncertain current role does not establish a leadership shell game. The decisive gap is operational belief. My flip condition is one named utility confirming that engineers used Evardi weekly for 90 days and documenting a field-verified inspection or switching improvement attributable to its recommendations. That would show this future has started changing somebody’s behavior.
