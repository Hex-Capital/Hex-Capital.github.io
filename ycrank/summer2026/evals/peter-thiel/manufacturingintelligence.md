# HERA -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

More than 200 customer-discovery conversations reveal HERA’s analytical signature: HERA is a regulated-sector schlep bet whose customer environment is more defensible than its product thesis. HERA’s strongest dimension is proximity to aerospace, medical-device, and pressure-vessel regulation; its weakest is the absence of a contrarian secret naming why a specific incumbent cannot respond. Four overlapping products—F4, Axial, NexCAD, and RapidDraft—suggest mimetic convergence, not a truth the market rejects. The atoms are nearby. The monopoly is not.

Palantir is the high rung because regulated customers, accumulated institutional rules, and integration depth compound into pricing power. HERA shares part of that mechanism: HERA checks ASME Y14.5-2018, BPVC, B31, AWS, ISO, and plant-specific requirements while preserving a source-traced evidence chain. The analogy breaks where it matters. HERA has no government contract, security certification, proprietary corpus, or disclosed retention, and HERA promises deployment within one week rather than demonstrating years of embedded workflow. Palantir owned the institutional relationship; HERA currently borrows the customer’s regulation.

Asana is the low rung on the secret dimension: consensus workflow software without a named incumbent failure mode, rescued only by Dustin Moskovitz’s Facebook pedigree. HERA exhibits the same missing secret without the exception. Manual senior-engineer review is expensive, but HERA does not explain why established CAD vendors or NexCAD—already claiming 1,000-plus engineers and 2,500-plus reviewed drawings—cannot absorb source-traced standards checks. F4, Axial, and RapidDraft are presented as validation through feature comparison. Competition is for losers; HERA has described the competition more clearly than the secret.

NexCAD’s 2,500-plus reviewed drawings expose how HERA’s monopoly mechanism could fail over the next 18 months. Customer-specific rules and accepted findings could become switching costs if HERA becomes the authoritative memory of engineering judgment. Yet HERA’s free 20-drawing review, one-week deployment, unpublished accuracy, and lack of security certification imply shallow integration today, while RapidDraft already retains decisions across revisions. Without exclusive data rights or workflow control, HERA’s evidence chain becomes a feature and HERA’s pricing converges toward commodity review software.

A one-week deployment is an execution promise, not a definite plan. HERA names neither a two-year technical roadmap nor accuracy, certification, and integration milestones that HERA will refuse to pivot away from. The score would move into invest territory if HERA committed to a narrow five-year destination—for example, becoming the certified release gate for ASME pressure-vessel drawings—with dated benchmarks for recall, false-positive rate, on-premises deployment, and CAD/PDM integration. Until then, “review 20 drawings and learn” is indefinite optimism wearing industrial clothing.

Affirm is the relevant founder-lineage anchor because Max Levchin had already shipped PayPal to scale before the re-up. Meera Patel and Noelle So possess authentic factory proximity—UW industrial engineering, TE Connectivity and Carmelray experience, healthcare ML research, and So’s 40 L/s water-treatment facility—but HERA has no comparable shipped-to-scale operator signal. HERA’s 200-plus interviews demonstrate diligence; they do not demonstrate the unusual technical or biographical identity that overrides a missing monopoly thesis.

ASME Y14.5-2018 and BPVC specialization lead to a falsifiable forecast: within 36 months, HERA will have narrowed from general drawing review to ASME pressure-vessel compliance and will remain below $2 million ARR. HERA can build a useful business because regulated manufacturers pay to avoid a $40,000 scrap event, but HERA’s weak secret and four-way product overlap will force vertical specialization before broad system-of-record economics emerge. HERA is more likely to own a profitable niche than a power-law category.

Meera Patel’s manufacturing projects and Noelle So’s industrial-park experience support the strongest bull case: HERA’s 200-plus interviews may have uncovered plant-specific knowledge that public materials deliberately omit, and the free under-NDA review may be a data-acquisition wedge into regulated factories. The Asana exception shows that a missing secret need not be fatal—but HERA lacks Asana’s operator-pedigree override, while NexCAD already reports substantially more usage and RapidDraft already retains review decisions. A paid, multiyear Boeing division-wide contract making HERA the drawing-release system of record would flip my verdict; absent that institutional lock-in, HERA remains useful software without monopoly shape.
