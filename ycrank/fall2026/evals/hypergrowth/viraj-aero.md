# Viraj Aero -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Repeatable direct sales** is the strongest path. Data center developers facing grid delays and cooling-water constraints could buy GENA for one project, then use the same modular turbine and installation process at additional sites. The memo documents developers procuring on-site gas generation to bridge grid delays, which supports urgency for on-site power. Viraj Aero reports more than 1.3 GW of interest under letters of intent and says its first power-and-water turbine is under construction. The reported payment from an undisclosed engine manufacturer has an undisclosed scope, so it cannot establish that a data center buyer has paid for GENA. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The buyer and operating need plausibly carry across markets where sites can secure gas supply and air permits. The first link likely to break is the handoff from founder-led sales to a paid, operating installation that a delivery team can repeat. The memo gives no usable record of that handoff, repeat purchases at the buyer’s project cadence, or the labor and capital required to deliver the next unit. The letters of intent leave the first segment’s budget and workflow commitment unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Grid delays could create one-off bridge projects, and developers with adequate cooling water could choose on-site generation without water recovery. Even where both needs are urgent, site-specific engineering, permitting, and capital could rise with each installation. The memo identifies those constraints and leaves continuous-duty power, emissions, and water yield unverified at a data center. The structural downside gate remains open because the memo contains no deployment evidence showing that delivery costs scale proportionally with no credible way to improve. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
