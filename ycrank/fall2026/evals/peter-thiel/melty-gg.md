# Melty -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Melty’s potentially interesting secret is that players want games to become interchangeable components, and that installation friction has concealed this demand. That is my reconstruction of the opportunity; the dossier establishes neither a prevailing belief that rejects it nor an experiment that tests it. The Windows launcher is shipped, which makes the proposition testable: do players repeatedly return to mashups after the novelty disappears? Automating downloads and dependencies demonstrates a product. It does not yet demonstrate the contested truth that would justify forming this particular company.

The strongest attempt at value capture appears in Melty’s October 18 terms: creators grant exclusive marketplace distribution while listed and for twelve months after removal. This is a concrete contractual mechanism, deserving limited credit. But exclusive supply becomes valuable only when it is supply players want. The dossier establishes neither a sought-after exclusive catalogue nor a technical advantage that makes creators dependent on Melty. Nexus Vortex already manages mods and collections; Overwolf already distributes and monetizes creator content. Cross-game mashups distinguish the initial product, but the evidence does not establish an enduring position. Publisher restrictions also limit what creator exclusivity can secure: a creator’s agreement cannot supply rights the creator does not possess.

The strongest objection to passing is that Melty has assembled the beginnings of a distribution loop. Creators publish experiences, multiplayer links recruit friends, and public lobbies give those friends somewhere to go. The reported 10,611 Discord members could provide an initial audience, while David Miller’s documented experience coaching millions of gamers supplies relevant customer context. This is more concrete than merely promising “community.” Yet the membership figure comes from a third-party snapshot, and the dossier reports no invitation conversion, repeat play, or creator-driven acquisition. My consumer and financial investments make clear that physical technology is no prerequisite for conviction. Melty’s consumer orientation is acceptable; the unproved compounding mechanism is the issue.

Miller’s gaming background does not answer the separate founding question. Full-time status, ownership, compensation, and prior collaboration are undisclosed, so founding alignment receives the stated default. The supplied zero-person team field is contradicted by LinkedIn’s two associated employees and is not evidence of an empty company. Nor do unverified allegations about inherited recording components establish misconduct. The current privacy policy does describe selected launcher-window recordings, making user trust a concrete diligence issue. Neither that policy nor the ambiguous product history proves a foundational red flag.

Melty is free today. Paid mashups, tips, and creator payouts are contemplated, but no price, platform take, or margin establishes one scalable revenue engine. That earns the absent-evidence default, without treating zero revenue as a pre-seed failure. The distinction matters: a popular launcher could create substantial entertainment value while publishers, creators, and incumbent distributors capture most of the economics.

I pass on the present evidence. The documented exceptions to my usual gates require independent technical proof, persistent transaction demand despite hostility, or a specific validated execution path; this dossier supplies none of those overrides. Equally, it documents no wind-down order, part-time founder, or investor-dominated capitalization that would justify a categorical rejection. My single flip condition is an independently verifiable 90-day cohort showing that a publisher-authorized, exclusively distributed mashup retains players and recruits additional retained players through multiplayer invitations. That would connect Melty’s contractual supply mechanism to actual customer behavior and make further investigation worth my time.
