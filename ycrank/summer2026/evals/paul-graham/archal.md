# Archal -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Archal’s controlled DNS/TLS routing and stateful GitHub, Slack, Stripe, and Linear clones make Archal a schlep-heavy infrastructure company whose weakest signal is the founders themselves. Noah Song and Aidan Tiruvan have built something technically real, but 59 weekly downloads and no revenue are not evidence that either founder is an animal. The analytical signature is good machinery without the cereal-box moment.

**Coinbase** (2012) is the high rung for schlep blindness: compliance was not incidental to the product; enduring it became the advantage. Archal shares that mechanism because preserving referential integrity, service error shapes, credentials, and cross-tool state is work most AI founders would rather pretend mocks can handle. Archal diverges because clone fidelity is a technical schlep, not yet an institutional one: Braintrust and LangChain have raised $125M and $160M respectively and can assign engineers to simulation, while Archal has no customer-derived failure corpus proving the grind compounds faster than their spending.

**Kiko** (2005) is the low-rung warning for resourcefulness: shipping software is not enough if the decisive evidence becomes an exit-shaped escape rather than an unscalable act of persistence. Archal is above that rung because Archal has produced 30 CLI releases, a self-serve `npx archal` installation, security documentation, and a functioning clone runtime before meaningful public traction. But the dossier contains no rejected customer won back, no manual deployment, no surprise revenue, and no Armstrong-style prototype used to overturn a rejection. Animals leave tracks. Archal’s tracks presently prove programming, not relentlessness.

Aidan Tiruvan’s agent-red-teaming work at Scale AI gives Archal a plausible organic origin. If Archal arose from failures Aidan personally could not reproduce, that pain should compound over the next 18 months: every ugly production incident becomes a regression scenario, every scenario improves clone fidelity, and every successful repair PR makes Archal harder to remove. The dossier calls that connection inferred, however, and Archal has not published the founding incident that would make it Dropbox-level organic rather than a well-chosen adjacent opportunity.

Noah Song’s cited `hubbard` repository has 0 stars, while Aidan Tiruvan’s `ASM-3D-Game` has 1. Those numbers do not negate Archal’s technically non-trivial runtime, but they put Archal below the hacker genealogy of founders whose artifacts attracted users before the startup existed. The counterfactual is simple: a pre-Archal service emulator, agent-testing tool, or research system with sustained outside use would move this dimension into the top tier. Academic work in quantum chemistry, geometric deep learning, and NASA-related ML establishes ability; it does not yet establish hacker pull.

**Stripe** (2010) is the useful single-anchor parallel for founder configuration: two technical founders working on infrastructure that initially looks like plumbing. Archal also has two founders with CU Boulder ties and complementary evidence across computational science and agent red-teaming. But the Collisons had years of shared building and Auctomatic behind them; no public source says when Noah and Aidan met or what Archal’s founders built together before Archal. The pair shape is right. The relationship evidence is neutral.

Archal’s 59 weekly deprecated-CLI downloads predict a narrow rather than explosive first three years. By July 2029, Archal will have concentrated on CI regression testing for repository- and messaging agents, dropped broad claims of cloning every business service, and reached $1M–$3M in ARR rather than becoming the general agent-evaluation default. Archal’s schlep can win a durable technical niche, but the missing animal evidence makes founder-led distribution the limiting reagent.

Braintrust’s $125M and LangChain’s $160M fund the strongest bear case: Archal could spend two years perfecting clones while incumbents bundle adequate simulation into evaluation platforms customers already use. Archal’s tiny GitHub footprint, 3 weekly `@archal/vitest` downloads, inferred founder-problem fit, and absence of revenue all sharpen that objection. The reason I still lean in is that Archal has built the unpleasant subsystem rather than another trace viewer; stateful replicas and rerun-verified repair PRs are a real artifact, not a deck. If Archal cannot show three external repositories running repeat weekly regression scenarios by January 31, 2027, I would flip from Invest to Pass.
