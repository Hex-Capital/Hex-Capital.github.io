# Pomerado -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Pomerado eliminates repeated model calls by turning website exploration into reusable executable integrations. That is a sensible engineering decision. The investment question is how much expensive work disappears because of it. Insurance, healthcare, and logistics contain consequential administrative burdens, but the dossier supplies no baseline for labor displaced, deployment costs eliminated, or people newly served. I can see the mechanism for cheaper execution. I cannot yet calculate a consequence large enough to make an improbable outcome worth buying.

The architecture provides more substance than a promise of better prompting: Chromium and Playwright exploration, generated operations, live-site tests, and a separate review model. Those are inspectable components. But Browser Use already offers replayable scripts, Browserbase generates Stagehand scripts, and Skyvern describes compilation and repair. Avoiding inference on each execution can beat repeatedly reasoning through a browser; that comparison does not establish superiority over competitors that also reuse successful paths. Pomerado has demonstrated an implementation, while its claimed speed, cost, and reliability advantage remains unverified. That earns limited technical credit.

The strongest argument against passing is that the founders may understand the deployment bottleneck better than the infrastructure vendors. Akshay Manglik built enterprise agents at Scale AI, including Mayo Clinic deployments; Alan Ma brings enterprise data and AI strategy experience from McKinsey. Hosted authentication and maintenance could turn an available technical mechanism into infrastructure customers depend on. I fund execution businesses as well as scientific breakthroughs, and a tiny open-source team can attract a valuable technical community. Pomerado’s 1.1k stars and 100 forks make that possibility credible. They do not establish repeat production usage or a community contributing difficult integrations. The founders’ backgrounds establish relevant context, but provide no documented belief revision or recruiting achievement against a named weakness.

The largest uncertainty is whether saved integrations remain economical when authenticated websites change. Pomerado’s terms acknowledge interruptions without a universal restoration deadline. A separate review model addresses part of generation quality; it does not establish sustained production correctness. No disclosed milestone specifies a failure threshold, experiment budget, or response if maintenance consumes the savings. Likewise, $50 per month for 1,000 calls per day is a customer price, not evidence of delivery cost. Browser execution, integration generation, credential handling, and human repair all belong in that calculation. Missing evidence receives the stated defaults; neither a two-person team nor an undisclosed revenue history is a reason to deduct further.

I pass with my own money today. The exception for an extraordinary instigator attacking a quantified giant value pool is not established here, and the exception for missing milestones still requires an independently compelling consequence of success. Pomerado currently offers plausible engineering savings in a market where the core mechanism is already shared. My verifiable flip condition is one independently audited, 90-day deployment study across 100 authorized production workflows showing at least 10x lower fully loaded cost than the strongest competing reusable-script approach, at matched correctness and with generation, repairs, and human intervention included. That would establish whether maintenance becomes a repeatable platform advantage rather than an expanding service obligation.
