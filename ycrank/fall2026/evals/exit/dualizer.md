# Dualizer -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. An industrial simulation vendor such as Emerson could buy Dualizer to add engineer-approved model editing and plant-data calibration to its software suite. Dualizer reports that its in-house KinetIQ solver connects these workflows to equipment costing and 3D layout. Emerson’s purchase of the remaining AspenTech shares demonstrates willingness to own process simulation software. That transaction involved an established vendor and offers little basis for pricing Dualizer. The acquisition hypothesis is that owning the integrated workflow could save a buyer enough development and deployment effort to justify a purchase over building or licensing. The memo gives no measured buyer benefit or evidence of buyer interest. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is a repeatable paid deployment across live plants, with measured engineering time saved, validated model accuracy, and manageable onboarding effort. The public demo and reported active pilots give Dualizer a way to test that milestone. The memo records a $100,000 prize and lists a $125,000 seed investment, but gives no burn, runway, or pilot payment terms from which to estimate the capital needed. The reported in-house solver makes a software acquisition plausible, with IP assignment and plant-data transfer rights unverified. The memo supplies no operating cash flow for a sponsor buyout or company-funded payout, eligible share demand for a secondary, or operating scale for an IPO. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Under the illustrative terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million gross. It requires about $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% hurdle is about $88.3 million in enterprise value. After that debt and before taxes, assuming pro rata distribution and no additional fees or senior claims, a $25 million sale at year eight returns $600,000 (1.2×) and a $100 million sale returns $2.85 million (5.7×). At 2% ownership, the same $100 million sale returns $1.9 million. These are hurdle sensitivities. The memo gives no Dualizer-specific price or shareholder terms. If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. Any wind-down recovery would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo does not quantify. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

ProcAgent can add conversational automation to existing HYSYS models, and MaximaLabs already offers a copilot with a deterministic solver and plant calibration. Those alternatives give a buyer ways to obtain overlapping capabilities without acquiring Dualizer. Dualizer’s published calibration demo used synthetic histories, earlier tests failed to generalize, and site-specific customization could make each deployment labor-intensive. Together, these facts weaken the case for a buyer paying an acquisition premium. The memo establishes neither a structural barrier to a sale nor a supported price range that leaves the holder materially short, so the Pass gate does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Dualizer show a repeatable paid deployment across live plants that saves enough engineering time over incumbent workflows to give a strategic buyer an economic reason to own the software at a price near or above the illustrative $88.3 million enterprise-value hurdle? Customer-verified gains, repeatable onboarding, and a buyer-side valuation grounded in those gains would move the action to Reach out. Paid pilots alone would keep it at Watch. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
