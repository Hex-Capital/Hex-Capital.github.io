# Pango -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what happened when something broke. Pango has built a surprising amount: 100+ integrations, operations in 40 countries, and software entrusted to alter orders, refunds, discounts, and store credit. But the dossier never identifies a blocker, what Rahimi or Reszczynski personally did about it, or how quickly the situation changed. Nor does it show a rejected route followed by an evidence-driven revision. Those are defaults, not negative evidence, but they prevent me from passing the founder gate.

The strongest evidence is the artifact itself. The Shopify app has been public since October 2024, five merchants have reviewed it, and the system reportedly automates 99% of shipping and returns operations. The automation figure lacks a published methodology, so I would not treat 99% as a measured benchmark. Still, a deployed system coordinating carriers, warehouses, ERPs, and customer-service tools across 40 countries is more than a diagram with “agentic” written on it. This is a real technical proof built before YC permission.

There is also a plausible narrow well. Understatement, Eytys, Kukinos, GLAS, and Switch Nails are named customers, and Pango says 30 global brands have signed. Fashion, beauty, and home merchants plainly have recurring exceptions that multiply across countries and SKUs. But “signed” is weaker than paid, retained, and expanding. Pango discloses per-order and negotiated pricing without disclosing revenue, order volume, renewal, or repeat usage. Five favorable Shopify reviews establish some real use, not yet strong pull.

Rahimi’s seven years in e-commerce and his 2025 reverse-logistics thesis make this less like a manufactured “AI for retail” idea. Reszczynski previously built a patented configurator used in a $300 million sales process. The schlep is visible too: each additional carrier, warehouse, policy, country, and sensitive Shopify permission creates ugly operational work. That founder context and integration burden deserve credit. They do not prove the specific moment when either founder encountered this problem and decided it had to exist.

The bull case stings. A five-person company with 30 signed brands and 100+ integrations may have compressed several years of incumbent work into two years, which is exactly the sort of concrete pre-round proof spike I look for. The bear case is that Narvar and parcelLab already advertise autonomous agents, while AfterShip has 1,300+ carrier integrations. Pango’s request for agency introductions also approaches the dangerous plan whose main verb is “partner.” The free Shopify app and direct demos provide another route, but the dossier does not show which route actually acquires retained customers. A working product alone is not enough for an exception to the founder gate; I would need unmistakable customer pull or independently validated proof, and neither is present yet.

So I would not write the check today. Pango looks like a capable integration company that could become a useful commerce-operations vendor, but the evidence does not yet show the founders behaving like running backs under resistance or users pulling the product hard enough to distinguish it from well-funded suites. One verifiable fact would flip me: a named merchant case showing that a production blocker caused the founders to ship a workaround within two weeks, followed by at least 90 days of continued high-volume usage.
