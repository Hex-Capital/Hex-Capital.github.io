# Agent 37 -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Give every customer an agent with its own little apartment: files stay in the drawers, credentials open the right doors, and the meter slows when nobody is home. I can imagine that being ordinary in 2032. Agent 37 already supplies pieces of that world through persistent, isolated environments and preinstalled harnesses. But that future is my extrapolation. The dossier establishes a useful deployment service; it does not establish the intervening changes that would make Agent 37 essential as agents become more capable.

The customer behavior gets my attention. Boon reportedly spent six months building on Render, then deployed through Agent 37 in three days and cut monthly hosting from $85 to $4.94 per agent ([case study](https://www.agent37.com/case-studies/boon-gave-every-client-their-own-hermes-agent-in-3-days-with-agent37)). ETEC+ migrated more than 30 existing clients onto its branded product ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/agent-37)). Moving customers means accepting operational responsibility. That is stronger evidence than a launch award or repository stars. The agency returned is concrete: a small business can offer customers their own agents without first becoming an infrastructure operator. The six months of provisioning work exposes a real burden, although the dossier identifies no artificial restriction or gatekeeper collecting a protected rent.

Why can Agent 37 make that apartment so cheap? That question determines whether the customer savings become a business. The published $4.76 monthly price for a continuously running 2-vCPU, 4-GB configuration excludes models and tools. The founder also acknowledges memory-pressure restarts and explains that polling agents must remain awake to receive messages without an external relay ([HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50059096)). Those are consequential tradeoffs when customers entrust agents with ongoing work. E2B already preserves state, while Daytona supplies broader environments and also handles Agent 37’s template builds and image imports. Agent 37 occupies an enabling layer, which fits my recurring interest in the plumbing beneath a visible wave. Control over a scarce bottleneck remains unproven.

Vishnu Krishnaprasad has relevant operating experience from Amazon and Quantcast, plus earlier ventures, Real Voxel and Dota2Connect. That earns more confidence than a freshly assembled résumé around agent enthusiasm. It does not establish a dated, pre-company obsession or a record of recruiting first believers. I see no affirmative evidence of the governance shell games that stop me from signing; a duplicated YC founder listing establishes none. Likewise, handling credentials makes consent and custody important, but the dossier does not demonstrate a severe privacy violation. Those gates remain diligence questions rather than deductions.

The strongest argument for writing a check is that customers may already be teaching Agent 37 which deployment problems everyone else underestimates. Named migrations, founder-assisted onboarding, and an October report of more than 100 companies running agents could compound into valuable operating knowledge. My willingness to back an early prototype allows incomplete commercial proof when founder obsession and a specific future path are unusually strong; those compensating signals are weaker here. I would keep my money for now: **Neutral**, with substantial respect for the working product. One verifiable result would change my lean: a customer-verified six-month Cloud cohort showing expanding paid usage at positive gross margin after compute, support, and incident recovery. That would show the cheap apartment can support both its tenants and its landlord.
