# isoquant -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Open models are making specialized inference cheaper, production developers are buying performance through compatible APIs, and isoquant’s controlling variable is whether it can sustain a lower cost per successful task while retaining enough margin to reinvest. Faster inference creates demand. A durable cost advantage determines who captures the value. The dossier establishes a credible optimization mechanism, but does not yet establish that isoquant can keep that advantage as Together, Fireworks, and Baseten respond.

The strongest evidence is the workload-specific economic change. In its banking experiment, a trained Qwen3-8B specialist achieved **91.8% accuracy versus 74.7% for GPT-5.4 mini**, with estimated inference cost reduced **91.7%** across 3,079 requests. That is a meaningful capability-and-cost improvement, although it remains a company-published experiment whose transfer to other workloads is unproven. Recent open-model releases create the opening: a smaller, specialized model can replace a more expensive general model. Isoquant’s published token prices demonstrate an aggressive offer; they do not establish profitable delivery.

Demand is arriving quickly enough to take seriously. The CEO reports **18B tokens within 24 hours of launch**, followed by **1.2T tokens in one month and 1,000+ customers**. Sho Kaneko’s report of approximately twice-as-fast agent inference gives the aggregate usage a concrete customer outcome. I discount the totals because they are company-reported, the larger figures come through a search snippet, and neither paid usage nor retention is separated out. These are encouraging adoption signals for a newly launched product, rather than established product-market fit. Agent FM’s 60+ paying users belong to the prior application and do not establish inference revenue.

The strongest case for investing now is that isoquant could turn this initial traffic into a continuous specialization loop: evaluate a workload, train a smaller model, optimize execution, and deploy the improvement. Its kernel engineering, caching, orchestration, and training capabilities give that argument substance. My concern is where the accumulated value resides. Customer-data training requires written consent, durable customer-specific evaluation assets are not demonstrated, and endpoint compatibility makes switching straightforward. Better models may improve the service while simultaneously making competing services better. The product has operational depth, but ownership of a recurring customer workflow remains weakly established.

The founders have relevant operating experience: Deshpande worked on model infrastructure and reinforcement learning, while Patankar worked on AI developer tooling and distributed serving. More revealing than their employers is the decision to commercialize optimizations developed for Agent FM. They encountered the problem while building another product and carried working technology into the new offering. That earns credit for concrete startup learning, without implying a prior exit or undocumented personal sacrifice. My pattern of backing multiple layers of a technology transition supports examining this opportunity, but the market is already crystallizing around heavily financed providers. The rare-team frontier-research exception does not apply here, and the red flag around asserted data moats remains unresolved: the dossier does not establish what a competitor would spend to reproduce the optimization assets.

I would monitor isoquant rather than write a personal check today. Its technical evidence is stronger than its evidence of durable value capture, and that distinction matters in a market where competitors can cut prices and customers can move endpoints. **My flip condition is a documented production cohort renewing after 90 days that shows isoquant maintaining a quality-matched cost advantage over competing providers while earning positive contribution margin after compute and customer-specific support.** That would connect the demonstrated engineering improvement to a business that compounds.
