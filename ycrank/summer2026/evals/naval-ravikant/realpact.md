# RealPact -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

CourseMe reached 4,000 monthly users and roughly 90% of Dartmouth before acquisition. RealPact’s signature is therefore strong founder continuity paired with almost no network compounding: Ranvir Deshmukh and Erik Peterson have already shipped and distributed together, but every RealPact brokerage remains economically independent of the next. RealPact looks like capable repeat founders building vertical enterprise software, not permissionless infrastructure. Execution is the strongest asset. Architecture is the constraint.

Stack Overflow is the high rung for the founder-partner dimension: practicing developers built for developers, shipped publicly, and proved they could play long-term games together. RealPact shares the repeated-game mechanism because Deshmukh and Peterson built CourseMe together before RealPact, achieved campus saturation, and completed an acquisition. RealPact diverges because the dossier shows neither brokerage-specific lived experience nor a long public record revealing integrity under pressure. Intelligence and energy are visible. Integrity is merely unchallenged. That is promising, but not yet the full Buffett trio.

Perplexity’s low-rung gap on network effects was survivable because extraordinary AI-specific knowledge and permissionless product distribution overrode a single-sided architecture. RealPact has the same missing mechanism without the override: paying New Hampshire brokerages and the Four Seasons Sotheby’s pilot do not make RealPact more valuable automatically when another brokerage joins. Configurations and corrections might become proprietary data, but no cross-customer learning, marketplace liquidity, or exclusive dataset is documented. RealPact is below the network threshold, and the Perplexity exception does not rescue it.

Four Seasons Sotheby’s entered through a firm-wide pilot, while RealPact’s website says “Talk to Us.” Over the next 18 months, every new RealPact geography requires city-, county-, and state-level integration maintenance, SOC 2 work, and another brokerage sale. The code may reduce labor after deployment; it does not yet remove permission from distribution. RealPact therefore compounds implementations more readily than users. The red flag is not enterprise revenue—it is enterprise selling before evidence of unprompted product pull. Anchorage Digital does not provide an exception: Anchorage turned regulatory permission into an OCC-chartered moat, while RealPact’s unfinished SOC 2 work is an obligation shared with every competitor.

Ranvir Deshmukh’s prior exit and Erik Peterson’s React, React Native, Firebase, and Swift work move RealPact above the absent-evidence default for specific knowledge. The counterfactual that flips RealPact into Invest territory is concrete: one founder must demonstrate years inside brokerage operations, or RealPact must publish a technically singular property-record system that incumbents cannot reproduce. Neither is present. No brokerage employment, public repository, or real-estate operating history was found. RealPact has demonstrated knowledge of building software, but specific knowledge is knowledge that cannot be hired from the next competent Dartmouth engineer.

The $2.01 billion transaction-management market, projected to reach $4.93 billion by 2033, gives RealPact a real business but limited pivot-room. Uber could move from black cars to mass-market rides, delivery, and logistics because the underlying network was transportation. RealPact begins inside brokerage contracts and fragmented public-record retrieval; title, mortgage, insurance, and closing are possible adjacencies, but each brings new permissions, liability, and integrations. RealPact can pivot within real-estate workflow, yet RealPact cannot make two or three cheap pivots without rebuilding distribution and compliance.

SkySlope already serves more than 900,000 professionals; my falsifiable forecast is that SkySlope will acquire RealPact by July 2029 for under $50 million after RealPact reaches $2 million to $5 million in ARR. RealPact’s repeat founders can sell and ship enough to create a useful workflow wedge, while RealPact’s single-sided architecture, jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction maintenance, and incumbent overlap constrain 10,000x geometry. A tuck-in can be a successful company outcome. It is not the asymmetric angel outcome I am buying.

Four Seasons Sotheby’s is the strongest objection to that forecast: RealPact could use a prestigious pilot and paying New Hampshire brokerages to capture the transaction’s first mile, accumulate proprietary correction data, then expand into title, mortgage, and closing. CourseMe’s 90% campus penetration shows that RealPact’s founders can drive concentrated adoption, and Perplexity proves that a network is optional when distribution and specific knowledge become exceptional. The objection loses today because RealPact has no reported retention, exclusive data, defensibility, or organic channel, while SkySlope already overlaps across AI, forms, compliance, voice, and back office. I pass. I would flip to Invest if RealPact reaches 100 brokerages with more than 30% acquired through self-serve or customer referrals and can verify that cross-brokerage data measurably improves contract accuracy.
