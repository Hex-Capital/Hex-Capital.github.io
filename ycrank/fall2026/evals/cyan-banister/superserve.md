# Superserve -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine handing a computer a project on Monday and returning the following Monday to find useful work completed, with its memory intact and its permissions still bounded. Superserve supplies pieces that world needs. But persistent machines must first become reliable long-running workers, and those workers must earn permission to act without constant supervision. The dossier establishes infrastructure for that transition; it does not yet establish the transition itself. That distinction matters when I am writing a personal check.

Why should an agent’s computer disappear after 24 hours? E2B’s continuous Pro runtime limit is a concrete restriction, and managing servers and warm pools transfers operating work back onto developers. Superserve offers unrestricted session duration at published consumption prices of $0.0504/vCPU-hour and $0.0162/GiB-hour. That could return engineering time and control to small teams. But the dossier does not quantify the burden removed, and a competitor’s product limit is not evidence of an entrenched economic rent. I see useful workflow repair, with the size of the liberation still unmeasured.

The strongest argument for investing is that Superserve already has something people can use. Its October 2026 claim of over 100,000 machines weekly, with Lindy and YC named as users, deserves attention. This is substantially more informative than a waitlist. Yet machine launches do not tell me whether people repeatedly entrust weeklong projects to the product, pay for that behavior, or simply consume introductory credits. The functioning SDK and console support the claim that there is a real artifact; repository stars do not answer those behavioral questions. I would want to sit beside someone whose work now depends on it.

Superserve fits my recurring attraction to concealed infrastructure beneath a visible wave: coding agents and training environments both need execution machinery. Full-memory snapshots, persistent forks, and host-scoped credential brokering represent actual implementation work. The uncomfortable counterargument is that E2B already preserves memory and Modal already offers VM sandboxes. Superserve controls an implementation, but the dossier has not shown that it controls a scarce constraint. Keeping credentials outside the guest is encouraging architecture; it does not prevent unwanted authenticated actions against permitted hosts. That is an unresolved permission boundary, rather than evidence of a severe privacy violation. Nor does the dossier establish dependence on one closed model supplier.

Pavitra Bhalla’s Dosu engineering experience and founding-engineer-to-director history at LTSE give this team relevant operating context. They do not establish the dated, independently pursued obsession or recruitment proof that makes me comfortable backing an unfinished future. The November 2025 RayAI launch supplies product history, but the reason for the subsequent transition remains undisclosed. My exceptions for investing before revenue require unusually strong founder evidence and concrete technical validation; this record supplies only part of that combination. I pass today because the distinctive bet remains unproven, not because pre-seed revenue is missing. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: a referenceable Lindy renewal documenting repeated paid, weeklong workloads and a measured advantage over E2B and Modal on those same workloads.
