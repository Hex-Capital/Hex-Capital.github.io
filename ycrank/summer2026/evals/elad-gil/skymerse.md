# Skymerse -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Three million processed NOTAMs make Skymerse a domain operator’s product with a real shipped wedge, while the absence of disclosed revenue or month-over-month usage makes Skymerse a shipping story without a visible curve. That highest/lowest pairing is the analytical signature: Damian Szumski has converted dispatcher experience and ownership of Flightradar24’s API product into production software used by LOT Polish Airlines, but I cannot yet distinguish “just working” from a false signal carried over from Notamify.

**Perplexity** is the high rung for iterative shipping: Aravind repeatedly returned with the discussed product built. Skymerse shares the load-bearing mechanism because Szumski progressed from Flightradar24’s API to Notamify, processed more than three million NOTAMs, accumulated 3,500 registered users, and expanded toward planning-to-cockpit operations. Skymerse diverges because the dossier documents successive artifacts, not the week-to-week feedback cadence that made Perplexity exceptional; the evidence places Szumski above a credential-only founder but below the strongest shipping pattern in my portfolio.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism of mistaking activity for evidence that the decisive economics are resolving. Skymerse reports thousands of daily Notamify users, a LOT Polish Airlines deployment, and enterprise production use, but Skymerse discloses neither ARR nor cohort growth, and nearly all traction belongs to the narrower Notamify product rather than the broader autopilot. For an AI product, that attribution gap matters: $24.90 Pro pricing proves monetization exists, but it does not reveal whether enterprise revenue is compounding or whether pilots are merely aviation-industry tourism.

**Jeppesen’s 2026 Airflow launch** is evidence that aeronautical data is becoming agent-readable, but Skymerse has not yet shown that this technological change reopened airline procurement rather than merely giving an embedded incumbent another feature. Skymerse would flip into the top catalyst tier if Airflow’s launch coincided with airlines replacing legacy planning systems and Skymerse signed two additional named carriers within 12 months. Without that evidence, the catalyst is specific and recent but double-edged: it validates the architecture while improving Jeppesen’s ability to commoditize it.

**LOT Polish Airlines** gives Skymerse a plausible path to becoming an index on increasingly automated flight operations. Over 18 months, every operator manual, minimum-equipment list, runway constraint, correction, and alert can improve Skymerse’s client-specific operational context, making each integration harder to remove and creating the workflow-data compounding I like. The mechanism fails if Jeppesen retains control of authoritative data and distribution: Skymerse would then have to win airline by airline against an incumbent already embedded in dispatch, rather than automatically benefiting as aviation adopts agents.

**Color Genomics** is the closest single-anchor parallel for the miracle architecture: Skymerse builds workflow and decision infrastructure on top of an existing hard layer rather than inventing new aeronautical data or new physics. Skymerse primarily needs one miracle—that its contextual interpretation is reliable enough to become operationally indispensable—but the Notamify terms requiring verification against official sources weaken the parallel. Safety acceptance, timely third-party data, and airline integration cannot all remain independent miracles; Skymerse must turn them into ordinary implementation work before unattended automation becomes credible.

**3,500 registered users** support my 36-month forecast that Boeing/Jeppesen will acquire Skymerse for under $100 million after Skymerse reaches $3 million to $7 million in ARR. Skymerse has enough product execution and domain-specific data to become strategically valuable, but the low disclosed revenue velocity, authoritative-source disclaimers, and Jeppesen’s distribution advantage make acquisition more likely than Skymerse becoming an independent index on flight operations.

**LOT Polish Airlines’ production deployment** is the strongest objection to that constrained forecast: Skymerse may already have the wedge that becomes an aviation analogue to Decagon, with proprietary operational context compounding across every flight while incumbents remain source-oriented. Skymerse also fits my exception pattern—like Color, workflow on top of the hard thing, led by a founder who operated inside the workflow. The current evidence still loses on attribution and authority: Notamify supplies most usage proof, while Skymerse cannot be the sole basis for safety-critical decisions. I would flip to Invest if Skymerse discloses two additional named airline deployments and at least 20% month-over-month enterprise ARR growth sustained for six months.
