# Aerogen Systems -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Aerogen Systems is attacking a real physical bottleneck, but the consequence remains unpriced. Semiconductor manufacturers already spend billions controlling contamination, and molecular residue can destroy yield as hybrid bonding moves toward finer pitches. Yet the dossier never converts sub-1-ppb air into wafers saved, percentage yield recovered, nitrogen eliminated, or dollars removed from a fab’s cost structure. A $6.163 billion contamination-control market is not itself a 100x consequence.

Talos has more substance than a generic hardware concept. Vogel and Lavery carried a photocatalytic reactor through two years of NASA-backed research, more than $500,000 of R&D, multiple generations, and a reported run reaching 7N purity in 16 minutes. The founders also moved from spacecraft purification to industrial ducts and then semiconductor tools while retaining the core mechanism. That is useful belief revision: obstinate about contaminant destruction, flexible about the market.

But the technical comparison is incomplete. Entegris and Camfil already sell tool-level AMC products. Aerogen claims zero consumables and onboard monitoring, but it has not shown lifetime contaminant-removal capacity, energy consumption, destruction byproducts, uptime, maintenance burden, or cost per treated cubic meter against either incumbent. One company-reported PID run below 1 ppb is evidence of activity, not yet evidence of a 5–10x discontinuity.

The strongest bull case is that semiconductor qualification itself creates the moat. A four-person team with relevant aerospace manufacturing, sensing, and reactor experience could use early integrations to accumulate tool-specific adapters, performance data, and validated recipes that incumbents cannot rapidly copy. This matches the pattern of a scarce technical workflow becoming a repeatable production platform. The trouble is that no fab or equipment OEM is named, the pilots remain unverified, and each site evaluation may turn the platform into a custom integration business.

Capital should now attack one kill risk: whether Talos improves wafer yield enough to outweigh integration and qualification costs. The decisive experiment is not another clean-air demonstration. It is a side-by-side production-tool trial measuring contaminant load, defectivity, uptime, consumable savings, and total cost against a named Entegris or Camfil configuration. Aerogen has disclosed no pass threshold, experiment budget, or stop condition, so dollars-spent-versus-risk-removed remains unclear.

The terminal problem is economics. No pricing, reactor lifetime, power requirement, service burden, or manufacturing-cost model is public. “No consumables” could create excellent economics, or merely exchange filter replacement for expensive reactors and qualification labor. My verifiable flip condition is a named semiconductor manufacturer publishing or confirming a trial in which Talos delivers at least a 5x reduction in total contamination-control cost per tool while maintaining sub-1-ppb performance and improving measured yield. Until then, I would not write the check.
