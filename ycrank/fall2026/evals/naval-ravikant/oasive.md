# Oasive -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Anaïs Howland has invested in the instruments Oasive helps people analyze. Wells Fargo MBS quantitative work and Google Treasury portfolio management supply relevant operating experience; the CFA designation adds little beside that. More importantly, Oasive shipped MBS/rates tools and reported use across **15 institutional desks in September 2026**. That is evidence of turning specific knowledge into a product. Municipal credit remains an adjacent domain, and the dossier does not establish that those desks pay or use the municipal offering.

The central question is what Oasive keeps when a competitor reproduces its research pages. Source-linked financial claims make the product useful and inspectable. They do not make the underlying information exclusive. DPC DATA already supplies AI-ready municipal financials and news; MuniPro produces source-based research memos. Oasive advertises proprietary MBS prepayment and valuation models, which earns some credit, but supplies no benchmark establishing an advantage. Its licensed inputs also remain conditional on upstream access. A research workflow can save customers substantial time while leaving the supplier with little durable bargaining power.

The strongest case for investing is that Howland knows which analytical details institutional buyers actually value. Fifteen desks could provide the feedback needed to turn that judgment into models competitors cannot reproduce from public documents alone. Oasive also fits my preference for tools that multiply an individual's knowledge. But the dossier establishes neither a proprietary feedback asset nor customers improving the product for other customers. An institutional user is a prospective buyer; it does not automatically become a network participant. My exceptions for exceptional founder execution, an unusual capital-efficient prototype, or an emerging network require affirmative evidence of those mechanisms. The operating background and early usage make those possibilities credible, but do not yet establish them.

There is room to maneuver across municipal credit, MBS, and rates: the existing products and established vendors demonstrate adjacent institutional demand without requiring an invented TAM. Source retrieval and traceable analysis offer a shared capability, although municipal borrower assessment and mortgage prepayment modeling require different expertise. Direct monetization is also sensible: **$200 per user per month** for Macro & Rates, and an offered paid MBS pilot followed by an annual agreement. These are commercial terms, not disclosed realized revenue. They establish how Oasive intends to capture value, while leaving willingness to pay unproven.

I would keep Oasive under observation and decline a check today. There is no disclosed integrity problem, artificial financing deadline, or dependence on investor reassurance; a solo founder and limited public history are ordinary at this stage. My reservation is structural: the evidence for knowledgeable execution is substantially stronger than the evidence for compounding advantage. **My flip condition is a reproducible customer-held-out benchmark showing that Oasive's proprietary models materially outperform a baseline using the same licensed inputs.** That would demonstrate an asset beyond assembling available information and change what I am underwriting.
