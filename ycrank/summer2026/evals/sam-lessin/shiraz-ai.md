# Shiraz AI -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I need are missing: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and the exact experiment this check finances. That is not a generic pre-seed traction complaint. Price and capital purpose are my gates. With no evidence that this is a cheap, scarce first check—or that proceeds buy a dated, measurable result—I have to assume I am being asked to finance a category rather than underwrite a venture moment.

The company itself is more credible than the financing case. Navid Aghasadeghi worked on Spot commercialization and industrial manipulation at Boston Dynamics after Rethink Robotics; Mojtaba Mozaffar led perception, policy-learning, and manipulation work at Amazon Robotics. These are shipped operating receipts, not résumé decoration. Robots are already operational at customer sites, so the team has crossed the boundary between a robotics demonstration and factory deployment. What remains unproven is the other half of the translation layer: neither founder is shown having operated a contract manufacturer where weekly line changes, labor constraints, uptime, and integration costs become one economic system.

The potential dark pool is compelling but still prospective. Supplying the hardware, model, and on-site deployment could let Shiraz own demonstration, exception, and execution data inside high-mix factories. Yet the dossier gives no deployment count, named customer, repeat usage, purchase, retention, or task-level performance. “First customers” and customer-site operation are better than a lab video, but they do not establish that proprietary workflow access already causes a purchase.

The bear case bites because Skild S1 now claims the same single-video, no-fine-tuning mechanism and sits behind more than $2 billion of financing. Standard Bots and Micropsi attack adjacent integration boundaries with their own hardware or existing fleets. If the model capability commoditizes, Shiraz is left carrying hardware, safety, installation, and support—the expensive parts—without evidence of pricing power or a unique dataset. Physical complexity can create defensibility, but it can just as easily create a services business wearing an AI multiple.

The strongest exception precedent would be **Ando**: enterprise AI earned an override because measured accuracy, complete pilot-to-paid conversion, and complete retention proved that the base business worked beneath the model option. Shiraz shares the enterprise deployment shape, but none of those quantified receipts. There is also no revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or controllable cash metric showing that installations can fund subsequent installations without another round. The two-founder CEO/CTO split is directionally sensible, though the dossier does not establish who has final authority when product reliability, customer promises, and financing conflict.

I am passing today, despite an unusually credible technical team. One verifiable condition flips me: before the round closes, show a sub-$10 million post-money financing tied to a dated cohort of at least three paid factories where single-video tasks sustain at least 90% successful cycles and positive deployment contribution margin. That would simultaneously prove scarce pricing, a finite experiment, workflow conversion, and a base business that does not depend on the next model release or Series A.
