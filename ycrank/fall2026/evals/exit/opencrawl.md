# OpenCrawl -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest supported route is a strategic sale to a developer platform that wants to coordinate coding agents across tools. OpenCrawl combines searchable session history with a shared board across local and cloud environments. A platform buyer could use that integration to reduce duplicate investigations and improve handoffs for mixed-tool teams. The memo provides evidence of product capabilities, while the workflow savings and the buyer’s willingness to pay remain inferences. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The memo records 57 repository stars, 9 forks, and a $125,000 seed financing entry before the current product launched. The CTO reported one onboarding and a cross-user agent messaging interaction. Those signals make a small production test plausible. The next milestone is sustained use by paying mixed-tool teams with a measured reduction in duplicate investigations. The memo leaves the cost of that test and the runway to complete it unverified. (Market & Traction)

Documented self-hosting makes it plausible for an acquirer to operate the core software without importing existing customer transcripts. IP ownership, required consents, and holder participation remain unverified. Hosted overage pricing provides a possible revenue source, while the memo leaves payout capacity and eligible secondary demand unevidenced. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The proceeds bridge uses the rubric’s assumed $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. At 3% pro rata, that requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity. Both sale scenarios assume an eligible holder sells the entire stake for cash within eight years and receives 3% of distributable equity after net debt and deal adjustments, with no further reduction from senior claims.

| Scenario | Illustrative bridge | Holder cash |
|----------|---------------------|-------------|
| Modest sale | $30 million enterprise value less $5 million in net debt and deal adjustments yields $25 million in distributable equity | $750,000 (1.5×) |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value less $10 million in net debt and deal adjustments yields $90 million in distributable equity | $2.7 million (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no distribution or eligible share purchase within eight years | $0 |

The strong sale clears the objective by $200,000. If the holder can sell only half the stake on those terms, realized cash falls to $1.35 million. The memo supplies no buyer economics or comparable sale price to anchor either acquisition scenario. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A platform may already have enough coordination capability to avoid buying OpenCrawl. Claude Code offers native messaging and shared tasks, while Agent Relay already offers cross-tool messaging and searchable sessions. OpenCrawl’s push mentions depend on a supported listener, and team-visible transcripts with imperfect secret masking may limit enterprise adoption. Together, these facts weaken the buy-versus-build case. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the memo leaves both a structural closing block and a credible sale-price ceiling unestablished. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
