# Osmaura -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Osmaura’s contested truth is that law firms can identify demand before clients begin searching for counsel by matching public events to private relationship context. What would prove it wrong? If the surfaced opportunities do not become qualified conversations or paid matters at a meaningfully higher rate than ordinary partner networking, the “growth engine” is merely a more elaborate news alert. Daily customer logins offer weak behavioral validation, but Osmaura discloses neither customer count nor opportunity-to-matter conversion. The secret is precise enough to test, yet it remains largely untested.

The founders are the strongest part of the company. Kali Abeje and Jity Woldemichael were friends at MIT, left during the same period, and now work full-time on Osmaura. Their computer-science, ML, NLP, Meta, AWS, and Jane Street experience supports an in-house technical team rather than an outsourced product. This is real alignment, not two strangers assembled for YC. But friendship and synchronized departure are not yet evidence that they previously shipped together, while ownership, salaries, and economic exposure remain undisclosed.

The beachhead appears bounded—corporate-law partners and law-firm business-development leaders—but narrow is not the same as monopolistic. Nexl already serves more than 150 law firms, Darrow reported 50, and Intapp possesses the CRM and relationship surfaces into which proactive signal monitoring could be added. Osmaura has disclosed no exclusive data, measured technical advantage, dominant share, or compounding network effect. Firm-specific feedback could eventually become proprietary, but a hypothetical feedback loop is not a moat. Today this is feature versus platform, and platforms usually win that contest.

The strongest bull case is that daily use already indicates an urgent workflow, while the founders’ months of partner interviews and their willingness to abandon a two-month negotiation show discipline rather than desperation. If every accepted or rejected lead teaches Osmaura which corporate events produce matters for a particular practice, the product could accumulate a private map connecting events, relationships, and legal demand—something a generic CRM does not possess. That would turn noisy public information into proprietary institutional memory.

But the same facts presently support the opposite conclusion. A major-firm negotiation consumed two months and produced no customer, no pricing is public, and the only documented route is founder-led calls, early-access requests, YC exposure, and a booking link. Enterprise distribution is not solved merely because the buyer can be named. Confidential firm data, security review, integrations, partner trust, and long contracting cycles may make acquisition too expensive for an undisclosed SaaS contract. The $28.7 billion legal-technology TAM says nothing about whether this specific revenue engine can produce a power-law outcome.

I would pass today. The company has founders rather than professional managers, but it has not yet shown secret rather than assertion, monopoly rather than an adjacent feature, or distribution rather than founder effort. One result would flip the decision: documented deployment at ten corporate law firms showing that Osmaura-sourced opportunities convert into paid matters at least three times more often than each firm’s existing outbound process, with the underlying outcome data contractually retained for model improvement. That single test would validate the secret, begin the data moat, and justify the enterprise sales burden.
