# RATLOG -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** RATLOG could supply scarce launch slots to satellite operators whose payloads are ready before a suitable flight is available. The memo records hundreds of flight-ready Amazon Leo satellites awaiting launch. RATLOG claims some available slots are three to four years out. It also reports a prototype hot-fire of its turbopump-free engine and says the design uses 90% fewer parts. The hot-fire offers early support for the architecture. The investment inference is that simpler hardware could reduce turnaround work and increase usable launch capacity, pending flight-scale testing. Cheaper intelligence could reduce engineering and operating labor for RATLOG and its rivals. Any additional launch demand from orbital data centers remains a scenario assumption. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

RATLOG reports $400 million in signed reservations. Undisclosed customer identities, deposits, and cancellation terms limit the figure to an interest signal. The founders bring relevant propulsion, avionics, and startup experience, and the reported four-month, sub-$30,000 prototype effort shows early execution. The next planned step is a flight-ready engine in 2027. That is a reasonable seed-stage test. The memo gives conflicting headquarters signals, so fit with the primarily U.S. mandate remains unresolved. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

The scale case depends on flight cadence and paid payload per flight. Annual launch revenue would be flights per year × paid kilograms per flight × realized price per kilogram. Contribution per flight would have to cover integration, launch operations, recovery, and refurbishment, with enough left across flights to support vehicle and site investment. The 20-tonne target is a capacity assumption. Achievable cadence, paid utilization, and costs remain open. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

With abundant capital and cheap intelligence, incumbents could add suitable slots before RATLOG reaches its planned 2029–2030 orbital launch. SpaceX already sells rideshare, Arianespace flies constellation missions, and Blue Origin has recovered a booster after an orbital mission. RATLOG is still evaluating launch sites and has yet to establish a protected source of capacity or a demonstrated turnaround advantage. If incumbents meet the same payload and schedule requirements, buyers could switch and the scarcity case would break. The memo establishes real alternatives but leaves their availability for the claimed bottleneck open. The Path A outreach gate remains unmet. Path B has no separate evidence that automation makes a previously uneconomic launch transaction viable. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)
