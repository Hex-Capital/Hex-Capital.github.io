# hardware intelligence -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Price first: the post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and status of this check are all undisclosed. That earns 6/28, not an assumption that YC demand makes the round scarce or auctioned. Hardware intelligence could be a cheap, non-consensus semiconductor-tools bet, but the dossier does not establish it. I will override missing or expensive terms for unusually hard commercial proof; a July 2026 launch, an open waitlist, and pilot onboarding are not yet that proof.

The potentially valuable card flip is narrower than the company’s $20 billion EDA framing: can an on-prem terminal agent debug multi-gigabyte waveforms accurately enough that an ASIC team replaces part of its incumbent GUI workflow? Wave can trace signals, propose fixes, rerun a simulator, and produce an auditable waveform diff. That is a credible finite experiment. But hardware intelligence has not connected round proceeds to a named customer deployment, accuracy threshold, deadline, or purchasing decision, so the capital plan remains at the 6/24 default. “Build and onboard pilots” is activity; “three design teams replace Verdi debugging for a tape-out by December” would reveal truth.

The founders are the real asset. Athreya Anand led agentic-workflow and prediction-model work at Google and encountered chip design through AWS Inferentia and Tesla. Rishov Sarkar built AMD AI Engine simulators, worked on LLVM flows at Siemens EDA, and maintains the 77-star LightningSim simulator. This is an unusually legible translation layer between AI agents and semiconductor tooling, not two generalists applying a model API to an unfamiliar workflow. I score that 9/10. The decade-long relationship is useful, although the dossier does not identify who has final authority when the two equal co-founders disagree.

The strongest bull case is that public traction understates enterprise adoption: on-prem semiconductor tools are sold through demonstrations and painstaking integrations, not self-serve signups, and Wave’s customer-supplied model key, evidence-linked transcript, and waveform diff address real security and auditability constraints. A single successful deployment could become an embedded workflow. But today there is no documented purchase, completed pilot, repeat usage, or proprietary customer access. Personal onboarding and a waitlist do not prove that an owned workflow causes action, so this remains 5/20.

The market evidence makes that omission consequential. Synopsys Verdi already offers natural-language commands, AI failure analysis, signal tracing, waveform comparison, and the entrenched FSDB ecosystem that Wave does not yet support generically. ChipAgents reports deployments at more than 120 semiconductor companies and sixfold ARR growth in the first half of 2026. This activates the generic-AI red flag: the application layer can be erased by an incumbent or better-capitalized agent platform unless hardware intelligence owns a compact customer workflow they cannot copy. None of the exception logic applies yet because there is no measured conversion, retention, revenue, or rejected strategic offer.

Delete the next round and the dossier supplies no price, gross margin, burn, runway, or controllable cash metric. Enterprise on-prem licensing could support a profitable path, but that is inferred rather than demonstrated, earning the 7/18 default. I am passing despite the exceptional technical fit: the evidence currently describes a talented team with a working wedge, not a scarce-priced venture experiment or an owned commercial asset. One verifiable condition flips me: three named semiconductor teams paying to use Wave through a completed verification cycle, with at least two renewing without bespoke founder support.
