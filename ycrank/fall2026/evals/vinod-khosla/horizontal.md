# horizontal. -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

horizontal.’s consequential outcome would be factories coordinating production, materials, and quality without armies of people reconciling disconnected systems. That could remove an expensive expertise bottleneck. But the dossier does not quantify coordination labor, production lost to disruptions, or how much either could fall. The adjacent $20.7 billion MES market is software spending, not a measure of the resources these agents could release. I cannot yet calculate why a small probability of success buys an extraordinary consequence.

The decisive question is whether horizontal. can execute production decisions reliably enough to remove human work. Replanning a factory creates downstream obligations: materials must exist, quality constraints must hold, and order releases must remain feasible. Pelico already connects ERP and MES data to proposed disruption responses; Siemens announced governed agent orchestration in June 2026. Competitors do not make the opportunity unattractive. They make the missing experiment specific: what can these founders execute that those systems cannot, at what error rate, and with how much less supervision? The dossier establishes no benchmark or proprietary learning mechanism. It also does not establish that horizontal. is merely a model wrapper.

The strongest argument for investing is the founders’ proximity to the problem. Marxer reports bringing agentic AI into more than 150 industrial companies before horizontal.; Hermeler has Stanford robotics experience and coauthored autonomous quadrotor collision-recovery research. Those are substantive reasons to investigate whether this team understands operational exceptions better than a generic enterprise AI team. Their combination of industrial deployment and technical research fits my preference for teams assembled around difficult workflows. However, those prior deployments are not horizontal.’s customers, and the record supplies no observed belief revision or difficult complementary recruit. Relevant experience earns attention; it does not establish those particular founder behaviors.

Starting with one process inside existing systems is sensible. It could make the largest uncertainty cheap to test. Yet the dossier provides no completed reliability experiment, numerical acceptance threshold, test cost, or stop condition. Its statement that it works with international manufacturers supplies neither deployment scope nor measured outcomes. Similarly, enterprise contracts remain an inferred business model: there is no disclosed price, implementation burden, supervision cost, or customer savings. Plant-specific engineering could consume the economic benefit of automation. That is an unresolved possibility, not evidence that the economics fail.

I would pass on the current evidence. My willingness to fund execution-heavy businesses means the absence of a scientific breakthrough is not automatically disqualifying. The exception for an extraordinary instigator addressing a giant existing value pool also deserves consideration, but horizontal.’s relevant credentials do not yet establish that override. Nor does the dossier demonstrate the red flags of buying distribution or scaling headcount before resolving reliability; a hiring announcement alone proves neither. My single flip condition is an independently verifiable trial of the same production workflow across three unrelated plants, showing at least a fivefold reduction in human coordination hours, no increase in production or quality incidents, and positive customer savings after integration and supervision costs. That result would connect capability, repeatability, and economics. Today, those connections remain hypotheses.
