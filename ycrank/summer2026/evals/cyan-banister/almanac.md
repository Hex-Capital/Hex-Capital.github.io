# Almanac -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

743 GitHub stars make Almanac an artifact-first software utility: unusually easy to install and observe, yet almost maximally distant from the capital-intensive, regulated worlds where refusal becomes moat. That high-touchability/low-hardness pairing is Almanac’s signature. Almanac has made agent memory visible as repository-owned Markdown, but visibility alone does not make the underlying problem difficult to copy.

**Flock Safety** is the high rung for physical-world verifiability because the product announced itself through a Wi-Fi network and then appeared as cameras in neighborhoods. Almanac shares the important part of that mechanism: a developer can install version 0.1.5 from PyPI, watch connected pages appear in Git, and judge the result without trusting a founder’s testimony. Almanac diverges where the analogy matters most—the artifact is still a software abstraction on macOS, not a camera, ride, rocket, or delivery entering ordinary life. I can touch the output, but I cannot encounter Almanac accidentally in the world.

**HQ Trivia’s** low-rung failure mechanism was a software-only artifact whose immediacy obscured how little structural hardness existed underneath it. Almanac shows the quieter version of that pattern: an Apache-2.0 CLI, repository-local Markdown, and no identified structural barrier while Cognition, Swimm, Mintlify, and Greptile attack adjacent surfaces. Almanac is neither frontier hardware nor an ugly regulated category; a coding-agent vendor already controlling conversations and repositories can reproduce the core loop. The product may be useful, but useful is a rebuttal, not a moat.

Kushagra Chitkara’s 12-hour-plus and 16-hour Codex runs give Almanac a credible moment of contact with the problem, while Rohan Sharma’s retrieval research and Divit Sheth’s Arcesium engineering experience supply the “Tiz.” Over 18 months, that lived usage could compound into unusually good judgment about which decisions deserve durable memory. Almanac still reads biography-second: Harvard, IIT, MIT, CMU, and Vector credentials are documented, but no evidence shows that Kushagra, Rohan, or Divit suffered this problem for years before forming Almanac. The founders are working inside the problem now; the dossier does not establish that the problem found them first.

Cognition’s DeepWiki and Devin Search make Almanac look less magically weird than merely timely, especially beside Mintlify’s 20,000-company footprint and Greptile’s 2,000 customers. Almanac would flip this dimension if credible investors dismissed repository-owned agent memory as aesthetically trivial while developers kept adopting it, or if the local conversation-to-wiki corpus produced a measured retrieval advantage competitors could not recreate. The present evidence—743 stars, five displayed users, and no revenue signal—shows curiosity in a consensus AI-developer category, not the delicious wrongness of **Crusoe** burning stranded gas for Bitcoin.

**GameCrush** taught me that unresolved authority eventually lets conflict or outside investors strip the product’s DNA. Almanac lists Kushagra Chitkara, Rohan Sharma, and Divit Sheth identically as “Founder,” with no named CEO, ownership split, vesting protocol, or tiebreaker. Almanac therefore triggers the red flag directly. Three smart people can build 843 commits together; the question is who decides on commit 844 when product-led open source conflicts with enterprise sales. No dossier evidence answers it.

Five displayed engineering users—cmux, Agnost AI, Click, Conifer, and chromie.dev—suggest that Almanac can earn repeat usage before earning a budget. My falsifiable 36-month forecast is that by July 2029 Almanac will have pivoted from a self-updating wiki into enterprise audit and provenance for coding agents and reached $1 million–$3 million ARR, rather than succeeding as a standalone developer-memory product. Almanac’s observable Markdown and local privacy posture create the wedge; Almanac’s lack of frontier hardness forces the wedge toward compliance, review, and purchasing authority.

843 commits, 743 stars, and 68 forks make the strongest opposing case sting: Almanac may already have the tactile product love that let **HQ Trivia** override my governance rule, while repository ownership could become the neutral memory layer across Codex, Claude Code, and future agents. Almanac’s five named users strengthen that bull case. The dossier still shows no revenue, no measured retrieval advantage, no CEO, and an Apache-2.0 implementation facing Cognition’s $492 million run-rate platform, so the exception has not activated—stars are applause, not addiction. I would flip to Invest if Almanac verifies $1 million ARR from the self-updating-wiki product before July 2027; explosive commercial pull would prove that the artifact, like HQ at its best, is overriding the structural weaknesses.
