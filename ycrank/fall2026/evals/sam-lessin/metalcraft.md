# Metalcraft -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Metalcraft has built something concrete, but I cannot yet underwrite the check. Proposed post-money: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. Whether my money would be the first genuinely scarce capital: unknown. YC backing answers none of those questions. I will use the missing-evidence defaults rather than invent an auction, but the distinction matters: a technically interesting company is not automatically an attractive investment at an unknown price.

Kiely is the strongest reason to keep looking. First-author Nature and Science papers in computational biology are actual research outputs, and Metalcraft’s runnable tasks and graders demonstrate software execution. That combination fits my preference for someone who can translate between scientific practice and AI engineering. His Perplexity finance and Notable Health engineering roles add context; they do not establish customer access or a prior commercial outcome. I give the founder meaningful credit for the research-to-software bridge without assuming he already owns the buyer relationship.

The strongest bull case is that Metalcraft can turn biological judgment into something laboratories repeatedly buy. Its metabolic-model product contains 1,724 variant cases, with 424 tested per model; another 36 Auto-research tasks target experiment selection. Those are more substantial artifacts than a slide promising an AI scientist. But the economic question is who owns the scarce input. Public samples and leaderboards lead to a calendar link, and the dossier documents no resulting purchase, pilot, or retained workflow. Case counts measure inventory. They do not establish a proprietary dark pool. LABBench2’s public harness and laboratories’ ability to build internal evaluations make that distinction consequential.

What exact truth does the round reveal? The dossier supplies no funded experiment, deadline, or pass/fail milestone. Whether performance on these tasks predicts useful experimental decisions would be an important hypothesis, particularly where graders use estimated judgments, but that is my proposed test—not Metalcraft’s documented capital plan. Nor can I delete the next financing from a financial model: pricing, revenue, delivery margins, burn, and runway are undisclosed. These omissions do not prove bad economics or serial-financing dependence. They leave both underwriting questions at their specified defaults.

I pass on the evidence available. Metalcraft’s technical work and founder fit make the opposing case credible, but they do not supply the measured conversion or cash economics that would justify overriding my financing gates. There is also no basis to allege a hot-round pressure tactic, an equal-founder committee, or a margin model that assumes AI errors disappear. My objection is narrower: the team has demonstrated an ability to make evaluations, while the investable asset—owned access that causes recurring purchases—remains unestablished. One verifiable flip condition would reopen diligence: a signed, prepaid laboratory contract for a private taskset, with contractual rights to reuse resulting failure data and documented positive contribution margin. That would connect the scientific work to a customer-funded, compounding asset.
