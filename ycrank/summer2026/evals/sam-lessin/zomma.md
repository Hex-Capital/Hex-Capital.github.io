# Zomma -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot price this check because the dossier gives me no post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is genuinely scarce capital. I also cannot identify the card the round is meant to flip. “Hire engineers and deploy more workflows” would be a capital plan; it would not be a venture moment. Until Zomma names a dated experiment—such as eliminating the dedicated deployment engineer while preserving accuracy across five production workflows—the financing is buying activity rather than revealing a discrete truth.

The team is the strongest part of the case. Jihyun Kim has operated inside Goldman Sachs, Endowus, and Arrowpoint; Cashel Fitzgerald built systems supporting more than $20 billion in annual transactions; and Varun Arumugam worked on verification infrastructure and ML agents. That is a legitimate translation layer between investment operations and unreliable computer-use systems. The product reflects this understanding: isolated credentials, approval gates, policy checks, and seven-year append-only audit logs address the reasons financial institutions cannot simply turn a browser agent loose.

The dark pool is less established. Zomma says hedge funds, asset managers, and fintechs use it, and customer corrections become firm-specific memory. But no named customer, paid contract, repeat deployment, retention figure, or workflow-expansion result shows that this access has caused a purchase. The accumulated memory may create switching costs inside each firm, but it does not yet constitute a proprietary cross-customer advantage. Browser Use and Asteroid commoditize the control layer, while Hadrius already serves more than 500 financial firms from a system-of-record position.

The strongest bull case is the **Ando** exception: regulated enterprise AI can overcome model-risk skepticism when it sits on top of a functioning workflow and produces measurable conversion and retention. Zomma shares the workflow orientation and human-control architecture. The material difference is that Ando supplied greater than 90% forecast accuracy, complete pilot-to-paid conversion, and complete retention; Zomma supplies unquantified usage. The exception is therefore available, but the evidence required to invoke it is not.

The current deployment model also works against a one-and-done outcome. Every engagement requires workflow mapping, supervised runs, and a dedicated engineer; the founders themselves describe deployments as slow and heterogeneous. Reviewer fatigue, 2FA, failure recovery, and an unfinished SOC 2 Type II audit compound that labor burden. There is no revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or controllable cash metric demonstrating that these implementations become profitable before the next financing. My 36-month base case is a $1 million–$3 million ARR, services-heavy finance-automation business still using forward-deployed engineers—not a compounding software system.

The move from enterprise sales and application support in April 2026 to investment operations by August adds another question: did customer evidence pull Zomma into finance, or did the team reposition around a better-funded narrative? The founders’ complementary receipts make the first explanation credible, and Kim’s CEO title at least identifies an accountable leader. But the product-positioning change, adjacent well-capitalized competitors, and dependence on newly capable computer-use models make this an out-of-the-money option without a demonstrated base business.

I am passing at the current evidence level. My one verifiable flip condition is a sub-$10-million post-money round devoted to a 12-month cohort test in which three named customers each expand from one paid workflow to at least five while Zomma removes the dedicated deployment engineer and sustains gross margin above 65%. That single result would establish scarce pricing, a real venture moment, purchase-causing workflow ownership, and a path that does not require a capital factory.
