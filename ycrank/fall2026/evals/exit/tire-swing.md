# Tire Swing -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to EliseAI is the strongest supported route. The memo documents that EliseAI prequalifies tenants during leasing outreach. Tire Swing says its software handles the next step by extracting income and asset data, applying program rules, and assembling certifications for staff approval. Owning that workflow could help EliseAI sell a more complete leasing product. The founders report dozens of live properties across eight states, which suggests the workflow can serve multiple programs. The memo gives no measured customer savings, buyer interest, or transaction prices that would establish what EliseAI might pay. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

A proposed 12–18 month milestone is to show across multiple paying operators that Tire Swing cuts staff time per completed certification without increasing audit exceptions, and that those operators renew. The reported deployments give the team a starting point. The memo gives no runway or test budget for reaching that milestone. A company sale is plausible, with customer contract terms, IP ownership, tenant-data permissions, and third-party processing rights still to verify. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale cases below are illustrative assumptions. Both assume an eligible holder sells the full stake, receives 3% pro rata after net debt and deal adjustments, and gets cash at closing within eight years. Actual holder rights and the preference waterfall are unknown.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|-----------|-----------------------------|----------------------|
| Modest sale | $45M enterprise value less $5M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $40M distributable | $1.20M (2.4×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M distributable | $2.70M (5.4×). If only 90% of the stake can be sold on those terms, cash falls to $2.43M (4.86×). |
| Prolonged private | The company continues operating and reinvests available cash | $0 realized within eight years |

The memo provides no basis to value either sale scenario and no evidence of an independent route that would pay this holder. The gap is whether a strategic buyer would pay enough for the workflow to clear the holder’s hurdle.

### Countercase

A buyer may be able to build or partner for the workflow at lower cost. Yardi and ExactEstate already bundle affordable housing certification with property management, and Roost markets overlapping AI certification features. Tire Swing reports thousands of compliance checks, but the memo has no retention or comparative performance data showing that its implementation would be costly to replicate. That weakens the case for an acquisition premium. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible price ceiling below the proceeds hurdle, so the Pass gate remains open. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

### Decisive diligence question

Would owning Tire Swing’s certification workflow create enough incremental profit or avoid enough build cost for EliseAI to pay a price that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Buyer-side feedback tying a credible price range to measured operator savings and renewal data would move the action to Reach out if the range supports $2.5 million in holder cash after deal adjustments and the waterfall. Evidence that even the strongest credible route falls materially short would move the action to Pass.
