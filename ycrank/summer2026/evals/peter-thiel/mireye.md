# Mireye -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Mireye’s contested truth is that physical-world agents will not obtain their advantage from better reasoning alone; they will require a provenance-linked data layer that converts fragmented public records into typed, machine-usable facts. What would prove this wrong? If data-center developers can reproduce the same workflow through Mapbox, CARTO, Fused, or direct model browsing without materially worse accuracy, latency, or cost. The reported 100-times-faster sourcing of off-market data-center land is an encouraging test of the proposition, but it is a customer testimonial, not yet independent proof that Mireye owns a secret rather than packaging an inconvenience.

The narrow beachhead is better than the broad phrase “infrastructure for physical-world AI agents.” Data-center developers have costly site-selection questions, fragmented government sources, and enough transaction value to justify specialized tooling. Mireye has integrated 85 sources, exposes field-level URLs, timestamps, and confidence values, and has grown its catalog to more than 300 fields at 20% week over week. Yet catalog breadth is not monopoly. Accepted field requests may compound into reusable infrastructure, but no request volume, retention, exclusive data, or declining fulfillment cost demonstrates that loop. Meanwhile, Mapbox already offers a unified MCP endpoint, CARTO operates agentic GIS over warehouse data, and Fused supplies programmable geospatial workflows. The market presently looks competitive; Mireye has only outlined the mechanism by which it might escape competition.

The strongest bull case is that the company is quietly building the missing data substrate beneath physical AI. It fits the unstated movement from standalone software toward the complete AI production stack, and provenance matters more—not less—when agents act on land, energy, insurance, and industrial decisions. Chokshi has also built and sold before: Poker Pit reached $250,000 ARR in four months before acquisition. His manual search for 50 prospects and iteration to a 10% outbound response rate show definite action rather than the fashionable incantation of product-led growth.

But the economics currently contradict the ambition. The public plans charge $19 or $99 per month, with overages of only $1 per 1,000 credits. Those prices can support developer adoption, but the dossier gives no paid-customer count, enterprise contract, gross margin, or evidence that one revenue engine can produce a multibillion-dollar outcome. Mireye could become a useful feature layer while Mapbox or another incumbent captures the durable value. My 36-month base case is that it pivots into a higher-priced data-center site-selection platform and reaches $1 million to $3 million ARR; remaining a horizontal API at present pricing would more likely produce acquisition than monopoly.

The founding structure is also unresolved at precisely the point where ambiguity is dangerous. YC reports a team size of two but lists only Chokshi as an active founder, and there is no public evidence about the second person, prior shared work, full-time status, ownership, or whether all core engineering is internal. This is missing evidence, not proof of a defective foundation, so it receives the stated prior rather than a penalty. Still, neither the founder’s prior exit nor YC affiliation overrides the absence of founding-team evidence.

No known exception resolves these gaps. The 100-times workflow improvement resembles a zero-to-one result, but unlike the verified breakthrough that justified overlooking uncertain monopoly economics at OpenAI, Mireye’s claim is neither independently measured nor shown to persist across customers. I would not pass outright because the data-center wedge, provenance architecture, catalog growth, and outbound response constitute real signals. I would investigate, not invest today. The verifiable flip condition is twenty paying data-center customers at more than $25,000 annual contract value, with twelve-month retention and evidence that reused requested fields materially reduce fulfillment cost; that would connect the secret, monopoly mechanism, and power-law revenue engine.
