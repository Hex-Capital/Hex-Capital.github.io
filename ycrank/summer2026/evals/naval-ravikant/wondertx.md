# WonderTx -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The strongest evidence is not the founders’ résumés. It is that they have already converted years inside AI drug discovery into experimentally confirmed binders against four targets with no on-target training data. Abraham Heifets co-founded Atomwise, which raised more than $220 million and announced over $5 billion in partnership value; Saulo de Oliveira worked across structural biology, cheminformatics, and machine learning; Cameron Ferroni built technical platforms at Microsoft and later operated at Atomwise. All three worked together before WonderTx. This is specific knowledge revealed through repeated action in the exact problem, not credential theater.

The extrapolative model matters only if its advantage survives another laboratory copying the architecture. WonderTx has more than code: similarity-aware partitions and gradient matching feed an automated design-make-test loop, while four structurally diverse targets have produced physical binder evidence ranging from screening hits to crystallographic structures. That gives the company a proprietary model plus experimental feedback substrate. It does not yet prove a compounding moat. Dataset size, exclusivity, patents, and clinical assets are undisclosed, while Terray already reports more than five billion measured interactions and Isomorphic Labs has raised $2.7 billion. The strongest bear case is that extrapolation proves to be a benchmark technique rather than an owned advantage, leaving better-capitalized competitors to absorb the method.

The wedge is unusually valuable if it works: convert biologically validated targets served by injected drugs into conventional oral small molecules. The same capability could extend across many targets and diseases, giving the team room to maneuver beyond any first program. But the claimed $200 billion market lacks a base year, methodology, or serviceable-market calculation. Four binders establish technical breadth; they do not establish oral bioavailability, selectivity, safety, manufacturability, or therapeutic efficacy. Discovery has crossed one boundary, while most of the drug remains unproven.

WonderTx does not fit my permissionless-network pattern. Pharmaceutical partners do not yet create utility or distribution for one another, and the automated loop compounds company-owned knowledge rather than customer-created value. The relevant known-exception pattern is a demonstrated prototype built by an unusually capital-efficient team: three people producing four validated target results is enough to waive the missing network at pre-seed. It is not enough to waive both the missing network and missing commercial proof indefinitely.

The platform has reportedly been used on internal and partner programs, which is more informative than an untouched research prototype, but no partner is named and no price, revenue, take rate, or deal structure is public. Direct value capture is plausible through partnerships and owned therapeutics; it is not proven. I see no disclosed unfair transaction, artificial deadline, or founder-behavior red flag, but silence on integrity and round dynamics earns no affirmative credit.

I would write a small, option-sized check. My base case is that by August 2029 WonderTx has disclosed at least one paid pharmaceutical partnership and nominated one orally bioavailable lead candidate, but has not yet produced a clinical-stage asset. The verifiable flip condition is technical: I pass if none of the four validated target programs produces, within 18 months, a disclosed compound combining cellular potency, selectivity, and oral pharmacokinetics. Binders are evidence of intelligence; drugs are evidence of leverage.
