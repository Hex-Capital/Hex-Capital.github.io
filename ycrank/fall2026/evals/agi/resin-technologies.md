# Resin Technologies -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is Resin’s strongest path. Resin says its browser-based tools turn requirements into editable schematics, generate missing component footprints from datasheets, and route boards against project rules. The founder reports that unnamed teams shipped boards in hours versus an estimated weeks. That comparison describes elapsed time and leaves engineering hours, board complexity, and fabrication outcomes unverified. Resin’s Fall 2026 YC backing and listed convertible note make seed-stage entry plausible. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

**Scenario assumption:** A small hardware team starts making low-volume board revisions it previously deferred because the engineering time made them uneconomic. That market opens only if Resin reduces total labor per fabrication-ready board at equivalent quality, including review, corrections, retries, and any redesign after fabrication. The company-described routing engine and reusable component libraries give teams a plausible reason to prefer Resin when models are equally capable. Paid preference under comparable functionality remains untested. The memo identifies no scarce input Resin controls that would support a separate Path A case. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The founder’s report is an early usage signal, with payment, buyer commitment, and repeat use unknown. The founders’ prior work in applied AI deployment and electronics design supports their ability to run a representative board trial. For scale, a three-seat Pro workspace would generate $347 per month at advertised rates, assuming all seats pay list price. The memo reports a conflicting price schedule and gives no realized pricing or cost per workspace for inference, retries, and support. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea)

### Countercase

Resin could speed up work existing PCB teams already do while leaving previously deferred boards uneconomic. Resin warns that its beta autorouter can produce wiring, component, or clearance errors and requires engineering review before fabrication. Cadence offers constraint-driven routing, Quilter works with existing design files, and Resin’s editable imports and exports could make switching easier. Those facts make it plausible that review absorbs the labor savings and that buyers can get comparable results in tools they already use. Neither failure has been demonstrated. Both Path B conditions remain open, which supports Watch and leaves the Reach out gate unmet. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent hardware teams pay Resin for a second board revision they previously would have deferred when they can use a competing workflow with equally capable models and comparable design functionality? A paid comparison should track total engineering hours through fabrication acceptance, including review, retries, and rework. Repeat paid use at equivalent quality, with enough labor savings to make those revisions viable and a demonstrated buyer preference for Resin, would warrant rescoring for Reach out. A representative trial that rules out either condition would move the action to Pass.
