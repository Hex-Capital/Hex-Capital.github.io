# Proto -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Proto’s strongest path. A design or coding platform could buy Proto to own the workflow that lets designers compare working variants, link them into user flows, and share them for review. Proto says designers have moved into developer IDEs because canvas tools lag their AI workflows. The memo records Proto’s early-access launch and Figma’s addition of code-backed prototype comparison to its own canvas. Figma’s investment in that feature shows the workflow matters to a potential buyer. The case for buying Proto depends on whether its shared review history and handoff to engineering would take materially longer to reproduce than to acquire. That advantage is an inference. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The seed milestone is paid use across multiple production projects, with teams returning to shared flows and getting prototype changes through engineering review faster than with incumbent tools. Early access and Albert’s prior work on a visual coding environment make a pilot plausible. Launch attention provides a way to recruit pilot teams. The memo gives no paid usage, repeat-use results, pilot budget, or runway. It also leaves IP ownership and transfer terms unverified, and supplies no independent path to cash for this holder. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The proceeds test uses the thesis’s illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation. Initial ownership of 5% falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year target is $2.5 million in gross cash, or 5× total checks. Under simple pro rata allocation before investor taxes, that takes $83.3 million of distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. Proto’s investment terms and buyer-supported valuation are unknown. (The Idea, Key Facts)

For illustration, the sale cases assume zero net enterprise-to-equity adjustments and a pro rata waterfall with no preference adjustments. Equity consideration is paid in cash at close, with $0 each in stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation. The prices and dates are scenario assumptions.

### Countercase

Figma already offers code-backed prototype comparison where design teams work, and Onlook offers open-source visual editing that writes back to code. Proto’s handoff could also shift work into engineering review, eroding the value of faster prototyping. A platform could therefore build or integrate enough of the workflow to cap what it would pay for Proto. The overlap is concrete. Whether those substitutes cover the full workflow well enough to cap a bid below the hurdle remains open. The evidence warrants Watch, with the structural Pass override untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid, multi-project pilot show a potential platform buyer that owning Proto is worth at least the price needed to deliver $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds under the modeled terms? Buyer-validated evidence of faster engineering handoff versus its own tools, paired with a build-versus-buy estimate that supports that price, would move the action to Reach out.
