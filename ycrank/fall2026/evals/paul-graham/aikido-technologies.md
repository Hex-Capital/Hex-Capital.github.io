# Aikido Technologies -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Aikido Technologies has done something more convincing than describe an offshore platform. It built a quarter-scale system and demonstrated its upending operation in front of ABS, which issued a statement of maturity in February 2025 ([ABS](https://ww2.eagle.org/en/newsdetail.ff14c0e2.html)). That is real engineering evidence. But the founder question remains unanswered: what went wrong, what did Kanner, Yu, or Reeves do about it, and how quickly did their action fix it? The dossier establishes that these founders can build difficult hardware. It does not document the response to resistance that would make me invest in the people.

The strongest argument for writing a check is that this team may already possess the difficult part. Kanner led R&D at Principle Power; Yu led hydrodynamics analysis there; Reeves designed helicopters ([company biographies](https://www.aikidotechnologies.com/about)). Two founders have worked together before. Their proposed combination of wind generation, batteries, accessible data halls, and hull-based cooling addresses concrete constraints on terrestrial datacenters. Much of the equipment already exists. Aikido could be assembling an overlooked configuration rather than waiting for several scientific discoveries. Offshore installation and servicing are also exactly the sort of unpleasant work that can conceal an opportunity. Their prior work earns partial credit for encountering that difficulty, although it does not establish how they discovered the datacenter need.

The distinction that matters is between demonstrating the platform and demonstrating the business. The witnessed test concerned floating wind. The datacenter retrofit remains planned, and PUE below 1.08 remains a projection ([technology page](https://www.aikidotechnologies.com/technology)). Four-plus hours of batteries do not by themselves establish continuous compute availability through variable wind and marine servicing. Aikido fits my pattern of technical teams producing an inspectable artifact before making a larger claim. But that artifact currently supports the floating structure more strongly than the offshore compute proposition. This is also why the proof-based exceptions do not yet override my founder gate: the independently witnessed result leaves the new product’s central operating question unresolved.

Who wants this badly enough to use the first awkward version? Aikido reports inference-customer interest and discussions for a UK site targeting 2028, but the dossier identifies no customer repeatedly running workloads or paying for capacity ([March 2026 announcement](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/03/3248183/0/en/Aikido-Launches-Offshore-Datacenter-Technology-to-Deploy-GW-scale-Sovereign-AI-Compute-Onboard-Their-Floating-Wind-Platform.html)). That earns the absent-demand default, without treating silence as rejection. The $4 million seed round and NVIDIA Inception membership cannot answer the user question. Nor does the March expansion establish an evidence-driven pivot: there is no documented rejected hypothesis or customer observation behind it. I see no evidence of the launch-dependent or partner-dependent distribution red flags, but their absence supplies no affirmative demand evidence.

I would pass on Aikido Technologies today. The objection that stings is that waiting could mean missing capable marine engineers turning an existing platform into scarce powered capacity. Their hardware demonstration makes that possibility credible. Still, resourcefulness remains at its missing-evidence default, below my required founder threshold. My flip condition is one documented paid offshore compute pilot in which the founders overcome a named blocking failure within weeks and the customer continues running workloads afterward. That single episode would connect the engineering to both founder behavior and an actual need.
