# Elithia -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Agency-led sales is Elithia’s strongest hypergrowth path. Medico-legal agencies could buy a standard workflow and roll it out to multiple physician experts, who reconstruct medical histories and sign off on chronologies case after case. The memo describes an editable chronology with source-page links, Word and PDF export, and organization-specific templates. Those features make it plausible that the same workflow could serve buyers in a second market through template and hosting changes. Buyer geography is unreported, and U.S. business associate agreement terms remain unconfirmed where HIPAA applies. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Elithia reports two agency contracts, sales to physician experts and medico-legal firms, and more than 70 experts using the product on live cases. Those are early signs of workflow commitment. The memo does not connect the live cases to either agency’s rollout or show how an agency moves from contract to paid use. It also gives no account-level repeat usage or marginal delivery costs. Even if first-segment fit strengthens, the first link at risk is the handoff from an agency sale to a standardized rollout across its reviewers. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The contracts and live-case count could reflect founder-assisted evaluations that require substantial setup or correction on each rollout. The only quantified time saving in the memo, from nearly a week to about two days, comes from the founder’s account of his mother’s work. The free first 1,000 pages also leave paid conversion unclear. Expert sign-off is part of the customer’s workflow. The cost risk is whether Elithia itself must repeatedly intervene to deliver a usable chronology. The memo provides no affirmative evidence of persistent bespoke delivery or proportional costs, so the structural downside gate does not apply. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can both contracted agencies independently onboard physician reviewers to paid live cases using the same setup and training process? Rollout records showing paid case completion and limited Elithia setup and support time at both agencies would move the action to Reach out. If both rollouts instead show that each new reviewer requires bespoke Elithia work and the records show no reusable handoff, the action would move to Pass.
