# Perceptual Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic sale to an AI lab with editable model weights. Perceptual proposes targeted weight edits with an audit trail. A lab could have a reason to own that capability if it reliably reduces post-training cost or turnaround time while preserving unrelated model behavior. The memo documents an early-access waitlist. The founders report cost and performance gains from Bryel Foundry’s earlier custom training work, which suggests the team could run a production pilot. The economics of the current weight-editing product remain unmeasured in the memo. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale scenarios below assume $5 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, no further senior or preferred claims, and an eligible holder selling its entire stake for cash by year eight.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Cash to holder |
|-----------|-----------------------------|----------------|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M = $45M | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $5M = $95M | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private | Operating cash is reinvested, with no share purchase or distribution | $0 |

These are sensitivities. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction evidence that supports either sale price. A production pilot is a plausible milestone given the team’s prior training work. Pilot funding, transferable rights, and holder eligibility remain unverified, and the memo offers no evidence of an independent payout route. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A lab with editable weights could build internally using published methods. The memo also reports that frontier agents failed WeightBench’s patching tasks under its test constraints. If Perceptual’s edits produce regressions in production, a lab could continue using its existing post-training workflow. The prior Bryel engagements involved custom training, so conversion to the new product remains uncertain. These risks leave the strategic case conditional. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported sale price that would trigger the Pass gate. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can one prospective acquirer validate enough economic value from Perceptual’s weight-editing workflow to support a price that clears the holder’s hurdle? A paid production pilot on weights the lab has permission to edit would move the action to Reach out if it shows repeatable savings against the lab’s current workflow, passes agreed regression tests, and gives the lab a credible economic rationale for a price above the hurdle.
