# Rindler -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Michael Serrano is the named CEO of a two-founder company, while Rindler is selling pure software abstraction at $1 per successful session. That pairing is Rindler’s signature: unusually clear leadership wrapped around a decidedly ordinary, crowded AI-infrastructure bet. I can identify the tiebreaker, but I cannot touch the artifact or find the autobiographical scar that made Serrano need to build it. Rindler has Tiz, perhaps Biz, and no dossier evidence yet of the Rizz that makes me follow a founder before I understand the market.

Anduril is the high rung because Palmer Luckey’s authority was inseparable from the product: one founder, one conviction, one person carrying the consequences. Rindler shares the visible part of that mechanism—Serrano is explicitly Founder and CEO while Arthur De Los Santos is Founder—but Rindler discloses no equity split, majority operating authority, vesting arrangement, or conflict protocol. A title is a useful signal, not a tiebreaker agreement. Rindler sits below Anduril because I know who occupies the chair, but not whether the chair has legs.

The consensus YC AI-agent darling fails when credentialed founders discover a hot market in a memo and build an abstraction another polymath can reproduce within a quarter. Rindler carries that failure pattern: two MIT/CSAIL founders, an MCP wrapper launched into a market containing Browserbase, Browser Use, Kernel, and Firecrawl, with no physical or regulated moat. Rindler does handle authenticated banking and registry workflows, but it remains software riding Kernel with Browserbase as fallback—the suppliers can climb upward into Rindler’s layer more easily than Rindler can climb downward into their infrastructure. This is not SpaceX-hard or Mindbloom-uncomfortable. It is a well-engineered booth in a crowded bazaar.

Michael Serrano’s Roblox machine-learning work and LLM research, combined with Arthur De Los Santos’s vision-language navigation research, can compound into better mapping and recovery over the next 18 months. Every failed login gate, dynamic control, and CAPTCHA could teach Rindler how to make the next authenticated workflow more reliable. But technical adjacency is not lived experience: the dossier gives no moment when Serrano or De Los Santos personally suffered through the ATS, bank-export, or state-registry work Rindler now automates. Without that origin, Rindler risks optimizing whichever workflow asks next rather than developing the founder-shaped instinct that tells it which failures matter.

Browserbase has raised $67.5 million, Browser Use $17 million, Kernel $22 million, and Firecrawl $16.2 million, so Rindler is undercapitalized but not non-consensus. The score flips if Rindler can show that sophisticated investors rejected authenticated actions as legally radioactive or operationally impossible while named customers repeatedly pay $1,000 monthly because Rindler solved them anyway. Rindler currently offers company-reported benchmarks of 3x fewer failures, 4x faster completion, and 6x lower cost, but publishes no customer count, mapping count, revenue, or independent reliability history. Magically weird requires someone credible to laugh before the magic works; here, everyone already agrees agents need better browsers.

Niantic made software physically verifiable because I could walk outside, open Ingress, and watch the world acquire another layer. Rindler’s output is observable to a developer, but a hosted MCP endpoint returning typed records is still several steps removed from ordinary human experience. Rindler’s free chat application helps, and a user can watch Rindler export a bank record or complete an ATS action, yet the artifact remains a developer abstraction whose success is largely asserted through server logs. I can use it, but I cannot encounter it unexpectedly in the world.

Rindler’s $1-per-successful-session model leads me to a falsifiable 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Rindler will have less than $2 million ARR and will be acquired by Browserbase primarily for its typed-mapping and authenticated-session technology. Rindler’s clear CEO can execute a useful product, but per-site mapping labor, absent certifications, and supplier dependence compress the independent-company outcome. I’m never playing the game I seem to be playing: Rindler looks like a platform, but the current evidence describes a valuable feature layer awaiting vertical integration.

ATS workflows, major-bank read/export flows, and state-registry lookups already running in production form the strongest bull case. Rindler could be a DeepMind-style exception: Serrano and De Los Santos may be polymaths whose MIT physics, LLM, computer-vision, and navigation work compounds beyond the anti-pedigree heuristic, while verified mappings accumulate into a proprietary failure-resolution network. That case stings because 3x fewer failures and 6x lower cost would matter enormously if independently reproduced. It does not yet win: Rindler names no paying customer, holds no SOC 2, HIPAA, or PCI certification, and depends on Kernel and Browserbase for core execution. I would flip from Pass to Invest if Rindler reaches $1 million ARR from at least ten named customers while independently demonstrating above 95% successful completion across 100 authenticated site mappings.
