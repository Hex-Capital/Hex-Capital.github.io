# Databuddy -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** The strongest case is that startups without a data analyst could routinely investigate conversion drops and trace launches to paying customers. The prior workflow requires someone to reconcile traffic, product events, errors, and payments across tools. Databuddy says Databunny queries those data together, exposes the underlying query for review, and can send scheduled investigations to Slack. The market opens if teams that previously skipped these investigations can afford to run them routinely and get reliable answers. (The Idea)

**Scenario assumption:** For a team that now investigates only urgent drops, compare the full cost per correct, actionable diagnosis at the same cadence. Count manual reconciliation and validation in the old workflow. For Databuddy, count the subscription, setup and maintenance, human review, retries, and manual handling of ambiguous results. The listed plans with scheduled investigations cost $299 and $799 per month. Self-serve, event-metered pricing provides a plausible expansion model, while contribution per account still depends on event processing, inference, and support costs that the memo does not quantify. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The November snapshot records 38 active subscriptions and $3,345 in company-wide MRR. It does not identify buyers who pay for repeated Databunny investigations or previously went without them. Solo founder Issa Nassar’s prior analytics work and the launch of the original tracker support his ability to ship. Reliable investigation across customer datasets remains the critical execution milestone. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team)

### Countercase

Databuddy may make existing analytics work faster without opening a new market. The memo’s named customer reports switching from Framer Analytics, so that example establishes adoption by an existing analytics user and leaves the investigation use case unresolved. Under model parity, Mixpanel’s anomaly monitoring and PostHog’s agent access give buyers a way to use intelligence on data they already collect. Databuddy’s configured event schemas, funnels, and investigation history could make its workflow worth keeping, though the memo provides no retention or buyer comparison that demonstrates this. Human review of misleading correlations could also absorb the labor savings. These risks leave both Path B gates open for testing and support Watch. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will startups without analysts that previously skipped routine conversion investigations renew at a price that covers delivery costs after trying an equivalent-model workflow on their existing analytics stack? A paid repeat-use pilot would justify **Reach out** if it shows lower full cost per correct, actionable diagnosis than manual reconciliation, sustained use at the new cadence, and renewals tied to a benefit buyers experience in Databuddy’s workflow. Stay on **Watch** if either comparison is inconclusive. Move to **Pass** if a representative pilot shows that review erases the labor savings or that buyers readily switch to an equivalent workflow.
