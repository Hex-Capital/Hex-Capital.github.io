# Verdun -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032: a property owner should be able to specify what matters at a site and receive an intervention when it matters, without employing someone to watch a queue of waving trees. Verdun’s contextual reasoning and site protocols suggest a route there. But two transitions remain unproven: reliable interpretation must become economical, and interpretation must produce timely action. The October 2026 dossier describes the architecture; it does not show an operating artifact demonstrating that those transitions have begun. I can imagine the future more clearly than I can locate Verdun’s foothold in it.

Why does a security alert wait more than 20 minutes? Verdun says nuisance alarms overwhelm human reviewers. That is a specific institutional failure: the customer buys protection, but the monitoring workflow consumes the time in which protection would be useful. Filtering before escalation could return that time to property operators and let guards concentrate on actual incidents. This earns more credit than calling security “broken.” Still, the dossier does not establish who captures an excessive margin, what customers currently pay, or whether Verdun transfers meaningful control to them. A faster monitoring contractor could repair the workflow while leaving its economics largely intact.

Tai and Hoang are the strongest reason to take this seriously. They previously worked together at Actuate; Hoang built its first gun-detection model, and Verdun’s leadership materials attribute more than 110,000 cameras across 15,000 sites to that earlier company. Those are prior operating accomplishments, not Verdun customers. They nevertheless establish something much more useful than impressive degrees: these founders have already encountered physical-security deployment. Tai’s explicit concern about ingestion costs and negative inference economics also suggests respect for the machinery hidden behind the demo. The missing founder evidence is a dated trail of independently pursued obsession and first-believer recruitment, rather than domain competence.

The strongest objection to passing is that experienced operators can recognize the next architecture before outsiders can measure it. My Flock Safety exception is relevant: both opportunities involve extracting useful security information from cameras, but that investment followed recognition of a concrete technical capability; Verdun currently offers descriptions of a capability that Hakimo already markets. Pre-revenue would not stop me if a working prototype supplied that missing conviction. Neither would an unglamorous service business: repairing an irritating workflow fits my unstated patterns. What I cannot establish here is the concealed enabling asset beneath the service. The proposed operator-feedback loop might become one, but its scale, ownership, and downstream dependencies remain hypotheses.

Two engineering vacancies and YC participation do not show customers changing their behavior. There is no documented repeated use, payment, or operational reliance on Verdun, so product behavior receives the absent-evidence default. Likewise, a CV/VLM cascade does not by itself establish control over a scarce bottleneck. I also cannot clear my privacy gate from this dossier: consent, footage custody, and retention architecture are unspecified. That is an unresolved question, not evidence of severe abuse. CEO and CTO accountability are identified; there is no affirmative evidence of equal-control deadlock, concealed leadership, or dependence on one closed model supplier.

I pass with my own money today. Verdun has founders who plausibly understand the problem better than its public evidence demonstrates the solution. My verifiable flip condition is one independently audited, paid 90-day deployment showing materially faster verified intervention than the customer’s incumbent at positive contribution margin. That would connect the engineering to the purchased outcome. Until then, the founder history carries the bet while the product’s distinctive advantage remains unestablished.
