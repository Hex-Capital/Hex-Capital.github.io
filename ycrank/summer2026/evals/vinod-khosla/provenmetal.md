# ProvenMetal -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Johnny Doyle and Will Carkner previously worked together on Syncra before pivoting to ProvenMetal, while ProvenMetal now coordinates domestic PCB production without owning the factories. That pairing is the signature: unusually credible learning-rate evidence attached to a required-capital profile too small for my edge. ProvenMetal may become a useful manufacturing business. Useful is not the same as “too important not to do.”

**Slash (2024)** is the high rung on founder learning rate because its documented pivot exposed rapid updating and produced an entirely new market thesis. ProvenMetal shares the pivot mechanism: Doyle and Carkner moved from Syncra, and ProvenMetal evolved from a benchtop X-ray inspection concept into managed PCB fabrication and assembly. The divergence matters. Slash’s pivot had a visible causal story and market validation; ProvenMetal provides neither the reasoning behind its change nor evidence that five LOIs from more than 30 conversations validate the present service rather than the earlier inspection product. I credit the update, but not yet the learning loop.

**The generic GPT-4 sales-call summarizer** is the low rung on required capital because modest software economics make my capital irrelevant. ProvenMetal exhibits the physical-world version of that failure mechanism: ProvenMetal promises a seven-day turn by routing fabrication and assembly through vetted U.S. partners rather than financing an owned plant, automated line, or new manufacturing process. Tempo Automation raised $74.6 million around an owned factory footprint; ProvenMetal’s disclosed roadmap contains no comparable capital milestone. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself” does not mean inventing a need for my check.

America’s PCB-production share falling from roughly 30% to 4% gives ProvenMetal a real consequence mechanism, but only if ProvenMetal rebuilds capacity rather than brokers existing capacity faster. Over 18 months, revision histories, supplier-performance data, and repeat orders could compound into a domestic production control layer for drones, defense, and robotics. Today the seven-day promise reallocates orders among partners; it does not make JLCPCB’s 21-million-order infrastructure disappear. Supply-chain resilience is important. A faster procurement layer inside the same constrained system is still an optimization play.

MacroFab’s network of more than 100 North American factories establishes the technical counterfactual. ProvenMetal would move up a tier if its 99.8% inspection-confidence claim came from proprietary automated inspection, if its production records measurably predicted defects, or if ProvenMetal could demonstrate that software scheduling creates capacity competitors cannot access. The dossier provides no methodology for the 99.8%, no structural barrier, and no proprietary process. The present miracles are supplier availability, partner quality, and seven-day coordination. Those are operational dependencies, not falsifiable scientific breakthroughs.

**DoorDash (2013)** is the relevant single-anchor parallel on founder defiance: no science miracle, but an ambitious reordering of commerce made the capital-light exception worthwhile. Doyle’s IoT manufacturing experience and Carkner’s Lumindt PCB-testing work give ProvenMetal genuine earned insight, while choosing domestic electronics production against two decades of offshoring is mildly contrarian. ProvenMetal has not yet articulated the DoorDash-scale economic mechanism or publicly contradicted PCB orthodoxy; the proposition remains “American, traceable, and faster,” not “the existing production model becomes obsolete.”

Five LOIs from more than 30 conversations lead to my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, ProvenMetal will have pivoted explicitly into defense-focused PCB procurement and traceability, reaching $3 million to $6 million in annualized gross revenue without owning fabrication capacity. ProvenMetal’s demonstrated willingness to change direction supports the pivot; its partner-dependent seven-day service and low required-capital profile constrain the outcome. That can be a good business. It is not yet a Commonwealth Fusion-shaped distribution of outcomes.

JLCPCB’s 21 million annual orders make the strongest opposing case sting: a domestic challenger does not need new physics if defense demand, geopolitical pressure, and process data create a trusted network that compounds like DoorDash, while ProvenMetal’s five LOIs and founder-market fit provide the first signal. ProvenMetal could also repeat the Slash exception, where learning rate overwhelms modest consequence. The evidence that wins today is zero disclosed paid conversions, no structural moat, and an unverified 99.8% quality claim attached to partner-controlled production. I would flip to Invest if ProvenMetal reaches at least $5 million annualized revenue from 20 or more repeat customers while publishing order-level evidence that its inspection system cuts defects or turnaround by at least 50% against MacroFab.
