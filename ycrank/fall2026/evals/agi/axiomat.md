# Axiomat -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the stronger path. Axiomat’s November 2026 launch and product demo are evidence of early execution, and its San Francisco base and Fall 2026 YC backing make seed entry plausible. The company describes a workflow that shows users how it interpreted a business question and uses Lean to verify that the generated query matches the formal definition before execution. If cheaper models reduce query drafting costs and approved definitions can be reused, finance teams could run more decision-grade pricing checks than they can economically review by hand. That is a scenario assumption. The memo has no matched-quality labor comparison or evidence that buyers have taken up those additional checks. It also identifies no separately controlled bottleneck for Path A. (Axiomat, The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The customer rule-approval workflow gives Axiomat a possible basis for switching costs if teams come to rely on a library of approved definitions. Buyer preference under functional parity remains untested. The memo records demo availability and outreach but gives no buyer commitments, repeat use, or revenue signal. The founders’ experience in formal verification, applied ML, and real-time control software supports their ability to build the product. The economics of onboarding the next customer remain unknown, especially the labor required to formalize, approve, and maintain customer-specific rules. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A valid proof can certify a query against the wrong business definition. Rule approval, retries, and manual handling of abstentions could consume enough analyst time to erase the savings per correct, usable answer. Even if the labor savings hold, Snowflake’s semantic views and approved SQL examples may give buyers sufficient assurance inside their existing warehouse. The memo establishes that those alternatives exist. It leaves their adequacy for Axiomat’s target workflows untested. Either outcome would close Path B. Both remain unresolved at seed, which supports Watch. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent finance teams repeatedly pay Axiomat to run pricing checks they previously skipped because manual review cost too much, when a warehouse-native alternative using a comparably capable model delivers equivalent answer quality and governance? A paid production trial should compare the same checks across the manual baseline, Axiomat, and the alternative. Measure human time per correct, usable answer, including rule setup and approval, retries, abstentions, and manual fallback. Then observe which product buyers choose to keep paying for. Repeat paid use at a newly viable frequency and sustained preference under parity would support Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 with at least medium confidence. If review labor erases the savings or buyers readily switch under parity, move to Pass.
