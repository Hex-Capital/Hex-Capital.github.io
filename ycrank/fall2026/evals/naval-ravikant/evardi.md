# Evardi -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Shah’s work in Kenya Power grid operations matters more than her Berkeley credentials. Utility integrations at Autodesk and the January 2026 mini-grid product give Evardi a credible foundation in knowledge acquired through action. Borele brings additional energy-engineering experience. But the dossier does not establish which production systems the founders personally shipped or what outcomes those systems delivered. I give substantial founder credit without treating self-reported acquisitions as verified exits.

Evardi’s central question is what remains after a competitor copies the software. A utility-specific network model, reconciled against SCADA and embedded in engineering decisions, could retain value beyond its implementation. The documented architecture earns some credit. Exclusive data rights, accumulated operational learning and demonstrated switching costs remain unproven. The published results—61% inspection hit rate versus 6% from billing flags, and 42% fewer unsafe switching actions—come from simulations on public data ([case studies](https://evardi.ai/case-studies)). They support testing the product. They do not establish a proprietary asset that compounds through deployment.

Each utility currently appears to supply its own data and receive its own model. The dossier shows no mechanism by which one customer improves another customer’s product or brings the next customer into the system. Evardi therefore does not yet express my preference for permissionless contribution or tools whose users create reusable value for one another. Nor does it aggregate fragmented supply into a market. That is a limitation of the demonstrated business mechanism, not an objection to selling utility software.

The strongest bull case is that transformer visibility becomes the trusted foundation for switching, maintenance and eventually capacity planning. Those workflows could share the same underlying model. But the independent market evidence establishes a broader digital-twin category—$1.2 billion in 2026—not Evardi’s access to adjacent budgets. Neara already competes around infrastructure modelling. Meanwhile, the claimed footprint of utilities serving 10 million customers describes their service territories; it establishes neither Evardi’s revenue nor deployment depth. Negotiated licensing is inferred, and no price, completed transaction or revenue is disclosed.

I would keep Evardi under observation, but I would not write a check today. My exceptions for exceptional founder execution, unusual prototypes or emerging communication networks do not supply the missing evidence here: the founder signal is credible, while extraordinary execution and customer-created compounding remain unestablished. There is no documented integrity violation, financing pressure or reassurance dependence; the earlier product change alone does not establish wavering conviction. My one flip condition is a referenceable paid renewal at a second utility demonstrating that an exclusively reusable model learned from the first utility’s operating feedback and measurably improved the second deployment. That would connect domain knowledge to a business that becomes harder to reproduce with use.
