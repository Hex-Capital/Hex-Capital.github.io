# Theseus Robotics -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

The strongest path is **New Economies**. Theseus could make reliable overnight replenishment viable for grocers that currently leave some shifts or aisles uncovered because staffing them costs too much. Today, store staff unload cartons and stock shelves by hand. Theseus says overnight shifts are hard to staff, but the memo gives neither the manual cost per correctly shelved unit nor an example of a grocer that forgoes coverage because of that cost. The memo reports two robots deployed across two stores and describes Zürich launch footage. The company’s reported peak of 400 units restocked per hour supports the feasibility of the task in an existing store. Full-shift throughput, human intervention time, and the cost per correctly shelved unit remain unmeasured in the memo. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

In a plausible scenario, one remote operator covers exceptions across several robots, reducing total human time per correctly shelved unit. Even with comparable manipulation models available, a grocer could still pay Theseus to integrate store-specific planograms, keep the fleet running, and resolve exceptions. Theseus trains on in-house demonstrations and can capture operator corrections, though cross-store transfer and a resulting service advantage remain unproven. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The expansion unit is one robot on a monthly fee. Buyer economics depend on the fee and remaining store labor per correctly shelved unit versus the cost of manual coverage at the same service level. Theseus’s contribution depends on whether the fee covers hardware financing or depreciation, maintenance, deployment, computing, and remote supervision. Daytime picking could spread those costs over more productive hours if stores use both services. The memo gives no fee amount or sustained utilization data. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Theseus may automate work grocers already buy while shifting much of the labor to remote operators. Its hardware is off the shelf, HIVE is already piloting overnight replenishment, and the memo documents no cross-store learning advantage or retailer preference under comparable performance. The two store deployments show operational interest, while the company’s claim of pilots for up to 10 additional robots in 2027 leaves payment and repeat demand open. The evidence leaves both required conditions unproven. A paid field test can resolve them, so the action is Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
