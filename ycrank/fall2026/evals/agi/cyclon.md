# Cyclon -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path B — New Economies. Cyclon says its OS agent can run tasks across signed-in Android apps and build and test personal tools from a request. The market-opening hypothesis is that users could automate recurring tasks they currently defer or leave undone because performing them manually or building a custom tool takes too much time. The memo’s contract-sharing sequence illustrates the workflow. To test the economics, compare the old delivery cost per successful task with Cyclon’s cost after setup, review, retries, manual identity checks, and error correction. The memo provides no field measurements of those costs or evidence that users complete previously skipped tasks more often. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Under model parity, users might still pay for dependable execution across their apps and for continuity of personal tools and rules they use repeatedly. That switching cost is an inference. The memo reports launch attention and an offer to reserve a handset with a refundable $199 deposit toward an estimated $699 price. It gives no deposit count, paid conversion, or repeat usage. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The Pixel-based prototype and the founders’ Android and hardware backgrounds support seed-stage execution credit. The proposed expansion unit is a handset sale. At the estimated price, contribution would depend on device and fulfillment costs, support, and ongoing AI usage. The closed beta includes AI usage, and the memo provides no realized cost data. Custom handset delivery also depends on FCC authorization. (The Idea, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Cyclon could save time on tasks users already do while giving them little reason to buy a separate handset. Gemini already automates supported tasks on existing Android phones, and Nothing offers phone-based app generation. Cyclon also acknowledges that compatibility limits, manual checks, and execution errors can interrupt the workflows it aims to automate. These risks leave both Path B gates open. The memo contains no representative comparison that establishes equivalent substitutes or shows that oversight consumes the labor savings. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will users who routinely skip specific cross-app tasks pay a price that covers delivery costs and keep using Cyclon when their existing phones offer equivalent model quality? A paid field comparison should show sustained completion of those previously skipped tasks, lower total labor per successful outcome than the manual baseline, and continued preference for Cyclon after trying the alternative. That result would move the action to Reach out. A representative result showing that oversight erases the savings or that users readily switch would close Path B and move the action to Pass.
