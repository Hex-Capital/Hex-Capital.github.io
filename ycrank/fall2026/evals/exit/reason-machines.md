# Reason Machines -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest path. An AI coding platform could buy Reason’s harness to lower the cost of completed repository work across its installed base. Ownership could matter if integrating Reason’s batching, cache reuse, and session management into the buyer’s own agent produces durable savings beyond what it can build or license. The memo describes a released harness and records 1,800 GitHub stars for Reason Network. Reason reports 22% lower cost per solved task than Codex on the same model in its benchmark. Production savings and the value of owning the harness remain unverified. The memo records an adjacent acquisition of Cursor, with limited relevance to the price of Reason’s harness. (*The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks*)

A quality-matched production trial could measure cost per merged pull request and establish whether the savings persist across repositories. The released product gives Reason a starting point for that test. The founders said they were raising seed funding, and the memo gives no closed round, runway, or funded trial. It also provides no terms establishing which assets and rights a buyer could acquire. No separate route has usable evidence of cash reaching this holder. (*The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts*)

**Proceeds bridge.** Using the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 40% relative dilution takes ownership from 5% to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in holder cash, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, fees, or other price adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. Cash is paid at closing and allocated pro rata, with no preference adjustments. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation each contribute $0. These are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no usable acquisition price or buyer economics to anchor them.

| Strong | $100 million cash sale in year 7 | $3 million at 3% ownership, or $2 million at 2% | 6× at 3%, or 4× at 2% |

### Countercase

A buyer could capture comparable savings with its own harness. Reason builds on public Pi, Cursor already runs isolated cloud VMs, and Cognition has its own cost-optimized harness. Reason’s fixed-task benchmark leaves open whether the advantage survives in production. These facts weaken the ownership rationale. The memo leaves the cost and performance of a buyer’s best substitute unresolved, so the structural Pass gate is unproven. (*Defensibility, Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a prospective platform buyer verify in a quality-matched production trial that owning Reason would lower cost per merged pull request enough, at the buyer’s actual task volume, to support a price that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? A buyer-backed comparison against its best build or license option would answer it. A durable advantage that supports the required price would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that an adequate substitute caps the price materially below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
