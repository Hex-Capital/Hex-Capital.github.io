# LATO -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The founders possess specific knowledge earned through action. Tymek Staniszewski spent six years inside Verdane, reaching senior investment associate before building software for the work he had performed; Tien Chu pairs eight years in AI and software with published deep-learning research. They have already moved from an Excel model checker to interviewing 30 recruiters and producing research for Blume Equity and FoodLabs. This is not credential theater. It is a commercial investor and a technical founder building against a problem they know from opposite sides.

The problem is that founder knowledge compounds only if the product creates an asset. Today, LATO sources experts, conducts interviews, combines the output with fund data, and produces a study-specific simulation. A competitor copying the workflow can purchase the same models and recruit from the same expert pool. AlphaSense already has AI-led calls, more than 280,000 transcripts, and over $600 million in ARR. LATO discloses no exclusive corpus, proprietary model, simulation benchmark, or workflow lock-in. The 30-recruiter study proves execution; it does not prove durability.

The bull case is real. Two named customers at pre-seed are meaningful, 70% of reported use cases have converged on research, and the team changed positioning within months as it learned—from an Excel assistant in April 2026 to interview-grounded studies by July. The earlier product also found a claimed $67.9 million valuation error. This could be rapid discovery by founders who understand the buyer, not undisciplined pivoting. No disclosed founder behavior triggers an integrity, artificial-deadline, or emotional-instability veto.

But the current operating loop runs in the wrong direction. Each study requires LATO or its agents to source another group of experts; no evidence shows that one customer improves utility or distribution for the next. Reusable interviews and simulation feedback are hypotheses, while the incumbent already owns the transcript network LATO hopes to accumulate. This is neither a permissionless contributor system nor a marketplace moving fragmented expertise into a demonstrably self-reinforcing network. More usage may create more work before it creates leverage.

Market breadth and value capture remain plausible rather than proven. Investor diligence is expensive—LATO cites studies above $300,000—and adjacent corporate market research could be large, but the dossier supplies no buyer-by-buyer expansion path or independent market sizing. Blume Equity and FoodLabs validate access to buyers, yet there is no disclosed contract value, recurring revenue, take rate, or unit economics. The known exceptions do not rescue the missing substrate: those require either observable network formation, an unusually durable prototype, or founder evidence so exceptional that product ambiguity becomes secondary. LATO has strong founder-market fit, but not yet that override.

I pass at this price-independent stage. The likely outcome is a capable, services-heavy research platform unless the data loop becomes proprietary faster than incumbents can reproduce it. One verifiable condition flips the decision: within twelve months, LATO must show paid recurring contracts and a benchmark demonstrating that its accumulated interview corpus measurably improves simulation accuracy or retention across customers without proportional expert-sourcing labor. Then usage would be manufacturing an asset rather than merely completing projects.
