# CONUS -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest supported route is a strategic acquisition by a hospital workflow vendor. CONUS combines multi-manufacturer implant documentation with hospital-specific device lists and protocols. A vendor such as Casechek could use that product to extend its implant logistics and billing workflow into surgical preparation. CONUS reports catalog integrations and relationships with major device manufacturers. Those claims make the integration rationale specific to CONUS, while buyer interest and the economic benefit of ownership remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

CONUS reports 28 live hospitals and 53 onboarding, with an earlier founder Q&A reporting 25 live European hospitals. The memo records an iOS release history beginning in January 2025 and a paid institutional AI offering alongside free core access. A measurable next milestone is converting a cohort of live hospitals to paid institutional licenses, retaining them at renewal, and measuring time saved in surgical preparation. The reported deployments give CONUS sites where it could run that test. Realized revenue, test funding, and the capital needed to reach the milestone remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-ons. The eight-year objective of $2.5 million in gross cash requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3% pro rata ownership. The scenarios below are sensitivities. The memo supplies no transaction pricing or buyer economics to support a realizable range. Each sale case assumes the holder can participate, sells the entire stake, receives a pro rata share with no preferential diversion, and collects the cash within eight years. Actual holder terms and closing conditions are unknown.

| Scenario | Illustrative proceeds to the holder |
|---|---|
| Modest acquisition | $40 million enterprise value with no net deductions leaves $40 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $1.2 million, or 2.4×. |
| Strong acquisition | $100 million enterprise value less $10 million in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $2.7 million, or 5.4×. |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with reinvested cash and no eligible share sale or funded payout yields $0 realized within eight years. |

Hospital control of local protocols and limits on manufacturer access to hospital-specific usage data make the rights a buyer could obtain a consequential diligence issue. The memo leaves change-of-control permissions and holder eligibility open and provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A workflow vendor could build or partner for document search. M-Eido already offers AI search over uploaded implant manuals, and the memo identifies no structural barrier to replication. A device manufacturer might also weaken the appeal of a cross-manufacturer platform by owning it, given CONUS’s commitments against paid placement and sharing hospital-specific usage data with manufacturers. Free core hospital access further limits what the reported footprint establishes about willingness to pay. These facts weaken the case for an acquisition premium. They establish neither a structural closing block nor a supported sale range that falls materially short of the holder objective, so the Pass gate does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a hospital workflow vendor justify an acquisition price that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle based on measured results from paid CONUS deployments? Paid conversions and renewals, measured preparation-time savings, and a buyer’s quantified case for owning the product would answer it. Move to Reach out if those results support a credible price range that clears the hurdle after deal adjustments and holder terms. Keep Watch while that evidence is missing.
