# Databuddy -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Databuddy has a plausible industry-enabled path in recurring conversion investigations for startups without a data analyst. The company describes a manual workflow that requires reconciling traffic, product events, errors, and payments across tools. It says Databunny can query those signals, show the underlying query for review, and deliver scheduled investigations in Slack. The seed-stage hypothesis is that automating data reconciliation and query writing makes weekly investigations affordable for teams that previously investigated only when a problem became urgent. Databuddy lists a $299 monthly Business plan with scheduled investigations. Whether that price plus setup and review time is affordable per accurate, actionable diagnosis remains untested. Under model parity, the integrated tracker, revenue connections, query history, and scheduled delivery could still matter as a managed workflow. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The building-blocks path scores zero. Databuddy uses AI to deliver product analytics, and the memo identifies no scarce input Databuddy supplies to a specific AI workload or part of the buildout. (The Idea)

The memo records 38 active subscriptions and $3,345 in company-wide MRR. Those figures support early demand for Databuddy, with no breakdown showing who pays for or repeatedly uses scheduled investigations. Nassar's prior analytics work and delivery of the original web analytics product support execution, while dependable automated diagnosis remains unproven. The memo gives subscription prices but no delivery costs for the next account, so scale economics receives the default. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Databunny may make an existing analytics workflow faster without making a new use viable. Mixpanel already offers proactive metric monitoring, and PostHog exposes existing customer data to AI agents. Configured event schemas and investigation history could create switching costs, but the memo supplies no retention evidence, and the reported switch from Framer Analytics does not establish adoption of automated investigations. If incumbent tools can deliver equivalent diagnoses at a comparable cost, or if human review keeps recurring investigations uneconomic, the industry-enabled path would score zero and trigger Pass. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will startups without a data analyst pay for and keep using weekly conversion investigations they previously could not afford to run? A paid pilot would compare accurate, actionable diagnoses on the same customer data against each team's manual workflow and best available incumbent setup, counting subscription, setup, review, and rework costs. Repeat paid use at a viable price, with a materially lower cost per successful investigation than either baseline, would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that the workflow opens no new use or that an incumbent delivers an equivalent result at comparable cost would move it to Pass.
