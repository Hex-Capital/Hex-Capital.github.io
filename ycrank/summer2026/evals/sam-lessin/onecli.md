# OneCLI -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Jonathan Fishner’s Axis Security and Unit 8200 history makes OneCLI a missionary execution company trapped inside an unpriced, consensus AI-security round: founder conviction is OneCLI’s strongest dimension, while demonstrable value-seed mispricing is its weakest. OneCLI has real pre-pitch artifacts—ChartDB reached 22.7K GitHub stars and more than $10K MRR before the founders pivoted—but “identity gateway for AI agents” is precisely the kind of legible 2026 story that attracts factory-model capital. I believe the operators before I believe the asset price.

**Drop.io** is the high rung for asymmetric conviction: I built against free incumbents, kept turning the crank, and converted an unglamorous file-sharing primitive into a Facebook acquisition. Fishner and Guy Ben-Aharon share the load-bearing mechanism—years across ZTNA, supply-chain security, Axis, Argon, Aqua, and Unit 8200, followed by two open-source products built together. OneCLI diverges because OneCLI itself began in January 2026; the dossier establishes durable security obsession, but only six months of commitment to this particular credential boundary. That places OneCLI below Drop.io’s company-specific missionary arc, though well above an opportunistic agent wrapper.

**Robinhood’s** later consensus markup is the low rung on price discipline; the exception worked because Slow entered before the heat, not because paying the heat was clever. OneCLI supplies no valuation, round size, competitive-process evidence, or tier-1 pass, so I apply the 9/30 absent-evidence default. Worse, OneCLI sits at the intersection of AI agents, identity, security, open source, and infrastructure while Oasis has raised $195M and Astrix $85M. OneCLI may be early in product maturity, but nothing in the dossier proves I am early in price or consensus.

**$199 Team, $499 Scale, and $1,499 managed-VPC pricing** give OneCLI a plausible capital-efficiency mechanism over the next 18 months: convert open-source adoption into high-margin hosted control, then let accumulated policies and audit history increase switching costs. OneCLI already has 2.9K GitHub stars, 170 forks, 4,523 weekly SDK downloads, and NanoClaw distribution, so the pre-Series-A regatta could require only one commercial tack. The failure mode is equally structural: Apache-2.0 code plus no disclosed revenue turns OneCLI into a free developer project subsidized by a conventional SaaS cap table, forcing repeated rounds before default-alive status.

**Infisical’s competing Agent Vault** defines the counterfactual for category fit. OneCLI would move up a full tier if managed-VPC customers demonstrated that the gateway is a Wizard-of-Oz security operation becoming software—security teams repeatedly paying OneCLI to author policies, review exceptions, and govern agents—rather than another AI-infrastructure abstraction. Today Infisical already offers a similar open-source HTTP credential proxy, and Oasis, Astrix, and Entro sell into the same security budget. OneCLI has some **Sublime Security** adjacency, but the dossier does not yet establish that OneCLI occupies a pre-consensus security sleeve rather than the consensus AI-agent sleeve.

**John Fish / Bookshelved** is the useful founder-distribution parallel: owned attention existed before the financing instrument, making the person an underwritable asset. OneCLI’s founders each have roughly 2K LinkedIn followers, and their earlier ChartDB repository reached 22.7K stars; OneCLI has also accumulated 320K claimed downloads and a 160-point Show HN launch. OneCLI therefore owns a meaningful developer community, but the attachment appears to be to useful open-source projects rather than to Fishner or Ben-Aharon as durable founder-brands. That is valuable distribution, not yet a creator asset on the cap table.

**320K claimed downloads without disclosed revenue** leads to a specific 36-month forecast: by July 2029, OneCLI reaches $3M–$7M ARR and is acquired by 1Password for less than $100M rather than becoming an independent category leader. OneCLI’s founders are strong enough to convert a portion of the open-source funnel, while the proxy-bypass limitation, Apache license, and Infisical convergence keep OneCLI’s gateway from becoming an unassailable system of record. That is a good operating outcome and a mediocre venture outcome if today’s SAFE already prices in category leadership.

**NanoClaw’s default integration** is the bull case that stings: OneCLI could become the credential boundary embedded in every new agent framework, with 2.9K stars and 170 forks representing the start of protocol-level distribution rather than vanity adoption. OneCLI could then resemble the **Sublime Security** exception—an enterprise-security wedge bought before consolidation—while the founders’ ChartDB record proves they know how to compound open-source attention. I still cannot underwrite that outcome against an identical Infisical architecture, no published retention or revenue, and traffic that can bypass the proxy entirely. I would flip to Invest if OneCLI reaches $2M ARR from at least 100 paying organizations without paid acquisition before its next financing.
