# Super Powers Mobility (SPM) -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

SPM’s strongest exit path is a strategic sale to a military power-system supplier. The memo records EnerSys’s $208 million acquisition of Bren-Tronics, an adjacent military battery and charging business with approximately $100 million in prior-year sales. A buyer could acquire SPM’s combined pack, power-electronics, and vehicle-integration capabilities to shorten customer development cycles. That benefit is an inference. The memo provides no measured time savings or buyer interest. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

SPM reports participation in four federal contracts and production bookings into February 2027. It also reports $421,815 in realized revenue in one month, while its launch separately describes more than $400,000 in contracted revenue in one month. The figures may overlap and establish neither a recurring run rate nor production margins. Funded repeat orders with demonstrated delivery margins would strengthen the acquisition case. The prior business raised $1.3 million, but the capital and time needed to qualify and scale the current product remain unclear. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity. For illustration, assume 2× annual sales as enterprise value, loosely anchored to the adjacent transaction’s headline price-to-sales ratio, less $5 million in combined net debt and deal adjustments. Assume a full cash sale within eight years, holder eligibility, and a pro rata waterfall with no preference deductions or holdbacks.

- **Modest sale.** $20 million in annual sales implies $40 million in enterprise value, $35 million in distributable equity, and $1.05 million in holder cash.
- **Strong sale.** $50 million in annual sales implies $100 million in enterprise value, $95 million in distributable equity, and $2.85 million in holder cash. At 1.5× sales, the same business yields $2.10 million to the holder.
- **Prolonged private operation.** Reinvestment without a funded payout or eligible share purchase yields zero realized proceeds.

The strong case clears the hurdle under its stated assumptions. The memo leaves contract and IP transfer terms, holder participation, and any independently supported liquidity route unevidenced. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Inventus Power already supplies complete military battery and power systems. An acquirer could rely on an existing supplier if SPM’s integration provides little measurable advantage. Prime-dependent orders, external cell sourcing, and production scale-up could also keep SPM below the volume needed for meaningful holder proceeds. Those risks support Watch. The memo establishes neither a ready substitute for SPM’s specific integration work nor a structural closing block or proceeds shortfall across the strongest credible route. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can SPM turn its initial prime-contractor work into profitable repeat production? A funded reorder, delivered-unit margins, and a capacity plan with capital requirements would answer it. Move to Reach out if those results support a credible path to roughly $44 million in annual sales within eight years, the level needed under the 2× sale scenario. Otherwise, keep watching.
