# Kanopy -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Kanopy’s strongest evidence is 3,093 mapped miles. Yann’s utility advisory work and Nicolas’s computer-vision development at ShapeScale establish relevant experience; putting their own product into the field establishes action. The September–October release notes describe pole-association and wire-fitting corrections—the details through which specific knowledge accumulates. These founders have earned substantial credit for building into the problem. Their advertised 2–4-inch accuracy still needs independent validation.

The investment question is who owns the learning from those miles. Kanopy converts ordinary video into infrastructure measurements, avoiding specialized capture hardware. That could make deployment cheaper. But the dossier establishes no exclusive training rights, proprietary model advantage, or accumulated asset records that customers would struggle to replace. Public reconstruction models such as VGGT make the durability question concrete without proving that Kanopy itself uses commodity technology. I give the missing moat its default score. Field experience becomes an investment advantage only when the company can retain something valuable from it.

The strongest case for writing a check is that Kanopy is already encountering the conditions under which that asset could emerge. Six utilities and contractors signed LOIs or MSAs covering 270,000 scoped miles in April 2026; the company subsequently reported mapping more than 300 miles across four states in two weeks. Repeated surveys could generate valuable corrections across weather, vegetation, and infrastructure types. That possibility deserves attention. The announced scope, however, establishes neither purchased volume nor rights to reuse the resulting data. Counting it as revenue, proprietary data, and distribution would award three assets for one expression of customer interest.

There is a substantial economic problem here. The cited $31.95 billion vegetation-management market includes physical treatment, so Kanopy cannot claim that entire expenditure as software demand. Utilities and inspection contractors are identifiable buyers, and geospatial measurements could support broader asset inspection. The dossier supplies little evidence connecting that expansion to an addressable budget. Meanwhile, Schneider’s announced AiDASH acquisition gives a competing approach a potential route through installed grid relationships. Kanopy needs a reason customers will choose and retain its measurements beyond inexpensive capture.

My preferred unstated patterns are only partly present: Kanopy addresses physical infrastructure and could increase an inspector’s output, but no permissionless contribution system or customer-created distribution loop is demonstrated. Another utility joining does not yet measurably improve the product for existing customers. I would make an exception for an emerging network before monetization, extraordinary founder execution before a clear wedge, or an exceptional prototype with unusual capital efficiency before a user loop. The strongest exception here is founder execution; it warrants continued attention, but does not establish the missing durable asset. There is no disclosed integrity, financing-pressure, or founder-behavior red flag.

I would hold off on a personal check at **46/100: Neutral**. Kanopy has credible builders and meaningful field activity, while pricing, payment, and compounding ownership remain unresolved. My one verifiable flip condition is a documented deployment study showing that contractually reusable corrections from earlier customers materially improve clearance accuracy for a new utility without proportional manual correction work. That would turn accumulated activity into an asset whose value grows with deployment.
