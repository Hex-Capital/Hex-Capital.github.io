# COACH -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot underwrite COACH’s round because the dossier omits the post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and use of proceeds. Those are not clerical details. The company has already reached a company-reported €1 million ARR within eight months, so this capital should flip a specific card—not merely hire, expand in America, and produce the next financing milestone. With no price or finite experiment disclosed, I may be looking at a cheap first check into an unfashionable French operator, or paying YC-auction pricing for a thesis Rilla and Siro have already made obvious.

The operating evidence is substantially better than the financing evidence. COACH reports 100% pilot-to-paid conversion, zero churn, thousands of reps, and 74,473 analyzed meetings across 200 companies. That is not follower or integration theater: customers place the product inside a recurring workflow and then purchase it. The claimed 32% conversion lift would explain the purchase mechanism, although the conflicting 27% claim and absent cohort methodology mean I would underwrite paid conversion and retention—not the precise performance improvement.

The founders also form a credible translation layer. Mathieu and Thomas Perez operated a 44-rep field-sales business that generated €3.5 million in 14 months; Nicolas Sebag brings a decade of software work and mobile-product experience. They have shipped on the sales side and the capture-software side rather than discovering field sales from customer interviews. The unresolved organizational issue is accountability: three close co-founders—a pair of brothers and their best friend—are presented without evidence of who has final authority over product, hiring, or financing.

The strongest bear case is that this is an effective implementation of a commodity. Rilla and Siro already provide mobile recording, transcription, analysis, and coaching, while COACH relies on Anthropic for AI and transcription. Its corpus may be proprietary, but meeting count alone is not a moat; it matters only if customer-specific outcomes make the workflow improve, retain, and expand faster than competitors can reproduce it. Recording-consent friction in California further weakens a simple France-to-U.S. replication story.

Still, the generic-AI red flag is partially overridden by a functioning base business: €1 million ARR, complete reported pilot conversion, and zero churn are measured commercial outcomes rather than promises about future model capability. What is not overridden is the missing cash model. Revenue appears, but gross margin, burn, runway, inference cost, and a route to break-even do not. A $100,000–$200,000 design hire suggests COACH is beginning to spend ahead of the disclosed economics, and I cannot yet delete the Series A from the plan.

My 36-month base case is that COACH reaches $8–15 million ARR as a Europe-led field-sales coaching vendor but remains one of several credible products rather than establishing a category monopoly. I am Neutral today: the company is more convincing than the deal. I flip to Invest on one verifiable financing plan showing a post-money valuation at or below $10 million, no competing term-sheet auction, and proceeds committed to a dated pass/fail U.S. expansion test rather than general hiring.
