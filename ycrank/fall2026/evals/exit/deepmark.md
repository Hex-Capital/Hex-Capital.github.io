# DeepMark -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is DeepMark’s strongest path. A contact-center fraud platform could buy DeepMark to add issuer and authorization checks for AI agents on ordinary calls. Ownership could matter if it secures control of transferable watermarking IP and relationships with voice providers that would be costly to replicate or unavailable through a license. That buyer rationale is an inference. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

The memo records a peer-reviewed robustness benchmark and Vigil’s C2PA registration. DeepMark reports that its first voice-platform license is in production, with the licensee and scope undisclosed. Confident Security reports production audio watermarking. The next milestone is a paid production deployment that measures reliable end-to-end authorization across real phone networks, with both a voice provider and a receiving contact center integrated. Prior funding and existing integrations support pursuing that test. Current runway, test cost, IP ownership, and assignment rights are undisclosed. The memo supplies no usable acquisition-price evidence or independently supported fallback that pays this holder. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

**Proceeds bridge.** Using the thesis’s illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership dilutes to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in gross cash to the investor, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, fees, and senior claims, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration and cash is allocated pro rata before investor taxes. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each modeled at zero. The memo gives no terms to verify those assumptions. The sale values illustrate the hurdle and carry no price prediction.

| Scenario | Assumed outcome and timing | Investor cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest | $50 million cash sale in year 5 | $1.5 million | 3× |
| Strong | $100 million cash sale in year 7 | $3 million | 6× |
| Prolonged private | Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested through year 8 | $0, with shares still held | 0× |

The strong scenario clears the hurdle at 3% but yields only $2 million at 2%. Buyer economics and ownership terms therefore matter even if the product milestone succeeds.

### Countercase

DeepMark already offers platform licenses, and Resemble AI markets phone-compatible watermarking with real-time verification. A buyer could license DeepMark or use a competing product if either delivers comparable authentication. DeepMark also needs adoption on both sides of the call, and transcoding, compression, and noise can impair watermark recovery. These facts make the ownership premium uncertain. The memo establishes neither an equivalent substitute that caps the acquisition price nor a structural failure that triggers the Pass override. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

After a paid deployment demonstrates reliable authorization across real phone networks, can a contact-center fraud platform quantify enough incremental value from owning DeepMark’s transferable IP and integrations, compared with licensing or building, to support a price that clears the holder’s hurdle? A buyer-backed assessment supporting that price would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that available substitutes cap the price materially below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
