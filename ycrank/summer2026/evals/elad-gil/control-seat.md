# Control Seat -- Elad Gil Evaluation

**Perplexity** is the high rung for founder iteration: Aravind shipped the discussed product between meetings, turning execution cadence into the investment signal. Control Seat is an execution-rich, curve-poor industrial workflow bet—its strongest dimension is shipping and its weakest is visible revenue velocity. Warren Shepard and Jack Grodnick have built a proprietary SCADA, historian, analytics stack, and Virtual PLC, then deployed Control Seat at Battex Chile; that is substantially more than résumé signaling. Control Seat still sits below Perplexity because the dossier shows no repeated customer-feedback loop or week-to-week release cadence, and its nine-of-ten CODESYS benchmark wins exclude I/O latency, scheduler jitter, motion, and safety certification.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism for revenue velocity: activity can look like momentum without proving the economic curve required to overcome market structure. Control Seat has one named Battex Chile deployment, but Control Seat discloses neither contract value, ARR, repeat usage, deployment expansion, nor month-over-month growth. A seven-day free trial and custom-scoped pricing are packaging, not buying behavior. At pre-seed I do not require millions in revenue, but I need evidence that something is “just working”; Control Seat currently provides one customer logo without enough data to distinguish durable demand from integration-project tourism.

**$12.89 billion growing at 9.2% annually** describes a steady SCADA market, not a re-opening. Control Seat says AI-assisted software generation lowered the cost of building industrial software, but that is a supply-side catalyst for Control Seat—not evidence that plant operators suddenly have new budgets or urgency. The score flips into invest territory if a recent event such as mandatory cybersegmentation, acute controls-labor scarcity, or widespread edge-AI adoption forces plants to consolidate legacy systems now. Without that demand catalyst, Control Seat is skating into a mature market occupied by Ignition, Siemens, Rockwell, AVEVA, and CODESYS.

**Battex Chile** consolidated MQTT brokers, virtual machines, databases, dashboards, and APIs onto Control Seat, showing the beginnings of an index-like position. Over 18 months, every Control Seat integration could add reusable connectors, validated tag mappings, and operating histories, allowing Control Seat to compound with industrial digitization across Allen-Bradley, Siemens, and Beckhoff installations. The failure mode is equally mechanical: custom integration work scales with engineering headcount, while incumbents retain the underlying customer relationship. Control Seat becomes an index on industrial operations only if its vendor-neutral tag layer spreads faster than its services burden.

**Color Genomics** worked because workflow and data infrastructure sat above commodity sequencing rather than requiring a new scientific breakthrough. Control Seat has a similar architecture when Control Seat overlays existing SCADA and explicitly leaves safety functions independent. The analogy breaks where Control Seat also owns the Virtual PLC: reliable real-time execution, brownfield compatibility, enterprise distribution, and eventual safety credibility become separate dependencies. Keeping Virtual PLC adoption optional preserves a single miracle—winning the operational data layer—while making it central creates a multi-miracle controls company.

**Warren Shepard’s SpaceX manufacturing work and Jack Grodnick’s Tesla and SpaceX SCADA experience** make a modest but falsifiable outcome more likely than either breakout or collapse. By July 2029, I expect Control Seat to reach $4 million to $8 million in annualized revenue, with more than half tied to managed deployments and integration work rather than repeatable software subscriptions. Control Seat can win because its founders ship and understand the plant floor, but without a disclosed buying curve or demand-side catalyst, Control Seat is more likely to become a strong industrial software integrator than the Stripe-like default layer for industrial operations.

**Battex Chile plus cross-vendor support** is the strongest opposing case: Control Seat could use integration projects exactly as Stripe used developer adoption, landing inside plants and expanding from monitoring into historian, automation, forecasting, and control. Shepard and Grodnick have unusually direct founder-market fit, the overlay avoids costly rip-and-replace, and the proprietary stack could turn each deployment into high-switching-cost infrastructure. That bull case stings because it matches my unstated preference for workflow software on top of a hard technical layer, much as Color Genomics did. The current dossier still loses on one fact pattern: one unnamed-value deployment, no growth curve, no production-deployment count, and a benchmark that omits the operational constraints industrial buyers care about. I would flip to Invest if Control Seat demonstrates more than 20% monthly software-ARR growth for six consecutive months across at least ten independent production plants.
