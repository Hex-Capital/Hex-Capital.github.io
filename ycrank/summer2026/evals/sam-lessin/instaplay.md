# Instaplay -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and use of proceeds are all missing. That leaves two gates unresolved: whether this is still a cheap, non-consensus belief and what discrete truth the check buys. Astrocade has raised $56 million, Verse8 $5 million, and adjacent social-game companies are already well financed. That proves category heat, not that Instaplay itself is being auctioned, but I will not pretend YC participation makes capital scarce.

The real asset is the behavior already inside the product. Twenty thousand creators and 100,000 monthly users are substantial pre-seed evidence, while 3,000 blinded FunBench votes show that Instaplay can induce more than passive account creation. Browser links moving through group chats and Discord also provide the beginnings of an owned creation-and-discovery loop. The company records prompts, edits, generated outputs, player preferences, and engagement; that could become a proprietary dark pool. But it has not yet shown that this loop causes a purchase, durable retention, or even superior model performance.

The present economics cut directly against that promise. Each delivered game costs a measured $0.86–$7.11 in model spend, the product is free to start, and no paid tier or creator revenue share is disclosed. Median engaged play lasts only 3.0–7.9 seconds, with inverted controls, broken interfaces, and incomplete progression recurring in generated games. Delete the next financing and the controllable metric is not MAU; it is gross profit per retained creator. Instaplay supplies no evidence that this number is positive.

The strongest bull case is uncomfortable: 100,000 monthly users arrived organically before the company had even disclosed monetization, and every creation gives the team more evaluation data. Gary Wu has worked on Agentforce at Salesforce and previously co-founded an LLM-evaluation startup; Frank Liu brings consumer-social engineering experience from Instagram; Akhil Aggarwal has operated globally deployed AWS security systems. That is credible shipping experience across AI infrastructure and social distribution. It is not, however, evidence that anyone has built a game business; being a top-1,000 Teamfight Tactics player is user knowledge, not an operating receipt from the other side of the bridge.

The known AI exception does not apply. Instaplay has usage, but not the measured conversion, retention, or cash effects needed to establish that the base business works independently of improving foundation models. Its telemetry resembles a defensible workflow, yet Astrocade, Verse8, Pops, and general-purpose coding agents can generate comparable experiences, and Instaplay has not demonstrated that its data closes the quality gap. My 36-month base case is therefore a pivot toward game-generation evaluation and QA tooling, with less than $1 million ARR, rather than a durable consumer social network.

I am passing today. The product has real pre-seed pull, but the round is unpriced in the dossier, the capital has no named venture moment, and the strongest behavior signal stops before payment or retention. One verifiable flip condition would change the decision: three consecutive months of at least $50,000 in monthly gross profit after inference costs. That would prove the audience causes purchases, the generation loop can finance itself, and the telemetry is producing economic rather than merely entertaining behavior.
