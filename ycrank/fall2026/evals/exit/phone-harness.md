# Phone Harness -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest path. An agent platform or real-device testing provider could buy Phone Harness to add native mobile workflows. The candidate asset is the hosted fleet operation, including persistent device configurations and one command interface across personal and hosted phones. Ownership could give a buyer control over fleet reliability and integration. That advantage is an inference and depends on what the buyer can achieve by building, partnering, or forking the local harness. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The memo records a cloud beta launch, 5.1K GitHub stars, and 522 forks. The company advertises dedicated iPhones at $80 per month, which establishes an asking price. Paying-customer counts, repeat usage, realized revenue, and fleet costs remain unverified. The operating milestone is repeat paid use of hosted phones for permitted production workflows, with measured reliability and cost savings. The live beta gives the one-person team a way to run that test. Available runway and the capital needed to scale the fleet remain unclear. The rights needed to transfer the hosted operation are unverified. The memo supplies no usable evidence of an independent route to holder cash. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, or 5×. Under a simple pro rata waterfall before investor taxes, that takes $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale scenarios assume zero net debt and no preference or other closing adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. All consideration is cash at close. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. The memo provides no usable acquisition valuation or waterfall terms, so these are sensitivity cases.

| Scenario | Assumed outcome | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $40 million enterprise value in year 5 | $1.2 million (2.4×) |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value in year 7 | $3.0 million (6.0×) at 3% ownership, or $2.0 million (4.0×) at 2% |
| Prolonged private | Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested through year 8 | $0 (0×), with shares still held |

### Countercase

A buyer could fork the MIT-licensed local harness and rent devices from an existing provider. Mobilerun already bundles hosted phones with an agent task runner. Third-party app rules can also exclude workflows the harness can technically perform, shrinking the workload a buyer could justify paying for. Together, these facts make a purchase premium hard to underwrite. The memo leaves open whether operating a persistent fleet for permitted workloads creates enough value over those substitutes. It establishes neither a structural price ceiling nor a probable eight-year cash drought, so the Pass overrides do not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production pilot with a prospective strategic buyer show that owning the hosted fleet creates enough incremental value over building or renting to support a price that clears the holder’s hurdle? Buyer-validated workload volumes, reliability, and operating costs that support that price would move the action to Reach out.
