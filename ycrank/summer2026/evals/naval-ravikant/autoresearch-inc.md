# Autoresearch, Inc. -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Autoresearch, Inc.’s strongest evidence is that its founders have actually conducted the research they propose to sell. The October 2026 release includes training code, a campaign spanning 4,188 experiments, and 25 confirmation runs reporting median validation bits-per-byte of 0.9016. That is specific knowledge exposed through action. Karan and Thomas’s production ML backgrounds and Jad’s formal-verification expertise help explain the execution, but the published artifact carries more weight than their titles. I give the team substantial credit for building and testing in the exact problem domain.

The investment question is what Autoresearch owns after a capable competitor reproduces the workflow. Persistent memory, experiment lineage, orchestration, and statistical verification are useful capabilities. The dossier establishes no exclusive dataset, proprietary model, or accumulated asset that makes the next campaign progressively harder to compete with. Customer ownership of research artifacts and enterprise data restrictions also constrain the proposed cross-customer learning advantage. Those restrictions are commercially understandable. They leave the central compounding mechanism unproved.

The strongest bull case is that reliable experimental judgment becomes the scarce product as agents make experimentation cheap. A three-person team combining production infrastructure and formal verification could build that judgment into reusable research policies. Repeated confirmation runs show attention to measurement noise, an important distinction from merely generating thousands of trials. My exception for an unusual prototype before broad adoption deserves consideration here. But the dossier does not establish unusual capital efficiency, exceptional recruiting, or proprietary learning sufficient to override the missing durable asset. The exception for uncertain monetization likewise needs an already-forming network; none is demonstrated.

Autoresearch fits my pattern of tools that turn individual knowledge into scalable output. It has yet to demonstrate the complementary pattern of participants improving the system for one another. Founder-led campaign engagements provide no observed contribution, referral, or matching loop. The product spans training, prompts, tools, and systems, but this breadth remains a menu of capabilities: the dossier does not name an evidenced expansion from the initial AI-team buyer into a large adjacent pool of spending. Predibase’s acquisition demonstrates strategic interest in neighboring infrastructure; it does not establish Autoresearch’s expansion economics.

Paid plans billed at signup and enterprise MSAs establish a direct charging mechanism. They establish neither completed purchases nor attractive contribution margins. The demonstration used 50 B200 GPUs over two days; without customer pricing and delivered economic gains, I cannot tell whether the company captures the value its experiments create. There is no documented integrity violation or artificial fundraising deadline to trigger a veto. My concern is that substantial technical competence could produce a useful research service whose costs grow with its engagements.

I would pass on writing a personal check today. Autoresearch, Inc. has earned conviction in its ability to execute experiments; ownership of a compounding advantage remains an open question. **My flip condition:** a documented series of paid deployments showing that a company-owned research policy improves held-out customer results at lower compute cost because it learned from earlier deployments, under contractual rights permitting that reuse. That would connect the founders’ demonstrated knowledge to an asset that grows with the business.
