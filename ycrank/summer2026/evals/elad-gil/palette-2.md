# Palette -- Elad Gil Evaluation

**Palette’s 130+ regression tests reveal a technically executable, single-miracle workflow product whose weakest signal is whether anyone is actually buying it.** Palette benefits from already-capable video models rather than requiring new model science, but Palette discloses neither current-product revenue nor usage growth. This is a plausible product searching for a durable market position—not yet something “just working.”

**Color Genomics** is the high rung on single-miracle architecture: workflow infrastructure built on commodity sequencing, with science risk deliberately removed from the critical path. Palette shares that mechanism by routing Seedance, Veo, Kling, MiniMax, and LTX rather than financing a proprietary world model. Palette’s 35% faster and 50% cheaper internal results suggest execution work, not new physics. Palette diverges from Color because the underlying models are controlled by suppliers that can change pricing, while Higgsfield and Krea can reproduce the same orchestration; Palette still needs distribution and defensibility to work simultaneously. **9/12.**

**Lyft’s** low-rung failure mechanism was an inability to prove share inversion in a market where a better-positioned competitor already had the curve. Palette has the analogous evidence gap on revenue velocity: $0.01-per-credit pricing and a “Book a call” funnel establish availability, not buying behavior. Higgsfield reports a $200M annual revenue run rate, while Palette provides no customer count, revenue, repeat usage, or growth rate. Pre-seed calibration prevents me from penalizing small numbers, but Palette has disclosed no number at all—the absent-evidence default applies. **8/25.**

**Video models added native audio, multi-shot generation, character consistency, and editing during 2025–26**, creating a credible recent catalyst for Palette. Over the next 18 months, that catalyst should compound through rapidly expanding model capabilities and falling generation costs, but it may also erase Palette’s routing advantage as each provider absorbs more of the workflow. Palette is skating for the puck in a reopened market; Palette has not shown that the puck is moving toward an independent orchestration layer rather than toward Runway, Higgsfield, or Krea. **18/28.**

**Serena Pei’s diffusion, computer-vision, and multimodal research at CSAIL, Media Lab, and LIDS** gives Palette relevant technical depth, while Josephine Lee’s Broad Institute dashboard work shows some product construction. The score would move into the invest range if Palette shipped meaningful workflow improvements between conversations and showed repeat use from named creative teams. Palette’s public pivot from an HR-agent product in June to multimedia generation in July could represent fast iteration, but without shipped artifacts or user-feedback loops it could equally represent unresolved product selection. **10/20.**

**Stripe** became an index on e-commerce because every internet merchant eventually touched the payments rail. Palette aspires to become an index on proliferating video models through shared-credit APIs and cost-gated routing, but Palette remains an optional interface competing with Runway, Krea, and Higgsfield rather than infrastructure every creator must touch. Palette benefits when model fragmentation increases; it loses leverage when model suites integrate editing and routing themselves. **7/15.**

**Palette’s $0.01-per-credit product and technically credible routing layer** lead me to predict that by July 2029 Palette will have pivoted from horizontal AI-video orchestration into enterprise training-content production and reached $1–3M ARR. Palette can build the product without another technical miracle, but the missing buying curve and crowded horizontal market should force Palette toward a narrower workflow where retained context and customer-specific assets matter more than model access.

**Palette’s strongest bull case is the Color exception:** Pei’s multimodal expertise, 130+ regression tests, 35% faster generation, 50% lower costs, self-serve access, and an API could make Palette the workflow layer above commoditizing models. Palette could then compound proprietary routing data while competitors burn capital training world models. The objection stings because this is precisely my infrastructure-on-top-of-the-hard-thing pattern. The dossier still loses on distribution: Higgsfield already combines proprietary and third-party models at a reported $200M run rate, Krea offers overlapping aggregation, and Palette has no usage curve showing that its workflow is pulling away. Three consecutive months above 20% revenue growth with named enterprise customers would flip my verdict.
