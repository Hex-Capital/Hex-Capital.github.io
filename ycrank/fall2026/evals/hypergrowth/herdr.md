# herdr -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Product-led adoption.** Herdr’s initial user is a developer who repeatedly supervises long-running coding agents across machines. Its host runtime keeps sessions alive through client disconnects, and its clients surface jobs that need attention. GitHub, command-line installers, and app stores give the next thousand users a self-service route in. DigitalOcean’s one-click image and runtime plugin add a concrete installation route. Herdr reports 2.16 million cumulative installs for the original TUI, which measures distribution. A community-built process-relaunch mod shows someone invested effort in keeping the workflow usable. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

The paid growth scenario assumes that some developers with recurring multi-host workloads will pay Herdr Cloud to connect their own machines and add paid hosts as usage grows. U.S. App Store ratings give Ankara-based Herdr an early signal of use abroad. The same SSH-based product could serve U.S. developers, while paid adoption would test whether billing, security requirements, or support create extra work in that market. If users continue to supply their own compute, incremental costs would likely come from connection infrastructure, security operations, and setup and support time. The memo gives a Cloud waitlist with undisclosed pricing. Paid conversion, cohort retention, expansion, and marginal costs remain unmeasured. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

The free TUI may have attracted an audience that has little reason to buy Cloud. Existing TUI sessions are unavailable in the desktop and mobile apps, so moving to the newer runtime requires starting new sessions. Developers who use a single agent provider may already get enough parallel-task supervision from that provider’s app. These are concrete risks to conversion. The memo leaves a structural ceiling on demand and proportional delivery costs unproven, so the Pass gate remains unmet. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a U.S. cohort of developers with recurring multi-host coding-agent workloads activate and pay for Herdr Cloud through self-service, then keep using it across subsequent work cycles? Cohort records showing that pattern without recurring founder help would move the action to **Reach out**. Free installs and waitlist signups would keep it at **Watch**.
