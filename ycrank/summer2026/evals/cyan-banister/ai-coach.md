# COACH -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

$500,000 ARR after five months makes COACH a lived-problem operator wearing a consensus AI-SaaS costume. COACH is strongest where Mathieu and Thomas Perez have actually carried a quota-shaped problem—door-to-door selling for Réseau ACEH after building Alsace Test to €3.5 million in 14 months—and weakest where I look for a frontier, regulated, capital-intensive swing. I see Biz and enough Tiz; the unanswered question is whether the Rizz belongs to a durable artifact or merely to excellent founders selling a familiar conversation-intelligence product.

**Crusoe** is the high founder-origin rung: Chase Lochmiller could walk onto an oil field because he was of that world, not visiting it with a market map. COACH shares that mechanism because the Perez brothers worked beside field representatives in door-to-door and in-home meetings before COACH analyzed those meetings. The divergence matters: Crusoe’s origin and product were inseparable, while COACH followed Lucy, six pivots, and a capability threshold in 2026-era models. COACH still clears my most important test, but the biography points to sales execution more strongly than to an inevitable lifelong obsession with coaching.

**HQ Trivia** is the low capital-and-regulation rung: delightful software can produce tactile pull while leaving little hard infrastructure underneath, and explosive usage only temporarily concealed that fragility. COACH shows the same softness—speech transcription, post-meeting analysis, and a customer-specific knowledge base that Rilla can already describe—without HQ Trivia’s impossible-to-ignore consumer virality. Recording consent and data retention could become an unsexy regulatory moat, but COACH has published no security, consent-management, or retention controls. For COACH today, regulation is exposure rather than accumulated advantage.

Rilla’s estimated $70 million ARR explains why COACH is only modestly magically weird. Over 18 months, COACH’s recordings could compound into team-specific maps of what winning closers actually do, creating switching costs as coaching becomes calibrated to each customer rather than to a generic model. Yet Rilla already records, transcribes, analyzes, and coaches in-person sales conversations, while Gong has more than $500 million ARR. COACH’s 120% monthly growth and 100% pilot conversion reveal a sharp wedge; they do not reveal an ignored category or an underpriced round.

Mathieu Perez is the named CEO, with Thomas Perez as COO and Nicolas Sebag as CTO, so COACH has a visible tiebreaker rather than three people sharing a title. The counterfactual is simple: documented voting control, founder vesting, and an exit-conflict protocol would move COACH near the top of this dimension; evidence of equal control among the two brothers and a third co-founder would drop COACH toward the GameCrush pattern. COACH receives credit for explicit roles, but blood relation plus silence on ownership means I will not pretend the hard conversation has already happened.

Niantic is the single-anchor parallel for physical verifiability: I played Ingress before I needed a spreadsheet to explain why location could become magic. COACH similarly begins with an observable artifact—a representative records an actual in-person meeting, receives coaching, and can test whether behavior changes on the next visit. COACH’s reported 32% conversion-rate lift is the right kind of physical-world feedback, although the missing sample size keeps it from feeling like Pokémon Go appearing in the street. The product touches reality even if the moat still lives in software.

Rilla’s scale and Gong’s missing face-to-face depth produce my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, COACH reaches $8 million–$15 million ARR and is acquired by Gong as its field-sales conversation layer. COACH’s forward-deployed pilots, reported 100% conversion, and founders who previously built €3.5 million of revenue provide the distribution engine; COACH’s outcome-linked meeting corpus provides the asset Gong would buy rather than rebuild customer by customer. I am never playing the game I seem to be playing: the bet is less “new AI coach” than “three operators become the bridge between digital revenue intelligence and the front door.”

Six pivots, an inaccessible website, undisclosed customer counts, and Rilla at roughly $70 million ARR form the strongest bear case: COACH may be founder-selling pilots whose reported $500,000 ARR dissolves when procurement, recording consent, and renewal cycles arrive. The HQ Trivia exception does not rescue COACH because no explosive consumer pull overrides those weaknesses, and the DeepMind exception does not apply because no proprietary technical leap makes the founders irreplaceable. The bear case stings, but €3.5 million at Alsace Test, field selling alongside Réseau ACEH, and $500,000 ARR in five months outweigh a generic credential deck; COACH’s founders repeatedly turn contact with customers into revenue. Independently verified annual retention below 70% for COACH’s first post-pilot cohort would flip my verdict to Pass.
