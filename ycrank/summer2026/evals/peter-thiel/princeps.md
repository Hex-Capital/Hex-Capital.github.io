# PRINCEPS -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

PRINCEPS’s implied secret is that compute infrastructure creates risks sufficiently novel and measurable to support a new underwriting category. What would prove it wrong? If carriers cannot price those risks more intelligently than ordinary commercial insurers—or if competing MGAs can reproduce the same policies from public market, grid, and contract data. The dossier supplies no falsifying experiment, bound policy, or independent pricing result. More importantly, “compute needs specialized insurance” is no longer a secret: Forward Compute already markets the same outage, contractual-liability, and GPU residual-value coverage.

The beachhead is named but not bounded. Operators, neoclouds, AI labs, infrastructure lenders, SLA breaches, outages, residual values, and model-output liability describe several markets and products, not one small market PRINCEPS can dominate. Its workbench combines useful inputs, but no exclusive rights protect them. Parametrix monitors 7,000 cloud services and 750 data centers; American Compute reports 600,000 hardware-pricing datapoints. Against those assets, an undisclosed model and a nonexclusive relationship with Ornn are features, not a monopoly mechanism.

The team is intelligent and adjacent to the problem. Anya Trofimova has data-center project-finance experience; Wilhem Hector has modeled energy systems and uncertainty; Conrad Frøyland Moe has sold events generating more than £15,000 each. But adjacency is not yet alignment. The founders share Oxford affiliations, while the dossier establishes neither substantial prior work together nor full-time status, ownership, or economic exposure. Recruiting an actuary also reveals that the essential underwriting competence is not demonstrably inside the founding team. Missing evidence is not refutation at pre-seed, but foundations cannot be inferred from résumés.

The strongest case for PRINCEPS is that the absence of long loss histories may itself create the opportunity. More than $20 billion of GPU-backed debt and forecast 2026 data-center spending of $822 billion could force carriers to outsource a new risk model; the first MGA to bind policies could turn claims and telemetry into proprietary data. Ornn could become an initial distribution surface, and two GTM hires show that the founders do not regard sales as an afterthought. Yet none of this has crossed the line from plausible architecture to observed economics: there is no carrier commitment, policy, premium, fee schedule, named insured, signed LOI, conversion rate, or sales-cycle evidence.

My 36-month base case is that PRINCEPS has pivoted from proprietary compute underwriting into compute-risk brokerage or analytics and remains below $1 million ARR. The market may become enormous while the obvious investment remains competitive: value creation by AI infrastructure does not imply value capture by this intermediary. One verifiable condition would flip my view—a carrier binder granting PRINCEPS delegated authority for one standardized policy covering at least ten paying neoclouds. Until then, this is a fashionable category with competent founders, not a demonstrated secret with a path to monopoly. I pass.
