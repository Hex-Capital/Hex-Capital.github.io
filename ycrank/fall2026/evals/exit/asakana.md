# Asakana -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a food-distribution ERP vendor is Asakana’s strongest path in Fall 2026. The vendor could buy Asakana to embed its catalog matching and back-office workflows for orders, returns, credits, and purchase orders. Owning the software and any transferable distributor-specific mappings could save the buyer the work of recreating them and give it control over how the workflows evolve. That ownership advantage is an inference. One distributor reported roughly 60 manually entered orders a day and reps spending about 30% of their day on the work. Asakana reports live seafood-distributor deployments and says they return 40%+ of order-desk time to selling. The memo supplies no buyer economics or evidence of acquisition interest. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The operating milestone is to reproduce the labor savings across independent distributors and different legacy ERPs while measuring fulfillment errors, exception-handling labor, and integration costs. Live deployments give Asakana a starting point. The memo records two awards totaling $20,000 and confirms YC participation, but gives conflicting financing estimates and no burn, runway, or rollout budget. A buyer would also need to verify IP ownership and whether customer data, contracts, and integrations can transfer. The memo supplies no usable evidence on those mechanics or on an independent fallback that could pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The illustrative seed case assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in holder cash, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The sale scenarios below use hypothetical prices and timing. They assume enterprise value equals equity consideration, with zero net debt and deal fees, no senior preference deductions, and pro rata allocation. All sale consideration is cash at close, with zero stock, escrow, earnout, rollover, or compensation. Actual financing and waterfall terms are undisclosed. (Market & Traction)

### Countercase

Choco already posts orders into existing ERPs and flags exceptions, and Pepper already extracts orders from voicemail and email. An ERP vendor could build or license enough of the workflow to serve distributors. Asakana’s broader coverage could still justify ownership if it delivers materially better results at a lower deployment cost. The overlap weakens buyer rationale, but the memo does not establish a price ceiling or show that every credible outcome leaves the holder short. The Pass override is untriggered. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a food-distribution ERP vendor find that owning Asakana creates enough incremental value over building or licensing equivalent workflows to support a price above the holder’s proceeds hurdle? A buyer-validated comparison grounded in repeat deployments, with measured labor savings, error rates, and integration costs, would move the action to Reach out if it supported a price capable of yielding at least $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds under the 3% case.
