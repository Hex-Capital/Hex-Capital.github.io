# Limnion -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Limnion has a founder I would take seriously and a round I cannot yet underwrite. Todd Persen co-founded InfluxData and Era Software, sold Era to ServiceNow in 2022, and subsequently served as Hydrolix CTO. Those are operating receipts in the actual problem area. But the proposed post-money, competing offers, and use of proceeds are undisclosed. Array Ventures, Foundation Capital, and YC backing establishes that capital has arrived; it establishes neither a cheap entry nor an auction. I would pass on the evidence available.

The interesting economic proposition is customer ownership of telemetry. Siglake writes OpenTelemetry logs and traces into externally readable Iceberg tables on customer-controlled storage. That could let an engineering team escape a proprietary backend without surrendering its data to the next vendor. Limnion fits my pattern of backing bridges between systems with conflicting incentives: customers want portability; vendors benefit from captivity. The difficulty is capturing the value created. Apache-2.0 licensing and open storage formats also let another operator sell the managed service. Limnion needs an advantage in deployment, reliability, or customer access that survives everyone understanding the architecture.

Persen is the strongest argument against passing. He has built across time-series databases, log management, and streaming analytics; this is substantially more than an impressive title attached to an unfamiliar market. His history earns the highest dimension score, although the dossier does not establish proprietary access to telemetry buyers or direct operation on their side of the purchasing decision. The bull case is that he knows precisely which expensive parts of existing systems customers will happily discard. A three-person team can exploit that knowledge before a broader observability vendor reorganizes its product. But 577 GitHub stars and three forks, observed November 3, 2026, do not establish that anyone has made that choice in production.

What would this check prove? The unsuccessful 72-hour soak attempt and outstanding tenant-isolation and failover qualification identify concrete engineering work. They do not establish a capital-funded experiment, a deadline, or a buyer willing to pay after it passes. A successful soak test would reduce technical risk; it would not prove a business. The managed BYOC offering remains in development, prices are unpublished, and there is no disclosed revenue, margin, burn, or break-even plan. Those are missing underwriting inputs, rather than evidence of bad economics. The “agentic era” framing adds no credit: competitors already expose telemetry to agents, and the storage business must work on its own.

I checked the exceptions to my price and capital-plan gates. Limnion has the prior founder exit, but lacks the documented conversion, retained demand, cash economics, or alternative financing structure that would justify an override. Nor does the dossier show pressure tactics, equal-founder paralysis, or economics dependent on model errors disappearing; I am not inventing those red flags. My concern is narrower: demonstrated engineering ability has not yet become demonstrated commercial leverage. One verifiable flip condition would be an underwriting package showing a sub-$10-million post-money entry, three unrelated customers renewing contribution-positive BYOC deployments, and a budget reaching break-even from this round. That would connect the founder’s experience to an asset my money can actually own.
