# Risklytics -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition by an established commercial insurance brokerage** is the strongest path. A buyer could acquire a renewing book of frontier-tech clients and the placement workflow that serves them. Ownership could be worth paying for if it secures recurring commissions and helps the buyer place difficult robotics and autonomy risks more efficiently than building a specialty desk or using a wholesale partner. [The Idea; Defensibility]

The memo documents a public tracker covering 736 AI-exclusion filings across 49 states and 33 insurers. The founders report placing coverage for three previously denied businesses in one week and say carriers typically pay commissions of 10–20% of premium. Those signals support testing whether early placements become profitable renewals. Actual commission revenue, retention, servicing costs, and runway are unverified. State licensing and carrier-controlled underwriting affect the ability to scale, and the memo gives no terms for transferring the book or carrier relationships. There is no separately supported fallback that pays this holder. [Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Facts; Key Risks]

**Proceeds bridge.** The illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-on checks. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds. The hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% ownership and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. The memo provides no usable acquisition-price evidence.

The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, no price adjustments or transaction costs, and a simple pro rata waterfall with no senior preferences. Enterprise value therefore equals distributable equity consideration. All consideration is cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each $0.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Gross cash to holder | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale at $40 million enterprise value | Year 5 | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | Year 7 | $3 million | 6× |
| Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested | Through year 8 | $0, with shares retained | 0× |

### Countercase

A specialist broker can use the public filings and its own carrier relationships, and Alton Risk already places robotics coverage. A potential buyer could meet the same client need through a wholesale partnership while avoiding the cost of acquiring Risklytics. The memo establishes a credible substitute for the placement service, while a retained book of profitable renewals could still give a buyer a reason to own it. The evidence supports Watch because it leaves both that ownership advantage and the price ceiling unresolved. [Defensibility; Market & Traction]

### Decisive diligence question

Can Risklytics build a transferable renewal book that a commercial brokerage would value above the investor’s proceeds hurdle? A documented renewal cohort showing retained commissions and servicing costs would move the action to Reach out if those results support a buyer’s economic case for ownership over partnering at roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity value under the 3% scenario.
