# Prized -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Hudson Griffith built Gander’s core architecture and AI agents before its October 2025 acquisition, while Prized’s weakest feature is that nothing about it escapes the browser. Prized is an operator-built security layer for AI-generated internal tools: credible lived adjacency at the founder level, paired with almost none of the ugly, regulated, capital-intensive physicality that usually makes me reach for my own checkbook. The signature is competent proximity without magical weirdness.

Anduril is the high rung because Palmer Luckey shipped a difficult physical product before carrying that accumulated obsession into defense. Prized shares part of that mechanism: Griffith was Gander’s first engineer, Marinos Eliades worked beside him there, and both built software and integrations before Prized. The divergence is load-bearing. Prized documents professional adjacency, but not the specific moment Griffith or Eliades personally felt an internal-tools permissions failure. They know the workshop; I cannot yet tell whether the splinter is in their own hand.

HandUp is the low rung on capital intensity because dental financing lacked a frontier moat; the override was a founder story that touched my own history of homelessness. Prized is similarly software-only, but Prized has no comparable biographical override. Database roles, a secret broker, outbound-call controls, and destructive-migration approvals are useful plumbing, not a rocket, drone, camera, or regulated care system. “Secure AI infrastructure” sounds hard until Retool and Superblocks are already selling neighboring controls.

Hudson Griffith is the named CEO, which gives Prized more structural clarity than the HQ Trivia partnership ever had. Over the next 18 months, Prized’s hardest decisions will involve whether to loosen connector permissions for growth, who accepts liability for generated writes, and how quickly to ship around administrator approvals; those decisions compound only if Griffith is the actual tiebreaker. Prized names him CEO, but provides no evidence about equity, founder vesting, or an exit-conflict protocol. That is a rebuttal, not a hard no, but I have seen the movie.

Prized’s self-service sign-in and live workspace make the product more observable than a bare B2B API, yet Prized remains an abstraction behind company connectors and administrator approval. The physical-verifiability score would flip upward if a normal operations employee could enter a sandbox, build a working tool against representative data within 48 hours, and show me the resulting audit trail without founder assistance. Today there is no public customer deployment, repeat usage, or even a named design partner demonstrating that experience.

Crusoe was magically weird because burning stranded gas to mine Bitcoin repelled investors for reasons intrinsic to the idea. Prized is entering the opposite environment: Lovable reported $200M ARR, Retool has raised $140.12M, Superblocks has raised $60M, and every enterprise app platform is racing toward governed natural-language generation. Prized’s bring-your-own-agent design and per-tool security controls may be clever, but clever positioning inside a consensus wave is not non-consensus. I am never playing the game I seem to be playing; here the game looks exactly like the game.

Retool, with $140.12M raised and overlapping natural-language internal-app capabilities, will acquire Prized within 36 months as a security-control-plane tuck-in while Prized remains below $5M ARR. Prized can build valuable secret brokering, permission mapping, and audit infrastructure without building independent distribution, and Prized’s administrator dependency makes an incumbent channel more valuable than another year of founder-led outreach.

Gander’s October 2025 acquisition supplies the strongest bull case: Griffith and Eliades have shipped together, Griffith later worked at Suno, and Eliades combines computer science, mechanical engineering, AI onboarding, and Cyprus National Guard service—the polymath pattern that made DeepMind an exception to my normal founder-origin test. Prized could become the governed execution layer that every coding agent needs, with Retool and Superblocks validating demand rather than foreclosing it. The evidence that wins today is still 38 LinkedIn followers, no disclosed revenue, no named customer, no demonstrated moat, and four well-capitalized competitors able to extend into Prized’s scope. I would flip to Invest if Prized verifies at least $1M ARR from 20 named organizations with twelve-month retention and repeated employee-created tools inside each workspace.
