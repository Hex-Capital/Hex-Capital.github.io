# Experiential Labs -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Kion Fallah led mixed-reality simulation at Waabi, while Silen Naihin contributed to AutoGPT and researched continual learning before Experiential Labs existed. That is Experiential Labs’s signature: unusually domain-native founders building an entirely cerebral software layer. The highest signal is WHO arrived at the problem; the lowest is the absence of anything capital-intensive, regulated, or physically stubborn. Experiential Labs has Tiz in abundance. I am still looking for the strange material constraint that turns research into destiny.

Niantic is the high rung because John Hanke spent a decade inside maps and location before Pokémon Go made that history visible. Experiential Labs carries a real version of that mechanism: Fallah built autonomous-vehicle simulations, Naihin worked on agent evaluation, and the two researched together for six months. Experiential Labs diverges because neither founder has yet operated the production-agent system whose pain they claim to solve; they studied and built adjacent machinery, but no dossier fact shows the autobiographical moment when a deployed agent failed in their hands. They are of the technical neighborhood, though not yet demonstrably of the exact house.

HQ Trivia is the low rung on frontier defensibility: a vivid software artifact whose virality could not compound into a physical, regulatory, or capital moat. Experiential Labs shows the same softness without HQ Trivia’s tactile override. Experiential Labs is an OpenAI-compatible endpoint competing with OpenPipe, Together AI, and Fireworks AI; no factory, license, specialized hardware, or ugly regulated workflow slows those platforms down. A 216-star optimizer is an artifact, but it is not a rocket, drone, camera, well pad, or ketamine clinic.

Experiential Labs has 216 GitHub stars and 26 forks, which is enough signal to compare its weirdness with Crusoe—but only on this axis. Crusoe stapled Bitcoin, stranded gas, and North Dakota infrastructure together when sophisticated investors recoiled from all three. Experiential Labs combines simulation, continual training, token compression, and routing, yet it occupies the hottest neighborhood in venture: AI infrastructure backed by YC and angels associated with NVIDIA, Meta, Waabi, and DeepMind. Experiential Labs is technically unusual, not aesthetically forbidden. “Magically weird” requires people to laugh before they copy; the dossier shows funded incumbents positioned to copy already.

Kion Fallah is explicitly Founder and CEO, while Silen Naihin is listed as Founder, giving Experiential Labs a visible captain but no documented tiebreaker. Experiential Labs would move up a full conviction tier if Fallah held clear operating authority under a founder-vesting and deadlock protocol; evidence of a 50/50 structure would move it sharply down. Six months of prior research together proves collaboration, not governance. I learned from HQ Trivia that friendship and intellectual chemistry do not answer the question that arrives at 2 a.m.: who decides?

Experiential Labs already offers a hosted endpoint, PyPI installation path, and OpenAI-compatible API. Over 18 months, Experiential Labs can make developer-world verifiability compound: every trace can improve simulations, routing, and customer-specific reward data, while every fork tests whether the optimizer works outside the founders’ laboratory. Yet a normal human cannot ride, scan, photograph, or feel the product; even the claimed 40% cost reduction conflicts with the README’s 27% RouterBench result. The artifact is inspectable by engineers, but its value remains mediated by benchmarks and founder testimony.

Together AI has raised $533.5 million and already operates the broader training-and-inference layer, which shapes my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Experiential Labs reaches roughly $2 million to $5 million in ARR and is acquired by Together AI rather than becoming an independent platform. Experiential Labs’s domain-native founders can build a valuable continual-learning capability, but the low frontier moat lets a distributor absorb that capability once customers demand it. This is what the high-founder, low-hardness signature predicts: excellent researchers create the feature; the platform buys the researchers and their loop.

The CLaaS paper reports three times the final pass rate and half the forgetting of in-context learning, which makes the bull case sting. Experiential Labs could be a DeepMind-style Known Exception: credentialed polymaths whose cross-domain compounding matters more than tactile origin, with 216 stars and a working endpoint supplying earlier validation than most pre-seed companies possess. The evidence that wins against that case is narrowness—one adversarial task, no revenue, no named production customer, no audited support for the 40% guarantee, and three well-capitalized platforms capable of product overlap. I remain Neutral and do not write the personal check today; three unrelated customers each renewing contracts above $100,000 ARR after six months of continual optimization would verifiably flip me to Invest.
