# Touchmark -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. AI companies are not buying inference one API call at a time; they are budgeting, hedging, and reselling model capacity as routinely as airlines manage fuel. Touchmark has one small artifact leaking from that future: live order books for named models and delivery months, including one billion GLM 5.2 tokens at $3,800 and MiniMax-M3 tokens at $870. But the dossier does not map the intervening machinery—standardized contracts, provider guarantees, clearing, credit, and enough liquidity to make resale real. This is a plausible future with several unnamed miracles still inside it.

The present-day wound is much clearer. Buyers negotiate private, non-resellable commitments while providers finance GPUs without a transparent forward-demand curve. Touchmark turns that bilateral lockup into something transferable and returns balance-sheet agency to the buyer. That is the best part of the company. The poison is also visible: inference prices fell roughly 600-fold from 2020 to 2026, so buyers have every reason to delay commitments unless the discount is enormous, while sellers have little reason to expose their forward economics. Each model and delivery month further fragments the order book. A market cannot manufacture liquidity merely by displaying prices.

I want to touch the strange behavior, and the dossier gives me only the storefront. GLM 5.2 and MiniMax-M3 books are labeled live, but there is no verified buyer, provider, executed trade, resale, revenue, or volume. The OpenAI-compatible key reduces delivery friction, yet it does not prove anyone entrusted Touchmark with money or capacity. Ornn already trades compute and derivatives, SF Compute reportedly manages more than $100 million of hardware, and OpenRouter has approximately $50 million in annualized revenue plus the distribution to add reservations. Against those systems, an empty forward book is not infrastructure; it is a market-design proposal.

The founders have unusually sharp raw material. Ilia Bolgov combines mathematics and finance with trading-product exposure at Revolut; Roman Yanushevskyi brings Citadel Securities quantitative research, Lovable AI engineering, and an IOI gold medal. That supports their ability to price contracts and build delivery plumbing. It does not yet show pre-company obsession, customer-earned operating knowledge, or a first-believer artifact. The July 2026 announcement concerned quality-based output pricing, while the August product is a forward-capacity market. A one-month conceptual migration makes me wonder whether they discovered a durable system wound or are rotating through attractive AI-finance abstractions.

The bull case stings: inference is becoming a major operating expense, these founders speak both finance and software, and the first exchange that establishes credible settlement could accumulate proprietary price history and liquidity. Pre-revenue does not bother me when a functioning artifact, committed counterparties, or lifelong technical obsession substitutes for revenue. Touchmark discloses none of those substitutes. It also controls no scarce capacity, exclusive distribution, or unique data today. Both team members are presented simply as founders, with no disclosed co-founder history or accountable decision-maker; that is not evidence of a governance defect, but it leaves an important gate unresolved.

My base case is that within 36 months Touchmark has pivoted from resellable model-month contracts into ordinary inference procurement or routing because the forward books never develop repeat liquidity. I would not write the check today. One result flips me: verifiable settlement of at least $1 million in capacity across three model providers, including completed resales and repeat buyers. That would prove the little artifact is becoming a market rather than merely depicting one.
