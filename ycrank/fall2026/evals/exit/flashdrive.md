# FlashDrive -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is FlashDrive’s strongest path. The memo records a November 15 launch, 28,040 launch-post views by November 17, and plans by Dropbox to add filesystem capabilities to its agent platform. FlashDrive says it merges simultaneous Markdown and CSV edits across local mounts and agent sandboxes. The memo supplies no production results or paid deployments that verify the benefit. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

An agent-platform operator could buy FlashDrive to make reliable cross-environment writes a native capability. Ownership could justify a premium if a production test shows materially fewer lost edits or manual reconciliations and the operator finds that building or licensing equivalent behavior would cost more or take materially longer. The launch makes a pilot plausible. Its funding and FlashDrive’s runway are unknown, and SOC 2 reports in progress could delay enterprise testing. IP ownership and transfer terms are also unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The memo provides no investment terms or usable acquisition price evidence. The illustrative seed case assumes one $500,000 check for 5% initial ownership, diluted to 3%, with no follow-on checks. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

For the modeled sales, assume zero adjustments from enterprise value to equity consideration, a simple pro rata waterfall, and all cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each $0. Actual debt, preferences, and deal terms are unknown. The sale prices below are scenario assumptions.

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value and distributable equity | Holder cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $40M | $1.2M | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100M | $3.0M | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No sale or payout | $0 | 0× |

The assumed strong sale clears the hurdle at 3% and yields only 4× at 2%. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public-market scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The investor retains the shares. The memo supplies no recurring cash flow, demand for existing shares, or other evidence of an independent route to holder cash. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

AWS and Daytona already bundle shared storage with their agent environments, and Layven offers shared MCP storage with attributed versions. If those options handle the workflows buyers care about, an operator can bundle or license storage without buying FlashDrive. The memo leaves open whether those substitutes match reliable concurrent merges across environments and what that difference is worth. The ownership and price gaps warrant Watch. The evidence does not establish a binding price cap or rule out meaningful proceeds after a successful milestone, so the Pass override does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a platform operator’s paid production pilot establish an economic case for acquiring FlashDrive near the $83.3 million illustrative equity hurdle? Measured reductions in write conflicts and recovery work, together with the operator’s costed comparison of buying, building, and licensing, would answer it. Evidence that ownership supports a price near the hurdle would move the action to Reach out. A bundled or licensed option that delivers comparable results and caps the price materially below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
