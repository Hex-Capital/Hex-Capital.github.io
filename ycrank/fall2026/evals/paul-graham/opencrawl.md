# OpenCrawl -- Paul Graham Evaluation

OpenCrawl changed products. What did the founders learn? September 29 coverage describes OnePatch as an AI SRE; by October 27, the company is selling search infrastructure. The useful evidence would be the observation that killed the first hypothesis and the experiment that established the second. Neither is documented. Changing direction can reveal resourcefulness, but a different product name does not establish it. I cannot identify a named obstacle, a founder-built workaround, and the time it took to work. That leaves my most important criterion at its missing-evidence default.

There is evidence that these founders can build. Their previous product reportedly ingested **32 TB of telemetry** and detected or investigated **600+ incidents**. Those are concrete technical claims, worth more than an architecture diagram, though they remain founder-reported and concern observability. I give them partial technical credit. I cannot transfer those numbers to OpenCrawl’s retrieval quality, crawl freshness, or permission enforcement. The current offering describes crawlers, maintained indexes, and MCP, CLI, and API access; the dossier supplies no measured demonstration of that system.

The strongest argument for investing is the combination of the two founders’ experience. Shalin Patel built ingestion infrastructure at Pocus. Deep Utkarsh built GTM agents there and worked on a query compiler at Sigma. They also share a workplace history. These are credible people to discover that assembling useful context for agents is harder than it looks. OpenCrawl fits my recurring interest in programmable infrastructure, and their previous product supplies some evidence of continued building through a change of direction. But the dossier does not show that this particular idea arose from a problem they personally encountered. Nor does it explain which ugly integration burden they have already mastered. Relevant experience makes that story plausible; it does not establish the story.

Competition makes the missing user especially consequential. Firecrawl’s September 2026 Alexandria announcement already combines source discovery, connectors, and hosted indexes. OpenCrawl could still win by doing the tedious work for a small group whose sources existing products handle badly. Founder-led deployment calls are a sensible way to find that group. But who is it? The **$25 starter grant**, **$10 monthly allowance**, and **$10 minimum top-ups** describe an invitation to try the product. They establish neither payment nor return usage. The prior incident count belongs to the prior product. Current demand therefore receives the default score, without treating undisclosed traction as rejection.

I would pass today. There is no documented dependence on a large partner, ceremonial launch, or premature sales hiring, so I am not finding an active founder red flag. I am finding that the central founder test remains unanswered. I also checked the exception: a compelling working prototype or unmistakable user pull could outweigh incomplete founder history. OpenCrawl’s current evidence does not establish either strongly enough. One verifiable flip condition would be a documented customer deployment in which an existing search tool failed on a specific source, the founders shipped a working fix within weeks, and that customer then paid and used the resulting search repeatedly for a month. That single sequence would reveal the founders, the product, and the narrow well.
