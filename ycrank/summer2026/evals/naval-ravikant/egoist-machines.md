# Egoist Machines -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

David Khachaturov’s Cambridge machine-learning PhD, OpenAI PaperBench work, and 4.7K-star BetterJoy repository make Egoist Machines a specific-knowledge company trapped, for now, inside a non-network product. Egoist Machines knows how to build the passport, but has not shown that one passport makes another passport more valuable. The highest signal is rare technical knowledge. The lowest is user-added compounding. Code without a network is leverage, but not yet escape velocity.

**Replit** is the high rung for specific knowledge: Amjad Masad spent years building programming environments before the market formed around him. Egoist Machines approaches that rung through Khachaturov’s AI-security research and shipped open-source work, while Erin McGurk contributes privacy interests and a 1-million-plus-follower consumer audience. Egoist Machines diverges where it matters: Replit itself was the public artifact, whereas LodeDB has only 89 stars and is adjacent to an unreleased AI Passport. The founders possess non-trainable knowledge; founder-product-market fit is promising but not yet proven through sustained Passport use.

**Perplexity** is the low rung on network compounding: each user initially received a better search product without directly improving every prior user’s experience. Egoist Machines currently has the same weakness, only sharper—no live integration count, retention evidence, or Passport switching-cost data exists. Egoist Machines could become a two-sided identity layer where applications follow users and users attract applications, but that is inferred architecture, not observed behavior. The Perplexity exception partially applies because strong technical knowledge and permissionless software can waive the network requirement; it does not justify pretending the network already exists.

Erin McGurk’s audience of more than 1 million followers gives Egoist Machines genuine media leverage, while a free-for-life passport gives Egoist Machines code leverage with low marginal distribution cost. Over 18 months, that combination can compound: media acquires users, user demand attracts developers, integrations increase passport utility, and utility produces more media. The break in the loop is gatekeeper permission. OpenAI, Google, and every third-party application must expose enough surface area for portability, so the product is permissionless at sign-up but permissioned at integration. That places the architecture below Twitter’s open media loop, though well above ordinary SDR-led enterprise software.

OpenAI and Google already store assistant-specific memories, proving that the market for persistent context is broad while also threatening Egoist Machines at the protocol layer. Egoist Machines has pivot-room into consent infrastructure, personal-data vaults, agent identity, developer memory APIs, or compliance receipts. The market score would flip down a tier if providers standardize interoperable memory and make the independent layer redundant; it would flip up if three major non-affiliated AI applications adopt Passport and users carry context among them without bilateral implementation work. The first idea rarely works. The surrounding market is large enough to survive that fact.

**Stack Overflow** is the useful partner parallel: practicing builders shipped for builders before asking the market for permission. Egoist Machines has a similar intelligence-and-energy read through Khachaturov’s 178-commit LodeDB work and McGurk’s million-person creator reach. Egoist Machines has no evidence of contested exits, ethics promotion, or unresolved leadership—the CEO and CTO roles are named—but also no public record showing how the founders treat collaborators through adversity. Intelligence and energy are legible. Integrity receives the absent-evidence default. Play long-term games only after the people prove they can play them.

The July 13, 2026 waitlist launch leads to a falsifiable forecast: by July 2029, Egoist Machines will have pivoted from a consumer-first AI Passport into developer-facing consent and personal-context infrastructure, producing $1 million to $3 million in ARR while the consumer passport remains below 100,000 monthly active users. Egoist Machines has stronger specific knowledge than consumer-network evidence, so the technical layer should monetize before the network does. The passport is likely to become the distribution surface for an infrastructure business, not the business itself.

**Mem0’s** $24 million financing frames the strongest bear case: Egoist Machines may be squeezed between developer-memory vendors below and OpenAI or Google above, while the sales@egoistmachines.com address and developer-pilot solicitation drift toward permissioned enterprise distribution. Egoist Machines would then violate the unstated organic-adoption pattern, and the Anchorage exception would not save it because integration dependence is not yet a regulatory moat. The evidence keeping Egoist Machines investable is Khachaturov’s shipped 4.7K-star artifact plus McGurk’s million-follower media channel—technical and distribution leverage already exist before Passport launch. I would flip to Pass if Egoist Machines cannot show three independent production integrations and at least 25% 90-day consumer retention within twelve months.
