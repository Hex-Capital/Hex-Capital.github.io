# Sutrix -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with hospital and academic research teams, Sutrix has a plausible route to serving the same recurring job across markets. Its product reconciles EHR, REDCap, and wearable records, then applies investigator-defined study rules. The same preparation workflow could carry over where teams can use standard exports and configure their own protocols. Documented FHIR connectivity is limited to sandboxes, so the production work required at a new institution remains uncertain. Founder introductions and direct outreach to biostatistics cores provide a concrete acquisition channel. Individually scoped agreements and deployment by arrangement point to a high-touch path to payment and first use. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Versioned study rules, field-level provenance, and saved reviewer decisions give teams a reason to return when data or protocols change. More studies could expand account spend if they generate additional paid work. Recurring billing and paid expansion within existing accounts remain unverified. Sutrix reported more than $150K in revenue over a few months before the software release and paying teams at more than 10 institutions by November, with a listed team of two. Customer testimonials corroborate workflow value. The revenue mix and comparable post-release growth remain unclear. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

In a standard-export rollout scenario, contribution on the next engagement would be its fee less attributable sales and setup time, data processing and storage, and support for exceptions. Automated normalization and investigator-led review could reduce Sutrix’s delivery labor. Engagement prices and Sutrix’s staff time are undisclosed, so positive incremental contribution remains a scenario assumption. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Sutrix could become a successful study-preparation service whose delivery effort grows with each institution and protocol. Individually negotiated scope and timelines, production data access dependencies, and investigator verification support that risk. The structural gate stays open because standard exports and configurable rules offer a plausible transfer path, and the memo leaves the amount and cost of required Sutrix labor unresolved. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can several new biostatistics cores, including one in another market, get from purchase to a second paid study using standard exports and configurable rules, with minimal Sutrix staff time and positive contribution? Fees, tracked staff hours, and processing costs across those deployments would answer it. A repeatable result would support Reach out if the updated score reaches 65. Evidence that unavoidable bespoke work makes faster growth uneconomic would change the action to Pass.
