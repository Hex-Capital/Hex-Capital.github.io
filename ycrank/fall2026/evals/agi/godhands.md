# GodHands -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**New Economies** is the strongest path. GodHands says its desktop app captures work in approved apps and turns it into source-linked playbooks that employees and agents can use. A company-published fintech testimonial says a replacement controller closed the books on time in month two, compared with two quarters previously. Another says coding agents began following internal conventions. These reports support the mechanism that captured workflow knowledge can help people and agents handle exceptions. The labor cost per successful outcome and the opening of a new market remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

A plausible scenario is a support team with a low-volume, exception-heavy queue that was previously too costly to automate. The baseline includes expert time spent shadowing, writing and maintaining procedures, and resolving cases manually. GodHands would need to lower the cost per correctly resolved case after accounting for setup, inference, approvals, and rework. The team is a plausible expansion unit under the quoted subscription model. Contribution depends on what GodHands spends on deployment, inference, and support, while customer approval time affects the buyer’s savings. Those inputs remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Key Risks)

A logistics software support lead reported that GodHands documented its escalation process within two weeks. That is an early usage signal from a target team, with payment and repeat use still unknown. The founders’ prior work together, the CTO’s systems experience, and the October product launch support seed-stage execution credit. Reliable handling of exceptions across deployments remains the next execution test. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The strongest countercase is that GodHands makes existing handovers and workflow documentation cheaper while buyers can get comparable results from Scribe or Skan AI. Retained, source-linked playbooks give customers a specific reason to stay if rebuilding their exception history is costly. That switching cost is inferred, and buyer preference or network effects under functional parity remain unproven. The memo establishes product overlap while actual buyer switching and equivalent outcomes remain untested. It also supplies no independent case that GodHands controls a scarce complement. The New Economies path remains open at Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent support team renew at a price that covers GodHands’ delivery costs after using it to automate an exception queue it previously handled manually because automation was too costly, when it can choose a functionally comparable alternative? A paid deployment with a measured reduction in cost per correctly resolved case, including approvals and rework, followed by renewal under that choice would move the action to Reach out. An inconclusive comparison would keep it at Watch.
