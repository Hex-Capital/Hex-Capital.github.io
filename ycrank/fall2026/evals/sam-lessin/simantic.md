# Simantic -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Simantic has raised approximately $600,000, but I cannot tell what price I would pay or what my check would prove. The dossier supplies neither post-money valuation nor competing offers; YC acceptance establishes neither cheap entry nor an auction. More consequentially, comparing simulation against silicon is a technical validation plan, but it is not yet a capital plan with a deadline and a measurable result. The interesting financing question is whether one check can establish reliable simulation for a bounded set of commercially valuable hardware targets. I cannot credit Simantic with that experiment until the proceeds actually fund it.

The strongest argument for the company is that better firmware generation makes trustworthy testing more valuable. A model release could improve the inputs while leaving the verification bottleneck intact. Hong’s firmware experience at Tesla and robotics companies, Shahriar’s Neuralink implant/charger software and Tesla hardware experience, and their shipped SDK and Cloud Sim beta give this team a credible bridge between embedded engineering and automated development. This fits my pattern of backing people who can reorganize difficult physical workflows through software. The public product is a shipped receipt; the prior employers establish relevant operating exposure, although the dossier does not document individual deployment outcomes.

The decisive distinction is between owning trusted verification and selling convenient access to simulation. Simantic’s SDK defaults to Renode, which already offers peripheral modeling and CI capabilities. Its private engines and alternative Rust backend establish development work, but the dossier does not demonstrate superior fidelity or an accumulated proprietary regression corpus. The SDK also asks users to defer production pipelines. That matters causally: embedding reliable tests into a customer’s release process could create durable workflow ownership, while an unstable interface makes that position harder to earn. Embedder, BoardRepo, and WATFlight are plausible routes into those workflows; announcements and planned testing do not establish customer conversion.

The bull case stings because these founders understand a real bottleneck, have shipped something, and could build infrastructure that benefits from improving AI. I would override missing financing evidence for exceptional paid conversion, retention, or demonstrated proprietary access; those are the mechanisms behind my known exceptions. Simantic has not documented them yet. Equally, I see no evidence of an auction, founder deadlock, or a business whose margins assume agent errors disappear. The specific economic uncertainty is narrower: $100 monthly Developer pricing establishes an offer, while custom enterprise pricing leaves room for a business, but neither supplies margin, burn, or a seed-funded path to profitability. The $200,000–$300,000 researcher opening makes the cost of expanding model coverage worth understanding; it does not establish actual spending or financing dependence.

I pass on the evidence available. The founder fit is stronger than the demonstrated investable asset: Simantic has credible builders, but has not yet shown that it owns purchasing behavior or that scarce capital resolves one valuable uncertainty. One verifiable condition would reopen underwriting: a signed BoardRepo contract that funds a dated, silicon-validated deployment through customer acceptance without another equity raise. That would turn a partnership announcement into evidence about workflow ownership, a finite experiment, and customer-funded economics. I would still need to price the check.
