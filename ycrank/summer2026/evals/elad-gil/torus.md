# Torus -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Marcus Lima has led more than $100M of capital projects and previously built Heimdal to $9.7M raised, while Rahul Thayil built Orion and announced a $3.5M financing. Torus is therefore an execution-rich, curve-poor company: Torus scores highest on founders who have shipped independently and lowest on whether customer deployments are “just working” in dollars rather than generating pilot theater. That pairing describes a credible vertical-AI operator bet whose product maturity is ahead of its commercial proof.

**Perplexity** is the high rung for iterative shipping: Aravind Srinivas repeatedly turned conversations into working product within a week. Torus shares the important underlying mechanism—two founders who have already created funded companies, plus Marcus’s direct experience delivering physical infrastructure—but Torus has not disclosed comparable iteration loops, release cadence, or customer-requested features shipped between meetings. Heimdal, Orion, and the working document-analysis product put Torus well above credential-only founders; the missing feedback-loop evidence keeps Torus below the Perplexity pattern.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism where visible activity did not establish the economically advantaged curve needed to overcome market structure. Torus presents an earlier version of that ambiguity: multiple Fortune 500 companies and major EPCs are reportedly deploying Torus, but Torus discloses no names, contract values, revenue, usage expansion, or retention. At pre-seed, deployments are meaningful, so I score Torus above the absent-evidence default—but without paid conversion or cohort expansion, “multiple enterprises” remains a false signal until proven otherwise.

The **$4.86B AI-in-construction market growing at 24.8% annually** compounds with two recent pressures: AI-related data-center and energy construction, and an estimated 349,000-worker industry shortfall in 2026. Over the next 18 months, those forces should increase project volume while making document review labor harder to staff, giving Torus a real automation tailwind. The limitation is that Torus’s catalyst is inferred from adjacent demand rather than tied to a discrete enabling event like Ukraine for Anduril or APIs for Stripe; modern multimodal models help Torus, but funded competitors show that the same catalyst is broadly available.

**Trunk Tools**, with $70M raised, already interprets drawings against full project-document corpora and powers RFI, revision, bid, and submittal agents. Torus becomes an index on physical-infrastructure construction only if its parameter graph becomes the persistent control layer through which specifications, procurement approvals, and engineering packages flow across projects. If Torus instead remains a five-to-six-document demonstration and consistency checker, Torus must win account by account against Trunk Tools, Augmenta, and incumbent engineering software. Evidence of recurring cross-project graphs or mandatory approval routing would flip this dimension into the top tier.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow infrastructure on top of a validated hard layer, not a wager on new science. Torus similarly assumes capable document models and concentrates the remaining work in parameter extraction, more than 50 consistency-check categories, provenance, and human approval. Torus does not require new physics or new regulation, but engineering-grade accuracy, enterprise security, and workflow adoption are partly independent dependencies; SOC 2 remains in progress, and no accuracy benchmark exists. That is closer to a single execution miracle than a science project, although liability makes the miracle less clean than the website suggests.

Multiple Fortune 500 companies and major EPCs already deploying Torus support a falsifiable forecast: by July 2029, Torus reaches **$6M–$12M ARR** as an engineering-document QA and approval layer, remains independent, and has not yet become the broader construction system of record. Torus can reach that range through repeat-founder execution and painful rework economics without first establishing an index-like position; the missing public buying curve is why I forecast a useful vertical business rather than a Stripe-scale default.

**Trunk Tools’ $70M capitalization** supplies the strongest bear case: Torus has no disclosed moat, no completed SOC 2, no accuracy benchmark, and no evidence that opaque Fortune 500 deployments are paid rather than pilot tourism. Trunk Tools could absorb Torus’s upstream consistency checks while Augmenta owns generated design, leaving Torus squeezed between better-funded systems. Marcus’s $100M capital-project history and two founders with prior funded startups make “unable to ship” the wrong diagnosis, but they do not make Torus investable from my own money yet. Named customers converting to paid deployments with greater than 20% month-over-month revenue growth for three consecutive months would flip my verdict to Invest.
