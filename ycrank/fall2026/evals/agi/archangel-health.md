### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements** is the strongest fit. Frontier labs and medical AI developers need to know whether an agent actually completes a clinical workflow. A static test can miss an agent that documents an intended order but never places it in the EHR. Archangel describes a sandbox that grades actions inside de-identified charts, with physician review and subsequent outcomes for multi-visit tasks. If cheaper models lead developers to test more agents across more workflows, reusable clinical scenarios that expose consequential failures could become a bottleneck. The memo establishes the testing gap and describes Archangel’s approach. It leaves buyer-side performance and the transfer from sandbox results to live care unresolved. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

Archangel reports specialist grading and dataset provenance records. Those practices could help buyers audit the data. Durable value capture would depend on whether Archangel has enforceable rights to reuse longitudinal records across customers and can supply useful coverage that well-funded competitors cannot readily reproduce. The license scope is undisclosed. Path B remains unproven because the memo provides no delivered-labor comparison or evidence that automation opens a previously uneconomic transaction. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*, *The Idea*)

The U.S. seed profile fits the mandate. The public listing of the current offering shows early execution after a pivot, and the founders bring experience in clinical-agent deployments and training-data supply. Archangel claims use by frontier labs and medical AI developers. The memo gives no buyer names, paid commitments, or repeat usage. At seed, meaningful buyer integration work could count before revenue, but the memo does not establish such a commitment. (*Key Facts*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*)

The scale case assumes one specialty evaluation suite can be licensed to multiple buyers. Under that assumption, each additional license could spread the cost of acquiring records, de-identifying them, building scenarios, and validating them with physicians. If each customer instead requires bespoke charts and substantial regrading, those costs recur with each sale. The memo describes licensing and custom evaluations, with pricing, reuse rights, and physician effort per deployment still unknown. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Stanford’s public PhysicianBench already tests multi-step EHR workflows, and Osseus offers overlapping medical evaluations and training environments. With cheap models and ample capital, labs could use public benchmarks for baseline testing and build or buy custom evaluations from competing providers. If those options catch the same consequential failures at acceptable cost, Archangel’s grading and provenance would give buyers little reason to pay a premium. The memo establishes credible alternatives and leaves like-for-like performance unresolved. That blocks outreach today and leaves a feasible test for Watch. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)
