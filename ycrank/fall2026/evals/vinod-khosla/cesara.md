# Cesara -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Cesara could make hospitals materially better at collecting what their contracts already promise. The consequence I would underwrite is the administrative work eliminated: contract interpretation, payment reconciliation, and appeals. How many dollars of recurring labor disappear? How much cheaper does delivering healthcare become? The dossier establishes a painful workflow but does not quantify that consequence. The adjacent $1.83 billion software market measures vendor spending; it supplies no baseline for an order-of-magnitude improvement in healthcare economics.

The strongest evidence is the founder-reported pilot result: over 90% identification of valid underpayments and over 70% recovery of those identified. That deserves attention at pre-seed. Contract extraction, expected-payment calculation, and clause-supported appeals form a concrete operating mechanism. But those percentages leave precision, human intervention, sample size, and comparative performance unresolved. A system can recover money while consuming expensive expert labor. MD Clarity already offers overlapping contract intelligence and agentic investigation, including its September 2026 expansion. Cesara has some evidence of functioning software, but no demonstrated 5–10x discontinuity against that incumbent.

The bull case has substance. Rahman previously co-founded Auctor and reports healthcare implementation exposure; Lee brings inference and systems experience from Tesla AI and financial engineering roles. Outcome-based pilots put commercial consequences behind the product’s claims. My actual investment pattern includes execution-heavy software businesses, so demanding a scientific invention here would be too narrow. Cesara might turn implementation knowledge into repeatable infrastructure for healthcare transactions. What remains unproven is the expansion mechanism: neither those credentials nor the CIO endorsements demonstrate exceptional recruiting, rapid belief revision, or a proprietary learning loop. My exceptions for extraordinary founders attacking enormous existing value pools require evidence connecting the initial wedge to that larger consequence. This dossier does not yet supply it.

I would spend the next dollar testing whether Cesara can safely operate with very little human correction across unfamiliar contracts and amendments. Its approval thresholds, customer-data validation, and clause-linked audit trails are sensible controls. They do not disclose the decisive experiment, numerical acceptance threshold, cost, or response to failure. The pilot recovery figures establish activity; they do not establish a disciplined sequence of risk removal. There is also no evidence that the founders are scaling recklessly ahead of validation, so I assign the missing-evidence default rather than inventing an adverse signal.

Outcome-based pricing helps secure a trial, but Cesara’s economics still depend on implementation effort, expert review, appeal handling, and the fee earned from successful recoveries. None is quantified. The relevant red flag is competitive replication: an incumbent is already shipping much of the advertised workflow. That does not prove Cesara is merely a model wrapper, but it makes measured superiority essential. Customer-specific mappings could produce switching costs; the dossier labels that possibility as inference, and I give it no moat credit.

I pass with my own money today. The likely opportunity established here is a useful recovery business; the evidence has not established the much larger option I want to purchase. My flip condition is an independently audited deployment across three unrelated health systems demonstrating at least 10x lower fully loaded administrative cost per correctly recovered dollar than the incumbent workflow, with comparable recovery and erroneous-appeal rates, and a quantified spending baseline showing that the result can eliminate substantial recurring administrative expense. That single study would connect technical performance, risk removal, and economic consequence.
