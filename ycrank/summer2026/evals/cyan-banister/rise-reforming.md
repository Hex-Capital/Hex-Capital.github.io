# Rise Reforming -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

$1.7 million raised and a shipping-container pilot already sitting at a Chicagoland wastewater facility make Rise Reforming a frontier-hardware company with a governance-shaped shadow. Rise Reforming scores highest where chemistry must leave the slide deck and become steel, heat, feedstock, and permits; it scores lowest where three UChicago co-founders have titles but no disclosed tiebreaker protocol. I can touch the machine. I cannot yet see who wins the argument when the machine breaks at 2 a.m.

**SpaceX** is the high rung: capital-intensive hardware whose technical risk frightened away investors and whose physical progress made the risk legible. Rise Reforming shares that mechanism through an integrated reforming process, 1,800-plus hours of stable syngas production, and a delivered pilot container. Rise Reforming diverges because George Rose did not arrive with Elon Musk’s prior operating proof, the first live-wastewater-biogas commissioning remains scheduled for 2027, and M2X has already raised $94.28 million to attack overlapping modular methane conversion. The apparatus is magically weird; the operating proof is still one feedstock short.

**HQ Trivia** is the low rung’s warning: ambiguous founder authority can turn product intensity into a knife fight, and explosive user love only postpones the bill. Rise Reforming names George Rose as CEO, Lucas Zubillaga Maharg as CTO, and Jona van Oord as COO, but Rise Reforming discloses no majority authority, founder-vesting arrangement, or deadlock protocol. The HQ Trivia exception does not rescue the structure because a conditional DME offtake is not tactile consumer virality. Three molecular engineers who met at UChicago may divide responsibilities beautifully; “may” is a rebuttal, not an answer to who decides.

George Rose worked at Nexamp, Highland Electric Fleets, Mantel Capture, and GenH, while Lucas Zubillaga Maharg built Rise Reforming’s bench-scale reformer after Argonne and offshore-wind work. Over the next 18 months, that accumulated technical contact can compound into faster commissioning, feedstock troubleshooting, and process knowledge that outsiders cannot download. Yet Rise Reforming began with plastic waste before pivoting to biogas in 2025, so the origin looks like committed climate engineering more than a wastewater problem personally lived. The outsider pattern is weak, the **HandUp** biographical exception has no analogue, and the **DeepMind** polymath exception remains unearned until Rise Reforming demonstrates unusual cross-domain invention rather than excellent academic preparation.

$750,000 soft-committed toward a $6 million round places Rise Reforming on the interesting side of non-consensus, but not yet at **Crusoe**-level mispricing. For Rise Reforming to flip into the highest rung, credible investors would need to reject the round specifically because decentralized chemical plants are ugly, capital-intensive, or permit-heavy—and George Rose would need to explain why those objections create the moat. M2X, Oberon Fuels, WasteFuel, and Carbon Recycling International show that waste-to-chemicals is no secret category. Rise Reforming may be overlooked, but the dossier does not establish that sophisticated capital has misunderstood it.

1,800-plus stable-production hours give Rise Reforming a **Flock Safety** quality: an artifact exists outside founder testimony. A person can photograph Rise Reforming’s container, watch equipment operate, and eventually observe waste gas enter one end and saleable chemical leave the other. The parallel stops before full conviction because the Chicagoland unit has not processed live wastewater biogas, commissioning is not expected until 2027, and no chemical sale has closed. Physical truth is close enough to touch but not yet close enough to buy.

The 2027 commissioning schedule leads me to predict that by July 2029 Rise Reforming will have one operating wastewater installation producing specification-grade chemical, less than $1 million in annualized chemical revenue, and a formalized founder hierarchy after at least one operating role changes. Rise Reforming’s hardware depth should get the pilot across the line; its undisclosed tiebreaker and build-own-operate capital burden will slow replication. I am never playing the game I seem to be playing: the near-term asset is not methanol revenue but the operating knowledge created when dirty biogas meets a real reformer.

M2X’s $94.28 million financing, commercially validated modular systems, and Rise Reforming’s absence of chemical revenue form the painful bear case. Biomethanol costing roughly three times marine gas oil could leave Rise Reforming owning expensive containers that customers admire but do not economically need, while three academically adjacent founders discover that process engineering, project finance, and commodity sales are three companies stapled together. Rise Reforming earns the check because the delivered container, binding biogas-supply agreement, conditional DME offtake, and 1,800-plus production hours are unusually concrete for pre-seed. I would flip to Pass if the pilot cannot sustain 1,000 continuous hours on live wastewater biogas while producing contract-specification DME or methanol by December 2027.
