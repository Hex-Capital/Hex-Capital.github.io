# Pango -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Steve Rahimi spent seven years building e-commerce businesses before researching reverse logistics, while Pango remains a software layer in a category where Narvar and parcelLab already advertise agents. That pairing is Pango’s signature: founder biography is the strongest signal; frontier difficulty is the weakest. Pango looks less like a tourist’s AI memo than an operator’s accumulated scar tissue, but the scar tissue currently produces a useful orchestration product, not a magically weird artifact.

**Niantic — John Hanke compounding a decade of Keyhole and Google Earth into location-native play** is my high rung for lived origin. Pango shares the load-bearing mechanism: Rahimi started in e-commerce at 17 and wrote a reverse-logistics thesis, while Lukasz Reszczynski previously built logistics infrastructure and a configurator used for more than $1.5 billion in quotes. Pango diverges because neither founder has yet shipped a comparable commerce system to Hanke-scale adoption; 30 brands over 12 months is meaningful pre-seed evidence, but Pango still sits below the high rung where biography and proven scale become inseparable.

**The consensus YC AI-agent darling — replaceable founders wrapping a fashionable model** is the low rung for frontier difficulty. That failure mechanism appears uncomfortably close to Pango: Narvar already markets agentic post-purchase actions, parcelLab markets AI agents, and AfterShip owns more than 1,300 carrier integrations. Pango handles money, addresses, refunds, and EEA data transfers, but ordinary GDPR obligations and an unfinished ISO 27001 process do not create the regulated-category moat of Mindbloom or Vigil Labs. Pango is software integration complexity dressed in an “agentic OS” coat; I can see the coat, but I cannot yet see the rocket underneath it.

Steve Rahimi is explicitly CEO and Lukasz Reszczynski is explicitly CTO, which gives Pango more structural clarity than a shared-title partnership. Over the next 18 months, however, Pango’s 100-plus integrations will generate disagreements about product breadth, autonomous refund risk, enterprise customization, and whether Checkout+ should remain free. Pango compounds only if “CEO” means actual tiebreaker authority; with no public co-founder history, equity structure, vesting terms, or conflict protocol, the dossier leaves the mechanism half-built. A title is a clue, not a constitution.

Pango’s $550,000 pre-seed round and backing from HEARTFELT, SSE Business Lab, Wave Ventures, Karaoke Club, and angels do not prove a consensus auction, but “AI for e-commerce operations” is not magically weird on its face. Pango would flip upward if sophisticated investors rejected autonomous returns as too operationally dangerous, and Pango then demonstrated that merchant-defined agents could safely execute refunds across messy carrier systems when incumbents could not. Instead, Pango enters beside funded platforms already adding autonomous actions, so the non-consensus thesis remains a rebuttal awaiting evidence rather than a reason to write the check.

**Flock Safety** was observable before the investment argument existed: a camera and even a Wi-Fi name leaked the founder’s obsession into the physical world. Pango offers a weaker but real parallel—merchants can install the Shopify app, customers can use Pango-generated return portals, and Switch Nails reports 99% self-service returns with 19% retained as exchanges or credit. Pango is therefore more tactile than a developer API, yet the claimed 99% automation occurs behind screens and integrations; five Shopify reviews are not the involuntary, ambient product signal that made Flock impossible to ignore.

Pango’s 30 brands, 60% month-over-month growth, and 100-plus integrations point to a specific 36-month outcome: by July 2029, Pango reaches $4 million to $7 million in ARR and is acquired by parcelLab for its Shopify workflow layer rather than becoming an independent post-purchase category leader. Pango’s founder-market fit should produce execution and merchant adoption, while Pango’s low frontier moat lets larger suites copy the agent interface and turns the integration footprint into acquisition value rather than sovereign infrastructure.

Pango’s strongest bull case is the **DeepMind exception**: Reszczynski’s astrophysics, molecular-biology, patented-configurator, and logistics background looks like the polymath compounding I will occasionally fund over a conventional outsider biography. Add 30 brands, 60% monthly growth, 99% reported automation, and 20% reported cost reduction, and Pango may be a quiet systems company hiding under an AI wrapper—“I’m never playing the game I seem to be playing.” The case still loses today because Pango reports no revenue, retention cohort, proprietary dataset, or exclusive integration while named incumbents already possess distribution and adjacent agents. I would flip to Invest if Pango verifies at least $2 million ARR with greater than 120% net revenue retention across the existing merchant cohort; that would show the operating layer is compounding rather than merely integrating.
