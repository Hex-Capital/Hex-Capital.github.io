# Capto -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Sankaralingam built RFP-search automation for a contractor, then visited approximately 50 contracting offices in Virginia. That is how specific knowledge begins: build something, encounter the buyer, discover what you misunderstood. The August 2026 UMD account gives me more useful founder evidence than the LinkedIn and Amazon credentials. Capto’s pivot has a concrete learning path behind it; I see no evidence of repeated abandonment or a founder changing direction merely to obtain approval.

The unresolved question is what that learning leaves inside the business. Capto’s Company Brain grounds matching in customer documents, and source-linked requirements make the output reviewable. Both are useful product mechanisms. Neither establishes an asset that survives a competitor reproducing the software. GovDash already overlaps discovery, capture, and proposals while extending into post-award management. Private pursuit histories could eventually make replacement costly, but the dossier supplies no demonstrated switching cost, exclusive rights, or proprietary model advantage. I assign the missing-evidence prior here rather than treating a possible moat as an existing one.

The strongest argument against withholding a check is that these founders are already converting discovery into customer behavior. Snovation reports finding an RFI its existing pipeline missed. The company claims customers tripled proposal output and won awards, although the measurement is unspecified. Those could be the first traces of a product customers refuse to lose. My early Notion investment illustrates why an observable user network need not precede conviction: a small team and working product can justify the exception. The material difference is that Capto has not yet demonstrated similarly distinctive product capability or unusual capital efficiency. A functioning prototype alone does not resolve the competitive question.

There is room beyond one narrow search tool. Company knowledge and solicitation extraction apply across federal and state/local/education procurement, providing a concrete route into additional contractor workflows. The dossier does not establish the adjacent software spend, so I give that route partial credit. Paid pilots also matter: contractors reportedly pay for the product, although the evidence comes through a founder-profile search snippet and discloses neither price nor continuation. Customers’ $250 million in obligated contract value belongs to their businesses; it establishes neither Capto revenue nor Capto’s share of the value created.

Capto also organizes fragmented opportunity supply, which fits one of my recurring interests. But public notices originate outside the product, and there is no evidence that one customer makes another customer’s experience better. Office visits demonstrate founder effort; they do not establish customer-created distribution. I find no documented integrity, financing-pressure, or morale-dependence red flag. My hesitation is structural: demonstrated founder learning has not yet become demonstrated compounding advantage.

At **49/100**, I am neutral and would keep my money uncommitted today. One verifiable result would change that stance: a paid customer renewal documenting that accumulated pursuit history materially improves qualification accuracy over an otherwise equivalent fresh installation. That would show Capto’s use creating a durable asset. Today, the founders have earned attention; the product has not yet earned that conclusion.
