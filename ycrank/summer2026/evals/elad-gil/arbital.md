# Arbital -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Perpetual-DEX volume grew from $1.5 trillion in 2024 to $6.7 trillion in 2025; active traders now buy cross-venue execution, and the controlling variable is whether Arbital can retain a measurable net take rate on recurring routed volume before exchanges or larger terminals absorb the interface. The dossier does not answer that variable. It reports $377 million in current monthly volume but no revenue, pricing, retention, rebates, or split between the existing market-making engine and the forthcoming mobile multi-asset product. Volume is evidence of demand, not yet evidence of value capture.

The product pull is nevertheless real for a pre-seed company. Arbital reached 2,600 traders and $1.3 billion in cumulative volume within seven months. Even discounted as company-reported data, those figures are substantially more informative than registrations or LOIs. The strongest bull case is that this team already knows the users and distribution: Karon Pangestu helped Aevo scale from roughly $1 billion to $100 billion in cumulative volume, Steven Mulyawan onboarded more than 10,000 traders, and Vincent Owen Toniwan operated sub-2-millisecond infrastructure processing 100,000 events per second. If the reported volume represents repeat, economically rational usage, the company may have found pull before articulating its final product.

The technology shift also matters. A 346% annual increase in perpetual-DEX volume creates enough fragmented liquidity for cross-venue routing and automated market-making to become useful. Arbital owns more workflow than a superficial trading dashboard: it routes maker-only orders across multiple DEXs, maintains inventory limits, and enforces loss thresholds and circuit breakers. Those integrations and execution controls retain operational state. However, venue fee schedules, incentive programs, and liquidity remain outside Arbital’s control, so the same market expansion that creates routing demand can leave most economics with the exchanges.

The most damaging counterargument to an investment is the product transition. The website presents a decentralized market-making engine; the YC page presents a social, mobile-first terminal spanning perps, memes, and stocks. Arbital does not separate which product generated the $1.3 billion of volume. Breadth across a discontinuity can be attractive when each product compounds shared distribution, but here it may indicate that the team has not decided whether it sells execution infrastructure or consumer trading attention. Axiom has already shown that a focused terminal can generate more than $100 million in cumulative revenue, while Hyperliquid generated $844 million in 2025 by owning the underlying venue. Arbital currently occupies the less-defensible layer between them.

I assign no value to the inferred execution-data moat or trader social graph. The dossier supplies no reproduction cost, exclusive data rights, retention loop, or structural barrier, and better-capitalized terminals can build overlapping integrations. The known-exception logic does not rescue this: Arbital is neither frontier research with scarce technical talent that competitors cannot assemble nor a physical-market reconstruction with demonstrated margin transformation. Its founders have unusually relevant operating experience, including Karon’s earlier Launchy work, but the evidence establishes domain fit more clearly than costly commitment, desperation, or second-time-founder learning.

My 36-month base case is that Arbital pivots toward B2B cross-venue execution infrastructure and reaches $1 million to $4 million in annualized revenue, rather than winning the consumer multi-asset terminal category. I would monitor rather than invest today. The verifiable flip condition is three consecutive months showing at least a 10-basis-point net take rate on more than $300 million of monthly volume, with over 60% of that volume coming from retained traders rather than exchange incentives. That would demonstrate roughly $3.6 million in annualized net revenue and resolve the market variable the current dossier leaves open.
