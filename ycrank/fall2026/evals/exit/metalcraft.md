# Metalcraft -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a life-science model developer is the strongest path. A buyer could pay to control a taskset library if it measurably improves model selection in live research workflows and ownership gives the buyer an advantage over licensing or building it internally. The memo documents a six-taskset catalog with samples and an access request flow. Metalcraft reports taskset-level model scores. The value to a buyer and the potential advantage of ownership are inferences. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

A paid production pilot that measures how well taskset scores predict performance in a buyer’s workflow would build on the existing catalog and the founder’s biology and AI experience. Pilot cost, funding, and runway are undisclosed. The memo also leaves data ownership and transfer rights unverified and supplies no independent route to cash for this holder. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Defensibility, Key Facts)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** With no investment terms supplied, assume a $500,000 check at $10 million post-money, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds. The equity hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% ownership and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. Both sale scenarios assume zero net debt and other price adjustments, a simple pro rata waterfall, and all cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. The memo supplies no valuation evidence for either scenario. (Key Facts)

### Countercase

Model developers and scientific software vendors already build overlapping evaluations. If an internal benchmark or ordinary license delivers comparable results in live workflows, a buyer has little reason to pay an ownership premium. The memo establishes overlap but leaves equivalent performance and the resulting price ceiling unresolved. That gap supports Watch. Neither the substitution evidence nor the modeled $0 outcome establishes a Pass override. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Metalcraft complete a paid production pilot that demonstrates enough incremental value from owning its tasksets to support an acquisition that clears the investor’s hurdle? Move to Reach out if the pilot shows a measurable improvement in a live workflow and the buyer’s own build-or-license comparison supports a path to roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, with transferable rights to the tasksets responsible for the gain.
