# Particularity -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Particularity’s strongest asset is the founders’ proximity to the problem. Kumaravel worked on consumer protection at the Senate Banking Committee; Bannon worked on security and compliance infrastructure at AWS, including EU Sovereign Cloud services. Those experiences could teach them where regulatory judgment meets engineering constraints. But the dossier does not identify a regulator-facing system either founder personally shipped, sold, or operated. I give their relevant work credit without inferring specific knowledge that the record has not yet demonstrated.

The decisive question is what Particularity owns after another team reproduces the website-navigation and evidence-capture software. Timestamping and hashing can preserve a record’s integrity. They do not establish that the recorded interaction proves a statutory violation. A proprietary corpus connecting website behavior to resolved investigations could become valuable, but the dossier demonstrates neither that corpus nor rights to reuse it. Existing agency mystery-shopping and recording tools, documented by UNCTAD, make the distinction consequential: automating an investigation is useful; owning something that improves with each investigation is a different business.

The strongest argument against passing is that these founders may understand that distinction better than competitors do. Their combination of consumer-protection policy experience and compliance engineering fits the difficult boundary between finding suspicious behavior and producing usable evidence. Starting with state attorneys general and city consumer-affairs departments gives Particularity concrete buyers. Enterprise compliance also supports real spending: PerformLine reported more than $10 million ARR in 2021. But that establishes adjacent demand, not Particularity’s route into it. Different buyers may require different workflows, incentives, and evidence standards. The initial wedge is legible; the expansion mechanism remains unproved.

Particularity also has not demonstrated that one agency makes the product more useful for another. Investigation histories could remain isolated inside each customer. That would produce software revenue without the customer-created compounding I prefer. Its October 15, 2026 emergence establishes a launch, while pricing, payments, and repeat usage remain undisclosed. I assign those omissions the pre-seed defaults. The dossier contains no evidence of dishonesty, artificial financing pressure, or founder instability; possible detection errors are an engineering question, not an integrity accusation.

I would pass with my own money today. Particularity fits my pattern of turning specialized knowledge into scalable output, but has not demonstrated the proprietary asset or participant contribution that makes that output compound. My exceptions for unmonetized networks or unusually compelling prototypes do not resolve this gap: neither mechanism is established here. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: a documented cross-agency deployment showing that proprietary, legally reusable investigation outcomes from one agency measurably improve validated detection accuracy for another. That would turn accumulated work into an asset competitors cannot obtain simply by copying the software.
