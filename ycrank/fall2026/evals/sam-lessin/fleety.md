# Fleety -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Fleety has a credible reason to exist. I cannot yet tell what my check buys. Proposed post-money, competing term sheets, and whether this is the first genuinely scarce capital are all undocumented. YC participation establishes none of those facts. There is also no stated use of proceeds tied to a measurable experiment and deadline. I apply the missing-evidence defaults to both gates; I am not assuming an auction or inventing a financing plan.

The founders are the strongest part of the underwriting. Ben Zhou founded B&A Roofing and built Timrep to six-figure revenue; Ariyaman Molanorouzi operated property-management automation covering 3,000-plus units and built a six-figure-ARR SEO business. Both worked as dispatchers. Those are operating receipts across trades and software, which is exactly where a useful translation layer can emerge. Their previous businesses establish execution experience, not Fleety revenue. This fits my less-public pattern of backing founders who understand the physical operation well enough to reorganize it.

The strongest argument against passing is that this experience may already be getting them through doors that generic software founders cannot open. Fleety names Albert Nahman, Ace Plumbing, and Apex Plumbing, describes weeks spent inside customer shops, and reports work across California and Florida. Combining call transcripts, equipment records, technician skills, and live schedules could make that access economically valuable. But the missing causal link matters: the dossier does not establish that an owned relationship or proprietary workflow produced a purchase, renewal, or referral. Customer references justify investigation; they do not yet establish a dark pool. That dimension stays at its default.

ServiceTitan makes the distinction consequential. Dispatch Pro already assigns work using predicted job value and technician performance. Fleety advertises compatibility with ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, and Jobber, but compatibility alone gives it no ownership over distribution or underlying records. The AI application red flag therefore has a concrete mechanism here: an incumbent already owns the system of record and offers overlapping functionality. Dispatcher review is a sensible response to assignment errors, although the labor required for that review and onsite configuration remains unquantified. The founders’ operating knowledge could overcome this disadvantage, but the dossier has not demonstrated that outcome.

Delete the next financing from Fleety’s plan. Advance subscription billing could help working capital, but contractual billing terms establish neither collected revenue nor profitable delivery. There is no evidence of negative contribution economics, so I retain the economics default rather than score an imagined failure. Equally, the exceptions that would override my financing gates are not established: measured paid conversion and retention, demonstrable cash economics, or an alternative security with an explicit capital-use plan. The CEO/CTO roles also give me no basis to invent an equal-founder governance problem.

I pass at **33/100**, despite unusually relevant founder experience. My one verifiable condition for reopening underwriting is a written round proposal below $10 million post-money that budgets a single 90-day test of positive contribution cash per paying shop, including onboarding and human-review costs. That would connect price, capital, and a truth worth discovering. Today, Fleety’s founders have earned serious attention; the proposed investment has not yet earned my money.
