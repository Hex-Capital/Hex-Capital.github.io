# Almanac -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

743 GitHub stars, 68 forks, and 843 commits make Almanac a technically obsessive founder bet trapped inside my least attractive sleeve: consensus AI developer software. Almanac’s strongest signal is asymmetric conviction—the founders are already living inside 12- and 16-hour Codex runs—while Almanac’s weakest signal is category fit, because persistent agent context is exactly the feature that Cognition, Codex, or Claude Code can absorb. “Software is no longer investible” is deliberately too strong; software without distribution or structural leverage is no longer investible at a venture price.

**Pinterest** is the high rung on founder conviction: Ben Silbermann’s missionary persistence was strong enough to override a consensus-looking round. Kushagra Chitkara’s “convince you to get a wiki for your codebase,” Almanac’s five displayed engineering-team users, and 843 commits show more than opportunistic prompt-wrapper energy. Almanac nevertheless sits below Pinterest because the dossier shows no three-year obsession, category-defining writing, or preexisting founder thesis; 12-hour Codex sessions demonstrate acute product pain, not yet the “so many turns of the crank” temperament that made the Pinterest exception underwritable.

**Fin** is the low-rung category warning: a compelling Wizard-of-Oz assistant still pivoted into workplace analytics because the assistant layer was difficult to own. Almanac shows the same structural danger in sharper form. Almanac is an Apache-2.0 context layer sitting between repositories and agents, while Cognition already controls DeepWiki and Devin Search and coding-agent vendors control the conversations Almanac ingests. Repository-owned Markdown is a good product decision, but local portability also makes Almanac easier to substitute and leaves the category outside my crypto, DTC, healthcare-rail, creator, and structure-first sleeves.

Almanac’s Apache-2.0 CLI and zero active job postings could compound into a capital-efficient distribution loop over the next 18 months: stars create installs, installs create documentation history, and documentation history creates team-level switching friction. The break in that mechanism is monetization. Almanac publishes no pricing or revenue, and repository-local Markdown limits proprietary lock-in, so every successful open-source adoption may create usage without creating a default-alive business. This looks bootstrappable and potentially venture-acceleratable, but Almanac has not shown why a standard SAFE is the right instrument rather than a small, patient open-source business.

No round size, valuation, named investor, or visible pass is disclosed for Almanac, so I apply the absent-evidence default and then mark down the consensus context. Almanac flips from a low value-seed score only if the actual entry price is below the Summer 2026 batch median or if credible investors passed because a repository-owned wiki looked too narrow and illegible. At a hot YC AI price, 743 stars do not create underwriting edge; at a genuinely discounted price, those same 743 stars could make Almanac an interesting regatta tack.

**John Fish / Bookshelved** had an asset Almanac does not yet possess: distribution attached to a named person before the financing arrived. Almanac has the beginnings of owned community—743 GitHub stars, 68 forks, and founder LinkedIn audiences of roughly 1,000, 286, and 1,165—but Almanac’s users are attached to an open-source repository rather than to Kushagra, Rohan, or Divit as durable founder-brands. That is better than paid-acquisition dependence and materially above the absent-evidence default, but it is not creator-style distribution sitting on the cap table.

Cognition’s reported $492M run-rate revenue supplies my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Cognition acquires Almanac for less than $30M after Almanac remains below $3M ARR, folding its repository-local Markdown and agent-conversation ingestion into Devin rather than leaving Almanac as an independent system of record. The mechanism is founder strength without category ownership—Almanac can build a useful product faster than Almanac can prevent the agent platforms from bundling it.

Five displayed engineering-team users, 743 stars, and local processing form the strongest bull case: Almanac could become the platform-neutral memory standard precisely because engineering teams do not want Cognition, OpenAI, or Anthropic owning their institutional history. **Pinterest** is the relevant known exception—missionary founders can overwhelm my category and pricing objections—and Almanac’s Git-reviewable Markdown could turn portability into trust rather than weakness. The evidence that still wins is no published revenue, no proprietary retrieval advantage, an Apache-2.0 core, and multiple capitalized substitutes; if Almanac reaches $1M ARR without paid acquisition by July 2027, I flip from pass to invest.
