# Decawork -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot underwrite Decawork’s round before knowing the post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or what the check uniquely finances. YC participation does not make the round auctioned, but it does not establish scarce capital either. More importantly, “build and sell an agent control plane” is a category plan, not a finite experiment with a dated pass/fail result. At an unknown price, I am being asked to buy the agent-security story without knowing what truth my money reveals.

The founders are the real evidence here. Sarthak Aggarwal worked on NVIDIA AI systems reportedly deployed at OpenAI and Meta and on enterprise AI used by Microsoft and Hitachi. Aman Raj built Barclays compliance tooling used by more than 240 teams and previously shipped products reaching 52,000 families and 100,000 monthly users. Together they plausibly bridge agent infrastructure and enterprise control. The prior Decawork product also ran across multiple Indian and U.S. enterprises. Those are operating receipts, not résumé decoration.

But the company has changed the object being validated. Prior deployments concerned autonomous IT administration for employees; the current product assigns identities to agents, mediates tool calls, and governs their lifecycle. No customer, paid deployment, repeat usage, or current-product revenue connects that new workflow to a purchase. SOC 2 status as of July 20, 2026 proves execution discipline, not demand. Eleven thousand combined LinkedIn followers prove neither proprietary distribution nor conversion.

The bear case is brutally straightforward: Okta already offers agent registration, ownership, credentials, and access governance, while Astrix, Noma, WitnessAI, and Zenity have each raised more than $55 million around overlapping territory. Decawork’s gateway may become valuable infrastructure, but it may equally become a feature sitting between better-distributed identity and security platforms. There is no disclosed proprietary dataset, measured detection advantage, or embedded customer workflow that prevents this. This activates the generic-AI red flag: the public category is obvious, the functionality is converging, and the dark pool is still hypothetical.

The strongest bull case is that the team has already crossed enterprise technical and compliance boundaries, obtained SOC 2, and learned from deployments on both sides of the problem. That could let Decawork own operational deployment and maintenance while incumbents merely govern identity. Yet the dossier supplies no gross margin, burn, runway, pricing, or route to break-even, so deleting the next financing leaves no economic model. Current evidence does not qualify for an exception based on hard conversion or proprietary access.

I pass today. One verifiable condition flips me: within 12 months, three named enterprises must replace an existing internal or incumbent workflow with Decawork’s current control plane, convert to paid annual contracts, and renew or expand usage. That would show the founders’ access causes purchases and that lifecycle operations—not generic agent identity—constitute the owned workflow.
