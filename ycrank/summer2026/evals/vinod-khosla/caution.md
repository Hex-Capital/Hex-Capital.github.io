# Caution -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is underwritten, not disproven. Caution cites a confidential-computing market growing from $5.5 billion in 2023 to $153.8 billion by 2030, but market size is not societal consequence. The dossier never quantifies breach losses, audit labor, deployment delays, or the number of workloads blocked by unverifiable binaries. Source-to-runtime trust could become important infrastructure; today, there is no evidence that it changes a cost pool by 10x, much less 100x.

The technical mechanism is the strongest part. AWS Nitro proves hardware measurements, while Caution lets a third party rebuild from reviewed source, compare hashes, and verify the running enclave’s source, configuration, kernel, and toolchain. That is a real missing link, not another security dashboard. Production use across crypto infrastructure, digital-asset lending, consumer hardware, post-quantum security, and private AI inference suggests the workflow survives contact with heterogeneous workloads. But the dossier supplies no measured improvement in audit cost, deployment time, or security outcomes. Worse, fidelity is not safety: perfectly reproducing vulnerable source still produces vulnerable software.

The largest kill risk is whether independent verification becomes a repeated customer behavior rather than an assurance feature applauded during procurement. Caution identifies AWS dependence, attestation-chain complexity, and open-source substitution, yet gives no cheapest decisive experiment, numerical threshold, budget, or stop condition. Intel TDX, AMD SEV-SNP, and TPM support are “under development,” which is a roadmap, not risk removal. Closed beta and production deployments are encouraging, but unnamed usage with undisclosed revenue cannot distinguish a platform from a consulting-assisted deployment.

The team is unusually credible for this problem. Anton Livaja worked in security at Ledn and OpenZeppelin; Lance Vick worked across Fitbit, BitGo, Turnkey, enclaves, HSMs, and supply-chain security; Ksenia Lesko adds operations after working with them at Distrust. Their relationship predates Caution, reducing basic team risk. What is absent is evidence of fast belief revision or magnet recruiting around the remaining risks. Five people drawn largely from the same prior organization may execute coherently, but the dossier shows neither a material thesis change after customer evidence nor a specialist recruited to own multi-hardware verification or enterprise adoption.

The bull case is that an execution-heavy infrastructure wedge can expand into a platform: five production workload categories, a reproducible open-source stack, and compatibility with installed AWS infrastructure are better evidence than follower counts. The known exception for an initially non-discontinuous product entering an enormous value pool—**Block**—is relevant because both could expand from a narrow technical wedge. The material difference is that Caution has not yet shown a large monetized pain pool or an evolving proprietary platform. OPAQUE, Edgeless Systems, and enclaive already overlap with its orchestration and attestation functions; cloud vendors own the trust roots; and AGPL self-hosting exposes the entire current product. Inferred switching costs from future integrations do not constitute a moat.

My 36-month base case is that Caution becomes a managed AWS confidential-computing compliance vendor with $1 million–$3 million ARR, not a cross-cloud verification standard. I pass today. There is no objectionable exit language, permanent subsidy, or generic AI wrapper here; the problem is insufficient leverage, not founder quality. One result would flip the decision: an independently documented paid deployment showing at least a 10x reduction in audit or verification cost versus a Nitro-only security pipeline, followed by renewal.
