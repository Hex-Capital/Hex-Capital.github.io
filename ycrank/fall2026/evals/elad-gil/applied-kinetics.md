# Applied Kinetics -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Applied Kinetics is selling time into an equipment shortage. Data center and energy developers will buy from a new supplier if it can deliver acceptable equipment materially sooner than established channels. The controlling variable is **time to customer acceptance**, including approvals, testing, freight, and installation readiness. The founders’ claim of seven-week transformer production versus up to 65 weeks on the open market identifies a potentially valuable wedge. It does not yet establish the complete delivery advantage: the quoted ranges remain nonbinding until specifications and scope are confirmed.

The market can carry a substantial business here. Developers facing equipment delays have a concrete reason to consider alternative supply, and NVIDIA’s May 2025 announcement of an 800 VDC architecture introduces additional specification work. But Applied Kinetics has not established that its technology changes the economics of supplying that equipment. Faster access to partner factories could produce valuable orders while leaving the company with distributor margins, working-capital exposure, and warranty obligations. The adjacent $25.8 billion data center power market establishes context; it does not establish how much economic value this intermediary retains. I give credit for identifying the bottleneck, but the dossier provides no measured technology-driven cost or margin improvement.

The strongest bull case is that Applied Kinetics can become the operating layer across fragmented manufacturing capacity. Its reported 40+ partner plants provide a starting supply network. The described agents review specifications, allocate production, track milestones, and flag delays, while the company manages approvals through service. That is meaningful workflow scope. Better models could improve specification interpretation and exception handling while the company retains responsibility for delivery. However, there are no documented customer integrations, accumulated workflow state, or measured automation results. Separately licensing the agents offers another business model; it does not yet demonstrate reusable software distribution.

The distinction between available capacity and customer pull matters. The reported 10+ GW belongs to the manufacturing base’s experience, not Applied Kinetics’ deliveries. The dossier identifies no completed shipment, repeat buyer, or paid deployment. I use the stated pre-seed default for demand rather than treating that absence as failed adoption. Likewise, Rohit Naras’s GPU telemetry work at Crusoe and Adhik Durga’s AI-agent experience at IBM are relevant technical context. Neither establishes prior startup learning or costly personal commitment, so those credentials do not substitute for the founder evidence this criterion requires.

This could still be an unusually effective way to enter physical infrastructure without financing a factory. That fits my pattern of backing software that changes physical-market economics. A successful transformer order could also create a buyer relationship for switchgear, generation, and cooling. The objection to passing therefore has substance: an operationally strong intermediary might capture the customer before proprietary equipment startups reach scale. But the physical-business exception requires demonstrated economic reconstruction, and the frontier-research exception requires a different kind of technical evidence than this dossier supplies. The relevant red flag—software attached to unchanged equipment economics—remains unresolved rather than proven.

I would pass on a personal check today. The market bottleneck is credible enough to clear my first gate; the combined market-and-economic-change gate does not clear. **My flip condition is a verified cohort of three paid customer orders reaching final acceptance within ten weeks at gross margins of at least 25%, after freight, testing, and warranty reserves.** That would turn the central proposition from a production quote into evidence of a repeatable business. Until then, I can underwrite an interesting supply operation more readily than a technology company with expanding economics.
