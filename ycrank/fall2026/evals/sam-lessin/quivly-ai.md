# Quivly AI -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Quivly AI has customers and acknowledged revenue, but I cannot yet price the investment. Proposed post-money valuation: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. Whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital: unknown. Karman Ventures and VitalStage Ventures are disclosed investors; their presence establishes neither an auction nor a bargain. The second question is equally unresolved: what specific truth would this round reveal? The dossier supplies no capital-use experiment, deadline, or pass/fail metric. Both dimensions receive my missing-evidence defaults. YC membership does not answer either question.

The strongest reason to keep looking is that the founders have actually operated across the systems Quivly connects. Maheshwari built internal customer-intelligence tools; Agrawal led pricing and billing infrastructure at JPMorgan Payments and previously worked as a founding engineer/product manager at Atlan. That is concrete experience joining customer context to revenue infrastructure, beyond a collection of impressive employers. It fits my preference for founders who can translate between operating environments. Their current product turns conversations into account projects and executes actions across connected systems. The bridge is credible; exclusive ownership of what crosses it remains unproven.

Actual deployment makes this more interesting than another agent demo. Quivly reports managing more than **10,000 client accounts**, names Siftly, Octolane, and Hyperspell, and publishes Mycroft’s claim that customer-success managers handle up to **60% more ARR** per book. Those are accounts belonging to clients, not 10,000 paying Quivly customers. Still, the combination of operational use and acknowledged revenue supports intermediate credit for embedded workflow. The strongest bull case is that Quivly becomes where post-sales work happens, making accumulated account context and configured actions expensive to replace. Founder follower counts and eight repository stars add nothing to that argument.

The problem is who captures the economics once this workflow becomes obvious. Gainsight already offers agents, connected customer context, and managed renewals; its October 2026 announcement also describes account/ARR-based pricing. Quivly’s coverage pricing therefore supplies little protection by itself. The customer owns the underlying records, and the dossier establishes no exclusive data rights. Approval thresholds and action logs address a real execution risk, but they do not show what human supervision costs. Revenue amount, gross margin, burn, and runway remain undisclosed. The **4.3× customer revenue-expansion claim is a customer outcome**, not evidence of Quivly’s revenue or profitability.

That leaves a real business possibility and an unproven investment proposition. My exceptions for AI businesses require unusually strong conversion, retention, or cash evidence; named users and promising outcomes without cohort methodology do not establish that override. There is also no documented prior exit and financing structure supporting a person-level exception. Conversely, I see no evidence of an auction, forced serial financing, or an equal-founder decision deadlock, so I will not invent those red flags. **I pass at present.** One verifiable flip condition would be a signed seed proposal below **$10 million post-money** funding a defined six-month experiment: renew five named customer deployments at positive contribution margin, with the seed budget sufficient to reach the result without another financing. That would give me a price and a card worth paying to flip.
