# Verno -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by an industrial CAD or engineering software vendor is Verno’s strongest path. Verno documents native DWG/DXF write-back, downstream updates across customer files, and revision histories tied to engineer approval. These capabilities could let a vendor add cross-document change control to an existing CAD product. Ownership could be worth paying for if it gives the vendor control over the connectors and approval workflow and gets a proven capability into customers’ hands materially faster than building it. That advantage is an inference. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Verno reports an active pilot and says it found three conflicting design pressures for one tank in documents already issued for construction. The memo also records reports of live customer sites. A paid production rollout across multiple EPCs that measures rework avoided and verifies edit accuracy would test whether the benefit is repeatable. The recorded $125,000 seed entry leaves runway and rollout costs unknown. IP ownership and the transferability of customer contracts and data rights are also unverified. The memo supplies no separately supported route to cash for this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The memo supplies no terms for the evaluated check or usable acquisition-price evidence. The scenarios use the thesis’s illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The 5× objective is $2.5 million in cash within eight years. Under the assumed pro rata waterfall, it requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

Both sale scenarios assume zero net debt, fees, working-capital adjustments, and senior preference claims. Enterprise value therefore equals distributable equity cash, allocated pro rata at closing before investor taxes. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. The prices and timing are scenario inputs.

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value → distributable equity cash | Cash to holder at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $40M → $40M | $1.2M | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100M → $100M | $3.0M | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No sale or distribution | $0 | 0× realized |

At 2% ownership, even the modeled strong sale returns $2 million, or 4×. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The investor retains the shares.

### Countercase

Autodesk Plant 3D already validates P&IDs against project models. A CAD vendor could extend its own validation into downstream documents or license Verno’s capability. Either option could cap what the vendor would pay for ownership. Verno’s reported discrepancy shows a concrete use case, while the memo leaves savings, repeatability, and retention unmeasured. There is no evidence that a substitute already delivers equivalent results or that every credible sale price falls below the hurdle. The structural Pass override does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would an industrial CAD vendor’s build-versus-buy assessment, using measured rework savings and verified edit accuracy from paid deployments at multiple EPCs, support an acquisition price that yields at least $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at the illustrative 3% stake? Buyer-side evidence supporting that threshold would move the action to **Reach out**. Evidence that building or licensing delivers equivalent results and caps the price materially below it would move the action to **Pass**.
