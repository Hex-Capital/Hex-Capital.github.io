# Rise Reforming -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Rise Reforming’s 1,800 hours of stable syngas production reveal an unusual kind of pre-seed startup: strongest where the work becomes an industrial schlep, weakest where the idea should emerge from a founder’s own pain. Rise Reforming’s founders know molecular engineering, but the dossier never says George Rose, Lucas Zubillaga Maharg, or Jona van Oord personally needed waste gas converted into methanol. Rise Reforming looks less like Dropbox’s forgotten USB stick than three capable engineers deliberately choosing a hard problem. The schlep is real. The organic origin is not.

Coinbase turned compliance grind into an asset because Brian Armstrong kept running toward work everyone else avoided. Rise Reforming has the physical version of that mechanism: raw wastewater biogas, site-specific permitting, chemical offtake, feedstock contracts, maintenance, and a build-own-operate model that puts plant CAPEX on Rise Reforming’s balance sheet. The binding biogas-supply agreement, conditional DME offtake, and delivered Chicagoland pilot container show that Rise Reforming has already entered the unpleasant part. But Coinbase had a functioning wallet in users’ hands; Rise Reforming will not commission its first live-biogas pilot until 2027. The analogy is strong on appetite for schlep and incomplete on proof.

Loopt’s weak product timing survived only because Sam Altman was enough of an animal to override the missing evidence. Rise Reforming has a related weakness on organicity: Rose’s work at Nexamp, Highland Electric Fleets, Mantel Capture, and GenH placed him near climate infrastructure, but nothing says Rise Reforming began as a tool any founder personally needed. The 2025 pivot from plastic waste to biogas makes the origin look more like technically informed problem selection than an itch being scratched. Loopt is a dangerous precedent because known exceptions are not templates. Rise Reforming receives credit for deep adjacency, not for an origin story the dossier does not establish.

George Rose and the other founders converted a 2025 pivot into 1,800 production hours, a $650K pre-seed round, a binding supply agreement, multiple supplier MOUs, a conditional offtake, and a delivered container. Resourcefulness compounds here because every hour operating the 2027 pilot can improve catalyst behavior, permitting packages, feedstock handling, and the next site sale simultaneously. If Rise Reforming gets the first installation working, Rise Reforming becomes easier to finance and harder to copy with each deployment. The missing animal track is revenue: raising more than $1.7M and seeking another $6M are not substitutes for persuading someone to buy chemicals from a machine that works.

Lucas Zubillaga Maharg built and operated Rise Reforming’s bench-scale reformer, while Jona van Oord developed automated electrochemical-analysis equipment. That is hacker behavior expressed in stainless steel instead of GitHub. Rise Reforming would move into the top hacker-genealogy tier if the founders had built a surprising technical artifact before forming Rise Reforming, published process work other engineers adopted, or demonstrated live-biogas operation through an improvised prototype. No public repositories, Hacker News history, or comparable pre-startup artifact were found. The founders can build, but the evidence does not yet show the tribal marker that made the Collisons legible before Stripe.

Airbnb’s three founders supplied complementary skills and had enough shared history to survive an idea that initially looked absurd. Rise Reforming also has three cofounders drawn from UChicago molecular engineering, with Rose and Zubillaga discussing the concept during finals week in 2023 before van Oord joined. That is a coherent trio with technical and operational coverage, and Rise Reforming has survived a substantive pivot together. The dossier does not establish two years of building together before the startup, so the configuration is promising rather than proven. Still, there is no solo-founder or recently matched-cofounder red flag.

Rise Reforming’s delivered pilot container makes my 36-month prediction concrete: by July 2029, Rise Reforming will have commissioned between one and three live-biogas installations and reached $1M–$3M in annualized chemical sales, but will still be financing deployments individually rather than operating a broadly repeatable fleet. The schlep strength produces the installations; the weak organic origin slows the learning loop because Rise Reforming must discover customer pain through wastewater operators and chemical buyers rather than already knowing it from personal use.

M2X’s $94.28M of financing and commercially validated modular methane-to-methanol systems make the bear case sting. Rise Reforming has no chemical revenue, biomethanol cost roughly three times marine gas oil in 2025, its first live-feedstock commissioning is delayed until 2027, and the proposed $6M round plus a $125K engineering hire could become manager-mode spending before product proof. Rise Reforming nevertheless has more than a science project: 1,800 operating hours, a container already at a wastewater facility, contracted feedstock, and conditional demand. Those facts beat the generic hard-tech failure story, narrowly. I would reverse the verdict if Rise Reforming cannot document 1,000 continuous hours on live biogas and an unconditional chemical offtake agreement by July 2028.
