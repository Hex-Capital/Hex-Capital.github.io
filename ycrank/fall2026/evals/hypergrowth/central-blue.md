# Central Blue -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is **channel-led or repeatable sales**, starting with direct sales to spacecraft builders and operators that need power during recurring eclipses. The mission intake form asks for power, location, and timing. That gives Central Blue a way to qualify missions. The underlying job plausibly carries across markets, while compatibility with the dedicated receiver and the ability to serve each mission’s orbit and timing remain open. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Central Blue reports a nearly complete prototype and a planned ground propulsion demonstration with Wave Motion. The named collaboration is an early signal of interest. Wave Motion’s budget and commitment to adopt the receiver remain unknown. The memo provides too little evidence of customer activation, buyer commitment, repeat paid use, or marginal rollout costs to move those scores off their defaults. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The growth scenario assumes multiple operators can integrate a common receiver and buy recurring power from shared HELION capacity. Under those assumptions, Central Blue could reuse its integration work and sell more power before adding another satellite. The first likely bottleneck is turning a qualified mission inquiry into a funded deployment with an integration process a second operator could reuse. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Dedicated receivers could narrow the eligible fleet and require substantial engineering for every sale. Star Catcher’s use of standard solar panels gives operators a plausible lower-integration alternative. If each new customer also requires dedicated orbital capacity, sales and capital costs could rise in proportion to revenue. The planned ground demo leaves both risks unresolved. The structural downside gate requires affirmative evidence that these constraints block every credible path. At the prototype stage, receiver reuse and capacity sharing remain testable, supporting Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent spacecraft operator fund an orbital rollout that delivers its agreed power during eclipse using a receiver integration package another operator could reuse? A completed rollout with buyer-confirmed power delivery and documented integration effort within an agreed budget would move the action to **Reach out**. A ground demonstration alone would keep it at **Watch**. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
