# Nex -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Najmuzzaman Mohammad’s 48,000-user ChatGPT plugin makes **Nex** a high-shipping, low-revenue-visibility company: Nex has unusually credible builders for pre-seed, but no disclosed customer, ARR, retention, or production-workflow curve. That pairing is the analytical signature. Nex can clearly produce artifacts—WUPHF has 1,229 GitHub stars and 98 forks—but in AI, developer attention without buying behavior remains a false signal until proven otherwise.

**Perplexity** is the high rung on founder iteration: Aravind repeatedly built the discussed product within a week. Nex shares the mechanism because Mohammad shipped the 48,000-user plugin and a profitable e-commerce business, while Francisco Dias was HubSpot CRM’s fourth engineer and later worked on its AI infrastructure. Nex diverges because the dossier shows prior and current artifacts, not repeated customer-feedback cycles; Nex’s shifting descriptions—GTM automation, context engineering, and WUPHF-led direction—could represent fast learning, but they do not yet prove Perplexity-like convergence. I score the founders well below the ceiling but still as Nex’s strongest dimension.

**Lyft** exposed how apparently impressive activity can be a false signal when the underlying economics leave the asset structurally behind the leader. Nex has an even earlier form of that revenue-velocity problem: 1,229 GitHub stars, Product Hunt rank #7, and 1,030 LinkedIn followers, but no disclosed revenue, active seats, workflow runs, retention, or named customers. Nex charges $100 per seat per month, yet a price page is not a curve, and Clay is already at $100 million ARR. For an AI product, silence on usage growth triggers my absent-evidence default; Nex has not shown that it is “just working.”

**Gumloop’s** claim that model capability improved rapidly over the preceding six months provides Nex with a real recent catalyst, but the catalyst compounds ambiguously over the next 18 months. Better models should expand the number of workflows Nex can automate, while Relay.app’s shutdown removes one competitor; the same gains simultaneously improve Clay, Unify, Gumloop, CRM-native agents, and every new entrant. Nex therefore benefits from a technological step-function without owning it. This is skating for the puck in a re-opened workflow market, but Nex is arriving after Clay reached $100 million ARR, so the window looks open at the edges rather than newly opened at the center.

**Clay’s** $100 million ARR defines the counterfactual for Nex’s index position: Nex would move into the invest range if its knowledge graph became the neutral context-and-permission layer that Clay, Outreach, Gong, agencies, and CRM agents all had to touch. Nex instead currently sells a $100-per-seat GTM application competing against those platforms, while agency partners may retain 70% of resale revenue. Nex could become an index on agentic GTM execution if its 1,000 integrations and RBAC form shared infrastructure; today Nex must win workflows from well-funded incumbents, which is share capture rather than automatic category compounding.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel because Color placed workflow infrastructure on top of commodity sequencing rather than underwriting new chemistry. Nex similarly places a synchronized knowledge graph, tenant isolation, SOC 2 Type 1 controls, and executable workflows on top of increasingly capable commodity models. Nex’s single miracle is that LLM-generated automations can execute reliably across changing APIs and sensitive systems; model capability itself has largely happened. The concern is that Nex promises deterministic execution across 1,000 integrations without publishing workflow-success or error-rate data, but reliability, permissions, and GTM are coupled product work—not three independent scientific miracles.

**HubSpot** will acquire Nex by July 2029 for under $250 million after Nex reaches roughly $5 million to $15 million ARR, rather than Nex becoming the independent system of record for GTM automation. Nex’s strongest asset is a HubSpot-native founding pair that can ship a credible context and execution layer; Nex’s weakest asset is evidence that customers are pulling the product into a standalone category. The likely mechanism is useful product depth plus weak index economics: Nex builds valuable technology, Clay and horizontal automation platforms constrain independent distribution, and HubSpot buys Nex to fold the knowledge graph and agent runtime back into CRM.

**Mistral** is the strongest objection to that forecast and to my Neutral verdict: Mistral entered a crowded, capital-intensive market after the foundation-model miracle had occurred, and prior Llama shipping made the known exception rational. Nex can make the same bull argument—Dias built HubSpot CRM and AI infrastructure, Mohammad reached 48,000 plugin users, WUPHF has 1,229 stars, Relay.app is shutting down, and GTM agents remain open territory in *AI Market Clarity*. Nex still loses the comparison because Mistral paired prior shipping with a specific post-ChatGPT category rupture, while Nex shows three concurrent product descriptions and no customer curve against Clay’s $100 million ARR. Three consecutive months above 20% net ARR growth, ending above $50,000 MRR with named production customers and documented workflow reliability, would flip my verdict to Invest.
