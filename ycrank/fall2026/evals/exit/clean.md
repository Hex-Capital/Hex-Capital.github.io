# Clean -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a **strategic acquisition by a sales-intelligence platform**. Clean’s product launched in November 2026 and is designed to identify buying windows from dated records such as permits and shipments. Vivameda reported $60,000 in signed contracts over two weeks and a rise in reply rates from about 4% to 19%. That testimonial suggests the timing signals may have value, but it does not isolate Clean’s contribution. Clean reports that it combines its own datasets with licensed records. A platform could have a reason to buy if owning the underlying data and timing models consistently surfaced opportunities before supplier selection that it could not match by building or licensing a feed. The memo does not establish that advantage. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*)

The operating milestone is a repeatable lift in pre-selection outreach and closed deals across paying customers compared with their existing tools. The recent launch and reported first paying customer make that test plausible. The CEO described a round of about $1.2 million, but the memo gives neither final proceeds nor burn or runway, so funding for the test is unverified. The reported ARR figures provide an early revenue signal but do not establish retention or cash generation. The memo supplies no documented change-of-control rights for the licensed data and no independently supported fallback that would pay this holder. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

The memo supplies no terms for the proposed check, so the bridge uses the illustrative $500,000 investment at $10 million post-money, diluted from 5% to 3% with no follow-on checks. The eight-year target is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The sale cases below are sensitivity tests. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction prices to substantiate either case. Both assume no net debt or other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, simple pro rata allocation with no preference deductions, and full cash payment at close. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0.

Even the modeled $100 million sale returns only $2 million, or 4.0×, at 2% ownership.

### Countercase

Salesmotion already bundles account monitoring into CRM, Apollo offers account research and outreach, and Clean offers API access. A platform could license Clean’s signals or extend its own tooling if either approach delivers similar results. The memo also describes an aircraft example where outreach began after supplier selection, illustrating how the timing advantage can fail. Licensed-data dependence could further narrow what an acquirer can use after closing. These are credible reasons to wait. The memo establishes neither that a substitute performs equally well nor that transfer is blocked, so the structural Pass override does not apply. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a sales-intelligence platform show in a head-to-head pilot that owning Clean’s transferable data and timing models creates enough incremental value to support a sale that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A repeatable lift in closed deals from outreach before supplier selection, measured against the platform’s existing and licensed options and backed by buyer-side economics, would move the action to **Reach out**. Comparable performance from those options would move it to **Pass** on the evidenced routes.
