# Stacktrace -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

What does Stacktrace know that Noma, Zenity, and Oligo believe is false? The dossier establishes an engineering difference: local transcript processing with component attribution, rather than process instrumentation or broader identity context. It does not establish a disputed truth. A possible secret is that component attribution makes otherwise unmanageable agent behavior governable through deterministic rules. But that is my hypothesis, not a demonstrated company insight. A controlled comparison showing no improvement over existing controls would undermine it. The October 27 release of CLI version 0.17.0 establishes that the team ships software; it does not settle this question.

The decisive problem is ownership of the opportunity. Stacktrace targets engineers and security teams supervising unattended agents, but the dossier identifies no bounded segment in which its advantage compounds. Noma already advertises steering, masking, policy enforcement, and kill switches. Stacktrace’s Apache-2.0 collection and composition-analysis packages make part of its foundation available to competitors, although the complete product remains proprietary. Open source can accelerate adoption. Here, there is no demonstrated mechanism converting that adoption into exclusive data, network effects, or scale economics. This is affirmative competitive pressure, beyond merely an undisclosed moat.

The founders give me a reason to take the product seriously. Kone and Benedict previously worked at Twitter; Kone and Ganta share an IIT Guwahati connection. Those relationships provide some evidence against a team assembled solely to enter YC, although shared schooling does not establish prior execution together. Their infrastructure and security careers make the product choice intelligible. Founder employment status, ownership, and economic exposure remain unverified; I would neither invent alignment nor infer a founding defect from that silence. The team is the strongest part of this dossier, but capable operators can still build a business whose customers capture most of the value.

The strongest objection to passing is that Stacktrace could become the independent control point across agent vendors. Its local processing, component attribution, browser sandbox, and installable CLI offer a concrete route into developer workflows. Disclosed design partners operating agents locally and in the cloud suggest contact with the actual problem. This also fits my interest in adversarial information systems: attributing an agent’s dangerous action to the component responsible could become valuable infrastructure. Yet the endpoint sidecar reads logs and cannot itself stop calls; blocking requires the gateway, and dynamic steering is forthcoming. The move from useful observer to authoritative control point remains consequential and unfinished.

Distribution is tangible but its economics are unresolved. Self-service installation, founder demonstrations, and design-partner recruitment are more specific than a declaration of “enterprise sales.” They do not yet establish a repeatable conversion process. The free tier accommodates 100 agents and 200 session-hours per month; Enterprise and Gateway have annual custom pricing. Without contract values or margin information, I cannot derive a power-law revenue engine from those terms. The broader market’s projected $17.8 billion size in 2033 supplies no answer about how much value Stacktrace can retain.

I would pass with my own money. The competitive gate fails on present evidence, while the secret remains unproved. The exceptions that justify accepting uncertain monopoly economics require a demonstrated technical discontinuity or compelling transaction behavior; neither is documented here. My verifiable flip condition is an independent, production-based comparison demonstrating an order-of-magnitude advantage in correctly attributing actionable agent incidents against Noma and Oligo at matched false-positive rates. That would turn the product’s architectural difference into evidence of proprietary value. Today, Stacktrace has a credible team and a useful implementation, with insufficient evidence that either leads to a durable monopoly.
