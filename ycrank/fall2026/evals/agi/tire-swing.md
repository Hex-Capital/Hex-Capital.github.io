# Tire Swing -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Tire Swing automates parts of affordable-housing certification. Operators currently coordinate income and asset verification through calls, emails, spreadsheets, and office visits. Tire Swing describes software that extracts tenant data, applies property and program rules, and assembles packets with links to the supporting documents. Staff still review and approve each certification. The memo therefore supports a labor-saving mechanism for an existing workflow. It identifies no buyer or certification use that becomes economically viable through that mechanism, so Industry enabled by superintelligence scores zero. Building blocks of superintelligence also scores zero because the product serves housing operators and supplies no scarce input to an AI workload. Both zero scores trigger Pass. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The strongest commercial case is a subscription that fits operators’ existing forms and review workflows. The company reports thousands of compliance checks, and customer-specific rules and review histories could create switching costs. That advantage remains untested given overlapping features from Roost and bundled certification from Yardi. Manual workarounds and founder-reported live use at dozens of properties across eight states support partial demand credit. The memo gives no independent buyer commitment, paid conversion, or renewal evidence. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Witten’s digital forensics work and Irvine’s rules-engine experience are relevant to document checks and eligibility logic. The pivot to a launched housing product supports execution credit, with certification accuracy and exception handling still unverified in operation. The memo gives subscription terms and identifies required staff review, but offers no realized pricing or delivery-cost figures for the next property or approved certification. Scale economics stays at its default. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

If manual verification makes timely certification unaffordable for an understaffed operator, AI-assisted packet preparation could let that operator process cases it routinely defers. The memo describes delays that can leave units vacant, and the property-specific rules, source-linked records, and approval workflow could still matter when comparable models are widely available. This scenario depends on savings that survive staff review and rework. The reported deployments do not establish that an operator crossed that threshold, so the Pass gate still applies. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid pilot show that an operator now completes accurate, timely certifications for cases it previously deferred because of staffing cost? A buyer-verified comparison against its manual baseline should include the subscription, staff review, and rework in the cost per accurate, approved certification. If the operator meets its affordability threshold and commits to continued paid use of the rules and approval workflow, the action moves to Reach out.
