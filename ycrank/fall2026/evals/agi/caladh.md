# Caladh -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Scarce complements is Caladh’s strongest path. If AI makes maritime drone incursions more frequent or harder to stop, operators could pay for a qualified system that closes a gap left by detection and physical barriers. Caladh says Eclipse is designed to disrupt hostile maritime drones and Cloak is designed to conceal subsea assets from drone-mounted sensors. The memo contains no Caladh field results or evidence that AI is already increasing the protection gap. Site qualification and integration could help Caladh retain buyers, but exclusive IP and control of a hard-to-replace deployment capability remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The Coast Guard’s request for information shows independent interest in counter-underwater-drone technology. Caladh-specific buyer commitments and willingness to pay remain unverified. Kita’s documented undersea research and engineering experience, together with her reported DARPA Sync leadership, support seed-stage execution. A site installation is a plausible expansion unit under a hardware-contract scenario. Pricing, installation and support costs, and repeat orders remain unverified. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Established suppliers could win the same security budgets by extending systems buyers already use. Ultra Maritime has demonstrated underwater detection and classification, and D-Fend offers maritime mitigation for aerial drones. Those offerings make substitution credible, while comparable performance against Caladh’s proposed subsea protection remains untested. The substitution gate stays open pending a field comparison. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)
