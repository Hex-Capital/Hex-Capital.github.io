# Mass Magnetics -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The contested truth is precise: heterogeneous magnet scrap can be sorted and converted into fully dense, qualification-grade magnets in 60 seconds, making domestic recycling economically superior to conventional production. What would prove it wrong? Repeated lots failing independent composition, magnetic-performance, or customer-qualification tests. Mass Magnetics has stated a falsifiable secret, but not yet demonstrated it: its compositions are “illustrative only,” certificates are promised rather than published, and no independent results establish that rapid sintering preserves performance across variable feedstock. This is a technical claim, not merely a fashionable slogan—but today it remains a claim.

The more immediate problem is monopoly versus commodity. Defense, aerospace, robotics, motors, and drones do not constitute a narrow beachhead; they are several markets with different grades, geometries, qualification regimes, and buyers. Noveon already recycles magnets, Vulcan already manufactures domestic NdFeB magnets, and Niron proposes to eliminate rare-earth feedstock altogether. Machine-vision sorting and rapid sintering could become proprietary technology, but Mass Magnetics discloses no patents, exclusive feedstock, dominant application, or measured 10x result. A faster process inside a competitive supply chain does not automatically create a monopoly.

The founders are the strongest part of the company. Eric Silver has built lithium-sulfur cathode pilot lines; Matthew Groll brings MIT manufacturing training and NASA and Draper experience; and they have known each other since grade school. This is a coherent hard-technology team assembled through durable personal alignment, not a convenience match formed for YC. Yet friendship is not the same as having shipped together, and the dossier does not establish founder ownership, economic exposure, or whether every essential process is developed in-house.

The bull case is stronger than the current traction suggests. China’s April 2025 export controls and the January 1, 2027 DFARS restriction create a dated supply shock, while 60-second sintering could reduce the capital intensity that normally kills domestic materials businesses. This also fits the recent pattern of rebuilding neglected physical infrastructure for defense and robotics. But the known exception for coordination-heavy hard technology requires a validated pilot and a concrete deployment path. Mass Magnetics currently has open preorders, not named production orders; Greentown membership and 334 LinkedIn followers do not remove qualification risk. The DFARS nonavailability exception further means regulation cannot substitute for performance, volume, and price.

The revenue engine is similarly possible but unproved. Custom per-lot quotations are appropriate for specialized magnet manufacturing, and a $21.98 billion global market leaves room for a consequential company. However, there is no published price, gross margin, qualified customer, contracted volume, or expansion arithmetic. Distribution consists of soliciting quotes and preorders across multiple industries, with no named buyer, sales cycle, conversion rate, or implementation burden. The plan therefore asks feedstock sorting, technical validation, qualification, scaled manufacturing, and customer acquisition to work together before one independently validated production lot is visible.

I pass—not because domestic magnets are unimportant, but because importance is not ownership. The strongest evidence supports the founders; the weakest supports a defensible beachhead, making this a credible team entering a capitalized commodity contest. One verifiable event would flip my decision: a named U.S. defense prime accepting a paid multi-lot production qualification manufactured with the 60-second process. Until then, the secret is unverified and the monopoly is absent.
