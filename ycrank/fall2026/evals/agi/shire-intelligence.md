# Shire Intelligence -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Shire uses existing restaurant CCTV to detect table events, alert staff, and route seating decisions using live server workloads. The memo describes a floor workflow that depends on managers scanning the room and POS records of orders and payments. The seed-stage hypothesis is that restaurants could monitor every camera-covered table throughout a busy shift without assigning a dedicated observer. If alerts are accurate and require little staff verification, Shire could lower the cost per correctly detected and resolved service event enough to make that continuous coverage affordable. Camera ingestion, POS and seating integration, and alert routing would still be required if restaurants had access to equally capable vision models. (*The Idea*)

Shire reports 13 live restaurants and a 12% average reduction in table-turn time. Live use gives customer demand some support, but the reported outcome lacks a published baseline or measurement window. The memo gives no paid conversion or repeat-use data for floor intelligence. The processing-savings testimonial concerns a separate POS benefit. The building-block path scores zero because Shire consumes vision models for restaurant operations and supplies no scarce input to AI deployment. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *Defensibility*)

Comparable camera-based offerings and limited restaurant-data rights constrain durable value capture. The documented prototype and founders’ restaurant and technical experience support execution credit, with inconsistent classifications leaving peak-shift reliability open. Quote-based pricing leaves realized revenue and delivery cost per site unresolved, so scale economics stays at its default. (*Defensibility*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*, *The Idea*)

### Countercase

Shire may help staff perform their existing floor checks faster while leaving their monitoring workload largely intact. Prototype classification errors could require staff to verify alerts, and Toast’s bundled table status already covers part of the host workflow. The reported improvement in table turns cannot establish that continuous coverage became economical. Early live use supports Watch while that gap remains testable. If the product only speeds up existing checks, the enabled-industry path would score zero and the action would become Pass. (*Key Risks*, *Market & Traction*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will a restaurant that previously could afford only intermittent floor checks pay to keep Shire after a matched peak-shift trial shows that it makes continuous coverage affordable? Compare service events correctly detected and resolved on time under Shire and the existing manager-and-POS workflow. Include the subscription, setup, staff verification, and time lost to false alerts and missed events. A paid renewal backed by newly sustained coverage at an affordable cost per resolved event would move the action to Reach out.
