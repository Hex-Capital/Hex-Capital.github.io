# Reconero -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Reconero’s most useful number is its claim that paperwork consumes over 40% of an officer’s day. That identifies a resource bottleneck. Assuming the remaining 60% is productive time, eliminating all paperwork would increase available capacity by roughly 67%, before review overhead. That is consequential for a department. The dossier does not establish an orders-of-magnitude expansion in public-service capacity, however, and the broader $20.4 billion software market measures vendor spending rather than the societal consequence Reconero could create. I can see the benefit. I cannot yet price an extraordinary upside from the evidence supplied.

The technical question is whether Alyx can produce trustworthy, usable records with radically less human work. Searching evidence, extracting entities from body-camera footage, and prefilling reports describe capabilities; they do not establish a discontinuity. Axon already offers Draft One, while Mark43 combines records and dispatch. Reconero provides no measured advantage against either. Officer review makes sense, but correction work could consume much of the promised saving. The comparable Axon trial’s errors identify a test Reconero must run; they do not prove Reconero has the same defect. I assign the technical absent-evidence default because neither superiority nor an inherently commoditized implementation has been demonstrated.

The strongest argument for writing a small check is the founders’ demonstrated initiative. Azeroual previously built a business reporting $150,000 ARR, and Liu left Stanford to build this product. Police-unit trials and iterative officer feedback put them in contact with operational reality. Those are meaningful pre-seed signals. My investment pattern includes execution businesses and difficult regulated workflows; a scientific breakthrough is not mandatory. My exceptions also allow an unusually capable instigator to attack an enormous existing value pool. But the prior revenue belongs to another business, and this dossier documents neither a material belief revised after evidence nor complementary recruiting against a named weakness. Those exceptions keep Reconero worth watching; they do not supply the missing investment case.

The next dollar should resolve evidence-to-report reliability. Trials are useful access, but unnamed trials without accuracy thresholds or correction-time measurements do not tell me how much existential risk has been removed. The dossier supplies no decisive experiment, budget, or stop condition, so milestone discipline receives its default. Likewise, agency subscriptions remain an inferred business model: there is no disclosed price or cost model incorporating migration, support, inference, and officer review. Economic parity receives its default too. Reconero’s integrated RMS/CAD scope makes those implementation costs particularly relevant. There is no evidence of premature sales spending or founder resistance to contrary evidence; I will not manufacture those red flags.

I pass with my own money today. Reconero has credible builders and access to a costly workflow, but the evidence currently supports product activity more strongly than transformative leverage. One verifiable condition would reopen my underwriting: an independently audited agency pilot showing at least 10x lower fully loaded cost per completed, legally usable report than its existing workflow, including review and allocated implementation costs, with no increase in material factual errors. That would establish a capability and an economic mechanism worth investigating further. More demos alone would leave the central uncertainty intact.
