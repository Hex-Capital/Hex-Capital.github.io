# Rise Reforming -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The strongest evidence is not the founders’ molecular-engineering degrees. It is that Lucas Zubillaga led construction and operation of a bench-scale reformer, and the team sustained more than 1,800 hours of syngas production. Rise Reforming has also delivered a pilot container to a wastewater site after pivoting from plastic waste to biogas in 2025 ([YC, Aug 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/rise-reforming); [company website, Jul 2026](https://www.rise-reforming.com/)). This is specific knowledge acquired through hardware, chemistry, and failure—not résumé knowledge. The discount is that the exact current problem remains only partially solved: live wastewater biogas, with its sulfur, siloxanes, moisture, and variable composition, will not enter the system until the 2027 pilot.

Copy the software and little changes. Copy the flowsheet and the difficult work still remains. The physical process, catalyst behavior, feedstock cleanup, modular integration, and field operating history can become durable assets. But “proprietary process” and an IP filing are claims of protection, not proof of protection. M2X Energy has already completed multiple field deployments and raised a $40 million Series B, while CRecTech also markets containerized biogas-to-methanol reactors. Rise has demonstrated stable syngas production, not yet stable live-biogas conversion or commercial chemical output. The substrate is real; the advantage is not yet established.

The market has room to maneuver. Wastewater plants are the first supply wedge, dairy farms and landfills are adjacent sources, and the same reforming capability can feed DME, methanol, or dimethyl carbonate production. Methanol represented a reported $41.6 billion global market in 2025, with DME adding another $11.09 billion ([Grand View Research, 2026](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/methanol-market); [Fortune Business Insights, Jul 2026](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/dimethyl-ether-market-104309)). That is better than a single-purpose climate product. The missing link is economic rather than conceptual: no independently calculated serviceable market shows how many small plants can support Rise’s equipment, cleanup costs, logistics, and chemical offtake simultaneously.

Rise resembles a network only from a distance. It aggregates fragmented offline waste-gas supply, which fits a recurring pattern I like, but it does not yet create permissionless leverage. One wastewater plant does not make the product more useful to another. Every new site requires capital, maintenance, a supply contract, and an offtake relationship from the company. Operating data could eventually improve every subsequent module, but that asset is inferred because the live-biogas fleet does not yet exist. Today this is a vertically operated infrastructure business, not a customer-created network.

The bull case stings because the commercial chain is unusually concrete for pre-seed: one binding biogas-supply agreement, multiple producer MOUs, one conditional DME offtake, $1.7 million raised, and a physical container already at the host. Direct chemical sales could capture value without a network effect. There are also no disclosed integrity, artificial-deadline, or morale-service red flags; the 2025 pivot may show rational updating rather than weak conviction. But the known-exception logic for backing a capital-efficient prototype before an observable user loop does not resolve this case. Rise intends to finance plant CAPEX and maintenance itself, while neither price, commercial output, revenue, nor unit contribution margin is public. Capital intensity amplifies every unresolved technical assumption.

I would not write the personal check today. I expect the team can operate a live-biogas pilot within 36 months, but I would underwrite less than $2 million in annual chemical revenue by then because replication and economics—not molecule production—will become the bottleneck. The option becomes investable upon one verifiable flip condition: a second paid site operating on live biogas with disclosed positive unit-level gross margin. Until then, Rise Reforming is an impressive technical team inside a large market, but the durable asset and scalable value-capture system remain prospective.
