# Wondersearch -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Eliminating embeddings matters if it eliminates a substantial cost of making knowledge usable. Wondersearch has demonstrated a search product; the dossier has not demonstrated that consequence. The cited search and knowledge-discovery market exceeds $6 billion, but that is a spending category, not a calculation of what becomes radically cheaper. How much document preparation, infrastructure, and expert labor disappears? For which workloads? Without that bridge, I cannot price the extraordinary upside that would justify accepting extraordinary failure risk.

The technical evidence deserves attention. An own-model approach using sparse retrieval is more substantive than another interface around a standard model. Production Large scores 80.06 nDCG@10 on SciFact, and the published methodology identifies H100 testing. That earns technical credit. It does not establish a 5–10x discontinuity: an absolute relevance score supplies neither the comparative improvement nor production economics. Pinecone already automates embedding generation; Weaviate combines vector and keyword retrieval. Wondersearch must beat the resulting customer workflow, including ingestion, updates, query latency, and relevance. Removing one preparation step does not by itself establish superiority across that system.

The strongest bull case is that these founders have discovered a different retrieval architecture while building database infrastructure. Dalton’s DSO research and technical presales experience complement Dale’s experience growing Swig Energy to 5,000 customers. They have shipped together, moved from an AI-native operating system into Polygres, and obtained an enterprise POC for that prior product. Their parent’s pgGraph has 1,074 GitHub stars. This resembles my unstated preference for tiny technical teams developing a platform through an initial wedge. But the community belongs to a prior product, and the dossier does not establish that the pivots followed decisive experimental evidence. Those facts support taking the founders seriously; they do not establish Wondersearch adoption, fast belief revision, or complementary recruiting.

I also test my exceptions before passing. I can accept limited initial technical novelty when an exceptional instigator has a credible route into an enormous existing value pool. I can accept missing commercial plans when the demonstrated technical trajectory makes the consequence overwhelming. Wondersearch supplies neither override yet. Its September 28 launch attracted 128,943 X views by October 12, but attention does not establish that customers repeatedly choose its retrieval over alternatives. Equally, the red flags of copying standard models, buying distribution, or scaling before resolving technical risk are not established here. This is insufficient affirmative evidence, rather than an active contradiction of my thesis.

My money would need to buy resolution of the largest uncertainty: whether avoiding pre-embedding produces a durable improvement after costs move elsewhere in the retrieval system. The dossier supplies no costed experiment, threshold, or stop condition. Published prices of $0.001/query and $0.33/GB/month are customer charges, not evidence of inference cost, utilization, margins, or incumbent cost parity. **I pass.** One verifiable result would reopen the decision: an independently reproduced trial on a paying customer’s changing corpus showing at least 10x lower fully loaded retrieval cost than its existing Pinecone pipeline, with relevance and production latency maintained. That would connect the architecture to an economic consequence worth underwriting.
