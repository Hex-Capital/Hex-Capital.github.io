# Outworld Labs -- Paul Graham Evaluation

What happened when the simulated robot behaved differently from the real one? That is the question I would ask Outworld Labs first. Measuring hardware behavior and fitting the simulator to it is a sensible answer in principle. But the dossier gives no particular failure, founder-built workaround, or elapsed time to a better result. Gardea and Grant have relevant research backgrounds. I cannot turn those backgrounds into evidence of what they do when blocked. That leaves my most important criterion at its absent-evidence default.

Horizon earns some technical credit. The founders describe a dexterous-manipulation physics engine, reconstruction tools, and a calibration workflow, rather than merely proposing to research these things. But these are company descriptions. There is no numerical agreement with hardware, reproducible benchmark, or demonstrated improvement in transferred policies. Grant’s reported tie for first at the September 28, 2026 YC showcase suggests the presentation impressed people. It does not establish which physical predictions the engine gets right. My pattern of backing technical teams outside familiar sectors depends on concrete proof; Outworld has supplied the technical proposition without that proof.

The strongest case for investing is that these founders may know exactly where existing simulators fail. Both worked at BAIR; Grant’s research includes legged-robot control and experience at Google DeepMind, NASA, and Oxford. Contact dynamics is a specific difficulty, and collecting hardware measurements is tedious work that could conceal an opportunity. Free alternatives such as MuJoCo and Isaac Sim would matter much less if Horizon reliably saved a robot-learning team weeks of physical experiments. That possibility deserves attention. Yet proximity to the problem does not establish that either founder personally encountered this failure, and the dossier gives no dated origin story or evidence-driven change of route. Those dimensions remain at their defaults.

Who needs the crude version badly enough to keep using it? Outworld names robot foundation-model and evaluation teams as its intended users, but identifies no returning user, paid engagement, or customer workflow. The 23 company-account followers tell me essentially nothing. Neither does the absence of published revenue disqualify a company this young. The important missing fact is whether anyone’s work already depends on Horizon. Pricing is also undisclosed, so there is no evidence yet that the cost of calibration leaves a useful commercial product. Outworld resembles my pattern of backing programmable technical infrastructure, but developer dependence is what makes that pattern meaningful.

I would pass on the present evidence. Outworld has not demonstrated the founder action required by my investment gate, and its technical claims are not strong enough to supply an exception. The working-product and independently validated-proof exceptions deserve consideration, but neither is established here. I also see no evidence that the founders are waiting for permission, outsourcing customer learning, or depending on a large partner; an invitation to collaborate is not itself such a dependency. My one verifiable flip condition would be a customer-confirmed calibration trial documenting a specific simulator failure, a founder-built correction within two weeks, and improved hardware performance that makes the customer return. That single episode would connect the team’s technical promise to the behavior I actually invest in.
