# Egoist Machines -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Egoist Machines’s contested truth is that personal AI context should belong to the user as a portable permission object, not to the application that collected it. The falsifying event is straightforward: if consumers will not repeatedly approve context sharing, or independent AI applications will not integrate a neutral passport, then this is a privacy preference masquerading as infrastructure. The inbox, field-level permissions, duration limits, and access receipts make the proposition concrete. But the dossier reports a waitlist, not a shipped experiment demonstrating that users or developers actually choose this architecture.

The central problem is monopoly, not usefulness. Egoist enters a field containing Mem0, Supermemory, Letta, Personal AI, and increasingly capable native memory from OpenAI and Anthropic. Its pass-and-receipt interaction is differentiated, but differentiation is not ownership. There are no disclosed integrations, active users, exclusive data assets, technical performance advantages, or structural barriers. The proposed network effect requires both sides of the network to exist first; until applications request Passport data and users accumulate portable context, it is a diagram of a moat rather than a moat.

The founders are the strongest evidence. David Khachaturov brings a Cambridge computer-science PhD, work on OpenAI’s PaperBench, AI-security experience at FrontierOne, and a 4,700-star GitHub project. Erin McGurk ranked first in Land Economy at Cambridge and built educational channels reaching more than one million followers. They knew each other at Cambridge before forming the company, rather than meeting at an entrepreneurship event to manufacture a team. Still, the dossier does not establish that they previously shipped together, that both are unambiguously full-time, that core engineering is entirely internal, or that they retain material ownership. This clears the obvious founding red flags without proving unusually durable alignment.

Distribution is more definite than the business model. McGurk reports 515,000 Instagram, 205,000 TikTok, 55,000 YouTube, and 5,000 Substack followers, giving Egoist a real consumer-attention asset; the team is also conducting direct developer outreach and planning office and university visits. Yet consumer reach does not solve developer adoption, and the Passport is advertised as free for life while application pricing remains unpublished. A two-sided protocol with no demonstrated paying side, sales cycle, or integration economics cannot yet support power-law arithmetic.

The bull case is sharper than the current traction suggests. A consumer founder with unusual reach and a technically credible security founder could establish a trusted identity layer before incumbent applications recognize that users demand portability. This also fits the less visible pattern of investing in consumer information systems that make control over contested data legible. But the known-exception logic does not rescue the company: there is no independently verified zero-to-one technical event or persistent user behavior strong enough to override commodity competition. The software version of a coordination failure remains—consumer adoption, application integrations, secure custody, and monetization must reinforce one another before any one has been validated.

I pass today. The likely outcome is a pivot from a universal consumer passport toward a narrower developer or enterprise consent SDK, because businesses can have a direct compliance budget while consumers have been promised a free product. The single condition that would flip the decision is three unaffiliated consumer-AI applications integrating Passport and at least 10,000 users carrying approved context across two of them monthly for three consecutive months. That would convert the secret from an assertion into behavior and the network effect from a possibility into evidence.
