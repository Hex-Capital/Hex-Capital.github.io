# STASIS -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with screen-heavy consumers, the same glasses could address recurring eye fatigue across markets. The company describes a reusable core of inward-facing sensors, automatic tint, and break prompts. How much work prescription fulfillment and connected-screen setup would require across markets remains uncertain. (The Idea)

The founder’s launch post directs buyers to a checkout with a $99 refundable reservation. That establishes a direct path to reservation, while completed purchases and independent setup remain unverified. Reservations opened November 9, 2026, with shipment planned for March 2027. The reported 1.62 million launch-post views measure attention. Reservation counts, realized revenue, and paying-customer counts remain unknown, so initial traction takes the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Repeated screen use gives buyers a reason to keep wearing the glasses, and the optional $10 monthly Premium tier offers a path to recurring revenue. The memo leaves paid adoption, renewal, and further spending growth within an account unestablished. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Even with strong fit, STASIS could sell primarily one-time hardware purchases while incurring meaningful costs on every order. At the listed $299 first-batch or $399 regular price, per-order contribution would depend on what remains after manufacturing, prescription lenses, fulfillment, acquisition, returns, and support. Those costs are unquantified, so cost economics takes the absent-evidence default. The structural gate remains open because the memo leaves both the cost of serving the next buyer and the work required to enter another market unresolved. These gaps keep confidence Low. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can STASIS repeatedly convert web traffic into fully paid, independently activated orders with positive contribution in its initial market and one additional market using substantially the same product? Successive cohorts showing full payment, independent setup, and positive contribution after acquisition, fulfillment, returns, and support would support Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 with at least Medium confidence. Evidence that either market requires substantial bespoke rebuilding or structurally uneconomic per-order work would move the action to Pass.
