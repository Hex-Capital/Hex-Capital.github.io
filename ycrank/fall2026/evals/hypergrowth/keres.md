# Keres -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** Military organizations responsible for protecting personnel and installations face a recurring cost problem when defending against lower-cost drones. Keres claims Garmur can return to service after an intercept, which could lower the buyer’s cost per intercept if the aircraft sees enough repeat use and is economical to return to service. The U.S. defense accelerator award is an observed sign of access to acquisition officials. Its value is undisclosed, and the memo documents no paid deployment or verified intercept-and-recovery result. The award supports a route to buyer evaluation. Paid activation, current fit, and repeat purchasing remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Growth scenario.** If a U.S. buyer validates the economics, Keres could sell a standard system to additional installations through a repeatable procurement and deployment process, then expand within accounts as buyers add sites. Purchases of alternative systems in the U.S. and UK show that the underlying job recurs across markets. Portability of Garmur remains an assumption. A UK rollout could require separate procurement qualification, export clearance, and local integration and servicing. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

For Keres, each additional deployment would bring in hardware and any contracted service revenue against manufacturing, inventory financing, buyer qualification, integration, and support costs. The growth case depends on reusing enough qualification and deployment work across sites to improve contribution as volume grows. The memo supplies no pricing or deployment cost data to test that assumption. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Buyers already procure alternatives, and Garmur’s claimed reuse advantage remains unverified. If returning each aircraft to service is costly and each installation requires its own qualification and support effort, additional orders could add production capital and delivery costs roughly in proportion to sales. The memo establishes neither that cost pattern nor a structural ceiling on international demand, so the Pass gate is untriggered. A completed buyer evaluation would resolve more than a conversation today. (Defensibility, Key Risks)
