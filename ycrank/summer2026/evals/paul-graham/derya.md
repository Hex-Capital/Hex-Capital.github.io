# Derya -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The most important fact about Derya is missing: I cannot find the moment when something broke and the founders bent the system to keep a shipment moving. The dossier shows founder-led introductions, workflow reviews, and first-month sales, but no named customs failure, rejected booking, unavailable integration, or carrier problem followed by a dated workaround. Karaca and Turna may be resourceful. Their credentials do not prove it. This matters because logistics produces obstacles more reliably than software produces demos.

The strongest evidence is that someone already wants the crude product. Derya reports $80,000 in contracted revenue and $3.2 million of sourced freight opportunities in its first month, while TVO signed for 20 containers. Contracted revenue is weaker than collected and renewed revenue, and opportunity GTV is not revenue. But TVO is a named customer accepting real shipment execution, not a waitlist applauding “agentic supply chains.” That is unusually informative at pre-seed.

The founders also reached this problem organically. Karaca worked in maritime and international-trade law at CEVA/CMA CGM. Turna built ship-operations software used across more than 30 vessels representing $200 million in fleet value. Customs, insurance, carrier quotes, storage, invoicing, and shipment exceptions are genuine schleps. Their eight-year relationship and prior shared business reduce the risk that the team was assembled for YC. The origin is strong even though the dossier does not identify the particular frustration that caused them to start Derya.

What I cannot yet distinguish is software from energetic freight service. The company apparently has enough machinery to source and book loads, but there is no working-API description, automation rate, exception-resolution benchmark, or technical threshold. The unstated pattern I care about is a concrete proof spike before the round. Derya has a commercial proof spike, not yet a technical one. Nor is there evidence of a rejected product hypothesis and rapid route change, so flexibility remains untested rather than disproved.

The bull case stings. Two domain-native founders have found a narrow well immediately, charge a recurring monthly fee rather than marking up freight, and personally sell an operations review before activating the plan. They are not waiting for press, a sales team, or an unspecified large partner. This could be precisely how a proprietary operating system is assembled: perform the work manually, record every exception, and automate the repeated parts. The known-exception pattern does not rescue the remaining uncertainty, however, because there is neither an independently measured artifact nor unmistakable repeat usage sufficient to substitute for the missing resourcefulness evidence.

The flat-fee model is also where this can quietly become a consultancy. Specialists perform the services while Derya absorbs variable complexity across bookings, customs, insurance, storage, and exceptions. Flexport, Forto, Nuvocargo, and 5U AI already overlap portions of the workflow, and Derya has disclosed neither gross margin nor a proprietary data substrate. My 36-month forecast is that by August 2029 Derya will have narrowed into workflow software for existing freight forwarders and remain below $5 million ARR, rather than scaling the full outsourced logistics office as currently described. I would not write the check yet. The single flip condition is a dated customer case showing that a shipment exception forced the founders to ship an automated workaround that subsequently handled the same exception for renewing customers at positive contribution margin.
