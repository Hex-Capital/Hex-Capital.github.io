# OpenRelay -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: accelerator proliferation has fragmented inference capacity, AI teams now buy a reliable workload outcome rather than a particular GPU, and OpenRelay becomes large only if cross-provider routing produces consistently better availability-adjusted economics than customers can obtain from a single cloud. The controlling variable is therefore liquidity at the workload level—not nominal GPU supply. OpenRelay must repeatedly match region, latency, throughput, model, and security requirements while preserving meaningful gross margin. The dossier explains this mechanism but does not give the decisive threshold: provider depth per configuration, routing success rate, or margin after capacity payments.

The strongest evidence is that this is already more than a market narrative. OpenRelay reports processing over 100 billion tokens weekly across 22 locations, four continents, and 11 accelerator SKUs. A production B300 deployment reportedly reduced infrastructure cost by more than 25% versus reserved-cloud pricing. These are unusually substantive pre-seed signals, although they remain company-reported and are not accompanied by revenue, named customers, retention, or workload concentration. Token volume can demonstrate technical throughput while concealing weak monetization; at the posted prices, an enormous token count need not imply enormous revenue.

The technology shift is real but not yet fully quantified. AMD, Google, and NVIDIA accelerators create enough hardware heterogeneity for continuous benchmarking and routing to matter. The product also owns operational actions—deployment, scaling, metering, hosted-inference failover, and hardware selection—rather than simply formatting model output. Better chips and models should expand the routing surface instead of absorbing the product. However, dedicated VMs do not automatically migrate, and the OpenAI-compatible interface makes adoption easy while also making departure easy. OpenRelay has built a useful control plane, but it has not yet shown durable proprietary state or a structural barrier.

The bear case is severe. RunPod reports $120 million ARR, Together AI is estimated near $1 billion in annualized revenue, and Lambda generated $520 million in 2025. Any of them could add broader routing, subsidize inference, or bundle it with owned capacity. OpenRelay’s 25% customer saving could come from intelligent utilization—or from surrendering its own margin while reselling commodity hardware. This activates the red flag around physical infrastructure retaining commodity economics beneath a software layer. The portfolio pattern of buying multiple layers of a discontinuity supports considering the company, but it does not excuse undifferentiated capacity brokerage. Nor does any known exception apply: this is not frontier research whose scarce technical team alone overrides missing market economics.

The founders are the reason I believe the current operating evidence is credible. Prashant Patel worked on 10,000-plus-GPU orchestration at Voltage Park and led an Amazon Bedrock product to multi-million-dollar ARR. Jaden Wang previously founded Heaviside Compute, built a half-megawatt data center at age 20, and worked across TensorDock and Voltage Park. Together they cover both inference software and physical capacity. Wang’s earlier company and unusually costly infrastructure work provide evidence of second-time learning and commitment, although the dossier does not establish the personal desperation required for a maximum founder score.

I would invest, but this is a measured infrastructure bet rather than a market-ending move already in view. My 36-month base case is $15 million to $30 million ARR, with hosted inference becoming the core product and dedicated GPU rentals remaining secondary. The upside is much larger if routing data compounds into superior reliability and provider density; the downside is a low-margin broker that funded clouds can copy. I would flip to pass if, by August 2027, OpenRelay cannot demonstrate a cohort of at least ten external production customers with greater than 120% net revenue retention at greater than 30% gross margin.
