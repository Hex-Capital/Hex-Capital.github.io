# RouterPlus -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

RouterPlus’s strongest possible secret is that inference capacity is sufficiently fragmented that aggregating demand can secure production economics unavailable through existing marketplaces. The test is straightforward: can customers obtain the same capacity, reliability, and total cost through OpenRouter? The dossier establishes a useful procurement service, but it supplies neither a named prevailing belief that the founders reject nor a comparative result establishing their opposing proposition. A secret may exist here. It has not yet been demonstrated.

The decisive problem is value capture. RouterPlus reports relationships with 40+ inference providers, but supplier access without exclusivity gives competitors a shopping list. OpenRouter already offers customer-specific evaluations and dedicated deployments, overlapping the proposed differentiation. Aggregated procurement data might eventually improve purchasing, but permission to retain data does not establish a proprietary advantage. The initial customer segment—production AI teams seeking better inference economics—is also broad. RouterPlus has yet to identify a bounded market in which its position becomes stronger with each transaction. This is affirmative competitive pressure, rather than merely missing pre-seed evidence.

The strongest objection to passing is the reported customer running 3 million agent conversations daily. If RouterPlus is serving a substantial share of that workload, the founders may already be learning capacity requirements that generic gateways miss. The reported reduction in another application’s P95 latency from 4.8 seconds to under 4 seconds also suggests operational usefulness. These are more informative than launch attention. They do not establish how much traffic RouterPlus handles, whether the customer pays, or whether the improvement is reproducible against competing procurement options. My known exceptions require evidence that removes the failed gate: persistent demand can establish usefulness, and a technical breakthrough can overturn incumbent assumptions. Neither result here establishes why RouterPlus will own the resulting economics.

Narayana’s four years building Samsung GPU infrastructure and prior role as Trupeer’s first hire and CTO make the operating competence credible; Bishoyi’s edge-AI deployment experience is relevant too. But these credentials do not answer the founding-structure question. Both attended IIT Bombay; prior work together, full-time status, ownership, and economic exposure remain undisclosed. I retain the stated default for this dimension. Bishoyi’s investing affiliation alone does not establish divided commitment, and the dossier documents none of the disqualifying ownership or outsourced-engineering arrangements. RouterPlus fits my observed interest in the AI production stack, but that pattern earns no exemption from establishing a durable position within it.

The commercial route is concrete enough to test: a playground, API documentation, workload submissions, and founder demos reach developers with an existing infrastructure bill. That earns limited distribution credit, although conversion, sales cycles, and implementation costs remain unknown. The revenue engine is less established. Prepaid token sales identify what is sold; separately contracted reservations identify another way to purchase capacity. Neither reveals RouterPlus’s retained margin. Free BYOK inference makes that distinction consequential. Three million customer conversations cannot be converted into marketplace revenue, and OpenRouter’s estimated annualized revenue belongs to OpenRouter. There is no supported arithmetic yet connecting RouterPlus’s transactions to a power-law outcome.

I would pass with my own money. RouterPlus could become a capable capacity broker, but the current evidence supports customer assistance more strongly than market ownership. My verifiable flip condition is a paid, renewed customer cohort in one defined beachhead whose contracts demonstrate an exclusive capacity advantage that RouterPlus retains at positive gross margin and OpenRouter cannot match. That would turn supplier relationships into evidence of a monopoly mechanism.
