# herdr -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

herdr has demonstrated demand for controlling coding agents. It has not demonstrated that controlling them changes the economics of software production by an order of magnitude. Those are different investment propositions. Persistent terminals, semantic agent states, and independently attachable clients can remove real friction. The dossier supplies no baseline for engineering hours recovered, additional useful work completed, or expensive expertise displaced. I cannot price an extraordinary consequence from installation counts alone.

The strongest argument against passing is substantial: Can Celik built from his own multi-agent workflow and attracted an ecosystem around the result. By October 27, 2026, herdr had 46,194 GitHub stars, 3,691 forks, and a reported 1,789 community plugins. DigitalOcean and Fly.io integrations suggest infrastructure providers see a useful distribution surface. This matches my unstated preference for tiny open-source teams that assemble a technical community around a platform-shaped wedge. If herdr becomes the common control layer across agents and machines, its importance could grow far beyond terminal convenience. That possibility deserves attention.

But the mechanism that makes herdr useful also makes value capture uncertain. The Apache-2.0 runtime is free; Herdr Cloud proposes connecting customer-owned machines without SSH configuration. Warp, Conductor, and Paradev already attack adjacent execution and coordination problems. Herdr Extended demonstrates that another distributor can ship the upstream software with additional functionality. Open source can accelerate a standard, but adoption does not automatically give its originator control over the commercial layer. The dossier contains no measured technical advantage that would force customers or ecosystem participants to remain with herdr.

There is genuine engineering here. A background server owns processes, and the socket API exposes agent state and controls. I would not dismiss that as an interface wrapped around a model API. Nevertheless, there is no benchmark showing a 5–10x improvement against tmux, Zellij, or another named alternative. Server restarts still destroy the original processes. Unsandboxed plugins inherit the user’s permissions, introducing a separate trust problem as the ecosystem grows. These are specific engineering concerns to test. They do not establish either technical impossibility or technical discontinuity.

Celik’s ultrasound and fintech AI experience provides relevant technical context. It does not document belief revision after contrary evidence or complementary recruits brought in to own these risks, so I retain the founder default. Likewise, the September 8 announcement of $6 million in seed financing establishes available capital, not risk removed. There is no disclosed experiment budget, pass/fail threshold, or stop condition. I would spend first on determining whether customers will entrust persistent workflows to the system and pay for the coordination layer. Cloud pricing, support burden, and operating costs are undisclosed; free local execution cannot establish sustainable paid economics.

I pass with my own money. My exceptions permit an extraordinary instigator addressing a quantified giant value pool, foundational capability advancing exponentially, or a mission with exceptional resource leverage. The community is affirmative evidence, but the dossier does not establish one of those overrides. Missing revenue at this stage is not the objection. The objection is that herdr’s strongest demonstrated property—distribution—has not established the consequence or defensibility I require. **My flip condition:** a reproducible customer deployment showing herdr delivers at least 5x more accepted coding work per engineer-hour than a named alternative, with customers renewing a paid deployment at a positive contribution margin. That would connect the ecosystem to a consequential, economically sustainable capability.
