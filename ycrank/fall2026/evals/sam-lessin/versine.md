# Versine -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Versine has specified how money moves more clearly than how investor money earns a return. The proposed post-money, competing-term-sheet count, and financing history are undisclosed. I cannot establish that this is the first scarce check. That earns my missing-terms default; YC membership and competitors’ financing do not establish an auction. The second gate is also unanswered: no disclosed use of proceeds names a finite experiment, measurable result, and deadline. Building agent payments is a product direction. What truth does my check buy?

There is a plausible experiment inside the product: will people repeatedly delegate personal payments when they can approve recipients and cap spending? Versine’s passkey-controlled USDC wallets, approved rules, and account-free recipient links address concrete obstacles to that behavior. But the payments product had not launched as of November 5, 2026. Its 1,076 LinkedIn followers and consumer/developer waitlists establish visibility, with no documented payment or repeat usage. I assign the missing-conversion default rather than treating an unlaunched product as failed conversion. The potential asset is a trusted, recurring payment workflow; the dossier does not yet show Versine owns one.

The strongest case against passing is that these founders could connect two systems whose participants distrust each other: probabilistic agents and financial infrastructure. Harshith’s Stripe engineering experience supplies relevant exposure, while Param’s work on Databricks vector search, Quora advertising models, and Glean evaluations provides concrete technical experience beyond school credentials. That earns modest founder credit. It does not establish that the team has shipped and operated a payments system or secured proprietary access to its users. Versine fits my unstated pattern of translation layers in its design, but demonstrated cross-system execution remains missing. The documented exception mechanisms—paid conversion, retention, external acquisition validation, or a prior exit paired with explicit financing terms—are also absent.

The distinction matters because the difficult part may become liability without becoming ownership. Versine discloses agent manipulation risk, potentially irreversible transfers, and dependence on Bridge, Coinflow/Brale, and Coinbase infrastructure. Those disclosures are not evidence of reckless operation. They do identify costs and dependencies that a free consumer product must eventually support. Accounts, sending, and bank payouts are free; company monetization, margins, burn, and runway are undisclosed. I use the economics default because losses are unproven. Still, deleting the next financing leaves no documented cash engine. Stripe’s existing merchant relationships and agent-payment infrastructure sharpen the question: which recurring relationship belongs to Versine after everyone can offer agent interfaces?

I pass at **29/100**. This is infrastructure for agents, so dismissing it as a generic AI wrapper would miss the real issue: Versine has relevant builders and a coherent permission mechanism, but no demonstrated scarce distribution or capital-funded venture moment. My one verifiable flip condition is a completed, seed-funded cohort test: within six months, 100 households each make at least three delegated payments monthly, with positive contribution after provider fees, support, and payment losses. That is a proposed underwriting test, not an existing company commitment. Passing it would show that Versine owns recurring behavior worth paying to serve and give me a reason to negotiate a check.
