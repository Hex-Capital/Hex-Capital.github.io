# Ample Computer -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Ample Computer could remove the cloud operator between a coding agent and a running application. That would matter if it made reliable software creation accessible to people who cannot assemble databases, networking, and deployment tooling. But the dossier establishes a shorter deployment workflow without quantifying how much expensive expertise disappears. There is no baseline of operator hours, spending displaced, or additional people served. I can imagine a consequential outcome. I cannot underwrite the imagined magnitude as evidence.

The technical question is whether Ample changes the economics of operating applications. Its MCP connector and CLI provision infrastructure, deploy into isolated microVMs, and return an HTTPS URL with logs. That is a functioning product proposition. Railway and Render already expose deployment operations to agents, however, so agent access itself does not establish a discontinuity. Creating an application before a human claims it could remove an important onboarding constraint. The dossier supplies no comparative deployment-success rate, recovery performance, or operator-time measurement demonstrating that advantage. I leave technical superiority unproven; I do not assume the underlying engineering is trivial.

The strongest argument against passing is distribution arriving at precisely the right interface. Ample secured an Anthropic-verified connector in October 2026, maintains a Hermes plugin, and displays six applications. Vercel’s reported 28-fold growth in agent-triggered deployments suggests the workflow is expanding rapidly, although that is market evidence rather than Ample’s traction. My actual investing pattern includes execution businesses without scientific breakthroughs. A small team could win by owning this new deployment relationship before incumbents reorganize around it. But marketplace availability does not establish repeated selection, and the showcased applications have undisclosed ownership and payment status. Neither a committed technical community nor a proprietary learning loop is demonstrated.

Ravi’s previous startup acquisition and Shopify product experience, alongside Ishan’s published integration work, give the team relevant execution context. They do not document belief revision or complementary recruiting against a specific weakness. Likewise, the company reports putting its Personal Agent Protocol implementation into production on October 17, 2026, but supplies no experiment showing which existential uncertainty that deployment resolved. These dimensions receive the missing-evidence defaults. Two people and no disclosed revenue are ordinary pre-seed conditions. The unanswered question is how cheaply the founders can establish that agents repeatedly choose and successfully operate Ample when competing infrastructure is available.

Usage billing and per-second compute metering describe how Ample charges, without establishing its delivery costs or customer savings. There is no evidence of permanent subsidy dependence, and no disclosed route to superior unsubsidized economics. I also find no documented instigator-plus-quantified-consequence case that would justify my usual exceptions for investing before technical or commercial proof. **I pass on the current evidence.** The relevant AI red flag is interface convenience becoming the entire advantage; the dossier leaves that risk unresolved. My one condition to reopen is a reproducible comparison across 100 matched application deployments showing at least 10x less human operating time than Railway or Render, including failure recovery, at comparable reliability and no higher total cost. That experiment would establish whether Ample removes scarce expertise at a different economic scale.
