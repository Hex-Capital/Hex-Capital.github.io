# Perit.AI -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** Perit’s strongest case is supplying frontier labs with consented, quality-checked human interactions for live voice evaluation across accents, interruptions, and noisy calls. The memo identifies a concrete new testing need as voice models gain the ability to listen and speak simultaneously. Perit says static datasets miss failures that surface in live interaction and reports delivering 6,000+ speech hours across nine locales. The memo provides no like-for-like benchmark showing that Perit’s live data finds consequential failures that existing test sets miss. (*The Idea; Market & Traction*)

Perit reports using its Foundry workspace to calibrate reviewers and run batch QA. That process could make qualified data easier to deliver repeatedly. The memo documents separate pricing for exclusive dataset rights, while access to qualified contributors remains unproven. The CEO reports six lab customers. The company reports $11M+ in revenue over 4.5 months, and the memo records a separate $9M+ figure for the same period without reconciling the two. These are meaningful early demand signals, with buyer-level payment, renewal, and contract terms still unverified. (*Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Facts; Key Risks*)

The memo records a live dataset catalog and describes relevant founder experience in localized routing and map-quality systems. Perit is based in San Francisco and plausibly fits the seed-led mandate, although the financing stage is unspecified. For voice, the scale test is contribution on the next accepted audio hour after contributor pay, reviewer time, rejected takes, and consent administration. Unit-based quotes and itemized contributor pay make that test practical. The memo gives no realized contribution per hour or reachable program volume. (*Founders & Team; Market & Traction; The Idea; Key Risks*)

### Countercase

**Scenario assumption:** With abundant capital and the same cheap models, labs and competing vendors could recruit from the same contributor pools and replicate Perit’s QA. Its standard dataset license is non-exclusive, the reported expert roster figures of 1,600+ and 16,000+ are unreconciled, and vendors already sell overlapping robotics and reinforcement-learning data services. That overlap does not establish an equivalent live voice offering. Path A remains plausible, with control of qualified supply unresolved. Path B has no separate supported case because the memo shows neither a newly viable buyer or transaction enabled by lower delivery labor nor a reason that buyer would keep paying Perit under model parity. (*Defensibility; Key Risks; The Idea; Market & Traction*)
