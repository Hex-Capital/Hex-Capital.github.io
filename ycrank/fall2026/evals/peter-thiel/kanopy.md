# Kanopy -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Kanopy’s candidate secret is that ordinary cameras can produce utility-grade clearance measurements without specialized sensing hardware. The advertised 2–4-inch accuracy makes that proposition testable: compare its measurements against independently surveyed ground truth across representative vegetation, wire configurations, and capture conditions. The company reports 3,093 mapped miles, which establishes more than a research proposal. It does not establish measurement accuracy. Nor does the dossier identify a competitor that explicitly rejects the technical proposition. There is enough substance to investigate the secret, but insufficient evidence to believe it.

The investment question is whether proving that secret would give Kanopy something it could own. Utilities inspecting vegetation around power lines constitute a concrete beachhead. Yet customer-owned cameras remove a hardware requirement without necessarily creating proprietary technology. Public reconstruction models could lower development costs for competitors as well. AiDASH, LiveEO, and Overstory use different sensing approaches, so their existence does not prove Kanopy is interchangeable with them. Equally, the difference in sensing does not prove a monopoly mechanism. Exclusive data, a measured technical advantage, and durable customer dependence remain unestablished.

The strongest case against passing is commercial. Six utilities and contractors signed LOIs or MSAs covering 270,000 miles, according to Antler’s April 2026 report. Combined with reported field mapping—including over 300 miles across four states in two weeks—this suggests buyers may accept an inspection workflow that avoids specialized equipment. The founders have also demonstrated at Trees & Utilities, giving their sales effort a specific customer gathering rather than an abstract enterprise-sales label. But scoped mileage is neither purchased mileage nor evidence of repeat acquisition economics. Prices, implementation costs, sales cycles, and payment status remain undisclosed.

Yann’s utility advisory experience and Nicolas’s computer-vision work make this a credible team to conduct that experiment. Their being brothers establishes a relationship predating the fundraising process; it does not establish prior operating alignment, full-time status, or economic exposure. Those founding questions receive the cautious default rather than an adverse judgment. Kanopy fits my broader interest in bringing software into neglected physical infrastructure. It also benefits from an existing customer capture device, avoiding much of the factory-and-financing coordination stack that can destroy an industrial startup. Neither observation supplies the missing ownership mechanism.

The known exceptions do not yet change my decision. An independently demonstrated technical breakthrough could justify investing before market ownership becomes clear; persistent paid usage could reveal demand stronger than the public commercial record suggests. Kanopy currently supplies neither form of overriding evidence. Release-note corrections to pole association and wire fitting warrant testing, but they do not establish a collapsed technical claim. Likewise, the dossier shows no part-time-founder, outsourced-engineering, or damaged-cap-table red flag. My concern is therefore specific: useful inspection software can create substantial customer value while retaining little bargaining power. The $31.95 billion vegetation-management estimate includes physical treatment, and without pricing or margins it cannot support Kanopy’s own power-law arithmetic.

I would pass on writing a check today and keep the technical diligence open. One verifiable flip condition would be an independently audited, paid multi-utility deployment demonstrating a proprietary clearance-measurement advantage at the advertised accuracy, with repeat purchasing at disclosed, attractive software margins. That would connect the proposed secret to customer dependence and an economic engine. Today, Kanopy has a plausible route into utilities; it has not demonstrated why it becomes their indispensable supplier.
