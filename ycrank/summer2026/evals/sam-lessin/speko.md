# Speko -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Beknazar Abdikamalov spent four years selecting voice stacks and previously built voice-enabled AI as Hupo’s CTO, making Speko a missionary operator trapped inside my least attractive asset class: consensus AI plumbing. Speko’s highest dimension is founder conviction; Speko’s lowest is category fit. “OpenRouter for voice AI” is legible enough to earn YC attention, but legibility is not mispricing—the value-seed question is whether I own an overlooked asset or merely a 5% toll inside someone else’s infrastructure stack.

**Drop.io** is the high rung on founder conviction: I worked against free incumbents long enough that the pre-pitch behavior proved the obsession. Speko shares the load-bearing mechanism because Abdikamalov reports four years of repeatedly testing voice providers before building Speko, rather than discovering voice routing during Summer 2026. Speko diverges because there is no public category writing, no personal GitHub work, and no visible non-obvious claim beyond “benchmarks should choose providers”; the experience is real, but the missionary temperament is reported rather than independently demonstrated.

**OpenAI-of-[X] copycats** define the low rung’s failure mechanism: consensus AI exposure gets repackaged as a thin startup abstraction while economics remain with models and scaled platforms. Speko is better than a generic agent shell—seven dated benchmark boards covering 56 providers and 159 models are actual work—but Speko still sits exactly where LiveKit already routes inference and Vapi already permits provider swapping. The unstated-pattern check is worse: Speko is neither pre-consensus crypto, DTC brand, prescription-rail health, creator infrastructure, holdco, nor demonstrated Wizard-of-Oz operation. Speko is software on top of AI providers, the sleeve I have explicitly de-rated.

No public round price, investor roster, or financing terms exist for Speko, so I apply the 9/30 absent-evidence default and shade down for the hot-deck framing. Over the next 18 months, Speko’s 5% routing fee compounds only if routing volume grows faster than providers and adjacent platforms compress the toll; otherwise LiveKit’s $181.3 million capital base and Vapi’s $72 million turn Speko’s integration-maintenance asset into a feature treadmill. Speko could still be mispriced, but without terms I have no demonstrated value-seed edge—only a Summer 2026 YC infrastructure company in a consensus category.

Speko charges provider cost plus 5% and $0.09 per hosted minute, which could make Speko capital-efficient if benchmark maintenance remains cheap and enterprise minimums fund operations. The capital-structure score flips upward if Speko can become default-alive on usage revenue before another institutional round, or if Abdikamalov structures the asset around revenue share rather than a generic SAFE. Today Speko shows pricing but no revenue, committed minimum, margin profile, or unusual instrument, so I see a potentially bootstrappable API being force-fit into the standard SaaS regatta.

**John Fish / Bookshelved** is the relevant single-anchor parallel for owned distribution: the audience existed before the investment, so customer acquisition was already an asset on the cap table. Speko has 219 company LinkedIn followers, 90 weekly MCP downloads, 85 weekly SDK downloads, and two GitHub stars per principal SDK repository; those are discovery traces, not community. Speko reports no founder reach, so Abdikamalov’s reputation cannot yet subsidize acquisition or create the John Fish-style downside cushion.

LiveKit’s existing cross-provider routing makes my 36-month forecast blunt: by July 2029, Speko will remain below $3 million ARR and be acquired by LiveKit primarily for Abdikamalov and the benchmark integrations. Speko’s experienced founder can build a credible measurement layer, but Speko’s weak category ownership lets the better-capitalized real-time platform capture that layer before the 5% toll becomes an independent control point. That is a respectable operator outcome and a poor personal-angel underwriting case unless the entry price is exceptionally low.

Seven benchmark boards spanning 159 models are the strongest bull case: Speko could accumulate workload-specific failure data, turn automatic fallback into the voice control plane, and collect 5% across a market forecast to reach $35.24 billion by 2033. **Pinterest** is the Known Exception that stings—missionary founder quality can override my price filter when the network channel and entry terms are special—and Speko’s four years of voice-stack work could be the analogous founder signal. The objection still loses because Speko has not demonstrated proprietary data, customer-specific routing advantage, revenue, owned distribution, or a personal-network price exception; **Ro**’s capital-intensity exception also does not apply because Speko’s dependency is not a regulatory moat. I would flip to Invest if Speko reaches $2 million ARR from routing and hosted-infrastructure fees by July 2027.
