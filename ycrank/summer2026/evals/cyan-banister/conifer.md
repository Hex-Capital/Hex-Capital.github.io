# Conifer -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Sage’s 268 GitHub stars make Conifer an observable developer artifact wrapped around an unresolved human structure. Conifer scores highest where I can download Juniper, run a model on an M3, and test the reported 194.6 tokens per second; Conifer scores lowest where Michael Jeffords and Charles Muehlberger are both merely “Co-Founder,” with no named CEO, tiebreaker, or co-founder history. The artifact is real. The adult in charge is not yet visible.

**Niantic** is the high rung for physical verifiability because I lived inside Ingress before anyone needed to explain the product to me. Conifer shares that load-bearing mechanism: Juniper is downloadable, Sage is open source, and the claimed 60% decoding advantage over llama.cpp can be challenged on actual Apple Silicon. Conifer diverges because only a technically equipped developer can feel that magic, the benchmark covers selected models and hardware, and no production user has verified that cheaper routing still completes the job correctly.

**HQ Trivia** is the low governance rung because an irresistible artifact temporarily concealed an unresolved Yusupov-Kroll authority structure; the feud eventually collected its debt. Conifer shows the opening bars of that song: Conifer has two identified co-founders, no public CEO designation, and no evidence explaining how Jeffords and Muehlberger resolve an unresolvable disagreement. One foot in love does not mean closing my eyes. Until somebody can answer “who is the tiebreaker?” in one sentence, governance is a hard structural risk rather than missing pre-seed paperwork.

Charles Muehlberger built edge-AI devices for Department of War brain-injury modeling, giving Conifer genuine technical adjacency, but **Anduril** is the sharper founder parallel: Palmer Luckey’s biography made defense hardware feel inevitable, while Conifer’s dossier never shows either founder personally drowning in enterprise token bills or operating the systems Conifer wants to govern. Conifer gets meaningful credit for Muehlberger’s edge-inference research and Jeffords’s ML work, yet HeartCheck’s 10,000 screenings through 20-plus barbershops belong to a different problem. Tiz is present; the autobiographical reason Conifer had to exist is not.

OpenRouter’s approximately $50 million in annualized revenue means model routing is already a recognized market, not a lonely North Dakota gas flare. Conifer’s local-first sequence can compound over 18 months if every routed request improves knowledge of task complexity, latency, hardware, and model cost; without retained outcome data, Conifer remains a faster Rust engine beside OpenRouter, Portkey, Martian, and Not Diamond. “Local before cloud” is pleasantly strange, but the 100-person waitlist and 10 GitHub forks do not yet prove Conifer is magically weird or underpriced.

Conifer’s Rust inference engine touches hardware without becoming a frontier-hardware bet. For Conifer to flip this dimension into an invest signal, Juniper would need to become a deployed appliance or certified local-inference layer for hospitals, defense systems, or regulated financial institutions where data physically cannot leave the premises. Today Conifer offers a local-only switch but no third-party security audit, certification, or named regulated customer. That is software making a hardware-shaped shadow, not yet a **SpaceX**-class artifact.

OpenRouter’s 400-plus cloud models and Portkey’s support for more than 1,600 models make the standalone gateway layer a brutal place to remain small. By July 2029, I predict Conifer will have pivoted from least-cost model routing into an Apple-Silicon-focused local inference runtime for security-sensitive enterprises, reaching $1 million to $3 million in ARR rather than displacing OpenRouter. Conifer’s tangible engine can survive; Conifer’s undifferentiated routing control plane probably cannot.

Conifer’s strongest bull case is the **DeepMind** exception joined to the **HQ Trivia** exception: Muehlberger may be a genuine edge-inference polymath, while a downloadable product performing 194.6 tokens per second can create enough user love to outrun imperfect founder structure. Conifer could turn 268 stars into the default local runtime just as an apparently narrow artifact becomes infrastructure in disguise. The evidence that wins today is narrower: no routing-quality benchmark, no revenue, no named customer, no security audit, and no CEO against funded competitors with established distribution. A named CEO with documented tiebreaker authority plus three enterprises paying at least $100,000 annually for verified local-only deployments would flip my verdict. At 48, Conifer is a rebuttal, not a hard no—but I do not write the check yet.
