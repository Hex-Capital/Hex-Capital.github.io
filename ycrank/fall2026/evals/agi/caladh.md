# Caladh -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Neither AGI path is supported. Caladh describes Eclipse as a maritime drone mitigation product and Cloak as a subsea asset concealment product. The memo identifies no AI workload these products enable or scarce input they supply to the AI buildout. Caladh says existing detection can leave operators without a way to stop an incursion and that physical barriers are difficult to scale. The memo provides no evidence that AI changes the cost per successful protection outcome or makes protection viable for a previously excluded buyer or use. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The strongest commercial case is selling site-specific hardware to maritime operators, an inferred business model. The Coast Guard’s request for information establishes formal buyer interest in the category, with no Caladh-specific trial or commitment in the memo. Kita’s completed underwater acoustics research and undersea engineering experience support seed-stage execution credit. Caladh’s claimed ability to integrate with third-party detection could help adoption, but the memo documents no exclusive IP or deployed integrations that would protect pricing. It also gives no pricing, paid deployments, or field costs to establish the economics of the next installation. Those gaps drive low evidence confidence. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Caladh could fill a gap for operators that already have detection and need a separate mitigation product. The Coast Guard inquiry supports the category need, and Caladh says its products can integrate with existing detection. If a site pilot confirmed that workflow, it would strengthen the commercial case. The AGI gate would still require evidence of a scarce AI input or an AI-driven change in affordability. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
