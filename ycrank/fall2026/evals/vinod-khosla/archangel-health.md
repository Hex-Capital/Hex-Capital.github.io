# Archangel Health -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Archangel Health’s important possibility is making scarce medical expertise safely reproducible through software. That could matter enormously. But the dossier does not connect its EHR environments to a quantified reduction in clinical labor, treatment cost, or preventable harm. The $645.4 million healthcare training-dataset market measures a supplier category; it does not establish the consequence of success. I would buy a low-probability option on radically cheaper medical care. Archangel has yet to establish that this is the option it sells.

The product addresses a real failure: an agent can describe the correct treatment while failing to execute the required EHR actions. Stanford’s reported 46% single-attempt completion on harder PhysicianBench tasks makes that distinction consequential. Archangel packages interactive environments with physician grading, which gives developers something more useful than another collection of medical questions. However, Stanford also releases benchmarking infrastructure, while Centaur and iMerit supply overlapping expert evaluation. There is no Archangel-specific benchmark demonstrating superior failure detection, better downstream clinical execution, or a 5–10x technical advantage. The technical score receives the missing-evidence default; competition alone does not prove inferiority.

The strongest argument against passing is that frontier labs and medical AI companies reportedly already use Archangel’s infrastructure. If those users repeatedly expose difficult clinical failures, physician adjudication could produce reusable training assets that improve the next evaluation. That would fit my pattern of scarce scientific workflows becoming production platforms. It could also make the dataset-market estimate largely irrelevant. But the users are unnamed, and the proprietary learning loop remains inferred. My exceptions for companies without conventional validation require a documented instigator attacking an enormous value pool or compelling evidence of a transformative trajectory. The dossier establishes neither override sufficiently. It also establishes no advertising, exit-seeking, or founder-integrity red flag; my pass rests on the mechanism remaining unproven.

Bhatia and Patel have relevant starting material: robotics-data experience, Cedars-Sinai ML research, and clinical-agent exposure through SimCare. More informative than their titles is the change from surgery-center work under CMS TEAM in April 2026 to medical AI infrastructure by October. That is an observed change in direction and earns modest credit for tactical flexibility. Its cause is undisclosed, so I cannot call it demonstrated fast learning. Likewise, claimed board-certified contributors do not establish that the founders recruited exceptional people to own specific technical weaknesses.

The largest kill risk is clinical transfer: does improving performance inside Archangel’s environments improve execution on unfamiliar clinical workflows? The dossier supplies no decisive experiment, threshold, budget, or stop condition addressing that risk. Economics are similarly unresolved. Contributor compensation of $25–60+ per case is an input cost, not evidence of either attractive margins or permanent labor dependence. Pricing, adjudication effort, and reuse per case would determine whether the company compounds software value or repeatedly purchases expert hours. Missing revenue is normal at this stage; missing proof of the central mechanism is what limits my conviction.

I pass with my own money today. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: an independently audited, controlled study showing that training with Archangel’s assets produces at least five times fewer serious execution failures than training with the buyer’s existing evaluation stack on held-out workflows from two previously unseen hospital EHR configurations, at equal total training and physician-review cost. That would demonstrate both transfer and economic leverage. Until then, Archangel has identified an important bottleneck without establishing that its product can remove it.
