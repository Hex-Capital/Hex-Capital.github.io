# Streamline Systems -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** Streamline’s strongest case is that drone manufacturers need reliable positioning to keep existing aircraft flying autonomously when GPS is unavailable. If cheaper intelligence expands autonomous drone adoption, more aircraft could need that capability. Even with equal access to models, manufacturers would still need a navigation system that meets their requirements on the aircraft. Streamline reports an autonomous flight demonstration and compatibility with existing platforms. Independent flight-test results and customer qualification remain unverified, so the retrofit is a plausible solution at seed and its scarcity remains unproven. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Streamline reports more than $80,000 in contracted module revenue and deposits reserving more than $87,000 worth of modules. The deposit amounts, overlap between the figures, contract conditions, cash collection, and shipments are undisclosed. These are early commitment signals from manufacturers tied to one procurement program. The founders bring relevant aerospace, software, and hands-on aircraft experience, and the memo records a founding engineer hire. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Founders & Team)

The expansion unit is one module per aircraft equipped. A scale case depends on how many platforms Streamline can qualify, how many aircraft each customer equips, and what remains of the selling price after hardware, integration, and support costs. The memo gives a market estimate but leaves those inputs unmeasured. Path B lacks evidence that lower delivery labor makes a new transaction viable or that buyers would keep paying Streamline under model parity. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The procurement program may create a burst of demand for navigation hardware that established suppliers can also serve. ANELLO Photonics and Advanced Navigation already sell navigation hardware. If either can meet the same requirements on the same aircraft with comparable integration effort, Streamline’s retrofit focus may confer little pricing power. Qualification could create switching costs, but the memo establishes neither a customer qualification nor the cost of replacing a qualified module. That leaves the Path A control gate unresolved. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will a drone manufacturer convert a reservation into a firm paid order after independently qualifying Streamline against its best available alternative on an existing airframe? Buyer-verified results showing that Streamline meets the required performance where the alternative falls short, or that switching would require material requalification, would support Reach out if the new evidence lifts the score to at least 65 with medium or high confidence. Equivalent performance and easy switching would close Path A and move the action to Pass on the current evidence.
