# Torus -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Marcus Lima has led more than $100M of capital projects, while Torus remains a single-sided enterprise product with no user-added network. That pairing is Torus’s signature: unusually strong specific knowledge applied through unusually weak compounding architecture. Marcus knows where engineering work breaks; Rahul Thayil brings AI experience from Orion. But expertise trapped inside pilot-led software compounds by hiring, integrations, and approvals. Torus can become valuable without becoming asymmetric.

Replit is the high rung for specific knowledge: Amjad Masad spent years building the product he was uniquely equipped to build. Torus shares the important mechanism. Marcus has operated capital projects rather than merely studying their $4.86B software market, and Rahul previously built an AI threat-intelligence company that raised $3.5M. Torus diverges from Replit because neither founder has disclosed a public artifact, open-source community, or long-running technical body of work around engineering documents. The knowledge looks real, but the founder-as-product feedback loop is not yet visible.

Perplexity is the low rung on network compounding: every user can receive value without making the next user’s product structurally better. Torus has the same weakness without Perplexity’s exception. Torus uploads five or six documents into a private project environment; one EPC’s participation does not automatically improve another EPC’s experience. Trunk Tools, with $70M raised, can pursue the same customers independently. No marketplace, shared protocol, or contributor network converts adoption into automatic defense.

Torus requests enterprise documents for founder-led demonstrations, offers customer-controlled network deployments, and has SOC 2 certification still in progress. Over 18 months, that mechanism compounds services burden before it compounds code: security reviews, bespoke document taxonomies, accuracy validation, and liability routing recur account by account. Torus does have software leverage after deployment—parameter extraction and more than 50 consistency-check categories can run repeatedly at low marginal cost—but distribution still requires permission from every enterprise gatekeeper. Code is leverage. Enterprise procurement is friction wearing a software badge.

The $4.86B AI-in-construction market is forecast to grow 24.8% annually, giving Torus room beyond one narrow document check. Torus could move from inconsistency detection into procurement approvals, engineering-package generation, asset operations, or energy-infrastructure workflows. The market score would flip into the top tier if Torus proved that the same document graph transfers across construction, energy, and industrial owners without rebuilding the ontology for each vertical. Today, overlap with Trunk Tools and Augmenta suggests a large arena, but no proprietary platform-shaped expansion path.

Stack Overflow paired domain-native founders with a clean history of shipping for developers. Torus offers the domain-native half: Marcus’s capital-project experience and Rahul’s autonomous-systems and AI background make intelligence and energy legible. Torus does not yet establish the 10-year-partner half. No co-founder history is public, and Rahul announced Orion’s $3.5M financing in November 2025 before joining Torus by July 2026. That may have a clean explanation, but long-term games require evidence of long-term behavior. Integrity cannot be inferred from credentials.

Multiple Fortune 500 companies and major EPCs are reportedly deploying Torus, so my 36-month forecast is specific: by July 2029, Torus reaches $6M-$12M ARR as an enterprise engineering-document system, but remains a single-sided SaaS vendor rather than a compounding network. Torus’s founder-market knowledge should win expensive accounts; Torus’s pilot-led distribution, private deployments, and customer-specific graphs should prevent permissionless escape velocity. A good enterprise business is not automatically a 10,000x angel business.

Anchorage Digital is the strongest bull precedent: permission can become the moat when regulation and trust are scarce. Torus could similarly turn customer-controlled deployments, engineering provenance, and approval history into the trusted system of record for physical infrastructure. That case stings because a $500K rework order can justify meaningful contract value, and Fortune 500 deployment claims imply real urgency. Torus has not yet shown the evidence that made Anchorage exceptional: no completed security certification, disclosed dataset scale, retention, exclusivity, or structural barrier, while Trunk Tools already spans drawings, RFIs, revisions, and submittals. I would flip to Invest if more than half of new Torus deployments were demonstrably initiated by contractors, owners, or suppliers inviting one another into a shared cross-company project graph. Then each customer would begin acquiring and improving the next.
