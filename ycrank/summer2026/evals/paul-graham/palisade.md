# Palisade -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The impressive fact is not that Palisade calls its software an agent. It is that a solo founder has put it into production at Shorebreak and Vaulted Atelier and attributes more than $2.7 million in marketplace GMV to it since the Summer 2026 batch began. That is a working artifact producing a measured commercial result, not a diagram of what agents may eventually do. Jonathan Salama’s earlier work on Amazon’s Traffic & Marketing systems and shopping agents at Sage AI Labs makes the speed more believable. But the dossier omits the fact I most want: what broke during those deployments, what he personally did about it, and how quickly the workaround changed the outcome.

The product’s breadth is both the bull case and the danger. Palisade connects catalogs, booking engines, property-management systems, and CRMs; remembers shoppers across visits; contacts them by email or SMS; and can navigate marketplace interfaces. This is real schlep. It also means the product touches data security, checkout behavior, repricing, and code changes simultaneously. Human approval and handoff limit the damage from agent errors, but they may also hide how much of the apparent automation is still supervised service. I would rather see one ugly workflow working extraordinarily well than buyer, seller, and staff agents each working plausibly.

The narrow well appears to be marketplace operators losing high-intent visitors who browse without inquiring. Two named production customers and $2.7 million of GMV are far stronger than a waitlist. Yet GMV is not revenue, retention, or incremental lift. The decisive counterfactual is whether those purchases would have occurred without Palisade. There is no disclosed conversion experiment, customer payment, repeat-use period, or baseline comparison. So the traction is genuine evidence of use, but not yet proof that users pull the product back or that Palisade captures meaningful economics from the value it claims to create.

The strongest bull case is that this evidentiary gap reflects the company’s age, not its quality. A solo technical founder with relevant prior work has already deployed complicated software at two marketplaces and put millions of dollars through it. Persistent histories plus operational integrations could become a proprietary substrate before better-funded competitors can adapt their ecommerce products. The bear case stings more because Rep AI, Constructor, and Nosto already sell adjacent personalization systems, while general computer-use APIs make interface navigation progressively cheaper. If Palisade’s advantage is merely assembling these capabilities behind one script tag, the incumbents can reproduce the noun.

I cannot infer flexibility from Salama’s résumé or deployments. There is no rejected hypothesis, contrary customer observation, or materially revised route. Nor is there a documented founder-originated collision with the problem: Amazon and Sage provide credible domain exposure, but the claimed connection remains inferred. Most importantly, the operational gate fails. The dossier shows what Salama built, but not how he behaves when the system resists him. For an angel investing personal money, that distinction is large enough to keep Palisade at Neutral despite unusually promising early output.

I would change my mind on one verifiable condition: Palisade should document a dated production failure at either named customer, the founder-built workaround shipped in response, and subsequent repeat usage or measured incremental conversion from the same customer. That would establish resourcefulness, technical consequence, and user pull without asking one fact to do all three jobs.
