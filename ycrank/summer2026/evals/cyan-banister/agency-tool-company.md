# Agency Tool Company -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, field robots will receive software the way reliable cloud systems do now: continuously, selectively, and without a technician babysitting a flaky connection. The path there is legible. Robot fleets grow, payloads swell as hardware like Jetson Thor delivers 7.5 times Orin’s AI compute, and deployment failures become operational failures rather than developer annoyances. Agency Tool Company has found a small leak from that future: Burro, Tempo Works, and Gather AI are already testing one deployment layer across agriculture, construction, and logistics. The dossier does not fully map the intervening transitions or show how deployment becomes a durable control point, but it describes more than a fashionable robotics category.

The founders are the reason I take the leak seriously. Jack Morrison and Davis Foster built and deployed hundreds of autonomous mowers at Scythe from 2018 through its 2026 acquisition. Before that, they worked together at Occipital; Foster was an electrical engineer and Thiel Fellow, while Morrison carried the CEO role. ATC is the missing tool they wanted while operating physical robots, not an infrastructure thesis assembled after reading a market forecast. That is excellent first-believer proof: the operating wound preceded the company, produced years of artifacts, and kept the team together through an acquisition.

The broken rule is mundane and expensive. Docker pulls, SSH, and scripts assume identical machines with continuous connectivity; actual robots have device-specific artifacts and lose connections in fields, warehouses, and construction sites. Byte-level deltas, resumable downloads, fleet targeting, rollback, and deployment history return engineering time and release control to the operator. The company reports transfers as much as 20 times faster, but it has not quantified the present rent in engineer-hours, downtime, bandwidth, or failed missions. I understand the mechanism; I cannot yet price the pain.

The strongest bear case is that Agency Tool Company has built a valuable feature bundle, not a company. Balena, Mender, Formant, Foxglove, and Memfault already occupy adjacent layers and have each raised tens of millions of dollars. ATC uses standard OCI containers and arbitrary file bundles, advertises easy migration, and possesses no disclosed patent, proprietary dataset, or structural lock-in. Those choices are wonderful for customers and uncomfortable for investors. A well-funded platform could reproduce delta delivery and resumability, bundle them with observability, and erase ATC’s standalone budget before deployment history becomes a moat.

What prevents that objection from deciding the case is the shape of the beta. Three named partners in three different physical environments suggest a horizontal infrastructure dependency rather than one mower-company feature. This resembles the pre-revenue exception I made for **CosmicBrain AI**: both pairs earned credibility through years of robotics work and arrived with functioning systems plus industrial validation. The material difference is that ATC’s software bottleneck appears easier for incumbents to absorb, and the dossier provides no repeated, paid, or operationally costly user behavior. The 20-times claim is company-controlled; the waitlist is not usage.

I would invest, but below my highest-conviction range. Morrison is plainly identified as CEO and the founders have survived eight years of operating together, so I see no leadership shell game; I would still verify that equal ownership cannot create a deadlock. I also see no disclosed consent or surveillance violation, although deployment logs and device access require explicit custody boundaries. My 36-month expectation is $3 million to $7 million in ARR from production robot fleets, with deployment history and hardware integrations becoming the real product. One condition flips me to a pass: by August 2027, none of the three named beta partners has converted to a paid production deployment with documented repeat releases across an operating fleet.
