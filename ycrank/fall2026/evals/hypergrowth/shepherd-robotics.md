# Shepherd Robotics -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit for rack cable routing, comparable data center operators abroad could plausibly use Shepherd’s modular hardware, pretrained model, and local adaptation process for the same job. How much of a working deployment transfers remains uncertain. Founder introductions and direct inquiries provide a concrete acquisition channel. Site setup depends on technician demonstrations, and operation requires human supervision. In electrical assembly, installed policies could support repeated use on recurring work. Recurring support fees and paid expansion to more tasks or sites remain hypotheses. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

The memo records an October public demo and gives no usable evidence about paid deployments, revenue, or commercial growth. Under the memo’s inferred per-deployment model, incremental contribution would be the deployment fee plus any contracted support revenue, less hardware, sales, installation, task adaptation, and any ongoing supervision or support Shepherd provides. Reusing hardware designs and task policies could reduce integration labor. The memo leaves pricing, labor hours, and field utilization unknown. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Each installation could require fresh engineering and continuing skilled intervention, causing delivery costs to rise roughly in step with revenue. Shepherd reports 95% success on cable routing and 60% on cable insertion in bench tests. The memo also describes customer equipment available only on site. The structural gate remains open because the modular platform and local adaptation process offer a plausible way to bound that work. Production evidence is needed to establish how much work actually carries over. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *Defensibility*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Shepherd replicate a paid rack cable-routing deployment at a comparable site in another market using the same hardware and task policy with bounded adaptation? A second paid production deployment would answer this if it records what carried over, installation hours, operator interventions, and contribution at actual utilization. Move to Pass if the rollout establishes that unavoidable custom engineering and supervision make repeat deployments uneconomic. A successful rollout would warrant rescoring against the Reach out threshold.
