# Lantern AI -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Reach out  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**Path B. Repeatable sales with account expansion.** Enterprise recruiting teams have a recurring job: carry hiring managers’ criteria from sourcing through interviews. Lantern’s demo-led motion starts with one workflow on the buyer’s existing ATS. If customers can reuse the same integration and approved criteria across agents, Lantern can sell adjacent modules without repeating the initial setup. Demos already include checks on ATS connections and data flows. The memo leaves open how consistently those checks lead to a paid go-live and how much founder time that takes. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

Customer Hang Huang reported screening more than 1,000 candidates and hiring an engineer, a concrete signal that the screening workflow can deliver value. Lantern reports $1M in annual recurring enterprise contracts and one account growing from about $11K to $92K annually. That account supports the expansion mechanism. The reported 270% net dollar retention has no defined cohort or measurement window, and the expanded account’s usage is undisclosed. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

For geographic portability, Lantern reports that its earlier interviewer handled 100,000-plus interviews across ten Asia-Pacific markets. That supports portability for one module. Paid deployments of the broader platform across markets remain unverified. The contribution scenario assumes reusable ATS connectors and configurable hiring criteria. Each new buyer still adds sales, setup, and support effort. More screening and interviews add processing costs, and a new market may require changes to audit and candidate notice workflows. The memo gives no per-rollout or per-workload cost data. A conversation can test these assumptions against deployment records. (*Market & Traction*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

ATS incumbents can bundle screening and interviewing into the system recruiters already use. Lantern could face a fresh integration and compliance effort at each account while competing against that built-in option. Its analytics depend on the records each ATS exposes, and covered hiring uses in New York City require bias audits and candidate notices. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the memo establishes neither a structural limit on buyer demand nor proportional delivery costs across deployments. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)
