# Avout -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to a specialty lender is Avout’s strongest potential exit. A lender could pay for a repeatable source of hardware borrowers and underwriting data that helps it screen loans or assess collateral. Avout says it uses borrower operating data and helped arrange a $1.5 million term sheet for a data-center company. Closing and disbursement are undisclosed. The term sheet measures proposed borrower financing. The memo supplies no placement revenue, measured lender benefit, or acquisition pricing. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The next milestone is repeat funded placements with documented fees and evidence that lenders use Avout’s underwriting to make decisions. The founders’ experience in real-asset investing and regulated financial infrastructure supports attempting that milestone. Third-party lenders would supply the loan capital under the described model. Avout’s operating capital needs, time to reach repeat volume, and ability to transfer borrower data and lender agreements in a sale remain unverified. The memo supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (The Idea, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3%, with no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The following sale prices are illustrative assumptions. Both sale cases assume an all-cash closing within eight years, sale of the holder’s entire stake, full eligibility to participate, and pro rata distribution with no senior preference reducing the holder’s share.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $30 million enterprise value less $5 million of assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $25 million | $750,000 (1.5×) |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value less $10 million of assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million | $2.7 million (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested, with no distribution or eligible share sale within eight years | $0 |

In the strong case, $20 million of net debt and deal adjustments would leave $80 million of equity and reduce proceeds to $2.4 million before any additional waterfall impact. The memo gives no basis to underwrite either sale price. Watch reflects the specific gap in lender economics that could support a price clearing the hurdle.

### Countercase

Trinity Capital already provides equipment and asset-based financing, and GPU Capital already uses server appraisals and customer contracts to size loans. Those capabilities narrow the reason to acquire Avout, especially if its broader borrower reach yields few funded deals or its operating data adds little to existing underwriting. The single reported term sheet leaves both possibilities open. The competitive overlap weakens the sale case, while the memo leaves buyer pricing and transaction barriers unresolved. The Pass gate is therefore untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What would a specialty lender pay to own Avout’s origination and underwriting operation once it demonstrates repeat funded placements? Documented closings and fees, paired with a prospective acquirer’s quantified estimate of the value of owning the operation, would answer it. A defensible price range that could leave at least $83.3 million distributable within eight years would move the action to Reach out.
