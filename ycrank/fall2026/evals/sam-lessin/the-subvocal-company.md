# The Subvocal Company -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The Subvocal Company has raised approximately $1.3 million—or $1.5 million, according to its CTO—but I cannot establish what equity costs or what that money must prove. Post-money valuation, competing offers, and remaining capital needs are undisclosed. Afore and YC backing establishes access to financing; it establishes neither an auction nor a cheap entry. I give the round the missing-evidence default. An interesting interface does not tell me whether this is an interesting security to buy.

There is a potentially legitimate venture moment here: can deliberate internal articulation become useful text after someone removes the device, puts it back on, and starts another session? Answering that could materially change the business’s value. But the recruitment postings identify cross-user, cross-session, and placement robustness as unresolved, alongside the transition from laboratory equipment to portable hardware. The dossier supplies no funded experiment with a deadline and a measurable pass/fail result. I can describe the experiment I would finance; I cannot credit the founders with having committed this round to it.

The strongest argument against passing is that these founders have already made something difficult work. Company-reported decoding above 200 words per minute is worth investigating. Yadav previously co-founded GreenSat, which reportedly exceeded $2 million in annual revenue, and Kumar shipped a separate consumer app reaching more than 80,000 users. Those are operating receipts, not just university credentials. Their combination of sensing development and consumer software fits my preference for people who can join different systems. It earns the strongest score here, although neither prior business demonstrates delivery of reliable physiological-input hardware to consumers.

The technical claim still leaves the economic mechanism unresolved. An earlier prototype reportedly achieved 45% accuracy; the dossier does not establish the current accuracy accompanying the newer speed claim. Two hundred words per minute creates little value if correction consumes the time saved. AlterEgo pursues directly overlapping silent speech, and Subtle’s whisper-based product could satisfy some customers with a simpler mechanism. Hardware complexity itself does not disqualify this company under my operating-infrastructure pattern. It does make reliable daily use, rather than a showcase win, the important proof.

Nor is there an established proprietary dark pool yet. A dataset spanning users and placements could become valuable, but its scale and exclusivity are inferred. Planned pre-orders establish no purchases, and website traffic establishes no owned route to customers. Separately, pricing, manufacturing costs, margins, and burn are undisclosed, so the profitable-path dimension receives its default. GreenSat’s revenue belongs to GreenSat. I also cannot infer dependence on another financing, equal-founder paralysis, or an auction from this dossier; those red flags remain unestablished.

I pass at present. The exceptions that could override my missing price and capital-plan gates require documented conversion, cash economics, or an unusually protective financing structure. None is established here. The strongest bull case is that capable builders have found a genuine new input channel before its reliability becomes publicly legible. My verifiable flip condition is a completed 30-day trial showing that paying customers repeatedly choose the wearable over their existing input method, with positive contribution after hardware, inference, support, and returns. That would turn the central claim from decoding performance into an economic behavior I could underwrite.
