# Stacktrace -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Stacktrace has a reported $500,000 YC investment, but no disclosed post-money valuation, competing offers, or terms for my check. Those are different facts. The funding amount tells me somebody financed the company; it does not tell me whether I can buy uncertainty cheaply. I will neither infer an auction from the YC badge nor infer scarce capital from financing silence. With my own money, the deal remains ununderwritten.

The founders are the strongest evidence here. Kone’s Mesos contributions and software supply-chain security responsibility connect infrastructure operation with security enforcement. Benedict brings infrastructure, security, and AI-platform experience; Ganta brings AWS and Amazon Ads generative-AI experience. They have also shipped a product, with CLI version 0.17.0 published October 27, 2026. This fits the translation-layer pattern I like: people who understand the systems generating risky activity and the organizations responsible for controlling it. That earns substantial founder credit. It does not establish customer access that already produces purchases.

The strongest argument against passing is that Stacktrace could own the independent record of what agents actually did. Local transcript processing and component attribution offer a concrete starting point across agent environments. Deterministic detection also avoids making the entire business depend on another model becoming infallible. But the control boundary matters: the endpoint sidecar reads logs; blocking requires the inline Gateway, and dynamic steering is still coming soon. Noma already advertises overlapping enforcement capabilities. Stacktrace therefore needs to demonstrate why customers will insert its Gateway into their workflows. Its Apache-2.0 collection foundations can encourage adoption, but cannot by themselves establish exclusive access.

The disclosed design partners give that argument some substance, yet the dossier supplies no named deployment, purchase, retained usage measurement, or evidence that a proprietary relationship caused adoption. I leave distribution at its absent-evidence default. The free Team tier—100 agents and 200 session-hours monthly—creates room to try the product, while annual Enterprise and Gateway pricing creates a possible charging mechanism. Neither establishes cash economics. Without revenue, deployment costs, margins, or burn, I cannot determine whether customer payments can finance the business. These are limits on what I can underwrite today, rather than evidence that a three-person pre-seed company is failing.

What exact truth does my capital reveal? Stacktrace has not disclosed a funded experiment with a metric and deadline. Shipping more detections could improve the product while leaving willingness to pay unresolved. A useful proposed test would be whether a named design partner will pay for inline enforcement and renew after seeing measured detection quality and operating overhead. That is my suggested experiment, not a capital plan the founders have supplied. One verifiable event would reopen this pass: a named design partner prepaying a twelve-month Gateway contract whose cash covers the measured cost of deployment and support.

I pass at **32/100**. The technical team makes the bull case credible, and the acknowledged detection misses are not evidence of deceptive claims or an assumption that errors disappear. Nor does the dossier establish financing pressure or an equal-founder decision committee; Kone is identified as CEO. But my known exceptions require demonstrated conversion, cash economics, exceptional external demand, or an explicit financing structure that changes the underwriting. Stacktrace supplies none of those yet. Strong operators building useful security infrastructure can create a real business. I still need evidence that this particular check buys an attractive experiment and a claim on durable customer value.
