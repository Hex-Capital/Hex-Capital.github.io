# hiloop -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: autonomous research agents now generate experiments faster than teams can provision, reproduce, and compare them; model developers become the buyer, and the controlling variable is verified model improvement per compute dollar. If hiloop can repeatedly increase that ratio across customer workloads, experiment lineage becomes infrastructure. If it merely makes experimentation easier, funded platforms will absorb the feature.

The 4,188-trial campaign is meaningful technical proof. Fifty B200 GPUs ran for two days, results were confirmed through 25 paired runs with interleaved controls, and the resulting NanoGPT implementation was 12.54% faster than upstream. This shows that hiloop can operate a large, measurable search loop rather than merely demo an orchestration interface. It does not yet establish the market variable at a commercial threshold: there is no customer baseline, compute-cost comparison, or evidence that the 0.9016 validation result created enough economic value to support recurring infrastructure spend.

The timing is directionally right. Karpathy’s autoresearch repository reaching 93,600 GitHub stars indicates that agent-run experimentation is becoming legible to developers, while growing experiment volume makes reproducibility and observability more valuable. But the category is already crystallizing. Prime Intellect has raised more than $150 million and reported over 10,000 beta training jobs; Runloop and OpenPipe/CoreWeave occupy adjacent execution and post-training layers. The generic $2.19 billion MLOps estimate does not explain how much new spending hiloop creates or captures.

The strongest part of the company is architectural. Snapshottable compute, forkable runs, queryable traces, customer-cloud execution, and campaigns spanning data, prompts, tools, harnesses, and systems form an owned operational loop. Support for multiple models and user-provided harnesses also avoids dependence on one frozen model: stronger agents should generate more experiments for hiloop to manage. I assign no moat credit yet to inferred proprietary experiment data, and incomplete request-time secret delivery is material for enterprise deployment, but this is more durable than a thin model wrapper.

The opposing case is strong: at pre-seed, running thousands of controlled trials on scarce hardware may be more informative than small, noisy revenue, and the founders worked directly on ML, post-training, and platform systems at Reducto and DynamoAI. That could justify a research-stage exception. I do not make it here because Erdos Miller is the only displayed customer signal, with no published engagement, revenue, repeat usage, or capacity bottleneck. The founders have relevant experience, but the dossier does not document prior startup learning or the costly commitment that distinguishes necessity from another technically sophisticated infrastructure project.

I would monitor rather than invest today. My 36-month base case is that hiloop narrows into a managed post-training service or is acquired by CoreWeave before reaching $5 million ARR as an independent horizontal platform. The verifiable flip condition is three unaffiliated customers each paying at least $15,000 MRR, running more than 10,000 monthly experiments, and expanding usage for two consecutive quarters; that would demonstrate that lineage and forkable execution—not founder-run research campaigns—control the market.
