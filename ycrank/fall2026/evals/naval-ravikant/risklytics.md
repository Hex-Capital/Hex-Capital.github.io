# Risklytics -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Risklytics’s strongest evidence is work the founders have actually done. Risio obtained a producer license; the team shipped an insurance application and compiled 736 AI-exclusion filings across 49 states and 33 insurers. Their August 2026 account describes underwriters disagreeing over whether robot motor commands constitute generative-AI output. That is specific knowledge acquired inside the problem. Harvard adds little to my decision; learning where an insurance contract breaks adds considerably more.

The question is whether that knowledge compounds into an asset the company owns. Copy Risklytics’s application software and exclusion tracker: what can the original still do that the copy cannot? The dossier establishes operational competence, but no exclusive carrier access, proprietary loss model, or demonstrated switching costs. Licensed brokers still approach insurers and review wording before binding. Almost 50 application users do not establish that customer fifty improves coverage for customer fifty-one. I give the founders credit for building the machinery without assuming the machinery already produces a network effect.

The strongest argument for investing is that Risklytics may be entering through the transaction that permits physical AI to reach customers. Three businesses reportedly obtained coverage after earlier declines: robotics rental, bridge-collision technology, and teleoperation. Optional ISO AI-liability exclusions introduced in 2026 could make this expertise increasingly valuable. Carrier-paid commissions of 10–20% connect payment directly to successful placement. These are meaningful early signals. But premiums, collected commissions, renewal behavior, and servicing costs remain undisclosed. Deloitte’s projected $4.8 billion AI-insurance premium market is neither Risklytics’s addressable brokerage revenue nor evidence of a demonstrated expansion path.

My preference for moving fragmented offline supply into accessible markets supports the bull case. Risklytics could make opaque insurance capacity searchable and usable. The limiting mechanism is permission: carriers control capacity, while wholesalers and MGAs mediate access. Nothing yet shows customer contributions improving the system without comparable company effort. My exceptions for businesses without visible user loops require unusually strong alternative evidence, such as exceptional product development and capital efficiency; that evidence is not established here. There is also no documented integrity violation, artificial financing deadline, or dependence on investor reassurance. The wildfire-modeling pivot alone does not establish instability. The concern is structural ownership, not founder character.

I would not write a personal check today. Risklytics could become a useful, profitable specialist brokerage, but the dossier has not shown how its learning becomes durable economic power. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: a signed carrier agreement granting exclusive delegated underwriting authority for a defined physical-AI segment, allowing the company to bind qualifying policies directly. That would turn expertise into a scarce contractual asset and change the answer to the copying question.
