# Biomes -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with an initial investment firm, Biomes’ best growth path is to deploy an agent at one operating company and reuse the workflow and its failure tests across the sponsor’s portfolio. The buyer and the job of automating routine work plausibly recur across markets. Biomes describes adapting deployments for new acquisitions, but the memo gives no example showing how much of a deployment carries over across different systems and data formats. That supports limited portability credit. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

Biomes solicits inquiries from investment firms and describes embedding engineers with operating teams to discover workflows, build integrations, and get agents into use. One sponsor could introduce several operating companies, but the described path to first value remains hands-on. Once installed, an agent that handles recurring tasks in live systems and is reviewed with the customer could support continued use. Rollouts to additional companies could expand the sponsor account if the sponsor pays on a recurring basis for each deployment. That is a scenario assumption. The memo establishes neither recurring billing nor paid account expansion. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

The memo records a forward-deployed engineer opening at $400,000 per year plus equity. It gives no paid deployment, realized revenue, or commercial growth figures for the current offering, so initial traction takes the absent-evidence default. For a hypothetical paid rollout, incremental contribution would be the deployment fee and any ongoing charges less attributable sales, engineering, infrastructure, and support costs. Reusing integrations and failure tests could reduce engineering time, but the advertised salary establishes neither hours per deployment nor actual cost. Pricing and delivery effort are too uncertain to judge contribution, so cost economics also takes its default. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *The Idea*)

### Countercase

Biomes could win repeat introductions from a sponsor and still have to rebuild each deployment. The memo identifies differences in systems and data formats across acquisitions, and Biomes’ own description puts embedded engineers at the center of delivery. If discovery, integration, and reliability work recur at roughly the same effort for each site, growth could require adding engineers in step with deployments. The structural downside gate remains open because the memo shows neither a market-specific rebuild requirement nor enough delivery or pricing evidence to establish that per-site work makes faster growth economically infeasible. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Biomes put the same workflow into paid production at a second company under the same sponsor, on a different technology stack, with materially fewer engineering hours and positive incremental contribution? Compare the two deployments’ reused components, engineering hours, fees, and attributable operating costs. A successful repeat would strengthen the case for Watch and warrant rescoring against the Reach out threshold. Evidence that each site requires a rebuild whose cost makes faster growth uneconomic would change the action to Pass.
