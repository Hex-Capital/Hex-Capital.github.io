# Luca IQ -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Angelo Policicchio is a CPA who lived the tax-season problem; Luca IQ is therefore strongest on specific knowledge and weakest on compounding network value. The 1,100 hours saved and 57% reduction in administrative preparation time show that Luca IQ understands the work. But understanding work is not the same as escaping work. Luca IQ currently looks like a deeply informed vertical software business whose customers gain independently, not a network where each new customer makes every prior customer richer.

**Stack Overflow** is the high rung for specific knowledge: developers built the product they themselves needed, then encoded that knowledge into a platform. Angelo brings the analogous lived experience at Luca IQ, while Akash Sadashivapeth adds engineering training and prior data-science leadership. Luca IQ diverges from Stack Overflow because its public technical artifact is thin—Akash’s GitHub has 15 repositories and three followers—and the deterministic 1040 engine remains in pilot. The knowledge is real; the difficult code has not yet been publicly proven. That places Luca IQ below Stack Overflow but well above a generic MBA-discovered market.

**Anchorage Digital’s** low-rung network mechanism is institution-by-institution value: the next custody customer does not automatically improve custody for the previous one. Luca IQ shows the same structural weakness. Fixed per-return pricing, firm-specific workflows, and founder-led sales make each accounting firm an independent account. Historical reviewer corrections could create a proprietary data advantage, but that is a learning loop inside Luca IQ, not user-added network value among Luca IQ customers. A better model gets smarter; a network gets more valuable because its users exist. Those are not interchangeable.

Luca IQ’s 101 LinkedIn followers, founder-led demos, outbound sales internship, and CPA referrals describe labor-leveraged distribution wrapped around a code-leveraged product. Over 18 months, the REST API, webhooks, and bulk endpoints could let Luca IQ replicate computation at near-zero marginal cost, but integrations, migrations, human review, and IRS authorization keep acquisition permissioned. Code is leverage only after distribution stops scaling with meetings. Luca IQ has built software leverage in production; Luca IQ has not yet built permissionless leverage in distribution.

SafeSend’s $600 million acquisition proves that tax workflow is valuable, but it does not prove that Luca IQ has enough pivot-room. The score flips upward if Luca IQ’s deterministic engine becomes horizontal tax infrastructure for fintech platforms, payroll providers, wealth managers, and consumer applications—the April path—rather than remaining workflow software for CPA firms. Without that expansion, Luca IQ is bounded by seasonal selling, incumbent integrations, and a narrow professional buyer. The first idea rarely works; a company needs adjacent terrain before it needs refinancing.

**Notion** is the useful founder parallel: founder-product-market fit survived multiple rebuilds because Ivan Zhao had both obsession and time. Angelo’s CPA experience and the founders’ interviews with more than 300 firm owners give Luca IQ intelligence and energy signals, while completing a first customer tax season demonstrates execution beyond an HBS Demo Day pitch. Luca IQ provides no evidence about prior partnerships, treatment of colleagues, or completed ventures, so integrity receives the absent-evidence default rather than a halo from credentials. Play long-term games with long-term people; Harvard logos do not answer the ten-year question.

Hundreds of tax-engine pilot applications support a falsifiable forecast: by July 2029, Luca IQ will reach roughly $3 million to $7 million in ARR as review-assisted workflow and calculation software for CPA firms, with most new business still coming through sales and referrals rather than a self-propagating network. Luca IQ can become a good vertical software business because the founders know the workflow and the customer benefit is measurable. The same low-network architecture makes a 10,000x outcome unlikely unless the API becomes the default tax-computation layer outside CPA firms.

SafeSend’s $600 million exit is the bull mechanism, and **Anchorage Digital** is the known-exception precedent: regulatory permission can become a moat when incumbents cannot reproduce the authorization, traceability, and deterministic infrastructure. Luca IQ could combine hundreds of pilot applicants, 1,100 hours saved, IRS-authorized e-filing, and proprietary reviewer corrections into the tax operating system that Thomson Reuters failed to build internally. The evidence that wins against that case today is that Luca IQ’s core engine is still “coming soon,” every return still requires human review, and distribution already includes outbound hiring. I would flip to Invest if, by the next tax season, Luca IQ processes production returns for at least 25 firms and more than 30% of new volume arrives through third-party API integrations without founder-led selling.
