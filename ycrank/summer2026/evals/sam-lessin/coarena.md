# CoArena -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Price first: the proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and status of this as the first scarce check are all missing. That earns no YC halo credit. The round’s purpose is equally undefined: I can infer that capital would expand task collection and sell more evaluations, but CoArena has not named a dated experiment, pass/fail metric, or truth the financing uniquely reveals. I am therefore being asked to underwrite a promising product without being shown the deal or the venture moment.

The real signal is $60,000 of current-product revenue within two weeks. The free arena turns user-submitted work into paired trajectories and blind preference labels, then licenses data and evaluation access under consumption-priced contracts. That is not Product Hunt theater; it is an owned workflow causing a purchase. Users doubling weekly, 14 ranked agents, and 2,550 listed hackathon participants provide supply-side momentum, although the causal evidence is the contract revenue—not followers, points, or participation.

This could become the translation layer between agent builders and the messy computer workflows fixed benchmarks miss. The founders have shipped on the builder side: Coasty reportedly reached thousands of active users and paying customers across four continents, while its agent scored 82.81% on OSWorld. What is not established is equivalent operating depth inside an AI lab’s evaluation procurement or data pipeline. They understand how an agent fails; the dossier does not yet prove they understand how a lab repeatedly buys the resulting evidence.

My 36-month base case is that CoArena pivots from a public benchmark into private enterprise evaluation and trajectory-data infrastructure, reaching $2 million–$5 million ARR rather than displacing LMArena as the public arena. The bear case stings: the dataset page shows neither task-category data nor collected annotation fields, raw prompts and screenshots pass to model providers without SOC 2 or an external audit, and Coasty’s ownership of both the arena and a computer-use agent creates a neutrality problem. If labs distrust the provenance, privacy, or operator, the supposed proprietary corpus never becomes a dark pool; it remains an under-annotated lead-generation demo while LMArena, Patronus, and Braintrust absorb the serious budgets.

The $60,000 also does not yet demonstrate a one-and-done business. There is no gross margin, burn, runway, renewal, or controllable cash metric beyond the launch revenue. Consumption pricing could support attractive economics, but computer execution, human voting, security controls, and data cleaning all cost money. Delete the Series A and I cannot determine whether CoArena compounds from customer cash or needs another financing to manufacture dataset scale.

I am neutral and would not write the check today. The known-exception logic does not clear the missing deal gate: two weeks of revenue is unusually good pre-seed evidence, but it is not yet a named buyer, renewal, or independently credible corpus. One verifiable flip condition would change my decision: a named AI lab renewing and expanding its CoArena contract to at least $250,000 annualized after six months of production use. That would show the workflow creates durable value rather than launch revenue—and make the financing terms worth underwriting.
