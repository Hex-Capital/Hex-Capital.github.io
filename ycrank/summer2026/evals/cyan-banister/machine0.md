# machine0 -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Barnaby Malet is a solo founder with cloud-resource-allocation research behind him, but machine0 is strongest where authority is unmistakable and weakest where technical difficulty becomes a compounding physical moat. machine0 has one person who can turn the wheel without a committee; machine0 does not yet own the road beneath it, because the KVM/QEMU product runs on DigitalOcean and competes with E2B, Daytona, Modal, and RunPod. The signature is a credible infrastructure founder driving a rented machine. I am more interested in Barnaby than in today’s SKU.

Anduril is the high rung for unambiguous command: one founder’s conviction survives because nobody has to negotiate the mission at the moment of decision. machine0 shares that clean operating shape—Barnaby Malet is explicitly Founder/CEO, and the team size is one—but machine0 lacks evidence of founder vesting, future co-founder authority, or an exit-conflict protocol. A solo founder eliminates the 50/50 grenade; it does not document what happens after the first senior hire receives meaningful equity. machine0 therefore sits just below the high rung rather than inside it.

The consensus YC AI-agent darling fails when ordinary software is painted with fashionable vocabulary and called a moat. That low-rung mechanism appears inside machine0: DigitalOcean supplies the infrastructure, E2B and Daytona have each raised roughly $30 million, Modal has approximately $50 million in annualized revenue, and machine0’s public image repositories have only 13 and one GitHub stars. KVM machines are real machinery, but machine0 is not **SpaceX**-style capital intensity; a customer can substitute another cloud without waiting for a factory, license, or physical network to exist. “Cloud computers for AI agents” is useful, yet today it is more wrapper than frontier.

Barnaby Malet compounds founder-market fit over the next 18 months through operational scar tissue: published cloud research, payment-product work at Hike, prior CTO/CPO responsibility at Upflow through a $15 million Series A, and the Director MCP gateway that produced machine0’s underlying VM tooling. machine0 did not emerge from a TAM slide; machine0 emerged from Barnaby watching late-2025 agents outgrow ephemeral sessions and then rebuilding around that observation. The missing piece is autobiography rather than competence—I see someone who worked on the machinery, not someone trapped inside the exact customer problem for years. The **DeepMind** exception matters here: cross-domain technical compounding can override my outsider bias, and Barnaby’s researcher-builder-operator path earns that override without receiving the full lived-origin score.

E2B, Daytona, Modal, and RunPod make machine0 adjacent to consensus rather than magically weird. To flip this dimension into my top tier, machine0 would need evidence that sophisticated buyers or investors rejected persistent named VMs as unfashionable while users repeatedly kept week-long agent machines alive anyway. The 96-point Show HN launch and 37 comments show curiosity, not rejection-driven mispricing; the 299 LinkedIn reactions show reach, not a round nobody wants. machine0 earns some weirdness from suspendable NixOS environments and static agent endpoints, but machine0 is entering a party with several well-funded hosts already pouring drinks.

Niantic made software physically legible: I could walk outside, play Ingress, and feel location infrastructure rearrange human behavior. machine0 offers a weaker but genuine version of that test—a developer can install the CLI, provision an Ubuntu or NixOS VM, SSH into a static IP, suspend it, and resume the same environment within 48 hours. machine0 is observable through use, but only to a technical operator; I cannot ride it like Uber or notice it from a café like Flock Safety. The $5 minimum top-up helps make the artifact testable, while the absence of disclosed repeat usage keeps machine0 below the tactile-product rung that has overridden other weaknesses for me before.

machine0’s $0.013-per-hour CPU pricing points toward a specific July 2029 outcome: machine0 will pivot from undifferentiated persistent VMs into managed security and governance for long-running enterprise agents—the territory Barnaby previously explored with Director—and reach roughly $2 million to $5 million ARR. Generic compute margins will force machine0 upward into policy, credentials, snapshots, auditability, and workload control, because DigitalOcean owns the underlying capacity while machine0 can own how agents inhabit it. I would write a small personal check for that founder-led pivot option, not because the present VM reseller is magically weird enough on its own.

Thirteen GitHub stars make the bear case sting: machine0 may be a DigitalOcean tariff wrapped in MCP language, launched into a consensus category where better-capitalized competitors can copy persistent instances and static IPs in a quarter. machine0 also fails my unstated sourcing pattern—there is no environmental signal, persistent cold pursuit, or founder behavior observed outside the pitch—and it triggers my red flag that a polymath could rebuild a software-only abstraction quickly. The evidence that narrowly wins is Barnaby’s prior Upflow operating history, cloud-allocation research, working pay-as-you-go product, and willingness to kill Director’s positioning rather than protect sunk work. If machine0 cannot show at least 10 paying customers repeatedly resuming the same VM across four consecutive weeks by January 2027, I flip from Invest to Pass.
