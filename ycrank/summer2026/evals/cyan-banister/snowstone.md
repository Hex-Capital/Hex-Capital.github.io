# Snowstone -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years forward, I can imagine a portfolio company whose operating problems become visible before anyone commissions a consulting engagement. A delayed invoice connects to a purchasing decision, its cash consequence, and an intervention someone can actually execute. Snowstone’s access to email, ERP, and CRM records points toward that world. But the transition from a source-linked diagnosis to a dependable operating memory is my imagined future. The dossier establishes a diagnostic product; it does not establish the intervening steps. I will not give Snowstone credit for a destination I supplied.

Why does the consultant need to interview everyone? Because the company’s records do not explain themselves. Snowstone attacks that information bottleneck by reconstructing workflows and tracing problems upstream. That is useful, and the emphasis on EBITDA gives the buyer a reason to care. But there is no quantified consulting fee, diagnostic delay, or institutional restriction here. The immediate beneficiary is the PE operating team. Whether employees gain time and control, or simply become more legible to an owner pursuing margin expansion, depends on implementation. I see a concrete workflow repair, with limited evidence of the agency returned that particularly attracts my money.

The founders give me more to work with than their school names. Waldorf shipped quantum visualization tools at QuEra and maintains a public quantum-animation repository. Moreland and Baur built projects and competed in hackathons together; Baur’s Winterberg experience included diligence and MedTech LBO modeling. Those are complementary building and commercial signals. They are not yet a record of independently pursuing this particular operating problem before the company existed. The July Compendium and August Cerenovus launches show experimentation, but do not establish either durable obsession or opportunism. I would neither reward nor punish the rebranding itself.

The strongest argument against passing is that customers may already be letting this team inside the machinery. Snowstone reports **$2B+ in transaction value reviewed** and **$50M+ in opportunities identified**. A functioning diagnostic handling real business records could be a much more consequential artifact than its modest public profile suggests. Yet those figures establish neither repeat customer behavior nor realized savings, and the private benchmark puts dollar estimates within 20% of ground truth in only **66% of cases**. A source citation lets someone inspect a recommendation; it does not make the estimate correct. My willingness to back pre-revenue technical companies applies here in principle, but the dossier lacks the unusually strong domain history and independently supported prototype commitments that would justify that exception. Undisclosed revenue is not the reason I pass.

Snowstone fits my recurring interest in unglamorous workflow repair. The more exciting possibility is concealed infrastructure: reviewed entity matches and version histories becoming a reusable map beneath many operational tools. Today, the evidence supports persistence within reviews, rather than control of a scarce dependency that downstream products require. Celonis and Skan already occupy adjacent territory. Privacy also matters precisely because the product reads communications: isolated environments and read-only permissions are useful controls, but neither establishes employee consent or limits how owners use the findings. That is an unresolved gate, not evidence of a severe violation. Waldorf is identified as CEO, and nothing supplied establishes the leadership shell game or equal-control problem I reject.

I pass with my own money today. The team has credible complementary skills, but Snowstone’s strongest evidence concerns its ability to build and diagnose; its weakest concerns the path from this engagement to a different operating system for companies. One verifiable deployment would reopen the decision: a referenceable PE customer using Snowstone’s persistent workflow map to power two distinct operational automations across three portfolio companies, with documented permission boundaries. That would show the diagnostic becoming an enabling asset, rather than leaving that possibility to my daydreaming.
