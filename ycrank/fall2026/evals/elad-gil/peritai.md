# Perit.AI -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Voice models create a buyer for expert speech data whenever their remaining errors prevent deployment in valuable transactions. Perit.AI’s controlling variable is the cost of eliminating those errors relative to alternative suppliers. Sector practitioners could catch transaction details and interruptions that general raters miss, making their work disproportionately valuable. The dossier establishes that mechanism, but provides no comparative evaluation showing how much improvement Perit buys per dollar. I see a credible market hypothesis with unusually strong early demand; I cannot yet establish the economics that make it a large company.

The strongest argument for investing is that customers may already have answered that question through procurement. In its October 2026 announcement, Perit reports six major AI labs and “multiple millions in ARR.” Its website separately reports 4,000-plus audio hours delivered across nine locales. Those are substantial pre-seed signals, even after discounting company-reported figures. I give them meaningful credit. However, unnamed customers and an unspecified ARR methodology leave open whether these are recurring production budgets or annualized project deliveries. The public dataset’s 350 monthly downloads demonstrate researcher exposure, but add little evidence about commercial retention.

The economic distinction matters because Perit’s [pricing](https://perit.ai/pricing) separately itemizes contributor compensation and varies with annotation depth, equipment, exclusivity, and turnaround. That structure can support a valuable business, but it does not establish improving margins as volume grows. Fifty audio hours delivered per working day measures production throughput; it does not reveal automation leverage. Meta’s June 2025 investment in Scale supplies a plausible procurement opening for an independent vendor. The dossier labels the resulting opportunity as inferred, and provides no evidence that customers switched because of it. I would not award an economic discontinuity premium on that basis.

Foundry gives Perit more substance than a recruiting marketplace alone. Collection, transcription, alignment, grading, and calibration gates form an operational workflow, while live caller simulations could connect customer failures to subsequent evaluation. Better models could increase demand for harder examples and more realistic tests. They could also reduce the value of routine annotation. Perit has documented workflow machinery, but customer-specific feedback loops, persistent evaluation state, and switching costs remain unproven. Its 650-person annotation bench is an execution asset; without exclusive access or demonstrated calibration advantages, Mercor and Turing have a credible route to reproduce it. This is precisely where I would challenge a claimed data moat.

The founders improve my assessment independently of the revenue story. Kshitij previously co-founded ZeoAuto, reported as profitable at $2 million ARR, and Poorva co-founded SalaryBook. That is evidence of prior startup experience, although the dossier does not establish particular lessons learned or costly personal commitment. Their employer credentials alone earn no motivation premium. Perit fits my broader pattern of backing infrastructure around an AI adoption wave, but this category already contains well-capitalized suppliers, so participation in the wave needs a sharper winner case. The frontier-research exception does not apply: the dossier presents an operating data supplier without evidence of scarce foundational research. Nor does it demonstrate the economic transformation that would override concerns about labor-intensive delivery.

I would monitor Perit.AI and hold my personal check today. Its demand evidence is substantially stronger than its evidence of durable value capture. That is a specific underwriting gap, rather than a concern about ordinary pre-seed incompleteness. My flip condition is one customer-verified renewal showing that Perit’s expert workflow reduces cost per successfully resolved deployment failure versus the buyer’s incumbent supplier while generating a positive contribution margin after contributor and review costs. That result would connect the impressive commercial claims to the variable that determines whether this business compounds.
