# Raven -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Voice agents can now execute phone actions, and Raven wants employees to buy a device that coordinates their work across applications. The controlling variable is the **incremental work completed because Raven owns the phone**. If the same agent can deliver the same result on an employee’s existing device, Raven inherits hardware costs without gaining a corresponding advantage. The dossier does not establish a completion-rate or time-savings threshold that makes adopting another phone worthwhile.

That distinction determines the relevant market. Gartner’s $393.3 billion GenAI-smartphone spending forecast includes purchases Raven’s workflow proposition may never influence. Brain already has SoftBank retail distribution, Deutsche Telekom sells assistant-equipped phones, and Rabbit’s September 2026 release executes across existing devices. These are competing routes to the customer. Raven has a device-request form, but no disclosed pricing or unit economics showing how its route produces better margins or cheaper adoption. The economic step change remains unproven.

The technical evidence is more encouraging. The organizer-confirmed prototype performs calendar actions, searches flights, and generates interfaces. That earns credit for execution beyond conversational output. Better models could improve those actions. However, the Gmail-to-Drive-to-Slack sequence is a website illustration; it does not establish a deployed workflow with persistent permissions, recovery, and accumulated operational state. Raven has demonstrated part of the execution layer without yet demonstrating that it owns the recurring work around it. A stronger model could improve Raven while also making competing implementations easier.

The strongest objection to passing is Isa Usmanov’s prior operating experience. His YC biography reports a Reinio sale, $3.4 million-plus GMV and 32,000-plus users at droov, and $1.1 million-plus ARR at an earlier SaaS business. Those figures belong to previous companies, but they provide substantive evidence of startup learning. Combined with a working prototype, they support a credible bull case: the phone could become a controlled deployment environment through which Raven acquires teams and later distributes additional workflows. Founder learning is the strongest dimension here. It does not establish that customers need the hardware.

Initial orders reportedly shipping provide a small demand signal, discounted because quantity and payment status are undisclosed. The 160,000 demo views and hundreds of community signups establish attention rather than repeat usage. I do invest across multiple layers of a technology discontinuity, but each layer still needs a value-capture mechanism. My exceptions for scarce frontier-model talent or physical products with transformed unit economics are not supported here. The relevant red flag is the possibility of carrying device economics while customers value interchangeable agent software; the dossier leaves that possibility unresolved.

I would pass on Raven today. My base case is that by October 2029 it has shifted its primary offering toward workplace-agent software on existing Android devices. The founder’s demonstrated ability to build businesses makes that adaptation plausible; the current evidence does not make a separate phone compelling. One result would reopen the decision: a paid, 90-day team deployment comparing Raven with the same agent on existing phones, demonstrating that device ownership produces materially greater completed work at positive contribution margin after hardware, inference, and support. That would test the proposition on which this investment turns.
