# Boxes.dev -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Boxes.dev has solved an irritation its founders actually encountered. Running coding agents in parallel meant competing processes, shared state, and keeping laptops awake. But the dossier leaves out the episode I most want to see: what blocked Nick Bushak and Drew Regitsky, what they tried, and how quickly they got past it. Their history building Gem together makes the team credible. It does not establish how they behave under resistance. I would keep that distinction rather than turn their résumés into evidence of resourcefulness.

The product deserves substantial credit. Prepared environments, snapshots, isolated Linux devboxes, and shipped mobile applications constitute a concrete system. Supporting native Claude Code and Codex also gives users a straightforward reason to try it: they can keep their agents and move the execution elsewhere. This is considerably beyond an architecture diagram. It earns credit for shipping, though the dossier supplies no reproducible reliability, setup-time, or isolation measurements that would establish exceptional technical execution.

The founders’ own use gives Boxes.dev a plausible starting point. Environment setup and credential handling contain precisely the tedious work that can conceal a useful business. The documentation’s warning that devbox code can read available GitHub credentials makes that burden concrete. But the origin account is retrospective, and encountering this problem personally does not establish that other developers need this particular solution urgently. Product Hunt’s 113 points and the Android application’s 10+ downloads show exposure. They do not show developers returning every morning. Demand remains untested in the available evidence.

The strongest case for investing is that two experienced collaborators have already built something they themselves wanted, and can now improve it through close contact with similar developers. That is a respectable pre-seed bet. My recurring preference for useful developer primitives supports it only partly: Boxes.dev hosts existing agents, but the dossier does not show developers composing unexpected workflows on top of it or a numerical proof spike. Meanwhile, Conductor already overlaps in cloud execution, native agents, and mobile access. At $19 per month for Starter, Boxes.dev needs to become a habit; feature availability alone gives me little reason to expect that outcome. The absence of realized revenue is acceptable at this stage. The missing evidence is why users would keep choosing it.

I would pass on writing a check today. There is no documented evidence-driven route change, so I assign the default rather than infer either flexibility or stubbornness. Nor does the dossier establish premature delegation, reliance on a distribution partner, or an operating plan that ends at launch. My exception cases require unusually convincing product evidence, real-world pull, or validated technical results sufficient to overcome the missing founder evidence; this record does not yet supply that combination. One verifiable result would change my decision: a dated case in which the founders fix a specific adoption blocker within two weeks and five unrelated developer teams subsequently use Boxes.dev weekly for eight weeks. That would connect founder action to a product people pull back.
