# Erinys -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I begin with three blanks: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and the experiment this round purchases. YC backing does not establish that Erinys is a cheap, scarce first check, and “building the first network of AI-native law firms” is a destination rather than a venture moment. The company has not named a dated test—one launched firm, a defined case cohort, or a regulatory structure—with a pass/fail result that dramatically changes value.

The interesting mechanism is not the AI. It is the proposed bridge between lawyers who want their own practices and the operational machinery—intake, staffing, billing, collections, compliance, and working capital—that makes a firm viable. If Erinys operates that machinery across firms, its internal deployments could produce proprietary workflow and matter data that Eve and Supio cannot obtain by selling software seats. But this is currently a diagram of a dark pool, not a dark pool: no member firm, case volume, retained workflow, referral conversion, or purchase has been documented.

The strongest bull case is that three technical founders are attempting the harder full-stack structure while heavily funded competitors remain software vendors. Zyuzin and Guo did ship an OSINT prototype querying 22 government databases in April 2026, so the team can build across messy public-data systems. Plaintiff-side legal work is also large enough to reward an operating model that improves case selection and execution. The problem is that the prototype addressed False Claims Act investigations, while the August positioning spans launching and converting plaintiff firms. That movement may be intelligent discovery, but it does not yet prove legal operating access.

This is where the translation-layer thesis breaks. The team has security research, OSINT, software-defined radio, finance, and real-estate experience, but no documented bar admission, legal practice, plaintiff-firm operation, or shipped product on the lawyer side. Erinys is proposing to manage professional independence, client confidentiality, fee structures, and working capital under rules that can prohibit nonlawyer ownership and fee sharing. Three equal-presented co-founders also leave unanswered who makes the final product, financing, and regulatory decisions when those constraints collide.

Deleting the next round makes the economics harder still. Erinys may finance cases and provide trained staff before collections arrive, yet there is no revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, case-payback period, or evidence that affiliated-firm economics can legally accrue to the company. The AI option does not override that absence: competitors already serve live plaintiff workflows, Eve reports more than 1,000 firms and 200,000 cases, and incumbents can adopt similar software without surrendering their operations. None of the exception evidence—measured conversion, retention, cash economics, or a rejected acquisition—exists here.

My base case is that within 36 months Erinys has pivoted from a law-firm network into investigation and case-intake software for existing plaintiff firms, with less than $3 million ARR, because the founders have shipped on the technical side but have not demonstrated the legal access or economics required to own firms. I would flip if Erinys documents one legally compliant operated firm completing at least 100 matters, with positive contribution margin and at least 50% of new matters coming through an Erinys-owned referral or data channel. Until then, I am buying several simultaneous experiments without knowing the price of any one of them.
