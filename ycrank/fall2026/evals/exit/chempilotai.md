# ChemPilot.ai -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition by a defense or aerospace prime** is the strongest path. The memo records a Fresh Defense-led pre-seed announcement with no disclosed amount. The founders report that robots are running experiments alongside scientists in the first lab, that the platform uses proprietary experimental data, and that the Technion team collaborated with an unnamed defense prime before incorporation. A prime could pay to own the lab workflow and a reusable dataset if exclusive control gave it a measurable advantage across multiple materials programs. The memo provides no cross-program benchmark or disclosed commercial contract. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

A paid prime program that demonstrates repeatable savings against the customer's current workflow at its required acceptance standard is a plausible pre-seed milestone. The reported operating lab gives the team a way to run that test. Its cost and funding remain unknown, and aircraft composite qualification could extend the timeline. The memo leaves ownership and transfer rights for the Technion-origin IP and data unverified. The pre-seed announcement gives no evidence of demand for outstanding shares, and the memo supplies no revenue or payout evidence for an independent fallback. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** With no actual check terms disclosed, the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% and dilutes to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash. Under a simple pro rata waterfall, that requires $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, transaction fees, and senior claims, so enterprise value equals distributable equity. All consideration is cash at closing, with zero stock, escrow, earnout, rollover, or compensation. Actual terms and the waterfall are unknown. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Timing and assumed outcome | Gross investor cash |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | Year 5 at $50 million enterprise value | $1.5 million, 3× |
| Strong sale | Year 8 at $100 million enterprise value | $3 million, 6× at 3% ownership. At 2%, $2 million, 4× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested | $0, 0×. Shares remain held |

The modeled sale prices illustrate the hurdle. Buyer economics and comparable transaction prices are unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A prime may get equivalent value by commissioning projects. ChemPilot already solicits customer-specific briefs, and Periodic Labs offers evidence that another team has built software and physical lab infrastructure with overlapping defense R&D. If contracting or building internally delivers comparable results, ownership may carry too little premium to clear the proceeds hurdle. The memo leaves that substitution question open. It supports Watch, with insufficient evidence to trigger a structural Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid prime program establish an ownership premium large enough to support roughly $83 million in distributable equity? Buyer-validated results showing repeatable savings and quantifying the additional value of ownership over a project contract would move the action to **Reach out** if they support that hurdle. Evidence that a contract captures equivalent value and caps the acquisition price materially below the hurdle would move it to **Pass**.
