# herdr -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Can Celik has already answered my first question: what have you learned by building? He built herdr out of his own multi-agent workflow, shipped a persistent terminal runtime, and attracted substantial adoption. The repository had 46,194 stars by October 27, 2026; the company reported 340,000 downloads four months after launch. These are imperfect measures of usage, but they corroborate an actual product meeting an actual problem. His specific knowledge is visible in the artifact. I give that substantially more weight than his prior engineering titles.

The harder question is what belongs to the company once somebody copies the artifact. Herdr Extended already distributes upstream-based binaries with additional functionality. That makes substitution concrete. Persistent processes, semantic agent states, and independently attachable clients are useful capabilities, but the dossier establishes no exclusive asset behind them. The Apache-2.0 license permits the ecosystem to grow around compatible alternatives. A successful open standard can create enormous value while leaving its original author with limited economic power.

The strongest argument against waiting is the community. Herdr reports 1,789 plugins, and DigitalOcean and Fly.io have demonstrated integrations. Contributors are supplying functionality that subsequent users can consume without equivalent work from Celik. This fits my unstated preference for permissionless participation and tools that turn individual knowledge into scalable output. It deserves meaningful credit for user-created value. It does not separately establish proprietary distribution or switching costs: the dossier supplies no evidence that these extensions become unavailable when a user moves to a compatible fork.

**Twitter** illustrates why unresolved monetization need not prevent an investment when participants already create value for one another. Herdr shares that contribution mechanism, but the material difference is portability: a communication network depends on other people remaining reachable, while these extensions may travel with the runtime. That difference prevents the exception from settling this decision. The prototype exception also has limited force here: Celik has demonstrated execution, but capital efficiency is unmeasured and a working substitute already exists. The $6 million financing changes neither point. There is no documented integrity or artificial-deadline red flag; unsandboxed plugins instead create a concrete trust burden as the ecosystem expands.

Herdr Cloud offers a plausible route from individual terminal sessions into paid coordination across customer-owned machines. The same runtime could serve developers managing increasingly persistent agents across projects and teams. That is credible adjacent demand, although the dossier does not establish its spending or willingness to pay. Pricing, revenue, and contribution economics remain unknown, so I assign the monetization default. I would wait before writing a personal check. My flip condition is a twelve-month paid Cloud cohort retaining at least 90% of revenue, with customer evidence that shared operational state creates material costs of switching to compatible alternatives. That would show herdr capturing something durable from the useful system Celik has already built.
