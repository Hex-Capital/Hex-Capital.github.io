# Ryhla -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a utility software vendor is Ryhla’s strongest exit path. Owning Ryhla’s feeder models and developer filing workflow could give a buyer an earlier role in project siting and a way to carry those projects into interconnection review. The memo records E.ON’s majority acquisition of envelio as an adjacent transaction. Ryhla reports that screening out eight of ten planned battery projects saved one customer about $160,000 in fees. That illustrates the potential value of the workflow to developers. The value of owning it to an acquirer remains an inference. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Ryhla reports a live Midwest solar deployment, models in 11 utility territories, and 91% utility approval for sites it flagged as viable in its 2025 cohort. The disclosed results omit the cohort size and outcomes for rejected sites. A proposed 12-month milestone is repeat paid use by developers across two existing utility territories, with documented fee savings and utility-study checks on a sample of both accepted and rejected sites. Existing coverage makes that test plausible for the two-person team. Confirmed YC backing provides a funding signal, while the memo leaves test costs and runway unresolved. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3% without follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. At 3%, that requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity. The memo gives no acquisition price, revenue, or holder terms to support a realizable range. The following figures are scenario assumptions. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

- **Modest sale.** At $40 million enterprise value, assuming zero net debt or deal adjustments, no senior claims that alter the pro rata split, and an eligible holder selling the full stake for cash within eight years, distributable equity is $40 million and the holder receives $1.2 million, or 2.4×.
- **Strong sale.** At $100 million enterprise value under the same assumptions, the holder receives $3 million, or 6×. If net debt and deal adjustments reduce equity to $90 million and the holder can sell only 80% of the stake, cash falls to $2.16 million, or 4.32×.
- **Prolonged private.** If Ryhla keeps operating and reinvests available cash, the holder realizes zero within eight years. The memo provides no evidence of an independent payout or eligible secondary. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could cover enough of the workflow with public hosting-capacity maps and existing PowerClerk and envelio tools to make acquisition unnecessary. Ryhla’s territory-specific calibration may also require fresh data and validation as coverage expands, and utilities retain control over approvals and upgrade requirements. Those risks could limit what a buyer would pay. The memo establishes neither a structural closing or participation barrier nor a credible price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a utility software buyer’s build-versus-buy analysis support enough cash at closing for the benchmark holder to receive $2.5 million within eight years? Move from Watch to Reach out if repeat paid deployments across utility territories establish buyer-relevant economics and a credible acquisition range and sale structure clear that hurdle after debt, deal adjustments, senior claims, and any limit on the holder’s sale fraction.
