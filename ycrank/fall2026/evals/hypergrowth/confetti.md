# Confetti -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Conditional on strong product-market fit, Confetti’s strongest path is self-serve design software for makers who build repeatedly. Its English-language workflow turns prompts and reference images into editable CAD, parts plans, assembly instructions, and firmware for supported electronics. Makers can export fabrication files and choose their own supplier, making the core workflow portable across markets where buyers can use English and source parts and fabrication. Integrated ordering depends on eligible designs and supplier availability. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Public projects give makers a way to discover Confetti and start from a remix. The documented web flow and 25 free monthly credits give them a way to try it independently. The memo establishes published subscription prices and leaves independent checkout unverified. It records an October 28 launch and 36 public design pages in November. The founders report users working on a custom keyboard, a portable analyzer, and personalized gifts. These signals show early design activity for the two-person team, while realized revenue, paying customers, and commercial growth remain unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Revision history gives repeat makers a reason to return. Monthly plans at $29, $79, and $199 offer an upgrade path, and repeat eligible orders can add fee revenue. Whether enough makers build frequently enough for expansion to offset churn is uncertain. At the $29 tier, incremental contribution depends on generation, storage, activation, and support costs. In an illustrative $50 supplier order where the fee applies and the supplier charge passes through, Confetti earns $3 before payment and order-support costs. File export and supplier fulfillment limit Confetti’s direct fabrication burden, while generation retries and troubleshooting could still absorb much of the revenue. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Confetti may be useful for a single project and rarely needed afterward. The product asks makers to verify fit, strength, and electrical compatibility themselves. If those checks routinely require hands-on help, a self-serve design session could turn into a costly path to a successful build. The structural downside gate stays open because makers can validate their own designs and use alternative fabricators, and the memo establishes no required per-buyer intervention by Confetti. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can successive cohorts of makers who arrive through public remixes upgrade on their own, complete a physical build, and renew the following month? Cohort records showing a growing number of makers completing that sequence without founder help, with positive contribution after generation and support costs, would move the action to Reach out.
