# Hubble -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I start with three financing facts: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. Hubble discloses none of them, while calling its pre-seed oversubscribed. That is affirmative auction evidence, not harmless pre-seed silence. The company may be good, but the round has already attracted enough agreement that I am being asked to buy consensus at an unknown price.

The capital plan is equally underwritten by omission. Hubble has a plausible venture moment—prove that API, browser, and voice retrieval can reliably assemble complete patient records across hostile systems—but the dossier never says that this round funds that experiment. There is no dated deadline, completion-rate target, or pass/fail result. “Build more integrations and sell more” would be a capital factory; I need to know what specific truth this check reveals.

The operating proof is considerably better. Forty-plus production tools span Epic, Cerner, athenahealth, UnitedHealthcare, and Aetna, with deterministic identifiers, source traceability, and per-call audit logs. Customers report $340,000 in denied claims recovered in 90 days, estimate accuracy rising from 50% to 94%, and paralegal work falling from hours to minutes. Those are real workflow consequences, not 453 LinkedIn followers dressed up as distribution. But the customers are anonymous, B2B pricing is unpublished, and Hubble provides no paid conversion, retrieval volume, retention, or accuracy cohort. The workflow causes value; the dossier does not yet prove that Hubble owns the resulting purchase.

The founders are the strongest part of the case. Prabha Dublish shipped healthcare products as Grow Therapy’s first product hire, while Aaron Leon built Amazon Care’s data platform and One Medical infrastructure serving more than one million patients. That is a credible translation layer between healthcare operators who need usable records and the EHR machinery that obstructs them. Their decade-long relationship also lowers interpersonal formation risk, although CEO/CTO titles do not establish who has final authority over product, hiring, and financing when they disagree.

The bull case stings because the reported customer outcomes resemble the mechanism that justified the exception for Ando: software embedded in an enterprise workflow producing measurable economic results. The material difference is that Ando disclosed complete pilot-to-paid conversion and retention; Hubble offers anonymous testimonials without payment or cohort evidence. Meanwhile, Particle Health, 1upHealth, Health Gorilla, and PicnicHealth have each raised at least $49 million, and Hubble still depends on portals, phone systems, APIs, and release processes it does not control. Provider-specific retrieval histories could become a proprietary dark pool, but today that advantage is inferred rather than measured.

Delete the next round and the model disappears: Individual Access Services are free, while B2B revenue, gross margin, burn, and runway are undisclosed. The agent layer is not itself disqualifying, but neither model improvement nor 40 integrations answers whether the business can finance its own operations. I am passing on this round. My one verifiable flip condition is three named B2B customers that have paid and renewed six-month contracts at positive contribution margin; that would show owned workflow conversion, support a one-and-done path, and provide the hard operating evidence required to override an auctioned seed.
