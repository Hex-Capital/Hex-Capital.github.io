# Salem Robotics Inc -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I start with three blanks: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether YC is the first genuinely scarce check. Salem Robotics Inc may be an unfashionable hard-technical bet, but the dossier does not establish that the round is cheap or non-consensus. The $25,000 accelerator prize is evidence that someone supplied early capital, not evidence about current price or competition. I cannot underwrite the deal without inventing the deal.

The founders are the real signal. Caleb Horan worked on autonomous radiation-survey systems at Los Alamos; Janak Panthi brings Los Alamos deployment experience, six years around products and quadrupeds, and a 2025 manipulation planner tested across ten Spot and UR5 tasks. Together they have shipped at the intersection of nuclear operations and robotics rather than merely acquiring credentials adjacent to it. This is an unusually credible translation team for a two-person pre-seed company.

The missing piece is a capital-funded venture moment. Salem offers one-to-two-week facility validations, which could become a clean experiment: can its system complete a prescribed radiation-protection route, manipulate the required doors and instruments, and produce records an operator accepts? But the dossier names no facility, deadline, acceptance threshold, or use of round proceeds. Current contract-scoping conversations in nuclear and oil and gas do not convert that possible experiment into an actual one.

The bull case is painful because the technical and market conditions fit. Humans still perform hazardous surveys manually, the founders have direct operating access, and a hardware-agnostic layer could accumulate facility maps, inspection histories, instrument integrations, and compliance workflows. Yet none of those proprietary assets exists publicly today. The planner is BSD-licensed, the available implementation is prepared only for Spot and a Kinova Gen3 arm, and Energy Robotics, ANYbotics, and Gecko already attack overlapping inspection workflows with substantially more capital. Salem has demonstrated technical capability, not a dark pool that causes purchases.

Delete the next financing and the dossier stops answering questions. There is no revenue, gross margin, burn, deployment cost, or evidence that a validation converts into recurring software economics. Hardware-agnostic deployment could improve capital efficiency, but every additional robot, instrument, facility, and safety case can also turn the company into a bespoke integration shop. My 36-month base case is a handful of paid nuclear or oil-and-gas validations but less than $1 million ARR, with Salem operating primarily as a deployment-services business rather than a repeatable software company.

I pass today. The one verifiable flip condition is a named nuclear operator completing a paid validation and signing an annual license after Salem autonomously performs a defined inspection route and delivers accepted compliance records. That single event would prove customer action, begin the proprietary workflow asset, and clarify whether the finite experiment leads to repeatable cash economics. Without it, exceptional founder fit cannot override four unanswered capital-allocation gates.
