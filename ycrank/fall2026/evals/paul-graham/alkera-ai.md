# Alkera AI -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Alkera AI has shipped software. What I cannot find is the incident that tells me what these founders do when shipping becomes difficult. There is no named customer obstacle, founder-built workaround, or elapsed time between the two. That leaves my most important test unanswered. Yale, HRT, Ramp, and ByteDance establish relevant experience. They do not establish resourcefulness under resistance.

The strongest evidence is Databench, an inspectable Apache-2.0 workspace. Alkera also reports an 83.28% DataAgentBench pass rate against Claude Code’s 68.49%, averaged over five runs in July 2026. That deserves technical credit, with a discount because the comparison is company-reported and production safety is a different claim. The team has moved beyond diagrams. Alkera partly fits my pattern of backing programmable tools that technical users can build upon, but the dossier does not show developers appropriating this workspace for unexpected jobs. Shipping the substrate establishes capability; discovering what users do with it remains ahead.

The founders’ financial ingestion pipelines, distributed-compute tooling, and production agent work make their choice of problem credible. Column-level lineage, permissions, and human-verified organizational knowledge also put Alkera near genuinely tedious work. Those integrations could conceal the opportunity. But the dossier does not connect a particular founder’s frustrating experience to the company’s origin. Nor does it document a rejected hypothesis followed by a revised product. I leave both questions open rather than turn relevant employment into evidence of an organic idea or adaptability.

Who wants this badly enough to keep using it? Product Hunt’s #4 daily ranking on October 7 and 475 followers do not answer that. Neither do 13 GitHub stars and one fork. The $50 Plus and $200 Pro subscriptions establish an offer, not purchases. A small data team repeatedly trusting Alkera with one troublesome pipeline would tell me more than all these audience counts. I see no evidence that the founders depend on a ceremonial launch, a large partner, or delegated sales, so those red flags are unproven. The missing evidence is repeat product use.

The strongest argument for investing is that these founders have already built something difficult in a problem area they understand. Customer-specific definitions and downstream dependencies might reward painstaking installation long before competitors recognize the resulting advantage. But Altimate and Hex already publish overlapping validation, context, and governance capabilities. Alkera needs customer learning to turn its technical work into a distinctive product. My known exceptions do not resolve that gap: the prototype exception addresses cofounder uncertainty, which is not my objection here; the other exceptions depend on unmistakable real-world pull or independently validated proof that this dossier does not supply.

I would pass today. This is an evidence gap in the founder test, not evidence of passive founders. One verifiable condition would flip my decision: a customer-confirmed six-week deployment record showing the founders personally overcoming a named blocker, replacing a failed approach after observing usage, and reaching repeated paid weekly use across five data teams. That would establish the causal chain Alkera currently lacks: resistance, learning, and users who return.
