# GrayPass -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

GrayPass’s founders have started acquiring specific knowledge the useful way: building a product and persuading people to try it. European pilots and a reported customer win carry more weight than Waterloo or McGill. The public platform is an artifact; the neuroscience research is adjacent preparation. Neither establishes that the team has solved behavioral authentication under adversarial conditions. I give them credit for entering the problem through action, with limited evidence so far about what those actions have taught them.

The decisive question is what remains if a competitor reproduces the SDK and action-bound proofs. GrayPass claims ownership of its models, but ownership does not establish a proprietary advantage. The dossier identifies no exclusive training corpus or demonstrated improvement with deployment. More consequentially, behavioral continuity remains experimental and unapproved for live enforcement ([platform](https://graypass.com/platform), [terms](https://graypass.com/terms)). A signed proof can preserve the integrity of a decision. It cannot make the underlying judgment accurate. With TypingDNA and Twosense already supplying overlapping capabilities, GrayPass needs evidence that its judgment becomes harder to reproduce.

The market offers room to maneuver. Payments, permission changes, and data exports share a useful primitive: establish fresh confidence before an irreversible action. That capability could carry GrayPass from financial platforms into business software and developer infrastructure. BioCatch’s reported revenue above $185 million demonstrates substantial spending on the related fraud problem; it does not establish GrayPass’s addressable share. The expansion path is credible, while the initial buyer and repeatable purchasing motion remain loosely defined.

The strongest argument against passing is that customers may already be teaching the founders something competitors have missed. Pricing starts at $500 per month, and the CEO reports five figures in revenue, although the period and recurring portion are unspecified ([evaluation form](https://graypass.com/evaluate), [founder report](https://www.linkedin.com/in/aravmathur)). Application-enforced authorization could become a valuable control point even without a breakthrough biometric model. But embedded policies create only hypothetical switching costs today. Founder outbound acquires customers; the dossier shows no mechanism through which one customer improves another customer’s experience. My preference for infrastructure bottlenecks fits the ambition, while my recurring preference for participant-created value lacks supporting evidence here.

I can accept missing monetization when a network is visibly forming, or missing distribution when an exceptional prototype and capital efficiency justify the exception. GrayPass has some monetization, but neither overriding signal is established. There is no documented integrity violation or artificial financing deadline; its explicit compromised-endpoint limitation is a useful boundary, not evidence of misconduct. I pass because the durable asset remains unidentified. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: an independent customer deployment study showing that an exclusively controlled model improves fraud detection as additional deployments contribute data, at a fixed legitimate-user rejection rate. That would connect customer acquisition to compounding advantage.
