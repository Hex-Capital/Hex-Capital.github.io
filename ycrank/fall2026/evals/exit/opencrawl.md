# OpenCrawl -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a coding-agent platform seeking shared session search and coordination across tools. OpenCrawl combines searchable transcripts with a shared board, and the Codex app’s parallel sessions illustrate where that workflow could matter. The memo records 57 repository stars and nine forks. The CTO reported an onboarding in which agents messaged across users. These signals support testing the workflow. Buyer interest, recurring use, and measurable savings remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

A 90-day paid pilot with a mixed-tool engineering team could measure repeat use and reductions in duplicate investigations. The launch and reported onboarding make a pilot plausible, although the listed seed entry leaves available runway unclear. Self-hosting suggests the software could be deployed in a buyer’s environment. IP and license rights, transcript permissions, and closing terms remain open. The memo supplies no evidenced independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at $10 million post-money, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale cases below assume an eligible holder sells the full stake for cash within eight years, with no additional preference leakage or escrow.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Holder cash |
|----------|-----------------|-------------|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $35M for equity | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less the same $5M leaves $95M for equity | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested and no eligible share sale or payout occurs within eight years | $0 |

The prices are scenario assumptions. The memo provides no buyer economics or transaction evidence to support either price. Even in the strong case, dilution to 2.5% would reduce cash proceeds to $2.375 million, below the objective. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A platform could build coordination itself. Claude Code already offers shared tasks and messaging, and Agent Relay offers cross-tool messaging and searchable sessions. OpenCrawl’s listener-dependent mentions weaken its cross-client advantage, while default team visibility and imperfect secret masking could limit enterprise adoption. These risks weaken the case for a buyer paying above the hurdle. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible price ceiling, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a coding-agent platform’s build-versus-buy analysis, grounded in a paid pilot that demonstrates recurring cross-tool use and measurable workflow savings, support an acquisition price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle after deal adjustments? A buyer-side assessment that supports such a price would move the action to Reach out. A credible price ceiling below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
