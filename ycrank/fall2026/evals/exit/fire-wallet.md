# Fire Wallet -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest path. The memo documents a managed per-request payments preview from AWS with Coinbase and Stripe, evidence that platforms are pursuing this workflow. Fire markets a combination of revocable agent spending limits and private per-request settlement. A payments platform could pay to own Fire if acquiring its software and live merchant integrations would bring recurring payment volume to the platform faster or at lower cost than building or partnering. That ownership advantage remains an inference. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Fire reports an e402 mainnet test that returned a paid response in under two seconds, a Trustboop LOI, and one enterprise merchant onboarding. The reported $1.5 million per month is the merchant’s existing volume. The $100 million per year is potential volume associated with introductions. Both are prospective for Fire. The test, onboarding, and recorded $500,000 YC financing support a path to a production pilot. The milestone is repeat settlement through Fire with measured merchant benefit. Burn, runway, and the capital required to reach that milestone are undisclosed. (Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The memo provides no terms for the check being evaluated, so the illustrative seed case applies. A $500,000 check at $10 million post-money buys 5%, diluted to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million in gross holder cash. It requires $83.3 million of distributable equity proceeds at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Enterprise value → distributable equity | Holder cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $40M → $40M | $1.2M | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100M → $100M | $3.0M | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested | $0, shares retained | 0× realized |

The sale prices are scenario assumptions. Both sales assume all-cash consideration at closing, zero net debt, zero other price adjustments, and pro rata allocation with no preference claims. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. At 2% ownership, even the modeled strong sale returns only $2 million, or 4×. The memo supplies no usable acquisition price evidence, ownership waterfall, or transfer terms for the relevant software and dependencies. It also supplies no independent fallback that could deliver cash to this holder. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A platform could assemble programmable wallets and confidential settlement from existing providers. Fire also documents that merchants can offer e402 alongside x402, which weakens the case that buying Fire is necessary to secure merchant access. The memo gives no measured production advantage or buyer economics that would establish a premium for ownership. It also gives no evidence that available substitutes fully replicate Fire’s combination or cap the price below the proceeds hurdle. That gap supports Watch, and the modeled zero-cash downside alone does not trigger the Pass override. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a live merchant deployment show a payments platform that buying Fire would create enough incremental value over building or partnering to support the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Recurring Fire-settled volume and a platform’s quantified comparison supporting at least $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership would move the action to Reach out.
