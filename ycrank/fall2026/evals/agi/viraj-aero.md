# Viraj Aero -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Viraj Aero’s strongest AGI path is a scarce complement. AI data center developers are procuring on-site gas generation to bridge grid delays, and some sites also face water constraints. GENA could bring those sites online sooner by generating electricity and recovering cooling water from its own exhaust. Viraj Aero lists 10 MW, 45% electrical efficiency, and 2 ppm NOx as specifications. Its first power-and-water turbine was still under construction, leaving continuous-duty performance and usable cooling-water yield unmeasured in the memo. The company reports a patent on its compact condenser, which provides a specific basis for defensibility if buyers need the combined output and competing systems have difficulty matching it. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Viraj Aero reports more than 1.3 GW of interest under letters of intent. It also reports a paying engine manufacturer whose contract scope and payment are undisclosed, so that payment provides limited evidence of data center demand for GENA. The aviation prototype’s ground tests and the team’s prior power-project work support seed-stage execution, with stationary qualification still ahead. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

The proposed expansion unit is a 10 MW genset. If all stated interest converted at that rating, it would represent more than 130 units. Repeat deployment depends on whether avoided grid delays and water costs justify the installed price, fuel, and maintenance at realistic utilization, and whether the price covers Viraj Aero’s manufacturing, commissioning, and service costs. The memo supplies too little cost and pricing evidence to establish either side of that calculation. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Developers may be able to meet their needs with an available turbine or reciprocating-engine package and separate cooling arrangements. GENA still needs site gas and air approvals, which could erode any time advantage. The relevant question is whether its water recovery changes the earliest feasible opening date or the cost of operating at a site where both constraints bind. The memo identifies credible substitutes for power but leaves their adequacy at those sites unresolved, so the path remains open and warrants Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can GENA win and pass a paid qualification with an independent data center developer at a site constrained by both grid access and cooling water? A sustained site test that meets the buyer’s power, emissions, and usable-water requirements, together with a documented cost or commissioning advantage over the buyer’s best available alternative, would move the action to Reach out. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
