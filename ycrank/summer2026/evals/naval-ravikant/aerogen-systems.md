# Aerogen Systems -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Vogel and Lavery know purification because they built it. They co-authored NASA’s 2024 VOC-removal report, spent two years developing photocatalytic reactors, iterated through multiple hardware generations, and carried the same technical mechanism from spacecraft air treatment into semiconductor tooling. The application changed; the specific knowledge survived. SpaceX manufacturing and H3D instrumentation add relevant integration experience, although neither founder has yet sold qualified equipment into a fab.

Talos is not copyable code wrapped around a commodity model. Aerogen has physical hardware, patent-pending IP, more than $500,000 of prior R&D, onboard monitoring, and a measured run reaching 7N purity in 16 minutes and below 1 ppb VOC concentration. Those are real artifacts, but not yet a compounding moat. The result is company-reported, and Entegris and Camfil already manufacture qualified tool-level AMC products. If a competitor copied the reactor geometry tomorrow, Aerogen has disclosed neither exclusive inputs nor accumulated qualification data that would stop it.

The market has room to maneuver. Semiconductor contamination control was estimated at $6.163 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $11.103 billion by 2032; advanced packaging and hybrid bonding make surface contamination more economically consequential. Talos can enter through individual tools, while CleanPipe could extend the same purification capability into wafer movement between tools. The causal adjacency is credible, but qualification—not market size—is the bottleneck.

The bear case is that Aerogen becomes a custom engineering consultancy disguised as a hardware platform. Every deployment begins with an on-site assessment and equipment integration; no fab, OEM, price, paid pilot, or recurring revenue is named. Each customer currently creates more work for Aerogen, not value or distribution for another customer. That contradicts the permissionless and user-compounding pattern I prefer, while incumbents can bundle a competing solution into established qualification and aftermarket relationships.

The relevant known exception is Notion: a capital-efficient, working prototype can justify investing before observable distribution. The shared mechanism is that substantial product language was developed before the market validated it. The material difference is that semiconductor equipment must survive customer qualification, and incumbent access can matter more than product taste. Aerogen also fits my recent shift toward physical infrastructure bottlenecks, and the dossier contains none of the behavioral red flags—unfair dealing, manufactured deadlines, or reassurance dependence—that would veto the founders.

I would write a small pre-seed check. My 36-month base case is that Aerogen becomes a specialist supplier with two to four qualified fab or equipment-OEM customers and $2 million to $6 million in annual revenue by August 2029, rather than a network business. The one flip condition is production qualification: if an independent fab evaluation cannot reproduce sub-1-ppb performance without consumables, I pass rather than financing further laboratory iteration.
