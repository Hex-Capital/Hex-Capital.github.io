# Axonnic -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B. Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** The initial buyer is a manufacturer trying to automate a recurring, force-sensitive task such as sewing. Axonnic’s sleeve adds grip and force estimates where video misses occluded fingers and fabric tension. The memo documents a live demonstration of EMG control on a robotic hand. Axonnic reports paid design-partner pilots and sleeve shipments, but the memo gives no pilot economics or production results. Those are concrete early signals of technical feasibility and buyer commitment. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The growth scenario assumes direct sales can bring factories into a standard capture and deployment process. If the first policy delivers value on a production line, the factory could pay to add stations or tasks. The memo provides no evidence of repeat paid use or expansion. The company-reported Italian sewing LOI is an early signal that the same job exists overseas. Gen 2 preorders are limited to US delivery, so an Italian rollout would test whether Axonnic can deliver and support the same kit abroad and adapt its policy to the local robot and workflow. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The binding constraint could be factory integration. Recording interference has already forced repeat data collections, deployment requires mapping human demonstrations to the robot’s controls, and data reuse rights are negotiated per engagement. The next customer adds sleeve manufacturing and fulfillment, plus potentially more on-site capture, calibration, policy engineering, and support. The advertised $8,000–$12,000 sleeve price gives no estimate of contribution after those costs. Wired electronics could reduce repeat visits, but the memo provides no measured rollout savings. The evidence supports Watch because proportional deployment costs remain a plausible risk and the Pass gate requires an observed structural ceiling. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

A live installation handling recurring sewing work, with recorded setup hours, repeat visits, and delivery and support costs that leave positive contribution at the contracted price, would move the action to Reach out.
