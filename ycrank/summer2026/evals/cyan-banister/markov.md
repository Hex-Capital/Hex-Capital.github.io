# Markov -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Dev Mandal worked at Sarvam AI, while Harish Ashok previously built Zenith around hardware workflows. Markov is strongest as founder-adjacent AI infrastructure and weakest as a frontier-hardware or regulated swing: Markov looks built by young operators who have touched the machinery, but the machinery remains software data plumbing rather than a rocket, drone, camera, or ugly regulated artifact. That pairing makes Markov credible without yet making Markov magically weird.

Niantic is the high rung because John Hanke spent a decade living inside location data before Pokémon Go materialized. Markov shares the mechanism: Harish encountered computer workflows through Zenith, while Dev built doubt.ai and worked at Sarvam AI before Markov began collecting GUI trajectories. The divergence matters. Markov’s founders have adjacent repetitions, including doubt.ai’s reported 700 users and 5,000 questions, but neither founder has yet shipped a comparable system at Niantic scale. I see authentic contact with the problem, not a biography that makes the outcome feel inevitable.

GameCrush exposed what happens when a software product has no hard artifact protecting its DNA: investors and governance can pull the experience apart. Markov has a similar soft center because its flagship 48,478-video corpus is CC-BY-4.0, Turing has already delivered 10,000 supervised GUI tasks, and no structural barrier is documented. Markov touches privacy and workplace-data regulation, but Markov has disclosed neither a contributor-consent system nor a rights-clearing apparatus that turns that ugliness into a compounding moat. This is nowhere near the SpaceX side of my portfolio.

Scale AI reached $870 million in 2024 revenue, while Surge AI reportedly exceeded $1 billion, so computer-use data is not a category sophisticated investors find ridiculous. Markov’s non-consensus opportunity must compound through specialization: over 18 months, every proprietary Salesforce, AutoCAD, Blender, and Photoshop workflow could improve Markov’s task library, contributor routing, and quality controls. If Markov keeps releasing the valuable layer openly while Scale, Surge, and Turing internalize the same collection loop, specialization collapses into lead generation. The 150,000 Hugging Face downloads are distribution, but they are not evidence that Markov’s round is underpriced.

Dev Mandal is explicitly identified as Markov’s CEO, which is better than two founders hiding behind symmetrical titles. Markov still provides no evidence about the co-founder relationship, equity split, vesting, or Dev’s authority when Harish disagrees about selling a proprietary dataset rather than releasing it openly. The governance score flips upward only if Markov can show that Dev has documented operating control and a founder-conflict protocol; without that, “CEO” is a label, not yet a tiebreaker.

Flock Safety could be noticed as a Wi-Fi name and then encountered as a camera protecting an actual street. Markov offers a weaker but real parallel: a researcher can download Markov’s 48,478 videos, inspect mouse and keyboard traces, and observe whether an Excel or Blender workflow is usable. Markov is therefore more verifiable than an invisible B2B API, but 150,000 downloads still describe an artifact for model developers rather than something a normal human can install, ride, scan, or photograph within 48 hours.

Markov’s 150,000-plus Hugging Face downloads and 10,000-plus recorded hours create a plausible funnel before Markov has disclosed a single paying customer. My falsifiable 36-month prediction is that by July 2029 Markov will reach $3 million–$8 million ARR selling private GUI-evaluation and reinforcement-learning environments, with Markov’s open datasets serving as customer acquisition rather than the core product. The mechanism is founder proximity plus workflow accumulation: Dev and Harish know enough about model development to convert research usage into bespoke environments, but Markov’s low frontier moat caps the outcome unless the private corpus compounds faster than the public one.

Turing’s 10,000 supervised GUI tasks make the bear mechanism sting: commoditization could arrive before conversion, leaving Markov with 150,000 free downloads, no revenue, ambiguous data rights, and four much larger vendors able to reproduce the supply. Markov’s rebuttal is execution density—two founders assembled approximately 12,300 hours and 48,478 videos before most pre-seed founders have a benchmark—but downloads do not pay a bubbe back. I remain Neutral because Markov has demonstrated production and distribution, not proprietary demand. One verifiable condition flips me to Invest: a named frontier model laboratory signs a rights-audited contract worth at least $1 million annually for exclusive Markov trajectories or environments.
