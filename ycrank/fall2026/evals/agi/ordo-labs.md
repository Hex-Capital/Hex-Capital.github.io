# Ordo Labs -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest path. Ordo reports cutting one regulated lab’s post-analysis workflow from 40 hours to 4, including scientist review. If that reduction holds at equivalent quality after retries and exceptions, a CRO could afford to take on small reporting jobs it previously declined. That is a scenario to test. The reported paid pilots show early willingness to pay among regulated labs, while pricing, repeat use, and demand for previously uneconomic jobs remain unestablished. The Fall 2026 launch and pilot stage make this a plausible seed case. (*Ordo Labs*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

Ordo describes customer-specific validation environments and source files sealed at sign-off. Revalidating workflows and transferring traceability records could give labs a reason to stay when model capabilities converge. The memo provides no measured switching costs or buyer preference under parity. The team brings adjacent experience from Hoang’s pharma authentication pilot and Tovatt’s backend engineering. Repeat deployment in regulated labs remains unverified. (*Defensibility*, *Founders & Team*)

File-upload onboarding gives Ordo a plausible route into the next lab. Contribution from that lab would be subscription or usage fees less inference, support, and customer-specific setup and revalidation costs. Job frequency determines how widely those deployment costs can be spread. The memo gives no pricing or per-lab cost data to test that bridge. (*The Idea*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Ordo may make existing reporting cheaper while leaving the set of commercially viable jobs unchanged. Qualified staff still control release, and exception handling could narrow the reported labor savings. ScienceMachine already offers source-linked drafting, document QC, and electronic approval. That overlap makes substitution credible, though equivalent performance and actual switching remain untested. Both Path B conditions remain open, so the outreach gate is unmet and the thesis override does not close the path. Path A has no separate support because labs retain control of qualified sign-off and the memo identifies no scarce capability Ordo controls. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent CRO repeatedly pay viable prices to run a class of jobs it previously declined on cost, and choose Ordo when a competing workflow meets the same reporting and validation requirements? A paid production trial should measure all-in labor per accepted report, including retries, exceptions, and sign-off, and record the buyer’s choice at comparable prices. Repeat paid use and a demonstrated preference for Ordo would support **Reach out** if rescoring clears the outreach threshold. A trial showing the jobs remain uneconomic or that the lab readily switches at parity would move the action to **Pass**.
