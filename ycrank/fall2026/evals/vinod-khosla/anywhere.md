# Anywhere -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Anywhere makes personal location context available to software that can act on it. The interesting consequence would be eliminating the human work of reconciling places, conversations, calendars, and plans. But the dossier establishes the information being combined, not the quantity of work displaced. There is no measured time burden, spending baseline, or demonstrated multiplier. I can imagine a consequential personal-context platform. I cannot underwrite that consequence from a living map alone.

The product distinction is real but narrow: connected agents can read and modify places, notes, visits, and trips, extending beyond Mapstr’s saved addresses and Wanderlog’s itineraries. That establishes functionality, not a technical discontinuity. Apple Maps, Google Maps Platform, and OpenAI supply important underlying services; standardized agent connections make access easier for competitors too. Those dependencies do not prove Anywhere is merely a wrapper. Equally, an API does not establish proprietary capability. No benchmark shows that Anywhere solves a named incumbent constraint five or ten times better.

Delaroche is the strongest reason to resist an easy dismissal. Building mapping technology at Zenly and rebuilding Life360’s map experience gives him unusually direct knowledge of this product’s engineering problems. The strongest bull case is that this experience lets a two-person team turn an apparently small consumer feature into the context layer through which agents coordinate everyday life. My actual investing pattern permits execution-led consumer businesses. The **Block** exception also matters: a small interface wedge can expand into infrastructure. The material difference is that Anywhere has not established the enormous economic pool its wedge can capture. Nor does relevant employment establish rapid belief revision or complementary recruiting. Fenn’s earlier group-planning product suggests exploration, but the dossier does not document what evidence drove a change.

The next dollar should test whether users repeatedly delegate meaningful work to this map. Anywhere launched publicly on November 1, 2026; four App Store ratings averaging 2.0 cannot establish retention or product rejection. The reported verification failure does identify an obstacle to running that experiment. A connector listing establishes availability, not usage. There is no disclosed experiment with a threshold, budget, or stop condition, so I give no affirmative credit for risk sequencing. Likewise, a free download establishes neither cheap delivery nor sustainable economics: paid pricing and the operating cost of maps, inference, and support remain undisclosed.

I would pass with my own money today. The concern is the unresolved combination of consequence and mechanism, rather than company age or absent revenue. The standard-integration path raises my incremental-AI red flag, but the evidence does not establish architectural imitation, advertising dependence, or premature spending on distribution. No documented exception overrides the missing consequence case. My verifiable condition for reopening diligence is one paid, repeat-use customer cohort demonstrating at least a 10x reduction in the measured human cost of a defined coordination workflow, including corrections, with positive contribution margin after service costs. That would connect Anywhere’s context layer to economic leverage. Today, that connection remains a hypothesis.
