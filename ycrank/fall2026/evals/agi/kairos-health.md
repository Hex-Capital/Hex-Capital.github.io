# Kairos Health -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Kairos could make repeated outreach economical for overdue dental patients whom front-desk staff rarely have time to call. The former workflow relies on manual callbacks and automated texts. Kairos uses practice records to identify overdue patients, contacts them, and books directly under the practice’s scheduling rules. The market-opening scenario assumes that some overdue cohorts are too costly to pursue through the existing workflow and that automated outreach brings enough of those patients back for completed visits. The memo establishes the workflow and leaves that economic threshold unmeasured. (The Idea)

Pecan Dental’s testimonial reports more than 1,200 calls handled and more than $21,000 in average monthly dental production. It does not isolate incremental completed recall visits. A founder reports 13 paying practices, which supports early demand for the product. Repeat payment for the newly viable recall use remains unverified. The memo separately reports a March launch and 58 hours of calls by June, evidence of operating progress alongside the founders’ dental front-desk and patient call-routing experience. (Market & Traction; Founders & Team)

The cost comparison should measure staff time and full cost per completed recall visit under each workflow, including retries, booking corrections, escalations, and compliance. For Kairos, the practice’s cost includes its allocated subscription fee and remaining staff work. Clinic economics also depend on the contribution from incremental visits. The expansion unit is one practice location. Kairos quotes $1,200 per month per location and claims 85% gross margin. Realized pricing and per-location costs for onboarding, voice usage, support, and exceptions are unverified. (The Idea; Key Facts; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Kairos may improve an existing reception workflow while bringing few additional overdue patients back for care. Booking errors could return enough work to staff to erase the savings. Under model parity, schedule write-back and practice-specific rules give buyers a concrete reason to consider Kairos, but Arini already offers overlapping recall and insurance workflows. The memo contains no buyer comparison showing that Kairos’s configuration earns renewals or a premium against an equivalent substitute. Both required conditions remain open, supporting Watch. The memo identifies no independently supported scarce complement. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

In a paid trial across independent practices, does Kairos bring back overdue patient cohorts that practices rarely pursue, at a viable full cost per completed visit, and earn renewals when a functionally equivalent alternative is available? Compare matched cohorts across the existing callback and text workflow, Kairos, and the alternative. Measure incremental completed visits, staff minutes, and full delivery cost. Move to Reach out if the trial establishes viable incremental recall and practices renew at a price that covers Kairos’s delivery costs because of a demonstrated advantage in booking or exception handling. Pass if a representative trial defeats either condition. Keep Watch if the comparison remains inconclusive.
