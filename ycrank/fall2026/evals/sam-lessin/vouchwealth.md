# Vouch -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Vouch has a reported $500,000 YC investment. That tells me how much capital arrived, not what the business costs to own. Post-money valuation, competing offers, and whether another investor’s check is genuinely scarce are all undisclosed. I cannot call this an auction because it is in YC. I also cannot underwrite a cheap, non-consensus entry from a funding headline. With my own money, that distinction matters before the wealth-management story gets interesting.

What does that $500,000 prove? The reported November 2026 launch, conditional on licensing, supplies a calendar event but no defined commercial experiment. Authorization would let Vouch operate; it would not establish that Indian households will pay for consolidated investment and tax advice. There is no disclosed use-of-funds plan connecting this round to a measurable result that changes the company’s value. That is missing evidence, not proof the founder lacks a plan. It leaves the central purpose of my check unestablished.

Arnav Ghatiwala is the strongest reason to investigate anyway. Leading Robinhood’s Advisor Network buildout is a relevant operating receipt, and Jane Street trading-engine and operations work gives him experience behind the financial interface. The bull case has teeth: someone who understands both consumer financial products and their operating machinery could make fragmented holdings usable through a trusted advisory relationship. That fits my pattern of backing translators between systems. But the dossier does not establish his access to Indian advisory distribution or regulatory operations. His experience earns founder credit; it does not also establish customer access, conversion, or attractive financing.

The ownership question is harder. Vouch asks users to connect existing holdings, potentially creating a recurring workflow, but the invite-only WhatsApp waitlist has no disclosed size or observed conversion. A communication channel does not establish owned trust. Novelty Wealth already offers SEBI-registered AI advisory, aggregation, and tax planning, while Kuvera combines portfolio analysis with execution. Those alternatives make the advertised feature bundle difficult to own. The relevant AI red flag is reproducible functionality without a demonstrated proprietary asset; the dossier does not show that Vouch depends on model scarcity or promises error-free automation. Human experts could make the service trustworthy, but their cost must be counted. With no disclosed pricing, margins, burn, or runway, I cannot tell whether this becomes a self-funding advisory business.

I pass at **30/100**. This is an underwriting gap around a relevant founder, rather than evidence of an actively bad business. I checked the exceptions: there is no documented paid conversion and retention, extraordinary external demand, or alternative financing structure that would justify overriding the missing price and venture-moment gates. Nor is there evidence of auction pressure or compulsory serial fundraising. One verifiable condition would reopen diligence: a disclosed seed financing package below $10 million post-money that fully funds a dated, measurable paid-cohort experiment and a costed route to break-even, including human review. That would let me assess what scarce capital buys and what happens if no next round arrives.
