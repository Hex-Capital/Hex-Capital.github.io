# Lark -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition by a distributor ERP vendor. The buyer rationale is to add purchasing automation that pulls in email, CRM, and files and writes approved orders back to the ERP. Interline Hardware reported a 20% decline in inventory value and a drop from 70 to 10 team hours for reorder preparation and review. The detailed case study describes those hours as weekly, while Lark’s own post describes the savings as monthly. The inventory result gives a buyer a reason to investigate, and the conflicting time figures limit how confidently it can value the labor savings. The Idea, Market & Traction.

The operating milestone is multiple paid deployments across ERP environments that reproduce the inventory or labor benefit with limited custom work. Interline received initial recommendations in under a month, and Lark reports that most setups take one to two months. That supports a limited test over the next 12–18 months, an underwriting assumption. The main capital needs are integration engineering, customer onboarding, and validation of ERP writes. Revenue, deployment costs, and available runway remain unknown. Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts.

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check buys 5% at a $10 million post-money valuation and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale scenarios below assume an eligible holder sells the full stake for cash within eight years and receives 3% of distributable equity after debt and deal adjustments, with no further diversion under the preference waterfall.

| Scenario | Assumed proceeds bridge | Holder cash |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in debt and deal adjustments leaves $35M | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or payout | $0 |

The sale values are sensitivities. The memo supplies no buyer price range or Lark revenue to anchor them. Even in the strong case, selling half the stake would yield $1.35 million. Customer deployments support technical integration, while contract and IP transfer rights, deal funding, and holder eligibility remain unverified. The memo supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route. The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts.

### Countercase

Epicor already offers overlapping automation inside Prophet 21. If native tools cover enough of the workflow, an ERP vendor could build around the remaining gaps while avoiding Lark’s customer-specific mapping and the risks of automated ERP writes. That weakens the reason to buy and leaves the Pass gate untriggered. The evidence establishes overlap, while full substitution and a buyer price ceiling remain unresolved. Defensibility, Key Risks.

### Decisive diligence question

Would a distributor ERP vendor pay enough for Lark’s repeatable, paid cross-system deployments to leave at least $83.3 million in distributable equity within eight years? A buyer-side comparison of acquisition versus internal development, grounded in verified customer savings and deployment costs and supporting a price range that clears the hurdle after deal deductions, would move the action to Reach out.
