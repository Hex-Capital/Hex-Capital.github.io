# Raven -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Raven’s strongest path is New Economies. Its prototype demonstrated flight search and calendar changes through voice and tool use. Raven says its first test handsets reached early adopters in October. Usmanov also previously launched an email and calendar assistant. That progress supports his ability to build and field-test the product, while final hardware specifications and a commercial ship date remain open. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

The current delivery cost is the user’s time switching apps, gathering context, and completing tasks manually. In a plausible scenario, Raven makes low-value cross-app tasks worth doing routinely for people who currently leave them undone. The memo gives no baseline for manual effort or comparison of cost per correctly completed task after approvals, corrections, and retries. Raven reports more than 1,000 inbound contacts after the prototype debut. The $199 refundable preorder establishes the offer, and the memo gives no order count or evidence of renewal at the projected $99–$199 monthly price. Each additional active membership would need to cover handset cost over its service life, cellular service, inference, support, and returns. The memo provides pricing but no costs or retention data to establish contribution per member. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Raven may save time on work users already do while comparable agents make a dedicated handset unnecessary. Galaxy S26 already supports delegation for some multistep tasks, and Phone Harness offers agent control on existing phones. Saved workflows could make Raven stickier, but the memo shows no preference under comparable functionality or control of a scarce hardware input. The overlap leaves both market opening and value capture unproven. The alternatives cover parts of Raven’s workflows, and the memo documents no user switching at parity, so the evidence supports Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent users renew Raven at its intended price because it makes previously skipped work economical even when their existing phones have agents with comparable model capabilities? A paid cohort should log which tasks users previously skipped, compare cost per correctly completed task across manual work, Raven, and an agent on their existing phone, then track renewal after the introductory period. Repeat use of newly viable workflows and paid renewal under that comparison would move the action to Reach out. Comparable outcomes and ready switching to existing phones would close the path and move the action to Pass.
