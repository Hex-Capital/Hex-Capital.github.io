# FlowManual -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

David Shijoon Bae is the named CEO, but FlowManual’s founders entered construction through hundreds of interviews rather than years spent inside a contractor’s back office. That pairing is FlowManual’s signature: unusually clean operating roles wrapped around an analytically discovered problem. FlowManual may build disciplined software, but I do not yet see the contrarian biography that makes David Bae unable *not* to build it.

Anduril is the high rung for unambiguous authority: Palmer Luckey’s conviction and ownership of the mission remain legible even when the category becomes politically or technically difficult. FlowManual shares the visible CEO/CTO split—David Bae leads while Michael Lin builds—but FlowManual provides no evidence of majority operating authority, founder vesting, or a conflict protocol. A title is a useful sign; a tiebreaker tested under pressure is the thing I buy.

GameCrush is the warning rung for founder origin: when an autobiographical relationship to the problem is not the durable center, product DNA becomes negotiable when investors or circumstances apply pressure. FlowManual’s Harvard engineering backgrounds and hundreds of contractor interviews show hustle, but FlowManual still looks like two talented visitors learning construction from conversations. The DeepMind exception does not rescue FlowManual yet either: Michael Lin’s research paper and credited work during Dijie’s growth from $3,000 to $336,000 MRR are promising Tiz and Biz, but not evidence of Hassabis-level polymath compounding.

Document Crunch has raised at least $37 million, ConCntric at least $10 million, and BuildingConnected already reaches more than 700,000 construction professionals. Over the next 18 months, FlowManual’s “magically weird” potential therefore depends on company-specific price books compounding faster than competitors can copy clause extraction and bid leveling. FlowManual chose an unsexy back office, which I like, but FlowManual is still an Anthropic-powered AI workflow entering a visibly funded category—not Crusoe stapling three supposedly uninvestable worlds together.

Anthropic supplies FlowManual’s underlying model, while FlowManual’s own terms require users to verify AI output. The capital-intensive or regulated score flips only if FlowManual moves into a harder layer—procurement payments, contractor credit, insurance, compliance liability, or field-connected hardware—where underwriting data and regulatory permission compound. FlowManual today offers encrypted, account-isolated SaaS for PDFs; AES-256-GCM is responsible plumbing, not a frontier moat.

Niantic could be played before an investment memo existed; the artifact itself supplied the conviction. FlowManual’s free hosted plan creates a weaker but real parallel: a contractor can upload a specification, inspect source-linked clauses, and confirm exclusions without buying a sales promise. FlowManual is observable software, but FlowManual cannot yet be ridden, scanned, photographed, or encountered accidentally in the physical world. It is a window onto construction, not yet an object inside construction.

BuildingConnected’s 700,000-professional network makes breadth a hostile battlefield for a two-person entrant. By July 2029, FlowManual will have narrowed from an all-in-one construction back office to bid-to-contract reconciliation and will remain below $3 million ARR rather than become the contractor system of record. FlowManual’s clear CEO/CTO split can execute that focused product, but FlowManual’s borrowed model and interview-derived domain knowledge make proprietary workflow discovery slower than incumbent bundling.

$500,000 from YC after roughly one month of development, hundreds of contractor interviews, a working free product, and Michael Lin’s association with growth from $3,000 to $336,000 MRR form the strongest bull case for FlowManual. The HQ Trivia exception stings: a product with immediate, observable pull can override weak structural signals, and FlowManual’s clause-linked confirmations could become that addictive artifact for estimators. FlowManual has disclosed no repeat usage, paying contractor, revenue, or proprietary dataset proving that pull, so the exception has not activated. Ten paying contractors uploading documents weekly for six consecutive months, alongside at least $500,000 ARR, would flip my verdict because FlowManual would then be showing lived workflow ownership through behavior rather than biography.
