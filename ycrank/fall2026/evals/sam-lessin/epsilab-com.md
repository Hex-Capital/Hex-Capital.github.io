# Epsilab -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Epsilab’s proposed post-money valuation, competing offers, and use of proceeds are undisclosed. I cannot tell whether my check buys an unpopular belief cheaply or joins an adequately financed category. YC backing establishes neither. The underwriting question is what Epsilab can own that its AI-lab customers cannot build internally or purchase through HUD’s environment marketplace. Turning real workflows into executable training environments could create a valuable asset. Building environments on commission could also create a services business whose customers capture most of the upside.

The product description identifies the right technical problems: versioned states, tasks, rewards, verifiers, and failure replay for long-horizon and multiplayer agents. METR’s reported benchmark saturation and cheating failures make reliable environments consequential. But difficulty alone does not establish proprietary supply. The dossier documents neither exclusive workflow rights nor evidence that Epsilab’s verifiers resist exploitation. HUD offers competing suppliers a marketplace; Prime Intellect bundles environments with training and compute. Epsilab needs a reason buyers must return to its particular environments after the tooling becomes widely available.

The strongest argument against passing is the team’s demonstrated ability to ship and sell. Hu and Peng previously built Shortbread, where Peng’s biography reports $500K revenue and 2M episodes sold. Hu also shipped talk2arxiv, with 531 GitHub stars. Those are operating receipts, and they matter more than four impressive biographies. Epsilab also resembles the translation layers I like: connecting messy operational workflows with the formal requirements of model training. The missing bridge is documented access to the workflow owners and purchasing AI labs. Prior consumer-product execution earns founder credit; it does not establish that access or become Epsilab revenue.

I would separate the functioning business from the AI option here. There is no disclosed Epsilab revenue, paid pilot, renewal, margin, or burn figure from which to underwrite the base business. Its 46 LinkedIn followers establish no purchase behavior. That is normal informational scarcity at pre-seed, not evidence of failed conversion or bad economics, so I use the missing-evidence defaults. Nor does the dossier establish an auction, equal-founder deadlock, or dependence on agent errors disappearing. The relevant red-flag test is whether better models erase the supplier’s value or increase demand for its proprietary environments; Epsilab has not yet demonstrated which mechanism dominates.

I pass at **30/100**. The exceptions that could override my missing price and capital-plan gates require hard evidence—customer conversion, durable cash economics, proprietary access, or an independently validated asset. Prior shipping makes the founders credible enough to investigate, but supplies no such override for this business. There is also no disclosed round-funded experiment with a metric and deadline; I will not invent one from the product roadmap. My one verifiable flip condition is a named AI lab’s paid renewal for Epsilab environments built from exclusively licensed workflows, with positive contribution margin after construction, maintenance, and verification costs. That would establish that owned access causes repeat purchasing and gives this business something concrete to finance.
