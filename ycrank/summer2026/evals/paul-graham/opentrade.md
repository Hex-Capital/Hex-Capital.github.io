# OpenTrade -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Ojas Kandhare’s 26 public GitHub repositories and shipped CruiseAI app make OpenTrade hacker-rich, while OpenTrade’s disclaimer that it neither executes trades nor acts as an adviser makes it schlep-poor. That pairing is the signature: OpenTrade has founders who build, but they have constructed a finance product just outside the unpleasant part of finance. It resembles a clever game built near a hard business more than a hard business disguised as a game.

**Stripe** (2010) is the high rung for hacker genealogy: the Collisons had already built Auctomatic, hacked commerce infrastructure, and left unmistakable technical tracks. OpenTrade shares the artifact-first mechanism through CruiseAI, Ojas’s repositories, and Tario You’s 2025 paper on LLMs and portfolio optimization. OpenTrade diverges because Ojas’s leading repository has one star, Tario has no public repositories, and neither founder has yet shipped a technical artifact that other hackers visibly adopted. These are real hackers, but not yet hacker magnets.

**Kiko** (2005) exposed what happens when a technically competent team builds pure software without embracing a sufficiently durable schlep: timing can erase the product before determination has anything solid to grip. OpenTrade shows a sharper version of that weakness. OpenTrade sits beside brokerage, advisory, and compliance, yet explicitly keeps real-money functionality unavailable and conditions advisory services on future registration and approval. Coinbase’s paperwork was the business. OpenTrade has currently drawn a boundary around the equivalent work.

OpenTrade’s reported 5x user growth in five days, 3x weekly-active growth in three days, campus-ambassador recruiting, and public hunt for beta testers could compound into animal resourcefulness over the next 18 months if the founders keep inventing cheap distribution loops. But OpenTrade gives no absolute user counts, repeat-use cohorts, revenue, or cereal-box stunt. Shipping web games and an iOS app before fundraising beats a deck-first team; multiplying an undisclosed baseline does not yet prove the founders can make reality yield when ordinary distribution stops working.

Tario You’s quantitative-finance research gives OpenTrade credible proximity to investing, but OpenTrade never says that Tario or Ojas built the product to solve a problem they personally suffered. The score would flip upward if OpenTrade could show that the founders repeatedly used an earlier version to make their own investment decisions, then discovered friends demanding it without prompting. Dropbox began with Drew Houston forgetting his USB stick. “Young investors need a better interface” is an observation; it is not yet an organic startup idea.

**Reddit** (2005) had founders who knew one another and built something they themselves wanted to read. OpenTrade has the right numerical configuration—two founders, plus a three-person total headcount—and both founders attended the University of Washington. But OpenTrade provides no evidence that Ojas and Tario roomed, worked, or hacked together for years. A shared university is geography, not chemistry, so I would leave this question for the in-person animal test.

Invstr already combines fantasy investing with brokerage, while StockGro has raised $112.01 million to combine education, advice, and social investing. My 36-month prediction is that by July 2029 OpenTrade will have abandoned “the new interface for investing,” pivoted into a campus or enterprise financial-literacy game, and remain below $1 million ARR. OpenTrade’s strongest capability is producing engaging software and shareable competition; OpenTrade’s weakest is accepting the regulated grind required to turn engagement into an investing business.

OpenTrade’s 100K-view video, 1,941 LinkedIn followers, working products, and rapid reported growth form the strongest opposing case: consumer loops can compound suddenly, and Airbnb taught me not to reject a strange-looking product when resourceful founders keep shipping. The objection stings because OpenTrade may discover that friend challenges themselves are the unscalable wedge, with regulation postponed rather than avoided. But the July product-position discontinuity, missing denominators, absent revenue, and lack of founder-pain evidence win today. OpenTrade would flip me to Invest by demonstrating 10,000 weekly active users with at least 30% eight-week cohort retention on the current market-drills product.
