# Cyclon -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Cyclon asks me to finance a replacement phone before establishing what scarce capital would prove. Post-money valuation: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. Whether my money would be the first genuinely scarce check: unknown. YC admission establishes none of those things, and it does not establish an auction either. I use my missing-evidence defaults for both financing terms and the venture moment: the dossier supplies neither a round price nor a funded experiment with a deadline and a pass/fail result. “Build an AI phone” describes a product ambition. What specific uncertainty does my check resolve?

The economic question is why Cyclon needs to own the hardware to own the customer relationship. Its agents would operate existing apps through users’ accounts; its personalized apps could eventually embed the product in daily routines. But permission to access someone else’s service is not proprietary distribution. Brain already reached Japanese retail through SoftBank in April 2026, and Nothing already offers natural-language app creation. A detachable camera distinguishes the proposed device without establishing why consumers will switch phones. Cyclon has to show that integrating the device changes purchase behavior enough to justify the additional physical execution.

The strongest counterargument is the team. Kiefer has worked on Android at Google; Zinebi combines microtechnology training with research on LLMs and robotic agents, and previously sold music-generation models. There is a plausible bridge between operating systems, physical devices, and agents here. That fits my pattern of backing people who connect systems, including operationally difficult physical ones. The model sales are an actual commercial receipt, so I give the founders more than the biography default. But the dossier does not document their delivery of a consumer handset or a deployed agent workflow. Their backgrounds support taking the experiment seriously; they do not establish its outcome.

Cyclon’s early-access list and invitation-controlled demos could become a direct customer relationship. Today, there is no documented conversion or repeat use, so owned distribution receives its default—not a deduction for being pre-seed. Likewise, prototype-purchase terms establish a possible transaction, but disclose no price, margin, burn, or funded route to break-even; economics receives its default. My AI concern is specific: competing phones already address the same interface demand, while Cyclon has not demonstrated an owned workflow that survives those alternatives improving. The dossier does not claim errors disappear or another financing is mandatory, so I will not manufacture those red flags.

I pass on the evidence available. The exceptions that could overcome my financing gates require something concrete: demonstrated conversion and retention, unusually strong external purchase demand, or a financing structure backed by prior operating results. Cyclon supplies none of those overrides yet. My one condition for reopening is a completed, fixed-budget prototype trial showing that 25 paying customers use Cyclon as their primary phone for 90 days, with positive contribution after inference, support, and hardware costs. That would test the crucial proposition: whether owning the whole phone produces a durable paying relationship. I would then have a business result to price, rather than an interface thesis to admire.
