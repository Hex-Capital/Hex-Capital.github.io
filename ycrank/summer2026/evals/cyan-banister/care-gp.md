# Care GP -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Care GP has one named CEO and 400 clinic operators, making Care GP a governed operating machine whose weakest signal is the human origin underneath it. Melvin Chen was a healthcare-technology investor, operator, and commercial lead, but the dossier never gives me the physical moment when clinic administration became *his* problem. Care GP has Biz and some Tiz; the missing piece is the Rizz I cannot learn from an Oxford valedictorian line or 18,000 LinkedIn followers.

Anduril had Palmer Luckey’s unmistakable authority carrying forward from hardware he had already shipped; one person could break the tie because one person embodied the product. Care GP similarly names Chen as Founder and CEO, with no shared title or visible co-founder conflict. That puts Care GP close to the high rung on leadership clarity, but not on governance: there is no evidence of majority operating authority, founder vesting, or an exit-conflict protocol. A title is a signal, not a tiebreaker agreement.

The consensus YC AI-agent darling fails when credentialed founders discover a market in a memo and wrap a general model around it. That failure mechanism is uncomfortably present in Care GP: Oxford, Goldman Sachs, private equity, then 150 provider interviews. Chen did work inside healthcare technology, so Care GP sits above the pure tourist-founder archetype, but interviewing clinicians is not the same as spending years at a reception desk finding a pathology result misfiled under the wrong patient. Care GP processed three million documents; I still cannot tell whether Chen was *of* this place or merely unusually diligent while visiting it.

Care GP’s six hours of document work reduced to under 30 minutes is how physical-world verifiability compounds over the next 18 months. Every corrected referral, answered call, and avoided filing queue gives Care GP observable proof inside a clinic, while three million processed documents can improve allocation and integrations. Care GP is not as tactile as riding Uber or playing Niantic’s Ingress—a normal person cannot install Samantha within 48 hours—but a practice manager can watch yesterday’s paper pile disappear. That is real enough to matter.

Heidi Health has raised nearly US$100 million, Lyrebird Health A$12 million, and VoiceCare AI US$4.5 million, so Care GP is not magically weird merely because the agents are named Samantha and Veronica. Care GP would move into my Invest tier on this dimension if sophisticated investors were passing because autonomous filing was considered too dangerous, while 400 clinics kept expanding usage anyway. The dossier instead shows a crowded healthcare-AI category, YC admission, 5,176 company followers, and no evidence that Care GP’s round is underpriced. This is a rebuttal, not a hard no—but “AI agents for healthcare operations” is already a sentence investors know how to love.

Mindbloom is the useful single-anchor parallel: regulation makes an aesthetically awkward workflow harder to enter and more valuable once trust is earned. Care GP has ISO 27001 certification, Australian data residency, multi-factor authentication, and federal plus state health-privacy obligations; Care GP also carries a 5%–20% human edit rate where the wrong document can become a clinical event. The regulated grime is attractive, but Care GP remains software riding Best Practice Premier rather than a frontier artifact with a compounding hardware or scientific moat.

Care GP will reach A$15–25 million ARR within 36 months while remaining independent, driven by expansion from document allocation into calls, billing, and multi-site management rather than by a foundational AI moat. Care GP already reports 400 clinic operators, 85% pilot conversion, zero churn, profitability, and largely organic demand; a clear CEO can turn that distribution wedge into a durable Australian healthcare-operations business. I am never playing the game I seem to be playing: the asset is not Samantha’s model, but the permission to sit inside the clinic’s administrative bloodstream.

Care GP’s strongest bear case is that Heidi or Lyrebird bundles document allocation into a broader suite, Best Practice Premier changes integration access, and the 5%–20% edit rate prevents autonomous operation. Care GP’s three million processed documents and 400 operators beat that case today, but they do not prove exclusive data or structural switching costs. I would write a small check only if diligence verifies that Chen personally owns the operating tiebreaker and that cohort data shows paid clinic retention above 90% after twelve months; failure on either condition flips Care GP to a pass, because neither the HQ Trivia product-love exception nor the HandUp biographical exception applies.
