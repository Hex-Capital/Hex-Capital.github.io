# Roma -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Senderek has built Loom and worked on Dropbox mobile products. Eichenwald built Senic and has shipped another app with Senderek. These are actions worth underwriting. Roma adds a working consumer product: Mac distribution, an iPhone app, and an October 2026 launch with actual user feedback. I give the founders meaningful credit for shipping. Their previous products establish consumer-product judgment; they establish less about reliably executing someone else’s work across email, calendars, and browsers.

The decisive question is what accumulates when Roma completes a task. Today, the dossier establishes a useful task record and access to external tools. It establishes no proprietary execution model, exclusive data asset, or demonstrated switching cost. OpenAI supplies models and Composio supplies connection infrastructure. Those dependencies do not prove the product is trivial, but the team must create something durable above them. Todoist already connects tasks to outside AI agents. Keeping the task and its result together is a product improvement competitors can recognize.

Roma fits my pattern of turning individual knowledge into scalable output: a user expresses intent once, and an Operator works in the background. But the compounding mechanism remains private to that user, and even that improvement is unmeasured. One customer’s completed task has no demonstrated benefit for the next customer. MCP access supplies interoperability; it does not establish a customer-created network. The initial wedge could reach many kinds of personal and professional work, but the cited $7.11 billion assistant market supplies little evidence about which adjacent buyers Roma can economically serve. The free download and absence of disclosed pricing leave value capture at the pre-seed default.

The strongest objection to passing is that these founders may make delegation feel natural before larger companies understand the interface. Task history could become the place users trust with their intentions, giving Roma a durable relationship even as execution models change. My exception for a compelling prototype before observable network effects is relevant here: the team has shipped, and nine App Store ratings average 5.0. But those ratings do not establish repeated delegation, exceptional capital efficiency, or accumulated product taste that improves with usage. The other exception mechanisms—an already-forming communication network or extraordinary recruiting evidence—are absent. Browser verification blocks and user-completed payments also leave an important part of the promised delegation unfinished.

I would pass with my own money at this point. Nothing disclosed triggers an integrity or financing-pressure veto; the issue is the missing compounding asset. Roma can become useful while remaining vulnerable to whichever assistant already owns the customer relationship. My flip condition is a controlled cohort study showing that Roma’s accumulated execution history materially increases successful unattended task completion over the same underlying models without that history, with a reproducible advantage that grows over time. That would turn stored tasks into evidence of an asset.
