# WonderTx -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I need are missing: proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether YC is genuinely the first scarce check. Financing silence therefore earns 6/28, not a presumption that this is cheap. The capital plan is also absent. WonderTx describes an ambitious company, but not the specific experiment my money purchases, its deadline, or the result that would dramatically reprice the business. “Advance the platform” is not a venture moment.

The strongest evidence is technical. WonderTx reports experimentally confirmed binders across four structurally diverse targets with no on-target training data, including evidence extending to crystallographic structures, while its agent workflows reduced compound-selection time by 10×. That is materially better than an AI-drug-discovery story built around retrospective benchmarks. But the experiment already performed is not the experiment this round would finance, and a binder is several biological cliffs away from an orally available drug: selectivity, bioavailability, toxicity, manufacturability, and clinical efficacy remain unproven. I would want one named target, one dated development milestone, and an explicit pass/fail endpoint.

The founder case is exceptional. Abraham Heifets co-founded Atomwise, which raised more than $220 million and signed partnerships advertised at over $5 billion; Saulo de Oliveira has operated across cheminformatics, structural biology, machine learning, and biotech; Cameron Ferroni built Xbox Live and later worked at Atomwise. This is not résumé adjacency. The team has shipped computational products, participated in pharmaceutical partnerships, and operated around experimental drug discovery. It is the clearest outsider-insider translation layer in the dossier and the main reason I take the technical claims seriously.

The bull case is that those receipts plus four validated targets justify overlooking normal pre-seed omissions. The platform has also been used in partner programs, which could represent the beginning of proprietary workflow access. But the exception does not clear yet: WonderTx discloses no partner, paid pilot, upfront payment, repeat program, exclusive dataset, or retained usage. A potential design-make-test data flywheel is not yet a dark pool. The partner-program evidence earns partial credit, not the causal purchase proof required to override unknown pricing and capital use.

Competition makes that distinction decisive. Terray reports more than five billion measured target-ligand interactions, Iambic is already clinical-stage, and Isomorphic Labs has raised roughly $2.7 billion. WonderTx may genuinely extrapolate better, but the dossier has not shown that its advantage survives competitors buying more experiments or the next model release narrowing the algorithmic gap. Its 10× workflow metric is useful internally; without a customer action or drug-quality endpoint, it risks becoming the kind of AI metric that measures motion rather than value.

Now delete the next financing. There is no disclosed revenue, partnership economics, gross margin, burn, runway, or route to break-even. Developing proprietary therapeutics is structurally capital-intensive, while a partner-funded discovery business could be much more durable—but WonderTx has not yet shown which company it is building. The physical design-make-test loop fits my preference for software reorganizing difficult real-world systems; the unstated contradiction is that this version may still require a serial capital factory to cross every biological milestone.

I am passing on the present evidence, not dismissing the team or the technical result. One verifiable condition flips me: a signed pharmaceutical partnership with a meaningful upfront cash payment tied to delivering a named target milestone by a fixed date. That would simultaneously show that the workflow causes a purchase, define the venture moment, and establish a base business beneath the speculative drug-pipeline option.
