# Torus -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: multimodal models can now inspect engineering packages across drawings, specifications, and datasheets; EPCs and capital-project owners are beginning to buy; and Torus becomes large only if its consistency engine prevents enough rework to justify deployment across every project rather than remaining a document-review pilot.

The controlling belief is therefore measurable: project-wide parameter checking must catch expensive inconsistencies with sufficiently high precision that customers trust Torus before construction decisions become irreversible. The dossier identifies the mechanism—a potential $500K rework order, more than 50 check categories, source provenance, and human approval—but supplies neither an accuracy threshold nor evidence of avoided costs. That is better than a generic AEC-AI narrative, but it does not yet clear the market-first gate with conviction.

The product is substantially more interesting than a chatbot. Torus extracts parameters, maps relationships, checks the complete project corpus, and drafts RFIs, approvals, transmittals, and follow-ups. Better models should expand the documents and discrepancies it can process while Torus retains workflow state and responsibility for coordinating the result. The missing pieces are deployed integrations, accumulated approval histories, and evidence that customer-specific parameter maps are difficult to reproduce. Until those exist, the claimed moat is prospective.

There is genuine pre-seed pull. Multiple Fortune 500 companies and major EPCs are reportedly deploying Torus on data-center and energy-infrastructure projects, an informative signal even without disclosed revenue. The founders are also unusually relevant: Marcus Lima previously built a capital-intensive energy company after studying energy systems, while Rahul Thayil previously founded and financed an AI risk-intelligence company. This is repeat-founder and domain learning, not résumé decoration. The strongest bull case is that these deployments are already the beginning of a reusable enterprise channel through which Torus can add design review, compliance, procurement, and change-management products.

The bear case still wins today. The economic change is asserted rather than demonstrated: AEC AI adoption reached 27% in 2025, but the dossier does not show reduced review time, avoided rework, improved project margins, or paid expansion. Meanwhile, Trunk Tools, Transcend, Augmenta, and SWAPP have disclosed more than $147 million across adjacent workflows. This category is crystallizing, and Torus has not shown the market-ending variable that separates it from those funded competitors. The frontier-research exception does not apply; this is an application workflow whose success must ultimately appear in customer economics.

My 36-month base case is that Torus reaches $4–10 million ARR concentrated in data-center and energy EPC deployments, but has not yet established a defensible category leadership position. I would monitor rather than invest now. The single flip condition is two named EPC customers expanding from paid pilots to at least three projects each while documenting more than 10 times annual contract value in avoided rework or engineering-review savings.
