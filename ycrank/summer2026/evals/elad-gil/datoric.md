# Datoric -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Multimodal AI labs now buy rights-clean, project-specific human-interaction data because public contributor channels create integrity and consent risk; Datoric becomes large only if provenance converts bespoke collections into repeat buyer relationships.

The strongest evidence is demand before narrative polish. Nearly seven figures of revenue in the 30 days preceding August 2026 is exceptional pre-seed pull, even discounted as company-reported and potentially concentrated. The 300,000 active contributors and completed 250,000-trace, 15,000-hour computer-use dataset show delivery capacity rather than a waitlist. The governing belief is therefore testable: labs that purchase one secured collection must return for additional modalities. Datoric has supplied evidence adjacent to that variable, but no customer count, retention, or repeat-purchase data establishes it yet.

The market change is real but only partly proprietary. Meta’s $14.3 billion investment for 49% of Scale AI created an institutional opening for independent suppliers, while the training-dataset market is projected to grow from $3.2 billion in 2025 to $16.3 billion by 2033. However, independence is a channel, not a margin structure. Scale, Turing, Mercor, and Prolific already have large contributor networks and operating resources. Datoric discloses neither gross margin nor the labor required per delivered hour, so nearly seven figures could represent attractive infrastructure economics or a compressed, service-heavy project.

This is more durable than a model wrapper. Private capture applications, isolated customer projects, specification-based QA, contributor-linked samples, and reviewable consent and licensing records form an end-to-end collection workflow. Better multimodal models should increase demand for difficult human-interaction data rather than absorb this function. The weakness is reproducibility: Datoric has not shown exclusive contributors, buyer integrations, accumulated evaluation data, or qualification histories that a funded competitor could not rebuild.

The founders have unusually direct attack-path knowledge. Nikhil Reddy previously automated more than 15,000 hours of annotation work by bypassing platform QA, while Jeffrey Lin brings robotics and benchmark experience. That informs the product architecture. Their earlier Arzule project also shows they have built together, but the unexplained Winter-to-Summer 2026 transition cuts both ways: it may be rapid learning, or it may indicate opportunistic movement between markets. There is no documented prior exit, personal sacrifice, or other evidence of the desperation override I would normally credit.

The painful bear case is that one lab generated nearly all reported revenue through a custom collection, contributor activity is transaction-dependent, and provenance becomes a required feature that larger platforms copy. The gated Hugging Face repository’s zero likes and one organizational follower provide no evidence of organic distribution, while the absence of disclosed repeat purchases leaves the first product’s cross-sell channel unproven. None of the usual exception logic resolves that risk: this is not frontier research requiring a talent override, nor are new unit economics documented. I would nevertheless invest a small personal check because actual revenue, operational scale, and a substantive workflow clear the market and product gates. My 36-month base case is a $25 million–$50 million ARR independent multimodal-data supplier, not a general labeling winner. I flip to pass if, across the next two quarters, returning buyers account for less than 40% of revenue.
