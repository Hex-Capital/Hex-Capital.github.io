# Belvedir -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. A small company does not rent one generic intelligence forever; it owns a private model that learns its workflows, improves from every production trace, and becomes cheaper as it becomes more specific. Belvedir has assembled several necessary steps—trace collection, benchmark creation, reinforcement-learning updates, memory optimization, and private deployment—and its few alpha customers reportedly see up to 10x lower inference costs and 2x higher benchmark scores. The production trace is a plausible little leak from that future. What is missing is the map: which behaviors change, what technical and institutional transitions must occur, and why this company controls the path rather than packaging capabilities that infrastructure vendors will absorb.

Zachary Yu is the strongest part of the bet. He previously founded and served as CTO of Traverse, where he built reinforcement-learning environments, after engineering work at Mercor. Belvedir therefore looks less like a Summer 2026 category pivot than a second attempt at a problem Yu had already been inhabiting. That is real Tizz. But the dossier shows no public technical artifacts, no history of recruiting complementary talent, and a team of one. I see an accountable leader—good—but not yet evidence that he can recruit the people required to operate private training systems safely across many customer environments.

The interesting broken rule is that smaller companies must choose between static fine-tuning APIs and expensive forward-deployed model teams. Belvedir proposes to automate the latter while leaving customers in possession of their weights and memory. That could return meaningful technical control. But who captures the rent today, how large is it, and which SMB employee gains agency? The service is free in closed alpha, has no published pricing, and has not quantified the labor or expense it replaces. “Private AI for SMBs” is still a category claim; the dossier has not exposed the system economics beneath it.

The bull case stings. A founder with directly relevant reinforcement-learning experience has put a functioning loop in front of real customers, and the reported 10x cost reduction and 2x benchmark gain are unusually concrete for pre-seed. If those results recur without Yu manually tending each deployment, the product could compound customer-specific benchmarks and integration history faster than general infrastructure vendors can commoditize it. I do not penalize the absence of revenue. I do distinguish reported outcomes from costly behavior: there are no named customers, paid conversions, retention periods, repeated training cycles, or users risking production workloads in a documented way.

Belvedir also sits beneath the visible application wave, but it does not yet control a scarce enabling layer. Fireworks, Together AI, Baseten, and Applied Compute already span training, post-training, private deployment, inference, and feedback loops with between $160 million and $1.505 billion raised. Customer ownership of the resulting intelligence is attractive, yet it may limit Belvedir’s own compounding asset. Meanwhile, prompts, tool outputs, repository URLs, and credentials-related configuration can pass through outside infrastructure and model providers. Encryption, hashed keys, row-level controls, redaction, and training exclusions are responsible alpha mitigations; they are not yet a custody architecture strong enough for a product asking customers to surrender their most revealing operational exhaust.

My 36-month expectation is that Belvedir becomes a forward-deployed model-optimization service for mid-market companies at roughly $1 million to $3 million ARR, rather than the autonomous self-serve SMB infrastructure described today. That can be a good business, but it is not yet the six-years-ahead enabling layer I would fund with my own money. I would flip to Invest on one verifiable result: three named SMB customers renewing annual self-serve contracts after independently reproducing at least a 5x inference-cost reduction, with no Belvedir engineer operating their training loop.
