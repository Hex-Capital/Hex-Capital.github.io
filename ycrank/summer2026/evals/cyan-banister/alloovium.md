# Alloovium -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Zander Schweitzer operated construction equipment and worked as a site engineer before founding Alloovium, but Alloovium’s product remains an invisible software layer across SharePoint, Procore, Aconex, and Outlook. That high-founder-origin, low-tactile-artifact pairing is Alloovium’s signature: a founder who is *of* the construction site building something the people around him may value, yet nothing I can ride, photograph, or encounter in the wild. I have one foot in love with the biography, not yet with the object.

Anduril is the high rung because Palmer Luckey shipped Oculus before carrying his hardware instincts into defense. Zander gives Alloovium the same load-bearing mechanism—direct contact with the domain before discovering a market—but Zander is a recent civil-engineering graduate and former site engineer, not yet someone who shipped an adjacent product at scale. Deploying Alloovium across A$400 million of active projects, including a BMD subsidiary, moves Zander well above a pitch-deck tourist; the missing prior-product trajectory keeps Alloovium below the Anduril rung.

Trunk Tools has raised $70 million, Document Crunch at least $30.5 million, and Procore generated $1.3 billion in 2025 revenue, so Alloovium is not arriving before anyone sees construction AI. Over 18 months, Alloovium’s builder-specific IMS checks and project correction histories could compound into an ugly, valuable implementation moat, but every deployment also teaches funded competitors where to bundle next. Construction compliance is unsexy enough to earn Alloovium some magical-weird credit; “AI across your existing systems” is consensus enough to prevent the spell from fully working.

Zander Schweitzer is explicitly CEO and Cielo Nicolosi CTO, after Zander recruited Cielo and the two moved cities together in early 2026. Alloovium would move up a tier if Zander held documented tiebreaker authority, founder vesting, and an exit-conflict protocol; titles alone do not prove that conversation happened. The HQ Trivia exception cannot rescue Alloovium because no evidence shows explosive product pull capable of temporarily overriding governance ambiguity.

SpaceX made capital intensity itself part of the moat: rockets force technical truth into the physical world. Alloovium shares the willingness to enter a consequential, liability-bearing environment—compliance errors can affect notices, claims, and contractual obligations—but Alloovium remains software running on repositories controlled by Procore, Microsoft, and Aconex. Human approval and sentence-level citations make Alloovium responsible software; they do not turn Alloovium into a frontier-hardware swing.

DeepMind failed the tactile-artifact test because its early capability lived inside an abstraction; polymath founder compounding supplied the exception. Alloovium has a better physical context—reports and compliance findings are being used across A$400 million of active construction—but the product itself is still a B2B document interface whose value must largely be reported by contractors. A normal person cannot encounter Alloovium within 48 hours the way I could play Niantic’s Ingress or notice Flock Safety hardware, and Cielo’s four-startup engineering history is not yet a DeepMind-grade polymath override.

A$400 million of active-project exposure leads me to predict that by July 2029 Alloovium will remain independent and reach A$6–10 million in ARR, concentrated among Australian head contractors buying compliance and document-control workflows. Alloovium’s mechanism is not viral magic; it is Zander’s site credibility turning one contractor into repeated project deployments while builder-specific IMS configurations raise switching costs. Useful vertical software can become a real business without becoming world peace.

Trunk Tools’ $70 million war chest supplies the strongest bear case: Procore bundles retrieval, Document Crunch owns compliance, repository APIs tighten, and Alloovium becomes a feature caught between three systems of record. That case stings because Alloovium has disclosed no revenue, retention, accuracy, or proprietary dataset scale. The evidence that narrowly wins is Alloovium’s system-agnostic deployment across a BMD subsidiary, combined with a 30-year construction auditor designing the ISO-compliance product—workflow ownership that a generic model does not automatically inherit. If none of the current A$400 million deployments converts into a paid, multi-project renewal by July 2027, I flip from Neutral to Pass.
