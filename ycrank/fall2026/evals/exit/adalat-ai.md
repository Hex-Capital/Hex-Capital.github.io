# Adalat AI -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

A strategic acquisition is the strongest plausible route. The Kerala High Court designated Adalat AI as its primary tool for recording trial-court depositions and requires approval for substitutes. Adalat AI reports about 1,100 monthly active users and more than 25,600 hours transcribed in Kerala during the mandate’s first year. A court technology buyer could value the multilingual workflow and its adoption inside courts. The memo also records Tyler Technologies’ approximately $212.5 million acquisition of For The Record, an adjacent courtroom-recording business. That deal supports the buyer rationale. Adalat AI’s purchase price remains unsubstantiated. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

The decisive barrier is getting sale proceeds to an investor. The memo identifies the operator as the grant-funded nonprofit Legsys AI Technology Foundation and documents pro bono terms in the court agreements reviewed by the tax tribunal. A buyer could pay for the technology while the holder receives no cash. A liquidity milestone would be a paid court deployment through an entity that controls the transferable product rights and can distribute sale proceeds to the holder. The grants and rollouts show capacity to deploy the product. The cost, funding, and timing of that commercial milestone remain open. The memo supplies no independent fallback. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

The eight-year benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. In the **modest case**, a hypothetical $50 million enterprise value yields $1.5 million to the holder. In the **strong case**, $100 million yields $3 million, falling to $2.4 million if debt and deal adjustments reduce distributable equity value by $20 million. Both scenarios assume an eligible holder sells the full 3% stake for cash within eight years, with no senior claims and, before the sensitivity, no debt or deal deductions. These are scenario assumptions. The documented structure provides no basis for applying that pro rata waterfall to an actual asset sale. In the **prolonged-private case**, continued operation with cash reinvested and no funded payout or eligible secondary yields zero realized proceeds. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

### Countercase

A strategic buyer could pay for access to an established multilingual courtroom workflow. The Kerala mandate makes replacement harder, and a magistrate quoted in a company testimonial reported hearing five to six witnesses per day, up from two to three. The adjacent acquisition shows that a court technology buyer can pay above the benchmark for a courtroom product. Even if that case supports a valuable asset sale, the documented nonprofit structure and pro bono agreements trigger the structural Pass gate for the holder. The conclusion would change if diligence established an enforceable path from a sale of those assets to cash for the holder. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *The Idea*)

### Decisive diligence question

What enforceable right would let the investor receive cash from a sale of Adalat AI’s courtroom technology? An executed investor instrument, ownership records for the core product, and any required transfer consents would answer it. A documented right to a material share of sale proceeds would move the action to Watch while buyer pricing is tested.
