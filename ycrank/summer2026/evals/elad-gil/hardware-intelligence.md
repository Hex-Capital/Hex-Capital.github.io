# hardware intelligence -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Rishov Sarkar built AMD AI Engine simulators, Siemens EDA compiler flows, and LightningSim, making **hardware intelligence** a founder-shipping bet whose strongest dimension is demonstrated technical artifacts and whose weakest is a visible buying curve. Athreya Anand adds agent-workflow experience from Google plus chip exposure at AWS Inferentia and Tesla. **hardware intelligence** has the domain-built profile I want, but Wave’s July 2026 launch, open pilot onboarding, and absence of public revenue do not yet show something “just working.”

**Perplexity** is the high rung for iterative shipping: Aravind repeatedly returned within a week with the discussed product built. **hardware intelligence** shares the artifact-first mechanism because Sarkar developed LightningSim and Wave already traces faulty signals, proposes fixes, reruns simulations, and displays waveform differences. **hardware intelligence** sits below Perplexity because the dossier documents prior engineering output and one product launch, not customer-driven iterations between conversations or a measured release cadence. The founders look capable of shipping; the missing evidence is whether they ship toward urgent customer behavior.

**Lyft** Series C exposed the low-rung failure mechanism: activity can be a false signal when it does not establish improving economics or structural leadership. **hardware intelligence** is only slightly above that rung on revenue velocity because booked demos and open pilot onboarding are intentions, not buying behavior; there is no public revenue, repeat usage, paid conversion, or named customer. At pre-seed I do not require a long history, but **hardware intelligence** receives the absent-evidence default because an AI product launched into six-figure-per-seat EDA workflows should be able to produce an unusually informative pilot signal quickly.

CHIA’s June 2026 implementation across commercial tools, following AiEDA’s December 2024 framework, is a credible technical catalyst for **hardware intelligence**, but the mechanism must compound over the next 18 months. Agents can now invoke simulators and EDA systems; each successful debugging cycle could shorten verification work, expose another automation target, and pull Wave deeper into the workflow. The catalyst is less complete than Stripe’s API re-opening because Synopsys introduced an LLM Copilot in 2023 and already owns customer access. **hardware intelligence** benefits from a new technical capability, but the dossier does not yet show that purchasing behavior or incumbent failure changed with it.

ChipAgents has raised $74 million and reports collaborations with NVIDIA and Ambiq, defining the counterfactual for **hardware intelligence** as an index-like layer rather than a narrow debugger. The score flips upward if Wave becomes the vendor-neutral execution and observability path through which verification agents access every simulator, repository, and waveform; then **hardware intelligence** compounds with agentic chip development itself. Today the terminal interface and waveform wedge must win share against Synopsys, Cadence, Siemens, and a heavily capitalized adjacent platform. No deployed integrations, proprietary dataset, or structural distribution advantage establishes the index position yet.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow infrastructure on top of commodity sequencing isolated the bet from chemistry risk. **hardware intelligence** similarly builds software on existing RTL, simulators, and foundation models rather than requiring new semiconductor physics. Resimulation gives **hardware intelligence** a concrete validation loop, so the core miracle is that an agent can diagnose causality accurately enough for engineers to trust it. Customer-IP constraints, incomplete test coverage, enterprise integration, and incumbent distribution remain hard execution problems, but they are not independent scientific miracles; this is substantially better architecture than a science project.

Synopsys’ existing customer control and **hardware intelligence**’s lack of public benchmarks produce my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, **hardware intelligence** reaches roughly $1 million to $3 million in ARR through a small set of verification groups but does not become an independent EDA platform, and Siemens acquires it for product and talent. The shipping strength gets Wave into real deployments; the missing velocity and index position keep **hardware intelligence** from escaping the incumbent distribution layer before bundling compresses the wedge.

Athreya Anand’s Google agent experience and Rishov Sarkar’s Siemens, AMD, and LightningSim work form the strongest opposing case: **hardware intelligence** could be a Color-style workflow exception in which deep integration, not early publicity, creates proprietary verification history and severe switching costs. Six-figure incumbent seats leave enough budget for Wave to sell on engineering time saved, while the terminal-native wedge is focused enough to outship ChipAgents. The objection stings, but **hardware intelligence** currently has no named customer, paid pilot, accuracy benchmark, or deployed integration, so domain fit is still being mistaken for distribution. Three named semiconductor customers converting to paid deployments, with usage or ARR expanding above 20% month over month for three consecutive months, would flip my verdict to Invest.
