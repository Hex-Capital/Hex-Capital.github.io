# NebuSec -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. A developer changes a dependency; a defensive system finds the newly exploitable path, proves it in isolation, and repairs it before deployment. NebuSec’s threat modeling, repository scanning, verification, and patch generation point toward that world. But those capabilities appearing on a product page do not establish the transitions between them. The dossier does not yet show a customer delegating that complete loop. I can imagine the destination; I cannot credit my daydream as the company’s evidence.

The founders have paid real technical tuition. Eten Zou’s published CVE-2022-27666 exploit predates this company and the current autonomous-security rush. Yuan Tan’s DARPA-funded program-analysis work, Frank Wu’s automated-repair leadership, and Xiaochuan Yu’s Zellic experience bring complementary skills to the same difficult problem. The team also reports $400K+ in Google vulnerability rewards. These are people who have persuaded real systems to misbehave, rather than merely learned the vocabulary of security. That is NebuSec’s strongest signal. Commercial command and recruitment beyond this research network remain less established.

PingCAP’s confirmation of a completed investigation by October 23, 2026 matters more to me than 1.7K repository stars. An enterprise trusted this team with a consequential security problem, and the team delivered findings. That is meaningful customer behavior at pre-seed. It establishes trust in researchers, however, without demonstrating repeat reliance on the autonomous product. The vendor’s 88% maximum coverage on 76 known vulnerabilities cannot close that gap; performance on unseen repositories is unresolved, and dynamic exploit verification is disabled by default. Nor does replacing a fixed-scope audit yet reveal a specific institutional rent being removed: scan quotes are disclosed, but comparative costs, delays, and control returned to developers are not.

The strongest reason to write a check anyway is that investigations could finance the harder verification machinery underneath. That fits my recurring interest in an ordinary commercial beachhead supporting a more consequential technical destination. **CosmicBrain AI** demonstrated why missing revenue need not stop me when technical obsession meets a prototype and concrete counterparties. NebuSec has the obsession and a credible counterparty; the material difference is that PingCAP validates an investigation engagement, not autonomous operation. Meanwhile, Semgrep and Aikido already combine overlapping detection and remediation capabilities. Published exploits demonstrate expertise without establishing a scarce asset those competitors must depend upon. Exclusive case-data rights and a controlled verification bottleneck are still hypotheses.

I would hold my check today. NebuSec could become an excellent research business while the enduring automation advantage accrues elsewhere. That possibility changes what I am underwriting. Source-code custody also makes the privacy gate relevant: SOC 2 readiness underway is neither proof of severe privacy problems nor proof that consent and custody are adequately bounded. Zou is identified as CEO, and nothing here establishes a leadership shell game or equal-control deadlock. My one verifiable flip condition is three unrelated enterprises renewing the product after documented, researcher-independent discovery and verification of previously unknown exploitable vulnerabilities. That would show customers buying a repeatable machine capability from these unusually qualified researchers.
