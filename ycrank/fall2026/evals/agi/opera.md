# OpEra -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**New Economies** is OpEra’s strongest path. The company describes planners rebuilding schedules by hand after disruptions. OpEra says it converts plain-language changes into constraints and reruns a site-specific optimizer. The founders’ retailer routing project reportedly cut delivery costs by 38% and increased on-time deliveries by 10% on retailer data. That supports the potential value of better plans. The change in planner labor per successful replan remains unmeasured. *(The Idea, Market & Traction)*

**Scenario assumption.** A warehouse that replans only for major disruptions because manual rebuilding consumes too much planner time could afford to replan for smaller disruptions. The comparison is cost per valid plan adopted in time to act, including manual rebuilding and review before adoption, and software, planner review, and exception handling with OpEra. The memo gives no comparable measurements or paid evidence from a previously excluded use. *(The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)*

The founders’ joint routing work and applied optimization experience support early execution. Historical backtesting and a live-data pilot give them a way to test production readiness. Each site is a plausible expansion unit. Site revenue would need to cover data mapping, constraint configuration, monitoring, and support. Pricing, integration effort, and reuse across sites remain unverified. *(Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Risks)*

### Countercase

OpEra could improve an existing planning workflow while customers continue to buy from whichever vendor can implement it economically. The memo identifies routing and scheduling APIs and commercially available language-assisted modeling. Embedded integrations and site rules could make replacement costly, but buyer preference under functional parity remains untested. The memo also identifies no scarce asset OpEra controls. The market-opening and model-parity gates remain unproven. The alternatives establish a credible substitution risk, while actual switching remains unobserved. *(Defensibility, Market & Traction)*

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent warehouse pay to keep using OpEra for disruption replanning it previously skipped because of planner labor, after comparing it with a functionally comparable alternative? A paid live pilot would change the action to Reach out if it shows a material drop in cost per timely, adopted plan after review and exceptions, and the buyer renews at a price that covers OpEra’s site delivery and support costs.
