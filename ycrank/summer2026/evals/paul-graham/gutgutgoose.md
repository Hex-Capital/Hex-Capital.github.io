# Gutgutgoose -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Leon Mojarrabi built a pharmacy reportedly doing $4 million in annual revenue, while Anis Mihrshahi is a childhood friend of roughly a decade who co-built Gutgutgoose’s mechanistic algorithm. That makes Gutgutgoose a strong-pair, weak-organic-origin startup: unusually good founder configuration, but no evidence either founder personally suffered the gut problem or first built the product for himself. Gutgutgoose has people capable of executing a difficult idea. I am less certain the idea grabbed them by the throat.

**Stripe** (2010) is the high rung for co-founder configuration: two brothers with complementary abilities, years of shared history, and commerce software already under their fingernails. Gutgutgoose shares the load-bearing part of that mechanism. Mihrshahi and Mojarrabi have known each other for a decade, while Mihrshahi supplies the modeling and Mojarrabi supplies pharmacy operations and selling. Gutgutgoose diverges where Stripe was strongest: the Collisons had shipped conspicuous hacker artifacts before Stripe, while neither Gutgutgoose founder has a public GitHub trail and only Mihrshahi is described as building the underlying technology.

**Kiko** (2005) is the low-rung warning: determined founders can still build a product whose timing and pull are too weak, and selling it on eBay does not retroactively make the idea organic. Gutgutgoose gives Mihrshahi an AI scholarship and Mojarrabi a pharmacy, but Gutgutgoose never says either founder experienced IBS, failed with generic probiotics, or compounded a personal formula before deciding to sell one. No evidence of founder-as-customer means applying the absent-evidence default. The closest exception is **Loopt** (2005), where Sam Altman’s force of personality overrode weak product timing. Gutgutgoose has real evidence of competence, but not yet an Altman-sized reason to waive the organic-idea test.

Seven paid sign-ups on the first night and 20 waitlist additions from 50 endometriosis-event attendees are small numbers, but they are the right kind of small numbers for Gutgutgoose. Leon’s $4 million pharmacy is stronger evidence of resourcefulness than another credential would be. Over 18 months, that ability compounds if Gutgutgoose keeps recruiting through clinics, patient communities, naturopaths, and local events: every unpleasant hand-sold cohort teaches Gutgutgoose which patients will pay $200 per month and return a second stool sample. The missing animal track is a specific pre-funding stunt comparable to Airbnb’s cereal boxes. Gutgutgoose has hustle, but no moment yet when the founders refused an apparently final “no.”

**Coinbase** (2012) is the clean parallel for the schlep dimension. Coinbase’s compliance burden looked like execution risk until it became an asset; Gutgutgoose has stool collection, sequencing, strain modeling, custom compounding, health-claim restrictions, and a 90-day retest loop. Most software founders would recoil from that stack. Gutgutgoose appears to be running toward it. The danger is that some schleps are merely schleps: if consumers will not complete retesting or the unpublished 16% colonization and 31% persistence claims disappear under scrutiny, Gutgutgoose gets the work without the moat.

Anis Mihrshahi co-built Gutgutgoose’s algorithm, and Gutgutgoose has filed Australian patent application 2026902584 around metagenomic profiling, metabolic modeling, optimization, and iterative verification. That is more than a deck, but it is not yet hacker genealogy. The framework draws partly on public MICOM, AGORA2, and bacterial reconstructions, while no public repository, competitive-programming history, or pre-startup technical artifact appears in the dossier. Gutgutgoose would flip this score into the top tier if Mihrshahi could show that he personally built and ran the working model before fundraising, with predictions later confirmed by the alpha cohort. A patent application and “50,000 microbiomes” are claims about an asset; a working prediction made before the result would be evidence of a hacker.

The $200-per-month launch price makes my 36-month prediction specific: Gutgutgoose will pivot from a primarily direct-to-consumer probiotic subscription into a clinic-facing formulation and retesting platform, reaching $2 million to $4 million in annual recurring revenue within 36 months. Gutgutgoose’s strong founder pair can sell and operate the clinic workflow, while its weak organic-consumer origin makes durable direct demand less likely. The 90-day verification loop becomes more valuable when a clinic ensures compliance and interprets the result.

**Florē** already sells sequencing-based custom probiotics, Viome has raised $175 million, and Gutgutgoose’s beta claims remain unpublished. The strongest bear case is painful: Gutgutgoose may be a polished personalization layer over uncertain microbiome science, with FDA and FTC boundaries preventing strong claims and a $200 monthly price destroying retest completion. That could leave Gutgutgoose wedged between commodity supplements and regulated medicine, with neither supplement margins nor drug-grade evidence. Seven opening-night purchases, five referrals, Mojarrabi’s pharmacy history, and the planned 80-person study keep me narrowly on the invest side because the founders are already touching the schlep. If that study shows no statistically significant colonization improvement, I would flip to Pass.
