# Snowstone -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Snowstone has put dollar signs on the inefficiency it finds. I still cannot price the investment. Proposed post-money, competing term sheets, and whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital are all undisclosed. YC membership does not establish an auction; Harvard does not establish one either. But I will not manufacture a cheap, overlooked deal from financing silence. The dossier also supplies no capital-funded experiment with a deadline and a measurable result. Those two unanswered questions cover more than half my underwriting.

The product has a plausible economic mechanism: read the records a portfolio company already produces, reconstruct what actually happens, and connect an operational fix to cash. Source-linked recommendations and persistent, reviewed entity matches are useful foundations. Snowstone could become the translation layer between PE’s financial objectives and the messy operating systems beneath them. That fits my preference for businesses connecting ecosystems. The missing link is ownership: permission to inspect a customer’s confidential data does not establish a proprietary asset Snowstone can compound across customers. Celonis and Skan already pursue overlapping workflow intelligence.

The strongest argument against passing is that Snowstone may already have crossed the hardest enterprise boundary: getting access. Its reported **$2B+ in transaction value reviewed** suggests more substantive activity than a demo, and **$50M+ in identified opportunities** could give a PE sponsor a reason to introduce the product across its portfolio. Baur’s Winterberg diligence and MedTech LBO work could help translate the output into an investment committee’s language. But the dossier does not establish which reviews were paid, whether customers implemented recommendations, or whether one engagement generated another. I give the analysis scope credit as evidence of activity; I cannot turn it into observed distribution or customer savings.

Accuracy determines whether that access becomes a business. Snowstone reports dollar estimates within **20% of ground truth in 66% of cases** on its private benchmark. Source citations and calculation review make errors inspectable, which matters. The commercial question is how much expert labor remains between an estimate and a recommendation someone will act on. If every engagement needs substantial financial reconciliation, Snowstone could still build a useful service, but its cash economics would depend on pricing and delivery discipline. “Payment Required” establishes neither paid demand nor a profitable delivery model. There is no margin, burn, or runway evidence to establish a path that survives deleting the next financing.

The founders earn some affirmative credit beyond credentials: Waldorf built quantum visualization tools at QuEra, while Baur performed PE diligence and modeling. That is an early technical-to-financial bridge, although neither receipt establishes repeated deployment inside portfolio-company operations. Snowstone’s AI exposure deserves scrutiny because incumbents can use improving models too; the dossier does not show the team assuming errors disappear or another round is inevitable. Nor does it establish an equal-founder governance problem. The exceptions I would consider—measured paid conversion and retention, independent acquisition validation, or a prior-exit-backed financing structure—have no documented equivalent here.

I pass on the evidence available. Snowstone’s opportunity is to turn trusted access into repeat purchases, then deliver those purchases without consuming the savings in expert labor. My one verifiable flip condition is **a PE sponsor placing a repeat paid order covering three portfolio companies, with documented positive contribution margin after all delivery and review labor**. That would establish a sponsor-driven distribution mechanism and a functioning base business, enough to reopen the investment discussion. Today, the dossier documents diagnostic capability more clearly than an investable transaction.
