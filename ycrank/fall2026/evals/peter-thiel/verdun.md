# Verdun -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Verdun’s candidate secret is that contextual machine judgment can eliminate the nuisance-alarm queue while preserving threat detection and making monitoring profitable. That is a testable proposition: compare missed threats, intervention latency, and fully loaded cost against existing services on the same footage. But who believes it cannot be done? Tai identifies delays exceeding 20 minutes and dates the enabling AI capability to roughly a year before October 2026. The dossier establishes an operating problem and a proposed solution; it supplies neither a clearly disputed belief nor results testing the proposition. The secret remains undiscovered in the evidence.

The more consequential difficulty is ownership of the solution. Hakimo already offers contextual verification and autonomous responses; Deep Sentinel combines AI with live guards and existing-camera integrations. Verdun’s CV/VLM cascade and site protocols enter that competitive field without a demonstrated technical advantage or a bounded customer segment it can dominate. Operator corrections could eventually produce proprietary data, but a feedback loop becomes a monopoly mechanism only when its accumulated information produces an advantage competitors cannot readily reproduce. Verdun has described the loop without establishing that advantage. This is affirmative competitive overlap, beyond mere silence about a moat.

The founders make the strongest case against passing. Sonny Tai built Actuate, and Tung Thanh Hoang developed its first gun-detection model: this is a team with shared operating history inside the relevant problem. The formation fits my preference for companies assembled through an existing operator network. Actuate’s reported 110,000 cameras across more than 15,000 sites make their experience substantial, although those deployments belong to Actuate. Verdun has credible founding continuity; full-time status, ownership, and economic alignment remain unverified. Nothing in the dossier establishes convenience matching, outsourced core engineering, or another adverse founding condition.

Could that experience let Verdun win through execution? Certainly. Charlie Warren’s invitation to commercial-property buyers and former customers supplies a concrete referral route, and the website names an accessible sales channel. That earns some distribution credit. It does not yet establish conversion, implementation burden, or a sales cycle compatible with contract value. Likewise, recurring monitoring is a plausible revenue engine, but the business model itself is inferred. Without documented pricing or margins, the category’s projected growth from $2.382 billion in 2024 to $5.225 billion in 2031 cannot establish the economics of this particular investment. Tai’s explicit warning about video-ingestion and model costs identifies the calculation that must work.

The strongest bull case is that experienced security operators can use contextual reasoning to remove enough human review to transform service economics before incumbents reorganize. That possibility deserves attention. It does not currently resolve the competitive gate. My exceptions for uncertain monopoly positions depend on independent technical proof, demonstrated customer behavior, or a concrete deployment path that removes the relevant risk. Verdun reports no comparable validation. YC participation, two engineering vacancies, and an industry-newsletter mention establish activity rather than that override. Nor does the dossier establish a regulatory shutdown or a collapsed technical claim; the pass rests on competitive structure.

I would pass with my own money. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: an independently audited, paid deployment cohort in a named property segment showing a proprietary tenfold reduction in fully loaded monitoring cost versus Hakimo at equivalent missed-threat rates. That would test both the operating proposition and whether Verdun possesses technology capable of changing the market’s economics. Today, the founders are more differentiated than the company’s demonstrated position.
