# Adside.ai -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Adside.ai could make running paid advertising substantially easier. The dossier does not establish what consequential cost pool becomes orders of magnitude cheaper if it succeeds. The $11.97 billion adjacent marketing-platform estimate measures a vendor market, not resources released or expertise made universally accessible. More decisively, advertising tools fall within my explicit avoidance categories. A successful business here can still be a business I would decline to own.

The product has substance: agents research competitors, generate creatives, and operate advertising accounts. But Adside’s stated differentiation combines capabilities already associated with Foreplay, SpyFu, Motion, and other vendors. Its supplier describes collecting more than 133,000 ads across 6,055 YC companies and shipping research functionality in days using reusable infrastructure. That demonstrates implementation speed. It also makes the assembled capability easier to reproduce. There is no measured discontinuity against a named competitor, and the privacy policy prevents assuming a shared-model learning advantage from customer account data. The affirmative evidence supports an integration advantage, which puts technical differentiation below my absent-evidence default.

The strongest argument against passing is the team’s demonstrated ability to build and sell software together. Robin Choy and Guillaume Miara previously built Botdog, with Miara reporting a seven-figure exit within 18 months. Their engineering and operating experience now supports 30 live companies and a workshop attracting more than 40 founders and growth practitioners. Those are meaningful pre-seed signals. An execution-heavy business can create substantial value without a scientific breakthrough. However, these facts do not document belief revision or difficult recruits mapped to specific weaknesses, so I retain the founder dimension’s default. A previous sale is evidence of execution; it is not evidence that the founders are prematurely optimizing Adside for an exit.

The largest product uncertainty is whether Adside can manage accounts reliably while reducing the human work required. Its terms acknowledge inaccurate outputs and platform-access risk; managed-service customers approve creatives before spending. Thirty live companies provide an opportunity to test that uncertainty, but the dossier supplies no bounded experiment with an error threshold, cost, and decision rule. I give the milestone default rather than treating deployment count as risk removal. Likewise, charging 10% of advertising spend with a $3,500 monthly minimum establishes pricing, not economic superiority. Without human-management hours, inference costs, or a matched service-cost comparison, the economics remain at default.

I pass with my own money. The exceptions in my framework require a documented instigator opening a giant existing value pool, extraordinary technical progress, or a quantified societal consequence sufficient to justify missing milestones. Adside establishes none of those overrides. Its founders may build a profitable agency platform, but that bull case does not resolve the advertising-category exclusion or establish transformative leverage. One verifiable condition would reopen my decision: a demonstrated pivot into a non-advertising expert workflow with an independently reproduced 10x reduction in total delivery cost at matched quality.
