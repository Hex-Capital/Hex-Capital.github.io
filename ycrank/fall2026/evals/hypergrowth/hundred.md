# Hundred -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B. Repeatable sales with account expansion.** The initial buyer is the receiving-quality team at an electronics factory or contract manufacturer. The recurring job is to catch defective incoming parts before they reach assembly. The memo documents a public launch and a founder-led inquiry channel. Hundred says its controlled-light station scans whole shipping trays and uses one known-good part to detect and grade defects smaller than 100 µm. Those claims describe a plausible route to quick setup. Paid activation, production performance, and customer expansion remain unverified. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks; Key Facts)

**Scenario assumptions.** If factories pay per station and their quality teams can qualify new part types using the same setup, a first station could win recurring tray volume and lead to additional stations at other receiving sites. In a second geography, the same hardware and software would need to handle local trays, part finishes, and acceptance rules, with a repeatable installation and support process. Each station would still add hardware and shipping costs. Growth would be more attractive if sales, qualification, installation, and support hours fell on subsequent deployments. The memo leaves cross-market demand and those rollout costs unmeasured. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks; Key Facts)

### Countercase

Reflective finishes and acceptable part variation could force engineers to tune and requalify each part type. If false rejects or quarantine handling slow receiving, whole-tray inspection could become a bottleneck. Factories with installed cameras also have a retrofit option. Together, these risks could make each station a custom deployment with a weak payoff for the buyer. The memo gives no sustained throughput or production error rates and establishes no pattern of proportional delivery costs. The structural Pass gate remains unmet. (Key Risks; Defensibility)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent electronics factory in a second geography pay for a station and use it routinely on incoming trays with the same setup process as the first paid site? Two comparable rollout records showing paid use over multiple receiving cycles, buyer-agreed accuracy and throughput targets met, and logged installation, calibration, and support costs would move the action to **Reach out** if they show the second site can deploy with limited custom work.
