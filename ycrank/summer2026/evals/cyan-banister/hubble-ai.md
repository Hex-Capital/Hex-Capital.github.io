# Hubble -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032 and the attractive version is obvious: a patient authorizes access once, software retrieves a complete longitudinal record from every institution, and any care, legal, or life-sciences application can act on normalized information immediately. Hubble has found a real leak from that world—40-plus production tools spanning Epic, Cerner, athenahealth, UnitedHealthcare, and Aetna—but the dossier does not map the intervening transitions. It tells me how agents patch today’s portals, phone systems, and APIs, not why those patches become the enduring intelligence layer rather than temporary scaffolding.

The present system is wonderfully, painfully specific. A legal team waits weeks for PDFs assembled through faxes and release queues; paralegal work consumes hours; a dental group leaves valid claims unpaid. Hubble uses a right patients already possess under HIPAA, adds identity verification and authorization, and turns those institutional delays into one retrieval surface. The reported outcomes—hours reduced to minutes, $340,000 in denied claims recovered within 90 days, and estimate accuracy rising from 50% to 94%—show where the rent lives and whose time is being returned. This is the company’s strongest evidence.

Prabha Dublish and Aaron Leon have paid relevant operating tuition. Dublish was Grow Therapy’s first product hire and worked across telehealth, integrations, marketplaces, and AI care; Leon built Amazon Care’s data platform and One Medical infrastructure serving more than one million patients. Their decade-long relationship and earlier nonprofit work reduce team-formation risk, while the CEO and CTO responsibilities appear legible. What I cannot yet see is the pre-company obsession: no dated independent experiments, retrieval artifact, or personal wound shows that either founder was compelled to build this before healthcare agents became fashionable. They have domain command, but the dossier has not established first-believer behavior.

The product is more than a rendering. Forty-plus tools and three customer outcomes imply real deployments across different workflows. Still, every testimonial is anonymized, and Hubble publishes no retrieval volume, repeat-use cohort, completion rate, contract value, or B2B revenue. That caps how much behavior I can infer. My 36-month base case is therefore a useful $3 million–$7 million ARR workflow business concentrated in dental claims and legal record retrieval, not yet the horizontal patient-information layer described on the homepage.

The uncomfortable bull case is that I am mistaking early infrastructure for services work. Deterministic identifiers, source traceability, normalized outputs, and per-call audit logs could let every browser or voice retrieval improve a shared operational asset across care delivery, legal technology, and life sciences. The problem is control: Hubble remains downstream of APIs, portals, phone trees, HIEs, and provider release processes it does not own, while Particle Health, 1upHealth, Health Gorilla, and PicnicHealth already occupy adjacent layers with $49 million to more than $100 million raised. Provider-specific retrieval history could become a moat, but a hypothetical data exhaust is not yet a controlled bottleneck.

Privacy is the gate I would not wave through. Individual Access Services are not legally subject to HIPAA, protected categories require separate authorizations, and browser and voice agents enlarge the custody surface. Hubble has meaningful limiting architecture—TEFCA-aligned safeguards, source traceability, audit logs, and a dedicated privacy notice—so this is not an automatic rejection. I remain neutral because the team has proved a sharp repair of today’s broken system without proving either the six-year causal chain or durable ownership of the layer beneath it. One verifiable flip condition would change my mind: a named 12-month cohort of at least ten customers showing 70% expansion across multiple workflows while at least 90% of retrievals complete without manual intervention.
