# Verdict Machine -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The 2025 SEC and OCC changes allow banks to expand digital-asset activity, bank security teams now need enforceable on-chain controls, and the variable that determines whether Verdict Machine becomes large is whether those institutions will place its model directly in the transaction-approval path rather than confine it to evaluation.

That variable is plausible but not yet proven. Verdict Machine spans inventory, posture, diligence, screening, and pre-execution enforcement, which is more consequential than another monitoring dashboard. Yet the dossier does not identify the accuracy threshold, false-positive tolerance, procurement cycle, or production-conversion rate required for a bank to trust an irreversible control point. POCs with asset managers and infrastructure providers moving billions weekly, plus evaluations at large banks during the company’s first weeks, establish access and urgency. They do not establish operational trust.

The timing is real. Regulators removed barriers to bank crypto custody and distributed-ledger activity while preserving risk-control expectations. The economic step change is less clear. Verdict Machine has not quantified how its product reduces compliance labor, losses, insurance costs, or transaction-review time. Meanwhile, Blockaid processes more than 180 million transactions monthly, and Chainalysis-backed Hexagate combines simulation and prevention across more than 75 chains. This is a crystallizing market with funded leaders, so a new entrant needs a sharper winner case than “institution-specific AI.”

The product architecture could supply that case. Maintaining an institution’s asset inventory, policies, permissions, and proposed transactions creates an owned operational loop that should improve as underlying models improve. But the public record shows no production integration, decision-performance data, or proprietary workflow state. The inferred data moat is especially weak: there is no estimate of what it would cost Blockaid, Chainalysis, Fireblocks, or Check Point to reproduce it. Check Point’s earlier AI-assisted blockchain firewall also creates both commercial overlap and an unresolved IP boundary.

The strongest evidence is the team. Dan Danay and Isaac Grossman previously built keyTango together; Danay and Liran Kogan then led blockchain-security work at Check Point. This is repeat-founder learning, direct domain knowledge, and an existing working relationship—not résumé adjacency. Their immediate access to institutional POCs makes the bull case credible. A talent override still does not resolve the market gate, however: Verdict Machine is not pursuing frontier research in a pre-crystallization category, and competitors already possess distribution and data scale.

I would monitor rather than invest today. The verifiable flip condition is two large-bank evaluations converting into paid production deployments totaling at least $1 million ARR while permitting automated pre-execution blocking. That would demonstrate product pull, trust, and workflow ownership in one event. Without it, the more likely 36-month outcome is a strategic acquisition by Fireblocks for the team and policy layer rather than an independent category leader.
