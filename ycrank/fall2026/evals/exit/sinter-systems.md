# Sinter -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Sinter’s strongest path. A digital manufacturer such as Protolabs or a sourcing platform such as Fictiv or Xometry could buy Sinter to add low-volume molding for customers who revise parts frequently. The asset would be the workflow from automated design checks and quoting through printed photopolymer tooling and molding in existing presses. The memo documents live prototype ordering and molding pilot recruitment. Sinter claims its pilot produces parts in 48 hours at one-tenth the time and cost of traditional methods. Ownership could make sense if integrating the workflow lets a buyer win repeat orders at margins it could not achieve by building the process or contracting with Sinter. The memo provides no measured buyer benefit, acquisition price evidence, or confirmation that rights to the core process would transfer. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is repeat paid molding orders with measured tool life, acceptable part yield, and a sustained cost advantage over quick-turn alternatives. Sinter reports producing more than 500 kg of parts with customers in its first month. The memo gives no molding-specific breakdown, pilot budget, or estimate of the capital needed to reach repeat production. It also gives no company-specific support for a sponsor buyout, an eligible secondary, an IPO with holder resale, or a funded payout. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Illustrative proceeds.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target requires $2.5 million in gross cash, or $83.3 million of distributable equity. With an illustrative $5 million of net debt, the enterprise-value hurdle is $88.3 million. At year eight, a $25 million sale yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million gross (5.7×), assuming pro rata distribution before taxes and no further deductions for fees or senior claims. At 2% or 4% ownership, the distributable-equity hurdle is $125 million or $62.5 million, respectively. These are scenario values. The memo supplies no Sinter-specific debt, shareholder terms, or supported sale range. If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. Wind-down proceeds are unquantifiable without evidence on residual assets, obligations, and preferences. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Protolabs already advertises one-day aluminum molding, and mold shops can test commercially available photopolymer tooling. If heat and creep shorten insert life enough to erase Sinter’s claimed savings on repeat jobs, a buyer could meet customer demand with existing tooling or trial printed inserts in-house. The memo leaves the economics of comparable jobs unresolved. It establishes neither a structural block nor a credible sale range that falls short of the holder’s target, so the Pass gate does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can repeat paid molding orders demonstrate a durable per-job cost advantage that supports a strategic buyer paying enough to clear the holder’s return target? Job records comparing all-in cost, lead time, tool life, and yield against quick-turn aluminum for comparable parts would establish the operating advantage. Buyer-specific economics supporting a sale around or above the illustrative $88.3 million enterprise-value hurdle within eight years would move the action to Reach out. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
