# Routant -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with software vendors deploying agents into enterprise systems, Routant’s strongest growth path is to integrate each vendor once and reuse that integration across its customers. Routant describes one MCP endpoint for the vendor, outbound-only gateways for private or on-premises systems, and customer-controlled permissions, approvals, and audit logs. The standardized interface and the recurring job of governing agent access support broad geographic portability. Each customer still has to connect its systems and configure its policies. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Installed gateways and policies could keep customers using Routant as agents make recurring requests. If Routant charges a recurring fee that rises with connected environments or usage, vendors could spend more as they deploy to more customers. That pricing is a scenario assumption. The memo records launch engagement and demo bookings, leaving paid adoption and commercial growth unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The demo-led sales motion and customer-side setup could make each rollout a separate enterprise implementation. Under a recurring-fee model, revenue from the next deployment would have to cover attributable sales, setup, support, request routing, and audit storage. Reusing the vendor integration could reduce setup labor, but the memo gives no prices or operating measurements to assess contribution. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo identifies customer-side work without establishing that it requires bespoke work by market or enough Routant labor to make growth economically infeasible. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of vendors start and pay through a standard flow, then use the same integration to bring additional enterprise customers into paid production? Billing and deployment records showing rising recurring spend and positive incremental contribution after sales, setup, infrastructure, and support would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that every rollout requires bespoke Routant engineering that makes incremental contribution persistently negative would move it to Pass.
