# Hickory & Company -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** The clearest initial buyer is a bank compliance team working through a recurring BSA investigation queue. Hickory says its agents gather cited evidence and draft case documentation for human approval. The memo records demo booking and a Utah Bankers Association conference sponsorship as routes to buyers. It also reports that Hickory’s compliance lead used an internal AI compliance tool at Seis, an early signal that the workflow is worth testing. Paid activation through the demo channel, recurring customer use, and delivery costs remain unreported. (The Idea, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

The growth scenario assumes a bank pays to put one recurring queue into production, then adds queues or workloads using the same evidence connectors, review controls, and audit logs. To repeat that sale outside the U.S., Hickory would need to configure local policy and reporting requirements, connect approved data sources, and meet the buyer’s data-processing requirements. The terms allow hosted processing to be restricted or disabled. Whether the same workflow remains deployable and economical under those restrictions is untested. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Hickory could win banks and still scale like a services business. Each deployment could require fresh integration work, policy configuration, security review, and ongoing support, with model processing costs rising as case volume grows. Qualified human review remains part of the buyer’s workflow, so realized reviewer time savings matter to willingness to pay. Hickory markets 10× faster queue processing without a published benchmark method. Its terms also specify no payment-rail integrations and say the software is unsuitable for submitting consumer financial-account information, which could limit expansion into monitoring replacement. These are concrete constraints. The memo provides too little deployment evidence to establish the proportional-cost or structural-demand ceiling required for Pass. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent bank outside the U.S. pay for and repeatedly run the same investigation workflow as a U.S. bank using a shared deployment playbook? A paired paid rollout showing recurring production use, measured reviewer time savings, and limited incremental integration, compliance, support, and compute costs would move the action to **Reach out**. Evidence that each rollout requires a bespoke rebuild with costs that rise in proportion to revenue and no supported way to reduce them would move it to **Pass**.
