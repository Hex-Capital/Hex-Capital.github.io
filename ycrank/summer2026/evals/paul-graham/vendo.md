# Vendo -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Nour Zahzah and Yousef Helal have known each other since kindergarten, built an earlier retail AI assistant together, and now ship Vendo as an open-source product. That makes Vendo a strong-pair company with a weaker organic-idea origin: the founders clearly know one another and can build, but the dossier never says either founder personally needed users to generate features inside his own software. Vendo begins with unusually sound founder geometry and ends with an unanswered question about whether the idea was discovered through pain or through scanning the current AI landscape.

**Stripe** (2010) is the high rung for co-founder configuration: two brothers with years of shared history, complementary abilities, and Auctomatic already behind them. Vendo shares the load-bearing mechanism. Zahzah and Helal go back to kindergarten, attended Berkeley, and previously built in-store AI assistants together. Vendo diverges because the Collisons had already produced a $5 million outcome and unmistakable evidence of working together under commercial pressure; Vendo has 346 GitHub stars, not an Auctomatic. Still, this is nearly the configuration I would choose if assembling founders in Lisp.

**Loopt** (2005) is the low-rung warning on organic ideas: a product that outran usable infrastructure and required Sam Altman’s force of personality to override weak product timing. Vendo’s prior retail-assistant project puts Zahzah and Helal near the problem, but Vendo does not show that either founder ran a software product, drowned in customer feature requests, and built this tool for himself. “Product teams have divergent requirements” is true, but it sounds observed rather than suffered. The Loopt exception does not rescue Vendo because the dossier contains no equivalent animal anecdote that would let founder force overwhelm the missing organic origin.

Vendo’s 346 GitHub stars, 43 forks, 1,220 weekly npm downloads, and nightly tests against 16 applications are real tracks. Over the next 18 months, resourcefulness will compound only if Vendo turns those developers into founder-assisted integrations, watches where permissions and generated interfaces break, and fixes each failure faster than a larger platform would. Vendo has shipped before pitching, which clears my most basic red flag, but no named customer, revenue, or unscalable stunt shows Zahzah and Helal refusing to take no for an answer. A working artifact is evidence of hackers. It is not yet evidence of animals.

Vendo’s signed-in-user API access, approval gates, audit controls, sandboxing, and scheduled automations describe a genuine schlep zone. If every customer’s APIs, React components, themes, and permission rules fail differently, Vendo becomes a kind of **Coinbase** (2012) for agent permissions: the disagreeable integration work is the asset. If scanning becomes standardized through OpenAI Apps SDK, MCP, or Vercel AI SDK, Vendo becomes a thin convenience layer and this score falls a tier. Testing 16 open-source applications proves breadth in the laboratory, while a production customer allowing Vendo to execute privileged actions would prove the schlep is real.

**Reddit** (2005) had Aaron Swartz’s RSS work and a founder group visibly rooted in hacker culture. Vendo has a credible parallel in Yousef Helal: Berkeley EECS, Stanford EE, FPGA infrastructure at Apple, MicroBlaze V work at AMD, database research at Berkeley Skylab, and contributions to Spatialyze. Vendo falls short of the highest hacker-genealogy rung because Nour Zahzah has no public repository and Helal’s record is more credentialed systems engineering than a pre-company artifact with its own following. The unstated pattern is nonetheless favorable: Vendo looks built by people who can inspect the machinery, not by managers planning to hire those people later.

Vendo’s nightly testing across 16 applications points toward a narrower and more durable destination than “let users build features.” By July 2029, I expect Vendo to have pivoted toward embedded agent-permission and integration infrastructure and reached $3 million to $7 million in ARR. Vendo’s strong founder pair can accumulate the ugly integration knowledge, while the weaker organic origin makes the broad end-user micro-app vision less likely to survive contact with customers. The schlep will choose the product.

CopilotKit’s $27 million, Vercel’s general-purpose AI SDK, OpenAI’s Apps SDK, and Vendo’s Apache-2.0 license form the strongest bear case: Vendo may be an attractive repository whose useful pieces are absorbed by platforms before anyone pays $499 per month. Vendo has no disclosed production customer, proprietary dataset, or demonstrated learning effect, so that objection stings. What keeps me at Invest is the integrated approvals, persistence, identity propagation, and generated UI already attracting 1,220 weekly npm downloads, combined with founders who have worked together since childhood. I would flip to Pass if Vendo cannot show at least 10 paying production installations with retained monthly usage by July 2027.
