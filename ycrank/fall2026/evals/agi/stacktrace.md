# Stacktrace -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a scarce complement to AI agents: a shared enforcement layer that lets security teams approve unattended work across multiple agent runtimes. The memo documents the release of scheduled and event-triggered repository work, and Stacktrace says companies restrict agent access because teams can miss stalled runs or out-of-scope actions. Fleet’s documented hooks and Agent Gateway’s consent checks could make more unattended work acceptable to security teams. That is a plausible bottleneck, with scarcity and Stacktrace’s control still unproven. The New Economies path lacks evidence that oversight costs fell per successfully completed task or that the savings opened a previously uneconomic use. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

A founder reports unnamed design partners running local or unattended cloud agents. Payment, repeat use, and expansion remain unverified. The Fleet launch, relevant founder experience in security and infrastructure, and a founder-reported benchmark covering three rules across nine models support early execution. Fleet prices enterprise usage by session-hour, but the memo gives no evidence on how integration, policy maintenance, or human approvals affect the cost of serving additional volume. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, The Idea)

### Countercase

Anthropic already offers managed permissions and hooks, and Bay offers enforcement through existing security infrastructure. Stacktrace’s advertised ability to apply one policy across coding agents could matter in a mixed deployment, but its coverage depends on supported logs and enforcement points. Central policies and histories could create switching costs, with buyer preference and migration costs still unmeasured. The documented overlap makes substitution credible, while equivalent coverage and buyer switching remain unproven. That leaves the scarce-complement path open under Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent buyer pay to expand Stacktrace after a side-by-side production pilot on previously restricted unattended workflows across multiple agent runtimes? Move to Reach out if the buyer expands because Stacktrace meets its enforcement coverage and reviewer workload requirements where native controls or an existing security platform fall short. Move to Pass if those alternatives meet the same requirements in a representative deployment and the buyer declines to pay for Stacktrace. An inconclusive pilot keeps the action at Watch.
