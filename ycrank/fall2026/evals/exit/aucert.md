# Aucert -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to a mobile QA vendor is Aucert’s strongest liquidity path. QA Wolf already runs managed Appium regression tests. Aucert says its agents add scriptless exploration and dedicated design, accessibility, and store-policy review, using customer-approved expectations stored in an organization-scoped app map. Owning that capability could let a QA vendor expand what it sells to existing customers. The memo documents a public launch and a private-beta listing. The economic benefit to a buyer remains an inference, with no measured customer savings or repeat paid use reported and conflicting descriptions of feature availability. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The next milestone is repeat paid use across production releases, with customers measuring review time saved and confirming material findings their existing checks missed. The beta listing and the founders’ mobile QA and automated testing experience make that test plausible. It would require device and model operating costs, customer onboarding, and a workable way to handle sensitive test data. The amount of capital needed and available runway remain unverified. Aucert says screenshots and UI hierarchies go to AI providers as captured. Whether a buyer could take over the customer contracts and app maps, and whether the holder could participate on the modeled terms, remain open. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. At 3%, that requires about $83.3 million of distributable equity value. The sale scenarios below assume an eligible holder sells its entire stake for cash at closing within eight years, with a pro rata waterfall and no preference or escrow reducing or delaying the payout. The prices and deductions are illustrative. The memo provides no transaction pricing, quantified buyer economics, or holder terms to ground an exit range. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Holder cash |
|----------|----------------------------|-------------|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $35M | $1.05M, or 2.1× |

The strong case clears the objective by $200,000. At that sale price, total deductions above about $16.7 million, or dilution below about 2.78% at $90 million of distributable equity, would put the holder below the objective.

### Countercase

QA Wolf already maps mobile workflows and generates, runs, and repairs tests, giving it a plausible route to build overlapping features in-house. Google Play’s built-in reports also cover some Android accessibility and stability checks. If Aucert’s additional findings rarely change release decisions, a buyer may see little reason to pay a price that clears the holder’s hurdle. The evidence establishes partial substitution. A substitute for the full workflow or a supported price ceiling below the hurdle remains unproven, so the Pass gate is unmet. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a mobile QA vendor’s buy-versus-build analysis, grounded in repeat paid production use, support a price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity under the benchmark? A buyer-side estimate tied to measured review savings and confirmed findings missed by existing checks would answer it. A supported range that clears the hurdle after deal adjustments would move the action to Reach out. A supported ceiling below it for the strongest credible route would move the action to Pass.
