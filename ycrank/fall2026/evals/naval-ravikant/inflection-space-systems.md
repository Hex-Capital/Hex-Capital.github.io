# Inflection Space Systems -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Matt Hanley automated satellite integration and testing at Loft Orbital and worked on Lunar Photon at Rocket Lab. Alex Lisso has worked across spacecraft design, manufacturing, testing, and operations. Those are relevant encounters with reality. Inflection Space Systems is asking these founders to industrialize work they have personally done. That earns substantial founder credit; it does not establish that their new production system works ([founder profiles](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/inflection-space-systems)).

The decisive question is what survives after a competitor copies the design. Inflection reports completing more than half its internally designed stack, including flight and ground software. That is tangible development beyond a factory rendering. Hardware integration can preserve an advantage after software becomes cheap, but the advantage must reside in qualified components, production yield, test coverage, or manufacturing processes. None is yet demonstrated at repeat-production scale. Apex already pursues standardized, vertically integrated manufacturing. Building hardware puts Inflection in a category I favor; it does not automatically make its hardware defensible ([YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Vdy-inflection-space-systems-hyperscaling-spacecraft-manufacturing-10-000-year)).

There is room to maneuver within the market, although the spacecraft cannot maneuver outside its specifications. Exponent’s 100 kg payload capacity and 1.5 kW payload power define which commercial and government missions it can serve. The shared platform could carry different customers’ payloads without a fresh spacecraft design each time. SDA’s approximately $3.5 billion of awards for 72 tracking satellites establishes funded adjacent demand, but those awards went to established teams; they do not establish compatibility or procurement access for Inflection. The credible expansion path is repeated compatible missions, not a claim on all space spending.

The strongest case for investing now is that waiting for orbital proof could mean buying the same founders at a much higher price. A Q1 2028 launch is booked, development is underway, and published prices fall from $1.5 million for one spacecraft to $700,000 each for one hundred. Standardization could turn scarce engineering knowledge into repeatable output. But the price schedule demonstrates an offer, not a cost curve or a completed transaction. Nor does another customer demonstrably improve the product or distribution for existing customers. Manufacturing learning could eventually compound; the dossier has not shown that loop ([company website](https://inflection-space.com/)).

Inflection fits my pattern of backing physical infrastructure bottlenecks. It has not demonstrated the permissionless contribution or customer-created value present in my other preferred patterns. There is no documented integrity violation, artificial financing deadline, or dependence on investor reassurance, so I apply no founder veto. My exceptions for backing an unfinished product require unusually strong prototype or capital-efficiency evidence; “more than half built” does not establish either sufficiently here. I remain **Neutral**. My flip condition is a paid repeat order for ten standardized Exponent spacecraft, delivered with positive contribution margin. That single milestone would show that customers want repetition and that repetition creates economic value for Inflection.
