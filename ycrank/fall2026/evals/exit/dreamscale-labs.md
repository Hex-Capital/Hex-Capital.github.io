# Dreamscale Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest exit path is a strategic acquisition by a GPU infrastructure provider. Dreamscale’s robot-specific transport, regional GPU placement, and deployment tooling could give a buyer a faster way to serve robotics workloads. The memo documents a HUD evaluation integration and a joint excavator model evaluation with Actor Labs. Dreamscale reports running DreamZero 14B in 320 ms versus 350 ms for NVIDIA’s implementation, using half the GPU count. That company-run comparison suggests a potential compute benefit. Its value to an acquirer depends on whether the savings hold in paid production at acceptable tail latency and reliability. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A paid fleet deployment that measures compute cost, tail latency, and reliability against the customer’s existing setup is a credible seed-stage milestone. The A$100,000 grant and evaluation work support further testing. The memo leaves the cost and funding of a production rollout open. A sale centered on the transport and deployment software is plausible, with IP ownership and transfer rights still to verify. Third-party checkpoint licenses could limit which assets a buyer can take over. The memo supports no independent holder liquidity route. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. The sale scenarios assume the holder is eligible to sell its full stake for cash at closing and receives 3% of distributable equity after debt, deal adjustments, and any senior claims. With those deductions assumed to be zero, a **modest** $40 million enterprise-value sale returns $1.2 million (2.4×), and a **strong** $100 million sale returns $3 million (6×). In the strong case, $20 million of deductions reduces the payout to $2.4 million (4.8×), below the roughly $83.3 million distributable-equity hurdle. The **prolonged-private** case assumes continued reinvestment and yields zero realized cash. These are sensitivities. The posted $5 per connected robot-hour establishes pricing, and the memo supplies no paid usage, margins, buyer valuation, or holder terms to anchor realizable proceeds. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A GPU platform could build its own robotics layer, and customers already have hosted alternatives for overlapping checkpoints or can use an offline runtime where connectivity is unreliable. Dreamscale’s reported tail-latency improvement came from one home Wi-Fi test with synthetic frames and simulated compute. If the efficiency advantage fades in production, a buyer has less reason to pay for the software or assume the work of supporting safety-sensitive deployments. The evidence leaves a specific gap in buyer economics, supporting Watch. It establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible sale-price ceiling below the proceeds hurdle. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
