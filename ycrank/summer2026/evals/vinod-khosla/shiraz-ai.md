# Shiraz AI -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

If Shiraz AI works, a contract factory could convert a weekly production change into an adaptable robot task from one video instead of another integration project. That is potentially a different cost curve, not a better interface. But the dossier quantifies only a $37.8 billion industrial-robotics market. It does not quantify labor hours displaced, idle automation, deployment cost, or the population of economically automatable tasks. The consequence could be large; Shiraz has not yet shown it is 100x.

The technical claim is specific: one demonstration, in-context adaptation, no retraining. That is meaningfully better than Micropsi’s repeated hand-guided examples and rigid automation built around stable production lines. Yet Skild S1 now claims the same single-video capability. Shiraz publishes no comparative success rate, cycle time, exception-recovery rate, safety record, or evaluation method. Robots operating at customer sites prove the team can deploy hardware. They do not prove a 5–10x technical discontinuity.

The founders are the strongest evidence. Navid Aghasadeghi commercialized Spot at Boston Dynamics, worked on industrial manipulation at Rethink Robotics, and previously built a product reported at one million users. Mojtaba Mozaffar led perception, policy-learning, and manipulation work at Amazon Robotics after a robotics PhD and research-faculty role. This is complementary technical and commercialization experience around the exact system being built. What is missing is evidence of fast belief revision, a difficult recruit won, or a weakness deliberately assigned to a new owner.

The bull case is that full-stack deployment creates the proprietary learning loop the public dossier cannot yet reveal. Customer demonstrations generate policies; production exceptions generate hard cases; every installed cell improves the next one. The bear case stings more: supplying hardware, software, and on-site integration may produce bespoke automation revenue while better-capitalized foundation-model companies commoditize the intelligence. With Skild AI above $2 billion raised, an undisclosed dataset and switching costs are possibilities, not moats.

Capital should now attack one kill risk: whether a task learned from one video can run through real production variation without engineering intervention. Shiraz has disclosed no pass/fail threshold, experiment cost, or stop branch. It also provides no hardware cost, gross margin, utilization, labor comparison, or payback period. My 36-month base case is a technically credible but services-heavy deployment business below $3 million ARR, constrained by integration labor rather than model capability.

I pass today. The large installed value pool and exceptional domain team make this a legitimate exception candidate, but neither overrides direct capability overlap without measured superiority or scalable economics. My one flip condition is verifiable: ten customer production cells operating for 90 days at at least 95% autonomous task success after one demonstration, with deployment payback below 18 months. That would show both technical discontinuity and a route out of the integration trap.
