# OpenHack -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by an application-security platform. OpenHack combines application mapping, live testing, and business-impact triage. A platform could buy that workflow if it reliably surfaces important findings that its existing tools miss and reduces time spent on triage. OpenHack says Papermark fixed an exposure it reported, and its custom scanner led six configurations on F1 in its own benchmark. Those signals support a potential buyer rationale. The memo also records 444 GitHub stars, which show developer interest. It provides no measured production savings, paying customer count, or buyer pricing evidence. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A seed-stage milestone is repeat paid production use across several enterprise customers, with measured triage savings and consequential findings beyond incumbent tools. The reported $500,000 accelerator financing and named launch users make an initial test plausible. Runway and the capital needed to reach repeat paid deployments remain unknown. An acquisition of the software business appears feasible at this stage. Rights to the open-source components and transfer terms for customer agreements involving private repository access would need verification. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Under the rubric benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3%, with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× cash objective requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale scenarios assume an all-cash close within eight years, eligibility to sell the full stake, and a pro rata waterfall with no further preference deductions. Prices and deductions are illustrative assumptions.

- **Modest sale.** $40 million enterprise value less $5 million in assumed net debt and deal deductions leaves $35 million in distributable equity and **$1.05 million** to the holder.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million enterprise value less $10 million in assumed net debt and deal deductions leaves $90 million in distributable equity and **$2.70 million** to the holder. If the holder can sell only 90% of the stake at closing, cash received falls to **$2.43 million**.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued reinvestment with no eligible share sale or distribution yields **$0** in realized cash within eight years. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route.

The strong case clears the objective under the stated assumptions. The memo leaves the realizable sale price, waterfall, and holder participation open.

### Countercase

An incumbent could extend its own workflow or use an overlapping product. GitHub already offers native autofix, and Strix offers scheduled testing, remediation pull requests, and retesting. OpenHack’s own white-box evaluation found 277 of 761 planted vulnerabilities, leaving its incremental coverage in production uncertain. These facts weaken the case for paying an acquisition premium. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor inadequate proceeds across the strongest credible route, so the Pass gate stays open and the action is Watch. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can OpenHack demonstrate enough incremental value in paid production for a strategic buyer to justify a price above the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Repeat customer measurements of triage savings and consequential findings missed by incumbent tools, paired with a credible buyer’s estimate of what it would pay for that advantage, would move the action to Reach out if the implied price plausibly clears the hurdle after deal deductions.
