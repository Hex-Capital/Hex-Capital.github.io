# Mantle -- Paul Graham Evaluation

White’s account of Curo’s missed uptime guarantees is the most useful fact here. Contracts with Uber, Zoox and Google were affected when machines failed to stay available. He has encountered the problem Mantle proposes to solve. But what did he do when that happened? The dossier describes the obstacle without showing a particular workaround, how quickly he shipped it, or what improved afterward. That distinction matters. Recognizing an expensive problem earns credit for choosing the problem. Resourcefulness requires the next part of the story.

The strongest argument for investing is that both founders have earned this idea through operating experience. White’s previous company reportedly reached 5,000 charging stations before acquisition. Smith co-founded Babylon Micro-Farms, reports 450+ deployed units, and previously operated an electronics-repair business. Mantle brings together people who have built distributed equipment and experienced the burden of keeping it working. Installation, spare parts, diagnosis and emergency visits are precisely the sort of unpleasant work that can conceal an opportunity. The retrospective origin accounts deserve some discount, but the prior operating histories give them substance.

Those histories do not establish that Mantle has already solved the problem. There is no documented Mantle installation, completed repair, measured uptime improvement or functioning diagnostic system. Nor is there a named manufacturer repeatedly using or paying for its service. The founders’ announcements establish what they intend to offer. Their follower counts and zero advertised YC jobs say little about whether anyone needs it urgently. I also cannot identify an observation that caused them to revise Mantle’s approach. These are untested dimensions, rather than evidence of technical inability, rejected demand or stubbornness.

Taking responsibility for uptime could be a good way to learn what manufacturers will actually buy. Every difficult repair could teach the founders something a software vendor would miss. The counterargument is that this learning remains local: another machine type requires another specialist, and another customer requires another service territory. Roboworx already advertises overlapping services and nationwide coverage. Mantle must discover a repeatable advantage inside the work. The claimed $500B market does not establish that advantage, and undisclosed pricing leaves open whether uptime responsibility produces attractive contracts or expensive obligations.

I would pass today. Mantle fits my pattern of founders entering an ugly operational problem they know firsthand. It does not yet show the concrete proof spike or user appropriation that makes my less obvious bets compelling. I see no evidence of dependence on a large partner, premature delegation or refusal to change direction; those red flags should not be invented. But neither a working-product exception nor unmistakable customer pull supplies an override to the missing founder-execution evidence. My flip condition is one documented paid deployment where an unexpected failure leads the founders to ship a workaround within weeks, restore contracted uptime and earn the manufacturer’s expansion order. That would connect their experience to observable action and demand. At present, the connection is still a hypothesis.
