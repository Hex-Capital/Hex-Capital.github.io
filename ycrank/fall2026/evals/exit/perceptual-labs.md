# Perceptual Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest plausible route is acquisition by an AI lab with access to editable model weights. Perceptual Labs claims its product can make targeted, auditable weight edits. A lab could have a reason to own the tool if it repeatedly fixes costly model behaviors faster or more cheaply than its existing post-training process while preserving unrelated capabilities. The memo documents an early-access waitlist. The founders report training work for Apple, Framer, and other enterprises through Bryel Foundry, a prior offering. Those engagements and the founders’ relevant experience make a production pilot plausible. The memo provides no measured buyer benefit for the current product, buyer interest in an acquisition, or funded pilot with a budget and timetable. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in cash within eight years for a 5× gross return. The illustrative sale cases assume a full-company cash sale that closes within eight years, an eligible holder selling their entire stake, and zero net debt, deal deductions, or senior claims that reduce the pro rata payout. The memo leaves those terms unverified. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

| Scenario | Cash proceeds to the holder |
|-----------|-----------------------------|
| Modest sale at an assumed $40 million enterprise value | $40 million in distributable equity yields $1.2 million, or 2.4×. |
| Strong sale at an assumed $100 million enterprise value | $100 million in distributable equity yields $3 million, or 6×. Net debt, deal deductions, and senior payouts that together remove more than about $16.7 million from the pro rata pool would put the holder below 5×. |
| Prolonged private operation with cash reinvested | $0 realized within eight years. The memo gives no evidence of an eligible secondary sale or funded payout. |

The sale values are scenario assumptions. The memo gives no transaction pricing or buyer economics that support a realizable price above the roughly $83.3 million distributable equity hurdle. It also leaves IP transfer rights and any required consents unverified. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A strategic buyer could build comparable capabilities in-house. The memo cites published methods for overlapping capabilities and a benchmark in which frontier agents failed language-model patching tasks under its test constraints. Weight access narrows the buyer pool, and demand for Bryel’s training service may carry over weakly to weight editing. These risks leave a credible diligence gap. They do not establish a ready substitute, a structural closing barrier, or materially inadequate proceeds under the strongest plausible route. The Pass gate is therefore unmet. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production pilot at a lab with editable weights establish an economic case for acquiring the product? Buyer-validated results showing repeatable savings against the lab’s post-training baseline, preserved unrelated capabilities, and an ownership rationale that could support clearing the roughly $83.3 million distributable equity hurdle would move the action to **Reach out**.
