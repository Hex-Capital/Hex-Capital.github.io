# TrueMile -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

TrueMile has a plausible **industry enabled by superintelligence** path. Independent owner-operators described in the memo book one load at a time and can end up driving empty on the return leg. TrueMile’s lease-on model pools trucks for fleet-wide planning, while agents contact brokers and coordinate dispatch. The seed-stage hypothesis is that automating those tasks lowers dispatch labor per completed round trip enough to give owner-operators who rely on load boards access to continuous outbound and return-load planning. TrueMile reports that one dispatcher can manage nine times as many trucks. The memo gives no comparable cost per completed trip or evidence that these operators previously lacked an affordable equivalent. Even under model parity, delivering the service requires coordinating trucks, securing freight, and assuming carrier safety and compliance responsibilities. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

TrueMile earns zero for **building blocks of superintelligence**. The described product uses AI to dispatch freight, and the memo identifies no AI workload whose deployment depends on a scarce input TrueMile supplies. (The Idea)

Running the solver within its own carrier operation gives TrueMile a way to compare plans with actual trips and develop lane-specific operating knowledge. Competitors already supply parts of the workflow, and the memo establishes no exclusive lane access or advantage that improves with scale. TrueMile reports $57,000 in freight booked over three weeks, an early signal of transactions with realized revenue and repeat demand still unverified. The founder’s reported family-fleet turnaround and the founding engineers’ voice-agent and freight-operations experience support seed-stage execution credit. The memo gives no basis for estimating contribution from adding another truck after carrier delivery costs. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

### Countercase

TrueMile may be improving the margins of an existing dispatch service. Paid dispatch is already available to leased-on operators, every load still requires human approval and sign-off, and cancellations can invalidate linked loads. The reported dispatcher capacity could reflect faster booking while leaving enough review, recovery, and empty-mile costs to keep continuous trip planning uneconomic for the target operators. The reported operating result supports testing the mechanism, so the zero-path Pass gate does not apply. Watch reflects the unresolved affordability threshold. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can TrueMile repeatedly deliver profitable outbound and return trips for owner-operators who previously lacked affordable continuous dispatch? A paid cohort on comparable lanes should show repeat use and lower fully loaded cost per completed round trip than conventional dispatch, including driver payouts, empty miles, human approvals, cancellation recovery, and carrier overhead. Reach out if that comparison shows TrueMile crosses the affordability threshold at positive contribution. Pass if conventional dispatch already delivers the same outcome affordably for those operators. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
