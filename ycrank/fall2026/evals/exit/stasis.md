# STASIS -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic sale to an eyewear brand that could distribute STASIS’s fatigue-sensing, automatic tint, and screen-adjustment features through its existing channels. Prototype coverage and a refundable reservation offer establish that STASIS has a product to test. The founder reports discussions with eyewear brands, with buyer interest and willingness to pay still unverified. The March 2027 first shipment is a concrete opportunity to measure paid conversion and fatigue reduction in daily use. YC backing and the founders’ prior eye-sensing hardware work support a first field test. Production funding and unit economics remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. For both sale scenarios, assume a full cash acquisition within eight years, eligibility to sell the entire stake, no net debt or deal deductions, and no senior claims diverting proceeds from the holder.

| Scenario | Assumed outcome | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $40 million enterprise value and $40 million distributable equity | $1.2 million (2.4×) |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value and $100 million distributable equity | $3.0 million (6.0×) |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested, with no eligible share sale or payout within eight years | $0 |

The strong case has about $16.7 million of room for net debt and deal deductions before missing the hurdle, assuming the payout stays pro rata. The sale values are illustrative assumptions. Refundable deposits and listed retail prices give no basis for estimating an acquisition price, and the memo supplies no buyer economics or independent payout route. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An eyewear brand could build selected sensing features using available components and published blink-tracking work. Software-only blink training has also shown symptom improvement, giving customers a lower-complexity option for part of the problem. STASIS reports over 90% accuracy without a published reference measure or validation protocol, so that claim cannot establish a field advantage. Rights to transfer the reported training data are also unverified. These gaps weaken the reason to buy and leave closing feasibility open. The memo establishes neither a structural block nor a credible sale-price range that is materially below the hurdle, so the Pass gate remains untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Does a field pilot of shipped STASIS glasses show enough incremental fatigue relief over software-only blink training for an eyewear brand to justify buying the product instead of building comparable features? A buyer-reviewed pilot with a predefined fatigue measure and a quantified buy-versus-build case that could support the $83.3 million distributable equity hurdle would move the action to Reach out. An inconclusive result would keep it on Watch.
