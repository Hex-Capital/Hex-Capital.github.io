# Blinka -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Blinka is selling reliable access to US financial infrastructure to businesses operated abroad; the controlling variable is whether its banking partners can durably serve customers whom broader platforms exclude. Juan Pablo’s account closure establishes that the problem exists. It does not establish that Blinka has solved the underlying eligibility constraint. The published terms require a US business entity or qualifying sole proprietor, while LinkedIn advertises access for non-US entities. That discrepancy matters because customer acquisition has little value if the account eventually encounters the same restriction that created demand.

The strongest evidence is that customers are already moving money. Blinka reports more than $10 million processed in its first two months, 25 clients before launch, and named customers including Bricksave, Dapta, and Creditop ([October 9 report](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/asi-es-la-empresa-que-dos-colombianos-crearon-en-ee-uu-y-que-ya-ha-procesado-mas-de-us10-millones-en-pagos/)). Those are meaningful pre-seed signals, even after discounting company-reported figures. I would distinguish the 150 account-opening applicants from active customers, and payment volume from revenue. The early pull earns conviction that the problem is useful to solve; it cannot yet establish how much economic value Blinka captures.

The market structure makes that distinction consequential. Mercury reportedly reached $650 million in annualized revenue, and Airwallex reached $1.3 billion, demonstrating substantial spending in adjacent categories. But Slash already offers much of Blinka’s proposed bundle. This is a market with established competitors, so a narrower customer segment needs a durable access or distribution advantage. Colombia’s October 2025 Bre-B launch could improve local payouts, but the dossier does not connect it to measured settlement savings, pricing power, or higher margins for Blinka. The fee schedule is private, and Portage Bank, Third National, and Mural Pay supply important infrastructure. There is no demonstrated economic step change to underwrite yet.

The bull case becomes more compelling at the workflow layer. One reported customer manages more than 200 entities through a single portal. Combined with accounts, payments, cards, and treasury actions, that suggests an operational product with more substance than an interface around interchangeable providers. It fits my preference for a first product that becomes distribution for adjacent services: account access could bring the customer in, then invoicing and accounting could expand the relationship. But those latter workflows remain an inferred source of durability. The dossier does not establish proprietary reconciliation state, automated exception handling, or migration difficulty. An AI tag adds no investment credit without evidence that better models improve a workflow Blinka controls.

The founders strengthen this case through relevant learning. Juan Pablo personally experienced the account-access failure, and the brothers worked through credit, mortgage, and real-estate banking products before this pivot, according to [Pygma’s account](https://pygma.substack.com/p/we-bet-on-a-lawyer-and-a-drop-out). That is more informative than employer prestige or fundraising logos. It also makes the strongest objection to passing sting: these founders may have found the right wedge after several attempts, and customers are already entrusting it with meaningful transaction volume. Still, the exceptions that justify underwriting missing commercial economics do not apply here: Blinka has neither scarce frontier research nor documented technology that reconstructs incumbent margins.

I would monitor Blinka rather than write a personal check today. The gap is concentrated: customer pull is ahead of evidence that access remains durable and economically attractive. My single flip condition would be a verified 12-month cohort showing that at least 80% of the initial 25 clients remain active primary-account customers, with positive cohort contribution after partner, compliance, and support costs. That would connect the initial demand to a business Blinka can compound.
