# Agent Relay -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A. Product-led adoption.** The initial buyer is an engineering team lead whose developers repeatedly copy questions, decisions, and handoffs between coding agents. Agent Relay offers self-serve npm and desktop installs. If one engineer gets value from routing messages between existing tools, sharing sessions gives teammates a reason to join. The paid desktop upgrade could monetize that spread as teams share more sessions and connect more agents. The memo records 3,907 weekly CLI downloads, with team activation, founder involvement, and paid conversion still unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Pomerado reported coordinating 80+ agents and improving its product at 2–4× its previous speed. That customer claim gives the initial use case weight. Separately, Pomerado reported 38,000+ messages, and a founder reported 7,000+ messages from an unnamed prospect in one week and said the prospect expressed willingness to pay. Those counts show activity during reported deployments. Retained teams, paid expansion, and actual payments remain unverified. (Market & Traction)

**Second-market scenario.** Assume engineering teams abroad use a similar mix of coding tools. The same install and handoff workflow could carry over, though the memo gives no buyer adoption by geography. The first likely adaptation is data handling. Buyers with different requirements for hosted session content may need self-hosting or more procurement support. The next hosted workload could add message delivery, history storage and indexing, and third-party processing costs. The next enterprise buyer could add deployment and support labor. The memo leaves those costs and their relationship to paid revenue unmeasured. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Native messaging could make a separate relay worth paying for only in a narrow slice of mixed-tool teams. Claude Code already supports messaging within its own agent teams, and the memo identifies open cross-provider messaging standards as a potential route to broader substitution. If the buyers who still need Agent Relay also require assisted self-hosting or lengthy security reviews, the self-serve funnel may produce downloads while paid accounts require hands-on work. The memo establishes neither a structural demand ceiling nor proportional delivery costs, so the Pass gate remains open pending evidence. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent cohort of engineering teams outside the US install through npm or desktop without founder-led setup, convert to paid shared sessions, and keep using cross-tool handoffs after the initial project? Install, billing, and workspace activity records showing that sequence, with routine support effort recorded, would move the action to Reach out. Downloads and trial activity alone would keep it at Watch.
