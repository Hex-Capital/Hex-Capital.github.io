# Cesara -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Cesara’s pilot terms tell me when a hospital might pay. They tell me nothing about what I would pay for the company. Post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital are all undisclosed. I won’t infer an auction from YC membership. But I also cannot underwrite a cheap, non-consensus entry without a price. The dossier supplies no round-funded experiment with a budget, deadline, and measurable result. Both financing gates remain at their absent-evidence defaults.

The strongest argument for Cesara is that its product reaches an actual cash event. Rahman reports identifying over **90% of valid underpayments** and recovering over **70% of those identified** in pilots. Those are substantially more useful claims than an agent demo: contract interpretation leads to an appeal, which leads to recovered money. Outcome-based pilots also expose the company to whether its work delivers the agreed result. The objection to passing is serious: an enterprise implementation founder may already have found a narrow workflow where customers can finance expansion through recoveries. However, the October 8 interview discloses neither the cohort nor realized fees. Identification is not collection, and collection for the hospital is not contribution profit for Cesara. With fee rates, review costs, burn, and runway absent, the profitable-path dimension stays at default.

The team fits my pattern of connecting difficult operating systems. Rahman’s Auctor background and reported healthcare implementation exposure give him more context than a founder discovering hospital billing through a market report. Lee brings inference and systems experience from Tesla AI and financial firms. That earns limited founder credit. It does not establish that they have shipped reliable reimbursement infrastructure across both hospital operations and payer adjudication. The named CEO and CTO also give me no reason to invent an equal-founder governance problem.

What does Cesara own after this workflow becomes obvious? MD Clarity already markets contract extraction and recovery, and expanded agentic analysis on **September 30, 2026**. Customer-specific mappings could become valuable, but the dossier labels that advantage as inferred. Endorsements from Ed Marx and JD Whitlock establish neither exclusive access nor a demonstrated acquisition channel. The pilots support product usefulness; they do not independently prove that owned relationships, data, or distribution caused customers to buy. That dimension remains at default. Approval thresholds and clause-linked audit trails are sensible controls, but their labor requirements could determine whether automated recovery becomes a profitable business.

I pass at **30/100**. My exceptions for AI businesses require measured commercial conversion and durable operating economics; my exceptions to conventional financing require an explicit capital plan or unusually strong external validation. Cesara’s recovery claims make those possibilities credible enough to investigate, but do not establish either exception. One verifiable condition would change my decision: **a signed financing package below $10 million post-money that fully funds a 120-day, customer-verified test of positive contribution profit from recoveries across three health systems**. Those are proposed underwriting requirements, not existing company milestones. That package would give me a price, one truth to discover, and a cash outcome against which to judge the check.
