# Peer -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Peer addresses a real $21.28 billion market, but replacing telephone and email coordination with AI does not yet imply a consequential cost collapse. The dossier quantifies brokerage spending, not the labor hours, freight waste, empty miles, or shipper costs the product can eliminate. Quoting, carrier selection, tracking, and exception handling are useful functions. They are not a 100x societal consequence unless Peer proves that automation changes freight economics rather than merely broker productivity.

The technical case is weaker. FleetWorks already automates matching across 10,000 carriers, Cartage automates communication and escalation, and Loadsmart offers instant pricing and booking APIs. Against those named incumbents, Peer discloses no proprietary architecture, benchmark, operating data, or unique workflow. “AI-native” is positioning. Without a measured improvement in cost per load, response time, exception rate, or loads handled per employee, I am being asked to finance implementation labor in a crowded category.

The founders are the strongest part of the option. Aaron Zhang has built trucking voice agents; Felix Peng shipped AI tools used by more than 250 LADWP staff; and they have worked together through Harvey Mudd, the Claremont Accelerator, and a DeepMind hackathon. The May-to-August 2026 pivot from generic workflow automation into freight suggests tactical flexibility. The bull case is that two fast learners have found a giant existing value pool where software penetration remains poor, and normal shipment activity could create proprietary pricing and carrier-reliability data. That is plausible, but the dossier does not show that customer evidence drove the pivot or that either founder has recruited brokerage, compliance, or freight-operations expertise around the risks they do not own.

The largest kill risks are already visible: FMCSA authority and the $75,000 bond, reliable handling of in-transit exceptions, carrier liquidity, and positive contribution margin after human intervention. Peer supplies no experiment, threshold, cost, or sequence for retiring them. This matters because Cartage still escalates exceptions to humans. If Peer requires similar labor while lacking an incumbent network, automation may improve the interface without improving brokerage economics.

The known-exception argument does not rescue this. A mutable team can grow an initially ordinary wedge into infrastructure, but that override requires evidence of unusual founder learning plus either early usage or a platform mechanism. Peer currently has neither disclosed shipments nor a technical discontinuity. My 36-month base case is a pivot into carrier-operations software rather than a licensed brokerage exceeding $3 million ARR.

I pass with my own money today. One verifiable result would flip me: a paid shipper pilot demonstrating at least 5x more loads handled per operations employee than a conventional broker, with positive contribution margin, FMCSA authority, and no deterioration in exception-loss rates. That would convert AI-native branding into an auditable economic discontinuity.
