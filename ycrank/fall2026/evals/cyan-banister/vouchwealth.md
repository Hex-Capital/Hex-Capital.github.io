# Vouch -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, an Indian household could have one financial agent that remembers its obligations, moves between accounts, and explains what each decision costs. Vouch’s proposed holdings aggregation and human-reviewed planning could be an early step. But first, account visibility must become reliable advice; then that advice must become trusted, recurring action. The dossier establishes neither transition yet. A WhatsApp waitlist is an invitation to that future, not an artifact showing that households have started living in it.

Arnav Ghatiwala is the strongest reason to keep looking. Leading Robinhood’s Advisor Network buildout is relevant operating work: he has encountered the machinery connecting retail investors with advice. Jane Street trading-engine and operations experience adds technical context. Those facts earn more than a résumé-only prior, but they do not establish an independently pursued obsession, an Indian household problem he personally unraveled, or his ability to recruit first believers. I can see relevant preparation. I cannot yet see the particular wound that made him need to build Vouch.

Why are these accounts managed separately? Who benefits from keeping them separate? What does the household pay for that arrangement? Vouch identifies fragmentation, but the dossier does not trace it to a particular fee, restriction, or gatekeeper. Its business model is undisclosed, so I cannot tell whether it would remove a conflicted intermediary or introduce another one. This matters more to me than the broader market’s estimated $2.3 billion size. The quiet workflow-repair pattern I like is possible here; the transfer of economic control remains unproven.

The strongest argument against my pass is that Ghatiwala knows how to build financial products, and human experts could make a narrow initial service useful before automation becomes comprehensive. Pre-seed revenue is unnecessary for that argument. But Novelty Wealth already offers SEBI-registered, fee-only advice combining aggregation, taxes, and goals. Vouch needs evidence that its particular implementation changes behavior. The reported November 2026 launch remains licensing-dependent, and the $500,000 YC investment demonstrates financing rather than customer commitment. My exceptions for companies without revenue still require something affirmative—a working prototype, unusually deep obsession, or committed users. This dossier supplies none of those substitutes.

I would pass with my own money today. There is no demonstrated enabling asset beneath the advisory interface, and proposed personalization from portfolio histories is still a hypothesis. Nor does the dossier establish dependence on one closed model, governance dysfunction, or severe privacy misconduct; those red flags should not be invented. Connecting financial holdings does make consent and data custody consequential, and human review alone does not explain either. My single flip condition is a verifiable 90-day cohort study showing households repeatedly acting on Vouch’s recommendations, with measured benefits exceeding disclosed customer fees and human-adviser costs. That would reveal whether this is a useful financial relationship people entrust with their lives.
