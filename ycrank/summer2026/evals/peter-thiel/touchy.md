# Touchy -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Touchy’s implied secret is that a third-party iPhone app can become the user’s persistent action layer before Apple’s system-level assistant does. What would prove this wrong? Users would try its voice, camera, memory, and 1,000 integrations, then return to Siri because the operating system can access the same context with less friction. Apple announced personal context, visual understanding, and cross-app actions in June 2026; Poke, Pally, Rabbit, and TwinMind are pursuing adjacent versions of the same idea. The dossier therefore describes a fashionable race, not a falsifiable truth that named peers reject.

The monopoly question is worse. “iPhone users seeking hands-free assistance” is not a narrow beachhead; it is nearly the entire platform. Native voice and image input are product features, while the integrations are available to competitors and remain subordinate to Apple’s permissions. Memories might eventually create switching costs, but resemblance to a moat is not a moat. Touchy reports no proprietary data, technical benchmark, retention curve, exclusive channel, or dominant share in a bounded segment. This is competition at the application layer beneath a platform owner building the same product.

The founders are the strongest part of the company. Rohit Agarwal brings AI-alignment research and a Berkeley EECS record; Andrew Liu previously built educational software used by roughly 500,000 students; Kevin Wang has shipped AI-data and GPU infrastructure and maintains a repository with approximately 2,500 stars. All three met as Berkeley undergraduates, so this is not an obvious networking-event team, and the essential engineering appears internal. But the dossier does not establish founder ownership or unequivocal full-time status—Rohit is still described as a Princeton PhD student—and the shift from Tokenless infrastructure to Touchy occurred within roughly two months. Long acquaintance is not yet evidence of long alignment around this company.

The strongest bull case is that unusually technical founders have already shipped a working consumer product and may discover that voice plus camera produces a habit Apple’s generalized assistant misses. Rapidly abandoning Tokenless could represent learning rather than indecision, and falling inference costs could make a $14.99 subscription viable. But none of the known-exception logic applies yet: there is no independently verified zero-to-one technical event, persistent transaction behavior under adversity, or deployed result that overrides the absence of monopoly. An App Store listing without enough ratings for an overview and a launch post with approximately 11,000 views but only 63 likes do not constitute such an event.

The revenue and distribution machinery remain mostly hypothetical. Touchy Plus supplies one intelligible engine, but even its billing interval is undisclosed; there is no paid conversion, retention, gross margin, acquisition cost, or evidence that usage compounds distribution. “The App Store plus YC launch” names a storefront and an announcement, not a repeatable customer route. Worse, every acquired user must grant privileged access to messages, location, photos, and audio while trusting third-party model processors, increasing adoption friction for a product whose autonomous-action reliability the company itself says is unresolved.

I pass. By August 2029, I expect Tokenless, Inc. either to have discontinued Touchy or repositioned around B2B agent-routing and safety infrastructure, where this team’s technical strengths matter more and Apple is not the direct platform substitute. One condition would reverse the judgment: a disclosed post–Siri AI cohort in one narrowly named user segment showing at least 40% week-12 retention and 15% paid conversion from users completing five or more cross-app actions weekly. That would begin to transform the pitch from a general assistant slogan into a behavioral secret with an observable beachhead.
