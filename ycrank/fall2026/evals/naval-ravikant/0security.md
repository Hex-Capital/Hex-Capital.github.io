# 0.security -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Doruk has found vulnerabilities that someone else had to fix. The JSONata advisory credits his account; his cyber-defense work at Migros adds relevant operating experience. Those are useful signals of specific knowledge. The founders’ reported exits have undisclosed terms and uncertain relevance, so I give them little additional weight. At 0.security, the strongest evidence is already inside the problem.

The investment question is whether that knowledge becomes an asset the company owns. Repository context, deterministic scanning, parallel agents and remediation pull requests form a useful workflow. But scheduled execution and dedicated sandboxes are reproducible capabilities. The dossier establishes neither exclusive vulnerability data nor a proprietary model, and retained customer context has no demonstrated performance advantage. I credit the research under founder ability; it cannot also serve as proof of a moat.

The strongest argument for investing is that security expertise could compound through deployment. Real investigations might generate difficult examples, verified exploits and corrections that improve subsequent investigations. The public harness had 526 stars and 58 forks on October 27, 2026, providing a plausible route to developers without enterprise permission. This fits my preference for tools that turn individual knowledge into scalable output. Yet forks do not establish useful contributions, referrals or improvements for other customers. Permissionless access is present; customer-created compounding remains unproven.

There is room to maneuver economically. A developer scanning a repository can lead into an enterprise buying continuous application testing and remediation through the same investigation engine. Own-cloud deployment and audit logs support that buyer transition. The $10.65 billion application-security estimate supplies context, while Aikido’s reported $20 million ARR establishes actual spending in an overlapping market. Neither establishes 0.security’s obtainable share. Aikido also makes the central difficulty concrete: a funded incumbent already combines code security with autonomous testing.

The $30 monthly subscription, usage credits and custom enterprise pricing identify a direct payer and a monetization mechanism. They do not establish completed purchases or profitable investigations. Reported work with EY deserves attention, but the dossier does not identify contract economics. Likewise, the Spotify engineer’s account of scanning nearly 30,000 files in under a minute supports utility, without establishing exploit quality or a corporate purchase. These distinctions matter because an inexpensive scan can become an expensive investigation.

I would keep watching rather than write a check today. My exceptions for early products require demonstrated product insight, unusual capital efficiency or emerging self-reinforcing behavior strong enough to override the missing criterion; the available evidence does not establish that override here. No documented integrity issue or artificial financing deadline drives my hesitation. The founders are credible; durable advantage is unresolved. My flip condition is one independently reproducible benchmark showing that a legally reusable corpus accumulated through customer investigations materially improves verified vulnerability discovery over the identical system without that corpus. That would turn deployment history into evidence of compounding ownership.
