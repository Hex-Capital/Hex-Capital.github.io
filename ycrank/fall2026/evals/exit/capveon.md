# Capveon -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic sale to a procurement intelligence platform such as GovSpend. Capveon says its product links early agency records to projects and checks specifications, drawings, and addenda against a supplier’s capabilities. A buyer could add that workflow to its existing spending and meeting intelligence, giving customers a way to turn early leads into qualified bids. The memo records GovSpend’s majority acquisition as evidence of transaction activity in the category. Capveon’s buyer interest, customer savings, and acquisition pricing remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The October launch provides an operating signal. Capveon reports broad agency coverage and previously claimed paying customers for its workflow-handoff product. The relevant seed milestone is repeat paid use of the current specification workflow, with measured improvements in bid qualification time or accuracy. A six-to-twelve-month test using the existing product is a planning assumption. Pilot commitments, funding, and integration costs remain unknown. Product ownership, data rights, and customer contract assignability also remain unverified. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

With investor terms unknown, the benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× gross cash objective is $2.5 million within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale scenarios assume an eligible holder sells the full stake within eight years, receives cash at closing, and participates pro rata with no senior preference claims.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Gross holder cash |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M of assumed net debt and deal adjustments gives $45M of distributable equity | $1.35M |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M of assumed net debt and deal adjustments gives $90M of distributable equity | $2.70M |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or funded payout | $0 |

The strong case clears the objective by $200,000. At 2.5% ownership it yields $2.25 million. The assumed sale prices illustrate the hurdle. The memo supplies no buyer economics or priced transaction evidence to support either valuation. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

GovSpend already searches meeting records and ranks opportunities, while GovSignals offers cited bid analysis and proposal drafting. A buyer could extend an existing workflow and avoid paying an acquisition premium. An incomplete agency feed or a missed addendum could also undermine the lead time and accuracy that make Capveon worth acquiring. This supports Watch. The overlap makes a build decision credible, while the memo establishes neither a complete substitute for Capveon’s infrastructure specification workflow nor a supported sale-price ceiling that would trigger Pass. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a procurement intelligence platform value Capveon’s specification workflow above the holder’s proceeds hurdle once its benefit is demonstrated in paid production use? Repeatable reductions in bid-review time or qualification errors, paired with a credible buyer-side valuation bridge that clears $2.5 million in holder cash within eight years, would move the action to Reach out. A supported valuation range that leaves the holder materially short even at its upper end would move it to Pass.
