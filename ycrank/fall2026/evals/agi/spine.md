# Spine -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** The memo describes early-stage founders and operations leads handling payroll exceptions, carrier coordination, employee questions, and compliance deadlines themselves despite using HR software. Spine says it takes over that work in the customer’s existing systems, uses AI to draft responses for specialist review, and charges employers $0 while earning carrier commissions. If automation cuts the full labor cost of handling these workflows, commissions could fund ongoing delegated HR operations for startups that previously found outsourcing unaffordable. The memo gives no before-and-after labor measurements or evidence that adopters previously went without outsourced support because of cost. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

At model parity, Spine’s strongest reason for customers to stay is the execution it takes responsibility for as broker of record. Specialists coordinate with carriers and complete payroll, enrollments, and filings in the customer’s existing systems. The appointment gives Spine a role in delivery, with durability still untested at renewal. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*)

The founders report 30+ startups using Spine and about 15% average healthcare cost savings. The memo also reports roughly 4.4× commission revenue growth over six months. These signals support early demand, with repeat commitments and the starting revenue base still unknown. The founders’ prior work together, firsthand experience with startup HR administration, and Cokar’s legal training support seed-stage execution. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*)

The expansion unit is one startup account over a year. At $0 in employer fees, carrier commissions must cover onboarding and the full cost of successful delivery, including specialist review, retries, payroll exceptions, carrier coordination, compliance work, and software. The reported healthcare savings measure customer benefit spending. Account-level commissions, labor time, and delivery costs remain unquantified. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Carrier commissions could explain the $0 offer even if AI saves too little labor to open a new market. The memo identifies existing overlap in Rippling’s HR and compliance support and Bennie’s brokerage and benefits administration. With equivalent models, a rival that matches Spine’s hands-on execution could give customers a credible alternative at renewal. Spine also depends on carrier commissions and continued access to third-party HR systems. These facts leave both Path B conditions unresolved. The memo contains neither a representative cost comparison nor observed switching to an equivalent service, so the path remains open for a test. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a representative cohort of startups that previously handled HR in-house because outsourcing was unaffordable reach its first renewal with positive contribution for Spine and choose to retain Spine after evaluating a functionally equivalent provider with comparable model quality? Verify the prior outsourcing decision, track labor and full delivery cost per successfully completed workflow and realized commissions through renewal, and document why customers renew. Evidence that automation makes the service viable and that customers renew for Spine’s execution would move the action to Reach out. A representative result showing that full delivery costs erase the savings needed to serve this segment, or that customers readily switch under parity, would move it to Pass.
