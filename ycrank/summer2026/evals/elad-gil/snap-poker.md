# Snap Poker -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: interstate compacts expanded the addressable U.S. player pool by more than 50% to 38 million people, Snap Poker wants those players to create and consume poker content inside the game, and the controlling variable is whether that content loop produces enough simultaneous player liquidity to overcome fragmented regulation and zero-rake economics.

Player liquidity is the right variable, but the dossier does not show Snap Poker controlling it. There is no launch data, creator pipeline, referral rate, concurrent-player threshold, or evidence that followers become active table participants. The native public feed is a coherent hypothesis, yet GGPoker already offers video, voice, reactions, and shareable hands. Snap Poker therefore needs to prove that a follower graph changes acquisition and liquidity, not merely that poker hands make watchable content.

The institutional shift is real but insufficient. Pennsylvania’s 2025 compact entry and Michigan’s 2026 approval enlarge shared tables, while the $4.7 billion global market is growing an estimated 10.2% annually. However, Snap Poker has disclosed no licenses, and state-by-state eligibility still constrains distribution. Zero rake may attract players, but absent another monetization mechanism it removes the industry’s established revenue engine rather than demonstrating superior margins. “Monetize like a game” is not yet a business model.

The strongest bull case is that the feed becomes both product and distribution: creators publish interactive hands, audiences follow them into games, deeper tables improve the experience, and the resulting liquidity attracts more creators. That could compound faster than a conventional poker operator buying players. The problem is that every link remains inferred. An incumbent can reproduce feed mechanics more easily than Snap Poker can reproduce its licenses, liquidity, trust systems, and operating scale. No current content library, proprietary graph, or creator commitment reverses that asymmetry.

The product does attempt to own an integrated loop—play, narrate, publish, follow, and return—rather than exporting a hand to another social network. That earns some architectural credit. But the website was inaccessible on August 26, the team appears to have pivoted from CueBench during the prior month, and there is no technical proof that the loop works. The disclosed feature set is close enough to GGPoker’s existing capabilities that this currently looks like product packaging, not a durable change in poker economics.

The founders have meaningful early repetitions: Dillon Mehta and Rishan Hemrajani previously built CueBench, Neel Gadde worked at YC-backed Nuntius, and the team has engineering and research credentials. That is better than résumé-only evidence. It is not evidence of poker operations, gambling compliance, costly commitment, or lessons from a prior outcome. The known-exception logic does not rescue the company: there is neither a rare frontier technology team whose asset retains strategic value nor a demonstrated reconstruction of physical or regulated-market margins.

I pass. My 36-month base case is that this team either shuts Snap Poker down or pivots toward play-money poker communities and creator tooling before reaching $1 million in annualized revenue. The one condition that would flip me is a licensed launch reaching 10,000 weekly real-money players across at least two compact states, with 30% or more acquired through shared-hand content and 25% eight-week cohort retention. That would demonstrate that the feed actually creates liquidity rather than decorating it.
