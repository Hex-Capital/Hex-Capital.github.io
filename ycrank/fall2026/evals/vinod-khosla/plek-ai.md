# Plek AI -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Plek AI reports finding $30 million of annual EBITDA opportunities in a 3,600-person company. The consequential question is how much became real productivity without destroying useful work. Identifying compensation, location and automation scenarios can improve a spreadsheet while leaving the underlying production system unchanged. The dossier supplies neither realized savings nor the spending baseline against which to judge that $30 million. I cannot turn an opportunity estimate into an orders-of-magnitude consequence.

The strongest evidence is the mechanism: map an employee census onto 50,000 jobs and 250,000 tasks, then reconcile interventions employee by employee. Avoiding double-counted savings matters. Pierre Bouquet’s task-estimation research gives this more substance than a generic AI interface. But matching an unnamed adviser’s six-week assessment does not establish a 5–10x technical discontinuity. Plek discloses no comparable processing time, validation method or error rate. Orgvue already offers role classification; Workhelix connects task opportunities to measured AI usage. The decisive advantage would be reliably predicting which interventions actually work.

The bull case deserves attention. Bouquet has an MIT PhD, founded the MIT Work Analytics Lab and previously built Biot, with a reported 2022 exit. A researcher who has also built a company could turn expensive expert analysis into repeatable software. Plek’s operating-partners summit appearance supplies a concrete route to relevant buyers. This fits my broader willingness to fund execution businesses and research-derived workflows. However, the research code and data are public, exclusive rights are undisclosed, and the proposed proprietary learning loop remains an inference. Neither a material belief revision nor complementary recruiting against a named weakness is documented. Relevant credentials are affirmative evidence of domain knowledge; they do not establish those separate founder behaviors.

Plek’s largest kill risk is whether census-derived estimates predict achievable operational improvements. The dossier gives no experiment with a threshold, budget or stop condition, so I assign the missing-evidence default rather than inventing a de-risking plan. Negotiated statements of work also leave implementation labor and software economics unresolved. There is no disclosed cost model against consulting. The familiar AI red flag—competition through implementation rather than proprietary capability—is a risk here, not an established fact. Likewise, accuracy disclaimers do not prove inaccurate output. What matters is buying evidence before scaling distribution.

I pass on Plek AI today. My known exceptions allow weak initial technical differentiation when a documented instigator can rebuild a giant existing value pool, or incomplete milestones when the quantified consequence overwhelms the uncertainty. This dossier establishes neither override. Its adjacent $2.92 billion workforce-analytics estimate does not quantify the value Plek itself can unlock. One verifiable result would change my decision: an independently audited, paid deployment demonstrating at least 10x lower total assessment cost than a named advisory firm while achieving comparable, subsequently verified net savings, including human review and implementation costs. That would connect the research mechanism to an economic discontinuity. Today, that connection remains unproven.
