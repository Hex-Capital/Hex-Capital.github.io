# Coasty -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Prateek Jannu left Stanford to build Coasty, and Coasty’s 82.81% OSWorld Verified result makes the founders’ technical conviction its highest-scoring asset. Coasty’s lowest-scoring asset is the category containing that conviction: a horizontal computer-use agent exposed to foundation-model commoditization. The signature is a missionary operator trapped inside a consensus asset class—real technical work, but packaged in the exact AI-infrastructure sleeve where entry price and durable ownership matter more than another benchmark lead.

**Drop.io** turned conviction into a multi-year operating crank against free file-sharing incumbents; Coasty shares that willingness to build through an initially inadequate technical substrate, beginning in summer 2025 and publishing replayable benchmark evidence rather than merely narrating a moat. Prateek’s Stanford departure, Nitish Kovuru’s vision background, and the verified 82.81% score put Coasty above the founder-conviction midpoint. Coasty has not reached the Drop.io rung, however: the March 2026 “AI employee” product became a developer-controlled legacy-software platform by July, leaving one year of work and one material positioning tack—not yet proof of missionary endurance through nonlinear time.

**Fin** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism here: a horizontal AI assistant can contain impressive engineering while the underlying agent layer becomes somebody else’s feature, forcing the business to pivot toward a narrower workflow. Coasty already moved from isolated consumer/SMB “AI employee” VMs across 10 departments to developer APIs for legacy back-office software. Coasty also faces UiPath’s $1.43 billion revenue base, Adept’s $415 million financing, and foundation-model providers able to improve screen perception underneath it. The Wizard-of-Oz offer to build customer proofs of concept is attractive, but the category remains almost a perfect instance of my “AI is just more internet” red flag.

Coasty’s $0.03–$0.10 action pricing says nothing about the price of Coasty’s equity, and no round size, post-money valuation, competitive process, or tier-one pass is disclosed. No evidence—applying the 9/30 absent-evidence default. Over the next 18 months, benchmark parity from UiPath or a model provider would compress Coasty’s API pricing while forcing Coasty to spend on managed VMs, regulated-workflow support, and sales; that is how a superficially cheap usage product becomes a five-tack SaaS financing regatta. Without evidence of a discounted entry, I cannot call Coasty mispriced merely because it is early.

Thousands of active users and paying customers across four continents give Coasty more downside cushion than the ordinary pre-seed agent deck, while prepaid credits and BYOK calls could keep Coasty default-alive by shifting model expense to customers. The capital-structure score would flip into the invest range if those users belong to the current developer product, produce durable gross margin, and fund narrow workflow expansion without repeated infrastructure rounds. Today the March-to-July product discontinuity, unquantified revenue, and VM-heavy delivery model leave Coasty halfway between a capital-efficient metered utility and a standard agent company requiring successive rounds to defend temporary model performance.

**John Fish / Bookshelved** had distribution embedded in the asset: the creator and the customer channel were inseparable. Coasty has respectable founder reach—4,000 LinkedIn followers for Prateek and 3,000 for Nitish—but Coasty’s owned audience is only 315 company followers, while its Product Hunt launch produced 84 upvotes and three comments. Those are promotional surfaces, not a community on the cap table. Coasty’s reported paying users matter commercially, but they do not yet demonstrate that either founder can acquire customers because operators specifically trust or follow him.

Coasty’s 82.81% benchmark result will attract enough usage to keep the regatta moving, but by July 2029 I expect Coasty to have abandoned the horizontal computer-use API, pivoted into healthcare payer-portal automation, and reached roughly $2–5 million ARR rather than becoming an independent agent-infrastructure platform. Coasty’s strongest asset—the founders’ ability to build and recover through software failures—should survive the pivot; its weakest asset—the generic agent category—should not. Mock healthcare portals and replayable audit trails already point toward the narrower product, even though regulated production evidence does not yet exist.

Thousands of paying users, an 82.81% verified benchmark, and prepaid BYOK economics form the strongest bull case: Coasty could be a **Pinterest**-style exception where missionary founders override a consensus category, or a **Ro**-style exception where regulated complexity turns apparent capital intensity into the moat. That case stings because Coasty has materially more validation than most pre-seed companies. It still loses today because the user claim is not separated from the prior product, the healthcare demonstration used mock portals, Coasty’s terms permit broad processing of sensitive session data, and current-product revenue is undisclosed. One verifiable condition flips my verdict: Coasty reaching $1 million ARR from the current developer product at greater than 70% gross margin with twelve-month customer retention.
