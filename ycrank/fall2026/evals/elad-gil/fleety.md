# Fleety -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Fleety’s opportunity is to turn call information into better technician assignments for plumbing and HVAC operators; the controlling variable is incremental gross profit per completed job relative to native dispatch software. The company describes shops handling 100+ jobs daily and a $500,000 annual revenue gap between their best and average technicians. That creates a plausible optimization problem. It does not establish that dispatch causes the gap, or that reassignment can close it without sacrificing utilization elsewhere. I can identify the variable to underwrite, but Fleety has not yet supplied evidence that it controls the outcome.

The competitive baseline matters more than the broad market estimate. ServiceTitan already assigns technicians using predicted job value and technician performance, and reported $961 million in FY2026 revenue. Fleety therefore needs to improve an existing workflow sufficiently to support a separate purchase. Compatibility with ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, and Jobber creates a possible opening across platforms. It does not establish an economic advantage. The August 2025 voice infrastructure improvement cited in the dossier primarily helps call handling; there is no quantified connection to better dispatch economics. I give the technology-shift dimension its absent-evidence default.

The strongest case for investing is that the founders understand the operational details incumbents miss. Both worked as dispatchers. Ben Zhou previously built a roofing business and Timrep, which reached six-figure revenue; Ariyaman Molanorouzi previously ran property-management automation covering 3,000+ units and a six-figure-ARR SEO business. Those are meaningful prior operating experiences, credited to founder learning rather than Fleety’s traction. Weeks spent inside customer shops could produce a better product than a centrally designed CRM feature. Albert Nahman, Ace Plumbing, and Apex Plumbing appearing as customer references make that possibility more credible, although website references establish less than verified recurring use or paid expansion.

Fleety also has more substance than a document summarizer. Its advertised product combines transcripts, equipment records, technician matching, schedule changes, and dispatcher review. Better models could improve the quality of those actions while the application retains the operational context. That earns workflow credit. It does not establish exclusive outcome data or a defensible learning loop. My pattern of backing adjacent products through an existing customer channel could eventually apply here, but onsite onboarding is not yet evidence of reusable distribution. The immediate red flag is that a well-distributed incumbent already supplies overlapping functionality.

I would pass on a personal check today, despite a mixed rather than actively adverse score. The market gate fails because the economic step change remains unproven. None of the exceptions supplies an override: this is neither a documented frontier-research opportunity nor a category incumbents have abandoned. My single flip condition is a paid, 90-day controlled deployment across the three referenced operators showing at least 10% higher gross profit per technician-day, net of Fleety’s fees and implementation costs, against each shop’s existing dispatch system. That is my underwriting threshold, not a reported result. It would demonstrate that the founders’ operating insight creates measurable value beyond an available feature.
