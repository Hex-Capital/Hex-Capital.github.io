# GatherHQ -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

GatherHQ has evidence that enterprises want faster buyer research. It has not established what becomes radically cheaper if its technology works. Research, interviewing, and campaign production consume real budgets, but the dossier supplies neither a spending baseline nor a quantified expansion in access to expertise. “10× growth,” with the metric unspecified, cannot establish a 10× consequence. I can lose my entire check on a consequential technical experiment. Here, the consequence worth purchasing remains undefined.

The potentially important mechanism is converting real interviews into reusable buyer models. Gather reports 300,000 interviews and thousands more monthly; that could become an experimental asset. But accumulated interviews become a learning advantage only if the company can use them lawfully and demonstrate improving predictive validity. Neither follows from corpus size. Gather itself cautions against treating simulations as statistical predictions. Outset already offers interview-grounded digital twins, and Listen Labs combines interviews with simulations. There is no measured capability advantage against either. I leave technical discontinuity at the missing-evidence default; overlapping features do not prove that the underlying technology is trivial.

The strongest argument against passing is unusually concrete. Gather reports 65-plus enterprise marketing teams, including Fortinet and Envoy, while Cover Genius independently credits research covering 1,392 consumers in seven countries. Mayank and Anand have built companies together, and Jay has sold enterprise software alongside them. The move from restaurant and retail feedback into market research demonstrates a willingness to change direction. Those facts make an execution-led business credible, consistent with the broader commercial businesses my investment pattern permits. They earn founder credit. They do not establish fast, evidence-driven belief revision or recruitment specifically aimed at the simulation-validity risk. Nor do prior acquisitions imply that these founders are optimizing for an exit.

The immediate experiment should test whether the buyer models preserve information that matters to an actual decision. The dossier offers no completed prospective validation, numerical acceptance threshold, experiment budget, or stop condition. That leaves risk-removal discipline unproven. Economics are similarly unresolved: $10 simulations, $50 consumer interviews, and $100 B2B interviews are selling prices, not fully loaded production costs or demonstrated savings against human research. Recruitment, moderation, quality control, and model maintenance must all enter that calculation. A Growth job posting alone does not establish the red flag of scaling before resolving technical risk.

Gather’s marketing-to-campaign workflow also raises my concern about advertising tools, although customer research has uses beyond advertising and the dossier does not justify treating the whole business as an excluded category. The more decisive issue is that distribution may outrun differentiation: Salesforce’s announced Listen Labs acquisition could place overlapping capabilities inside existing enterprise relationships. I have made exceptions for execution-led platforms and extraordinary founders attacking enormous existing value pools. Gather’s repeat team makes that exception worth examining, but neither the platform economics nor the quantified consequence is established here. **I pass.** One result would move me into active diligence: an independently audited prospective study showing that Gather’s simulations reproduce decision-relevant findings from fresh human research at one-tenth the fully loaded cost, under a prespecified accuracy threshold. That would turn the central claim into a testable economic discontinuity.
