# Runtime.si -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Robot deployers can spend hours collecting teleoperated demonstrations and fine-tuning a policy when a manipulation task changes. Runtime searches for actions in a reconstructed scene and updates the simulation using physical feedback. It reports matching or beating unnamed robot foundation models on tasks including rope threading and egg grasping. The memo records a November 4 policy launch, while the company’s experiments leave trial counts, success rates, and labor savings undisclosed. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

**Scenario assumption.** A small-batch operator keeps frequently changing jobs manual because setup labor makes each run too short to justify automation. The expansion unit is one more task change on an installed robot. Runtime could make those runs economical if savings in demonstration and tuning labor outweigh planning compute, operator review, retries, and recovery, after allocating installation and maintenance costs per successful run. Under model parity, the operator might still pay Runtime for reliable execution across task changes. RoboSkillHub documents reusable components and physical verification, an early basis for that hypothesis. The memo contains no evidence of buyer preference under parity or of a fleet whose physical feedback improves performance across deployments. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The founders’ work in control and manipulation, together with the launched policy, supports seed-stage execution. The early-access signup and launch attention provide no evidence of target-buyer commitments. The pilot references concern the earlier Lambda data-center product. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Short demonstrations or recombined skills could cut setup labor enough to serve the same jobs. Simify’s research demonstration and open GPU physics tools give rivals a route to simulation search, while Runtime’s live planning latency and deployment overhead could erase its savings. The memo contains no representative comparison that settles either the cost threshold or buyer choice under parity. Both gates remain open for a test, supporting Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent operator pay for a second short-run job that it previously kept manual after comparing Runtime with a functionally equivalent alternative on the same robot? A paid repeat at a viable price would move the action to Reach out if the trial shows that lower setup labor brings full cost per successful run below the operator’s automation threshold and that the buyer chooses Runtime for measured execution reliability. A representative trial showing that Runtime misses the cost threshold or that the buyer can switch without losing the outcome would close Path B and move the action to Pass.
