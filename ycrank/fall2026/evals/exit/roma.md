# Roma -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Roma’s strongest exit path is a strategic acquisition by a productivity suite such as ClickUp or Notion. Roma describes a product that hands tasks across Claude, ChatGPT, and Muse and preserves decisions, edits, and attribution in a shared history. A buyer could acquire those workflows to serve teams that already use multiple agents. The economic rationale depends on whether those teams complete more work or stay longer than they would using the buyer’s existing tools. The memo provides no measured buyer benefit or evidence of buyer interest. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The App Store listing showed 35 US ratings, and its version history recorded updates addressing missed agent pickups. Roma reported that some 2.0 users connected at least 10 agents. A 90-day paid-team pilot could measure repeat cross-agent handoffs, successful completion, and time saved against the buyer’s native workflow. Roma has shipped enough functionality to make that test plausible. Paid conversion, cohort retention, and the budget and runway for the test remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, starting ownership of 5% diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× return requires $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale cases below are illustrative. Both assume the holder can sell its entire stake, receives its pro rata share in cash by year eight, and faces no senior preference that redirects proceeds.

- **Modest sale.** A $40 million enterprise value with zero net debt and deal adjustments yields $40 million in distributable equity and $1.2 million to the holder, or 2.4×.
- **Strong sale.** A $100 million enterprise value less $10 million in assumed net debt and deal adjustments yields $90 million in distributable equity and $2.7 million to the holder, or 5.4×. If those deductions rise to $20 million, the holder receives $2.4 million, or 4.8×.
- **Prolonged private.** If Roma keeps operating and reinvests available cash, the holder realizes $0 within eight years. The memo provides no evidence of a funded payout or an eligible secondary for Roma holders. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

ClickUp already offers assignable agents, and Notion already connects its agents to external tools through MCP. Either could extend its own product if customers get little added value from Roma’s shared history. Roma also relies on third-party services for task execution, and the memo leaves integration continuity after a change of control unverified. These risks weaken the buy-versus-build case. The memo establishes neither a closing barrier nor a supported sale range that leaves the holder materially short, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a productivity-suite buyer value Roma’s cross-agent workflow enough to support a sale that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? A buyer-validated paid pilot showing sustained gains over the suite’s native workflow, paired with an indicative valuation range that clears the hurdle after plausible deductions, would change the action to Reach out.
