# Yam -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**New Economies** is Yam’s strongest path. In an authored branching romance game, creators spend time writing and maintaining paths in advance. Yam lets a player specify a world and role, then generates a romance around a cast with persistent memories and relationships. The potentially newly viable use is a coherent, player-directed romance arc in a niche setting that would attract too few players to justify dedicated writing. The live product supports the delivery mechanism. The memo leaves both the former authoring cost and Yam’s cost per coherent, completed arc unmeasured. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

Yam’s public catalog covers 112 fandoms, giving players a reason to browse and creators a place to distribute their games. Yam rewards creators as players progress. That could improve the catalog as more creators join, but the effect on existing players and their preference under model parity remain untested. The top listing displays 2.4 million plays, and Yam reports one million cumulative beta play hours. Those are usage signals. Paid conversion, repeat purchases, and willingness to pay for the newly viable use remain unknown. (*Defensibility*, *The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

The three-person team shipped the romance product through beta and public launch, and the CTO previously built TikTok creator tools. The memo describes no test plan for measuring story quality and cost as usage grows. As an illustrative scenario, a $9.99 subscriber taking 100 turns a day over 30 days generates about $0.0033 in gross subscription revenue per turn before fees, moderation, and free-tier usage. Contribution per completed arc depends on inference cost, retries, and how often players reach a coherent ending. (*Founders & Team*, *Market & Traction*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Yam may be making an established entertainment category cheaper to supply. The memo describes existing authored romance games and AI roleplay products, and Yam relies on external model providers. A rival with comparable story quality and persistence could make it easy for players to switch. Rights-holder takedowns can also remove fan games from the catalog. The memo establishes credible substitutes and catalog risk, while actual switching under functional parity and the viability of previously uneconomic stories remain untested. Both New Economies gates remain open, and the memo supports no independent scarce-complement path. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will players repeatedly pay Yam for niche romance arcs that were too costly to author, even when a comparable product offers equivalent story quality and persistence? A paid cohort test would compare authoring labor and total delivery cost per coherent, completed arc, then measure repeat purchases when players can choose either product. A material cost reduction, positive contribution, and repeat paid preference for Yam would move the action to **Reach out**. Ready switching at parity would close the path and move the action to **Pass**.
