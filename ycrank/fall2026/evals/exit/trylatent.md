# Latent -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Latent’s strongest path to private shareholder cash. The memo records Cisco’s acquisition of Galileo, which shows that a security vendor has bought a runtime AI guardrails business. Latent reports that its activation-based detector stopped 99% of rule-breaking agents versus 69% for a frontier judge in one sandbox benchmark. A model-serving platform or security vendor could have a reason to own Latent if integrating its detector and calibration workflow across customer deployments reduces costly agent failures and keeps latency low, with an advantage the buyer could retain through ownership. That advantage remains an inference. The memo gives no acquisition price for Galileo and no buyer economics for Latent. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The operating milestone is a paid, self-hosted production deployment that maintains a detection advantage over available alternatives at acceptable false-hold rates, latency, and cost. Latent’s public launch, Python SDK, and advertised founder-led pilots make that test plausible for a seed company. The memo records a one-person team and an unconfirmed $125,000 funding estimate. It gives no paid pilot results, test budget, burn, or runway. IP assignment, customer contract and data rights, and required consents are unverified. The memo provides no usable evidence of an independent route to holder cash. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** A $500,000 check at $10 million post-money buys 5% initially and leaves 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in holder cash, or $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume an all-cash closing with zero net debt, fees, or preference adjustments. Enterprise value therefore equals distributable equity, allocated pro rata before investor taxes. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. The scenario prices are illustrative.

| Scenario | Timing and outcome | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 at $50 million enterprise value | $1.5 million, 3× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 at $100 million enterprise value | $3 million, 6× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested | $0, 0×, with shares still held |

At 2% ownership, even the illustrative strong sale pays only $2 million, or 4×. The operating evidence supports testing the acquisition mechanism. A price that clears the hurdle remains unsubstantiated.

### Countercase

Goodfire already distributes activation-based monitoring through Baseten, with review, refusal, and rerouting. Latent also lets customers export labeled examples, which may reduce the cost of switching. A prospective buyer could secure much of the same benefit through a partnership or license, limiting what it would pay for ownership. The memo establishes a credible substitute but leaves production equivalence and the resulting price ceiling unresolved. That gap supports Watch. The evidence establishes neither a structural price cap nor that prolonged private ownership is the probable outcome. (Defensibility, Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Latent demonstrate in paid, self-hosted production that its detector prevents enough costly failures, at acceptable latency and false-hold rates, to give a platform buyer a reason to own it at the proceeds hurdle? A side-by-side comparison against an available licensed or partnered monitor, with measured economics that could plausibly support an acquisition at that hurdle, would move the action to Reach out. If the alternative delivers comparable results at materially lower cost than ownership, the action would move to Pass.
