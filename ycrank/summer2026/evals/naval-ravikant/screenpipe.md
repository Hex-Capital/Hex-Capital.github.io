# screenpipe -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Louis Beaumont has already paid the tuition for this problem. He maintained an AI “second brain” from 2020, worked in infrastructure and machine learning, and converted that knowledge into a cross-platform product with 12,176 repository commits, 20.9K GitHub stars, and paying users. This is specific knowledge revealed through sustained action, not credentials arranged around a fashionable market. The strongest evidence is not the claimed 300K users; it is that one founder repeatedly shipped through capture failures, database corruption, transcription, permissions, encryption, and three operating systems.

The traction is credible but uneven. In February 2025, screenpipe reported 202 subscribers, $3,480 MRR, 4K monthly downloads, and 320 weekly active users. By mid-2026, Beaumont reported sixfold active-user growth and 20–30% weekly revenue growth. Prices of $21 per month for Basic and $42 per seat for Business prove direct value capture. But 20.9K stars beside 320 historical weekly actives also shows the difference between developer attention and durable behavior. Stars are applause; retained payments are evidence.

The product has implementation depth but not yet a defensible substrate. Cross-platform screen and audio capture, accessibility extraction, local search, MCP access, and permissioned agents are difficult to make reliable. They are not difficult for a well-capitalized competitor to attempt. The stack uses available operating-system APIs, SQLite, Whisper, OCR, and MCP; local storage and exportability weaken data lock-in; Microsoft can bundle Recall into Windows. Organization-specific histories and SOPs could become proprietary context, but “could become” is not an asset already compounding.

The permissionless pattern is only partially present. The source-available project has 2.1K forks and generated organic subscriptions, yet the dossier supplies no contribution, referral, or collaboration evidence showing that one customer improves screenpipe for another. The June 2026 license change may further trade community leverage for direct monetization. Capital efficiency can excuse an unproven user loop at pre-seed; it cannot convert a hypothetical loop into a network effect. Nor is there evidence of the behavioral red flags that would veto the investment—unfair dealing, artificial deadlines, or reassurance-seeking—but founder integrity remains unproven rather than affirmed.

The market is broad enough to maneuver. Persistent work context can expand from personal recall into meeting memory, enterprise search, agent supervision, workflow discovery, and organization-specific automation. The same capture layer plausibly serves those adjacencies, while the $11.2 billion productivity-tools market and Otter’s reported $100 million ARR establish real spending. The bear case still stings: privacy restrictions may prevent the richest enterprise capture, incumbents own distribution, and every new customer may require proportional security review and deployment labor. That would make screenpipe a useful feature business with a large repository, not a compounding platform.

I would make an option-sized investment. Within 36 months, I expect screenpipe to reach $5–12 million ARR, primarily through Business and enterprise deployments rather than consumer subscriptions. The founder’s shipping velocity and early monetization can produce that outcome without a network effect; the missing network is why I would not underwrite a defining platform yet. My verifiable flip condition is paid-team twelve-month retention: if it remains below 50% by August 2027, I would pass because accumulated work history is not creating the claimed switching value.
