## The Idea

- **Problem:** Employers with under 200 employees lack visibility into benefits pricing while relying on brokers or PEOs; Ignition’s survey found 39% of respondents who recalled their renewal experienced double-digit increases ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/ignition-benefits); [Risk & Insurance, August 2026](https://riskandinsurance.com/small-businesses-absorb-double-digit-health-insurance-hikes-with-little-insight-into-costs/)).
- **Approach:** Ignition analyzes workforce health risks, solicits carrier alternatives, and manages enrollment, renewals, compliance, and employee questions through software with licensed experts involved ([Ignition](https://www.ignitionbenefits.com/)).
- **Differentiation:** Ignition offers independent brokerage without the co-employment arrangement of PEOs such as Insperity, while documented Gusto customers retained payroll services and moved benefits brokerage to Ignition ([Ignition comparison](https://www.ignitionbenefits.com/blog/insperity-review); [AWM case study](https://www.ignitionbenefits.com/customers/awm)).
- **Business Model:** Carriers compensate Ignition as broker, with no separate employer fee; commission percentages and realized company revenue have no public data found ([YC Launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/TzC-ignition-the-ai-native-benefits-broker-for-modern-companies); [Ignition pricing disclosure](https://www.ignitionbenefits.com/blog/insperity-review)).
- **TAM/SAM:** Emergen Research estimates the broader US employee-benefit-broker market at $12.5B in 2024, growing at 5.1% CAGR through 2034; no public SAM data found for employers under 200 employees ([Emergen Research, updated 2026](https://www.emergenresearch.com/industry-report/us-employee-benefit-broker-market)).
- **GTM / Distribution:** Distribution includes search content, partnerships, and an ambassador program paying $1,000 after a referred prospect meets qualification requirements and supplies an employee census ([Scalerrs, October 2026](https://www.scalerrs.co/case-studies/ignition); [Ignition ambassador program](https://www.ignitionbenefits.com/blog/you-know-the-companies-we-know-benefits)).

## Key Risks

- **Carrier compensation incentives:** [Inferred]: Carrier-paid compensation creates potential tension between employer savings and brokerage income; Ignition states that compensation is disclosed upfront ([YC Launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/TzC-ignition-the-ai-native-benefits-broker-for-modern-companies); [Ignition disclosure](https://www.ignitionbenefits.com/blog/insperity-review)).
- **Savings depend on workforce risk:** Ignition states higher-risk groups see smaller savings, limiting applicability of its published customer outcomes ([Ignition comparison](https://www.ignitionbenefits.com/blog/insperity-review)).
- **Incumbent acquisition of overlapping technology:** [Inferred]: Alliant’s announced Nava acquisition combines similar AI capabilities with an established brokerage operation, potentially narrowing product differentiation ([Alliant, August 2026](https://alliant.com/news-resources/alliant-insurance-services-to-acquire-nava-creating-an-ai-native-model-for-the-future-of-employee-benefits/)).
- **Sensitive-data exposure:** [Inferred]: Workforce-data processing and external integrations create confidentiality and vendor-dependency risks; published protections include encrypted transit, access controls, and security reviews ([Ignition privacy policy, effective March 2026](https://www.ignitionbenefits.com/privacy-policy)).
