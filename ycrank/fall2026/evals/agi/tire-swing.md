# Tire Swing -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Tire Swing could make timely certification economical for small affordable-housing operators that defer files when staff time is scarce. That buyer segment and its willingness to pay are scenario assumptions. The memo describes a manual workflow spanning calls, emails, spreadsheets, and office visits. Tire Swing says it extracts income and asset data, applies eligibility rules, flags exceptions, and assembles packets with linked evidence for staff approval. The relevant comparison is labor and full cost per correctly approved certification, including review, retries, and exception handling. The memo provides no measured comparison or evidence that savings opened this use. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Under model parity, operators might still pay Tire Swing for property-specific rules, established forms, and review histories that are costly to recreate. The company reports thousands of checks, while the switching cost remains an inference. Founders report dozens of properties live across eight states, an early demand signal with payment, repeat use, and previously underserved buyers unverified. The memo records the housing product launch after a healthcare compliance pivot and describes relevant founder experience in forensics and rules engines. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

A property is a plausible unit of expansion. At that level, subscription revenue would need to cover document processing, configuration, rule maintenance, and support at actual file volumes. The memo documents subscription terms and identifies separate rule configurations across programs. Realized prices and delivery costs are unknown. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Tire Swing may speed up certification for operators already served by compliance software while leaving the staffing constraint intact. Staff still approve every certification, so review and exception work could absorb the savings. Roost already markets source-linked AI calculations, and Yardi bundles certification with property accounting. These are credible substitutes to test. The memo contains neither a representative cost comparison nor observed buyer switching, so both required Path B conditions remain unproven and the path stays open at Watch. (Key Risks, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent operator that previously deferred certification files because of staff cost buy a second cycle from Tire Swing at a price that covers delivery after comparing it with functionally equivalent automation? A representative trial would track total labor and full cost per correctly approved file against the former workflow and test whether the operator pays again for Tire Swing’s property-specific workflow under model parity. A paid repeat that meets both conditions would move the action to Reach out. A representative result showing that staff costs still keep the work uneconomic or that the operator readily switches under parity would close Path B and move the action to Pass.
