# horizontal. -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Horizontal describes agents that reconcile records across plant systems and coordinate responses to shortages and quality holds, with human sign-off for critical actions. A plausible seed-stage hypothesis is that cheaper intelligence makes it economical to resolve lower-priority exceptions that plants currently defer because manual coordination costs too much. At equivalent quality, the comparison is staff time and total cost per successfully resolved exception, including approvals, retries, and corrections. Horizontal claims it tests in operating plants and advertises 50% shorter release times. The memo records a public launch, while the release-time baseline, labor savings, buyer commitments, and repeat paid use remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The listed seed financing makes this a plausible seed entry. Marxer’s reported industrial enterprise sales and Hermeler’s industrial modeling work support the team’s ability to run a plant trial. Delivery across different plant systems remains unproven. Horizontal describes starting with one workflow and expanding within a plant. The scale hypothesis is that revenue from each added workflow or site can cover inference, support, and the cost of integration and validation. The memo leaves those inputs unmeasured. (Founders & Team, Key Facts, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Horizontal may make work that plants already fund more efficient while leaving the same exceptions uneconomic to resolve. Required approvals and plant-specific validation could consume much of the labor saved. SAP’s announced overlap in shortage detection and rescheduling, along with NVIDIA’s published factory-agent blueprint, gives buyers and competitors credible alternatives to test. Horizontal reports cross-system integrations and on-premises deployment, which give buyers a plausible reason to try it. Buyer preference under functional parity remains untested. The memo also identifies no scarce plant asset or access right Horizontal controls for a separate Path A case. The evidence leaves both Path B gates open and supports Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent plants repeatedly pay Horizontal to resolve a class of exceptions they previously left unhandled when a functionally equivalent alternative is available? A paid trial across plants should compare equivalent-quality outcomes, measure all-in cost per successful resolution, and give buyers a real renewal choice at viable pricing. Repeat paid preference and positive contribution after integration and support would support Reach out if the case clears the score and confidence gates. A trial showing that realistic review costs erase the market opening, or that buyers readily switch under parity, would trigger Pass.
