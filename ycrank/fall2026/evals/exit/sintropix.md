# Sintropix -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic sale to an ERP vendor seeking to add Latin American tax filing to its multi-entity accounting platform. Sintropix describes a shared ledger that feeds both consolidation and local filings. An acquirer could gain a faster route to market if the filing workflows can be integrated and maintained at lower cost than building them internally. The memo supports the product fit, while the buyer economics remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The founders reported 10 ERP customers across four countries. That gives Sintropix a starting cohort for demonstrating repeatable software deployments with accurate filings, limited manual work, and paid renewals. The reported $500,000 YC deal was pending disbursement in September, while a separate listing records it as raised. Available capital and the cost of reaching that milestone remain uncertain. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Using the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. Both sale scenarios assume a full cash sale by year eight, an eligible holder selling their entire stake, and pro rata distribution after net debt and deal deductions, with no preference reducing the holder’s share.

- **Modest sale.** $40 million of enterprise value less $5 million of net debt and deal deductions leaves $35 million of equity value. The holder receives $1.05 million, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million of enterprise value less $10 million of net debt and deal deductions leaves $90 million of equity value. The holder receives $2.7 million, or 5.4×. About $6.7 million in further deductions would erase the cushion.
- **Prolonged private.** If Sintropix keeps operating and reinvests its cash through year eight, the holder realizes $0. The memo supplies no evidence of a funded payout or an eligible secondary purchase.

The sale prices are sensitivity assumptions. The memo gives a monthly pricing model but no realized revenue, acquisition prices, or quantified buyer economics to support either price. Holder rights, the actual waterfall, and required consents are also unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

ERP vendors already have ways to pair consolidation with tax compliance. The memo documents Rillet’s native UK VAT support and Campfire’s sales-tax integration. Those approaches make internal development or a partnership credible alternatives. The reported improvement from a day-20 to a day-1 close came from Sintropix Services, and the memo leaves open how much of that improvement came from software versus manual work. Ongoing localization and review costs could reduce the value of owning the product. This weakens the acquisition case, but the evidence establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a credible sale-price ceiling below the proceeds hurdle. The Pass gate is therefore untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Sintropix’s Latin American filing software give an ERP acquirer a reason to buy at a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? Paid deployments showing accurate filings and limited manual effort, paired with a prospective buyer’s quantified estimate of the cost and time saved by acquiring the product, would test that case. A supported price range that clears $2.5 million in holder cash after deal adjustments would move the action to Reach out. A supported ceiling materially below that hurdle would move it to Pass.
