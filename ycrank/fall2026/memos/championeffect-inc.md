## The Idea

- **Problem:** Employees at companies rolling out AI struggle to apply it to their own tasks, and internal AI champions who teach peers have to fit that work around their regular jobs ([Kevin Law, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kvnlw)).
- **Approach:** The coach works in Slack and Microsoft Teams, joins live calls to guide employees through AI tools and tasks, and uses what it learns from those walkthroughs to help coworkers ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/championeffect-inc)).
- **Differentiation:** ChampionEffect uses coworkers’ workflows for live coaching, while Section offers structured AI proficiency programs, Multiverse runs work-based apprenticeships, and SAP WalkMe embeds guided steps in applications ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/championeffect-inc), [Section](https://www.sectionai.com/), [Multiverse](https://www.multiverse.io/), [SAP](https://www.sap.com/products/business-transformation-management/digital-adoption.html)).
- **Business Model:** No public data found ([AI Tool Index, Nov 2026](https://aitoolindex.org/tool/championeffect/)). Website not accessible at time of research.
- **TAM/SAM:** No public TAM/SAM data found for this specific segment.
- **GTM / Distribution:** The founders invite AI rollout leads to contact them directly on LinkedIn and describe Slack and Teams as employee delivery channels ([Kevin Law, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kvnlw), [Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/championeffect-inc)).

## Defensibility

- **Moat today:** The company describes reusing knowledge from employee walkthroughs within the same customer organization, an account-specific data signal ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/championeffect-inc)).
- **Future moat:** [Inferred]: A library of validated workflows could create switching costs if coworkers use it repeatedly, but public sources provide no reuse or retention measurements ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/championeffect-inc)).
- **Market structure:** No structural barrier identified at this stage.
- **Commoditization risk:** [Inferred]: In-app guidance and role-specific AI coaching are already available from SAP WalkMe and Section, giving both vendors a product base from which to add employer-specific workflow coaching ([SAP](https://www.sap.com/products/business-transformation-management/digital-adoption.html), [Section](https://www.sectionai.com/)).

## Key Risks

- **Platform permissions:** Slack and Teams administrators can restrict third-party apps, which can delay deployment of the coach through either channel ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/championeffect-inc), [Slack](https://slack.com/help/articles/222386767-Manage-app-approval-for-your-workspace), [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-US/microsoftteams/teams-app-permission-policies)).
- **Workflow access:** [Inferred]: Reusing walkthroughs across coworkers creates a need to enforce internal access boundaries when a workflow contains restricted information ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/championeffect-inc)).
- **Workflow accuracy:** [Inferred]: Guidance learned from past walkthroughs can become stale when a tool or company process changes, and no public accuracy measurements were found ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/championeffect-inc)).
- **Substitution:** Section already sells role-specific AI coaching and SAP WalkMe embeds step-by-step guidance in applications, giving buyers alternatives for parts of the same rollout ([Section](https://www.sectionai.com/), [SAP](https://www.sap.com/products/business-transformation-management/digital-adoption.html)).
