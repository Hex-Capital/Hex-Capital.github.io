# Dualizer -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with process engineers, Dualizer’s best growth path is to reuse the same simulator across plants and markets. Dualizer describes Vantage as turning drawings and engineering requests into model edits for engineer approval, and KinetIQ as solving thermodynamic, material, and energy balances. That core could serve the same recurring job abroad. How much adaptation real plant data and equipment models require remains uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Dualizer solicits introductions through its public launch and offers prospect-specific modeling demos. That gives the team a concrete way to find buyers, while the documented path to first value is team-led. The memo reports several active pilots for the three-person team, with payment terms undisclosed. Realized revenue and commercial growth remain unestablished. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

If engineers reuse calibrated models for operating decisions, the models and their calibration histories could support retention. The founders plan to convert successful pilots to subscriptions. Renewal behavior and a way for retained accounts to spend more remain unestablished. (The Idea, Defensibility)

### Countercase

Each new plant could require enough custom modeling and validation to make engineering labor rise alongside sales. The published calibration demo uses synthetic histories with 48 operating points each, and Dualizer is hiring a forward-deployed engineer for site adaptations. Incremental contribution would be the next customer’s fees less directly attributable sales, setup, validation, compute, and support costs. The memo gives no price or representative deployment effort to estimate that contribution. The structural gate stays open because the evidence leaves room for a standard rollout with bounded engineering work. (The Idea, Key Risks)
