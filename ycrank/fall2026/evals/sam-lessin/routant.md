# Routant -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Routant proposes to sit where an agent vendor’s promise meets a customer’s permission to act. That could be a valuable position. But what am I paying to own it? The dossier supplies no post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is the first scarce check. I cannot infer an auction from YC membership, and competitors’ financing says nothing about Routant’s terms. The deal gate remains unanswered.

The proposed product contains a plausible experiment: can customer-defined permissions, human approvals, and audit trails get an outside agent into production where broad API keys cannot? Routant has not documented that experiment as its capital plan. There is no named deployment, dated milestone, measurable success threshold, or explanation of why customer funding cannot finance the work. Those are questions for underwriting, not proof that the founders lack a plan. Still, my money needs to purchase a specific discovery.

The strongest argument against passing is that Routant targets a real boundary between mistrustful systems. In the January 2026 Okta-commissioned survey, 69% of 150 decision-makers said security concerns slowed agent adoption. A vendor integrates once; each customer controls what the agent can do. If that arrangement unlocks purchases, the permission layer could become embedded in the transaction itself. This fits my pattern of backing bridges between systems. But the survey establishes a buyer problem, while Routant’s implementation remains unverified. There is no documented customer action connecting the proposed bridge to an actual purchase.

Who owns the access after everyone recognizes the problem? Keycard already documents policy-bound credentials and delegation chains; Arcade documents action-level authorization, approvals, and audits. That overlap makes a proprietary customer relationship or embedded workflow particularly consequential. Routant has neither publicly demonstrated. It would also be premature to apply my generic AI application red flag mechanically: enforceable permissions could retain value as models improve. The missing evidence is whether Routant controls that enforcement and customer relationship sufficiently to sustain a business.

Deleting the next financing leaves another unanswered question. Pricing, revenue, margins, burn, and runway are unavailable; founder identities and prior operating results are also unavailable. The listed team size of zero is not evidence of an empty company. I therefore use the specified missing-evidence defaults throughout. I checked the exception mechanisms: there is no documented conversion, cash economics, proprietary access, prior exit, or alternative financing structure to override the unresolved gates. **I pass on the present record.** One verifiable condition would reopen diligence: a named customer renews a paid production deployment after 90 days at positive contribution margin, including integration and support costs. That would establish something worth pricing—a functioning business at this trust boundary.
