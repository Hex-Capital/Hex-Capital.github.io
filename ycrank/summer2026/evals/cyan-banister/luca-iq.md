# Luca IQ -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Angelo Policicchio is a Michigan-licensed CPA who lived tax season before Luca IQ existed; Luca IQ is a lived-problem founder wrapped around a product that remains difficult to touch. That pairing is the signature. The founder passes my first test better than the artifact passes my last one: 1,100 hours saved and a 57% reduction in administrative preparation are observable consequences, but an API, workpaper pipeline, and “coming soon” tax engine are still machinery behind the deli counter.

Anduril is the high rung for lived experience: Palmer Luckey carried a decade of building hardware into defense, so the biography was inseparable from the product. Angelo’s firsthand CPA work gives Luca IQ the same kind of social license inside an accounting firm; 300 founder interviews add listening without manufacturing the origin story. Luca IQ diverges because Angelo has not yet shipped an adjacent product to scale, while Akash Sadashivapeth’s data-science background came from energy analytics rather than tax computation. The Biz and Tiz are visible. The Rizz is suggested by tying for the most investor votes at HBS Demo Day, but a polished room is not the dinner table where I watched Travis.

DeepMind could not be physically verified; its low-rung weakness was overridden by Demis Hassabis being an unmistakable polymath. Luca IQ has no comparable exception yet. A CPA can inspect Luca IQ’s source-document traceability and receive a review-ready workpaper in 30 minutes, but a normal human cannot ride, photograph, or play Luca IQ the way I could use Uber, see Flock Safety, or obsess over Ingress. Luca IQ therefore remains more testimony-dependent than tactile, and Angelo’s credible domain origin is doing verification work the product itself should eventually do.

SafeSend’s $600 million acquisition makes tax workflow less magically weird every month. Over the next 18 months, Luca IQ’s non-consensus advantage will compound only if the deterministic 1040 engine turns IRS authorization, reviewer corrections, and completed-return mappings into infrastructure that incumbents cannot cheaply imitate. If Luca IQ remains intake automation plus human review of every line, Thomson Reuters, Canopy, April, and TaxGPT compress the weirdness into a feature war. Hundreds of pilot applications provide heat, but winning HBS Demo Day and entering YC S26 make Luca IQ visible enough that I would not assume the round is underpriced.

Angelo Policicchio is explicitly CEO and Akash Sadashivapeth is explicitly CTO, which clears the first governance gate for Luca IQ but not the tiebreaker gate. I would move Luca IQ into the top governance tier if Angelo held documented final operating authority, founder vesting, and an exit-conflict protocol; two Harvard MBAs presenting together at Demo Day do not answer who wins an irreconcilable argument. My HQ Trivia scar says titles are not governance. Silence here is a rebuttal, not a hard no, but it is a rebuttal I need answered before wiring.

Vigil Labs is the single-anchor parallel: regulated financial infrastructure becomes more defensible precisely because accuracy, authorization, and liability repel tourists. Luca IQ has that same ugly-beautiful shape through IRS-authorized e-filing, deterministic computation, document-level sourcing, and calculation liability. Luca IQ is software rather than a SpaceX-scale capital swing, but requiring human review of every filed position shows that the regulatory burden is real rather than decorative. I like categories where mistakes have consequences; they force the product to grow bones.

Thomson Reuters paid $600 million for SafeSend, and my 36-month forecast is that Luca IQ reaches $4 million–$8 million in ARR and is acquired by Thomson Reuters for $75 million–$150 million after proving its deterministic engine across multiple tax seasons. Luca IQ’s lived-domain founder can sell trust into CPA firms, while Luca IQ’s low physical verifiability and incumbent-dependent workflow make an infrastructure acquisition more probable than an independent consumer-scale breakout. I’m never playing the game I seem to be playing: the bet is not AI document entry; it is a credible tax-engine wedge that becomes strategically inconvenient to leave outside the bundle.

Luca IQ’s 1,100 hours saved, 57% administrative-time reduction, 30-minute intake-to-review cycle, and hundreds of pilot applicants form the strongest case that my forecast is too small. The bull mechanism is a SafeSend-sized workflow beachhead expanding downward into computation and upward into firm operations, with Angelo’s CPA credibility converting seasonal pilots into a proprietary correction dataset. Luca IQ has not earned that conclusion because the core engine is still “coming soon,” revenue is undisclosed, every return requires human review, and four well-funded competitors already occupy adjacent layers. I would flip from a measured angel check to Strong Invest if Luca IQ produces more than $10 million ARR from its own tax engine by July 2028.
