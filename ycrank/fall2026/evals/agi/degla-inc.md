# Degla Inc -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Degla’s seed-stage case is that onboard autonomy could make time-sensitive drone searches viable for public-safety teams that cannot routinely staff flight planning and aircraft coordination. The memo records a live flight demonstration in Chula Vista. Degla says its Jetson-based system takes spoken objectives and replans locally from camera input. The demonstration makes the mechanism plausible, while labor savings, search quality, and demand from newly served agencies remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Scenario assumption.** If onboard replanning saves enough operator time to make previously skipped searches affordable at equivalent search quality and response time, an agency could pay for a field-validated aircraft integration even when it has access to comparable models. The operating test is cost per successful search, including supervision, interventions, retries, and aircraft and field support. The scale test is whether each added agency generates enough paid missions to cover aircraft, integration, and ongoing support at the price it will pay. Degla advertises two aircraft tiers and invites pilots. The memo records no buyer commitment, pricing, or deployment economics. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Agencies may still need enough supervision and exception handling to erase the staffing savings. Even if the savings hold, incumbents with dispatch integrations and established sales channels could add comparable onboard autonomy and give agencies a reason to stay. Degla reports training on expert-pilot decisions, which could improve field performance, but the memo establishes neither exclusive access to those inputs nor a measured reliability advantage. Hanafi’s autonomous-systems experience and prior software delivery support the team’s ability to build the product. An authorized agency deployment remains unproven. (Defensibility, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

Both Path B requirements remain open, so the evidence supports Watch. Path A has no independently supported case. Flight authorization and validation can constrain deployment even with abundant capital, and the memo identifies no scarce access or qualified capacity that Degla controls. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent public-safety agency pay to repeat searches it previously skipped for staffing reasons after comparing Degla with a workflow using comparably capable onboard intelligence? A representative, authorized pilot should log total operator time and interventions per successful search at matched search quality and response time. A paid repeat commitment at a viable price, with the agency attributing its choice to Degla’s validated integration, would support Reach out if the updated score meets the outreach threshold. A like-for-like trial showing no viable labor savings or no reason to choose Degla under model parity would support Pass.
