## The Idea

- **Problem** Solar, storage, EV charging and small data center developers can pay application fees and wait months for a utility study before learning whether a site has capacity or requires costly upgrades ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Wkq-ryhla-faster-power-grid-connection-for-your-project), Nov 2026).
- **Approach** Ryhla models individual distribution and subtransmission feeders, estimates load and injection capacity, project size, timelines and upgrade costs, and manages interconnection filings for developers of 1–50 MW projects ([Ryhla](https://ryhla.ai/), [Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Wkq-ryhla-faster-power-grid-connection-for-your-project), Nov 2026).
- **Differentiation** Ryhla combines distribution-feeder screening with application management for developers, while Paces bundles power-flow studies with land and permitting diligence, GridCARE focuses on securing power for AI data centers, and envelio sells distribution-grid modeling and connection automation to utilities ([Ryhla](https://ryhla.ai/), [Paces](https://www.paces.com/post/the-power-development-funnel-from-siting-to-interconnection), [GridCARE](https://www.gridcare.ai/post/silicon-valley-ai-and-energy-pioneers-launch-gridcare), [envelio](https://us.envelio.com/news/clean-power-research)).
- **Business Model** No public data found.
- **TAM/SAM** Meticulous Research estimates the broader interconnection software, engineering services and process automation market at $1.2–1.8 billion in 2026 with 11% projected CAGR through 2034 ([Meticulous Research](https://www.meticulousresearch.com/meticulous-next/grid-interconnection-outlook-2026-2034), 2026). No public TAM/SAM data found for this specific segment.
- **GTM / Distribution** Ryhla solicits introductions to developers through its YC launch and invites developers to submit individual projects through its website ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Wkq-ryhla-faster-power-grid-connection-for-your-project), [Ryhla](https://ryhla.ai/), Nov 2026).

## Defensibility

- **Moat today** Ryhla says it trains feeder models on proprietary data, backtests them against utility study results and has deployed models with utilities ([Ryhla](https://ryhla.ai/), [Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Wkq-ryhla-faster-power-grid-connection-for-your-project), Nov 2026).
- **Future moat** [Inferred]: Repeat utility decisions could improve territory-specific calibration, with the advantage depending on continued access to outcomes and evidence that accuracy improves as those outcomes accumulate ([Ryhla](https://ryhla.ai/), [Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Wkq-ryhla-faster-power-grid-connection-for-your-project)).
- **Market structure** No structural barrier identified at this stage, and envelio already supplies utility-side feeder modeling and automated technical reviews through its PowerClerk partnership ([envelio](https://us.envelio.com/news/clean-power-research), Mar 2025).
- **Commoditization risk** [Inferred]: Public utility hosting-capacity maps can substitute for initial site screening where coverage is adequate, and utility software vendors already offer automated interconnection checks ([U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/vehicles/us-atlas-electric-distribution-system-hosting-capacity-maps), [envelio](https://us.envelio.com/news/clean-power-research)).

## Key Risks

- **Model validation** Ryhla’s 91% approval figure covers sites its models recommended, and the launch omits the sample size and results for screened-out sites, leaving false-negative rates and performance across utility territories unmeasured in the disclosure ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Wkq-ryhla-faster-power-grid-connection-for-your-project), Nov 2026).
- **Coverage expansion** [Inferred]: Entering new utility territories may require new feeder data and backtesting because Ryhla builds territory-specific models and validates them against utility studies ([Ryhla](https://ryhla.ai/), Nov 2026).
- **Utility approval and upgrades** Utilities retain control over permission to operate and determine required upgrades, so a favorable screen can still yield a delayed or expensive connection ([Berkeley Lab](https://emp.lbl.gov/news/data-power-publicly-available-information-can-help-speed-interconnection-distributed), [Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Wkq-ryhla-faster-power-grid-connection-for-your-project), Nov 2026).
- **Substitution** Utility-published hosting-capacity maps and integrated tools such as PowerClerk with envelio can cover parts of site screening and technical review ([U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/vehicles/us-atlas-electric-distribution-system-hosting-capacity-maps), [envelio](https://us.envelio.com/news/clean-power-research)).
