# Donkey -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Benjamin Martindale speaks Mandarin, advised Sany and Sinoboom, and previously found 31 paying Chinese factory customers in 39 days; Donkey’s strongest asset is specific knowledge. Donkey’s weakest dimension is leverage architecture: knowledgeable founders are wrapping software around a balance-sheet trading company whose customers do not compound one another. Specific knowledge without permissionless leverage creates a capable operator. It does not automatically create a power-law angel outcome.

**Stack Overflow** is the high rung for specific knowledge: developers built for developers, shipped publicly, and turned their lived problem into a compounding product. Donkey shares the lived-problem mechanism through Martindale’s China work and Minghao Tan’s customs-filings parser, factory-versus-forwarder classification, and trade-operations software. Donkey diverges because neither founder shows a public repository or technical artifact, while Prelude OS’s 31 customers validate selling ability more clearly than irreplaceable technical depth. The knowledge is real, but not yet uncopyable.

**Anchorage Digital** is the low rung for permissionless leverage because regulation and institutional sales made permission the architecture; its OCC charter eventually converted permission into a moat. Donkey also depends on permission: Donkey takes title, clears customs, finances inventory, offers net-45 terms, and must satisfy CBP’s bona fide-sale documentation for first-sale valuation. Donkey has no charter-like asset turning that dependency into structural leverage. Inspectors across seven provinces are labor leverage, credit insurance is capital leverage, and driving from Vermont to San Francisco to visit distributors is labor-intensive distribution. Code assists the operation; code does not distribute it.

**ImportYeti’s** 70 million bills of lading and two million users show how data can compound, but Donkey currently converts shipment data into bilateral trades. Over 18 months, Donkey’s order history may improve factory identification, pricing, and defect prediction, yet one importer joining Donkey does not automatically make Donkey more valuable to another importer or factory. The company takes the spread rather than opening the network. A proprietary data flywheel can improve margins; it is not the user-added-value network that produced Uber, AngelList, or OpenSea.

**$7.43 billion** in adjacent procurement-as-a-service spending gives Donkey a real market, but Donkey is concentrated on reordered goods from Chinese factories under tariff rules scheduled for another decision after November 10, 2026. Donkey would move up a tier if its photo-to-factory system generalized across Vietnam, Mexico, India, freight procurement, and customs compliance without rebuilding the operating network each time. Until Donkey demonstrates those adjacent pivots, China factory-direct procurement offers room to maneuver operationally, but not the platform-shaped pivot room of developer tooling or consumer media.

**Prelude OS’s 31 paying customers in 39 days** is the cleanest parallel to Notion’s founder persistence: Donkey’s founders have already built together, sold into Chinese factories, and changed products without losing the domain thread. Donkey therefore reads well on intelligence and energy. Integrity remains untested rather than impaired—there is no contested exit, promotional ethics claim, or unresolved co-founder authority in the dossier—but Donkey’s nine associated employees against a stated team size of seven and stale Prelude positioning leave small diligence questions. I could play a long-term game with these founders; I do not yet know enough to underwrite ten years.

**Net-45 terms** reveal where the architecture wants to go. By July 2029, I expect Donkey to have pivoted from taking title as a full-stack trading principal into customs-intelligence and landed-cost software for importers and freight forwarders, producing $1 million–$3 million ARR rather than becoming a scaled factory marketplace. Donkey’s specific knowledge should survive; Donkey’s inventory, credit, and inspection burden should force the software layer to separate from the trading layer. The founders are more scalable than the initial business model.

**Thirty-one paying Prelude customers in 39 days** makes the strongest bull case sting: Donkey may repeatedly acquire importers, use transaction margins to finance growth, and turn seven-province inspection coverage into a proprietary supplier graph. Anchorage Digital is the relevant exception—permission can become a moat when compliance is hard—and Donkey could make importer-of-record status similarly valuable. The evidence against that exception is structural: Donkey has no exclusive license, ImportYeti already possesses vastly larger shipment data, and current-product order volume is undisclosed. I pass today. I would flip to Invest if Donkey documents 100 repeat importers, at least 70% twelve-month reorder retention, and quoting accuracy that improves while working capital grows materially slower than gross profit.
