# Simulithic -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a **strategic acquisition by a device-cloud or test automation provider**. Simulithic documents a visitor-behavior SDK, workspace journey maps, and per-device comparisons of pull-request previews against a baseline. A buyer could use those capabilities to turn its device coverage into regression checks that prioritize the journeys customers actually use. Owning the technology could justify a premium if it lets the buyer improve coverage or launch materially faster than building or licensing comparable functionality. That ownership advantage remains an inference. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*)

The memo records an October QA launch and deployment through a GitHub App or CLI. The founders report several unnamed live deployments and more than 2× weekly growth on an unspecified metric. The launch, deployment tools, and reported $500,000 in YC funding make a production benchmark plausible at seed. Its cost, available runway, and repeatable customer benefit remain unverified. The benchmark would measure regressions caught and QA time saved against an incumbent’s existing tools at a comparable run budget. The memo documents visitor consent controls and a data processing addendum. IP ownership and post-sale rights to use customer journey histories remain unverified. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

The memo supplies no terms for the contemplated check or usable acquisition pricing evidence. Published run prices and quote-based plans establish how Simulithic charges. Realized revenue and buyer economics remain unknown. No independent fallback has evidence of cash reaching this holder. (*Key Facts*, *Market & Traction*)

Using the thesis’s illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Holder cash at 3% | Gross multiple |

| Modest sale | Year 5 | $40M to $40M | $1.2M | 2.4× |

The sale prices and timing are scenario assumptions. Both sales assume zero net debt, closing adjustments, and senior claims, with all equity consideration paid in cash at close and allocated pro rata before investor taxes. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. At 2% ownership, even the modeled strong sale yields $2.0 million, or 4.0×.

### Countercase

An incumbent may already cover enough of the workflow or be able to build or license the remaining functionality at a cost that caps its acquisition price below the hurdle. BrowserStack already combines a device cloud with testing agents, and TestSprite already discovers application flows. Simulithic’s native device coverage gaps and run-budget limits could further narrow its incremental value. The overlap is concrete, but relative coverage and substitute costs are unmeasured. That leaves a decisive gap for Watch. The evidence does not establish a structural Pass or show that prolonged private ownership is the probable outcome. ### Decisive diligence question

Can a device-cloud incumbent’s production benchmark show that owning Simulithic’s transferable journey-mapping technology creates enough incremental value over its best build or license option to support the modeled sale hurdle? A buyer-validated benchmark that measures the coverage and labor gains and translates them into economics consistent with at least $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership would move the action to Reach out.
