# Lamb Labs -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Lamb Labs targets 20,000+ tokens per second and 63× higher intelligence per watt, making frontier hardware its strongest dimension and physical proof its weakest. Lamb Labs is a magically weird silicon ambition suspended above an artifact vacuum: custom silicon is planned, but there is no tape-out, independent benchmark, or shipping product. I can touch the idea; I cannot yet touch the machine.

SpaceX is the high rung here: capital intensity and technology risk were the opportunity because conventional investors recoiled from them. Lamb Labs shares that load-bearing mechanism—architecture-specific silicon for sub-1W inference is hard enough to become a moat rather than another AI wrapper. Lamb Labs diverges where the hardware becomes real: Niki Kotecha and Thomas Lanning have impressive AI-and-physics training, but the dossier finds no semiconductor design, tape-out, or hardware-commercialization experience. The 63× figure is still a destination, not a rocket on the pad.

DeepMind’s low-rung failure on my tactile test was abstraction: there was nothing a normal person could ride, scan, or photograph, and only the polymath exception overrode that absence. Lamb Labs currently repeats the abstraction more than it escapes it. Lamb Labs reports an achieved 2× GPU conversion result, but publishes no benchmark methodology, model-accuracy retention, or usable demonstration; even the supposedly physical product remains planned silicon. Thomas Lanning’s physics background and Niki Kotecha’s graph-neural-network research hint at polymath compounding, but not yet at the Hassabis level that earns an exception.

Niki Kotecha’s Imperial AI PhD and Thomas Lanning’s Oxford theoretical-physics training give Lamb Labs real Tiz, but not the lived origin that compounds into customer truth over the next 18 months. Lamb Labs must learn semiconductor execution, OEM design cycles, and the actual constraints of robots, phones, and industrial systems simultaneously. Without evidence that either founder previously built chips or operated those devices in the wild, every integration lesson arrives after incorporation rather than before it. That is market adjacency, not the Anduril or Niantic pattern of being *of* the problem.

Hailo, Axelera AI, d-Matrix, and Etched have raised roughly $340M, $450M+, $450M, and $800M, so Lamb Labs inhabits a consensus market through a non-consensus technical door. The diffusion-conversion-plus-silicon combination is weird enough to interest me, but Lamb Labs would move into my top rung only if credible investors rejected it specifically because the architecture looked aesthetically or technically absurd—and the round remained priced accordingly. Today the evidence shows well-capitalized belief in specialized inference hardware, not sophisticated disbelief in Lamb Labs.

HQ Trivia taught me that two founders without an explicit tiebreaker can turn product magic into organizational shrapnel. Lamb Labs names Niki Kotecha and Thomas Lanning only as “Founder,” provides no co-founder history, and identifies neither as CEO. Lamb Labs therefore receives the absent-evidence governance default: I do not assume conflict, but I assume the hard conversation has not happened. A written operating split with one unambiguous CEO would change this score immediately.

No fabricated chip, no independent benchmark, and no prior semiconductor commercialization produce my falsifiable forecast: by July 2029, Lamb Labs will have pivoted from owning a sub-1W chip to licensing its diffusion-conversion and compiler stack to an edge-hardware vendor—most plausibly Hailo—with less than $3M ARR and no Lamb-branded production silicon. Lamb Labs can create valuable intellectual property without surviving the capital, manufacturing, and design-in journey required to become a chip company. I am never playing the game I seem to be playing; the software conversion may be the real company hiding inside the hardware pitch.

The achieved 2× GPU result is the strongest bull case: Lamb Labs may have discovered the algorithm before building the machine, while Niki Kotecha’s chemical-engineering-to-AI trajectory and Thomas Lanning’s physics-to-ML trajectory invoke the DeepMind polymath exception. Lamb Labs could co-design model, compiler, and silicon while Hailo and Axelera remain trapped optimizing conventional architectures. That mechanism stings, but the dossier does not disclose model coverage, accuracy retention, patents, benchmark methodology, or even a tape-out plan, so the claimed wedge cannot yet carry the exception. I would flip to Invest if a named robotics or device OEM signs a paid design-in for fabricated Lamb Labs silicon independently demonstrated below 1W with materially preserved model accuracy.
