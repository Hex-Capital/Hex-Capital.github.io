# Stoa -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first numbers I want are missing: proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. YC Summer 2026 is not evidence that the round is auctioned, but neither is it evidence that I am buying an unfashionable belief cheaply. Without terms, Stoa fails my first underwriting gate by omission.

The more consequential omission is what this check proves. Stoa has a potentially sharp experiment hiding inside the business—whether institutional GPU counterparties will complete blind RFQs through a neutral settlement workflow—but the dossier names no capital plan, deadline, or pass/fail threshold. “Build the market behind AI hardware” can become a capital factory. “Settle $25 million of repeat transactions by a specified date at a profitable take rate” would be a venture moment. Those are economically different propositions.

The bull case is real. Routing more than $300 million of RFQs in one month suggests Stoa has reached buyers and dealers inside an opaque market, not merely assembled 146 LinkedIn followers. Its contractual ownership of bid, offer, volume, and transaction data could become the proprietary price-history layer that GPU buyers, operators, and lenders lack. The team also fits the translation problem unusually well: Kaan Yigit built production trading and pricing systems at Uniper, Eren Berke Saglam traded derivatives at Citi, and Berat Celik is a repeat founder. They understand market infrastructure and have apparently persuaded hardware counterparties to submit meaningful demand.

But RFQ notional is dangerously close to metric theater when executed volume, repeat participation, fee revenue, and even the H100 index value remain undisclosed. A quote request is not a purchase. Contractual ownership of empty or non-repeating order flow is not a dark pool. Stoa also declines to inspect hardware, take title, or guarantee authenticity, delivery, or payment—the exact operational risks that make this market mistrustful. Compute Exchange already spans verified physical hardware, reserved capacity, and forward contracts, while Procurri owns inspection and logistics capabilities. If counterparties still need those functions elsewhere, Stoa may generate introductions that immediately invite disintermediation despite its 12-month non-circumvention clause.

The strongest objection to passing is that $300 million of first-month RFQs may be the pre-seed equivalent of overwhelming conversion evidence: perhaps settlement disclosure merely lags a functioning market, and the founders are already creating a proprietary dataset competitors cannot reconstruct. That could override missing round and capital-plan evidence. I cannot grant the exception yet because the dossier supplies no completed-trade value, customer retention, revenue, margin, burn, or route to break-even. My 36-month base case is that Stoa pivots toward GPU pricing and market-data intelligence rather than becoming the dominant transaction venue; the team and RFQ access are stronger than the demonstrated settlement economics.

I am passing, with one clean flip condition: disclose at least $25 million of completed GPU transactions from repeat counterparties over six months, including Stoa’s realized net revenue from that cohort. That would prove the workflow causes purchases rather than quotes, turn the data claim into a transaction-derived asset, and give me a controllable cash metric. I would then revisit price and ask what finite next experiment my check purchases.
