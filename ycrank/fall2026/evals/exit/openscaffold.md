# OpenScaffold -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition is OpenScaffold’s strongest supported exit route. Harness’s purchase of Cosmos and related assets shows that an engineering platform has bought adjacent technology. OpenScaffold describes a product that turns customer communications into deployment-specific requirements and checks coding-agent output against them. A platform buyer could value that capability if it measurably reduces implementation rework and is faster to acquire than to build. The memo provides neither measured savings nor buyer interest. (The Idea; Market & Traction)

The next milestone is a paid production deployment that demonstrates reliable request attribution and measurable reductions in rework. The founder reports a pilot, and Faraday’s CEO publicly identified himself as a customer. Those are early signals that a production test is possible. Payment, sustained usage, and the time and capital needed to reach the milestone are undisclosed. A software asset sale appears feasible in principle. IP ownership and the permissions a buyer would need to continue processing customer communications remain unverified. (Market & Traction; The Idea; Key Risks)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The scenarios assume a full cash sale where applicable, deductions for debt, taxes, and deal costs as shown, no senior waterfall claims, and an eligible holder receiving the full pro rata distribution within eight years. The memo supplies no acquisition price or investor terms, so the prices and deductions are illustrative. (Market & Traction; Key Facts)

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Holder cash |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M in deductions leaves $45M distributable | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $120M enterprise value less $10M in deductions leaves $110M distributable | $3.30M (6.6×) |
| Prolonged private | Operating cash is reinvested, with no payout or eligible share sale | $0 (0×) |

The strong case clears the hurdle mathematically. The adjacent asset sale has no disclosed price in the memo, and the memo contains no separate evidence supporting a secondary, listing, or funded payout. (Market & Traction; Key Facts)

### Countercase

Harness already owns adjacent context and coding capabilities, and open MCP integrations could let a platform build customer-specific briefings internally. The founder reported that the pilot failed to distinguish customer requests from deployment-team requests 82% of the time. If that persists, manual review could absorb the savings that would give a buyer a reason to acquire OpenScaffold. The evidence supports a substantial substitution and execution risk. It establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible upper bound on proceeds below the hurdle, so the Pass gate is unmet. (Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Does the net rework saved in a paid production deployment give a plausible platform buyer enough reason to buy OpenScaffold at a price that could leave at least $83.3 million distributable? Customer-verified savings after review time, coupled with a buyer’s build-versus-buy assessment supporting that range, would move the action to Reach out. If credible buyer economics cap distributable proceeds materially below the hurdle, the action would move to Pass.
