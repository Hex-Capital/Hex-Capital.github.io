# RightNow -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

RightNow’s consequential possibility is making repeated-context AI work radically cheaper. Coding agents repeatedly send context; retaining it near computation could reduce the resources required per useful task. But the dossier does not quantify that reduction or show how much additional work becomes economical. The $132.7 billion inference estimate includes hardware and applications outside RightNow’s segment. It establishes a large surrounding market, not a demonstrated consequence of this company succeeding. I can lose one check. I still need evidence that success buys something extraordinary.

The strongest evidence is executable technology. Autokernel contains GPU-kernel optimization code and a correctness harness. That distinguishes the founders from teams selling an interface around somebody else’s API. Session-aware routing also attacks a concrete source of wasted computation. Yet correctness does not establish superiority: RunInfra’s September methodology says historical benchmarks lack runtime details, while the engine-comparison harness and raw data are unavailable. Together AI already develops kernels, compilers, and serving infrastructure. The decisive uncertainty is whether RightNow has a substantial advantage under workloads competitors can also optimize.

The bull case deserves attention. Two founders have produced technical artifacts, coauthored model research, and attracted approximately 1,600 stars to Autokernel. My preference for small teams building technical communities applies here; open licensing alone does not disqualify infrastructure. The separate OpenFang project’s approximately 18,200 stars suggest an ability to attract developer attention, though they are not RunInfra customers. This is the strongest argument against passing: useful open infrastructure can earn distribution before its commercial advantage becomes visible. But neither those stars nor the July and October Product Hunt launches establish a committed inference community or a proprietary learning loop. The exception for an unusually capable small team addressing a giant existing value pool remains plausible, not demonstrated.

The economic question is sharper than the subscription prices suggest. RightNow offers $5 prepaid access and $10/$29/$99 monthly plans, but those are selling prices, not evidence of cost parity. Published cache rates describe the highest qualifying account, and hits remain best effort. A favorable effective token price can therefore depend on customer behavior and routing conditions that do not persist across the workload mix. I would underwrite the full GPU bill, including idle capacity, cache misses, and latency obligations. The dossier supplies no such model. It also supplies no evidence of permanent subsidy dependence; I will not invent that adverse conclusion.

Jaber’s technical work and the founders’ joint research give them relevant substance beyond their affiliations. They do not establish a documented belief revision or recruitment of complementary people to own a critical weakness. Similarly, acknowledging benchmark limitations is useful candor, but the dossier provides no funded experiment with a threshold, deadline, and stop-or-change response. Those dimensions receive the missing-evidence defaults. Two people, no reported revenue, and no hiring are ordinary pre-seed conditions; they are not my reasons to pass.

I pass at **27/100**. RightNow has an engineering foothold, but the evidence does not yet connect it to a discontinuous, economically durable advantage. My single verifiable flip condition is an independently reproducible, 30-day production trial showing at least **5x lower fully loaded serving cost than Together AI** on the same models and representative coding-session traces, with matched correctness and latency, including cache misses and idle capacity. That result would turn this from a plausible optimization business into an option I would seriously consider buying.
