# Aviern -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Aviern’s strongest exit route is a strategic acquisition by a parcel carrier seeking to bring regional feeder capacity in-house. Aviern is seeking introductions to FedEx, UPS, and DHL. It claims that a cockpit-free aircraft designed for a 1,000 lb payload could replace the final truck leg on some urgent shipments to smaller communities. If that design delivers faster service at a lower total route cost, a carrier could have a reason to own the aircraft and AviernOS. The memo records an adjacent manufacturing partnership between Grid Aero and Oshkosh, while Aviern’s own carrier evidence consists of requests for introductions. Aviern’s estimated $100 per flight hour is a company projection. The memo provides no measured route savings or evidence that a carrier would pay to own the platform. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Aviern reports more than 150 autonomous miles on a scaled demonstrator, and the memo describes a larger 50 lb test aircraft in development. The announced Q1 2027 retrofit flight and Q1 2028 purpose-built cargo aircraft flight are concrete steps toward a carrier trial. YC backing establishes early funding, while the capital needed to reach authorized, full-scale route validation remains unknown. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, equivalent to about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3% ownership. The following sale prices and terms are illustrative assumptions. Both sale cases assume an eligible holder sells the full stake, receives cash at closing within eight years, and participates pro rata with no senior preference deductions, escrow, or earnout.

- **Modest sale.** At $50 million in enterprise value with no net debt or deal adjustments, $50 million reaches the equity waterfall and the holder receives $1.5 million, or 3×.
- **Strong sale.** At $110 million in enterprise value with $10 million deducted for net debt and deal adjustments, $100 million reaches the equity waterfall and the holder receives $3 million, or 6×. If only half the stake can be sold, realized cash falls to $1.5 million, or 3×.
- **Prolonged private operation.** If Aviern reinvests available cash and the holder makes no eligible share sale, realized proceeds are zero. The memo supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route or a realizable acquisition price. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A carrier could source feeder capacity from a retrofit provider and avoid buying Aviern. Reliable Robotics is already pursuing certification on the Grand Caravan and was selected for an FAA pilot program that includes regional cargo operations. Aviern also faces a material approval risk. If airport-to-airport feeder service falls under the draft’s 110 lb total-weight cap for certificated package delivery, the planned A1000 could not serve those routes under that provision. The memo leaves the classification unresolved and provides too little pricing evidence to establish that the strongest credible sale would fall short. The Pass gate remains open, and the unresolved approval and buyer economics support Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an authorized, carrier-backed A1000 feeder trial demonstrate enough incremental value over retrofit service to support an acquisition that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Repeatable route cost and service data, paired with a carrier’s estimate of what it would pay to own the platform, would move the action to Reach out if the implied distributable equity value clears roughly $83.3 million under the benchmark.
