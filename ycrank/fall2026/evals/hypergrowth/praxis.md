# Praxis -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** Small and midsize law firms are the initial buyer, with client intake and engagement as the recurring job. Praxis reports live deployments at U.S. firms. An unnamed New York corporate-law partner said client setup and engagement letters previously took about 1.5 hours a day. That is a concrete early signal of workflow burden, with time saved and willingness to pay still unknown. The memo documents a waitlist and a co-hosted boutique-firm event, with no evidence that either consistently brings firms into paid use. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Scenario assumption.** If events with legal partners can repeatedly bring in firms, Praxis could activate them on a reusable, lawyer-approved intake workflow and expand into document verification and invoice follow-up. In a second country, that path depends on reusing the core workflow while adapting conflict rules, engagement letters, data handling, and integrations. Contribution would depend on setup and support hours per new firm, model and exception-handling costs per additional matter, and the adaptation effort for each new market. The memo describes the workflow and firm-specific configuration. Paid expansion, cross-market reuse, and incremental costs remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Clio and Filevine already offer AI intake inside systems firms use. Praxis may need to win firms on the value of handling more of the matter lifecycle while configuring each firm’s rules, connecting its tools, and supporting lawyer review. If that work grows in proportion to each rollout, the broader workflow could add delivery effort as quickly as it adds revenue. The memo establishes a credible risk, while rollout effort and cross-market demand are still unresolved. The structural Pass gate is unproven, so the action is Watch. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)
