# Nebula Security -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

1,162 validated findings and 98 public CVEs make **Nebula Security** a research-grade security operation trapped inside a capital-light software business. **Nebula Security** scores highest on technical risk because VEGA must find multistep vulnerabilities, construct proofs of concept, and produce credible patches; it scores lowest on required-capital fit because nothing in the dossier requires a $50M first check, a plant, a fleet, or a foundation-model training program. That pairing usually produces an excellent specialist acquisition, not a defining Khosla company.

**Commonwealth Fusion** is the high rung on technical and capital risk because plasma confinement, magnet engineering, and commercial deployment were independent, falsifiable miracles that repelled normal venture funds. **Nebula Security** shares the falsifiability: eight Chrome zero-days, 1,125 Linux-kernel bugs, and $400,000 in Google bounties are evidence, not an AI demo. The analogy stops where the financing starts. **Nebula Security** combines models, program analysis, and human researchers, while OpenAI, Gecko, ZeroPath, XBOW, and Semgrep can fund overlapping approaches through ordinary software economics. The technical work is real; the capital-hostile moat is not.

**DoorDash** is the low rung on required-capital fit: capital-light execution contradicted my science-risk pattern, and only extraordinary ambition plus consumer-commerce consequence justified the exception. **Nebula Security** shows the same low-capital shape without the DoorDash override. Pay-as-you-go access, per-repository subscriptions, and an enterprise private beta suggest that **Nebula Security** can reach its first commercial milestones with normal SaaS financing. My money adds little where $5M to $10M can answer the central questions. Seventy to eighty percent of VCs add negative value; an over-resourced investor can do the same.

A $55 billion application-security market does not establish a $55 billion consequence. Over the next 18 months, **Nebula Security** can compound validated exploits, patch outcomes, and researcher feedback into a proprietary dataset; if that loop removes most human security-review labor, **Nebula Security** begins attacking the economic mechanism rather than selling another scanner. Today every VEGA report is human-reviewed and receives a human-written patch. That makes the current success-state better security engineering, not the disappearance of security engineering. It is an optimization play until the labor bottleneck breaks.

Eten Zou’s cybersecurity PhD, Microsoft security work, and Linux-kernel research establish exceptional competence but also the career-expert continuity my framework distrusts. Yuan Tan, Frank Wu, and Xiaochuan Yu reinforce **Nebula Security** with Black Hat, DARPA AIxCC, DEF CON, and exploit-research credentials; none of the cited evidence shows **Nebula Security** publicly contradicting security orthodoxy. This score flips upward if VEGA demonstrates that autonomous exploit validation can outperform researcher-led review on maintained production repositories. Experts extrapolate the past. So far these experts are automating the profession they already know.

**Slash** earned an exception because a documented pivot exposed learning rate under pressure. **Nebula Security** provides no comparable thesis update: the UC Riverside research lineage, Yuan Tan’s initial VEGA development, and the current repository-scanning product form one largely linear path. I apply the absent-evidence default rather than punish a Summer 2026 company for being young. But **Nebula Security** has demonstrated technical output, not yet the “we were wrong about X” moment that reveals how quickly the founders rewrite their model of the world.

**Semgrep** will acquire **Nebula Security** within 36 months for less than $100 million after **Nebula Security** reaches roughly $3 million to $7 million in ARR. The mechanism is straightforward: the exploit corpus and researcher credibility are scarcer than the distribution layer, while Semgrep’s approximately 40-language footprint and $204 million raised provide the enterprise channel. Human validation will preserve quality but constrain operating leverage, making the research engine more valuable inside a scaled platform than as a standalone category winner.

$400,000 in Google bounties, eight Chrome zero-days, and four unusually credible researchers form the strongest objection to that forecast. The bull case is that **Nebula Security** converts 1,162 validated findings into the security equivalent of Glean: domain experts become acceptable because proprietary data compounds, the enterprise beta becomes distribution, and AI-generated code expands review demand faster than incumbents can respond. That case stings because the evidence of technical superiority is stronger than most pre-seed dossiers. The dossier still says every report is human-reviewed, identifies no customer-derived data advantage, and shows no paying-customer count; **Nebula Security** remains a service-constrained research engine facing OpenAI and four funded specialists. I would flip to Invest if **Nebula Security** exceeds $10 million ARR while autonomously validating and patching at least 90% of accepted findings without researcher review.
