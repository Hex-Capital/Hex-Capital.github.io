# COACH -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

**€3.5 million in prior revenue and no atoms-heavy barrier make COACH a proven-operator bet trapped inside a replicable software category.** COACH’s analytical signature is strongest on founder lineage and weakest on structural moat: Mathieu and Thomas Perez have already built Alsace Test to €3.5 million in 14 months, yet COACH records conversations using technologies available to every well-funded sales platform. COACH may become a good business. A personal angel check requires the possibility of a monopoly, not merely evidence that gifted salesmen can sell sales software.

**Anduril is the high rung on founder lineage because Palmer Luckey combined an adjacent billion-dollar hardware outcome with a thesis the incumbents could not execute.** COACH shares the repeat-operator mechanism: the Perez brothers built Alsace Test, sold door-to-door alongside Réseau ACEH, and then converted field knowledge into $500,000 ARR within five months. COACH diverges where the analogy becomes decisive—Alsace Test verifies commercial execution, but it does not establish that Mathieu Perez, Thomas Perez, or Nicolas Sebag possesses a technical capability unavailable to Rilla or Gong. This is strong pedigree, closer to an operator re-up than a generic YC founding group, but it is not yet the Luckey override.

**The low rung is the software-only “AI for X” company whose temporary model advantage becomes a competitor’s feature within nine months.** That failure mechanism appears directly in COACH: Rilla already records, transcribes, analyzes, and coaches in-person sales conversations, with an estimated $70 million ARR, while Gong can extend a $500 million-plus revenue platform into the same workflow. COACH handles consent-sensitive recordings, but no public security, certification, or compliance architecture converts that burden into a regulatory moat. COACH owns neither atoms nor a regulated distribution channel; it rents intelligence from the same model layer as its competitors.

**Rilla’s direct overlap means COACH has not yet named a contrarian secret—it has named a narrower interface.** The claimed insight is that team-specific best-closer behavior matters more than generic call analytics, and COACH’s reported 32% conversion-rate lift gives that claim substance. Yet the incumbent failure mode remains unproven: nothing in the dossier explains why Rilla cannot learn the same closer-comparison mechanism or why Gong cannot acquire it. For COACH to flip this criterion into invest territory, the recordings would need to reveal an outcome-linked behavioral ontology that broader conversation platforms structurally cannot construct, rather than merely a useful prompt over customer data.

**Every recorded meeting could compound into customer-specific switching costs over the next 18 months, but COACH’s data presently compounds inside each account rather than across the market.** COACH says its AI learns the differences between each customer’s best closers, which can improve coaching and make historical knowledge costly to abandon. The same design limits monopoly power: isolated customer datasets do not automatically create the cross-customer network effects that made Facebook defensible. Without a proprietary outcome graph, exclusive data rights, or demonstrated pricing expansion, COACH’s knowledge base supports retention but not a ten-year pricing-power hypothesis.

**SpaceX had a definite reusability roadmap; COACH instead reports six pivots and the termination of Lucy before reaching its current product.** Pivoting is not itself a sin at pre-seed, and $500,000 ARR after five months suggests COACH has now encountered something real. The dossier nevertheless provides no two-to-five-year architectural commitments, no named compliance milestone, and no plan for escaping Rilla’s product boundary. COACH is still evidentially closer to indefinite optimization—forward-deployed pilots, customer learning, feature refinement—than to a definite plan for owning field-sales intelligence.

**Within 36 months, COACH will reach roughly $8–15 million ARR and then sell to Gong rather than become an independent category monopoly.** The Perez brothers’ prior €3.5 million outcome, COACH’s 100% reported pilot conversion, and $500,000 ARR starting point support rapid commercial execution; the absence of structural barriers lets Gong or Rilla compress differentiation as COACH proves demand. COACH therefore has the shape of a valuable product and acquisition target, but not yet the One Ring in a power-law portfolio.

**COACH’s strongest bull case is the Asana exception: exceptional operators can outrun a weak initial moat until accumulated workflow data becomes the moat.** COACH’s 120% monthly growth, reported 0% churn, 32% conversion lift, and repeat-founder history make that case sting; the metrics could mean COACH has discovered a field-sales wedge that Rilla’s broader product cannot serve. The dossier still gives the Straussian answer against it: six pivots reveal search rather than a definite secret, and the metrics omit customer counts, cohorts, and measurement periods while a direct competitor already claims $70 million ARR. I would flip to Invest if COACH verifies at least 50 paying enterprise customers, greater than 120% net revenue retention over 12 months, and exclusive outcome-linked data that produces materially better coaching than Rilla on the same sales teams.
