# Robocurve -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The proposed post-money valuation is unknown, competing-term-sheet count is unknown, and the dossier does not establish that this is Robocurve’s first genuinely scarce check. That is not neutral for me. YC plus a fashionable robotics-evaluation narrative can turn uncertainty into an auction before the business proves anything. Without terms or evidence that other investors dismissed the company, I am underwriting neither cheap convexity nor a non-consensus entry.

The company nevertheless has a real founder-system fit. Jay Chooi contributed to Inspect Evals through the UK AI Security Institute, while Aris Zhu worked inside Amazon Robotics, Amazon AGI Labs, and Yondu Robotics. Together they span evaluation methodology and physical robot operation, and they have shipped approximately 400 commits across a working cross-embodiment framework. This is not two students discovering robotics from a market map; it is a credible translation layer between model evaluators and hardware operators.

The first real-robot frontier-model pilot is the important product proof. Inspect Robots can preserve configurations, grader scores, transcripts, traces, and visualizations across different policies and embodiments. If benchmark results repeatedly affect which models laboratories deploy or which robots buyers purchase, Robocurve could accumulate a valuable history that neither side can produce independently. But today the causal chain stops at one pilot. The 134 GitHub stars, 19 forks, 33 organization followers, and rapid PyPI releases show technical interest—not that Robocurve owns access or that its evaluations cause purchases, renewals, or retained usage.

The strongest bull case is that open source is the acquisition channel, not the product: widespread instrumentation establishes the standard, operated evaluations generate revenue, and the resulting cross-model, cross-robot records become the proprietary asset. The problem is that the MIT license gives away the instrumentation before Robocurve has demonstrated control of the service layer. Positronic already runs real-hardware VLA benchmarks, Instance is automating rollout judging and resets, and ReSim orchestrates evaluation across simulation and hardware. A public framework amid simultaneous competitive convergence is not yet a dark pool.

The capital plan is also missing. “Run more evaluations” is not a venture moment. I need one finite experiment: for example, whether independently measured performance changes a named laboratory’s deployment decision by a specified date. No use-of-funds, deadline, pass/fail metric, pricing, revenue, gross margin, burn, or route to break-even is disclosed. Delete the next financing and Robocurve currently looks like a capable open-source project attached to a hardware-intensive evaluation service whose labor and reset costs remain unpriced.

I expect the team can build technically; I do not yet know whether company equity captures the value they create. I pass today. The one verifiable flip condition is three robotics laboratories renewing paid benchmark contracts after Robocurve’s results directly changed a model-selection or purchasing decision. That would simultaneously prove causal workflow ownership and begin answering whether the operated layer can support a business without becoming another capital-dependent robotics lab.
