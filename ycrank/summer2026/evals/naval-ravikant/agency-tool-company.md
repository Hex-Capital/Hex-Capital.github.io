# Agency Tool Company -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Jack Morrison and Davis Foster spent eight years together shipping hundreds of Scythe robots before its 2026 acquisition. That gives Agency Tool Company a clear signature: exceptional specific knowledge wrapped in a product with almost no user-added network value. Agency Tool Company is a builder-tool built by its own users, but each new robot operator remains an isolated account. Specific knowledge creates the product; a network creates the 10,000x geometry. Only the first is visible.

**Stack Overflow** is the high rung for specific knowledge: developers turned a problem they lived into infrastructure other developers pulled from them. Morrison and Foster have the same load-bearing mechanism—Agency Tool Company is the deployment platform they needed while operating robots in more than 30 states. Agency Tool Company sits just below that rung because Deploy is already used by Burro, Tempo Works, and Gather AI, yet no public repository, technical writing, or organic contributor base demonstrates that the founders’ knowledge has escaped their heads and become a permissionless artifact. The knowledge is rare. Its distribution remains private.

**Perplexity** exposes the low-rung failure mechanism on network value: every user can receive value without making the product directly more valuable for the previous user. Agency Tool Company has the same structural weakness. A fourth fleet may improve Agency Tool Company’s failure-handling knowledge, but it does not automatically make Deploy more useful to Burro. The Perplexity exception required top-quartile permissionless product distribution, extreme founder-specific knowledge, and pre-consensus AI timing. Agency Tool Company strongly clears only the specific-knowledge gate; physical AI enthusiasm alone does not supply the missing network.

Three launch partners show code leverage at production depth, but Agency Tool Company currently distributes through founder relationships, a waitlist, YC Launch, and a ROS post with one like. Over 18 months, the software itself can scale cheaply across one to thousands of robots, while acquisition, integration, safety approval, and support still compound through labor. Agency Tool Company fits my unstated builder-tool pattern, but not my stronger pattern of organic adoption preceding the equity story. No paid-acquisition math or premature SDR hiring appears, which avoids the red flag; the architecture still lands midway between permissionless developer infrastructure and enterprise tooling sold account by account.

$703.46 million in 2025 fleet-management software, growing to $1.91 billion by 2032, is large enough for a useful business but narrow for repeated pivots. Agency Tool Company can expand from delta deployment into hardware-backed CI, observability, device security, or general edge infrastructure, yet Build is still “coming soon” and Formant, InOrbit, and Balena already occupy those adjacencies. The market score flips into the top tier if Agency Tool Company proves Deploy is the wedge into a cross-industry edge-computing platform rather than a robotics deployment feature. Until then, the first idea has room for one pivot, not three.

Jack Morrison led Scythe from 2018 through acquisition, while Davis Foster shipped hundreds of robots and earned a 2019 Thiel Fellowship. The single-anchor parallel is **Notion**: long-lived founder obsession and repeated building matter more than a polished seed narrative. Agency Tool Company benefits from an eight-year working relationship, a named CEO, and a completed prior venture—clean counters to the unresolved-authority and pedigree-without-artifacts red flags. Agency Tool Company reads as intelligent, energetic, and capable of playing a long-term game; integrity cannot be proven from a dossier, but nothing here asks me to take it on promotional faith.

Burro’s partner fleet exceeds 750 machines, but those machines belong to Burro rather than Agency Tool Company. My 36-month forecast is that Agency Tool Company reaches $3 million to $7 million in ARR across fewer than 50 robotics operators and is acquired by Formant for under $75 million by July 2029. Deep founder knowledge should produce reliable infrastructure and a handful of valuable accounts. Missing network effects and integration-heavy distribution should keep Agency Tool Company from becoming the default protocol before a broader fleet platform buys the capability.

Formant has raised $45.25 million, while Agency Tool Company already works across agriculture, construction, and logistics with Burro, Tempo Works, and Gather AI. The strongest opposing case is the **Perplexity** exception: a single-sided builder product can become enormous when rare founder knowledge meets a category inflection, and Deploy’s claimed 20-times-faster resumable updates could become the standard primitive for physical AI. The objection stings because Morrison and Foster have lived the problem more deeply than a generic infrastructure founder. It does not win yet: Agency Tool Company has three relationship-sourced partners, no public pricing or revenue, one ROS-community like, and direct overlap with InOrbit and Balena. I would flip to Invest if Agency Tool Company reached 50 independently acquired fleets through self-serve or open-source adoption, with at least 30% arriving without founder-led sales.
