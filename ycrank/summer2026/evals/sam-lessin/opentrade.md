# OpenTrade -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and use of proceeds are all missing. That is not clerical trivia; those are the deal. I cannot tell whether OpenTrade is a cheap first check into a discarded idea or another YC consumer-finance company being auctioned on a 100K-view launch animation. Nor does the dossier name one finite experiment the round will fund. Open internships across engineering, design, and growth describe activity, not the truth this capital uniquely reveals.

The strongest case is the team’s ability to manufacture attention and ship quickly. OpenTrade launched its iOS app on July 1, updated it six days later, built multiple browser games, and reports 3x weekly-active-user growth in three days and 5x total-user growth in five. Dylan Pak claims experience generating 600M-plus content views, while the company has 3,411 LinkedIn followers and a launch animation that exceeded 100K views. At pre-seed, that combination could become a proprietary acquisition engine: teach people through competition, observe their decisions, and convert the resulting behavioral data into personalized investing products.

But the evidence currently stops exactly where it becomes economically meaningful. The growth claims omit absolute users, cohorts, retention, referrals, and purchases. The iOS product is free, has too few ratings for an App Store overview, and discloses no subscription, advertising, brokerage-referral, or transaction model. A creator contest with more than $1,000 in prizes and social-platform reach are rented distribution until they cause an enduring customer action. They do not yet constitute an owned dark pool.

The product itself compounds the problem. Public materials have moved among an AI trade deck, real-time execution, fantasy leagues, swipeable research, and browser training games. Meanwhile, Market IQ reports a 50% historical hit rate and a nine-point calibration gap. OpenTrade neither executes trades nor owns custody or an advisory relationship, so Alinea, Blossom, Invstr, and StockGro can copy the interface while retaining their existing portfolios, communities, brokerage rails, or analyst networks. This looks less like a translation layer between two mistrustful systems than a rapidly changing engagement layer sitting above systems other people own.

The founders have real ingredients: Tario You has AI research and infrastructure experience, Ojas Kandhare has shipped through 67 Labs, and Pak appears capable of consumer distribution. What they have not demonstrated is shipped operating authority on both sides of the bridge—consumer growth on one side and regulated investing, brokerage economics, or financial trust on the other. The bullish exception would be distribution converting so violently that missing terms and monetization cease to matter, but the disclosed multiples and views are nowhere near that standard. My 36-month base case is that OpenTrade either shuts down or pivots into brokerage lead generation or financial-content media before reaching $1 million ARR.

I pass. The one verifiable flip condition is a disclosed eight-week cohort in which the drills produce at least 25% weekly retention and 5% paid conversion without prize-driven acquisition. That would show an owned workflow causing a purchase and identify a controllable cash metric. Until then, OpenTrade is measuring attention while the investable asset remains undefined.
