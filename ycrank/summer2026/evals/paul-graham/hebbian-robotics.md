# Hebbian Robotics -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what broke. Hebbian Robotics has shipped software, but the dossier never shows Ong or Kuan encountering a specific obstacle, constructing a workaround under pressure, and changing the situation within days or weeks. Pareto’s recommendation to isolate mutually untrusted users in separate deployments is a product limitation, not evidence of founder resourcefulness. Without the action sequence, I cannot infer the most important founder property from their résumés.

The strongest evidence is technical. Pareto already indexes synchronized LeRobot episodes, clusters behavior, detects anomalies and duplicates, supports frame-level semantic search, and exports training-ready datasets. Openpi-flash reduced one user’s inference round trip from roughly 4,000 milliseconds to 200 milliseconds. That is an inspectable 20-fold result built before permission. But it validates the team’s engineering ability more directly than Pareto’s central claim: the dossier does not show that its consistency metrics select data that produces better robots, and its task-velocity debiasing still stops at recommendations.

Who wants the crude product now? “Robotics teams” is a plausible niche, but not yet a demonstrated narrow well. Openpi-flash has 42 GitHub stars and one reported user result; Pareto has three stars, no named customer, no revenue, and no evidence of repeated use. YC participation and angels affiliated with Oracle, DeepMind, and OpenAI are investor legibility, not demand. Encord’s physical-AI revenue growing tenfold and its managed volume rising from one to more than five petabytes prove that the category is growing. They do not prove that users are pulling Hebbian’s product from the founders.

The founders plausibly belong near this problem. Ong left a robotics PhD after leading SEA-LION research, while Kuan brings infrastructure experience from Jane Street and Verkada. Robotics data is also full of worthwhile schleps: synchronized sensors, dataset isolation, expensive demonstrations, quality measurement, and integration with training pipelines. But the dossier only infers that the company originated in a problem they personally experienced. It also describes Pareto, an inference-speed project, and a data-center robot without documenting the observation that caused each change. This might be rapid search, or it might be three product nouns looking for a customer.

The bull case is that Hebbian Robotics is already behaving like a builder of programmable primitives: 18 public repositories, a self-hostable Apache-2.0 product, and a measured latency breakthrough. A two-person team with genuine robotics and systems depth could learn faster than larger incumbents. The sting in the bear case is that the evidence spike belongs to openpi-flash, while the proposed business is Pareto; meanwhile Encord already spans the physical-AI data lifecycle with $110 million raised, and Hebbian has neither a proprietary substrate nor validated outcome advantage. My 36-month base case is that the team pivots from standalone robotics-data curation toward robot inference or developer infrastructure and remains below $1 million ARR.

I would not write the check yet. One verifiable event would flip me: a named robotics team encounters a concrete dataset bottleneck, Ong and Kuan ship the resulting Pareto change within two weeks, and that team repeatedly uses it while measuring an improvement in downstream robot performance. That single sequence would establish resourcefulness, user pull, and proof tied to the product they intend to sell.
