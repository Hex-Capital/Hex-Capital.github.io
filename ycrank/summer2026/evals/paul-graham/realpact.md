# RealPact -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what happened when the founders were blocked. RealPact has crossed several obstacles—state-specific forms, MLS credentials, public-record retrieval, and contract accuracy—but the dossier never names one obstacle, the workaround they shipped, or how quickly it changed a customer outcome. “SignPact evolved into RealPact” describes a route change without revealing the observation that caused it. I cannot infer relentless resourcefulness from the finished product.

The product itself is real evidence. RealPact retrieves property records, cross-references them, and produces a signature-ready New Hampshire purchase-and-sale agreement in under five minutes. Ten-plus brokerages are running it, including a firm-wide Four Seasons Sotheby’s pilot. That is substantially better than a diagram or an AI-for-real-estate thesis. But the five-minute result is company-reported, and there is no disclosed accuracy rate, contract volume, or independent benchmark. It is a working technical proof, not yet an exceptional measured one.

The bull case is that users are already pulling the crude product back. RealPact names multiple brokerages and reports current-product sales rather than merely a waitlist. The founders also built CourseMe together, reaching 4,000 monthly active users and 90% of Dartmouth students, so this is not a team whose only evidence is credentials or a competition prize. The strongest interpretation is that they know how to put software in front of users and have now found a narrow group suffering 30–40 minutes of repetitive work per contract.

But “10+ brokerages running RealPact” conceals the decisive variable: what does running mean? A pilot can be one enthusiastic manager and almost no repeated use. No revenue amount, retention, contract volume, or weekly growth is disclosed. The $20,000 competition award, 1,039 LinkedIn followers, and funding announcement do not answer who needs this every week. The absence is especially important because SkySlope already reaches 900,000 professionals and processes three million transactions annually. General voice and language models make contract population easier for the incumbent to copy; only deep workflow use would show that RealPact is becoming more than a feature.

The state-by-state schlep could eventually be the company. Templates, MLS agreements, regulatory updates, security certification, and error handling are precisely the ugly work that can compound into an advantage. Yet the founders’ documented path begins with CourseMe and product research, not a prior collision with real-estate operations. There is no present moat, and the terms leave users responsible for checking generated data. The desired property resembles schlep blindness, but resemblance is not evidence that these founders discovered the problem organically or have already mastered the burden.

The red flags are restrained: the team has sold to experienced brokerage buyers, has previously built together, and is not hiring a sales organization before learning the market. Nor does distribution depend on one ceremonial launch. Still, none of the known exceptions replaces the failed founder-action gate: the technical result is not independently validated, and user pull is not yet unmistakable. I am not investing yet. One result would flip me: expand into a second state within eight weeks of documenting a specific regulatory or MLS blocker, then show five brokerages there processing at least 20 contracts monthly for three consecutive months. That would reveal both the running back and the narrow well.
