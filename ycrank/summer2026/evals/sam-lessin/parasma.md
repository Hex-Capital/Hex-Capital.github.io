# Parasma -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Sean Cole built a 200,000-neuron Doom experiment before founding Parasma, yet Parasma reported no product and no revenue on May 17, 2026. That pairing is Parasma’s signature: unusually visible missionary conviction attached to a capital structure that has not become venture-shaped. Parasma looks less like an AI startup than a serious research project searching for the instrument, substrate rights, and commercial wedge that could finance several Type 2-fun tacks.

Drop.io is the high rung for pre-pitch conviction: I kept building a file-sharing primitive against free incumbents because the operating belief preceded the fundraising narrative. Parasma shares that mechanism—Cole studied spiking neural networks, completed an MSc in AI with distinction, published `doom-neuron`, earned 250 GitHub stars, and got living neurons to interact with Doom before YC. Parasma diverges from Drop.io where the analogy matters most: Drop.io controlled a usable product, while Parasma’s biological learning has not been isolated from its surrounding software and depends on Cortical Labs’ CL1. The conviction is real; the proof is not yet commensurate with it.

Ro is the low rung on capital structure: high burn and repeated follow-on rounds ordinarily drain founders, with prescription-rail complexity providing the exceptional moat that justified the structure. Parasma has the capital intensity without Ro’s demonstrated regulatory barrier—external wetware, biological variability, experimental staff, and a founding-scientist grant of 1%-3% equity before commercial validation. Parasma may need multiple financing tacks merely to establish that neurons perform useful learning, then more tacks to escape CL1 dependency. SaaS is worthless; more precisely, a standard SAFE is the wrong abstraction for a wet-lab research option with no owned substrate, revenue cushion, or exclusive data rights.

Cortical Labs has reportedly raised $11 million and exposes the biological hardware and API on which Parasma’s public proof ran. Over the next 18 months, Parasma’s non-consensus advantage could compound if repeated experiments create proprietary stimulation policies and biological-response datasets; it could evaporate if Cortical Labs, $25 million-funded TBC, and Intactis reproduce the training layer around their own substrates. Parasma is genuinely illegible enough to interest me, but no financing terms, visible tier-one passes, or sub-batch-median price are disclosed. Weird technology is not automatically a mispriced security.

Sean Cole’s approximately 3,000 X followers and 250 GitHub stars give Parasma a small owned technical audience, not yet founder-brand distribution on the John Fish axis. Parasma would flip up a tier here if Cole converted that attention into 10,000-plus engaged researchers, a recurring benchmark community, or an integration pipeline that follows him rather than Cortical Labs’ hardware. Until then, press from The Guardian and Scientific American validates curiosity, while Parasma still lacks evidence that audience attention acquires paying customers.

Solana is the useful single-anchor parallel for category fit: pre-consensus technical infrastructure became investible because real on-chain economic activity made an otherwise illegible asset measurable. Parasma has the illegibility and contrarian surface area, but Parasma has no equivalent economic activity—no revenue, pricing, proprietary substrate, or independently isolated biological advantage. My unstated sleeves favor crypto, Wizard-of-Oz services, DTC brands, and unusual ownership structures; biological AI compute fits none cleanly, while the explicit red flag against foundation-model infrastructure still applies at the edge.

Cortical Labs will, within 36 months, either absorb Parasma for under $25 million or reduce Parasma to a sub-$1 million-ARR wetware benchmarking and experiment-control business rather than a general AI-compute platform. Parasma’s most defensible near-term asset is Cole’s training know-how, but Parasma’s open-source code, CL1 dependency, and unresolved neuron-versus-software causality place the value beside the substrate rather than above it.

TBC’s $25 million financing makes the strongest bull case sting: biological compute may be at its pre-consensus Solana moment, Cole may be the missionary who understands training before hardware incumbents do, and Parasma could turn a week-long Doom implementation into the default control layer across every wetware platform. Ro supplies the Known Exception—capital intensity can itself become the moat when complexity excludes tourists. Parasma has not earned that override because the principal code is public, no patent or exclusivity is disclosed, and replication could not isolate living-neuron learning. I would flip to Invest if Parasma independently demonstrates a repeatable advantage over random- and zero-signal ablations on two hardware platforms and secures exclusive response-data rights at a below-batch-median price.
