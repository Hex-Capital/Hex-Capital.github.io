# River -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

River’s strongest path is a strategic sale to a collaboration platform seeking to add cross-file editing. River offers native document, spreadsheet, and slide editors and says its agent makes reviewable edits across related files and connected apps. A platform buyer could acquire that workflow to handle more of the work its users already do across tools. The memo gives no measured workflow savings or buyer interest to support that rationale. (The Idea)

The next milestone is a retained cohort of paying teams with measured time savings on recurring, multi-file work. The desktop launch shows River can ship the product. River claims more than 100,000 creators, but the memo leaves paid conversion, retention, and usage frequency unknown. The reported $500,000 YC note is an early funding signal. Available runway and the cost of reaching the milestone remain unknown. (Market & Traction)

**Proceeds bridge.** The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3% with no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. Both sale scenarios assume a full cash sale within eight years, an eligible holder paid 3% pro rata at closing, and zero net debt, deal deductions, or senior claims. River lists a $36 monthly seat price. Seat counts and valuation multiples below are illustrative assumptions. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Gross cash to holder |
|-----------|------------------------------------------|----------------------|
| Modest sale | 5,000 paid seats × $432 per year × 5 = $10.8M | $324,000 (0.65×) |
| Strong sale | 25,000 paid seats × $432 per year × 8 = $86.4M | $2.59M (5.18×). With $5M in net deductions, equity falls to $81.4M and cash to $2.44M (4.88×). |
| Prolonged private operation | Cash is reinvested. No sale or payout occurs within eight years. | $0 realized |

The strong case clears the hurdle narrowly, and the memo gives no basis to underwrite its seat count or multiple. Acquisition consents, integration assignment rights, and holder participation are unverified. The memo supports no independent liquidity route. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Potential buyers may already have enough of River’s functionality. Microsoft offers cross-file execution and in-place editing within existing tenant controls, Anthropic offers file and app workflows while also supplying models to River, and Genspark combines native office tools with agents and a browser. The memo identifies no demonstrated moat or switching costs. These substitutes weaken the case that a buyer would pay enough to meet the hurdle. The evidence leaves open whether another collaboration platform would pay for River’s workflow, so neither the structural-block nor the inadequate-proceeds Pass gate is established. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Does River deliver enough incremental value over incumbent tools to support a strategic sale yielding at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? Renewal data from paying teams, measured time savings against their existing tools, and a prospective buyer’s estimate of what it would cost to build the same capability would test that case. Credible economics at the hurdle would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that the strongest viable sale would leave the holder materially short would move it to Pass.
