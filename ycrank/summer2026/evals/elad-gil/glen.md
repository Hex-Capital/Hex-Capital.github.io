# Glen -- Elad Gil Evaluation

MCP and proliferating workplace agents have made AI-forward software teams willing to buy shared context infrastructure; the determining variable is whether Glen’s captured organizational memory measurably improves agent task completion enough to become the persistent system of record rather than another retrieval layer.

The dossier does not yet establish that variable. Glen describes the mechanism—capture work from Slack, GitHub, tickets, calls, and agent sessions, then inject prior decisions and distilled skills—but provides no completion-rate improvement, retention threshold, or evidence that broader context changes customer economics. This matters because the market is already crystallizing: Glean reports $300 million ARR, while Dust reports 3,000 organizations, 51,000 monthly active users, and 300,000 deployed agents. Glen must prove displacement or own a distinct control point; describing fragmented context is insufficient.

The technology shift is also double-edged. MCP lowers the cost of connecting agents to workplace data, but it lowers that cost for Glean, Dust, Mem0, Zep, model providers, and new entrants simultaneously. A $5.86 billion enterprise-search market growing 9.03% annually is real, but the dossier does not quantify a new margin structure, faster adoption curve, or previously impossible workflow. The same discontinuity enabling Glen therefore increases commoditization pressure.

The strongest product evidence is architectural. Continuous capture, source-linked decision histories, prompt injection, reusable skills, access controls, and private mode go beyond a thin summarization interface. Better models could improve synthesis while Glen retains workflow state and permissions. But coverage excludes private channels and sessions, recalled context may be inaccurate or outdated, and there is no evidence yet that the distilled skills are difficult to reproduce. The asserted future data moat remains a red flag until the founder can state its reproduction cost in money, time, and access.

Three named testimonials from Amorphic Labs, Zaplar, and Composio are meaningful pre-seed evidence, particularly for a product launched in June 2026. They show more than a concept, but not yet product-market pull: the waitlist is undisclosed, onboarding is controlled in waves, and there is no revenue, repeat-usage, retention, or expansion data. I would not penalize a Summer 2026 company for lacking a long history, but the current evidence cannot distinguish enthusiastic design partners from durable operational dependence.

The bull case rests on Nikos Dritsakos. He worked inside agent integrations at Composio, built and sold SalesBop for $500,000 after roughly 18 months, and is now pursuing Glen as a solo founder. That is relevant second-time learning, not résumé decoration. His experience could let Glen choose a narrow workflow before larger competitors notice it, and the first successful integration could become a channel for adjacent agent functions. I sometimes buy multiple positions before a discontinuity settles, but this category already contains scaled, heavily financed competitors; nor does Glen qualify for a pre-commercial frontier-technology exception based on rare underlying research.

My 36-month base case is that Glen pivots from organization-wide memory to a narrower agent-observability or reusable-skills product because the broad context layer becomes a feature of larger platforms. I pass today. One verifiable condition would flip me: at least five paying organizations retaining Glen across three or more workflows for six months while documenting a 30% or greater reduction in agent rework attributable to Glen’s cross-source context.
