# Coasty -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Coasty’s 82.81% OSWorld Verified result makes Coasty a high-secret, low-structure company: Coasty sees that selector-based RPA breaks because software interfaces are visual, stateful, and adversarial to fixed scripts, yet Coasty has not converted that technical insight into a durable monopoly. The secret is real but incomplete. UiPath’s selector architecture is a named incumbent failure mode; Coasty has not shown why UiPath, Adept, or a foundation-model provider cannot copy the remedy. One year of superior grounding is a feature. Ten years of pricing power is a company.

**Stripe** is the high rung on the secret ladder because developer-first payments exploited an institutional blindness inside processors built for merchants and banks. Coasty shares the load-bearing mechanism: Coasty replaces brittle selectors with screenshot grounding, state retention, outcome verification, and mid-task recovery. The divergence is structural. Stripe’s secret compounded through payment integrations and financial infrastructure; Coasty’s 82.81% benchmark lead rests on commercially available multimodal models and cloud VMs. Coasty therefore sits above Lyft’s partially contrarian category bet but below Stripe: a named incumbent failure exists, while the reason the incumbent cannot respond remains unproven.

**Asana** is the low rung on the atoms-and-regulation ladder: pure SaaS workflow software had neither a capex barrier nor a regulated-customer moat, and only Dustin Moskovitz’s Facebook pedigree justified the exception. That failure mechanism appears inside Coasty. Coasty names healthcare, tax, insurance, and compliance, but its public healthcare demonstration used mock portals, while Coasty’s terms permit customer content to pass through its own AI and infrastructure providers. Regulated industries are not a moat merely because they appear on a customer list. Until certifications, exclusive integrations, or government-grade trust exist, Coasty remains atoms-light software selling toward regulation rather than protected by it.

UiPath’s $1.43 billion FY2025 revenue shows how Coasty’s monopoly trajectory could either compound or disappear over the next 18 months. Every recovered failure could enlarge Coasty’s proprietary trajectory corpus, improve evaluations, and make Coasty progressively harder to replace; every workflow completed without exclusive data rights instead teaches the same foundation-model ecosystem available to Adept, Browserbase, and UiPath. Coasty’s prepaid pricing—$0.03 per grounding request and as little as $0.04 per action—also begins as commodity metering, not monopoly pricing. Competition is for losers, and Coasty currently prices like an infrastructure competitor rather than an indispensable system of record.

Coasty’s March-to-July 2026 repositioning from isolated “AI employee” VMs across ten departments to developer APIs for legacy back-office software is evidence of indefinite iteration. Coasty would flip the definite-plan score into invest territory if Prateek Jannu and Nitish Kovuru committed to a narrow five-year destination—for example, becoming the certified execution layer for payer portals—and named irreversible milestones in reliability, integrations, and regulatory approval. Coasty instead requests workflow submissions and offers to build proofs of concept. Customer discovery is useful; customer discovery is not a definite theory of the future.

**Affirm** is the single-anchor parallel for founder lineage because Max Levchin had already shipped PayPal-scale infrastructure before receiving the re-up. Coasty has credible technical proximity but not that operator proof: Prateek Jannu worked in ML engineering and fault-tolerant pipelines, Nitish Kovuru taught graduate AI as a Purdue undergraduate and built agentic sales automation, and Coasty’s open-cowork repository reached 111 GitHub stars. Coasty’s founders are credentialed outsiders with relevant specific knowledge, but neither founder has yet shipped a product to institutional scale. The Stanford departure is biographically Thielian; it is not operationally equivalent to Levchin.

Thousands of active users and paying customers across four continents lead to a falsifiable forecast: by July 2029, Coasty will reach $3 million–$8 million ARR and be acquired by UiPath for less than $150 million rather than become an independent monopoly. Coasty’s technical secret is valuable enough to attract customers and an incumbent buyer, while Coasty’s low regulatory and atoms-heavy moat makes acquisition more probable than durable pricing power. The benchmark advantage becomes a product capability; UiPath supplies distribution, enterprise trust, and the installed base.

Coasty’s strongest bull case is that 82.81% OSWorld performance, thousands of paying users, and replayable recovery trails are the beginning of a Facebook-like data flywheel that the dossier cannot yet measure. Coasty could also invoke the Asana or Quora exception: exceptional founders and compounding software data can override weak atoms exposure. The objection stings because benchmark leadership plus real pre-seed usage is unusually strong evidence. It does not yet win because the earlier 700-person waitlist belonged to a differently positioned product, regulated workflows remain publicly unverified, and no exclusive-data mechanism prevents UiPath or Adept from learning the same lessons. Three Fortune 500 regulated customers signing multi-year contracts that grant Coasty exclusive rights to learn from production recovery data would flip my verdict to Invest.
