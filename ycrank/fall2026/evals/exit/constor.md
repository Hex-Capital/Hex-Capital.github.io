# Constor -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest private-company path. An estimating software vendor could buy Constor to add editable HVAC and insulation takeoffs and 3D routing to its bid workflow. The memo records TAG Software Group’s acquisition of Countfire, which establishes that a buyer has acquired an adjacent takeoff product. Constor reports five paying design partners, 12 paid preorders of unspecified overlap, and about 15 contractors bidding with the product. A2C Isolation says takeoffs went from hours to minutes and that it bid on and won more jobs. These are early signals of customer value. The ownership case is an inference: if historical drawings and estimator corrections produce a durable accuracy or review-time advantage, owning the software and the rights needed to improve it could give a vendor an advantage over building or licensing. The memo supplies no comparative production benchmark or buyer valuation. (*The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction*)

The operating milestone is repeat paid use and renewals from an HVAC and insulation cohort that shows materially lower estimator review time at comparable verified accuracy against existing alternatives. Reported live bidding makes that test plausible at seed. The memo records $500,000 in YC convertible financing, with burn, runway, and the cost of reaching the milestone unspecified. A company sale is a plausible structure, conditional on IP ownership and a buyer’s ability to keep using the drawings and corrections that support any performance edge. The memo describes internal training and confidentiality limits, while leaving post-sale usage rights and holder terms unverified. Paid preorders and reported launch profitability establish no independent route to shareholder cash. (*Market & Traction; Key Facts; Defensibility; Key Risks*)

**Proceeds bridge.** The memo gives no usable note conversion terms or ownership for this check, so the scenarios use the thesis’s illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3%, with no follow-on. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash. Under a simple pro rata waterfall, that requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. For both modeled sales, assume zero net debt, transaction fees, other price adjustments, and senior claims. Enterprise value therefore equals distributable equity consideration, paid entirely as cash at close. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. Any outstanding note balance or senior preference would need to be reflected in the actual bridge. The sale prices below are sensitivity inputs. The memo gives no usable purchase price or buyer economics to support a valuation range. (*Market & Traction; Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Beam AI already covers mechanical takeoff, Bobyard offers overlapping trade-specific automation, and Countfire connects takeoff to pricing. Constor also requires estimators to verify quantities before bidding. A strategic buyer could get sufficient functionality from an existing product or a license, particularly if Constor’s 3D routing and correction data yield little incremental benefit after review or if customer permissions limit continued training after a sale. That could cap the price below the holder’s hurdle. The memo establishes credible substitutes but leaves their adequacy and the price ceiling unresolved. The proceeds gap warrants Watch, and the evidence supports neither a structural Pass nor a conclusion that the prolonged-private scenario is probable. ### Decisive diligence question

What measured advantage would make an estimating software vendor pay at least $83.3 million in distributable equity value to own Constor at the modeled 3% stake? A buyer-side build-versus-buy analysis grounded in repeat paid deployments, verified review-time savings against existing products, and continued rights to the data behind those gains would move the action to Reach out if it supports that price. Evidence that licensing or building delivers equivalent economics below the hurdle would move the action to Pass.
