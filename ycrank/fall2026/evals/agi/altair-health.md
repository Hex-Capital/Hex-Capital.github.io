# Altair Health -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**New Economies** is the strongest path. Altair says it checks proposed claim actions against formalized payer policies in a practice’s existing systems and sends exceptions to in-house billers. The memo records the launch of an earlier co-pilot. The CEO reported that a billing team at an Arkansas behavioral-health clinic prechecked hundreds of claims and attributed avoided reimbursement delays to those checks. That field use supports the feasibility of payer-policy prechecks. Staff time saved and cost per successfully reimbursed claim remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The market-opening scenario is a small practice that currently handles billing through staff time spent reconciling payer requirements across systems, but cannot economically give every claim a precheck. Altair could make that use viable if it lowers the fully loaded cost per successfully reimbursed claim at comparable payment accuracy and speed. Each practice deployment must cover integration, policy upkeep, compute, and biller time for exceptions and rework. The memo gives neither a measured manual-cost baseline nor realized fees or exception workloads for the current service. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Under model parity, a practice could still pay Altair to own billing execution on its existing systems. Altair reports tracking policies across more than 3,400 payers and reusing payer-specific resolutions. The advantage of that history across practices and buyer preference for Altair under parity remain unverified. The founders’ software sales and clinical ML experience, together with the earlier launch, support seed-stage execution. Repeat delivery of the current service and paid demand among newly served practices remain open. (Defensibility, Founders & Team, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

Altair may improve an existing billing workflow without opening a new market. The reported co-pilot use came from a clinic that already had a billing team. Tebra bundles billing for independent practices and has announced AI investment, while Candid Health already markets configurable rules and agents. If Altair’s exception workload stays high, it may carry much of the existing labor cost while competing with vendors that can offer comparable checks. The memo shows credible substitution risk, with buyer switching and viable delivery costs still untested. It also establishes no independent scarce-complement path. The New Economies gates remain open, supporting Watch. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent practices that previously went without routine claim prechecks renew Altair at a price that supports positive contribution when they have a credible alternative with comparable automated checks? A paid cohort would move the action to Reach out if it showed lower fully loaded cost per successfully reimbursed claim against the prior workflow, comparable reimbursement accuracy and timing, and renewals attributable to Altair’s billing execution. Evidence that the cost reduction fails to make routine prechecks viable, or that buyers readily switch at parity, would move the action to Pass.
