# AnyAlpha -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

AnyAlpha makes it easier to express and execute a trading opinion. What becomes radically cheaper for society if this works? The dossier establishes a trader’s research-and-monitoring problem, but supplies no spending baseline, affected population, or mechanism for an orders-of-magnitude consequence. Automating market participation can produce a business. It does not automatically produce the consequence that makes a low-probability investment attractive to me. I pass on the evidence presented.

The strongest argument against that decision is the founders. Shirley Peng built trading systems at Coinbase and LSEG; Jerry Miao reports six years spanning quantitative data, models, backtesting, and execution. They have built together for three years, and their prior apps reportedly reached $500K ARR. These are relevant builders with demonstrated commercial execution. My broader fintech investing pattern argues against dismissing them because the first product looks ordinary. But prior-app revenue belongs to those apps, and Jerry’s reported personal trading returns do not establish customer results. Neither record documents the belief revision or complementary recruiting that would earn additional credit under my founder criterion.

The technical question is whether AnyAlpha removes a binding constraint that existing products cannot. Composer and Capitalise.ai already combine strategy creation, testing, and execution; Olas offers autonomous prediction-market trading. AnyAlpha’s user-defined thesis could be a useful starting interface, but the dossier supplies no comparative benchmark or proprietary learning mechanism. I cannot conclude that it is merely an off-the-shelf wrapper. I also cannot underwrite a technical discontinuity. The reported $53 gain on a $100 demonstration account establishes neither repeatable advantage nor reliable execution across market conditions. The 42 cumulative iOS ratings span earlier positioning and cannot validate the current agent product.

I would spend the next dollar testing whether a faithfully executed thesis creates durable customer value after fees, slippage, and execution errors. Backtests and paper trading are useful tools; the dossier does not describe a decisive experiment with a threshold, budget, or stop condition. That receives the absent-evidence default, without assuming the founders lack such a process. Economics receive the same treatment: listed purchases of $4.99–$79.99 disclose neither billing periods nor inference, data, support, and execution costs. A low sticker price does not establish a scalable cost advantage.

The sports-analytics history also puts AnyAlpha close to my explicit gambling exclusion, although prediction markets can serve legitimate forecasting and hedging needs. The present dossier does not establish that those productive uses drive demand. I checked the exceptions: there is no quantified giant value pool paired with a documented instigator, or demonstrated exponential capability, sufficient to override the missing consequence and technical evidence. My one verifiable flip condition would be a paid deployment demonstrating at least a 10x reduction in the all-in cost of a defined, non-speculative hedging workflow against an existing alternative, with equivalent execution quality. That would give me a consequential capability to reconsider.
