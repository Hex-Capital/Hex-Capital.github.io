# Definite -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Definite’s potential secret is that finance automation becomes reliable only when agents share a persistent model of the books, with deterministic calculations beneath probabilistic interpretation. That is a proposition worth testing. It is not yet a demonstrated disagreement: the dossier identifies no named peer rejecting it, and Workato already advertises semantic context. A useful falsification test would compare Definite with existing orchestration on the same reconciliation workflow: does its shared model materially reduce exceptions and repeated configuration as additional workflows are added? The architecture supplies a hypothesis; the dossier supplies no comparative result.

The investment question is who owns the financial definitions once that hypothesis proves true. Definite retains customer-specific mappings, rules, historical snapshots and source-to-cell lineage. These could make it progressively harder to replace. They could also become implementation artifacts that a customer exports or another vendor reconstructs. No retention, migration or exclusive-data evidence resolves the distinction. More fundamentally, “finance teams” is a customer category, not a bounded beachhead the company can dominate. Numeric addresses adjacent finance workflows; Workato and n8n approach from orchestration. Their existence does not establish that Definite is interchangeable, but its documented features do not yet establish a monopoly mechanism.

The strongest argument against passing is that Definite could become the durable financial context beneath interchangeable agents. Read-only access, deterministic rules and reviewable lineage address the particular reason a finance buyer might distrust autonomous software. If each implementation makes subsequent workflows cheaper to deploy, apparently burdensome integration work could produce a valuable installed base. This fits my revealed interest in financial infrastructure; an application need not involve rockets to deserve capital. But the dossier documents scoped connector work, not declining implementation costs or expanding customer dependence. The bull case rests precisely on the transition that remains unobserved.

The founders have relevant technical exposure: Gurshabd worked on BitGo approval controls, Mazin on Optiver trading systems and Boosted.ai financial agents, and Farhan on Meta recommendations. Their Waterloo connection establishes a shared setting, but not a documented history of shipping together. Full-time status, ownership and economic exposure are undisclosed, so I use the founding-structure default rather than invent either alignment or a red flag. University ties alone also do not establish the operator network behind some of my strongest company-formation bets. There is no documented part-time founder, outsourced core engineering or investor-dominated capitalization.

Distribution is more concrete than the monopoly thesis. The September 2026 interview names finance VPs as champions, while LinkedIn outreach, demos and the Dynamics Roadshow provide actual routes to them. The founders also learned that earlier compliance-product LOIs did not establish purchase commitment and moved toward contract-first selling. That is useful commercial evidence, although failed demos and undisclosed conversion leave repeatability unresolved. Monthly contracts with no deployment fee and a two-month opt-out reduce buyer commitment; against customer-specific integration work, they also leave implementation recovery uncertain. Without monetary pricing or margin evidence, I cannot construct a credible power-law revenue engine. This is uncertainty about the business mechanism, not a penalty for being pre-seed.

I pass on Definite today. My known exceptions would matter if independent technical proof or persistent customer behavior directly answered the unresolved secret and monopoly questions. The earlier regulatory-reporting product’s company-reported FinBalance result—exceeding GPT-5 using DeepSeek V3 and verification—does not establish that the current ontology product has done so. Nor does the product transition itself prove failure. **My flip condition is a verifiable cohort in one defined finance segment that renews and expands from one workflow to several while implementation hours per added workflow fall, with customer references attributing continued use to Definite’s accumulated mappings and lineage.** That would connect the architecture to compounding ownership. Today, the dossier establishes a plausible product before it establishes an exceptional business.
