# Aikido Technologies -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Aikido has a seed-stage building-blocks case for supplying **usable power for AI compute where firm grid connections take years**. The memo reports a global queue of at least 180 GW of advanced data center projects and multiyear connection waits in constrained grids. The queue covers data centers broadly, so it indicates the scale of the bottleneck without measuring how much AI load could move offshore. For an operator waiting on a connection, the cost is deferred use of its GPU capacity. Aikido’s proposed AO60DC pairs 10+ MW of IT load with a 15+ MW wind turbine, batteries, diesel backup, and seawater heat rejection. A plausible first use is batch inference that can tolerate offshore network latency, assuming sufficient bandwidth and reliable power through low-wind periods. The design could bypass the grid wait for that workload, with offshore delivery timing and operating performance still unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The industry-enabled path scores zero. The memo describes selling colocation and dedicated GPU capacity for existing workloads. It identifies no buyer or use that becomes economically viable because AI reduces the cost of delivering the service. (The Idea)

The granted folding-platform patent provides a specific transport and deployment advantage to test. Competing marine designs limit the evidence that Aikido can retain pricing power. Aikido reports prospective GPU renters, while its inquiry form is nonbinding and the memo provides no buyer-confirmed trial, contract, or payment. The founders’ floating-wind and marine engineering experience, the reported structural assembly of Aikido One, and the reported A1DC retrofit and permits support execution credit. Dependable compute delivery remains unproven. The memo gives no basis to compare contracted revenue for the next platform with financing, backup fuel, connectivity, vessel servicing, and downtime costs. Customer demand and scale economics therefore stay at their evidence defaults. (Defensibility, Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could secure usable compute sooner at another onshore site or through a competing marine design. Wind variability and vessel-dependent servicing could make Aikido’s capacity less dependable, and full-scale certification is still planned. The decisive gap is whether Aikido can deliver capacity that meets a buyer’s operating requirements before that buyer’s realistic alternative. The grid bottleneck and the proposed design still support the building-blocks path. The unresolved delivery comparison warrants Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an offshore pilot qualify a real buyer’s batch-inference workload on a credible schedule ahead of that buyer’s best onshore alternative? Buyer-confirmed pilot results meeting agreed uptime and connectivity requirements, paired with comparable delivery schedules, would move the action to Reach out. Until that milestone, the action stays Watch.
