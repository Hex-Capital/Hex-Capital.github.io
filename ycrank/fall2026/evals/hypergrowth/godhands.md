# GodHands -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** The strongest initial buyer is a head of support operations who needs to preserve how experienced staff handle recurring escalation exceptions. GodHands launched with demo bookings and a call for introductions to support executives. Its terms describe monthly subscriptions with quoted fees and plan-specific usage limits. A company-published testimonial says an unnamed logistics software support team documented its escalation process in two weeks. That is an early signal of value on a real workflow. Payment, acquisition source, and founder effort remain unknown. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Facts)

**Growth scenario.** If demo-led sales can repeatedly win support teams and those teams can reuse the same setup, GodHands could start with escalations and expand into more queues or agent-assisted workflows. Desktop capture without per-app API integrations could help the same product travel. In a second geography, the practical test is whether app allowlists, redaction, and retention settings satisfy local employee-data requirements without custom deployment. The memo gives no customer geography or cross-market rollout evidence. Incremental subscription revenue would need to cover sales and setup for each new account and processing, support, and any dedicated hosting as usage grows. The memo provides no measured costs or paid expansion. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks; Key Facts)

### Countercase

Buyers may use GodHands to document a process during a handover and have little reason to keep paying once the playbook exists. The support testimonial ends at documentation, and the finance testimonial concerns a controller handover in a different buyer segment. Sensitive desktop capture could also require account-by-account security review or on-premises setup, while human approval for agent actions could limit the value of expanding into execution. These are plausible constraints. The memo establishes neither a structural geographic ceiling nor proportional delivery costs, so the Pass gate does not apply. (Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a support-operations buyer in another verified market go from a standard demo to paid use on a recurring escalation workflow using the same deployment process? A paid rollout that documents setup hours, any local privacy changes, and continued use through the next escalation cycle would move the action to Reach out if it shows the product travels with limited bespoke work.
