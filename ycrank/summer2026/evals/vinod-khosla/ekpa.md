# Ekpa -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence-of-success case is too small. Ekpa may save retail investors research time, but the dossier does not quantify the hours, fees, losses, or expert-service cost it can collapse. A $6.8 billion category forecast is not societal consequence. The present product combines filings, news, analyst ratings, alerts, portfolio analysis, and backtesting around connected holdings. That is useful aggregation, not evidence of a 100x change in access to trustworthy financial expertise.

The strongest bull case is real for a pre-seed company. Two MIT founders have complementary exposure to agentic pipelines, economics, and investing; users have connected a company-reported $3.4 million in assets and run 2,400 analyses. Inference costs for GPT-3.5-level performance fell more than 280-fold between 2022 and 2024. A tiny team could therefore serve portfolios continuously at a cost unavailable three years ago. The exception case would be an ordinary-looking wedge into an enormous existing value pool, but Ekpa has not shown that research becomes proprietary infrastructure rather than a feature inside somebody else’s brokerage.

The technical evidence cuts against that exception. PortfolioPilot already provides portfolio-specific advice, Composer builds and executes strategies, and Robinhood Cortex can discuss and enact trades. Ekpa supplies no accuracy, latency, cost, or decision-quality benchmark against any of them. It has no documented proprietary dataset or learning effect. The reported return above 1.5% in one week has no published methodology and is closer to marketing noise than a technical experiment. This is currently an interface and systems-integration product competing against firms that own distribution, execution, and account data.

The founders may learn quickly, but the dossier contains no observed belief revision, difficult recruit, or risk-specific division of responsibility. More importantly, their milestones do not attack the kill risks. The decisive tests are whether research recommendations remain reliable out of sample, whether users will pay when brokers bundle similar functionality, and whether compensated portfolio-specific analysis triggers investment-adviser obligations. Early onboarding and 2,400 analyses test engagement; they do not supply pass/fail thresholds for those existential questions.

Software can eventually deliver research below human-adviser cost, and falling inference prices help. Yet Ekpa discloses neither subscription pricing nor a bottom-up cost model covering model calls, market data, compliance, support, and customer acquisition. Free early access hides rather than proves parity. My 36-month base case is a pivot into a B2B research or compliance copilot for RIAs, remaining below $1 million ARR, because the standalone consumer layer will be absorbed by broker-native products.

I pass. This is precisely the prevailing-AI-architecture red flag: common models, aggregated public information, and self-service distribution without a measured discontinuity or proprietary learning loop. My one flip condition is a named brokerage or RIA paying Ekpa at least $1 million annually after a blind evaluation demonstrates fivefold analyst throughput with no increase in material research errors. That would prove capability, economic value, and a distribution position the present dossier does not establish.
