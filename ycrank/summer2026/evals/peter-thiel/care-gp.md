# Care GP -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

What important truth does Care GP know? The strongest formulation is that fragmented GP administration can become an autonomous operating layer rather than remain a collection of human queues. The falsifier is straightforward: if practice-management vendors or Facere can match Care GP’s accuracy, workflow coverage, and economics, then this is a feature bundle rather than a secret. Processing more than 3 million documents and reducing one clinic’s allocation workload from six hours to under 30 minutes proves useful automation. It does not prove a contrarian insight: Care GP reports a 5%–20% human edit rate, and several companies are pursuing substantially the same transition.

The clearest evidence is distribution. Care GP reached US$200,000 ARR after 3.5 months, reported an 85% pilot conversion rate and US$5,000 ACV within six months, and tripled over 90 days through referrals and word of mouth rather than cold outreach. The Bp Partner Network listing places the product inside an existing purchasing and implementation channel. This is unusually strong pre-seed evidence that clinic operators have an urgent problem and that contract value can support onboarding. Distribution is not something Care GP plans to discover later; it is already part of the product.

But distribution can sell a commodity. Australia’s 7,000 GP clinics form a coherent beachhead, and more than 400 clinic operators provide meaningful density. Yet no monopoly mechanism has emerged inside it. The 3 million processed documents may improve models, but the dossier provides no independent accuracy advantage, retention cohort, exclusive data right, or measured switching cost. Facere already offers document allocation and voice scheduling through Bp Premier. Expanding from documents and calls into email, billing, referrals, and records transfer adds surface area; it does not answer why an incumbent cannot copy or bundle the system. Care GP has found a market, not ownership of one.

The revenue engine is coherent but presently bounded. At the reported US$5,000 ACV, complete penetration of the stated 7,000-clinic domestic segment implies roughly US$35 million ARR. That could produce an excellent company, but not yet a power-law outcome from my own check. International expansion or materially higher enterprise pricing might change the arithmetic, although neither has been demonstrated. Profitability within six months is evidence of sound economics; it is not evidence that this engine can become a multibillion-dollar platform.

The foundation remains unresolved rather than disproven. Melvin Chen is plainly committed enough to build a ten-person organization and brings prior company-building and commercial experience. But YC now lists him as the sole founder, while Startmate previously named Ethan Atkins and Andy Bui as co-founders. Their departure or continuing role is not reconciled. There is also no evidence concerning prior team alignment, founder ownership, salary, or who owns the core technical work. Missing evidence receives the default; the unexplained change prevents affirmative credit.

The strongest objection to passing is the customer behavior: 150 clinics within six months, zero reported churn, 140% average monthly usage growth, NPS rising from 11 to 57, and continued expansion to more than 400 operators. Those facts could mean Care GP is quietly becoming the default administrative layer before competitors appreciate the importance of the Australian wedge. But the known-exception threshold would require an independently demonstrated zero-to-one capability or a compounding mechanism that overrides competition. Self-reported growth and useful automation do not yet establish either.

My 36-month base case is that Care GP reaches US$8–15 million ARR, remains concentrated in Australia, and is acquired by Best Practice Software rather than becoming an independent global platform. That is a successful distribution outcome but not a last-mover outcome. I would reverse this pass if an independently verified 12-month cohort of at least 100 clinics showed greater than 120% net revenue retention and a document-exception rate less than half Facere’s on equivalent Bp Premier workflows. Until then: valuable product, strong sales, weak monopoly.
