# Topos -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest private-company path for Topos in Fall 2026. A scientific software platform or pharma R&D organization could buy Egregore to control and exclusively integrate its reasoning-verification capability. Ownership could justify a premium if exclusive access or control of development created measurable value beyond a license or an internal build. That ownership case is an inference. Edison Scientific’s announced Pfizer deployment shows that an overlapping research workflow can attract a pharma partner. It supplies limited evidence for an acquisition rationale and no usable price evidence for Topos. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Defensibility)

The memo records a beta intake form and the founders’ joint work in machine-checked mathematics. Topos claims Egregore can coordinate thousands of agents on one task. The next operating milestone is a paid production pilot that measures fully loaded cost per expert-accepted result against the buyer’s existing workflow at comparable quality. The beta and the founders’ technical work make that test plausible. Pilot funding, runway, and ownership and transfer rights for the core assets remain unverified. The memo supplies no usable evidence for an independent fallback that pays this holder. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Founders & Team; Defensibility; Key Facts)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** Assume a $500,000 check at $10 million post-money, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years. For the modeled sales, assume zero net debt and other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. Assume simple pro rata distribution before investor taxes, with no preference adjustment. All consideration is cash at close. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and separate compensation are each $0. These assumptions are unverified, and the sale prices are scenarios.

- **Modest, year 5.** A $40 million sale yields $1.2 million to the holder, or 2.4×.
- **Strong, year 7.** A $100 million sale yields $3.0 million, or 6×. The hurdle is about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, and $62.5 million at 4%.
- **Prolonged private, through year 8.** Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The holder retains the shares and realizes $0, or 0×.

### Countercase

A buyer could get comparable results from its own system or through a commercial agreement. The memo describes existing multi-agent research systems, available orchestration infrastructure, and a competing pharma deployment. It also flags coordination overhead and the continuing cost of empirical validation, either of which could erode Egregore’s savings. This evidence makes an ownership premium speculative. It leaves open whether a substitute can match Topos’s verification capability at comparable cost and quality, so the structural Pass gate is unproven. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production pilot with a prospective acquirer demonstrate repeatable savings at comparable quality and quantify an advantage of ownership over licensing or building that could support roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity at the modeled 3% stake? A documented buyer-side case at that level would move the action to Reach out. A pilot showing that a readily available substitute delivers equivalent economics would move it to Pass.
