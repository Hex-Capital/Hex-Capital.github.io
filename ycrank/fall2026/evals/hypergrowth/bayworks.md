# Bayworks -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Bayworks’ strongest path is to sell warranty documentation to a dealership group and roll it out across locations that share a supported dealership management system and warranty workflow. The memo documents a recurring job tied to each eligible repair order, a technician review step before writeback, and a downloadable PBS extension. Assuming strong fit with the initial buyer, dealer groups abroad could reuse the dictation, evidence capture, and review flow. Portability depends on whether the repair-order integration and manufacturer templates carry over with bounded changes. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Bayworks reports six dealership groups, more than 80 locations in 110 days, and nearly seven figures in cARR within four months. A named dealership testimonial supports real use. For the listed two-person team, the reported rollout is an encouraging adoption signal. The share of cARR tied to live, paying deployments is undisclosed, leaving the realized commercial slope unscored beyond the default. Prior repair narratives and integration into daily work give dealers a reason to keep using the product. Adding locations could grow account spend if contracts charge for them, but the memo gives no pricing or renewal evidence to establish that mechanism. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks, Defensibility, The Idea)

### Countercase

The buying path runs through a dealership demo and dealership-issued accounts. The PBS extension gives Bayworks something it can reuse, while the effort required to activate a typical dealer remains unclear. If dealer software and warranty requirements differ across markets, rollout could require new integration and template work. The contribution on the next site would depend on its recurring fee less attributable sales, setup, transcription and drafting, media storage, and support costs. Reusing the PBS integration could reduce setup costs, but the memo provides neither site pricing nor measured delivery effort. The structural gate stays open because the documented dependencies leave a plausible path through groups with compatible systems and warranty requirements. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
