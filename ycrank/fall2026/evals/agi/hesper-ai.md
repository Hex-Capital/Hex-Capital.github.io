# Hesper AI -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Hesper’s strongest seed case is that automating record collection, provider checks, and evidence assembly could make it economical for insurers, MGAs, and TPAs to investigate claims they currently leave uninvestigated. Hesper puts outsourced investigations at $1,500–$2,500 per claim. A founder reported 82% less average adjuster time per claim at one MGA over two weeks. That result supports a plausible labor-saving mechanism. The memo leaves open whether the trial covered claims previously skipped, held investigation quality constant, or counted all review, retries, and exceptions. Hesper reports paying production customers and pilots, with paid repeat use among newly served buyers still unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Scale scenario.** Assume an MGA starts investigating lower-value claims it currently skips. The transaction becomes viable if the expected benefit of a completed investigation exceeds the buyer’s usage fee and remaining adjuster cost. Hesper earns positive incremental contribution only if that fee also covers its inference, database queries, calls, human exception handling, and incremental integration and support costs. Claims are a plausible expansion unit under usage-based pricing. The memo leaves eligible claim volume, all-in cost per completed investigation, and repeat use at standard pricing unknown. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

The New York pre-seed company plausibly fits the mandate. The founders bring document automation and insurance sales experience and report rebuilding the product around investigations after their fraud-scoring product drew no buyers. That pivot shows early execution. Sustained delivery at acceptable accuracy remains the next operating test. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Hesper may be making existing investigations faster while incumbent claims platforms capture the value. The memo documents Duck Creek’s native agent infrastructure and FRISS’s Guidewire referral and case-status integration. Hesper lists integrations that return cited findings to existing claims systems. Carrier-specific rules and approvals could make switching costly, but that remains an inference. With equally capable models, a buyer could prefer its existing platform if it can reproduce Hesper’s evidence gathering and audit trail. Adjuster sign-off and exception work could also erase the savings on claims with thin expected returns. The memo establishes credible alternatives and leaves both Path B conditions open. It provides no separate evidence that Hesper controls a scarce input for Path A. A feasible seed-stage test supports Watch, with low confidence because the decisive evidence is largely company-reported or missing. (Defensibility, Key Risks, Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent MGAs or TPAs repeatedly pay standard per-claim rates for Hesper to investigate claims they previously skipped when their existing platform has access to equally capable models?

A paid comparative trial on those claims should measure completed investigations at equivalent accuracy and audit quality, count all human review and exceptions, and track repeat purchases after incentives end. Repeat paid use, positive contribution, and buyer preference under model parity would support Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 with at least medium confidence. If all-in costs erase the market opening or buyers readily switch to equivalent incumbent output, move to Pass.
