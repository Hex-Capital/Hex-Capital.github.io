# Munito AI -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Munito AI has shipped a service, but I cannot yet underwrite the check. Proposed post-money valuation: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. Whether my money would be the first scarce capital: unknown. YC backing establishes none of those answers. I will not manufacture an auction from financing silence, but I also will not assume I am buying a neglected opportunity cheaply. The dossier likewise gives no use-of-proceeds experiment with a metric and deadline. Those are my two largest investment gates, and both remain open.

Alexey Eryshev is the strongest reason to keep looking. Leading inference at Mistral and building Le Chat’s Deep Research are shipped receipts, while five years building Airbnb Hosting Services adds experience operating software beyond a model laboratory ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/munito-ai), [founder website](https://alexeyeryshev.com/)). This fits my preference for founders who can translate between systems: Eryshev has worked on both inference infrastructure and products consuming it. What is less established is his operating access to the European enterprises whose procurement requirements Munito must satisfy. I give substantial founder credit without turning his former employer’s customer relationships into Munito’s distribution.

The strongest bull case is that a genuine inference operator can turn an inconvenient enterprise requirement into a simple purchase. Munito offers familiar APIs, an EU endpoint, and zero prompt/output retention by default; free service launched October 7 and paid tiers followed October 15 ([FAQ](https://docs.munito.ai/faq), [changelog](https://docs.munito.ai/changelog)). A small team could serve customers whom larger providers make talk to sales. That is a plausible opening. But Mistral already offers regional inference and third-party open models, so the opening depends on service execution and customer relationships that competitors cannot immediately reproduce ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/regional-inference-open-models-new-compute/)). The self-improving serving stack is still under development. Neither the technical cost advantage nor the proprietary access is demonstrated today.

Munito’s published integrations and 371 LinkedIn followers do not establish that its access causes purchases. Equally, paid tiers existing for twelve days without disclosed revenue is not evidence that conversion failed. The economics remain unknown: $0.11 per million input tokens and $0.28 per million output tokens are selling prices, not contribution margins ([pricing](https://docs.munito.ai/models)). I need to know what remains after compute and service costs before calling this a business that can survive without another round. Missing audits and a default SLA also matter specifically because Munito’s proposed buyers care about assurance; transparent residency exclusions help define the product but do not prove procurement acceptance.

I pass at **32/100**. The exception case deserves consideration: unusually strong conversion, retained enterprise demand, or demonstrated cash economics could outweigh incomplete financing evidence. Munito’s dossier supplies none of those overrides yet. Its founder fits my translation-layer pattern; its distribution has not demonstrated the trust-to-purchase mechanism I look for, and the competitive overlap activates my concern about advantages available to every provider. I am not claiming this infrastructure service depends on agent errors disappearing or that it must raise repeatedly. I am saying the evidence does not yet distinguish a durable business from a capable operator selling a reproducible service. My single verifiable flip condition for reopening underwriting is three consecutive months of positive operating cash flow from repeat paying EU customers.
