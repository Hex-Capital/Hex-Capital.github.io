# Palette -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

If Palette succeeds exactly as described, creative production becomes 35% faster and generation costs fall 50%. Useful, but not consequential enough. The dossier identifies a $788.5 million AI-video market growing to $3.44 billion by 2033; it does not identify a societal cost pool, expert bottleneck, or population whose economics change by an order of magnitude. This is currently a workflow improvement, not an option on a radically different world.

The routing layer is the strongest evidence. Palette claims 100% task-capable routing, 130-plus regression tests, failure refunds, and model selection across image, video, music, and editing. But the benchmark is internal, its method is undisclosed, and the measured gains are below the 5–10x discontinuity threshold. Runway and Luma already aggregate models while also owning proprietary ones. Palette depends on models from Google, ByteDance, Kling, MiniMax, and others that can be substituted or retired. The suppliers can reproduce the orchestration; Palette cannot reproduce the suppliers.

The strongest bull case is the team. Josephine Lee brings USACO-level technical ability, while Serena Pei has worked on diffusion models and multimodal systems across CSAIL, the Media Lab, LIDS, and Amazon. They have already shipped a public studio and API with only two people. As model proliferation accelerates, an intelligent control plane could become more valuable than any individual model. I have made exceptions for incremental wedges when a tiny, adaptable team could expand into an enormous existing value pool. Palette has not yet earned that exception: there is no revenue, repeat usage, proprietary-data loop, difficult recruit, or documented belief revision, and the cited market is modest relative to the incumbents already surrounding it.

The largest kill risk is commoditization, yet the disclosed milestones mostly improve routing reliability rather than test whether routing can remain proprietary. Regression tests and credit refunds make the product less fragile; they do not create structural ownership. Meanwhile, SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 remain roadmap items, and the privacy policy and terms still identify themselves as starter templates despite an enterprise sales motion. This is capital being spent on operating a product before the central differentiation question has a pass/fail experiment.

The economics are similarly incomplete. Ten dollars buys 1,000 credits, described as enough for a character sheet, storyboard, and 15 seconds of consistent video, but there is no comparison with the fully loaded cost of producing the same result through Runway, Luma, or a human workflow. The claimed 50% generation-cost advantage has no disclosed model mix, quality threshold, gross margin, or durability after upstream price changes. A low sticker price is not a cost curve.

My 36-month forecast is that Palette pivots toward managed enterprise brand-video workflows and remains below $3 million ARR by August 2029, because services and retained brand context are more defensible than generic model routing. The AI-wrapper red flag is active: differentiation currently comes from orchestration, interface, and implementation rather than a novel architecture or proprietary learning system. I pass. The one condition that flips me is a named enterprise documenting that Palette reduced a $1 million annual creative-production workflow to $100,000 or less at equal quality, with the advantage attributable to proprietary routing rather than temporary model discounts.
