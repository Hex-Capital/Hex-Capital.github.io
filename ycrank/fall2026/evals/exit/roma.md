# Roma -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Roma’s strongest supported exit route is a strategic acquisition by a productivity suite. Roma describes a shared task history that preserves decisions, edits, and attribution as work moves among agents such as Claude, ChatGPT, and Muse. A suite could acquire that capability to coordinate work across agent providers. The memo records the 2.0 launch and 35 US iPhone ratings. Senderek reports that some users connected 10 or more agents, an early signal of interest. The economic case for a buyer depends on teams repeatedly completing work across providers and measurably reducing manual follow-up. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

The operating milestone is a retained cohort of paying teams that completes recurring cross-provider workflows with measurable time savings. The shipped handoff product and missed-pickup fixes make that a plausible seed-stage test. Free access through year-end, limited usage data, and unknown capital needs leave paid retention and the cost of reaching the milestone unresolved. Acquisition feasibility remains open, including whether a buyer could carry over the integrations and what rights the holder would have at closing. The Notion employee tender provides context for secondaries, while demand for Roma shares and this holder’s eligibility remain unevidenced. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

Using the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale cases below are illustrative assumptions. Both assume $5 million in net debt and deal adjustments, a pro rata waterfall with no senior preference deduction, and an eligible holder selling the full stake for cash by year eight.

| Case | Enterprise value and equity bridge | Cash to holder |
|------|------------------------------------|----------------|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M leaves $35M distributable equity | $1.05M, or 2.1× |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $5M leaves $95M distributable equity | $2.85M, or 5.7× |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no share purchase or distribution through year eight | $0 realized |

Even in the strong case, selling only 80% of the stake at closing would yield $2.28 million, or 4.56×. The memo leaves realizable pricing, the waterfall, and the sale fraction open, so plausible investor proceeds receive the absent-evidence default. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

ClickUp already assigns work to agents within its suite, and Notion connects its agents to external tools over MCP. Both could build enough cross-provider coordination to serve their customers. Roma also relies on third-party services to execute tasks, and it reported missed agent pickups before shipping fixes. That combination could reduce what a buyer would pay for the product. The evidence supports a credible substitution risk. A structural closing block or materially inadequate proceeds on the strongest credible route remains unestablished, so the Pass gate stays open. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Roma’s recurring cross-provider handoffs give a productivity-suite buyer enough reason to acquire the product at a price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? A retained paid cohort showing measurable reductions in manual follow-up compared with native workflows, paired with buyer feedback that supports that price after deal adjustments, would move the action from Watch to Reach out.
