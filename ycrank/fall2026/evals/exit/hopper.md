# Hopper -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. Deepgram is a plausible buyer because its announced Daily acquisition adds communications infrastructure and Pipecat to its speech APIs. Hopper could add customer-specific tuning across transcription, language models, and speech synthesis, along with custom inference kernels and cache-aware routing. Ownership could save Deepgram development time if Hopper’s stack delivers repeatable cost or latency gains over Deepgram’s own deployment or a managed alternative. That benefit and Deepgram’s willingness to pay remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Hopper’s CTO reports one signed customer in production and more than 10 billion tokens per day across the platform. The milestone is to reproduce a cost-per-call advantage at comparable output quality and p95 latency under peak load across multiple paying voice customers. The reported rise from about 11 to 42 seconds on one deployment, and the use of spare GPUs and overflow routing, make the cost of sustaining that performance material. Runway and capital needed for the milestone are unknown. Hopper’s in-house serving code gives a buyer an asset to acquire, while rights to reuse customer call data and tuned models after a sale remain unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility)

**Proceeds bridge.** With actual investor terms unavailable, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, a $25 million enterprise sale yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×), before investor taxes and any unverified fees or senior claims. The memo gives no basis for a realizable sale price. If paid voice usage plateaus, buyers stay absent, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash goes back into compute and tuning, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. Predictable cash flow for a buyout, an eligible secondary, IPO readiness, and funded payouts are all unevidenced. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Deepgram already has proprietary speech models and an announced route to bundling more of the voice stack. Self-hosting and Baseten’s managed deployment give it alternatives to buying Hopper. The reported latency spike and sub-$1 spend by initial users leave the value of Hopper’s tuning at paid production scale unresolved. That supports Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported sale price that would leave the holder materially short. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

What price could Deepgram justify paying for Hopper’s transferable tuning and serving stack? A buyer-side valuation grounded in repeatable paid deployments, measured against building or contracting for the same performance, would move the action to Reach out if it supports at least $83.3 million in distributable equity within eight years at the assumed 3% ownership.
