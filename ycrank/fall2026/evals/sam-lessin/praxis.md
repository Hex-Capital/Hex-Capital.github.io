# Praxis -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Praxis has evidence that its founders can commercialize AI. It does not yet establish what I would be buying with my own money. Post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and proposed use of proceeds are undisclosed. Those are unknowns, not evidence of an auction. But I cannot underwrite a cheap, non-consensus check or identify the specific truth that check would reveal. “Agents that run law firm operations” describes the product; it does not define the capital experiment.

The strongest argument for this team is Diana Khramina’s reported work taking Gorgias’s AI Agent to more than 1,000 customers and $8 million ARR in under a year. Those are prior-employer results, but they are meaningful operating receipts. Her work with law firms at McKinsey supplies access to the other side of the bridge, while Maxence Guegnolle--Santi has built multimodal systems at SightCall and software at NextDNS. This fits my pattern of backing people who translate between ecosystems. The evidence is strongest on commercializing AI and weaker on owning law-firm operations, but these founders have done substantially more than collect relevant titles.

The investment problem is where the durable asset accumulates. Praxis qualifies inquiries, checks conflicts, prepares engagement letters, and chases invoices across existing systems. If that becomes the workflow a firm repeatedly relies on, the company could earn a valuable position. Today, however, firm-specific configurations are a possible future advantage. Clio already offers workflow-executing agents, and Lawmatics added native intake qualification in February 2026. Both can distribute overlapping functionality through systems customers already use. Praxis must demonstrate that firms choose and keep its independent operating layer; access to their records alone does not establish that advantage.

Being live with U.S. law firms is the strongest objection to passing: this team has reached actual deployment, and Diana has previously turned an AI product into a business. Human approval also gives Praxis a practical way to deploy without requiring perfect autonomy. But payment status, repeat usage, and measured customer outcomes remain undisclosed. The testimonial’s 1.5 hours of daily administrative work establishes pain, not savings delivered. The unnamed firm’s estimated $350,000 annual loss is likewise neither Praxis revenue nor recovered customer cash. My exceptions require demonstrated conversion and retention, unusually strong external validation, or a structure backed by proven founder economics. This dossier does not establish an override.

I pass at **32/100**. Missing revenue is ordinary at this stage; the unresolved issue is whether Praxis can capture enough value after integration work and human supervision to finance itself. There is no disclosed pricing, margin, burn, or seed-funded break-even plan to answer that question. My verifiable flip condition is a signed round below $10 million post-money funding a single 90-day experiment: ten firms renew paid intake-to-engagement deployments at positive contribution margin after onboarding, inference, support, and required review costs. That would connect price, customer choice, and cash economics in one venture moment.
