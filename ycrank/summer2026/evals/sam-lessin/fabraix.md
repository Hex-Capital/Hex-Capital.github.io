# Fabraix -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot underwrite Fabraix’s round before I know the post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and what this check specifically buys. The dossier identifies YC and an undisclosed Byld investment, but not whether this is scarce first capital or another auctioned AI-security seed. More importantly, it names no dated, pass/fail use of proceeds. “Build continuous testing and sell to enterprises” is a business plan; it is not the finite experiment that tells me why this dollar of risk capital dramatically changes the company’s value.

The strongest fact is the team. Ahmed Aly led international-payments fraud at Monzo and built systems at Two processing more than $1 billion annually while reportedly preventing $50 million in losses. Ibrahim Abdu built production-debugging agents at Meta and previously worked on compilers and databases. Together they have shipped on both sides of the bridge: adversarial financial abuse and the agent infrastructure being attacked. That is real outsider-insider translation capacity, not résumé decoration.

Nyx also appears technically substantive. Fabraix reports 10,000-plus attack strategies, a self-learning loop, a 78% AgentHarm attack-success rate against GPT-5.6 Sol’s 67%, and first exploits in under an hour. The bull case is that these founders are assembling a proprietary corpus before enterprise AI testing becomes a standard security workflow. If recurring scans feed customer-specific regression data back into Nyx, the product could become harder to replace with each release.

But that compounding loop is still hypothetical. Fifty thousand Playground attempts, 934 Product Hunt followers, 71 repository stars, and vulnerabilities found in public agents at dozens of Fortune 500 companies demonstrate attention and technical activity—not purchase causality, retention, or ownership of an enterprise workflow. The dossier expressly does not establish that those Fortune 500 operators paid Fabraix. This is the metric-theater risk: a large pile of attacks can coexist with no controlled cash driver.

Deleting the next financing makes the problem sharper. Fabraix publishes custom scan and monthly usage pricing but no revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or conversion data. Continuous black-box attacks may also carry meaningful inference and human-remediation costs. Meanwhile, HiddenLayer and Virtue AI have raised $56 million and $30 million respectively, while Zscaler and Check Point have already acquired overlapping products for distribution through established security channels. A technically superior attacker is not yet a durable company if buyers procure the capability as a bundled feature.

I am passing today, despite unusually credible founders, because the evidence proves offensive capability much more strongly than it proves an investable capital mechanism. The one verifiable flip condition is three named enterprises converting paid one-off scans into continuous CI subscriptions, renewing after at least two model or prompt releases, with disclosed positive gross margin. That would show the attack corpus owns a recurring workflow rather than merely producing impressive exploits—and it would justify reopening the round terms.
