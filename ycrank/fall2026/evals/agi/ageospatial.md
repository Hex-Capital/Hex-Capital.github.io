# Ageospatial -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Ageospatial has a plausible path under Industry enabled by superintelligence. Commercial property insurers rely on owner and broker submissions because site visits take time and money. Ageospatial estimates that 97% of commercial properties go uninspected. The newly viable use would be routine verification of property attributes and changes across locations that carriers currently leave uninspected. The product matches addresses to parcels and buildings, combines imagery and records, and flags properties for physical inspection. If those records are accurate enough for underwriting, carriers could reserve site visits for exceptions and lower the cost per verified property. Even with access to comparable models, a carrier would still need current imagery, reliable parcel matching, dated evidence, and integration into its underwriting workflow. The memo does not establish whether this approach meets carrier cost or accuracy thresholds. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

Building blocks of superintelligence scores zero. The memo describes imagery as an input to Ageospatial’s insurance product and reports expanded commercial imagery supply. It identifies no input whose scarcity constrains a specific AI workload as deployment grows. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

Carrier integrations could make the service harder to replace, but durability is untested and third-party licensing limits control over inputs. The founder reports signing the company’s largest insurance customer, and the insurance page lists AXA and Die Mobiliar. The memo gives no engagement terms or evidence of repeat paid use. The insurance launch and Sheikh’s prior delivery of geospatial products support seed-stage execution credit, while dependable carrier operation remains unverified. The memo gives no unit economics for the next carrier deployment. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Moody’s CAPE and Nearmap Betterview already sell imagery-based underwriting products. Stale imagery, parcel mismatches, and manual follow-up could erase the savings needed to extend verification to previously uninspected properties. The overlap supports Watch today. If a carrier comparison shows Ageospatial only speeds up checks that carriers or incumbents already perform economically, the enabled-industry path also scores zero and the decision becomes Pass. ### Decisive diligence question

Can Ageospatial verify properties a carrier would otherwise leave uninspected at a cost and accuracy the carrier accepts for routine underwriting? A representative carrier pilot should compare results against the carrier’s current workflow and an available imagery-based alternative, counting data, integration, manual review, and follow-up inspections in the cost per successful verification. Carrier acceptance for routine use on that previously uninspected segment would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that the benefit is limited to existing checks would move it to Pass.
