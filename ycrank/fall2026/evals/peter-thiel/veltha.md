# Veltha -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Veltha’s potentially important truth is that evidence-linked AI can make claims administration cheap enough to build a superior administrator around it. The company reports reducing six hours of file work to ten minutes and quadrupling each adjuster’s caseload. Those are testable claims. An audit showing that corrections, missing-record collection, and licensed review consume the apparent savings would undermine them. But the dossier identifies no prevailing belief that named competitors reject; Sedgwick and Reserv are already pursuing AI-assisted administration. Veltha has an efficiency hypothesis. The evidence does not yet establish a secret.

Who keeps the savings if the hypothesis is correct? Becoming the TPA gives Veltha a plausible way to capture operational improvements directly. That is the strongest feature of its transition from Co-Adjuster software to administration. Yet workers’ compensation is an approximately $5 billion market containing established administrators, and Veltha has not identified a smaller customer segment it can dominate. Source-linked regulatory determinations are a concrete product capability, but the claimed capability gap against Guidewire and Duck Creek remains unverified. Sedgwick combines claims operations, AI, and proprietary data; Reserv combines explainable AI with human workflows. This documented overlap makes the monopoly gate adverse. Exclusive data rights, measurable learning advantages, and customer dependence remain unproved.

The strongest objection to passing is the founders’ ability to make this operational transition work. Edin Citaku’s government AI deployment experience and Roland Jaloszynski’s underwriting automation experience concern precisely the difficult boundary between software output and consequential institutional decisions. Keeping licensed adjusters responsible for determinations also gives Veltha a practical deployment architecture. The company fits my less public interest in systems that make contested facts legible. But shared employment at Palantir does not establish that the founders previously shipped together, and the dossier supplies no evidence about ownership, salaries, or long-term alignment. I apply the founding-structure default. There is no documented part-time founder or capitalization red flag.

Distribution contains modest affirmative evidence. The founders attended an industry conference, generated more than 50 leads, and improved outreach responses from 0% to 5% in the earlier software phase. That demonstrates some access to buyers and willingness to learn how insurance is sold. It does not establish conversion into TPA contracts, procurement duration, or implementation cost. The change in offering matters because a buyer willing to discuss software has not necessarily agreed to transfer claims administration. Contract pricing and gross margins are also undisclosed, leaving the proposed administration-fee engine without the arithmetic needed to support a power-law outcome. Reserv’s reported $100 million ARR belongs to Reserv.

I checked the relevant exceptions: independently demonstrated technical breakthroughs, persistent customer transactions, or a concrete deployment path can justify financing before monopoly economics are settled. Veltha’s reported productivity gains could become that evidence. Their undisclosed methodology and customer base prevent the override today. Human approval is a sensible control; the dossier provides no evidence of prohibited activity or a regulatory order. Likewise, the changing product scope raises a focus question without proving that several unrelated businesses must succeed simultaneously. My pass rests on competitive overlap and an unestablished secret, rather than those hypothetical failures.

I would pass on investing my own money today. Veltha could build a useful administrator while leaving much of the economic benefit with customers facing competing offers. One verifiable event would reopen the decision: a named carrier renews a competitively won workers’ compensation administration contract whose audited results show sustained cost advantages after human review, with exclusive rights to use the resulting claims corrections. That would begin connecting operational improvement to a position Veltha can own.
