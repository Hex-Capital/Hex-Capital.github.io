# OpEra -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is OpEra’s strongest path. A warehouse, ERP, or supply-chain planning software vendor could buy OpEra to embed live replanning across its customer base. The ownership case depends on whether OpEra can reuse its integrations and constraint models across sites. If it can, a buyer could accelerate rollout and capture enough incremental software margin to justify owning the technology. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*)

The memo records OpEra’s public launch and the founders’ MIT retailer routing project. The launch reports 38% lower delivery costs and 10% more on-time deliveries on retailer data from that project, plus a 10.4% higher premium over market for a sugar producer and 54% fewer unfulfilled orders for a services marketplace. These are reported results, and sustained production performance remains unverified. The founders’ optimization experience and the proposed backtest-to-live-pilot sequence make a paid multi-site test plausible at seed. Deployment cost, funding, and runway are undisclosed. IP ownership, customer contract assignability, and holder rights are also unverified. The memo supplies no usable evidence of an independent route to holder cash. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

**Investor proceeds.** With actual terms undisclosed, use the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation. Initial ownership of 5% falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale scenarios assume zero net debt and no other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. They assume a simple pro rata waterfall without preferences and full cash payment at close before investor taxes. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. The prices are illustrative because the memo supplies no acquisition pricing or buyer economics to support a valuation range. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A platform vendor could license OpEra or build overlapping workflows with Timefold’s routing and scheduling APIs and commercially available language-assisted modeling tools. If each installation still needs substantial site-specific engineering, buying OpEra could offer little rollout advantage over those alternatives. The memo identifies the overlap and integration risk but leaves substitute performance and cost unresolved. A price cap below the investor’s hurdle remains plausible, and the evidence supports Watch without triggering the structural Pass override. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid multi-site production rollout show that owning OpEra is worth enough to a platform vendor to clear the investor’s proceeds hurdle? Customer-verified savings, measured integration labor at each successive site, and buyer-side economics comparing ownership with licensing or building would answer it. Economics that support the roughly $83.3 million equity hurdle under the illustrative terms would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that licensing or building delivers comparable economics and caps the acquisition price materially below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
