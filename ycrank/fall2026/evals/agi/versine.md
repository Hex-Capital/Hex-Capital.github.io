# Versine -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Versine’s strongest evidenced commercial case is making existing consumer payments easier to complete from an AI assistant. The company describes an MCP integration that can initiate transfers with passkey approval or standing rules, batch up to 100 transfers per prompt, and send bank claim links to recipients without Versine accounts. This could remove manual steps from bill splitting and repayments that consumers can already complete in payment apps. The memo identifies no buyer or payment use that was previously uneconomic, so **Industry enabled by superintelligence** earns zero. It also identifies no AI workload constrained by access to Versine’s payment capabilities, so **Building blocks of superintelligence** earns zero. Together, those scores trigger Pass. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Versine’s wallet and permission rules give it control over the authorization workflow. Bridge handles identity verification and bank payouts, and the memo identifies no structural barrier to incumbents adding similar authorization. Saved rules and contacts could make switching harder if users rely on them regularly. The memo provides no retention evidence for that inference. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

The observed launch signals include 182 Product Hunt upvotes and an Android listing showing 5+ downloads. The launch offered a sending credit, and the memo supplies no evidence of repeat transfers or willingness to pay. The public launch and the founders’ relevant payments, observability, and AI tooling experience support seed-stage execution credit. Dependable handling of payment errors and payout exceptions remains unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

Under the disclosed pricing, standard transfers and payouts are free and instant payouts cost $1. Assuming instant payouts are the only revenue from the next cohort, revenue per completed transfer depends on how many paid instant payouts those transfers generate. Contribution then depends on verification, settlement, payout, support, and error costs. The memo gives no paid payout rate or delivery costs for that calculation. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Batching and standing rules could make recurring payments to many recipients practical for consumers who currently skip them because manual coordination takes too long. Even with equally capable models, those users would still need payment authorization, settlement, and recipient payouts. The company’s documented workflow makes this plausible, but the memo supplies no evidence that users previously skipped such payments or that automation made them viable after approvals and exceptions. The countercase leaves the Pass gate in place. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Versine make a recurring multi-recipient payment workflow viable for consumers who previously skipped it because of manual effort? An unsubsidized pilot with that cohort would change the action to Reach out if users repeatedly complete the workflow and the measured cost per successful batch, including approvals and error handling, falls within what they are willing to pay.
