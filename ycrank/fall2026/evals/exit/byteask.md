# ByteAsk -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a coding-agent or software-delivery platform is ByteAsk’s strongest path to shareholder cash. Its build-aware index and native verification could help a platform ship reliable C/C++ changes across complex build configurations. The memo records Harness’s purchase of Augment Code’s assets, which shows that a platform has bought an adjacent capability. For ByteAsk, the ownership case is conditional. Control of the verification engine and its roadmap could justify buying it if deep integration produces durable gains that a license or an internal build cannot match. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

Nodus reported 2.3× co-located decode throughput and 52% higher co-located training throughput during a 30-day pilot. ByteAsk separately reported 68% lower cost at matched task success in its own same-model C++ benchmark. The pilot shows a concrete use case, and the benchmark gives a platform a cost claim to test. The operating milestone is to reproduce measurable gains across multiple production build variants while accounting for engineer review time. The reported $1 million pre-seed gives ByteAsk some capital to pursue that work. The memo leaves burn, runway, and replication cost open. (*Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** The memo supplies no terms for the proposed check, so the scenarios use a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on. The hurdle is $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years. The sale scenarios assume a cash share purchase, zero net debt, transaction fees, or other price adjustments, and a pro rata distribution with no preference leakage. Under those assumptions, enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and separate compensation are each assumed to be zero. The sale prices and dates are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no usable acquisition price or buyer economics to anchor them. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

| Scenario | Timing from check | Distributable equity | Investor cash | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 4 | $40M | $1.2M | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 6 | $100M | $3.0M | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No sale or distribution | $0 | 0× realized |

The hurdle is $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, and $62.5 million at 4%. The $100 million sale clears at 3% and falls short at 2%. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and ByteAsk reinvests available cash. The investor still holds the shares. The memo leaves core IP transfer rights and the shareholder waterfall unverified. Paid CLI subscriptions are an early commercial signal, with no disclosed revenue or cash flow to support a buyout or payout. The memo supplies no usable evidence for a holder secondary or public resale. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A platform could license ByteAsk through its existing add-on or build its own C/C++ verification into tools customers already buy. The Nodus pilot used target H100 hardware and logged 150 engineer review hours, leaving open whether its gains extend across configurations at a cost that gives ownership a material advantage. These facts make substitution credible, but the memo establishes neither an equivalent substitute nor a price ceiling that would trigger a Pass override. The modeled zero-cash outcome also does not trigger the override on its own. Watch is warranted because the economics of ownership remain the decisive gap. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a platform-sponsored production pilot show that owning ByteAsk creates enough incremental value over licensing or building it to justify a price that clears the holder’s hurdle? Move to Reach out if the pilot reproduces gains across production build variants, accounts for human review costs, and gives the platform a quantified basis for a purchase price that could deliver at least $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership. If a license delivers comparable gains and that caps the supported purchase price below the hurdle, move to Pass.
