# Resin Technologies -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to an established electronic design automation vendor is Resin’s strongest exit route. Resin markets a workflow that turns requirements into editable schematics, generates missing component symbols and footprints, and carries the design through board layout. An incumbent could buy Resin to add the upstream schematic workflow to its existing layout tools. That rationale depends on Resin demonstrating an advantage worth buying. The memo documents editable KiCad and Altium imports and exports, which make integration plausible. Resin describes its routing engine as proprietary. IP ownership, assignment rights, and required consents remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The founder reports that unnamed teams shipped boards in hours on work he says would otherwise have taken weeks. The memo records a public launch, YC backing, and a listed $500,000 convertible note. Those signals make a focused production pilot plausible. The milestone is repeat paid use on fabricated boards, with measured engineering time and rework against the customer’s existing workflow. Resin labels autorouting beta and requires engineering review before fabrication. Pilot funding, runway, and the capital needed to reach repeatable production use remain unknown. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at $10 million post-money, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and zero follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The listed note leaves conversion terms and priority unknown. Both sale scenarios below assume an eligible holder, a sale of the full stake, cash closing within eight years, $5 million in combined debt repayment and deal adjustments, and pro rata distribution of the remaining equity with zero further senior claims. The memo supplies no buyer pricing or acquisition economics to anchor either sale value. (Market & Traction)

- **Modest sale.** $40 million in enterprise value leaves $35 million in distributable equity and pays the holder $1.05 million in cash, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value leaves $95 million in distributable equity and pays the holder $2.85 million in cash, or 5.7×. If only 75% of the stake can be sold on those terms within eight years, realized cash falls to about $2.14 million, or 4.3×.
- **Prolonged private operation.** Continued reinvestment yields zero realized cash within eight years. The memo leaves eligible secondary demand and funded payouts unevidenced. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

An incumbent could extend its own tools. Cadence already offers automated placement and constraint-driven routing, and Quilter works within existing design workflows. Resin’s beta autorouter and required engineering review make its full-workflow advantage uncertain, especially given the founder-reported time savings lack a measured comparison. The Pass gate remains untriggered. The memo shows overlap in layout while leaving both the value of Resin’s upstream workflow and the attainable sale price unresolved. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Resin demonstrate a repeatable production-board advantage that gives an incumbent an economic reason to buy at a price that clears the holder’s hurdle? Repeat paid projects measuring engineering time and fabrication rework against customers’ existing tools, paired with an incumbent’s buy-versus-build economics, would answer it. Move to Reach out if that evidence supports a credible price range that leaves at least $83.3 million distributable under the benchmark terms. Move to Pass if the strongest supported route leaves the holder materially short.
