# Caladh -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition by a maritime defense supplier such as Lockheed Martin. Its completed acquisition of Ultra Maritime gives it an underwater detection product. Caladh says Eclipse can add disruption and integrate with third-party detection. If an operating-site trial shows that Eclipse adds meaningful protection to an installed detection system, acquiring Caladh could give Lockheed a way to offer that capability through its existing product line. Buyer interest and Caladh’s field performance remain unverified. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

Kita’s undersea engineering background and Caladh’s pilot offer make a field trial plausible. YC backing is confirmed, but reported funding amounts conflict, and the memo leaves pilot funding, cost, and timing open. The memo also leaves ownership and transfer rights for the technology and any required consents unverified. A separate route to holder liquidity is unevidenced. (Founders & Team, The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

**Proceeds bridge.** Use the rubric’s $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% starting ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-ons. A 5× return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale scenarios assume an eligible holder sells the entire stake for cash by year eight, with a pro rata waterfall and no preference adjustments. The prices are illustrative, and the memo provides no Caladh-specific basis for underwriting them. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

- **Modest sale.** At $50 million in enterprise value with zero net debt and deal adjustments, $50 million reaches the equity pool and the holder receives $1.5 million, or 3×.
- **Strong sale.** At $100 million in enterprise value on the same terms, the holder receives $3 million, or 6×. If net debt and deal adjustments reduce the equity pool by $20 million, the holder receives $2.4 million, or 4.8×.
- **Prolonged private.** If Caladh stays private and reinvests available cash through year eight, with no eligible share sale or payout, the holder realizes $0.

### Countercase

Lockheed could keep selling Ultra Maritime’s existing detection capabilities and let customers source mitigation separately. Ultra has demonstrated detection and classification in a Navy port-protection exercise, while Caladh’s integration and field performance remain unverified. The Coast Guard’s market inquiry shows interest in the problem, and funded demand for Caladh remains open. This is a gap that a customer trial can resolve, so Watch applies. The memo establishes neither a structural transaction block nor materially inadequate proceeds on the strongest credible route. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Eclipse turn a paid pilot at an operating maritime site into a follow-on order by meeting a customer-agreed performance threshold alongside the site’s existing detection system? A customer-accepted trial report and a funded follow-on order would move the action to Reach out to test what a strategic buyer would pay for that capability.
