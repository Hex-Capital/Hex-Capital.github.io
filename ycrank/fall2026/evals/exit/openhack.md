# OpenHack -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

OpenHack’s strongest supported route is a strategic sale to an application-security platform. A buyer could add business-impact triage and remediation to its existing offering if OpenHack consistently finds material issues customers’ current tools miss. OpenHack says Papermark fixed a cloud-storage exposure it reported. The memo also records 444 stars for the open-source scanner, a separate signal of developer interest. There is enough evidence to make a paid production test plausible. The reported $500,000 in accelerator financing supplies some initial capital, while paid adoption, retention, and the cost of reaching that milestone remain unknown. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. Both sale scenarios below assume a full cash acquisition within eight years, $10 million in combined debt and other deal deductions, and an eligible holder receiving 3% of the remaining equity proceeds with no preference diversion.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Cash to holder |
|----------|-----------------|----------------|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $10M in deductions leaves $30M in distributable equity | $900K (1.8×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in deductions leaves $90M in distributable equity | $2.7M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | The company keeps operating and reinvests available cash, with no eligible share sale or funded payout | $0 realized |

The sale prices are illustrative assumptions. Even the strong case falls short at 2.5% ownership, yielding $2.25 million. The memo provides no buyer economics or transaction pricing to substantiate either sale value, no deal terms to verify transfer rights or holder eligibility, and no evidence of a separate liquidity route. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A platform buyer could rely on existing capabilities or build the remaining features itself. GitHub already offers native remediation, and Strix overlaps with OpenHack on recurring testing and remediation. OpenHack’s own benchmark found 277 of 761 planted vulnerabilities, leaving substantial coverage gaps. Together, these points weaken the case for a buyer paying a premium. The overlap leaves room for OpenHack to prove incremental value in production. The memo establishes neither a structural block nor a payout ceiling, so the Pass gate remains open pending better evidence. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can OpenHack show that paid enterprise customers renew because it repeatedly finds business-critical issues their existing tools miss? Renewal records and customer-verified comparisons, together with contract values, would help quantify what a strategic buyer could gain. If those results support a credible acquisition price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle, move to Reach out. Until then, Watch.
