# Autoresearch, Inc. -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years forward, I can imagine a small team assigning research objectives to machines and returning to verified improvements rather than a pile of experiment logs. Autoresearch, Inc. has a little piece of that world running already: its reported 4,188 experiments over two days on 50 B200 GPUs demonstrate automated experimentation at substantial scale. But the distance between running experiments and delegating research still matters. Reliable evaluations must capture what customers actually need; improvements must survive production. The dossier establishes the experiment loop, with much less evidence about those next transitions. That earns some future credit, without yet giving me a destination I can underwrite.

The founders make the machinery credible. Karan Brar ran ML infrastructure at DynamoAI; Thomas Boser brings production experience from Reducto, Discord, and other ML roles; Jad Ghalayini adds formal-verification expertise. Thomas’s NIPS2017 FPGA demonstration gives me an actual earlier technical artifact beyond job titles. These are complementary skills for a system that must distinguish progress from measurement noise. What I cannot establish is a long-running, independently pursued obsession with this particular problem or evidence that the founders recruited their first believers around it. Relevant experience raises my confidence in execution more than in their choice of destination.

I keep coming back to who gets control. Today, an application team must coordinate data generation, training, tools, and evaluation before it can improve a model. Autoresearch proposes to absorb that coordination. That could return considerable time to builders, but the dossier identifies no particular institutional restriction, quantified rent, or gatekeeper it removes. Its stronger fit is my preference for concealed infrastructure beneath a visible application wave: orchestration, experiment lineage, and verification could serve many downstream products. Released training code and 25 interleaved confirmation runs, reporting median validation bits-per-byte of 0.9016, make this more substantial than a sector label. They do not establish a scarce asset that customers must obtain from this team.

The strongest argument against passing is that dependable verification may become the valuable product precisely because generating experiments becomes cheap. Jad’s background and the repeated benchmark testing make that possibility worth taking seriously. I can back a company before revenue when a working prototype, deep founder obsession, and committed counterparties compensate for missing commercial history. Here, the prototype side is tangible; the other compensating evidence is thinner. Paid-plan terms and enterprise MSAs describe how customers could buy. They do not show a customer repeatedly spending money, integrating the system, or trusting it with consequential research. I give the team credit for building, while keeping customer behavior at the absent-evidence default.

The data policy also complicates the appealing story about accumulating research memory. Self-serve inputs and outputs may train company models by default, with prospective opt-out; enterprise data is excluded by default. Customer ownership and those restrictions limit the assumption that every engagement compounds a reusable advantage. My privacy gate requires attention here, although sandbox pods, scoped credentials, and enterprise exclusions provide explicit limiting architecture rather than evidence of an established severe violation. Likewise, Karan is clearly identified as CEO; the dossier supplies no affirmative evidence of a leadership shell game or founder deadlock. Those red flags do not justify an additional penalty.

I pass with my own money today. Autoresearch, Inc. has credible technical builders and functioning research machinery, but my bet requires a clearer connection between that machinery and durable customer agency. My verifiable flip condition is a named customer renewing a paid campaign after independently confirming that its gains survive an untouched production evaluation. That would turn an impressive experiment factory into evidence that someone can actually delegate research—and wants to do it again.
