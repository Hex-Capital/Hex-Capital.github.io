# OpenHack -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest fit for this seed-stage case. The scenario is that small engineering teams with budgets for only periodic security testing could afford useful reviews on every release. OpenHack says it maps application context, verifies and prioritizes findings, and prepares fixes for review. Its own benchmark found 277 of 761 planted vulnerabilities. That result supports technical feasibility in a limited setting and leaves open how much human work is needed to match the coverage of the current process. The memo provides no like-for-like measurement of delivery labor after triage, review, retries, and exceptions. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The memo documents a released CLI and a free entry tier with capped usage. OpenHack names six launch users and says Papermark fixed an exposure it reported. Those claims suggest early use and a concrete customer outcome. Payment, repeat use, and whether these teams previously went without frequent testing remain unknown. The CLI release and Arora’s software engineering and security research background support seed-stage execution credit. Project count and review frequency offer plausible units of expansion. Custom enterprise pricing leaves contribution per additional project unknown after inference, onboarding, and support. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

### Countercase

OpenHack could add another layer to an existing security budget while teams retain periodic testing to cover gaps. With equally capable models and ample capital, Strix’s overlapping scheduled testing and remediation workflow and GitHub’s native autofix give buyers credible alternatives. Customer-specific maps and triage rules could make OpenHack worth keeping, but the memo provides no evidence of retention, switching costs, or buyer preference under functional parity. These risks leave both Path B conditions unproven and block outreach today. The memo identifies no scarce capability OpenHack controls that would support a separate Path A case. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will teams that could previously afford only periodic testing repeatedly pay OpenHack for per-release reviews when they can choose a functionally equivalent alternative using comparably capable models? A paid trial over several releases should compare human hours against the prior workflow at equivalent coverage, count retries and exceptions, and track whether buyers renew at a price that covers delivery costs and why they choose OpenHack. Verified labor savings and repeat paid preference would support Reach out if the rescored case clears the outreach threshold. If the trial shows that human review erases the savings or buyers readily switch under parity, move to Pass.
