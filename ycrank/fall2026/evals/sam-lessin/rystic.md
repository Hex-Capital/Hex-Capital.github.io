# Rystic -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Rystic has built something useful, but I cannot yet underwrite the check. Post-money valuation: unknown. Competing term sheets: unknown. Whether my money enables an otherwise-unfundable experiment: unknown. YC membership establishes none of those answers. I am not treating financing silence as an auction; I am applying my low default where the deal and capital plan are undocumented. A simulator benchmark cannot establish that the equity is cheap.

The founders are the strongest evidence here. Evan Whitehouse helped build Roblox’s service mesh; Zachary Kiihne shipped AI integrations in Gmail and Google Docs. That is a credible bridge between operating infrastructure and applications that act on it, supported by shipped work rather than titles alone. Rystic fits my unstated preference for teams translating between systems: developers need reproducible tests, while external services expose changing state and imperfectly reproducible behavior. The founders have worked on both sides of that problem.

The strongest objection to passing is that the product already catches something existing tools miss. An unnamed pilot discovered that its Slack integration read only the first message in each thread. Rystic also reports 50 parallel Stripe jobs finishing in 19 seconds, versus four completed before the shared sandbox’s 40-minute cutoff. Those are concrete reasons to try the product. But successful testing does not yet establish my proprietary-access criterion: the dossier shows neither customer-owned traces becoming an exclusive asset nor an embedded workflow producing repeat purchases. The pilot earns attention; it does not establish a dark pool.

Technical honesty helps. The reported 99.1% agreement across 1,813 Stripe checks comes with disclosed disagreements, missing API coverage, and a recommendation to retain a real-sandbox smoke test. Rystic is not claiming agent errors magically disappear, so that AI red flag does not apply. The commercial tradeoff remains difficult: fidelity requires continuing maintenance, while Stripe supplies its own tools and Veris already offers stateful enterprise simulations. At $89.99 per seat monthly plus usage, a price list alone cannot tell me whether customer payments cover that work. Without margins, burn, or a funded route to break-even, the economics receive the specified default.

I pass on the current evidence. The known exceptions to my financing gates rest on hard conversion, cash economics, exceptional external validation, or a financing structure that removes repeated fundraising; Rystic’s useful pilot and technical measurements do not yet supply that override. Nothing here establishes forced serial financing, an auction, or an equal-founder decision committee, either. My one verifiable condition for reopening the decision is an executable seed package below $10 million post-money that budgets one dated experiment: can paying teams renew at contribution profit sufficient to reach break-even on that check? That would connect the founders’ demonstrated engineering ability to an investable venture moment.
