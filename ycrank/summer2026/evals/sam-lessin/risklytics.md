# Risklytics -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I have three blank boxes before I have an investment: no post-money valuation, no competing-term-sheet count, and no evidence that this is genuinely the first scarce check. YC acceptance is not proof of an auction, but it is not proof of non-consensus pricing either. With my own money, financing silence receives the low prior it deserves. The dossier also names no use-of-funds plan, dated milestone, or pass/fail experiment. “Build a frontier-tech brokerage” is a business description, not a venture moment.

The interesting mechanism is the sequence Risklytics must complete: obtain producer licenses, gain carrier access, bind policies, observe exclusions and claims, and convert those outcomes into proprietary routing data. Today it is still obtaining licenses and explicitly is not placing coverage. Its database of policy forms, endorsements, regulatory filings, and carrier treatment may be useful work, but without disclosed database scale or placement outcomes it is a corpus, not yet a dark pool. Wholesale brokers, MGAs, YC, and Harvard are prospective channels controlled by other institutions; 123 LinkedIn followers and a media feature demonstrate attention, not purchasing behavior.

The bull case does sting. ISO’s 2026 AI-liability exclusions create real policy variation just as autonomous systems are producing risks that generalist brokers may misunderstand. Alexander Risio has shipped the Ember catastrophe-model beta, conducted clinical-ML research, and passed an insurance licensing examination. Samuel Gold brings finance exposure. A commission brokerage can become cash-generative without consuming endless capital, and a focused team could learn faster than Vouch while avoiding the balance-sheet demands of Corgi’s carrier model.

But the dossier supplies none of the evidence that would override my missing gates. There is no bound policy, named customer, commission revenue, carrier commitment, repeat usage, gross margin, burn figure, or route to break-even. Alton Risk already advertises substantially the same policy-gap analysis, carrier matching, and physical-AI brokerage workflow. Risklytics therefore needs proprietary operating receipts, not merely a narrower description of a public market change. Its abandonment of Ember after calling it a non-starter also makes the present thesis less trustworthy until customers—not another narrative shift—validate it.

The founder evidence is the best part, but only on one side of the bridge. Risio has technical and prototype-shipping receipts; neither founder has disclosed insurance-brokerage employment, successful placements, claims experience, or carrier-side operations. An examination creates legal eligibility, not insider access. The equal-founder structure is another unresolved risk because the dossier does not identify who has final authority over product, carrier relationships, or financing.

My 36-month base case is that Risklytics pivots from being a standalone brokerage to selling AI-exclusion and policy-form analytics to established brokers, with less than $1 million in ARR. That outcome would preserve the team’s stronger software capability while conceding that licenses, carrier trust, and distribution were the scarce assets. One condition flips my decision: by December 31, 2026, show ten independently verified policies bound through Risklytics across at least three carriers, with disclosed commission revenue and renewals or customer referrals attributable to its exclusion analysis. Until then, this is a plausible market observation without a priced deal, a capital experiment, or owned commercial proof. I pass.
