# Inflection Space Systems -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition by a constellation integrator that needs additional spacecraft production capacity. Inflection publishes a standardized Exponent configuration and reports building more than half of the spacecraft stack in-house, including flight and ground software. If Inflection demonstrates that it can build and qualify batches faster or at lower cost than a buyer can source them, owning the platform could give that buyer a reason to acquire it. The memo leaves those savings and buyer interest unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The first milestone is a successful on-orbit demonstration, followed by a repeat batch with measured production cost and cycle time. Inflection reports fabrication and testing underway and a Q1 2028 launch booking, following an earlier 2027 target. The memo gives no budget or runway for reaching repeat production. Ownership and transfer rights, required consents, and holder participation remain unverified. The memo supplies no evidence for an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility, Key Facts)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, starting ownership of 5% diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at full pro rata participation. At published prices, a 50-unit order would have a face value of $45 million and a 100-unit order $70 million. Those figures illustrate commercial scale. Paid orders, margins, and acquisition pricing remain unverified. (The Idea, Key Facts)

The sale cases below are illustrative sensitivities. Both assume an eligible holder sells its full stake for cash at closing within eight years and receives 3% of the residual equity, with no preferential diversion or deferred payment.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|----------|-----------------------------|----------------------|
| Modest sale | $50 million enterprise value less $5 million in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $45 million | $1.35 million, or 2.7× |
| Strong sale after flight qualification and repeat production | $110 million enterprise value less $10 million in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $100 million | $3 million, or 6× |
| Prolonged private operation | Cash is reinvested, with no eligible share purchase or distribution within eight years | $0 realized |

Even in the strong case, the holder must sell at least 83.3% of its stake at the assumed price to clear the hurdle. The outcome therefore depends on both a supportable buyer price and the holder’s ability to participate.

### Countercase

Apex and Muon already offer repeat-production alternatives with flown spacecraft, and Amazon’s reported in-house production shows that some operators can meet demand internally. Even a successful Exponent flight could leave a buyer with sufficient capacity through suppliers or its own factory. Inflection has yet to demonstrate the production advantage that would justify an acquisition premium. This supports Watch while the acquisition economics remain testable. The memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor materially inadequate proceeds across the strongest credible route. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a named constellation integrator value a flight-qualified, repeatable Exponent production line at a price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity value? A buyer-backed price range tied to measured production savings or capacity gains, with a preliminary proceeds bridge that clears the holder’s hurdle, would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that the strongest credible buyer price leaves the holder materially short would move it to Pass.
