# Super Powers Mobility (SPM) -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest supported route. A military power supplier could buy SPM to add vehicle integration and modular power export to its battery offering. The memo records EnerSys’s $208 million acquisition of Bren-Tronics and H.I.G. Capital’s acquisition of Inventus Power at an undisclosed price. Those transactions show buyer appetite for adjacent businesses. For SPM, the potential benefit is less time coordinating pack and power-electronics suppliers. SPM identifies that customer problem, but the memo contains no measured savings or buyer interest in owning the capability. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The seed-stage milestone is a funded repeat-production award with demonstrated unit economics and a costed capacity plan. SPM reports participation in four federal contracts and bookings into February 2027. It also reports $421,815 in realized revenue in one month, while its launch describes more than $400,000 in contracted revenue in one month. The memo leaves the relationship between those figures unclear. They support early commercial activity but leave repeat volumes, margins, and production capital needs unresolved. The $1.3 million pre-seed was associated with the prior off-road business, and funding for the current scale-up is unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

Using the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. Both sale scenarios assume a full cash sale within eight years, $5 million in net debt and closing adjustments, no senior claims ahead of the holder, and eligible shares paid pro rata in full at closing.

| Scenario | Holder proceeds |
|---|---|
| Modest sale | $50 million enterprise value leaves $45 million for equity and pays the holder $1.35 million, or 2.7×. |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value leaves $95 million for equity and pays the holder $2.85 million, or 5.7×. At 2.5% ownership, the same sale pays $2.375 million, or 4.75×. |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested yields $0 realized. The memo identifies no funded payout or eligible share purchase. |

The sale prices are assumptions. Using Bren-Tronics’ reported price as an enterprise-value proxy gives roughly 2.1× reported sales. At that illustrative ratio, SPM would need about $42 million in annual sales to support the roughly $88 million enterprise value required under the assumed deductions. The memo gives no repeat sales or margin evidence sufficient to underwrite that scale. IP ownership and scope, contract transfer terms, and holder rights also remain unverified. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A strategic buyer could source packs and power electronics from an incumbent or keep integration in-house. Inventus already supplies complete military battery and power systems, and any switching costs from SPM’s customer-specific work depend on designs reaching repeat production. Prime-contractor dependence, external cell sourcing, and the move to automated production could delay or limit that conversion. The modest sale scenario misses the return objective, but the evidence establishes neither a structural block nor a supported price ceiling that would trigger Pass. Watch fits the unresolved question of whether repeat production can create enough value for a buyer. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can SPM turn an initial prime engagement into a funded repeat-production award that makes an acquisition above roughly $88 million in enterprise value credible? An award with committed volumes, demonstrated margins, a funded capacity plan, and measured integration savings would move the action to Reach out if those economics support a sale above the hurdle.
