# Mnemonic Labs -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Even assuming strong initial fit with a quant desk, the growth case depends on whether other desks can use the same model in their recurring strategy research and execution workflows. That buyer and use case are inferred. The memo documents a November 13 launch and an invitation to contact the founders. The company reports building its training pipeline and model architecture in under six weeks and using over 25 TB of tokenized market activity in the launch run. That establishes a reported engineering milestone. The memo leaves cross-market deployment, buyer onboarding, recurring spend, and realized revenue unestablished. Each of those dimensions receives its absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The strongest case is proprietary trading. If the model can discover strategies with a durable live edge, the founders could monetize it directly through trading profits. Both founders have quantitative trading experience, and the memo identifies a potential feedback loop from collecting live orders, fills, and outcomes for retraining. Under that scenario, incremental contribution would be trading gains on added volume after execution costs, market impact, and any additional data and compute costs. The memo gives no live returns or execution costs to estimate that contribution, so Cost economics receives its default. Market impact and data rights are plausible risks. The structural gate remains open because the memo does not establish that either makes scaling infeasible. The action is Pass because the current evidence leaves both live monetization and a scalable route to external buyers speculative. (The Idea, Founders & Team, Defensibility, Key Risks)
