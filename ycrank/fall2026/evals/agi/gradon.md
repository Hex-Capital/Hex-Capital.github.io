# Gradon -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is Gradon’s strongest path. Its Windows agent is designed to produce civil engineering work in the firm’s existing CAD tools using its project history and drafting standards. **Scenario assumption.** If the agent lowers the all-in cost of producing an approved plan set, a firm could profitably accept smaller jobs it previously declined. Gradon reports one project took 180 hours against a 750-hour manual estimate. That 570-hour gap comes from a company-reported comparison whose scope, approved quality, and inclusion of licensed review, retries, and rework remain unverified. The seed case supports testing the mechanism. It leaves the new transaction unproven. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

The CEO reports mid-six-figure contracted ARR in four weeks and names an unnamed civil engineering firm as the first customer for plan sets, CAD, and reports. Those claims suggest early demand, with collections, renewals, and purchases of previously uneconomic work still unverified. The founders’ prior product launch and their mechanical design, systems engineering, and civil engineering backgrounds support seed-stage execution. For scale, each additional seat must cover its inference, support, and share of firm-specific onboarding costs. Unlimited use makes heavy-seat economics important, and the memo describes at least a week of on-site setup per deployment. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Gradon may speed up jobs firms already accept while licensed review and error correction absorb enough of the savings to keep smaller jobs uneconomic. Autodesk’s assistant is already in technical preview for Revit and Civil 3D, giving the incumbent a route to offer similar automation inside the tools Gradon uses. Gradon’s firm-specific standards and learned corrections could give buyers a reason to stay, but the memo provides no evidence of buyer preference or retention under functional parity. The memo also identifies no scarce input Gradon controls that could independently support Path A. Both Path B conditions remain unproven. The available evidence leaves a feasible test, so the thesis override does not trigger a Pass. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent civil engineering firms repeatedly pay for Gradon on plan sets they previously declined, even after trying a functionally comparable alternative? A paid live-project trial should compare approved outputs at equivalent scope and quality, log drafting, licensed review, retries, and rework, and record which tool firms choose for follow-on work at comparable pricing. Verified savings that make those jobs profitable and repeat purchases under parity would support Reach out if the updated score clears 65 with at least medium confidence. If representative trials show that review and rework erase the market-opening savings or firms readily switch under parity, move to Pass.
