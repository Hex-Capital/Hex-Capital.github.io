# Open Actuation -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales with account expansion.** The initial buyer is a drone OEM that needs a motor modification without a long lead time or a large minimum order. Open Actuation’s specification form gives OEMs a common way to submit requirements, and the memo records early heavy-lift samples for prospective buyers. Open Actuation reports that one OEM faced a $50,000 minimum and a 13-week lead time for a shaft modification. It says it built the motor in five weeks, that the motor outperformed the alternatives in the buyer’s tests, and that it is now fulfilling volume orders. The reported ramp is an early signal of fit. The memo leaves the lead source, repeat-order cadence, and realized revenue unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The growth case assumes that the same software and tooling can turn specifications from successive OEMs into qualified motors with less engineering and setup per design. Production orders could then spread those upfront costs over more units. Materials, assembly, testing, and inventory still grow with volume, and the memo gives no yield or cost data to show how contribution changes. In a second geography, the test is whether Gardena can serve the same buyer through the same workflow while meeting applicable qualification and sourcing requirements and absorbing added shipping and support costs. The memo identifies a reason for U.S. buyers to source domestically and leaves overseas willingness to pay untested. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Open Actuation could win urgent jobs and still scale like a custom job shop. Each new design could require fresh engineering, tooling, and qualification, while volume adds labor and ties up cash in hard-to-source inputs. The U.S. reshoring demand described in the memo may also give the first segment a reason to buy that overseas OEMs lack. The evidence covers one reported ramp and leaves both cost reuse and overseas demand open. The structural Pass gate is therefore unmet. A second rollout with measured costs would be more informative than a conversation today. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent drone OEM outside the U.S. enter through the specification form and place a paid production reorder using the existing Gardena workflow? A verified reorder with job-level records showing reusable tooling, lower engineering and setup effort than the first rollout, and positive contribution after production, qualification, freight, and support would move the action to Reach out.
