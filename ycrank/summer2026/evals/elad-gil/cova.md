# Cova -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Cova is licensed and live in Michigan but discloses no revenue or case volume, making Cova a high-single-miracle, low-observed-velocity company. The product does not require new science: available AI handles administrative workflows inside an existing care model. The unresolved question is whether Cova is “just working” or merely operable. Michigan licensure proves Jordan Ibe and Ansh Tandon can cross a regulatory threshold; it does not show families, caregivers, or payers moving through Cova quickly enough to support a venture outcome.

**Color Genomics** is the high rung on single-miracle architecture: workflow and data infrastructure built on commodity sequencing, without betting on new chemistry. Cova shares that mechanism because Cova applies existing AI to intake, benefits coordination, enrollment, insurance, and billing rather than inventing a clinical technology. Cova diverges from Color because operating the provider adds caregiver recruitment, service quality, payer compliance, and April 2026 electronic-visit-verification enforcement. Those are execution dependencies rather than independent miracles, but their combination makes Cova less clean than pure workflow infrastructure.

**Mistral** was the low-rung revenue exception: pre-revenue capital intensity would normally fail the velocity test, but post-ChatGPT market acceleration and founders who had shipped Llama justified overriding it. Cova has no comparable exception. Cova reports being licensed and live, yet discloses no patients, reimbursements, repeat usage, named payer relationships, or revenue; 227 LinkedIn followers are a false signal, not buying behavior. Pre-seed explains limited history, but real home-care transactions produce legible artifacts quickly. Cova receives the absent-evidence default until the reimbursement curve appears.

Michigan’s April 1, 2026 EVV enforcement can make Cova’s automation compound over the next 18 months: each completed enrollment and claim could improve Cova’s operating playbook while spreading compliance costs across more cases. The catalyst is nevertheless mixed. EVV increases administrative pain, but it does not newly create demand, and the underlying U.S. market is projected to grow at a steady 7.4% CAGR. Cova’s vertical integration is a strategic response to a market, not evidence that the market itself reopened in the way Ukraine reopened defense or APIs reopened payments.

Jordan Ibe’s VA medical-center exposure and Ansh Tandon’s year building AI for home-care agencies give Cova more shipping evidence than their credentials alone suggest. Cova moved from agency software to becoming a licensed provider, which is a concrete, difficult artifact; Ansh’s two-star public GitHub project is much weaker evidence of repeated user-driven iteration. The score would flip into the top tier if Cova showed successive workflow releases tied to faster enrollment, cleaner claims, or higher caregiver retention across actual cases. Without that loop, Cova has shipped a substantial pivot, not yet demonstrated the Perplexity pattern of shipping between conversations.

**Stripe** became an index on e-commerce because every new internet business increased demand for the same software rail without Stripe supplying the merchant’s labor. Cova currently has the opposite scaling geometry: every additional Cova case requires caregiver capacity, enrollment work, service oversight, and reimbursement collection. Michigan also allows eligible caregivers to enroll directly, while Aidaly and Entyre already pursue family-caregiver compensation. Cova may build valuable workflow data, but Cova must win local supply and payer operations case by case rather than automatically capturing growth in home care.

Cova’s 80% Medicaid direct-care compensation constraint leaves only a narrow administrative pool from which automation can create margin. By July 2029, I expect Cova to remain a Michigan-first agency operating in no more than one adjacent state with $3 million to $8 million in annualized net revenue, rather than becoming a national software platform or venture-scale index on home care. Cova can reach that range because the work is technically buildable; Cova is unlikely to outrun it because caregiver capacity and regulated service delivery scale more slowly than software.

Cova’s strongest bull case is the **Color Genomics** exception: Cova could turn an unattractive regulated service into workflow infrastructure, with April 2026 EVV enforcement and the Medicaid 80% rule making low administrative overhead decisive. Cova’s founders spent a year inside home-care workflows and already obtained Michigan licensure, so the provider pivot could generate proprietary operational data competitors cannot acquire from outside. The evidence that wins today is Cova’s absence of case volume or revenue alongside direct caregiver enrollment and funded competitors such as Aidaly. I would flip to Invest if Cova reports at least $100,000 in monthly net revenue growing above 20% month over month for three consecutive months, with positive contribution margin after caregiver compensation.
