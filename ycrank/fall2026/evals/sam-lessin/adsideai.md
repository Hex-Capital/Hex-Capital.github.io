# Adside.ai -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Adside.ai has shipped a product; the dossier has not established what investment I am being offered. Post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and the purpose of the next check are all undisclosed. I will not infer an auction from YC membership. But I also cannot underwrite scarce capital without knowing the price or what discrete truth the money buys. Connecting more advertising platforms is a product roadmap. A venture moment requires a measurable experiment and a deadline; neither is documented here.

The strongest argument for writing a check is the founders. Robin Choy and Guillaume Miara built and sold Botdog, with Miara reporting a seven-figure exit within 18 months. They also operated paid advertising for their own software business. That gives Adside.ai a credible bridge between buying advertising and building the machinery that operates it. This fits my preference for founders who have worked on both sides of a system. Their four years sharing a coworking space and subsequent joint exit provide substantially more evidence than complementary job titles ([About](https://www.adside.ai/about), [Miara](https://www.guillaumemiara.com/)).

The problem is what that machinery lets them own. Adside reports **30 companies live**, which deserves attention at pre-seed, but paying status, repeat usage, and acquisition source are unspecified. The workshop attracted more than 40 people; the dossier does not connect attendance to purchases. Meanwhile, the 133,000-plus advertising records demonstrate implemented research infrastructure, not proprietary customer access. Public ad libraries and reusable Apify components make shipping faster for competitors too. Customer-specific workflows could become valuable, but the privacy policy excludes shared-model training on customer data, so I cannot quietly award the company a cross-customer learning advantage ([YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/VrT-adside-ai-ai-native-paid-ads-agency), [Apify](https://blog.apify.com/how-adside-unified-three-ad-libraries/)).

There is a plausible small, profitable business here. A **$3,500 monthly managed-service minimum**, or 10% of advertising spend, creates room to charge for accountable execution. Human approval before spending is also a sensible response to AI errors. The unresolved question is how much human work each account consumes. Pricing establishes an offer, not realized margin; the dossier supplies no receipts, delivery costs, burn, or break-even plan. Omneky and Madgicx already overlap the workflow, while advertising platforms control essential access. Better agents alone therefore give me little reason to assume durable pricing power ([Pricing](https://www.adside.ai/pricing), [Terms](https://www.adside.ai/terms)).

I pass on the evidence presented. The bull case stings: a proven two-person team could turn 30 live accounts into a cash-generating agency before a larger competitor finishes its integration roadmap. But my exceptions for AI businesses or missing financing gates require demonstrated conversion, retention, cash economics, or unusually strong external validation. A prior founder exit does not establish those properties in Adside.ai. Nor does the dossier establish auction tactics, equal-founder paralysis, or explicitly uneconomic operations; those remain unproven rather than additional deductions. My single flip condition is a verified 90-day operating record showing retained customer cash receipts covering delivery costs and company burn without new financing. That would establish a functioning base business worth underwriting at the actual round price.
