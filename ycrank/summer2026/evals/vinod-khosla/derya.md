# Derya -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The $1.3 trillion attributed to redundant logistics labor is consequential, but Derya has not shown an orders-of-magnitude mechanism for eliminating it. The product currently bundles sourcing, booking, customs, storage, tracking, and exception handling into an outsourced freight office. That may replace coordination headcount for hardware companies, but replacing one operating team with another is labor substitution, not yet a 100x resource multiplier. The consequence case is therefore large in dollars and weak in demonstrated leverage.

The evidence is strongest where most pre-seed dossiers are weakest: customers are paying. Derya reports $80,000 of contracted revenue and more than $3.2 million of sourced freight opportunities in its first month, while TVO committed 20 containers. Karaca brings maritime law, CEVA/CMA CGM experience, and a prior six-figure-ARR business; Turna built ship software used across 30-plus vessels representing $200 million of fleet value. This is a credible pair attacking an undeniable cost pool, not generic AI résumés searching for a logistics use case.

The missing fact is technical discontinuity. No benchmark shows that Derya processes a shipment five times faster, resolves exceptions more accurately, or supports materially more freight per operator than Flexport, Forto, or a conventional forwarding office. Its public differentiation—month-to-month service, no freight-rate markup, and specialists executing each function—is commercial packaging. Meanwhile, 5U AI is already automating quoting, booking, tracking, and invoice reconciliation inside incumbent forwarders. With no proprietary architecture, exclusive data, retention evidence, or measured productivity delta, the “agentic” label earns no technical credit.

The bull case is the known exception for a tiny team entering an enormous existing value pool before the initial product becomes technically exceptional. Derya’s first-month revenue, complementary founders, and broad operating mandate could make the freight office merely the wedge into a supply-chain control layer. The material difference is that no evidence yet shows the wedge compounding into software leverage: every additional booking may still create customs, compliance, insurance, and exception workload for specialists. Breadth can generate learning data, but it can also conceal an operations company inside an AI narrative.

Capital should now attack one kill risk: whether shipment volume can grow much faster than human operating effort without increasing compliance failures. The dossier supplies no experiment, threshold, cost, or stop condition for that question. It also supplies no bottom-up economics showing that a flat monthly fee covers variable specialist workload across difficult customers. My 36-month base case is a $1 million–$4 million ARR, services-heavy forwarding office—or a pivot toward selling automation to existing forwarders—rather than a defensible full-stack platform.

I pass today. One verifiable result would flip me: a mature customer cohort showing at least five times more shipments per Derya operator than a conventional forwarding office while sustaining above 70% gross margin and stable exception or compliance rates. That would simultaneously establish technical leverage and credible unsubsidized economics. Until then, the downside is one check, but the upside remains bounded by labor rather than software.
