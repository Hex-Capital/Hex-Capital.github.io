# Kebra -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, an HVAC job should close its own administrative loop: equipment identifies itself, field evidence becomes a diagnosis, missing facts are requested immediately, and claims, parts, invoices, and customer updates move without an office worker reconstructing the visit. Kebra has built pieces of that chain. What the dossier does not supply is the chain itself—no dated future behavior, no intermediate industry transitions, and no explanation of why this overlay becomes the operating layer rather than a feature inside existing field-service software. I can imagine the future, but I should not have to invent the company’s map to it.

The strongest artifact is Quality Air. Technicians and back-office staff are using a functioning product, and Kebra reports additional deployments across Florida, Pennsylvania, and California. That is materially better than a rendering or waitlist. The bull case stings: by sitting across existing systems, Kebra can capture the messy audio, photos, serial numbers, and repair decisions that vertically integrated incumbents see only after somebody cleans them up. The Company Brain could become the connective tissue precisely because contractors do not want another rip-and-replace migration.

The ordinary object I keep returning to is the incomplete technician report. Why does an office employee reconstruct it from FSM records, calls, texts, and photographs? Because information is captured at the wrong moment and each system owns only one piece. Kebra returns time to technicians and office staff by collecting missing evidence and completing claims, parts orders, invoices, and follow-ups. That is a real operating wound, but the dossier does not quantify its rent, identify who economically benefits from the fragmentation, or show that Kebra changes contractor ownership or bargaining power. This is useful workflow repair, not yet a broken institutional rule turned into a new market.

Luca Hadife’s multimodal work at Microsoft and Mohamed Hosny’s signal-processing, computer-architecture, and Arista systems experience fit the technical surface. They can plausibly build the machine. I cannot yet see the pre-company obsession: no dated field-service experiments, shipped artifacts, operator history, or evidence that either founder independently pursued this problem before the current agent wave. The relevant pre-revenue exception requires a working prototype alongside signed proofs of concept and unusually deep, pre-existing founder obsession; Kebra only clearly demonstrates the first. CEO and CTO accountability appear legible, so I do not manufacture a governance red flag from missing ownership data.

The unglamorous-workflow pattern fits me; the concealed-infrastructure pattern does not yet. Kebra is an application overlay dependent on outside AI, transcription, hosting, communications, and incumbent systems, while ServiceTitan, Jobber, and Housecall Pro already distribute overlapping automation from inside their installed products. The tenant-specific data policy, prohibition on selling customer content, and refusal to train generalized models reduce the privacy concern, but recordings, credentials, images, and transcripts still pass through third parties without a disclosed consent or custody architecture. There is no severe violation in the dossier, but this is a gate I would inspect before signing.

My 36-month forecast is that Jobber acquires Kebra for less than $75 million after it reaches roughly $2 million to $6 million in ARR, buying a capable cross-system capture layer before it becomes an independent system of record. I pass today because the working behavior is ahead of the strategic destination, and incumbents already possess the distribution and core operating data. One result flips me: a six-month cohort of at least 20 contractors in which Kebra autonomously completes more than 70% of post-job workflows across multiple incumbent FSM platforms. That would turn the overlay from a clever feature into evidence that the lower operating layer belongs to Kebra.
