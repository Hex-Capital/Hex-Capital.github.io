# ProvenMetal -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Five LOIs from more than 30 manufacturing conversations make ProvenMetal a schlep-heavy startup with strangely light evidence that its founders are animals. ProvenMetal is volunteering to coordinate BOM review, domestic component sourcing, fabrication, assembly, testing, and inspection records inside seven days. Most hackers unconsciously route around such work. But ProvenMetal has not yet shown the equivalent of selling cereal boxes or building a rejected application into a working wallet. The highest signal is appetite for the grind; the lowest is proof of relentless resourcefulness.

**Coinbase** (2012) is the high rung because compliance was not incidental to the product; enduring compliance became the product advantage. ProvenMetal has the same potentially useful pathology. ProvenMetal promises an all-U.S. supply chain, component traceability, partner coordination, testing, and per-order quality records while JLCPCB processes 21 million orders annually. That is real schlep blindness. The divergence is that Armstrong had already built the Android wallet, whereas ProvenMetal’s five LOIs and 99.8% inspection-confidence claim have not yet been validated by disclosed paid orders or methodology. ProvenMetal is running toward the swamp, but has not proved it can cross the swamp.

**Kiko** (2005) illustrates the low-rung failure mechanism: an exit can become an escape hatch unless the founders return with fresh evidence that they will not stop. ProvenMetal’s closest animal tracks are Doyle and Carkner pivoting from Syncra, conducting more than 30 industry conversations, securing five LOIs, and opening seven-day ordering. Those are better than credentials alone, but ProvenMetal shows no unscalable revenue stunt, no customer emergency solved overnight, and no artifact that forced a reversal after rejection. The **Airbnb** exception therefore does not apply: five LOIs are useful, but they are not 1,000 cereal boxes sold for $40 each.

Will Carkner built circuit boards from childhood and operated Lumindt’s PCB-testing pipeline, while Johnny Doyle shipped IoT building-management hardware. That makes ProvenMetal unusually organic. Over the next 18 months, this origin should compound because every failed component, delayed supplier, and board revision teaches ProvenMetal something adjacent to work the founders already understand. ProvenMetal is not a pair of MBAs who discovered a $98.9 billion market in a report. The danger is that the June benchtop X-ray concept and the July managed-manufacturing service represent customer learning—or idea shopping. Repeated orders from the same hardware engineers would settle that question.

Will Carkner’s nine public GitHub repositories and two-star `yeoty-2023` repository place ProvenMetal above technical-by-credential-only founders but below the hacker genealogy of **Stripe** (2010). ProvenMetal would move up a full tier here if Carkner or Doyle had shipped an open-source PCB tool, automated inspection system, or quoting engine that manufacturers were already using before YC. ProvenMetal instead offers an instant quote and inspection workflow without evidence of the underlying technical artifact. The unstated hacker pattern is present as hardware competence, not yet as hacker-community proof.

Doyle and Carkner attended Trinity College Dublin and worked together on Syncra before pivoting, which makes ProvenMetal structurally resemble **Stripe** more than a hackathon marriage. ProvenMetal has a two-founder configuration with complementary manufacturing and electrical-engineering coverage, and the prior pivot gives the relationship at least one stress test. The dossier does not establish that Doyle and Carkner built together for two years, so ProvenMetal falls short of the Collison brothers’ unusually deep shared history. Still, no solo-founder or newly assembled-pair red flag appears.

MacroFab’s network of more than 100 North American factories makes the 36-month prediction concrete: ProvenMetal reaches $8 million to $15 million in annualized revenue by July 2029, remains a managed domestic PCB service using partner factories, and does not become a vertically integrated manufacturer. ProvenMetal can reach that range by winning urgent drone, defense, and robotics orders where seven-day delivery and traceability matter more than JLCPCB pricing. ProvenMetal’s schlep appetite supports a useful business; the missing animal artifact makes me unwilling to forecast the breakout version yet.

JLCPCB’s 21 million annual orders provide the strongest bear case: ProvenMetal may be a thin service layer between customers and suppliers, with partner-dependent turnaround, an unverified 99.8% claim, and no structural barrier. ProvenMetal could discover that the five LOIs belonged to the abandoned X-ray-inspection pitch rather than the current fabrication service. That case stings because the unstated demo-first pattern is weak and none of the known exceptions—**Airbnb**’s cereal stunt, **Loopt**’s force-of-personality founder, or **Stripe**’s prior outcome—supplies an override. I would still invest modestly because ProvenMetal’s founders know the work and chose an excellent schlep. Ten paying customers completing three repeat builds each within six months would flip the verdict upward; failure to convert even two LOIs into paid repeat orders would flip it to Pass.
