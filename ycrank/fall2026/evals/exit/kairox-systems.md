# Kairox Systems -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition by a defense or maritime systems supplier. Kairox says Tempest can operate in air and underwater and reports Marine Corps engagement and concept feedback from U.S. and allied military end users. The memo documents a public launch and MassRobotics residency, alongside reported accelerator funding of $500,000. A supplier could have a reason to acquire Tempest if field results show that it reduces the staffing and logistics costs of running separate fleets. Kairox reports laboratory-stage validation, and the funding evidence leaves the cost and schedule of field qualification open. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. At 3%, that requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale scenarios below assume the holder can sell its full stake, the sale waterfall pays pro rata after the stated deductions, and all consideration is paid in cash at closing within eight years. The prices and deductions are illustrative assumptions.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M in debt and deal adjustments leaves $45M for equity | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M for equity | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with reinvested cash and no eligible share sale or distribution | $0 |

The strong scenario clears the hurdle by $200,000. If only half the holder’s stake is eligible for sale at the same per-share price, cash received falls to $1.35 million. The memo’s adjacent procurement award demonstrates spending in civilian marine systems. The award and Kairox’s unquantified revenue classification leave acquisition pricing unsupported. Holder rights, the actual waterfall, and any independent liquidity route are unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Customers can keep their separate fleets, and the memo contains no field evidence that Tempest offers enough savings to justify a switch. Kairox reports laboratory validation, while procurement remains a future milestone. A strategic buyer could therefore wait for field qualification and customer orders, leaving holders private for longer. This supports Watch. The evidence establishes neither a structural barrier to a sale nor materially inadequate proceeds across the strongest plausible route. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price would a specific strategic buyer consider if a funded field trial demonstrated measurable staffing and logistics savings versus separate fleets? A documented, trial-contingent price range that credibly clears the holder’s cash hurdle after debt, deal adjustments, and the sale waterfall would move the action to Reach out.
