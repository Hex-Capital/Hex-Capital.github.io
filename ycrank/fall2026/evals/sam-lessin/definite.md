# Definite -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Definite’s proposed post-money, competing-term-sheet count, and use of proceeds are undisclosed. I cannot establish that this is a cheap, scarce check or identify the finite experiment my money buys. Those are missing facts, not evidence of an auction. But they leave the central underwriting question unanswered: what truth does this round reveal that customers cannot finance through buying the product?

The strongest argument for Definite is where it puts the intelligence. Read-only connectors, deterministic calculations, source-to-cell lineage, and retained rules address the actual problem of reconstructing financial context. Customer-specific definitions could become the durable asset while models become interchangeable. That fits my preference for bridges between systems that do not easily trust one another. Workato already offers connectivity and semantic context, however, so a finance ontology becomes proprietary access only when customers depend on it. Documented architecture establishes a product mechanism; it does not establish that dependence.

The purchase evidence is the problem. The September 2026 founder interview describes deals dying after demos and earlier compliance-product LOIs failing to establish purchase commitment. Moving to contract-first selling is a sensible response, but the dossier supplies no resulting paid conversion or retained usage for the current product. I would not transfer those earlier failures wholesale onto the broader offering; I also would not erase the only observed purchasing friction. The 297 LinkedIn followers and Dynamics Roadshow outreach add no evidence that Definite owns distribution. Its potential dark pool is inside customers’ books, and we have not established that customers will pay to keep it there.

The founders make the bull case harder to dismiss. BitGo custody controls, Optiver trading systems, Boosted.ai financial-analysis agents, and Meta recommendation work provide relevant technical exposure. The shipped product also takes correctness seriously through deterministic rules and human review. That weakens the generic-AI-wrapper objection. Still, the record does not establish prior ownership of a scaled finance operation or recurring accounting workflow. Nor does it satisfy the known-exception standard: there is no documented conversion, retention, cash performance, or prior exit strong enough to override the missing deal and capital-plan evidence. Three founders alone establish no governance problem; Gurshabd is identified as CEO.

Delete the next financing and the unresolved business becomes clearer. Definite offers monthly contracts, no deployment fee, and an opt-out during the first two months while building scoped customer connections. That arrangement could make buying easy while leaving the startup financing implementation. Without pricing, implementation cost, margin, or burn, I cannot tell; this earns the missing-economics default rather than a claim of proven bad economics. I pass on the available evidence. My one verifiable condition for reopening underwriting is a cohort of three unrelated finance teams retained beyond 90 days with positive cumulative contribution after connector engineering, onboarding, inference, and review costs. That would demonstrate that the shared model compounds into a business customers finance—and give us something concrete to price.
