# Neuromorphic -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The scarce asset is not the founders’ résumé; it is accumulated contact with physical failure. Vatan has worked across PickNik, Rapyuta Robotics, KABAM Robotics, and Augmentus, Ege researches contact-rich manipulation and policy generalization, and the team reportedly helped deploy more than 1,000 robots ([FounderN](https://foundern.com/2026/07/neuromorphic-y-combinatordan-yatirim-aldi/), Jul 2026). They also built together in high-school robotics competitions. This is specific knowledge acquired through action, and it is Neuromorphic’s strongest evidence.

The action continued after incorporation. A strategy change produced a customer contract in five days, and that biotechnology customer reached daily robot operations within one week ([Neuromorphic website](https://www.neuromorphic.vision/), Aug 2026; [FounderN](https://foundern.com/2026/07/neuromorphic-y-combinatordan-yatirim-aldi/), Jul 2026). At pre-seed, deployment speed is more informative than press or financing. It suggests that the founders understand the integration bottleneck well enough to collapse weeks of onboarding into days. The segment change from facilities to wet labs reads as rapid updating, not conviction theater; there is no evidence of the integrity, artificial-scarcity, or morale-dependence red flags that would veto the team.

But execution is not yet leverage. Neuromorphic describes an onboard LLM composing reusable navigation, manipulation, inspection, and tool-use skills, while public evidence identifies no proprietary model, exclusive data, hardware, benchmark, or switching cost. FieldAI, Skild AI, Physical Intelligence, and Generalist AI have collectively raised more than $4 billion to attack overlapping cross-embodiment intelligence, while open research lowers the cost of reproducing software. If a competitor copies the workflow composer, the remaining advantage appears to be deployment labor. A company cannot compound while it sleeps if every new robot requires another engineer to wake up.

The bull case is real and stings: deployment labor could be the mechanism that creates the asset. Each wet-lab installation could generate recovery traces unavailable to foundation-model competitors, turning a services wedge into proprietary cross-body policies. The team’s move into physical AI also matches the shift toward embodied infrastructure, and the $24.23 billion robot-software market gives them room beyond the $2.4 billion laboratory-robotics wedge. Yet the dossier identifies only one deployment and labels the data moat as inferred. A possible feedback loop is not a network effect, and unmeasured data is not proprietary data.

Economics provide a partial signal. The prior offering named a direct payer and charged $5,000–$10,000 per robot per month for hardware, software, monitoring, and support, while the current company has signed a contract. But current wet-lab pricing, recognized revenue, gross margin, and intervention requirements are undisclosed. Bundling hardware and support can produce revenue while concealing negative operating leverage. The contract proves willingness to transact; it does not yet prove that value capture improves with scale.

I would not invest today. Neuromorphic presently looks like an unusually capable robotics team creating a high-touch integrator faster than incumbents, not yet a durable robot-brain company. The exception for a strange, capital-efficient prototype does not apply strongly enough because the missing evidence is the substrate itself, not merely conventional traction. One condition flips the decision: ten paying deployments across at least two robot bodies showing that intervention rates fall materially as proprietary execution data accumulates.
