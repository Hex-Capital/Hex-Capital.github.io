# Touchmark -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Zendesk’s 2026 shift toward independently verified AI-resolution pricing makes **Touchmark** a catalyst-rich, curve-poor infrastructure bet: the market is visibly re-opening, but **Touchmark** has disclosed no revenue, customer, or repeat-usage signal showing that its quality-adjusted billing is already “just working.” That pairing defines the deal. Outcome pricing is skating for the puck; a “Coming soon” website and an unverified SDK are still founder narrative rather than buying behavior.

**Stripe** is the high rung for market re-opening because APIs converted payments from merchant-account bureaucracy into developer infrastructure. **Touchmark** shares the load-bearing mechanism: AI vendors are moving from token consumption toward verified outcomes, with Zendesk providing a specific 2026 catalyst rather than abstract TAM math. **Touchmark** diverges because Stripe’s catalyst simplified an existing transaction, while output-quality pricing introduces evaluation accuracy, attribution, and billing disputes that the dossier says remain entirely unpublished.

**Mistral’s** pre-revenue capital intensity represented the low-rung revenue failure mechanism I normally avoid; its exception was a post-ChatGPT market step-function plus founders who had already shipped Llama. **Touchmark** has no equivalent override yet: no revenue, named customer, deployment, billing volume, or usage curve is public. YC participation and a July 2026 launch establish existence, but for an AI infrastructure company **Touchmark** remains a false signal until customers actually let its scores change invoices.

Roman Yanushevskyi’s Lovable AI-engineering internship, two Citadel Securities quantitative-research internships, and IOI gold medal give **Touchmark** unusually strong raw technical capacity. Over 18 months, this dimension compounds only if **Touchmark** turns the reported SDK into weekly deployment evidence: faster evaluator iteration produces better calibration data, which reduces disputes and accelerates adoption. Ilia Bolgov’s Revolut product experience helps, but neither founder has a named independently shipped product, public repository, or documented user-feedback loop comparable to the Perplexity pattern.

Metronome’s $128 million and Orb’s $44 million make the index-position counterfactual precise: **Touchmark** moves into the Invest tier if quality evaluation becomes a neutral control plane that sends billable events into every incumbent rather than another billing stack competing against them. **Touchmark** currently looks more like a feature supplier because Metronome, Orb, Stripe, Lago, or Valmi can accept a quality score as one more metered event. The score flips if vendors integrate Touchmark once and retain it while changing models, billing providers, and end-user workflows.

**Color Genomics** worked because commodity sequencing had already supplied the hard capability; the company built workflow and data infrastructure on top. **Touchmark** is less clean on the single-miracle test: Touchmark must make automated quality scores accurate enough, persuade buyers that those scores represent value, resolve attribution when customer behavior affects outcomes, and displace or complement established billing systems. These dependencies are correlated, but they are not merely GTM execution on top of a validated capability.

**Touchmark’s** “Coming soon” website anchors my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, **Touchmark** will have pivoted from standalone quality-adjusted billing into AI evaluation or observability middleware and will remain below $1 million ARR. The catalyst should create demand for measurement, but the absent revenue curve and weak index position make evaluation infrastructure more plausible than owning the invoice. Billing incumbents already control the transaction path; Touchmark’s natural product is the signal they ingest.

Roman Yanushevskyi’s quantitative background and Ilia Bolgov’s Revolut product experience create the strongest bull case: **Touchmark** could be the Mistral-style exception where exceptional technical talent catches a real category step-function before commercial evidence appears. A proprietary dataset connecting outputs, quality judgments, accepted prices, and customer retention could make **Touchmark** an index on outcome-priced AI. The dossier still gives that dataset zero rows, zero customers, and no evaluation accuracy or dispute rate, so the exception does not yet apply. I would flip to Invest if three named AI vendors were paying Touchmark, quality-priced billing volume compounded above 20% month-over-month for three consecutive months, and invoice disputes stayed below 2%.
