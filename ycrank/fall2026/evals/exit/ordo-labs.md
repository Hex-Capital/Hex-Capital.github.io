# Ordo Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Ordo’s strongest exit route is a strategic sale to an established lab software vendor. Ordo reports cutting one post-analysis workflow from 40 hours to 4, including scientist review. Its source-linked reports and customer-controlled sign-off could help an incumbent sell a broader workflow to regulated labs. IDBS’s integration with Alchemi shows that an incumbent will integrate adjacent authoring tools. The acquisition rationale depends on whether owning Ordo would generate more value than partnering. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The memo records approximately $500,000 in YC funding and a separate angel round of undisclosed size. Ordo reports paid pilots in the US and Europe. Converting those pilots into recurring, validated production deployments that reproduce the reported savings is a plausible seed-stage milestone. Pilot pricing, conversion, and runway remain unknown. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale scenarios assume $5 million in net debt and deal adjustments, an eligible holder selling their entire stake, pro rata distribution with no senior preference carve-outs, and cash paid at closing within eight years.

| Scenario | Value available to equity | Holder cash |
|-----------|---------------------------|-------------|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M = $45M | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $5M = $95M | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private operation | Operating cash is reinvested | $0 |

The sale values are sensitivities. Paid pilots signal customer demand, while the memo leaves buyer pricing, payout terms, and closing rights unresolved. No independent liquidity route is evidenced. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

ScienceMachine already offers source-linked drafting, document QC, and electronic approval. The IDBS–Alchemi integration also shows how an incumbent can add related capabilities through a partner. Ordo’s customer-specific validation and release approvals could make deployment slower and reduce the value of acquiring the product. These facts weaken the case for a price that clears the hurdle. They establish neither a structural closing block nor a credible price ceiling below the hurdle, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could a lab software incumbent justify for Ordo after the pilots convert to recurring, validated production contracts? Signed contracts and repeated workflow savings would establish the operating inputs. A credible incumbent estimate of the incremental profit from owning Ordo versus partnering would test the price. A supported range that could clear the holder’s $2.5 million cash objective after deal deductions and the waterfall would move the action to Reach out. A supported ceiling below that objective would move it to Pass.
