# EveryApp -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Senescu has shipped enough that I would want to meet him. EveryApp has a working Gateway for identity, permissions and credentials, with applications running in customers’ Cloudflare accounts. That is considerably more informative than having been a senior engineer at Klaviyo. But my first question remains unanswered: what stopped him, what did he do about it, and how quickly did it work? The dossier documents construction. It does not document a particular collision with resistance. I cannot manufacture the latter from the former.

The interesting possibility is that EveryApp makes the second application much easier to adopt than the first. Once a business has installed identity, permissions and infrastructure, another compatible app could become a small decision. That fits my preference for programmable tools from which other people build things. The Cloudflare restriction could help a two-person team make the system coherent. But coherence has a price: existing applications need adaptation, and EveryApp does not promise backward compatibility. The important technical achievement is the functioning shared infrastructure. An ecosystem of developers building on it remains a hypothesis.

OpenSEO supplies the strongest argument that this could become more than an elegant infrastructure project. Its 25.3K GitHub stars and claimed 3,000-plus entrepreneurs suggest Senescu can make something people notice. They do not establish that businesses repeatedly use EveryApp to run several applications. The distinction matters because the current platform launched on October 26, only one day before the dossier’s measurements. Its 351 stars and 128,561 launch-post views tell us almost nothing about return usage. The narrow well might be entrepreneurs already using OpenSEO who want another application without another subscription. We have not yet seen them pull that second application out of the founders.

There is also an encouraging sequence: an initial Gateway, a standalone application, then a return to the platform. But a sequence is not yet evidence of learning. What did users do that made the standalone route necessary, and what subsequently justified broadening again? Without that observation, I give no extra credit for changing direction. Likewise, building OpenSEO on this infrastructure demonstrates firsthand use, but the dossier does not establish that a personally encountered deployment problem originally caused Senescu to start EveryApp. Authentication and deployment are real schleps. Their existence does not by itself prove an organic founding insight.

The strongest case against withholding a check is that a working developer product can precede any sensible demand measurement. Dropbox’s prototype helped justify an exception to uncertainty about its newly assembled founding team; the shared mechanism is that inspectable software can reveal ability before operating history exists. The material difference is that EveryApp’s dossier supplies neither similarly clear rapid correction nor unmistakable user pull to replace the missing founder evidence. I see no affirmative evidence of premature delegation, reliance on a distribution partner, or treating launch day as the whole growth plan. Those red flags are unproven, not reasons to deduct points. But their absence does not satisfy the resourcefulness gate.

I would keep talking, but I would not write a check today. At an announced starting price of $10 a month, managed hosting needs substantial adoption or expanding usage to become a large business; MIT licensing and alternatives such as Coolify make software availability insufficient protection. My flip condition is one documented deployment experiment: Senescu personally gets five independent teams running multiple EveryApp applications, fixes a shared blocking problem within a week, and those teams remain weekly active and paying four weeks later. That would connect the missing founder behavior to the demand this particular platform needs.
