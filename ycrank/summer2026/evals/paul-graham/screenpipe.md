# screenpipe -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The useful fact is not that Louis Beaumont calls this an AI second brain. It is that he built the ugly machinery beneath one. Continuous desktop memory breaks in mundane ways: databases corrupt, audio streams fail, monitors stop capturing, applications expose different accessibility data, and users worry—correctly—about secrets being recorded. screenpipe’s changelog shows shipped repairs across those failures, while the repository has accumulated 12,176 commits. This is real resourcefulness, though not yet the cleanest form of evidence: the dossier does not say which blocker mattered most, how quickly Beaumont responded, or what measurable user behavior changed afterward.

The technical proof is much less ambiguous. A two-person company has produced a working system across macOS, Windows, and Linux that combines event-triggered screenshots, accessibility extraction, OCR, system and microphone audio, local SQLite storage, transcription, encryption, search, an API, MCP, and agent permissions. The 20.9K stars, 2.1K forks, and 4,000-plus monthly downloads show that this is not a diagram waiting for financing. It is a programmable substrate technical users can inspect and extend. That matches the strongest pattern in my recent checks: build the difficult primitive first, then let developers discover what it is for.

There is also genuine pull, but the numbers do not yet form a perfectly consistent proof. In February 2025, screenpipe reported 202 paying subscribers, $3,480 MRR, 320 weekly active users, and 40% weekly WAU growth. Beaumont later reported sixfold active-user growth over two months and 20–30% weekly revenue growth for several months. Those are unusually strong pre-seed signals. But they are mostly founder-reported, the current revenue base is missing, and “300K users” sits awkwardly beside the earlier active-user figures. More importantly, “knowledge workers” is not yet a narrow well. I want to know which users repeatedly open the history, what they retrieve, and whether the indispensable job is memory, meeting reconstruction, workflow automation, or context for agents.

The origin is credible. Beaumont says he maintained an AI second brain from 2020, after infrastructure, machine-learning, computer-use, RAG, and satellite-intelligence work. The associated schlep is substantial: cross-platform capture, permissions, storage, privacy filtering, enterprise controls, and the social problem of recording other people’s communications. This is not a generic AI-for-productivity idea produced from a market report. The risk is that the schlep becomes liability rather than moat. Microsoft can bundle Recall, and local exportability limits data lock-in. screenpipe must win because its programmable full-day context produces workflows Microsoft does not, not merely because it records more things.

The strongest bear case is that the impressive artifact is outrunning product learning. Hiring a Head of Virality and a Forward Deployed Engineer could become premature delegation before the founder has isolated the small group that cannot live without the product. The June 2026 move from MIT to a commercial source-available license may also weaken the developer adoption that created the 20.9K-star distribution advantage. And the dossier shows no evidence-driven change of route: Beaumont may be persistent, but I cannot yet tell whether he is a running back or merely very fast in one direction. If privacy friction or weak retention falsifies the broad continuous-memory product, will he narrow it quickly?

I would invest. The operational gate is satisfied by a founder who has already shipped through many concrete technical obstacles and by a measured product far beyond the usual pre-seed prototype. The unresolved question is not whether Beaumont can build. It is whether screenpipe can convert a broad, admired primitive into one urgent repeated job before incumbents absorb the obvious features. My flip condition is verifiable: I would pass if a new paid-team cohort shows less than 40% eight-week retention, because that would mean the repository’s popularity is not becoming a narrow well of durable use.
