# Moonshot -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. A useful agent remembers ordinary promises, notices emotional loops, and quietly closes unfinished tasks without requiring a separate device. That future is plausible. Moonshot has not yet mapped the road to it. The dossier gives me a 100-person beta, an App Store listing, and a reported compute reduction from $200 to $2 per user per day, but not the intervening technical or behavioral transitions—or proof that this product is the first leak from that future rather than another ambient-AI application arriving during the current wave.

The founders can make things move. OpenTrade reportedly reached 225,000 users in one month, Ojas built trading agents associated with $1.4 million in options activity, and Tario brings published AI research from Ai2 and the Allen School. That is real first-believer and distribution evidence. It is not yet pre-company obsession with memory, ambient audio, or privacy architecture. OpenTrade’s discontinuation over brokerage regulation also makes the September 2026 pivot important: this may be a resourceful team learning quickly, or a talented team searching for the next surface with momentum. I cannot distinguish those stories yet.

What is the broken rule? Moonshot says people lose commitments unless they manually capture them. That is friction, but the dossier does not quantify the cost, identify who collects a rent, or show whose occupational control is materially restored. Apple is the more concrete gatekeeper here: it controls microphone permission, background execution, recording indicators, and access to iOS 26 users. Moonshot is renting that system, not breaking it. At the reported $2-per-user-per-day inference cost, the product starts with roughly $60 in monthly compute expense before acquisition and support, while no price or paid tier exists.

The strongest bull case is hiding in the artifacts. There is a functioning listing, 100 beta places, 250,000 launch views from $500 of spend, and prior evidence that this team can attract users quickly. Amazon and Meta acquiring Bee and Limitless show that strategic buyers value ambient-memory products. I have made a privacy exception for **Flock Safety** when an accidental artifact exposed unusually specific technical utility; the material difference is that Moonshot has not yet shown the indispensable output or limiting architecture that earns such an exception. Deleting audio after transcription is good hygiene, but continuous speech still captures bystanders, relationships, and context before the product has demonstrated commensurate benefit.

The portfolio-pattern test cuts against the investment today. Moonshot does not control a lower-stack bottleneck: it depends on Apple, transcription infrastructure, and model providers, while Plaud already reports two million users and $100 million ARR. The proposed longitudinal data moat is inferred, with no retention, data-volume, or performance evidence. Nor is there costly or strange user behavior yet—beta capacity and launch views show attention, not that people repeatedly surrender battery, privacy, and intimate context because the agent has become part of their lives.

I pass. My 36-month base case is that Moonshot pivots from continuous consumer listening into a narrower professional meeting or follow-up workflow before reaching $1 million ARR. The team’s execution ability makes that outcome more likely than simple death, but it does not make this version defensible. One result would flip me: an independently verified cohort in which at least 40 of the first 100 users pay $99 per month and keep ambient capture enabled at least five days per week for eight consecutive weeks under an explicit third-party-consent protocol. That would show the weird, costly behavior—and the utility strong enough to justify it—that is currently missing.
