# isoquant -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an inference platform such as Baseten is Isoquant’s strongest route. Baseten offers custom model packaging, and Isoquant reports a median time to first token of 452 ms versus Baseten’s 1,320 ms on GLM‑5.3‑Flash. A buyer could acquire the tuning software to improve latency on workloads it already serves. That rationale depends on the advantage carrying over to the buyer’s hardware and production traffic. The benchmark covers one model and was run by Isoquant. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

A useful milestone would be to show lower GPU cost per successful request at matched quality and latency across three paying production workloads retained for 90 days. The company reports 1.5 trillion tokens served and a banking evaluation with 91.7% lower inference cost against its baseline. Those signals make the test plausible, while the paid workload mix, GPU budget, and runway remain unknown. A whole-company acquisition could carry the hosted service and tuning software. IP ownership and change-of-control terms would need verification, and buyer use of customer content for training would require written consent. The memo provides no evidence of an independent route to holder liquidity. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The benchmark is a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. For both illustrative sale cases, assume $5 million in combined net debt and deal costs, no senior preference claims, and an eligible holder selling the full stake for pro rata cash within eight years.

- **Modest sale.** A $50 million enterprise value leaves $45 million in distributable equity and $1.35 million in holder cash, or 2.7×.
- **Strong sale.** A $100 million enterprise value leaves $95 million in distributable equity and $2.85 million in holder cash, or 5.7×. If 15% of that payout arrives after year eight, cash within the horizon falls to about $2.42 million, or 4.85×.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested yields $0 in realized holder proceeds within eight years.

The memo’s prepaid token pricing and reported usage establish a charging model and usage volume. Paid usage, margins, and buyer pricing remain unverified, so the sale values serve as sensitivities. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

Together AI already offers agent-driven tuning, and NVIDIA Dynamo gives operators open-source scheduling and cache management. An acquirer could build enough of the capability internally, particularly if Isoquant’s gains vary across workloads. Customers can also switch providers with limited integration work, weakening the case for buying the customer base. This is a credible reason for buyers to decline or bid below the hurdle. The evidence leaves the Pass gate untriggered because it establishes neither a structural block across the strongest route nor a supported ceiling on sale proceeds. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would an inference platform’s build-versus-buy analysis support a price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? A buyer’s indicative range grounded in independently reproduced savings on retained production workloads would move the action to Reach out if the resulting holder payout clears the eight-year hurdle under plausible deal terms.
