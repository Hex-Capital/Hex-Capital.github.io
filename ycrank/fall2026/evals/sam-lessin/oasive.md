# Oasive -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Oasive has a credible buyer-founder and an undocumented investment proposition. Proposed post-money, competing term sheets, and whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital: all unknown. YC backing answers none of those questions. I am not assuming an auction, but I cannot underwrite a cheap, non-consensus entry from silence. The other primary gate is equally open: the dossier names no finite experiment, deadline, or pass/fail result that this round finances. I pass on the evidence available.

Anaïs Howland is the strongest argument against that decision. Wells Fargo MBS analysis and Google Treasury portfolio management put her inside the purchasing workflow; Google AI strategy and the shipped Captr_Windows tool provide evidence on the technology side. That is a credible bridge between institutional investing and software, precisely the kind of translation layer I want. Oasive also reports use across **15 institutional desks** at its September 24 launch. A founder who understands how analysts actually work and can already reach institutional users could build a valuable specialist business. These are substantive pre-seed signals.

The question is what Oasive owns after those institutions understand the product. The 15 desks concern MBS/rates, while municipal research still has an early-access form. Their reported use does not establish paid conversion, retention, or that proprietary access caused adoption. Source-linked borrower research is useful, but DPC DATA already supplies municipal financials, disclosures, and news, including AI-ready datasets released in March 2026. MuniPro already produces source-based credit memos. Oasive’s advertised proprietary MBS models could matter in that adjacent business; they do not establish a municipal advantage. The owned-workflow pattern remains unproven, and the risk of an institution or existing vendor reproducing the aggregation layer is specific enough to affect my decision.

The strongest bull case is that those first desks become the distribution and feedback loop: analysts correct the research, coverage improves, and institutional trust makes the workflow difficult to replace. But that chain is still a hypothesis. The dossier does not supply the measured conversion, retention, validated performance, or cash economics that would justify an exception to my financing gates. Nor does the accuracy disclaimer itself prove a defective product. What matters is whether source checking and human correction leave enough customer value and margin after licensed-data costs. The license-termination and export restrictions make control over the underlying inputs a real underwriting question.

At **$200 per user per month** for Macro & Rates, with custom paid pilots offered for Agency MBS, Oasive has a pricing architecture—not disclosed revenue or proof of self-financing economics. There is no margin, burn, runway, or funded break-even plan to evaluate, so I use the missing-evidence default rather than assume another round is compulsory. I would reopen this on one verifiable financing proposal: a sub-$10 million post-money round funding a six-month municipal experiment with a predefined result of five annual paying institutional desks at positive contribution margin. That would connect price, scarce capital, and a truth worth discovering. Today, Howland’s ability is more established than the deal I am being asked to buy.
