# Invertix -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Invertix becomes a large company if renewable capacity supported per operations engineer increases materially without reducing reliability. Agents could make that possible by connecting fragmented plant records to maintenance decisions and actions. The buyer is an energy operator whose portfolio is growing faster than its engineering team. That is a coherent market hypothesis. The dossier does not yet establish the controlling variable: how much operating capacity Invertix adds per engineer after accounting for its own implementation labor.

The strongest argument for investing is that customers appear to be buying before those economics are fully visible. Invertix reported “multiple six figures” of ARR in June 2026, and its investor reported more than 1.8 GW managed in May. Those are meaningful pre-seed signals, although neither establishes independently verified revenue or recurring usage. TotalEnergies confirms participation in its accelerator, providing a credible route to operational sites rather than proof of a production contract. I assign substantial credit to this demand evidence. The customers’ combined 50 GW portfolio is potential distribution, not deployed capacity.

The economic question sits inside the deployment model. Approximately 60 days from contract to deployment could be entirely reasonable for critical infrastructure. What matters is whether the second and tenth deployments reuse the first deployment’s engineering work. Embedded engineers mapping equipment, procedures, and responsibilities could build a repeatable software product—or a valuable integration business whose revenue requires proportional hiring. The dossier supplies no measured change in engineering hours, maintenance costs, generation losses, or delivery margins. The IEA’s forecast of 945 TWh of data-centre electricity consumption by 2030 increases the relevance of energy infrastructure; it does not establish an economic discontinuity for Invertix.

There is more architectural substance here than document summarization. Equipment relationships, operational records, and customer-defined action permissions give the product context around a recurring workflow. Better models could improve investigations while those integrations retain value. But the ontology description comes partly from a YC launch page carrying a future-dated December 17, 2026 heading, so I treat that evidence provisionally. Customer-specific state also does not automatically become a transferable data advantage under the disclosed processing restrictions. Delfos already combines operational data, domain rules, and language models. Invertix must demonstrate that its implementation compounds faster than a funded competitor can reproduce it.

This fits my pattern of backing software around a physical-industry transition, provided the software changes operating economics. Invertix is not manufacturing equipment, so commodity hardware margins are not the objection. The relevant red flags are implementation labor disguised as software scalability and a claimed data advantage without demonstrated reproduction costs; neither is proven, but neither is resolved. The strongest bull case remains the reported ARR alongside access to substantial operating portfolios. However, the known exceptions for frontier research or scarce technical talent do not justify bypassing the economic question here. Durmaz’s applied-AI background is relevant capability, while Perrotta’s Cubbit experience and departure from ESMT do not document prior startup learning or costly commitment sufficient for a founder override.

I would pass with my own money today. Invertix has stronger evidence of customer demand than of the mechanism that turns that demand into a large, scalable business. One verifiable condition would change my decision: a six-month cohort of three unrelated paying operators expanding deployment while Invertix maintains at least 70% gross margin, including embedded engineering and support, without increasing implementation hours per added GW or degrading operational reliability. That would directly test whether the workflow is becoming software infrastructure.
