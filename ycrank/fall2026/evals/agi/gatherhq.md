# GatherHQ -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path B, new economies, is the strongest fit. GatherHQ could make it economical for enterprise marketing teams to test a message before each campaign. Its workflow reuses existing buyer evidence, simulates likely reactions, and recruits and interviews real buyers when evidence is thin. A Gather survey found that half of 56 marketing and insights professionals could not fast-track research when needed. That suggests a use for faster research. The memo supplies no like-for-like comparison of total delivery labor or quality for tests that teams previously skipped. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The clearest observed customer use is a co-published Cover Genius study that surveyed 1,392 consumers across seven countries. Gather reports 65+ enterprise marketing teams, and a founder reported customer upsells. Those claims leave open whether customers repeatedly pay for smaller tests that were previously uneconomic. The founders’ shared enterprise research, engineering, and sales experience, along with the shipped Slack and API offerings, support seed-stage execution. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team, The Idea)

At model parity, the potential reason to pay Gather is access to fresh, relevant buyers and research that improves as a customer’s evidence accumulates. Gather claims a corpus of 300,000+ real interviews. Its size alone leaves open whether Gather has exclusive access, whether added interviews improve predictions or buyer reach, and whether customers would stay under parity. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

Assume one campaign test uses one simulation and three completed B2B interviews at list prices. That yields $310 before participant incentives, recruiting, inference, human review, retries, and fulfillment. Expansion depends on how many such tests customers actually run and the contribution on each additional test. Posted prices provide a starting point. The memo provides neither realized contribution nor repeat frequency for that use. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Gather may be speeding up research that enterprise teams already buy. If sparse or stale evidence routinely requires fresh recruiting and human review, small tests could remain uneconomic at equivalent quality. Listen Labs and Outset already offer AI-moderated interviews and automated analysis, so buyers with access to comparable models and recruiting could reproduce the parts they value. This evidence leaves both Path B gates unresolved. It does not establish end-to-end substitution or show that delivery savings disappear. The memo also identifies no separately controlled bottleneck to support Path A. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will enterprise marketing teams repeatedly pay Gather for campaign tests they previously skipped when a competing workflow has access to an equally capable model? Run a paid trial with several such teams. Compare completed tests against conventional research at equivalent quality, counting human time and costs for recruiting, review, retries, and exceptions. Then give buyers a real purchasing choice against a comparable-model workflow that can also recruit buyers. Material labor savings, positive contribution, and repeat purchases tied to Gather’s buyer access or accumulated evidence would support Reach out if the updated assessment clears the score and confidence gates. If either necessary condition fails in a representative trial, move to Pass.
