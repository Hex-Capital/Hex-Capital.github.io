# Last Accounting Company -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

**SpaceX** is the high rung because rockets turn capital intensity and technical difficulty into the moat; Last Accounting Company’s signature is the softer version of that pattern—an unsexy, regulated accounting swing with its strongest signal in liability-heavy finance and its weakest in founder authority. Last Accounting Company replaces the ledger and accounting firm while certified accountants approve Björn’s work, so regulation can compound into trust. The analogy stops before the launchpad: Last Accounting Company is software-only, has no accuracy data, and enters beside Balance, Pilot, and Truewind rather than crossing a frontier others cannot physically reach.

**HQ Trivia** exposed what happens when founder authority remains ambiguous: product magic can postpone a governance fracture, but it cannot erase one. Last Accounting Company names Riki Karjalainen, Sami Laakkonen, Veikko Pere, and Pietari Virkkunen as four co-founders who have known one another since middle school, yet names no CEO, tiebreaker, equity structure, or conflict protocol. Friendship is love, not governance. Last Accounting Company is even more exposed than a 50/50 pairing because four voices can produce consensus theater while nobody owns the irreversible decision.

**Riki Karjalainen and Sami Laakkonen** built petabyte-scale financial-data infrastructure at Token Terminal, while Veikko Pere and Pietari Virkkunen encountered finance workflows through BCG, private equity, and venture capital. That gives Last Accounting Company adjacency to the problem, not yet the autobiographical scar tissue of someone who personally closed books, defended an audit, or carried accounting-error liability. Over 18 months, Last Accounting Company can turn daily corrections and transaction histories into real lived knowledge; until then, the founders look like capable visitors carrying excellent maps rather than people who grew up on the well pad.

**Truewind’s $17 million-plus financing** pins Last Accounting Company inside a visible AI-accounting wave, while Pilot, Bench, and Balance make “agents plus human review” legible rather than magically weird. For Last Accounting Company to move into my non-consensus tier, credible investors would need to reject the proprietary-ledger replacement as too operationally ugly or regulatorily dangerous while named customers kept choosing it anyway. Last Accounting Company currently offers no named customer, revenue figure, investor rejection, or round-pricing evidence, so “agent-native accounting firm” reads more YC zeitgeist than Crusoe-shaped misread.

**Niantic** was physically verifiable because I could play Ingress and feel the world change before anyone explained the metric. Last Accounting Company has begun onboarding first customers roughly ten weeks after inception, but Björn cannot be installed, watched, or independently tested; the observable artifact is still a website claim about bookkeeping, VAT, payroll, and filings. Last Accounting Company could become tactile through completed closes and regulator-accepted filings, but no accuracy rate, integration result, or customer-visible close has been published.

**Balance** already combines AI bookkeeping with human accountant review, so by July 2029 I expect Last Accounting Company to have narrowed into a Nordic managed-accounting firm at roughly $2 million–$5 million ARR, with Björn and the proprietary ledger operating mainly as internal efficiency machinery rather than replacing accounting software outright. Last Accounting Company’s regulated-service strength can win customers, but four-founder ambiguity and migration complexity will favor a pragmatic service business over the clean category conquest promised today.

**Riki and Sami’s** petabyte-scale engineering plus Veikko and Pietari’s finance experience form the strongest bull case: Biz and Tiz are present, first customers arrived in about ten weeks, and the **DeepMind** exception says cross-domain polymaths can override my outsider-origin bias. The case stings because Last Accounting Company may be hiding infrastructure inside an accounting-service wrapper—I'm never playing the game I seem to be playing. Last Accounting Company still lacks the Rizz evidence, a named CEO, and any proof that its proprietary ledger survives a real close better than Balance or Pilot. A published cohort of 20 paying customers completing three consecutive month-end closes without accountant corrections would flip my verdict; tactile product truth can earn an **HQ Trivia** exception. Until then, I keep my own money in my pocket.
