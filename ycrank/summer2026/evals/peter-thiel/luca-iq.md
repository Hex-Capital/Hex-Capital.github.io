# Luca IQ -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

IRS-authorized e-filing is Luca IQ’s strongest dimension; a tax engine still described as “coming soon” exposes Luca IQ’s weakest. The signature is a regulated-workflow wedge with a definite-plan deficit: real institutional friction, but no disclosed two-to-five-year sequence for converting that friction into monopoly. Regulation can shelter a company. It cannot supply the plan.

**Anduril** is the high rung because regulated-customer trust, integration depth, and atoms-heavy deployment reinforce one another. Luca IQ shares part of that mechanism: IRS authorization, deterministic computation, document-level traceability, and mandatory human review can make reliability compound across tax seasons. Luca IQ diverges where the analogy matters most—Anduril had hardware capex and a named prime-contractor cost pathology, while Luca IQ remains software that Thomson Reuters, April, or another tax vendor can reproduce or acquire.

**Quora** is the low rung on definite planning because an indefinite consumer roadmap was excused only by Adam D’Angelo’s Facebook CTO pedigree and a plausible knowledge-network effect. Luca IQ likewise offers no named multi-year milestones beyond limited seasonal pilots and a forthcoming 1040 engine. Luca IQ has completed one customer tax season, but “coming soon” is a product status, not a definite future; neither founder has the shipped-to-scale exception that rescued Quora.

SafeSend’s dependence on external tax engines reveals the potential secret: Luca IQ believes intake, workpapers, deterministic calculation, and filing should collapse into one API-first system for CPA firms. Over the next 18 months, Luca IQ’s secret either sharpens into an architectural indictment—incumbents cannot preserve document-to-return provenance across fragmented products—or dissolves into ordinary workflow consolidation. Thomson Reuters already paid $600 million for SafeSend, so the dossier shows incumbent appetite but not incumbent incapacity. Competition is for losers; acquisition activity is not proof of a secret.

Thomson Reuters’ $600 million SafeSend acquisition makes Luca IQ’s monopoly problem concrete. Luca IQ has no disclosed proprietary dataset, retention rate, or pricing-power mechanism, while April, Canopy, and established tax engines occupy adjacent layers. The score would flip into the investment tier if Luca IQ demonstrated that reviewer corrections improve a proprietary engine across firms, that customers cannot export those learned mappings, and that renewal pricing rises without increased churn. Until then, fixed per-return pricing describes monetization, not monopoly.

**Affirm** is the relevant founder-lineage parallel because Max Levchin had already shipped PayPal to scale before asking investors to underwrite another regulated financial system. Luca IQ has authentic but narrower founder-market fit: Angelo Policicchio is a Michigan CPA with EY experience, and Akash Sadashivapeth formerly led data science at Welligence. Luca IQ therefore clears the domain-competence test, but Harvard MBAs and 15 public GitHub repositories do not constitute the operator-pedigree override that justified Affirm—or Asana.

Hundreds of tax-engine pilot applications lead to a falsifiable forecast: by July 2029, Luca IQ will reach roughly $2 million to $5 million in ARR and be acquired by Wolters Kluwer as a workflow-and-API tuck-in, without establishing an independent tax-engine monopoly. Luca IQ’s regulatory integration can produce a useful asset and credible customers, while Luca IQ’s absent definite roadmap and exposed software boundary make strategic absorption more probable than ten years of pricing power.

The reported 1,100 hours saved, 57% reduction in administrative preparation time, and 30-minute intake-to-review cycle form the strongest bull case against that forecast. Luca IQ could be the **Asana** exception: a software-only product whose workflow depth becomes the moat before outsiders recognize it, with hundreds of pilot applicants supplying distribution. Luca IQ has not earned that exception because Asana possessed Facebook-scale operator lineage, while Luca IQ’s engine remains in pilot and every return still requires line-by-line human review. I would flip to Invest if Luca IQ processes at least 100,000 production returns through its own engine, retains more than 90% of firms through a second tax season, and documents error rates materially below incumbent workflows.
