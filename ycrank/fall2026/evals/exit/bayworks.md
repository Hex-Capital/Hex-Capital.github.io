# Bayworks -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Bayworks’ strongest path. Snap-on is a plausible payer because it acquired Dealer-FX, a service-lane scheduling and shop-management platform, for $200 million net of acquired cash. Bayworks could extend that platform into warranty documentation by combining technician dictation, repair evidence, and manufacturer-formatted writeback in the repair order. Owning that workflow could let Snap-on integrate and distribute it across its dealer base. The memo provides limited evidence of what that benefit would be worth or why ownership would beat licensing. The Dealer-FX purchase establishes precedent for paying for dealership workflow software, but its broader scope gives it limited weight as a Bayworks price benchmark. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Bayworks reports nearly seven figures in contracted annual recurring revenue within four months, six dealership groups, and 80-plus locations. The Harvey Auto Group testimonial corroborates use at one group. Live paid revenue, renewals, and measured warranty-processing gains remain unverified. A milestone that would strengthen the acquisition case is repeatable reductions in technician documentation time or warranty rework across multiple renewing, paying dealer groups. The reported rollout makes that test plausible, while the disclosed funding signal leaves runway and the capital needed to reach it uncertain. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Proceeds under the shared scenario.** With actual investor terms undisclosed, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash target within eight years. Assuming $5 million of illustrative net debt and pro rata distribution before investor taxes, a $25 million enterprise-value sale yields $600,000 (1.2×) and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). The 3% holder needs about $83.3 million of distributable equity, or $88.3 million of enterprise value with that debt assumption. At 2% ownership, the same $100 million sale yields $1.9 million (3.8×). These scenarios establish the hurdle. The memo’s acquisition precedent supplies only thin support for a Bayworks price that clears it. Company-specific debt, fees, and senior claims are undisclosed. (Market & Traction)

If growth plateaus, buyer interest fails to materialize, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 in eight years and retains the shares. The memo supplies no evidence of cash generation to fund a sponsor buyout or distributions, demand for an eligible secondary, or a path to public resale. Wind-down proceeds are unquantifiable because obligations and preferences are undisclosed. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Wrench Voice already offers technician dictation and dealership-system writeback across multiple integrations. A buyer could license an overlapping product or build the feature into its existing platform. Bayworks distributes its own app and a PBS extension, which gives an acquirer a plausible software asset, but the memo leaves dealer-contract assignment and continued integration access open. Bayworks’ stated policy also gives dealerships ownership and deletion rights over their data, limiting the value a buyer can assume from cross-dealer learning. The evidence shows a credible substitute and unresolved transfer risks. It establishes neither a structural block nor materially inadequate proceeds at a supported sale price, so the Pass gate does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would owning Bayworks’ warranty workflow create enough incremental value over building or licensing it to support a sale that clears the holder’s cash target? Measured time or rework savings at renewing, paying dealerships, translated into a buyer-specific valuation range using transferable deployments, would answer it. A supported range that clears the hurdle under credible ownership and transaction terms would move the action to Reach out.
