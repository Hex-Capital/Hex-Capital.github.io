# Taan.ai -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Adithya and Devansh have done something more informative than choosing a fashionable market: they have built inside a problem they understand. Adithya’s music performance and prior music-generation venture provide context; the June 2026 beta and their co-authored TimingBench provide artifacts. That is specific knowledge expressed through action. I give the founders substantial credit here. Aalap’s self-reported $1,000 MRR belongs to their operating history; it does not establish demand for Taan.ai.

The investment turns on whether precise musical control becomes an asset competitors cannot cheaply reproduce. Taan reports **96.1% instruction success**, against **28.9% for ElevenLabs v2**, on its August benchmark. A meaningful gap, if it survives independent testing. But the same [benchmark](https://taan.ai/research/control-benchmarks/) reports worse audio fidelity: Fréchet Audio Distance of **4.8**, against competitors’ **2.6–3.5**. Customers must prefer the complete result. The author-labelled evaluator and unfinished independent annotation leave that unresolved. Patent applications and licensed training music add little defensibility credit until ownership, scope, and durable rights are established.

The strongest case for investing is that timing is the difficult part of making generated music useful in production. If the founders have solved that problem at the model level, Taan could supply scoring infrastructure to many editing applications. Its [REST API and MCP integrations](https://taan.ai/docs/api/) make that route concrete. This fits my preference for tools that turn individual knowledge into scalable output. However, ElevenLabs already offers video-to-music, and Google demonstrates soundtracking workflows. The approximately **$1.03 billion** AI music market estimate establishes a category; it does not establish how much adjacent production spending Taan can capture. The product has room to expand, but the economic path remains thinly evidenced.

Distribution and value capture are less developed than the technical work. One creator’s soundtrack does not demonstrably improve another creator’s result, and public integration documentation establishes access rather than a customer-created network. The **$15 Creator** and **$45 Agency** subscriptions establish a direct pricing model, but no completed purchases, retention, or inference margins are disclosed. The founder-reported **$1 million raised** buys experimentation; it supplies no evidence of customer willingness to pay. These are uncertainties appropriate to this stage, rather than evidence of failure.

I would keep Taan.ai under consideration and hold my check. I can accept missing network effects for an unusually insightful prototype, but the prototype exception requires enough product distinction and capital efficiency to carry the investment; this dossier establishes neither decisively. There is no documented integrity issue or financing-pressure red flag. The constraint is narrower: substantial founder knowledge has produced a promising technical result, while durable ownership of that advantage remains unclear. **My flip condition is an independent, blinded production-workflow evaluation showing that professional editors consistently prefer Taan’s finished scores to current ElevenLabs and Google alternatives, with materially less revision time.** That would connect the founders’ timing insight to a customer outcome strong enough to reconsider the bet.
