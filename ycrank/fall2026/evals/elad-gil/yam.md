# Yam -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Yam’s three hours of reported daily play could be its strongest asset or its largest expense. Generative AI lets creators publish interactive romance games, and women already consuming dating sims and fanfiction are the intended buyers. The controlling variable is whether retained paying users generate enough revenue to cover the inference and content costs of their engagement. The dossier establishes pricing and activity, but does not connect them. I would not write a personal check today.

The engagement deserves attention. As of October 27, 2026, two public game counters show 1.8 million and 516,400 plays, while the company reports more than three hours per daily active user. These are meaningful pre-seed signals of people finding something compelling. They are not unique-user counts or cohort retention, and pricing at $9.99 and $19.99 per month does not establish paid conversion. I give Yam substantial credit for demonstrated consumption, with a discount for company-reported measurements whose period and denominator are unspecified.

The strongest bull case is that Yam turns this consumption into reusable distribution. A creator describes a scenario, edits characters and settings, generates artwork, and publishes a playable link. Each game can attract players who become creators or discover another game. That is a credible mechanism for distribution compounding, and the reported 88% growth in companion-app downloads in H1 2025 supports the category’s timing. But shareable links establish a distribution capability; they do not yet demonstrate that this loop compounds. Likewise, generated authoring makes a broader supply of games feasible, but the dossier does not quantify production savings or delivery margins.

Yam does own more of the product than a chat interface: creation, editing, artwork, and publication form a complete authoring sequence. Better models could improve that sequence and the resulting games. What remains unproven is whether Yam retains something difficult to reproduce as those models improve—creator relationships, persistent game state, discovery advantages, or proprietary feedback that measurably improves outcomes. Its largest disclosed titles also use existing fictional characters. Rights clearance could therefore affect the durability of the very supply attracting players. I would not award a data moat simply because interactions generate data.

The founders make the bull case more credible. Shan Zhong reports growing a previous consumer fintech to 100,000 users and 2 million ARR, with currency unspecified; Yu Qin reports helping build TikTok creation tools and an 800,000-person creator community. Those figures belong to prior businesses, but they suggest relevant operating learning. The team also describes personal participation in fanfiction and dating sims. I credit that experience without inferring desperation or costly commitment from employer names. The consumer-model exception in my framework does not resolve the remaining uncertainty: this dossier documents application and creator-tool experience, rather than scarce frontier-model research. Nor does it establish an economic discontinuity sufficient to override concerns about serving intensive usage.

My decision is to monitor Yam while passing on a check now. The engagement could precede a large business, and missing public revenue is not evidence of failed monetization. But market excitement cannot substitute for the economic mechanism that makes engagement valuable. **One verifiable flip condition:** three consecutive monthly paying cohorts show at least 70% payer retention at day 90 and positive cumulative contribution after inference, payment, moderation, and attributable acquisition costs. That would connect the strongest existing signal—deep consumption—to a business capable of financing its own growth.
