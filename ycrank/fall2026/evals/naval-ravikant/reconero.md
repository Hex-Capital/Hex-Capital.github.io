# Reconero -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Reconero’s strongest evidence is that its founders have reached police users. Edward Liu co-developed ReconeroRMS, and September 2026 reporting describes police-unit trials and iterative officer feedback. That is knowledge acquired through action. Riwan Azeroual’s prior business reportedly reached $150K ARR, demonstrating some ability to sell software, although that revenue belongs to a different company. Their age does not concern me. The unresolved question is how much operational knowledge those trials have produced beyond a working prototype.

What survives when a competitor copies Alyx’s features? Reconero searches records, extracts information from body-camera footage, and prefills reports. Those are useful capabilities. Mark43 already combines records and dispatch; Axon already generates reports from body-camera audio. The dossier establishes no proprietary model, exclusive data rights, or deployed workflow dependency. Departmental configuration could eventually become an asset, but a possible switching cost earns no credit today. I assign the missing-evidence default here rather than assume the architecture is merely a commodity wrapper.

There is room to maneuver within the market. An officer’s reporting assistant could expand into records management and dispatch using the same departmental data connections. Reconero explicitly offers migration from legacy RMS, CAD exports, and scanned documents, giving that expansion a concrete product connection. Existing suppliers establish that these are purchasing categories. The cited $20.4B global market is much broader than Reconero’s opportunity, however, and supplies little evidence about the budget this particular wedge can capture.

The strongest objection to passing is that these founders are already doing the difficult part: getting officers to try software in an institutional market. A useful reporting tool could earn trust, accumulate workflow knowledge, and become the department’s operating system. That possibility deserves attention. My founder-led exceptions allow investment before revenue or a distribution loop exists, but they require unusually convincing product or founder evidence. Unnamed trials without repeat-use results establish progress; they do not yet establish that exception. The dossier also provides no evidence of an integrity problem, artificial financing pressure, or dependence on investor reassurance. This is a structural concern, not a behavioral veto.

Reconero currently lacks the compounding mechanism I seek. The dossier shows no user contribution or referral loop through which one department improves the product for another. Its departmental purchasing process also offers little support for my preference for permissionless participation or tools that let individuals independently scale their output. Agency subscriptions are inferred; there is no disclosed payer, price, or current revenue. Missing monetization is acceptable at pre-seed, but it cannot substitute for an unproven durable advantage.

I would pass with my own money. Reconero has more evidence of capable builders than of a business whose advantage compounds. My single condition for reopening is a documented paid agency renewal showing that an accumulated, deployment-specific workflow asset drove retention against an available incumbent alternative. That would turn the proposed switching-cost mechanism into observable behavior.
