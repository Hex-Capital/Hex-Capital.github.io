# UFO.ai -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

UFO.ai could make recurring knowledge work cheap enough to delegate continuously. That is a consequential possibility. But which work, how much spending, and what fraction can disappear? The dossier supplies reporting, follow-ups and software maintenance as examples, without a labor baseline or measured replacement rate. Glean’s $300M ARR establishes that companies buy enterprise context software. It does not establish that UFO can eliminate an order of magnitude of work. I can lose my entire check buying this option; I need a more concrete account of what happens if it succeeds.

The technical work deserves attention. UFO has published execution, durable identity, authorization and accounting layers, alongside experiments in continuing agent context without summaries. This is inspectable engineering, not merely a proposed interface. Yet self-hosting is a deployment advantage, not a demonstrated capability discontinuity. There is no comparative result against Glean, Dust or an existing human workflow showing that these mechanisms produce substantially more correct work. The familiar AI red flag—differentiation collapsing into integration and convenience—remains an unresolved risk, rather than an established description of this product.

The strongest evidence concerns what happens when the system fails. UFO documented an agent reusing an archived repository despite a stored correction, then implemented exact-string correction propagation. That removes one specific failure mechanism. Broader semantic changes remain unresolved. This matters because persistent context can preserve mistakes as effectively as knowledge; unattended execution compounds the consequence. The documented repair earns some credit for risk removal, but the dossier provides no broader pass/fail threshold, experiment budget or stop condition. Its local carrier’s host-read access and lack of kernel-enforced network egress restrictions add a separate deployment concern; recommending Docker or E2B is useful guidance, not evidence that the resulting system has passed an adversarial test.

The objection to passing is substantial: Alex Graveley created Copilot, directed Comet and founded Hackpad, which Dropbox acquired. Marshall Kiely brings a computational-biology PhD and experience at Notable Health, Mixpanel and Perplexity. These founders have unusually relevant experience building tools through which people delegate work. My actual investing pattern includes execution-heavy software and small open-source teams; scientific novelty is not compulsory. But 99 stars and 13 forks do not yet establish a committed technical community. Nor do impressive biographies establish fast belief revision or the recruitment of complementary owners for specific risks. I would not manufacture those signals from credentials. The known exceptions to my technical and economic gates require an exceptional instigator addressing a demonstrably enormous value pool, or extraordinary evidence of consequential progress. UFO’s record makes that exception worth considering; the supplied evidence does not complete it.

The economic question is cost per correctly completed task. Prepaid consumption, unlimited members and customer-supplied model keys describe billing choices. They reveal neither task economics nor the cost of retries, supervision and recovery. Missing revenue at this stage is ordinary. The investment question remains whether autonomous work costs less after including the human who checks it. My one verifiable flip condition is an independently auditable trial across three customer workspaces showing at least a 10x reduction in fully loaded cost per correctly completed recurring workflow against the existing process, including inference, retries and human intervention, with no material authorization failures during the trial.

I pass on the evidence today. UFO.ai has built enough to investigate an important reliability problem, but has not demonstrated the economic consequence that would make that technical risk attractive to me. More integrations would expand its surface area. Evidence that persistent agents reliably remove expensive work would change the investment.
