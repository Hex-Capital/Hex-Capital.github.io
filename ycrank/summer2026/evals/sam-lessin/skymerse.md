# Skymerse -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The proposed post-money valuation is blank, the competing-term-sheet count is blank, and I cannot tell whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. YC pre-seed status does not answer those questions. Price is evidence: without it, I do not know whether I am buying a discarded belief cheaply or joining an aviation-AI auction after the thesis has become obvious.

The next question is what exact card the capital flips. Skymerse names a large ambition—automating flight operations—but no use-of-funds plan, dated milestone, or pass/fail experiment. Converting three hours of planning into an operational recommendation could be a legitimate venture moment, but the dossier never says whether this round must prove regulatory acceptance, paid airline conversion, or safe automation at a specified error rate. A product roadmap is not yet a capital-allocation thesis.

The strongest asset is already operating. Notamify has processed 3 million NOTAMs across 487 days and extracted 2,005,126 affected-element exposures into a structured schema. LOT Polish Airlines uses it network-wide, with roughly 30-second processing and 15–20 filtered alerts per day, while the broader product reports 3,800 registered users and thousands of daily users. That is considerably better than an AI demo: the workflow is embedded enough to cause repeated operational action. But Skymerse does not disclose whether LOT is paying, how many Pro subscribers exist, or whether usage is retained because the schema is uniquely valuable rather than merely convenient.

The bull case stings. Damian Szumski is a licensed dispatcher who has worked in airline navigation, dispatch, airline-software implementation, and Flightradar24’s API product. He has both the operating vocabulary and the software-side access needed to translate fragmented aviation systems. LOT’s deployment and nearly 500 days of processing are precisely the kind of external receipts that can override missing financing evidence. They stop short of the override today because purchase conversion, retention, and customer economics remain undisclosed.

The bear case is that the apparent dark pool is built on public aeronautical inputs while better-capitalized systems own customer distribution. Jeppesen ForeFlight’s Airflow combines aviation data, segregated operator data, certification knowledge, and contextual reasoning; Overwatch AI already spans weather, NOTAMs, fuel, MELs, manuals, and flight plans. Skymerse remains a non-authoritative source, depends on synchronizing numerous safety-critical inputs, and has moved from an ATC transcription assistant to Notamify and then end-to-end operations. If the strategy is “integrate everything and let improving models remove the errors,” that is a commoditizing input plus several simultaneous goals—not a defensible business.

Notamify publishes a $24.90 monthly price, but Skymerse provides no revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or controllable cash metric. Delete the next financing and I cannot show that this business survives, much less compounds from profit. My 36-month base case is that Jeppesen ForeFlight acquires Skymerse for its structured NOTAM data and founder expertise before Skymerse reaches $5 million ARR. I would flip to Invest if a sub-$10 million post-money first check funds one dated experiment that converts three named airlines from pilot to paid deployment with positive contribution margin and at least 80% twelve-month retention.
