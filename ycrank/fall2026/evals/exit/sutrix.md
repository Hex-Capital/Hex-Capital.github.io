# Sutrix -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a clinical data software vendor is Sutrix’s strongest supported exit path. An EHR abstraction or registry vendor could use Sutrix to extend its product into study-ready datasets that combine multiple sources and apply investigator-specific rules. Emory’s Winship Cancer Institute reported faster preparation of interpretable results, and BlueRock Therapeutics reported less manual reconciliation with steps visible for expert review. Sutrix separately claims an 80% reduction in preparation time. These reports support workflow value to customers. A buyer’s willingness to pay for that capability remains an inference. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The next milestone is repeat production use and paid software renewals across institutions, with implementation effort measured. Sutrix reports paying teams at 10+ institutions and over $150,000 in revenue in the months before the software release. That traction makes the milestone plausible, while the recurring software contribution, deployment costs, and financing available to reach it remain unclear. A software acquisition is plausible given the product release and institution-cloud deployment option. Production EHR access and the transfer or reapproval of customer agreements and deployment permissions remain unverified. The memo leaves independent liquidity routes unevidenced. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years for a 5× return, which requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale scenarios assume closing within eight years, an eligible holder, a full cash sale, and a pro rata distribution after debt and deal adjustments, with no senior claims diverting proceeds. The prices are illustrative assumptions.

| Scenario | Gross cash to the holder |
|----------|--------------------------|
| Modest sale at $40 million enterprise value | With zero debt and deal adjustments, $40 million in distributable equity yields $1.2 million, or 2.4×. |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | After an assumed $10 million in debt and deal adjustments, $90 million in distributable equity yields $2.7 million, or 5.4×. If the holder can sell only 80% of its stake at that price, cash received at closing falls to $2.16 million, or 4.32×. |
| Prolonged private operation | Reinvested cash with no funded payout or eligible share purchase yields $0 realized within eight years. |

The memo supplies no buyer pricing, comparable sale, or payout capacity to anchor the proceeds range, so the proceeds dimension stays at its default. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Sutrix could remain a useful study-preparation service whose revenue depends on custom delivery. Pricing and scope vary by engagement, the reported revenue predates the software release, and production EHR access may depend on approved endpoints or file exports. Dyania and Layer already automate parts of the workflow, which could reduce what a buyer would pay for Sutrix’s incremental capabilities. This does not trigger the Pass gate. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible price ceiling that leaves the holder materially short. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can repeat production renewals and measured deployment costs establish enough economic value to a strategic buyer to support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? Renewed software contracts across institutions, documented implementation effort, and credible buyer feedback tying those economics to a price range that clears the hurdle would move the action to Reach out.
