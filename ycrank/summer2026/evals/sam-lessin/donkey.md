# Donkey -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot underwrite the check before I know its price. Donkey discloses neither post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, nor whether YC is the first scarce institutional capital. That is not evidence of an auction, but it is financing silence, so the deal receives the low prior. The second blank is equally important: no use-of-funds plan identifies the finite experiment this round buys, its deadline, or the result that would dramatically change company value. “Hire and grow” is not stated, but neither is a venture moment.

The company itself is more compelling than the round. Donkey is a genuine translation layer between Chinese factories and independent US importers: it identifies manufacturers from customs records and product markings, then takes title, inspects goods, clears customs, and delivers to the buyer. Benjamin Martindale speaks Mandarin, sold Prelude OS to 31 Chinese factories in 39 days, advised Sany and Sinoboom on overseas expansion, and is now visiting US distributors in person. James Tan is building the entity-resolution and customs-parsing machinery. This is unusually strong evidence that the team has shipped on one side and obtained direct operating access to the other.

The dark pool is not yet proven. Inspectors across seven Chinese provinces and accumulated supplier-pricing and reliability records could become proprietary, but the dossier does not establish the dataset’s depth or exclusivity. Donkey quoted $110,000 in its first sales week and found the same factory at a price 15% below one buyer’s incumbent supplier; those facts show that its workflow can produce economically relevant answers. But “first POs are signing” is still pipeline, not a completed purchase, repeat order, or retained workflow. Sourcify, Flexport, Inspectorio, and Zetwerk cannot copy title-taking merely by shipping a feature, but capital and operational competence—not software alone—are the present barrier.

Deleting the next financing exposes the hard part. Donkey embeds margin in a delivered price, but it supplies no gross margin, burn, runway, loss rate, or working-capital cycle. It pays factories, owns the goods through delivery, and may wait another 45 days for qualified buyers to pay. Credit insurance limits one risk; it does not make inventory, inspection failures, freight volatility, or stale tariff calculations disappear. Prelude was cash-flow positive, which proves Martindale can run a lean software business, but Donkey is a materially different balance-sheet machine.

The strongest bull case is real: this is not a generic AI wrapper, and the founders have already converted Chinese-factory access into paying customers once. A 15% landed-cost advantage could turn $110,000 of early quotes into repeat purchasing, and each settled order would improve the supplier dataset while creating the proprietary operating history needed for credit. That is the plausible known-exception route around missing seed evidence. It has not triggered yet because there is no completed Donkey order, measured contribution margin, or repeat buyer comparable to the hard conversion evidence required to override an unattractive or undocumented deal.

I am passing today. My 36-month base case is that Donkey reaches $10–30 million in annual merchandise volume and finances growth with trade credit or debt, becoming a useful trading business without producing a venture-scale Series A outcome. I would flip if the current round were offered below a $10 million post-money valuation and explicitly funded one dated test: delivering $1 million of orders from at least five repeat buyers at positive contribution margin by August 2027. That would price the doubt, name the card being flipped, and prove that the physical complexity compounds into economics rather than merely consuming capital.
