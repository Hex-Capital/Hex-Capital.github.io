# Osmaura -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move six years forward and the compelling version of Osmaura is not “AI growth for law firms.” It is a market where legal demand becomes machine-readable before a company begins shopping for counsel: regulatory activity, corporate events, relationships, and prior matters continuously resolve into the next useful conversation. The dossier identifies the present-day leak—customers reportedly log in daily—but does not map the transitions from suggested outreach to trusted matter origination, explain what must improve, or show that firms have changed how they win business. That leaves the future visible only in outline.

The working artifact is Osmaura’s strongest evidence. Daily logins imply repeated behavior, which matters more than 541 company followers or 1,144 reactions to a YC announcement. But I cannot tell whether two friendly design partners check a dashboard or fifty partners rearrange their business-development routines around it. There is no customer count, dated cohort, paid contract, or matter attributed to an Osmaura signal. This is product activity, not yet costly or weird user behavior.

The system wound is real but incompletely decoded. Partners reportedly lose at least ten hours each week pursuing clients through existing networks without knowing who is approaching a legal need. Osmaura proposes to return attention and timing to those partners. Yet the dossier does not identify who captures an economic rent, which institutional rule creates the waste, or why a law firm cannot repair it by extending Nexl or Intapp. “Lawyers prospect inefficiently” is a useful observation; it is not yet the hidden machinery of the market.

Kali Abeje and Jity Woldemichael have enough technical ability to build: MIT computer science, NLP and policy-prediction research, Meta, Jane Street, AWS, and computational astrophysics. Leaving school together and spending months interviewing law-firm partners are meaningful first-believer acts. What is missing is pre-company operating tuition inside law-firm growth, a dated trail of independently built artifacts, or evidence that either founder lived this wound before selecting the category. Their backgrounds prove intelligence and commitment, but not yet the peculiar obsession or commercial command required to navigate long legal-enterprise sales.

The sharpest bull case is that I am looking too early. Walking away after two months of negotiation with a major law firm may demonstrate qualification discipline rather than sales weakness, and daily usage may already be generating the feedback loop the public record cannot show. Firm-specific context, signal outcomes, and relationship data could compound into a controlled asset. But today that is a hypothesis. Nexl already serves more than 150 firms, Intapp owns adjacent workflow surfaces, and no proprietary dataset, security architecture, or outcome history separates Osmaura from a feature those platforms can add. The product currently sits on top of the AI wave rather than beneath it.

I pass for now. The privacy red flag is unresolved because internal matters and partner networks require explicit custody and access boundaries, while no public security or deployment documentation exists. The two-friend founding structure also leaves ultimate decision authority undisclosed. One condition flips me: three named law firms showing that at least twenty partners use Osmaura weekly and have opened attributable matters from its signals, with documented controls preventing confidential firm data from leaking across customers. That would turn daily logins into a strange new operating habit and the inferred feedback moat into evidence.
