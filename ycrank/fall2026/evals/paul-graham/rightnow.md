# RightNow -- Paul Graham Evaluation

RightNow has built something harder than a pitch. Autokernel contains an implementation and a correctness harness. That matters. But the question I most want answered is still missing: what broke for a particular user, and what did Jaber and Osama do about it? Shipping GPU infrastructure establishes technical ability. It does not establish how the founders behave when their first solution fails. The dossier contains no named obstacle followed by a dated workaround, so I cannot give the team resourcefulness credit merely because the software was difficult to build.

The strongest case for investing is that these founders may already be standing inside the problem. Both coauthored inference and model research, including RightNow-Arabic. RunInfra targets a specific waste: coding agents repeatedly sending conversation context. Routing sessions to replicas that retain that context could turn technical understanding into lower customer bills. This fits a pattern I like: technical founders building programmable infrastructure for other technical people. The public implementation makes that case substantially stronger than an architecture diagram would. But the [September methodology](https://runinfra.ai/methodology) says the engine-comparison harness and raw data are unavailable, and historical benchmarks lack runtime details. I give substantial credit for the artifact, with a discount on the claimed performance advantage.

The missing link is a developer who keeps coming back. RunInfra received 155 Product Hunt points on July 1 and 115 on October 20. Autokernel has roughly 1,600 stars. These show attention, but neither tells me whether a coding team trusts the service with its daily work. OpenFang’s approximately 18,200 stars belong to another product. The $5 prepaid entry point and $10/$29/$99 subscriptions make trying RunInfra inexpensive; they do not demonstrate purchases. More importantly, the displayed cache rates come from the highest qualifying account. The business depends on whether ordinary recurring workloads receive enough cache hits to make switching worthwhile. One retained customer’s actual bill would answer more than another launch ranking.

There is evidence of movement, but not yet of learning. The earlier editor won its Product Hunt awards in April 2025; RunInfra launched in July 2026. That sequence could contain an excellent story about discovering the infrastructure problem while building the editor. The dossier does not establish that connection, the observation that changed the plan, or the speed of the response. I therefore leave both adaptability and problem origin at their missing-evidence defaults. The founders’ research supplies relevant technical context, but it does not document their personal collision with this problem or a neglected operational burden they have undertaken.

I would pass today. Together AI’s existing kernel work makes the distinction between a useful optimization and a durable business consequential, though competition alone would not stop me. Nor do I infer that the founders depend on launch publicity, delegate customer learning, or lack a working relationship simply because those details are absent. A working prototype can justify overlooking uncertain cofounder history; RightNow has meaningful evidence in that direction. But there is no independently validated performance result or unmistakable user pull strong enough to override the missing founder-behavior evidence. My one flip condition is a verifiable customer episode: a coding team encounters a named serving or caching failure, the founders ship a fix within a week, and that team subsequently renews paid usage. That would connect the code to both resourcefulness and need.
