# Verdun -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to a physical-security platform is Verdun’s strongest exit path. The memo records Motorola’s acquisition of Blue Eye and its plan to integrate live intervention into its incident-management workflow. A platform with a dealer channel could similarly buy Verdun to add staffed monitoring to existing camera installations and reduce operator workload. Verdun markets white-label monitoring and claims its AI closed about 85% of alerts in a 14-day sample. The memo includes an anonymized screenshot of alarm activity across customer sites, while paid adoption, operator savings, and threat recall remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The seed-stage milestone is a paid rollout across multiple sites that reproduces operator savings while maintaining threat recall. Verdun’s parallel trial offer gives it a way to test both. The memo confirms YC backing, while the available trial budget and runway remain unclear. A company sale appears feasible in principle, with assignment and continued access to camera feeds and site data dependent on customer terms. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity. Both sale scenarios assume an eligible holder sells the full stake for cash within eight years, with a pro rata waterfall and no senior claims.

- **Modest sale.** Using Blue Eye’s reported $79 million price net of acquired cash as an illustrative enterprise-value proxy, and assuming no further adjustments, $79 million in distributable equity yields $2.37 million, or 4.74×. The memo supplies no Verdun valuation. (Market & Traction)
- **Strong sale.** An assumed $100 million enterprise value less $10 million in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million in distributable equity, yielding $2.70 million, or 5.4×. The premium requires evidence of buyer economics beyond the current trial signals. (The Idea, Market & Traction)
- **Prolonged private operation.** Continued reinvestment yields zero realized proceeds in the scenario. The memo supplies no evidence of a funded payout, eligible secondary, or other independent liquidity route.

### Countercase

Motorola already owns a live-intervention business, and Deep Sentinel offers AI detection with live guards. Verdun’s alert-closure rate could reflect easy dismissals while leaving buyers with little incremental savings or unacceptable missed threats. The adjacent deal falls just below the benchmark under the modest assumptions. The evidence leaves a resolvable pricing gap and establishes neither a structural block nor materially inadequate proceeds across the strongest credible route. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)
