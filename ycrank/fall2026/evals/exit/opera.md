# OpEra -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. An ERP or warehouse management software vendor could buy OpEra to add live replanning to the systems its customers already use. The asset would be the optimization engine, its integrations, and any reusable components of its site-specific constraint models. Ownership could make sense if the vendor could roll out that capability across its installed base faster or more profitably than building it or licensing it. The memo documents a public launch and describes the integrations, but supplies no buyer-side evidence for that advantage. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The founders report that their MIT retailer routing project produced 38% lower delivery costs and 10% more on-time deliveries. OpEra separately reports a 10.4% higher premium over market for a sugar producer and 54% fewer unfulfilled orders for a services marketplace. These results indicate potential customer value. Deployment volumes, paid usage, and sustained production performance remain unverified. The backtest-to-pilot process gives OpEra a plausible path to a paid multi-site rollout, but the memo gives no budget, burn, or deployment cost for reaching it. The memo also leaves ownership and transfer rights for the core software and customer-specific models unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** With actual terms undisclosed, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% relative to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3%. The hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% ownership and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% case requires about $88.3 million of enterprise value. At an eight-year sale, a $25 million enterprise value yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), while a $100 million enterprise value yields $2.85 million gross (5.7×). These scenarios use simple pro rata allocation before investor taxes. Actual debt, fees, and senior claims remain unknown. The competitor financings in the memo supply no acquisition price evidence. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

If growth plateaus, buyer interest fails to emerge, public scale stays out of reach, and OpEra reinvests available cash for eight years, the holder realizes $0 and retains the shares. A sponsor buyout lacks evidence of predictable cash generation or financing. A secondary lacks evidence of eligible share demand and sale terms. An IPO lacks evidence of operating scale and a path to holder resale. Dividends or buybacks lack evidence of distributable cash. Any wind-down proceeds would come from the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could use Timefold’s routing and scheduling APIs as a starting point, license OpEra for individual deployments, or build its own workflow as language-assisted modeling becomes commercially available. If each new site still requires substantial custom integration, buying OpEra may offer little advantage over those options. The reported outcomes leave that deployment burden unresolved. This supports Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural block nor a realizable sale price that leaves the holder materially short, so the Pass gate remains untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can OpEra show a paid multi-site rollout that sustains measurable operating gains while reusing enough of the same software and integrations to support an acquisition near the illustrative $88.3 million enterprise-value hurdle? Documented deployment costs and ongoing customer results, paired with a credible estimate of what a vendor would gain by owning the product versus licensing or building it, would move the action to Reach out if they support a price range that clears the hurdle.
