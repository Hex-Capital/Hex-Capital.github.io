# Telamont -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Telamont reports covering billions of dollars in construction projects. What does a dollar of that coverage earn its shareholders? The dossier does not establish that connection. Proposed post-money, competing term sheets, and whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital are all unknown. I will not infer an auction from YC membership, but I cannot underwrite a bargain either. The round also lacks a disclosed experiment, budget, and deadline. Those two missing gates receive my explicit defaults.

The interesting asset is the bridge between construction practice and software engineering. Charles Goulet brings construction-family exposure and experience at EBC and RGI Electrical alongside engineering roles at 1Password; William G. Gagnon brings industrial AI experience from Poka. Their product compares document revisions, identifies discrepancies, and drafts notices for human approval. That is a concrete application of their combined access. It fits my pattern of backing people who can translate between operational worlds. But previous employer names establish context, not prior shipped results or scaled operations; founder credit stops at 6/10.

The strongest objection to passing is that Telamont may already be entering the workflow where valuable information originates. Canmec, TCI+, and other named customer logos give that possibility substance. Company-reported outcomes of $1.4 million in avoided rework and $2–3 million recovered on subcontract packages suggest potentially consequential utility, although they concern unnamed clients and come through a search snippet. If repeat use produces expert corrections that competitors cannot access, this could become a proprietary pool of construction judgment. The missing link is ownership and conversion: the dossier does not demonstrate that exclusive data, relationships, or embedded workflow caused a purchase or renewal. An Autodesk listing supplies availability; it does not establish that mechanism. This dimension stays at its absent-evidence default.

Competition makes that distinction expensive. Trimble owns Document Crunch and can distribute overlapping risk intelligence through existing project-management and ERP relationships. Telamont advertises continuous revision monitoring and technical/code checks, but the dossier supplies no measured advantage that establishes why customers will keep buying separately. Mandatory human review sensibly acknowledges errors; it also leaves an unanswered economic question about how much review each account requires. Private subscription terms establish an offering, not collected revenue, gross margin, burn, or a route to break-even. I give no extra credit for project value, integrations, or update counts, while making no accusation that the founders are deliberately manufacturing metrics.

I pass at 30/100. The exception cases in my framework require demonstrated conversion and retention, hard external purchase demand, or a financing structure supported by prior operating receipts. Telamont has not documented those overrides. Nor does the evidence establish the red flags of an auction, equal-founder paralysis, or economics premised on eliminating AI errors. This is a plausible operating business with an unproven investment proposition. One verifiable condition would reopen underwriting: a signed round below $10 million post-money funding a fixed six-month test of whether existing contractor accounts renew at contribution margins sufficient to reach break-even within that round’s runway. That would connect price, a finite experiment, and survival without another financing.
