# Native -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Native sits at a useful market transition: generation models make content abundant, small businesses buy help deciding what to publish, and the controlling variable becomes how little human oversight is required to keep producing relevant marketing. My governing belief would be that Native can reduce that oversight enough to sustain recurring subscriptions at attractive margins. Norn’s ranking of ideas before generation addresses the right bottleneck. The dossier establishes the mechanism, but its observational research does not establish the predictive advantage: selecting retained accounts creates exactly the bias that could make ordinary personalization look like superior judgment.

The demand evidence is substantially stronger than the economic evidence. Native reports $2M ARR within six months in its October 2026 YC launch, alongside 2,200+ paying customers across 20+ countries. Those are meaningful purchases for this stage, even after discounting company-reported figures. The 40,000+ published posts show production activity, although the unspecified month limits interpretation. I would not confuse absent retention disclosure with demonstrated churn. Customers are paying; the unresolved question is what makes them continue paying.

At $79 per month for Pro, Native needs automation to carry most of the delivery burden. Frontier models generating assets and software scheduling publication provide a concrete route to lower costs, but the dossier supplies no measured reduction in customer labor or Native’s delivery costs. The Briggs on Homes engagement comparison—5.5% versus 2.9% during August–October 2026—is encouraging customer evidence, not proof of improved business economics. A large social-media-management market does not resolve this distinction. Native could grow revenue while human review, support, and generation costs prevent the margin expansion I want to underwrite.

The strongest objection to waiting is that the company already owns more of the workflow than a content generator: brand context, idea selection, approval and rejection feedback, asset creation, and publishing. Better models should improve execution inside that loop. This fits my pattern of backing applications around a technology discontinuity, particularly when the first product establishes a channel for adjacent products. But the corresponding red flag is claiming a data advantage before establishing its reproduction cost. Native documents feedback collection; it does not establish that a well-funded competitor cannot reproduce the benefit. Blaze already advertises a comparable strategy-to-publishing loop, and publishing through Ayrshare leaves an important integration available to competitors.

The founders’ prior work together on Wepost, which reportedly reached NOK80,000 MRR before being wound down, provides genuine second-time operating experience. Their content, agency, and machine-learning backgrounds reinforce that evidence without substituting for the market case. I would monitor Native rather than write a personal check today. No frontier-research or physical-economics exception changes that decision; this is a commercial workflow business with real demand and an incompletely demonstrated economic advantage. My flip condition is a prospective, matched customer test showing that Norn-selected ideas reduce human review hours per published post while maintaining engagement and paid renewal versus the same workflow without Norn.
