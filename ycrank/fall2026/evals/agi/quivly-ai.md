# Quivly AI -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Quivly could make hands-on onboarding viable for smaller accounts at usage-priced software vendors that could previously afford only self-service. Its agents turn kickoff conversations into assigned launch tasks and flag adoption risks. Octolane says it onboarded more than 100 customers without a dedicated customer engineering hire. Quivly separately claims eight hours saved per forward-deployed engineer per week. Those reports support a plausible labor-saving mechanism. Whether the automated workflow maintained equivalent quality after approvals, retries, and escalations, and whether it reached accounts that previously went without hands-on onboarding, remains unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Named customers provide early adoption evidence. The reported 18,000 downstream accounts measure the volume managed across customers, while realized revenue and renewals remain unknown. The founders’ customer-intelligence and billing infrastructure experience, together with the launch of the post-sales product and the June MCP release, support seed-stage execution. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team)

At full utilization, both listed monthly plans imply $10 in subscription revenue per managed account-month before discounts. That is a pricing scenario. Contribution from the next customer depends on inference, integration and support costs, and any review or exception handling Quivly absorbs. The buyer’s own review labor also matters because it determines which accounts become economical to serve. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Quivly may improve productivity for existing post-sales teams while incumbents deliver equivalent workflows using account data they already hold. ChurnZero already runs agents inside its workflows, and Gainsight overlaps in renewals. Mycroft’s use of Quivly to track implementation milestones and billable hours shows workflow embedding at one customer. The memo leaves migration costs and paid preference under functional parity untested. It also identifies connector dependencies without evidence that Quivly controls a scarce input that could support Path A. Both necessary Path B conditions remain unproven, so the outreach gate stays closed. The incumbent overlap makes substitution plausible without demonstrating it. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will software vendors keep paying Quivly to onboard accounts that previously went without hands-on service when an incumbent or general-purpose AI workflow can deliver equivalent results? A paid cohort trial should document the prior cost barrier, compare total human time at matched onboarding quality, and give buyers a renewal choice after trying the alternative. Repeat paid use with verified labor savings and a demonstrated preference for Quivly would support rescoring for Reach out. Evidence that the workflow serves only already-economic accounts or that buyers readily switch at parity would move the action to Pass.
