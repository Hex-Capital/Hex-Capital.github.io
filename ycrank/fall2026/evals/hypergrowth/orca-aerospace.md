# Orca Aerospace -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** Orca’s first buyer is a satellite fleet operator whose ground team may have to wait for the next contact window to respond to a fault. Orca proposes moving some of that work onboard. The memo records a signed Orbital letter of intent worth up to $20 million and a joint concept study with Lyra. These are concrete early signals of buyer interest. The memo leaves payments, flight use, and repeat purchasing unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Orca plans to integrate once with Reflex Aerospace’s flight software and collect a license fee for each spacecraft that runs it. **Scenario assumption:** If Orbital deploys its planned fleet and Orca can reuse the qualified integration, each added satellite could generate license revenue with limited additional engineering. Contribution would depend on how much of the first integration and qualification the integration fee covers, and how much validation, telemetry review, and support each additional satellite requires. In a second geography, a fleet operator with the same fault-response problem and compatible avionics could follow the same route. A different flight stack could require fresh integration and qualification. The decisive uncertainty is whether Orca can reuse that work across independent buyers. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The strongest case for Pass is that Orca scales one flight integration at a time. Memory and power constraints can limit compatible spacecraft, hardware-in-the-loop testing adds engineering effort, and Orca plans human telemetry review before expanding command authority. Even successful reuse within Orbital would leave license growth tied to Orbital’s fleet rollout. These risks make proportional delivery costs plausible. The memo leaves the actual cost of a second deployment unmeasured, so the structural Pass gate remains unmet. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent fleet operator in a second geography pay for and qualify a deployment that reuses Orca’s first flight-stack integration? A paid qualification test with documented engineering hours and per-spacecraft validation and support costs would move the action to **Reach out** if it shows the license can contribute after those costs. If the test shows that qualification and support costs rise roughly in step with licensed spacecraft and there is no demonstrated way to reduce them, move to **Pass**. Until the result is available, **Watch**.
