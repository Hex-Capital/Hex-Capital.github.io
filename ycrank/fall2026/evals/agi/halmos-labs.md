# Halmos Labs -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Halmos Labs launched Physical Validation to connect AI research software with external labs. The strongest seed case is that automating experiment scoping and lab sourcing could make small validation jobs affordable for biotech teams that currently defer them. Halmos reports that its earlier Halmos Bio product needed 30% fewer evaluations on average to match the best result in a simulated bioprocessing campaign. That supports the experiment-selection mechanism. Wet-lab labor, cost, and quality at equivalent outcomes remain unmeasured in the memo. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Halmos also reports that a food-tech collaborator changed its fermentation experiment plan. That is a limited demand signal for the earlier scoping workflow. Physical Validation buyer commitments and repeat orders remain unverified. The founders’ drug-discovery and quantitative modeling experience and the product launch support seed-stage execution. Managing external lab quality and exceptions is the next execution test. The reported initial funding is consistent with seed entry. The Swedish base leaves geographic fit open under a primarily U.S. strategy. (Halmos Labs, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

**Scenario assumption.** If automated scoping and sourcing lower the delivered cost enough to unlock deferred jobs, buyers could still choose Halmos under model parity if it reliably routes work to qualified labs and resolves fulfillment problems. The expansion unit is one additional completed job. Its contribution is the buyer payment less the lab payout and Halmos’s variable costs for inference, coordination, review, and rework. Project-specific quotes provide a pricing mechanism. The memo gives no platform fee or per-job cost data to establish that contribution. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Science Exchange already combines lab sourcing, supplier compliance, and an AI assistant. Medra and Ginkgo operate their own automated labs. Halmos controls redistribution of its catalogue and reviews supplier corrections. Those controls support curation, while buyers are directed to verify provider information themselves. With cheap frontier models and well-funded competitors, buyers could source equivalent lab work elsewhere and Halmos could have little leverage over price or delivery. Path A lacks separate evidence that Halmos controls scarce lab capacity. For Path B, the newly viable transaction and the reason to pay under parity both remain unproven, and the memo does not establish that buyers have already switched or rejected the service. That leaves a feasible test and supports Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Physical Validation win repeat paid orders from independent biotech teams for validation jobs they previously deferred, when those teams can use equally capable AI with another sourcing route? A paired trial should compare equivalent usable results, count human review and rework, and show positive contribution after lab and fulfillment costs. Repeat paid preference under those conditions would support moving to Reach out if the updated score clears the threshold and mandate fit is confirmed. If realistic delivery costs erase the savings or buyers readily choose the equivalent route, move to Pass.
