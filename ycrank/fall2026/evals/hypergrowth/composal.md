# Composal -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Verify is Composal’s strongest Hypergrowth path. Assuming strong product-market fit with web engineering teams, the same browser-based product could verify customer journeys before releases across many markets. It takes a running app and product specs, saves test scenarios, and returns failure evidence in the pull-request workflow. Authentication and network setup vary by customer environment. The memo identifies no country-specific dependency in the core workflow. Sparks remains on a beta waitlist. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The YC launch gives engineers a discovery channel, and Verify documents a self-serve discovery scan before checkout. The path to paid production is less established because early onboarding included founder assistance. The $500 monthly Team plan includes 3,000 browser minutes, with prepaid add-ons at $1 per minute. Saved scenarios and test history give teams a reason to keep running Verify across releases. If retained teams add journeys or run them more frequently and exceed the included allowance, their bills can grow enough to offset churn. That expansion path is inferred. Renewals and add-on purchases remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Composal reports finding more than 1,500 bugs in customer apps since mid-October, shortly after Verify’s October launch with a three-person team. That indicates early product use. Realized revenue, paying-customer counts, and billed production volume remain unknown, so Initial traction receives the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

At full use of the included minutes, the subscription yields about $0.17 per browser minute before browser infrastructure, model inference, storage, and attributable acquisition, setup, and support costs. Extra minutes bring in $1 each before their incremental costs. Reusing saved scenarios could reduce discovery work on repeat runs, but the memo gives no measured costs or utilization, leaving contribution uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Composal reported that authentication or network controls stalled five of ten setup calls. If ordinary buyers regularly need founder help to connect test environments, the self-serve scan may generate interest without producing a scalable path to paid use. Verify supports dedicated test accounts and private-network connections, so the structural gate clears at this stage. The reported stalls leave open how often those options resolve access without hands-on work. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can typical engineering teams go from a discovery scan to paid, recurring production use without founder help? A cohort of new Team accounts that includes authenticated or private test environments and shows most buyers completing setup independently, paying, and still running Verify through their first renewal would move the action to Reach out. If founder-assisted setup remains common, keep Watch.
