# Stacktrace -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a security platform that wants to add approval and audit controls across coding agents. The memo records Fleet’s public launch and documents a local sidecar for session telemetry alongside a gateway for approvals and decision records. Stacktrace advertises a single policy across multiple coding agents. The acquisition rationale is an inference. A platform could buy Stacktrace to add that coverage faster than it could build and maintain the integrations itself. The memo provides no measured buyer benefit or evidence of acquisition interest. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The seed-stage milestone is a paid production deployment across multiple coding agents that measures how much manual review Fleet saves and how often it interrupts legitimate work. The public launch and reported $500,000 YC financing support attempting that test. A founder reports unnamed design partners, with paid conversion, runway and the cost of expanding coverage still unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at $10 million post-money, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The memo gives no holder-specific terms. The following prices are illustrative assumptions. Both sale cases assume an all-cash close within eight years, eligibility to sell the entire stake, $10 million in net debt and other deal deductions, and pro rata distribution of the remaining equity proceeds with no preference or senior claim diverting cash from the holder.

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Cash received by holder |
|----------|------------------------------------------|-------------------------|
| Modest sale | $50 million less $10 million = $40 million | $1.2 million (2.4×) |
| Strong sale | $100 million less $10 million = $90 million | $2.7 million (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale | $0 realized |

The strong case clears the objective by $200,000. At 2% ownership, the same sale pays $1.8 million. The memo supplies no basis for underwriting either sale price, verifying the relevant transfer or approval terms, or crediting a separate liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could extend controls it already has. Anthropic’s managed permissions and hooks overlap with Fleet’s controls, and Bay offers policy enforcement through existing security infrastructure. Stacktrace’s cross-agent coverage is the clearest reason to buy, but its dependence on supported logs and enforcement points limits that advantage, and customer retention and switching costs remain unverified. This substitution risk supports Watch. The evidence leaves open whether a platform would pay enough and establishes neither a structural closing block nor a ceiling on holder proceeds that would trigger Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a security platform pay enough for Stacktrace’s cross-agent controls to leave at least $83.3 million in distributable equity value? A strategic buyer’s build-versus-buy assessment, grounded in measured results from a paid production deployment, would move the action to Reach out if it supported a price range that clears the hurdle after plausible deal deductions.
