# Invertix -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Invertix has raised €1.7 million, but I cannot price the check I am being asked to write. Post-money valuation, competing offers, and current round terms are undisclosed. Vireo leading an earlier financing does not establish either an auction or scarce capital. More importantly, the dossier never identifies the finite experiment another check buys. Deploying an AI workforce describes a business ambition; it does not specify what truth the money reveals, by when, or why that truth changes the company’s value.

The interesting asset is access to the operating workflow. More than 1.8 GW reportedly managed through Invertix’s workers is meaningful deployment evidence at this stage. TotalEnergies On provides another route into operating sites. This fits my preference for software connecting fragmented physical systems: equipment records, fault investigation, and maintenance responsibilities are an actual coordination problem. But customer access and ownership of the resulting advantage are different things. Customer-directed data processing does not establish a reusable proprietary data pool, and the customer-specific ontology claims carry less weight because the cited launch page includes a future-dated heading.

The strongest argument against passing is the company-reported “multiple six figures” of ARR within one month of financing. Eight people appear to have found something energy operators will buy. That could be the beginning of a functioning business whose AI upside comes cheaply. My known exceptions allow demonstrated conversion and durable customer economics to outweigh an incomplete financing story. Here, however, ARR is not accompanied by retention, pilot conversion, or measured operating savings. TotalEnergies program participation does not supply those missing receipts. The exception is plausible; its evidentiary threshold has not been met.

Delete the next financing and the central question becomes implementation economics. Vireo describes roughly 60 days from contract to deployment. Embedded engineering may produce valuable customer intimacy, but each additional installation could also consume another block of skilled labor. The terms require supervision and professional sign-off where applicable; that is sensible operating discipline, not evidence that automation has failed. Those costs nevertheless belong in the margin calculation. Revenue earns some credit toward a real business, while undisclosed gross margin, burn, and runway leave the seed-funded path to profit unresolved. Delfos already combines domain rules, operational data, and language models, so generic improvements in AI will also reach a direct competitor.

Perrotta’s commercial background and Durmaz’s applied-AI experience are relevant, but the biographies do not document prior shipped results across both software and energy operations. I would not manufacture that bridge from their titles or from an energy investor’s participation. Equally, I see no evidence of an auction deadline, equal-founder paralysis, or a business plan that explicitly assumes errors disappear. The concern is narrower: Invertix may have useful deployments without yet having demonstrated that deployment knowledge compounds faster than delivery costs.

I pass on the evidence available. The workflow foothold deserves attention, but it cannot answer the price and capital-purpose questions by itself. One verifiable condition would reopen my decision: a reconciled operating plan, supported by signed customer contracts and actual deployment costs, showing cash break-even within the capital already raised. That would establish that Invertix can choose its next financing rather than depend on it.
