# Raven -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Raven has shipped something. What would my check buy? The dossier gives no post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or use-of-proceeds experiment. I cannot establish either that capital is scarce or that this round flips one consequential card. YC membership does not establish an auction; financing silence does not establish a bargain. Both gates receive their missing-evidence defaults.

Isa Usmanov is the strongest reason to keep looking. His prior businesses report $3.4 million-plus GMV at droov, $1.1 million-plus ARR at an ed-tech SaaS, and a sale of Reinio to a laundromat chain. Those figures belong to earlier companies, but they establish operating receipts. Add Android development and the organizer-confirmed agentic-phone demonstration, and there is a credible builder connecting mobile interfaces with business workflows. That fits my pattern of backing founders who translate between systems. It earns substantial founder credit, although the dossier does not establish experience distributing or supporting phones.

The strongest bull case is that this builder has already crossed from demonstration into initial shipments. The founder reports more than 160,000 demo views and hundreds of community signups. A small group of employees could discover that delegating Gmail-to-Drive-to-Slack work is valuable enough to buy a dedicated device, giving Raven an embedded relationship that general assistants struggle to displace. But the evidence stops before that causal chain closes. Shipment quantities and payment status are undisclosed; no retained employee workflow is documented. Views show attention. They do not establish that Raven owns access that causes purchases. That dimension stays at its default.

The competitive mechanism makes the missing conversion evidence consequential. Brain already has SoftBank distribution in Japan, and Rabbit offers execution across existing devices. Raven must persuade someone to adopt another phone while alternatives remove that requirement. Its demonstrated calendar actions establish technical capability, but they do not establish dependable execution across sensitive employee workflows. This activates my AI commoditization red flag: useful functionality can spread faster than the company acquires a durable customer relationship. Raven fits the translation-layer pattern more clearly than the proprietary-access pattern.

I would also delete the next financing from the plan. Public pricing, device costs, support burden, margin, and burn are unavailable, so I cannot underwrite a seed-funded path to profitability. That earns the economics default, without assuming hardware necessarily makes the business uneconomic. My exceptions for AI-centric or expensive investments require concrete conversion, retention, cash economics, or unusually strong external demand. Raven’s prototype and founder history make the opposing case credible; the disclosed shipments do not yet supply that override. I pass on the available evidence.

One verifiable flip condition would reopen the decision: a documented round below $10 million post-money that funds a single, dated employee-device trial, with paid renewal and contribution profit after inference and support as its pass/fail measures. That would connect price, the capital-funded experiment, and a functioning business. Today, Raven offers stronger evidence that Usmanov can build than that my money purchases a scarce opportunity.
