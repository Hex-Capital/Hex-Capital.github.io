# Particularity -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Particularity’s potentially important truth is that consumer-protection investigations can begin with machines systematically finding violations, rather than citizens first recognizing and reporting them. That proposition is testable: have investigators review previously unknown cases generated by the agents and determine whether they establish actionable violations. The October 2026 launch describes checkout testing, cancellation-flow investigation, and statutory mapping. It supplies neither that result nor a named prevailing belief the founders have overturned. I see a specific hypothesis worth testing; I cannot yet identify the demonstrated secret.

The founding relationship gives me more confidence than the technical claim. Kumaravel and Bannon attended Berkeley, and Bannon calls Kumaravel his best friend. That is affirmative evidence of a relationship predating the pitch, although friendship does not establish that they have shipped together. Full-time status, ownership, and economic exposure remain undisclosed. There is no documented convenience matching, outside employment, or outsourced engineering to trigger a founding-team veto. There is also insufficient evidence to treat the foundation as settled.

The narrow customer choice is sensible. State attorneys general and city consumer-affairs departments investigate conduct; the listed competitors principally help businesses archive communications or monitor their own compliance. Particularity should not be condemned as an interchangeable competitor merely because PerformLine reports more than $10 million ARR in an adjacent market. But choosing a different buyer does not itself create a monopoly. Timestamped, hashed records establish integrity of captured material; they do not establish exclusive access, superior detection, or legal sufficiency. UNCTAD’s account of existing agency mystery-shopping and recording tools makes the ownership question concrete: what would an agency lose by replacing Particularity? The dossier demonstrates no mechanism that makes replacement progressively harder.

The strongest case against passing is that these founders understand the institutional boundary others overlook. Kumaravel worked on consumer protection at the Senate Banking Committee; Bannon built security and compliance infrastructure at AWS. Their product fits my revealed interest in adversarial information systems: making concealed conduct legible to an institution empowered to act. A successful investigation could establish trust that a generic compliance vendor cannot readily purchase. That is a serious opportunity. Yet policy experience does not prove purchasing access, and relevant engineering experience does not prove investigative accuracy. The exceptions in my framework require demonstrated technical discontinuity, persistent customer behavior, or a concrete deployment path. Particularity’s October 15 emergence from stealth and launch coverage establish none of those overrides.

Distribution is at least addressed explicitly: the founders solicit introductions to identifiable government buyers. That earns more credit than an unexplained promise of enterprise sales. It remains an untested route, with no documented procurement cycle, implementation burden, or contract value. Agency subscriptions are the researcher’s inference, not established pricing. Without a price, margin structure, or expansion arithmetic, I cannot turn this useful investigative function into a power-law investment case. Those are limits on conviction, not evidence that a twelve-day-old public company has failed.

I pass on a check today. My single verifiable condition for reopening diligence is a paid renewal by a state attorney general’s office following its documented use of Particularity-generated evidence in an enforcement action. That event would connect detection, institutional acceptance, and willingness to pay. It would not automatically establish a monopoly, but it would replace the central hypothetical with customer behavior consequential enough to investigate.
