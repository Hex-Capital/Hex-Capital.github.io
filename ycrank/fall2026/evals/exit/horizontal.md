# horizontal. -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic sale to an enterprise or plant software vendor. Horizontal describes a product that connects planning, quality, and batch release across existing plant systems, with human approval for critical actions. A buyer could use that cross-system capability to extend its own product if Horizontal proves that the same workflows can be deployed economically across plants. The memo records an October launch and lists $125,000 in seed financing, with total funding unverified. Horizontal says it tests in operating plants and advertises a 50% reduction in release time. It supplies no named customer or baseline for that figure. Its proposed single-workflow rollout makes a paid replication test plausible at seed, while runway and deployment costs remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The memo gives no investor terms or basis for estimating a realizable sale price, so the proceeds bridge uses the rubric benchmark. A $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The 5× gross cash objective is $2.5 million within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity at full participation. The sale cases below assume an all-cash close within eight years, $10 million in net debt and other deal deductions, a pro rata waterfall with no senior claims, and eligibility to sell the full stake.

| Scenario | Holder proceeds |
|---|---:|
| Modest sale at $50 million enterprise value | $40 million distributable equity × 3% = $1.2 million cash, or 2.4× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | $90 million distributable equity × 3% = $2.7 million cash, or 5.4× |
| Prolonged private operation with cash reinvested | $0 realized within eight years |

The strong case clears the hurdle by $200,000. If only 80% of the stake could be sold by year eight, it would return $2.16 million. These prices are illustrative assumptions. The memo leaves buyer willingness to pay, transfer and consent terms, and holder eligibility unverified. It supplies no evidence of an independent route to cash. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

SAP has announced overlapping shortage detection and rescheduling features. That gives SAP customers an embedded substitute for part of the workflow and weakens a buyer’s reason to acquire Horizontal if the remaining integrations require custom work at each plant. The memo gives no revenue or multi-plant rollout figures to show that customers pay for the broader capability or that deployments get cheaper with repetition. This supports Watch. Neither Pass gate is triggered because the evidence leaves both closing feasibility and the strongest credible sale price unresolved. (Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can one paying manufacturer replicate the same cross-system workflow at a second plant with customer-verified savings and low incremental deployment cost? A paid expansion with before-and-after results and tracked implementation hours at both sites would move the action to Reach out if it shows that the savings persist and the second rollout requires materially less work. That would give a strategic buyer a concrete economic reason to evaluate the product.
