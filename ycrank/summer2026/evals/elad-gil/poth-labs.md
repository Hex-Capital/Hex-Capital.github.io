# Poth Labs -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: cheaper inference now lets product teams query fragmented customer records, but Poth Labs becomes large only if resolving identities across those systems produces materially better decisions than incumbents’ existing feedback graphs. That is the controlling belief. The dossier identifies entity resolution as central and describes the causal loop—connect users, accounts, conversations, and behaviors, then collect missing evidence—but provides no accuracy benchmark, decision-quality measure, or threshold at which customers consolidate spending around Poth.

The 280-fold decline in GPT-3.5-level inference cost from November 2022 to October 2024 makes repeated analysis and adaptive interviews economically feasible. It does not yet demonstrate a change in customer budgets, delivery speed, or margins. This is a crystallizing market, not an unoccupied one: Enterpret already offers entity resolution, cited answers, a customer context graph, and more than 50 integrations, backed by $25.1 million. Dovetail, Sprig, and Marvin collectively cover repositories, surveys, replay, and AI-moderated interviews. Poth’s technical shift is real; its market opening is not yet distinct.

The strongest case is architectural. Poth is not merely formatting model output. It intends to retain company-specific entity relationships, reconcile operational evidence, show supporting and contradicting facts, and initiate follow-up research when the graph cannot answer a question. Better models should improve hypothesis generation and interviews while Poth continues to own permissions, integrations, and workflow state. The soft pivot from adaptive surveys to this broader loop is directionally sensible. However, the dossier documents the product architecture rather than its reliability: no integration inventory, entity-resolution error rate, research-validity benchmark, or evidence that customers depend on the resulting graph.

There is early product contact, but not yet demonstrated pull. Inkle, Sol, Composio, UnCircuit, and Illume appear under “Trusted by teams at,” and several Summer 2026 YC companies reportedly used the product. Those are useful pre-seed signals. They do not reveal whether usage repeats, whether anyone pays, or whether Poth displaced manual research or an incumbent product. The distinction matters because a few design partners can validate interest while leaving the controlling belief—cross-system resolution changes decisions—untested.

The bull case is that Matthew Wong learned the fragmented-enterprise-data problem as a Palantir forward-deployed engineer, Mojmír Horváth brings applied data-science and multimodal-model experience, and the team has already revised the product around customer interaction. That combination could let Poth execute faster than better-funded horizontal competitors. It does not qualify for a known-exception override: this is neither a scarce frontier-model team nor a reconstructed physical market, and the dossier documents no prior startup learning or costly commitment beyond founding the company. The unstated pattern I would want—using an initial product to establish reusable distribution into adjacent customer workflows—is possible, but not yet evidenced.

I would not invest today. The red flag is not that Poth uses AI; it is that its claimed differentiation substantially overlaps with Enterpret while its proposed moat remains an inferred accumulation of mappings and history whose reproduction cost is unspecified. The verifiable flip condition is three named customers paying at least $25,000 annually, using Poth weekly across three or more production systems, with audited entity-linking precision above 95% and documented renewal or expansion. That would validate both the controlling variable and a distribution wedge. Without it, I expect the company to remain a useful feature set in a crowded category rather than become the system of record.
