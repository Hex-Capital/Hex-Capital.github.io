# Cerenovus -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

$23.2 billion of knowledge-management spend gives Cerenovus room to maneuver, but Cerenovus is a broad-market code product whose knowledge compounds inside isolated customer vaults rather than across a network. That is the signature: high pivot-room, low user-added compounding. The upside geometry is respectable, not yet asymmetric. A better organizational database can become valuable software; a shared intelligence layer can become a platform. Cerenovus has demonstrated the first architecture, not the second.

**Notion** is the high rung for market optionality: a malleable workspace that survived repeated rebuilds because the underlying market included documents, databases, collaboration, and software creation. Cerenovus shares that breadth across documents, Slack, CRM, ERP, accounting records, workflows, and agent context. Cerenovus diverges where the analogy becomes load-bearing: Notion began as a builder-tool users could reshape permissionlessly, while Cerenovus currently splits itself between executive decision infrastructure and shared context for AI-native startups. Two buyer identities can represent optionality. They can also represent an unfound wedge. The $23.2 billion market and several adjacent pivots earn the strongest score, but the product has not yet shown which surface pulls users organically.

**Perplexity** is the low rung on network compounding: each user receives a useful answer without directly making every prior user’s product more valuable. Cerenovus has the same structural weakness. Each new document improves one organization’s private graph, but adding another customer does not automatically improve the first customer’s graph, supply, or distribution. Cerenovus therefore builds switching costs, not network effects. Perplexity overcame this weakness through Aravind Srinivas’s rare retrieval expertise, permissionless product distribution, and pre-consensus timing. Cerenovus has no equivalent founder-specific knowledge or organic distribution evidence yet, so the known exception does not apply.

Compendium’s free personal vaults, 14-day trial, and $39.99-per-seat plan give Cerenovus a code-leverage entry point. Over 18 months, Cerenovus can compound if one user imports context, receives an evidence-linked answer, and invites colleagues without a salesperson or security consultant touching the loop. The counterforce is visible already: Google Drive, Notion, Microsoft, Slack, CRM, ERP, and accounting integrations turn permissions, retention, deletion, and procurement into the product. Cerenovus reached only Product Hunt rank #16 with 123 points, while live pilots suggest the enterprise motion may become primary. Code is permissionless. Enterprise data access is not. The architecture sits between product-led software and labor-assisted deployment.

Jonathan Waldorf brings Harvard physics and electrical engineering plus quantum-algorithms work at QuEra; Oliver Moreland brings Math 55 and computer science coursework. Cerenovus therefore has evidence of intelligence, but intelligence is trainable and specific knowledge is not. No founder has documented enterprise-operations experience, a public repository, or years spent owning the broken handoffs Cerenovus proposes to diagnose. The score flips into invest territory if Jonathan ships a public technical artifact showing a materially novel organizational-graph system, or if named pilot customers demonstrate that Cerenovus discovers operational failures Glean cannot. Until then, the founders look capable of building the technology but not uniquely destined to build it.

**Stack Overflow** was built by developers who had already spent years writing for developers; the founders’ prior work made intelligence, energy, and long-term behavior legible. Cerenovus offers thinner evidence. Jonathan, Oliver, and Lucas Baur started together at Harvard in 2026, launched pilots by June, and expanded to five people, which supports energy and execution. Cerenovus provides no adverse integrity signal, but LinkedIn followings of roughly 1,000, 157, and 2,000 are not ten-year-partner evidence. Integrity should not be inferred from a polished biography or punished for silence. I leave Cerenovus slightly above the absent-evidence default: smart and energetic, with the most important trait still unknowable.

Glean’s more than $100 million ARR and existing Enterprise Graph define the likely gravitational field: by July 2029, Cerenovus will have pivoted from broad executive intelligence into a narrower context-and-audit layer for AI-agent workflows, reaching roughly $2 million to $6 million ARR rather than becoming a general organizational knowledge network. Cerenovus can win that wedge because evidence-linked answers and longitudinal decision history matter more as agents act autonomously. Cerenovus is unlikely to win the horizontal graph because private customer data does not create cross-customer compounding, while Glean already owns enterprise distribution.

$39.99 per seat is the bull case in miniature: Cerenovus could use self-service pricing to spread through AI-native startups, turn every imported decision into proprietary organizational memory, and follow the **Perplexity** exception—single-sided software winning through code leverage without a marketplace. That case stings because live pilots already exist and the market is growing at 13.8% annually. The evidence still favors the narrower forecast: Product Hunt produced 123 points, no retention or accuracy results are public, the founders lack demonstrated knowledge-management immersion, and Glean can reproduce overlapping ingestion and graph functions through an installed channel. I would flip to Invest if Cerenovus documents 20 paying organizations with at least 150% net revenue retention after twelve months and a majority acquired without founder-led sales.
