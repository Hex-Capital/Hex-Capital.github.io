# Amulet -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

$0.20 per GiB-month makes Amulet a permissionless-code product inside a non-network business. Amulet’s highest dimension is leverage architecture: sub-100-ms mounts, 3-ms copy-on-write forks, and immutable commits let software replicate work without replicating labor. Amulet’s lowest dimension is compounding user-added value: every AI infrastructure customer buys an independent filesystem, and the Nth customer does not improve the first customer’s workspace. The signature is powerful software leverage without network leverage—a good angel bet, but not yet 10,000x geometry.

**Replit** is the high rung because a developer tool became a permissionless creation surface: code produces more code, builders distribute what they build, and the product compounds through public adoption. Amulet shares the code-leverage mechanism—8.4 GB/s mounts and zero-copy writable forks turn scarce infrastructure work into an API—but Amulet diverges where distribution matters. Amulet remains a private beta behind a work-email waitlist and demo booking, with 115 LinkedIn followers and no public repository. Amulet has built permissionless production but not permissionless distribution. Code leverage exists; the channel is still labor-assisted.

**Perplexity** is the low network rung: a single-sided product where one user does not directly make another user’s experience more valuable. Perplexity earned the exception through Aravind Srinivas’s rare retrieval knowledge, product-native distribution, and pre-consensus AI timing. Amulet also lacks a marketplace, shared developer graph, or user-created ecosystem; stored histories and provenance create switching costs, not network effects. Amulet has not yet earned the Perplexity exception because no public repository, organic adoption, or independently verified benchmark demonstrates comparable founder knowledge and product pull.

Nithik Bala’s Rockset and Meta web-infrastructure experience gives Amulet a credible single-anchor parallel to **Stack Overflow**: practitioners building for practitioners. Bala has worked near databases and web infrastructure, while Varun Puru brings Meta backend work plus Amazon cloud-infrastructure experience. Amulet therefore clears the generic “ex-FAANG founder found a TAM” pattern, but only partially. Stack Overflow’s founders had public artifacts and years of visible domain obsession; Amulet’s founders have no located public repositories or technical writing. The specific knowledge is plausible in Amulet’s implementation, not yet legible in Amulet’s history.

Modal’s more than $300M in annualized revenue shows that AI infrastructure is not a niche, and Amulet’s market can compound over the next 18 months through adjacent workloads rather than customer count alone. Amulet can move from forkable datasets into agent-workspace provenance, training-data versioning, sandbox persistence, and conflict-safe publishing across E2B- or Modal-style compute. Each integration deepens operational dependency and lowers the cost of the next use case. The risk is that Modal, E2B, or a cloud platform bundles those primitives before Amulet becomes the independent data layer; pivot-room exists, but distribution time is finite.

Nithik Bala and Varun Puru both worked at Meta, which establishes shared operating context but does not establish the full intelligence-energy-integrity trio. Amulet’s recent pivot from customer-success agents to an AI filesystem is evidence of energy and willingness to discard sunk work; Amulet’s detailed technical surface is evidence of intelligence. Integrity and ten-year-partner behavior remain unobserved, so the score cannot reach the top rung. The counterfactual that flips Amulet upward is concrete: prior colleagues describing clean long-term collaboration, plus public technical work predating YC. A disputed founder split, exaggerated benchmark, or volunteered ethics narrative would flip Amulet downward immediately.

Amulet’s 3-ms forks and sub-100-ms mounts point to a falsifiable 36-month outcome: by July 2029, Amulet will have expanded from a standalone filesystem into the provenance and persistent-data control plane for agent sandboxes, reaching $3M-$8M ARR while remaining a single-sided infrastructure product. Amulet’s broad pivot-room produces the expansion; Amulet’s missing network effects cap the likely independent outcome unless integrations become a de facto protocol.

Archil’s $18M financing is the strongest bear case: Amulet has recently pivoted, its performance numbers are company-published, its beta is gated, and Modal or E2B can bundle storage into already-funded compute platforms. That pattern attacks Amulet’s unstated fit—no open-source adoption, no founder media leverage, and no organic community predating the round. The rebuttal is architectural: Archil accelerates existing buckets, while Amulet combines writable forks, content-addressed history, provenance, and conflict-checked publishing around a new agent workload. I would write a small personal check, not double down. If Amulet cannot demonstrate at least 10 independent production customers with more than 90-day retained usage by January 2027, the bundling argument wins and my verdict flips to Pass.
