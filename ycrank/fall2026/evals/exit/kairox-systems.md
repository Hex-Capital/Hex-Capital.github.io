# Kairox Systems -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A maritime defense systems integrator could buy Tempest to add a single platform for work currently handled by separate aerial and underwater fleets. Ownership could be worth paying for if exclusive control helps the buyer win procurement business or capture meaningful logistics savings. Kairox reports Marine Corps engagement and concept validation with military end users. The memo describes procurement as a future milestone and leaves buyer interest and the economic benefit of ownership unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The next milestone is a funded, customer-backed field trial that verifies integrated performance and measures logistics costs against separate fleets. Kairox reports laboratory component validation, and the memo records $500,000 in reported accelerator funding and relevant hardware and qualification experience on the founding team. Those signals make a trial plausible at seed. Its budget, runway, and funding commitment remain open. Ownership and transferability of the core technology are also unverified. (Key Risks, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Defensibility)

Under the illustrative seed terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted by 40% relative to 3%, with no follow-ons. The eight-year target is $2.5 million in gross cash before investor taxes. At 3%, that requires $83.3 million in distributable equity, or $88.3 million in enterprise value with illustrative net debt of $5 million. At 2% and 4% ownership, the equity hurdles are $125 million and $62.5 million, respectively. Using simple pro rata allocation and the same illustrative debt, a $25 million sale pays $600,000 (1.2×) and a $100 million sale pays $2.85 million (5.7×). The memo gives no company-specific debt, fees, preferences, or acquisition pricing evidence to support either sale scenario. The adjacent $21.6 million procurement award measures contract spending. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A sponsor buyout lacks evidenced cash generation, a secondary lacks evidenced share demand and holder eligibility, an IPO lacks evidenced operating scale and resale readiness, and a company-funded payout lacks evidenced excess cash. If procurement growth plateaus, buyers stay away, IPO scale remains out of reach, and available cash goes back into the business, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. Any wind-down recovery would come from the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Customers can keep their separate fleets, and an integrator could pursue licensing or a partnership if Tempest qualifies. Reported concept feedback and lab validation leave the cost of switching and the value of owning Tempest unmeasured. That gap warrants Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural barrier to a sale nor a supported price range that would leave the holder materially short. The prolonged-private downside is hypothetical. (Defensibility, The Idea, Key Risks)
