# RealPact -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

RealPact removes 30–40 minutes of contract entry and perhaps 200 hours per agent annually. Useful, but the consequence-of-success case stops there. The dossier identifies a $515.8 million transaction-management market, not a societal cost pool that this product could collapse by orders of magnitude. It does not quantify how many agents, transactions, or dollars would be transformed if RealPact became the brokerage operating system. This is currently a productivity product, not yet an option on a radically different real-estate infrastructure.

The technical claim is more interesting than the market claim. Address or voice input becomes a signature-ready New Hampshire agreement in under five minutes, versus 30–40 minutes of manual entry—a potential 6–8x workflow improvement. Ten-plus brokerages and the firm-wide Four Seasons Sotheby’s pilot show that this is a functioning product, not a slide. But the comparison is not a controlled benchmark against SkySlope, Dotloop, or manual processing, and RealPact disclaims the accuracy of populated MLS and public-record data. General voice, vision, and language models can reproduce much of the extraction stack, while SkySlope already reaches 900,000 professionals and processes three million transactions annually. The dossier supplies no proprietary technical discontinuity or learning loop that neutralizes that distribution advantage.

The founders are the strongest part of the case. Ranvir Deshmukh and Erik Peterson previously built CourseMe together; it reached 4,000 monthly users, one million page hits, adoption by 90% of Dartmouth students, and was acquired. They then evolved SignPact into RealPact and secured live brokerage usage despite lacking traditional real-estate résumés. That demonstrates shipping ability and some tactical flexibility. It does not yet demonstrate the risk-tuned recruiting I would expect: the three-person team has no publicly identified real-estate compliance, MLS, security, or contract-law owner for the risks most likely to kill nationwide deployment.

The risk-removal plan is missing. State-specific forms, MLS permissions, contract accuracy, and SOC 2 Type II certification are all named, but RealPact provides no ranked kill risk, decisive experiment, numerical threshold, cost, or stop condition. The Four Seasons pilot could become that experiment, yet the dossier reports neither error rates nor repeat transaction volumes. Capital spent expanding sales before proving accuracy and state portability would be a red flag. Likewise, subscription pricing is undisclosed, so there is no bottom-up proof that implementation, support, data access, and compliance costs permit an unsubsidized mass-market price.

The strongest opposing case stings: a tiny repeat-founder team has already turned a narrow automation wedge into usage at more than ten brokerages, including a recognized firm-wide pilot. If every completed transaction improves templates and exception handling, the wedge could compound into a defensible system of record. That is a legitimate platform-shaped exception to my normal demand for deep technology. But the dossier labels that learning loop as inferred, while the incumbent already owns vastly more transaction data and distribution. RealPact has proved customer curiosity; it has not proved an asymmetric technical or market option.

I pass today. Within 36 months, I expect RealPact to be a regional workflow vendor below $3 million ARR or to be acquired by a transaction-management incumbent such as SkySlope, rather than becoming a nationwide brokerage operating system. One result would flip my decision: an audited, paid deployment across at least three states showing a 10x reduction in contract-production time, under 0.5% material-field errors, and improving accuracy with transaction volume.
