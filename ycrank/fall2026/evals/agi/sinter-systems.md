# Sinter -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Sinter’s strongest commercial case is quick-turn molding for hardware teams that revise plastic parts frequently. Its pilot uses 3D-printed photopolymer tools in existing presses, which could shorten the wait for a first molded sample on compatible parts. **Building blocks of superintelligence scores zero** because the memo identifies neither a specific AI workload these parts serve nor a tooling shortage that constrains AI deployment. **Industry enabled by superintelligence scores zero** because the described cost and lead-time lever is printed tooling. The documented quote workflow automates manufacturability checks and mesh repair, with engineering escalation, but the memo gives no evidence that AI reduces labor per accepted batch enough to make a previously uneconomic order viable. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Sinter’s in-house tooling gives it control over turnaround, but published trials using commercially available materials and printers show a route for other shops to offer similar tooling. Sinter reports producing more than 500 kg of parts with customers in its first month, an early demand signal whose paid molding volume and repeat demand remain unknown. The live prototype quote workflow and the founders’ prior print-farm and switch-manufacturing experience support seed-stage execution credit. Scale economics stays at the absent-evidence default because the memo provides no job-level bridge from per-part pricing to contribution after tool wear, press time, engineering review, and scrap. These gaps limit evidence confidence. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Sinter claims 48-hour molding at one-tenth the time and cost of traditional methods and advertises per-part pricing with no minimum order. If those terms hold for a small batch that a hardware team would otherwise skip, Sinter could open a new use for molded prototypes. Protolabs advertises one-day aluminum molding, so the stronger argument is affordability at low volumes. That commercial possibility leaves the Pass gate in place because the memo attributes the improvement to printed tooling and supplies no evidence that AI makes the excluded use viable. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Sinter show a paid pilot where AI-assisted engineering made a previously uneconomic small molding run viable? A comparison against the same printed-tool workflow with manual engineering would need to show that AI lowered the fully loaded cost per accepted batch enough to meet the buyer’s budget, and that the buyer still needed Sinter to produce and qualify the parts. That result would move the action to **Reach out**.
