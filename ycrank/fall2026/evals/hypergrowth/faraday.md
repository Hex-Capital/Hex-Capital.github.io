# Faraday -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Conditional on strong product-market fit, Faraday’s strongest path is to start with quote preparation for manufacturers and distributors that work across disconnected systems. The memo documents quoting demos and a shared runtime that connects existing software. The same recurring job plausibly travels across markets. The growth scenario assumes Faraday can reuse the quoting workflow and adapt it to each buyer’s records with limited engineering. It also assumes employees approve quotes before they are sent, narrowing the initial scope of write permissions. The work needed to adapt the product across sites and markets remains uncertain. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Faraday currently offers demos on prospects’ own records and seeks referrals and trade-show leads. That supports a repeatable sales playbook with hands-on onboarding. Customer-specific rules and employee corrections could make the product more valuable with continued use, and the terms contemplate subscriptions. Recurring payments and a way to charge existing customers for expanding usage remain unverified. A founder reported one deployment involving a legacy system. Its payment status and the date of first paid availability are unknown, so the October public launch and two-person team give little basis for judging commercial growth. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

In a paid quoting deployment, the incremental contract revenue would need to cover attributable sales effort, integration, workflow validation, runtime and sandbox usage, and ongoing support. The reported sandbox fix shows a way to remove one legacy-system blocker. The memo leaves the billing unit, price, and deployment effort unknown, so the contribution from the next customer is unresolved. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Even with strong demand, Faraday could need bespoke engineering for every customer. It builds customer-specific simulations, says reliability must be tested against each customer’s exceptions, and requires separate authorization and agreed controls for write-enabled workflows. The reported benchmark improvement comes from test environments, giving limited evidence about the effort required in production. The structural downside gate remains open because the shared runtime and reported sandbox fix give Faraday a plausible route to reuse, and the memo does not establish that market-specific rebuilding or uneconomic per-buyer work is unavoidable. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can several comparable manufacturers, including one abroad, put the same quoting workflow into paid production with minimal Faraday engineering? Paid invoices and deployment records showing recurring use, customer-led setup, limited adaptation, and positive contribution after onboarding and support would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that every rollout requires enough bespoke work to make faster growth uneconomic would move it to Pass.
