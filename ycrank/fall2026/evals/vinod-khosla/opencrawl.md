# OpenCrawl -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

OpenCrawl could make maintaining specialized information sources cheap enough that agents can use data nobody could previously afford to collect. That would matter. But the dossier establishes automated discovery, crawling and retrieval; it does not establish an orders-of-magnitude reduction in the cost of supplying useful knowledge. There is no baseline for engineering labor displaced, population served or downstream spending eliminated. I cannot purchase a consequential breakthrough merely by assuming one behind the product description.

Demand for retrieval infrastructure is credible. Exa reports more than $100 million in annualized revenue, and Glean reports $300 million ARR in an adjacent market. Those figures establish spending, not OpenCrawl’s advantage. Firecrawl’s September 2026 Alexandria offering already combines discovery, connectors and hosted indexes. Bespoke pipelines could outperform shared infrastructure on difficult sources, but there is no measured comparison on coverage, freshness, accuracy or maintenance effort. I leave technical differentiation at the missing-evidence default. Calling this an off-the-shelf wrapper would also exceed the evidence.

The strongest argument against passing is the founders’ unusually relevant experience. Patel built ingestion infrastructure at Pocus; Utkarsh worked on Sigma’s query compiler and built agents at Pocus. They understand several layers of the system they propose to automate. The earlier OnePatch product reportedly ingested 32 TB of telemetry and detected or investigated more than 600 incidents. That is evidence of building and operating software, although it supplies no OpenCrawl customer validation. The apparent change from AI SRE to search infrastructure could reflect learning, but the dossier does not document what evidence changed their beliefs. Relevant engineering experience cannot substitute for demonstrated belief revision or recruiting against a specific weakness.

OpenCrawl fits my broader pattern of backing software infrastructure when a small team can turn a narrow wedge into a platform. My exceptions to the technical-breakthrough requirement would matter if these founders had demonstrated an exceptional foothold in an enormous workflow. Here, neither committed technical-community adoption nor a quantified displacement mechanism is documented. The competitive overlap raises the possibility of implementation work becoming the differentiation, but does not prove it. Likewise, there is no evidence of premature scaling, exit fixation or refusal to learn. This is insufficient affirmative evidence, rather than an active category mismatch.

The decisive uncertainty is whether each new source makes OpenCrawl more automated or creates another maintenance obligation. The dossier supplies no experiment, cost or pass/fail threshold addressing that risk. Its $25 starter grant and $10 monthly allowance describe acquisition incentives, not sustainable delivery economics; no rate card or cost model establishes parity. My one condition for reopening the decision is an independently reproducible customer pilot showing a 10x reduction in fully loaded cost per correct, fresh, permission-safe retrieval against Firecrawl Alexandria, including human maintenance. That would reveal a mechanism worth investigating. Today, I pass with my own money.
