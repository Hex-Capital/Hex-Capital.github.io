# Almanac -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Kushagra Chitkara’s reported 12-hour and 16-hour Codex runs make Almanac an organic tool with unproven hacker ancestry. Almanac appears to come from a problem the founders actually experienced, which is the strongest signal here. But Almanac’s three technical résumés contain no named pre-company artifact, open-source contribution, or hacker-community trail. The product looks more organic than the founders look animal.

**Dropbox** is the high rung for organic ideas: Drew Houston kept losing his USB stick, built what he needed, and showed the artifact before selling the vision. Almanac shares that mechanism because Chitkara was running Codex for 12-plus hours and Almanac preserves the context such sessions lose. The divergence is important. Dropbox’s pain was explicitly personal and its demo attracted users; Almanac has 743 GitHub stars and five displayed engineering users, but the dossier never establishes which founder first built Almanac for which repository after which concrete failure. Almanac is close to organic, but not yet canonical.

**Loopt** is the warning on hacker genealogy: Sam Altman’s animal force overrode the weak evidence that mobile infrastructure or a preexisting hacker artifact was ready. Almanac presents a similar gap without yet presenting the override. Rohan Sharma has ML research experience at the Vector Institute, CMU, and MIT, and Divit Sheth was a senior software engineer at Arcesium, but no founder has a public repository or named technical artifact predating Almanac. Almanac’s 843-commit repository proves someone can ship; it does not yet prove the founders had the hacker gene before deciding to start it.

Almanac’s 843 commits, 743 stars, 68 forks, PyPI release, and five displayed engineering users are tracks in the snow. Over 18 months, animal resourcefulness would turn those tracks into a tight loop: watch where agents forget context, manually repair each failure, and convert the repaired workflow into software. Almanac can compound because repository-local Markdown makes every customer correction product feedback. But stars are not cereal boxes, five logos are not revenue, and the dossier contains no rejected customer won through an unscalable stunt. Almanac has shipped before pitching; Almanac has not yet shown it refuses to take no for an answer.

**Cognition’s DeepWiki** exposes the counterfactual on schlep blindness. Almanac would score much higher if Chitkara, Sharma, and Sheth were personally migrating hostile monorepos, passing enterprise security reviews, reconstructing incident history, and reconciling contradictory documentation for customers. Almanac currently offers local indexing, Git-reviewable Markdown, privacy-preserving telemetry, and a daily “garden” process. Those are useful inconveniences, but still mostly software inconveniences. If Almanac embraces the enterprise migration and knowledge-cleaning grind that Cognition would rather absorb into Devin, the schlep could become the asset.

**Airbnb** shows why three founders can be more than three résumés: Chesky, Gebbia, and Blecharczyk had complementary abilities and enough shared history to survive an idea everyone disliked. Almanac has the right number—Kushagra Chitkara, Rohan Sharma, and Divit Sheth—and Chitkara and Sharma overlapped at Harvard from 2023 to 2025. Almanac does not document how Sheth joined, what the three built together before Almanac, or whether their skills divide cleanly across product, systems, and selling. The configuration is promising, but the relationship is only partly evidenced.

Five displayed engineering users give Almanac enough of a wedge that my 36-month forecast is falsifiable: Almanac will sell a repository-local agent-memory and governance product to engineering organizations and reach $2 million to $5 million in ARR by July 2029. Almanac’s mechanism is not documentation alone. The Apache-2.0 CLI attracts developers, while private deployment, conversation retention, review controls, and cross-agent history become the paid product. The organic pain is real enough to create adoption; whether Almanac becomes large depends on learning to love the security and migration schlep.

Cognition’s $492 million run-rate revenue is the strongest case against that forecast. DeepWiki, Devin Search, Codex, or Claude Code could absorb persistent memory, while Almanac’s Apache-2.0 Markdown format creates little lock-in. Almanac also triggers an unstated-pattern concern: none of the founders has a visible preexisting GitHub trail, and 743 stars can be launch attention rather than durable use. The evidence keeping me on the invest side is that Almanac already has 843 commits, 68 forks, five named users, and a neutral repository format that can span competing agents. **Loopt** is the known exception showing that incomplete hacker evidence need not kill a bet, though Almanac has not yet shown Altman-level force. Fewer than 25 paying engineering organizations or less than $500,000 ARR by January 2028 would flip me to Pass.
