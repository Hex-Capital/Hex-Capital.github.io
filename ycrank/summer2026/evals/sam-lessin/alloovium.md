# Alloovium -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot underwrite the deal before I can see the deal. Alloovium received US$500,000 with YC acceptance, but the dossier gives no post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is the first scarce check. The funded construction-AI landscape—Trunk Tools at $70 million raised, Document Crunch acquired by Trimble, and Nomic at $17 million—also says the category is recognized, even if Alloovium itself is not being auctioned. Financing silence is not an active red flag, but it earns only the default.

The proposed venture moment is similarly incomplete. A US Innovation Partner Program aimed at head contractors and ENR Top 400 firms suggests a useful experiment: land one contractor, then reach the roughly 50 subcontractors it encounters annually. But the dossier never says the YC proceeds fund that experiment, what conversion or usage threshold constitutes success, or when the card flips. “Expand into the US” is a direction; it is not yet a finite, truth-revealing capital plan.

The strongest evidence is inside the workflow. Alloovium is deployed across more than $400 million of active projects, including a BMD subsidiary, and is testing ISO compliance with a 30-year auditor. Sentence-level citations, read-only integrations, correction memory, and mandatory human approval are sensible architecture for a liability-sensitive industry. This is materially better than a generic document chatbot. Still, deployment value is not customer value: no named purchaser, paid conversion, renewal, or retained-use figure shows that the accumulated workflow has caused a purchase. The reported 10× professional growth lacks its starting base, while 1,936 LinkedIn followers prove attention rather than economic behavior.

The team is the cleanest thesis fit. Zander Schweitzer has civil-engineering and site-operating exposure; Cielo Nicolosi brings ML work and four founding-engineer roles; Mitchell Harmer adds an exited construction-software founder’s access. Together they can translate between contractors and software builders instead of merely selling AI into a sector they discovered last month. The limitation is depth: Zander graduated in 2025, Cielo’s prior operating outcomes are unspecified, and the dossier does not establish who has final authority when product, liability, and financing choices collide.

The bull case is that this resembles the mechanism that justified the AI exception in Ando: enterprise software embedded in a measurable operating workflow rather than model novelty sold as a moat. The material difference stings. Ando had greater than 90% forecast accuracy, 100% pilot-to-paid conversion, and 100% retention; Alloovium discloses none of those receipts. Meanwhile, Procore, Autodesk, Aconex, and SharePoint control important inputs, while well-capitalized competitors already overlap retrieval, drafting, compliance, and drawing intelligence. Organization-specific corrections may become a proprietary data pool, but today that is a hypothesis sitting atop rented systems.

Advance invoicing, automatic renewal, and the absence of a default free trial are encouraging contractual choices, not evidence of cash economics. There is no disclosed revenue, price, gross margin, burn, runway, or route to break-even without another round. My 36-month base case is that Alloovium remains below US$3 million ARR and concentrated in Australian contractor-compliance workflows rather than becoming a project-wide US system of record. I pass with my own money today. The verifiable flip condition is a sub-US$10 million post-money round explicitly funding one named ENR Top 400 deployment that converts to a paid renewal by August 2027.
