# Covera -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Covera earns zero on building blocks because it uses AI to process insurance submissions and supplies no scarce input to an AI workload. It also earns zero on industry enablement. The old workflow requires brokers to chase documents and re-enter risk data across agency systems and carrier portals. Covera automates parts of that work and keeps agency approval in the loop. The memo shows time savings on existing work and gives no concrete example of a buyer, risk class, or quoting frequency that became economically viable. Both path scores are zero, which triggers Pass. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Carrier mappings, agency-specific workflows, and read/write integrations with agency management systems give Covera a product that could remain useful under model parity. Covera reports connections to more than 100 carrier portals. Reusing those mappings could reduce onboarding work, but carriers can change their screens, and Broker Buddha offers overlapping intake and rating. The memo provides no evidence of retention or pricing power under comparable functionality. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

Hello Risk reported 155 tasks in two weeks through a Covera case study, an early signal of repeated use. The memo also records 174 extension users from a third-party tracker. Paid conversion and sustained use remain unverified. Both founders worked at a brokerage and previously built Simio together. Covera’s live workflow shows relevant delivery, while dependable handling of portal changes and extraction errors remains unproven. Without realized pricing or measured costs for review, corrections, support, and portal maintenance, scale economics stays at the default. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

If manual preparation costs lead small agencies to decline low-value submissions, Covera might make those submissions worth quoting. Hello Risk estimated ten hours saved per week, and Covera reports that Concorde Assurance cut preparation time per client response by 80%. Those reports make the labor-saving mechanism plausible. Neither identifies submissions that an agency previously declined on cost grounds and can now quote economically, so the countercase does not clear either path gate. (Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid agency pilot show that Covera makes a class of submissions the agency previously declined on cost grounds profitable to quote? Compare the fully loaded cost per accurate, agency-approved quote under the manual and Covera workflows, including review and corrections. Repeat paid use on that class, with the agency relying on Covera’s carrier and agency-system integrations to complete the work, would move the action to Reach out. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)
