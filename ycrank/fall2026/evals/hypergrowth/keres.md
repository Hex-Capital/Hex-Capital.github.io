# Keres -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with U.S. military buyers, Keres’s strongest path is to sell a standard Garmur configuration to multiple defense customers through repeatable government procurement. The memo documents a U.K. purchase of a comparable interceptor, which shows that the same buyer need exists abroad. A common airframe could serve that job across markets, though the adaptation and approval work remains uncertain. Keres’s accelerator award establishes access to U.S. acquisition officials, and the Counter-UAS Marketplace offers a potential channel. The ordinary path to purchase and deployment still appears buyer-led and high-touch. The memo provides no usable evidence of paid deployment, commercial growth, or confirmed pre-revenue status. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Keres says Garmur is designed to recover after an intercept and fly again. If recovery is reliable and turnaround is inexpensive, reuse could lower the buyer’s cost per intercept and support follow-on fleet orders. Under an illustrative hardware-sale model, Keres’s incremental contribution would be the order price less production, attributable sales and onboarding, included support, and any additional capacity required. The memo supplies no price or supplier cost inputs for that bridge, and it leaves recurring billing and paid account expansion unestablished. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Keres could win an initial U.S. customer and still face separate qualification, integration, and support work for each new buyer. The memo’s concrete access comes through a U.S. defense accelerator, and its core reuse claim lacks a documented intercept-and-recovery demonstration. If repeat deployments require substantial bespoke work or costly support that scales with every order, the growth path could remain constrained even with strong initial fit. The memo leaves the structural downside gate open because the extent and cost of that work are unresolved. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a second defense customer in a different market pay for and accept substantially the same Garmur configuration with bounded qualification, integration, and support costs? A paid deployment with documented adaptation effort and positive incremental contribution would prompt rescoring for Reach out. Evidence that each market requires substantial rebuilding with no credible near-term transfer path would change the action to Pass.
