# Bayworks -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Bayworks’ strongest route is a strategic sale to a dealership service software platform such as Snap-on’s Dealer-FX. Bayworks captures technician dictation and repair evidence against an assigned repair order, then prepares a manufacturer-formatted warranty narrative for writeback. Dealer-FX’s service-lane and shop-management products give it a place to distribute that workflow. The acquisition rationale is an inference. A buyer could use Bayworks to recover billable technician time and reduce warranty claim rework, but the memo leaves both benefits unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The shared benchmark assumes a \$500,000 check at a \$10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3% with no follow-ons. The holder needs \$2.5 million in gross cash within eight years. Snap-on’s \$200 million purchase of Dealer-FX, net of acquired cash, shows that a strategic buyer has paid above the benchmark for an adjacent asset. Dealer-FX’s broader product scope limits what that price establishes for Bayworks’ warranty workflow. (Market & Traction)

For the two sale scenarios, assume a full cash sale within eight years, \$5 million in combined net debt and other deductions from enterprise value, and a pro rata payout at close with no preference or escrow reducing the holder’s share. These are scenario assumptions.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Gross holder cash |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | \$50M enterprise value less \$5M in deductions leaves \$45M in distributable equity | \$1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | \$100M enterprise value less \$5M in deductions leaves \$95M in distributable equity | \$2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share purchase or payout | \$0 |

The hurdle is about \$83.3 million in distributable equity, or \$88.3 million in enterprise value under these assumptions. At 2.5% ownership, even the strong sale yields \$2.375 million (4.75×). The memo supplies an adjacent transaction but leaves Bayworks’ realizable sale price and holder terms unverified. It supports no independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The operating milestone is paid renewals across multiple dealer groups with measured technician time saved and fewer warranty files returned for missing details. The memo records a PBS extension and a named Harvey Auto Group testimonial. Bayworks reports six dealership groups, more than 50,000 repairs, and nearly seven figures in contracted annual recurring revenue. Those signals make a multi-group test plausible, while the live paying share, runway, and cost of reaching renewals remain unclear. A software sale is plausible, but continued repair-order access and permission to retain or reuse dealer-owned data after a sale remain unverified. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

A strategic buyer could obtain much of the functionality elsewhere. Wrench Voice already offers technician dictation, structured warranty documentation, and dealership-system integrations. Unless Bayworks shows better results across paying dealerships, a buyer has little reason to pay a price that clears the holder’s hurdle. Dealer ownership and deletion rights also weaken any premium based on cross-dealer learning. These are credible risks, but the memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a price ceiling below the proceeds hurdle. The Pass gate is therefore untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a dealership service platform’s acquisition team underwrite a price above the roughly \$88 million enterprise-value hurdle based on Bayworks’ results at renewing, paying dealerships? Buyer-side estimates of the value of technician time saved and reduced claim rework, weighed against the cost of building or licensing a substitute, would answer it. A credible range that clears the holder’s cash objective after deal adjustments would move the action to Reach out. A supported ceiling materially below that objective on the strongest route would move it to Pass.
