# Mirrors -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Production agents create a buyer for behavior correction: engineering teams that keep patching global prompts after inspecting failed traces. Mirrors becomes a large company if reviewed, conditional guidance reduces the engineering cost of maintaining reliable agents enough to support a separate infrastructure budget. That is the controlling variable. The dossier establishes the recurring work and a concrete delivery mechanism, but supplies no measurement of engineering time saved or correction effectiveness. I see a plausible market mechanism, with insufficient evidence to underwrite it.

The strongest argument for investing is the product architecture. Mirrors integrates at the user-turn boundary, lets engineers preview matches, and provides shadow and off modes. These are documented pieces of an operational workflow, which earn meaningful credit on durability. Stronger models could make customer-specific guidance more useful while leaving review and deployment controls valuable. The limitation is how much of that workflow Mirrors owns. Its SDK explicitly distinguishes an accepted server decision from context application or agent success. The documented 1,000-millisecond context-wait budget also creates a tradeoff: better targeting must justify another runtime dependency. The control layer exists; ownership of the completed improvement loop remains unproven.

The market evidence does not yet establish an economic discontinuity. LangChain reports production-agent adoption rising from 51% to 57.3%, which supports a growing population of potential buyers. It does not quantify how much those buyers spend correcting behavior or how Mirrors changes that cost. Custom pricing supplies no additional economic evidence. The website’s 711,933 agent sessions deserve limited demand credit, but the missing measurement period and uncertain allocation between the older replay product and current context product prevent treating that figure as repeat adoption of this offering. I would leave the economics dimension at its absent-evidence default and put demand only slightly above its default.

Market structure makes the distinction consequential. Papaya approaches the same correction budget through optimization pull requests; Mem0 approaches context through persistent memory. Mirrors has a distinct implementation, but a separate implementation does not establish a separate enduring budget. My pattern of investing across layers of a technology shift supports examining this company; it does not establish that conditional routing will remain an independent layer. Conversely, the reviewed SDK workflow is affirmative evidence against dismissing Mirrors as an output formatter. The unresolved question is whether its controls accumulate enough operational responsibility that a framework feature or internal implementation becomes an inadequate substitute.

Aryan Singhal’s language-model evaluation research is relevant technical preparation. Profine also establishes prior building experience, but the dossier documents neither specific startup lessons nor costly commitment, so the founder-motivation score stays at its default. The research-stage exception does not change my decision: this record does not establish scarce frontier-model capability or a capital path that substitutes for commercial evidence. There is also no documented fundraising-status or secondary-liquidity red flag. I would pass on a personal check today because the market gates remain unmet. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: a paid customer’s controlled 90-day deployment showing at least a 30% reduction in engineer hours spent correcting agent behavior, with task success maintained and latency inside that customer’s existing service target.
