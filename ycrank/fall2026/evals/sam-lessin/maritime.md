# Maritime -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Maritime has built something concrete, but I cannot price the check. Post-money valuation: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. Two Lanterns has already invested, so this is not demonstrably the first scarce check either. None of that establishes an auction; it leaves my primary gate unanswered. The dossier also supplies no capital-funded experiment with a deadline and a measurable result. Hosting billions of agents is an ambition. What finite piece of uncertainty would my money resolve?

The product makes the economics worth investigating. Persistent storage, encrypted secrets, and snapshot-based sleep/wake address an actual operating problem. At $100 a month for 100 agent slots, Maritime is effectively selling the economics of mostly sleeping computers. Concurrency limits and resource add-ons give the team mechanisms to manage consumption, but a price list does not establish that those mechanisms produce profit. I need the cash contribution from a retained customer after compute, storage, and support. There is no evidence of negative economics here, and equally no documented route from this seed to a business that survives without another round.

The strongest argument against passing is that these founders have already inhabited both sides of the problem. Maria Gorskikh and Aryaman Shrivastava report hosting hundreds of research agents at MIT NANDA; they have now shipped the managed infrastructure, and Maria’s Open Instinct project actually uses it. That is materially better than academic credentials attached to an infrastructure pitch. Maritime fits my unstated preference for founders translating between systems they have operated: agent builders want persistent computers, while infrastructure operators must ration expensive capacity. Flat subscriptions could make that translation commercially useful. This is also more substantial than an application whose entire advantage disappears with the next model release.

What I cannot find is the proprietary access that turns that competence into an owned business. Open Instinct’s 416 stars could become a distribution wedge, but the dossier does not connect them to independent customers adopting or purchasing Maritime. Product Hunt followers and estimated visits do not close that gap. Meanwhile, Docker announced managed microVM sandboxes on September 24, 2026. Maritime therefore needs evidence that its persistent workflow wins customer commitment despite an established vendor entering overlapping infrastructure. Stored files and secrets might eventually help retention; their existence today does not prove it.

I pass with my own money. The known exceptions do not rescue the deal: the dossier establishes neither exceptional commercial conversion, verified cash economics, nor a financing structure that makes repeated fundraising optional. Those are absent receipts, not evidence that a three-person pre-seed team has failed. My verifiable flip condition is an executable round below $10 million post-money, supported by retained-customer economics showing that its proceeds fund a specific twelve-month path to cash break-even. That would connect the team’s demonstrated operating insight to an investable price and a capital plan I can underwrite.
