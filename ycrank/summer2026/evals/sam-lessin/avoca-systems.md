# Avoca Systems -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I first write down three facts: proposed post-money unknown, competing-term-sheet count unknown, first scarce check unproven. That is not evidence of an auction, but it is not evidence that I am buying neglected uncertainty cheaply. Avoca Systems may be a good operating company; without terms, I cannot yet tell whether it is a good seed investment.

The product has crossed a much more meaningful threshold than the usual healthcare-AI demo. Avoca is live with several radiology networks, including groups operating more than 150 clinics. It reports 90% lower call abandonment, 86% call resolution, 4.6/5 satisfaction, and 30 bookings converted within half a day. These are customer actions inside an embedded workflow, not followers or integrations masquerading as demand. Clinic-specific rules and real-time RIS/EHR write-back make the product operationally consequential.

The strongest bull case is that this is the rare AI application whose base business already works. The Ando exception shares the mechanism—measured enterprise workflow improvement—but the material difference is decisive: Avoca discloses neither paid conversion and retention nor revenue, gross margin, burn, or runway. Bookings create value for clinics, but the dossier never shows how much of that value Avoca captures or whether human escalation and integration work leave attractive contribution economics.

I can infer a possible venture moment: establish U.S.-hosted infrastructure, integrate the missing American systems, and prove that the Australian deployment model transfers. But the round does not actually name that experiment, its deadline, or a pass/fail metric. “Expand into the U.S.” is strategy, not a truth-revealing use of capital. The Australia-only data architecture makes this a real technical and regulatory test, yet resemblance to an experiment earns no credit until the founders commit the check to it.

There is a genuine translation layer between radiology operations and legacy clinical software. Shand has shipped before—first employee at Afterpay, founder and seller of Serenade—and Mercer-Butcher has led engineering in insurance and property software. What is missing is prior radiology operating experience on either side. Avoca’s live deployments partially close that gap, but the dark pool remains uncertain: Epic, Oracle Health, MEDITECH, and RIS integrations are valuable access today, while openDoctor, AbbaDox, installed vendors, and cross-specialty agents can contest the same workflow. The accumulated exception data could become proprietary; the dossier does not show that it already has.

The obvious red flags do not fully fire. Avoca is not selling MAUs, and human escalation acknowledges that clinical errors compound rather than magically disappear. But revenue silence prevents the operating metrics from becoming cash evidence, and the dossier does not identify who has final authority between the founders. My 36-month base case is that Avoca reaches $4–8 million ARR across Australian and U.S. radiology networks but remains a useful vertical workflow vendor rather than a defensible operating system.

I pass today. The single flip condition is a signed sub-$10 million post-money financing plan dedicating this check to one dated test: deploy U.S.-hosted Avoca with named networks and reach $1 million ARR before the existing runway ends. That would establish scarce pricing, a finite venture moment, and cash conversion in one verifiable document.
