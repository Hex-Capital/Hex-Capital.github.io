# Luca IQ -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I need—post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and the amount of genuinely scarce capital—are absent. That is not evidence of an auction, but it means I cannot underwrite the deal. The capital plan is equally undefined: Luca IQ does not name a dated experiment, measurable pass/fail threshold, or the truth this round uniquely finances. Federal 1040 authorization is a meaningful completed milestone; it is not a use-of-proceeds plan.

The product itself is more serious than the usual AI tax wrapper. Human review and AI determine inputs, but a deterministic rules engine calculates the return, preserves a source-level audit trail, and transmits through IRS Modernized e-File. Passing Assurance Testing in July 2026 and completing a tax season with customers prove that the team shipped through a regulated boundary. Angelo Policicchio brings CPA, EY, and FBI forensic-accounting experience; Akash Sadashivapeth brings engineering and data-science leadership. This is a real translation layer between tax practice and software infrastructure.

The strongest bull case is the operating evidence. One customer reports 1,100 hours saved, a 57% reduction in administrative preparation time, and a 30-minute path from intake to review-ready; hundreds of firms applied for the 2026 pilot. Those are substantially better signals than followers or integrations. They show that Luca IQ’s workflow caused observed customer action and produced measurable value. The AI risk is also bounded by deterministic calculations, diagnostics, and an audit trail rather than a claim that model errors will disappear at scale.

But the commercial dark pool remains unproven. Luca IQ discloses no paid-customer count, revenue, retention, pricing, gross margin, burn, or runway. Hundreds of applicants are demand for access, not purchases, and the current distribution plan includes cold calling by a sales intern. Meanwhile April and Column Tax already offer authorized filing infrastructure, while Filed and TaxGPT can own adjacent preparation and review workflows. Firm-specific data and integrations may eventually create switching costs, but resemblance to a moat is not a moat.

My 36-month base case is that Luca IQ becomes a strategically useful product and is acquired by Aiwyn before reaching $5 million ARR, rather than building an independently distributed tax platform. The founders have shown they can ship regulated infrastructure; they have not yet shown that ownership of the workflow produces durable paid conversion or a business that survives without another round. The verifiable condition that flips me to invest is 20 CPA firms signing paid annual contracts after their pilots, with disclosed positive contribution margin.

I am passing today. This may be a good company, but I cannot buy an unspecified round funding an unspecified experiment on the strength of efficiency metrics alone. Give me the price and show that the completed tax season converts into controllable cash, and the decision changes quickly.
