# Primordiant -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a lab instrument or automation vendor is Primordiant’s strongest plausible liquidity route. Primordiant says its robots can adapt experiments as results come in. A vendor could buy that capability to extend its installed instruments into workflows where procedures change often. Bruker’s investment in Atinary shows strategic interest in instrument-linked automation. Primordiant’s buyer rationale still depends on proving that its robots produce repeatable savings across instruments and protocols. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Primordiant reports taking a prototype to a client’s wet lab and running a separate polymer coatings engagement in its own lab. Those engagements make a paid, multi-site production test plausible. The memo leaves the funding, hardware costs, integration effort, and field support needed for that test unquantified. A sale centered on the robotics product appears feasible in principle. IP ownership and contract assignment remain unverified, and the published terms require customer opt-in or written agreement for model training on customer content. The memo gives no basis to underwrite an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale scenarios assume closing within eight years, eligibility to sell the entire stake, full cash consideration, and a pro rata waterfall with no senior preference diverting proceeds.

| Scenario | Assumed bridge | Holder cash |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40 million enterprise value less $5 million of debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million of distributable equity | $1.05 million (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value less $10 million of debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million of distributable equity | $2.70 million (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no funded payout or eligible share purchase within eight years | $0 realized |

At the strong-case price, receiving 80% of the allocation in cash and rolling the rest would lower realized proceeds to $2.16 million (4.32×). The sale prices are sensitivity assumptions. Published pricing shows beta billing is disabled, and the memo supplies no acquisition pricing or buyer economics that would support a realizable proceeds range. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

Instrument vendors can partner for adjacent capabilities. Thermo Fisher’s NVIDIA collaboration and Bruker’s Atinary relationship give potential buyers alternatives. If Primordiant requires substantial site-specific integration and supervision, a vendor may see little reason to own it. The reported engagements and remotely controlled demo leave cross-protocol autonomy unverified. This supports Watch because repeatability and buyer economics are testable gaps, while a structural block or materially inadequate proceeds under the strongest credible route remain unestablished. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What would a lab instrument vendor pay to own Primordiant after seeing it run across customer sites? A paid production test that measures savings after integration and supervision costs, paired with a buyer’s valuation rationale supporting at least $83.3 million in distributable cash equity within eight years, would move the action to Reach out.
