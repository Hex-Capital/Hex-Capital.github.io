# Amorphic Labs -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. A buyer should be able to enter a software vendor’s world populated with her own organization, data shape, permissions, and likely workflow—without waiting for a sales engineer to build the scene. The path there requires reliable computer use, deterministic data seeding, safe credential handling, and generated experiences buyers trust. Amorphic Labs points toward that future, but its current artifact is still a narrated video. Public computer-use APIs from 2024–2025 are a small leak from the future; the dossier does not show that Amorphic has crossed the remaining reliability or trust gaps.

The present wound is real but shallowly specified. Sales teams spend hours preparing environments and recording personalized demos, and Amorphic would return some of that time by researching prospects, operating the application, and editing the result. But why does this work remain expensive? Who captures the rent? Which organizational rule prevents salespeople from producing demos themselves? The dossier names labor without quantifying it and does not identify an artificial gatekeeper. This looks like workflow compression, not yet the transfer of economic or occupational control I find most powerful.

Frank Li is the strongest evidence in the company. An OpenAI MTS and project lead with NVIDIA ML-infrastructure experience has paid real technical tuition in the machinery Amorphic needs. Dylan Kelly adds software, quantitative, and published AI-research ability, while their decade-long relationship across mathematics, debate, business, and hackathons is more informative than two strangers assembled for YC. Still, I cannot find the first-believer act: no dated pre-company demo obsession, shipped sales tool, customer wound, or proof that either founder repeatedly built around this problem before it became a fashionable agent application. The CEO and CTO roles are legible, but the dossier does not disclose control or a contractual tiebreaker.

The product’s legal documents describe agents clicking through connected applications and handling cookies, session state, passwords, prospect emails, screenshots, and model traces. That suggests a serious architecture has at least been contemplated, but terms and privacy policies are not evidence of a working artifact. There are no disclosed customers, repeat sessions, completed videos, revenue, or named design partners. Worse, bearer-link demos require no authentication, and the company admits agent actions may be inaccurate or incomplete. For a product entrusted with prospect data and live application credentials, consent and custody are not cleanup work; they are part of the product.

The sharp bull case is that these founders are unusually capable of solving exactly those problems. Operating and populating the real application is materially different from routing prerecorded segments or customizing a captured front end. If correction histories and workflow traces begin compounding across deployments, Amorphic could develop a reliability asset that Consensus, Reprise, and Walnut cannot bolt on casually despite their combined hundreds of millions in funding. But today that asset is inferred, while the dependency on third-party computer-use models is real. No cross-customer learning, controlled lower-stack bottleneck, or demonstrated reliability advantage appears in the dossier. The precedent for overlooking missing revenue would require a functioning prototype, signed proofs of concept, and a founder obsession that predates the market; Amorphic has not supplied those facts.

I expect the team’s technical ability to outlast this initial product, but I expect Amorphic to pivot within 36 months from personalized sales videos toward browser-agent QA or demo-environment operations before reaching $1 million ARR. That is a respectable company-building path, but it is not enough for me to invest my own money today. The one condition that flips me is verifiable repeated behavior: three paying software vendors each generating at least 50 customer-approved demos per month for three consecutive months, with at least 90% distributed without manual correction. That would turn the privacy documents and future data moat from narrated possibilities into a living artifact.
