# ChemPilot.ai -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032: a defense manufacturer loses a sole-source material supplier and qualifies a replacement without restarting a decade of chemistry. That would return real control to the manufacturer. ChemPilot.ai’s proposed sequence—generate molecules, rank synthesizability, simulate properties, then synthesize—offers a plausible route toward that world. But the dossier does not show a molecule traveling that route successfully. I can imagine the destination; I cannot yet identify the small experimental artifact that tells me it has begun arriving.

The founders are the strongest reason to keep looking. Two years of shared research in Alon Grinberg Dana’s Technion laboratory precede the company, and his computational-chemistry work includes co-authoring [ARC](https://github.com/ReactionMechanismGenerator/ARC). That is actual technical work, beyond a collection of impressive affiliations. Ofir Simhi adds experience shipping software at Skiff and StoreMaven; Sam Cogan brings biochemical-engineering training. Together, these support an independently developed research bet. They do not yet establish repeated commercial execution or how the academic team turns capital into qualified materials. I give the founders substantial credit without treating their former employers’ acquisitions as their own exits.

Why does replacing a material take over a decade? How much is discovering the molecule, how much is manufacturing it consistently, and how much is qualification? ChemPilot identifies sole-source dependence and a long development cycle, but does not separate those costs. That distinction determines whether its software removes the bottleneck. Faster candidate generation could leave the buyer waiting at precisely the same qualification gate. The agency returned is potentially meaningful—more sourcing choices—but the dossier has not identified the supplier rent or demonstrated how much of the delay ChemPilot can remove.

The strongest argument against passing is that this is exactly where early conviction should precede revenue: a technically grounded team, prior defense research relationships, and a difficult physical problem. My pre-revenue exception for **CosmicBrain AI** rested on a similar combination of specialized founders and physical enablement; the material difference was a functioning prototype and signed industrial proofs of concept. ChemPilot’s reported collaborations do not establish either a company product being used or a commercial commitment. Fresh Defense’s October 2026 backing adds another believer, but it cannot substitute for that evidence. Missing revenue is perfectly ordinary here. Missing evidence of what the system has actually accomplished is what limits my conviction.

ChemPilot also touches my pattern of backing concealed infrastructure beneath a visible wave. Materials could enable many downstream products, but the dossier establishes a defense focus without establishing control of a scarce enabling asset. The originating laboratory publishes tools; reusable experimental-data rights and company-specific IP remain undisclosed. Open source is compatible with an excellent business, provided something compounds inside the company. Otherwise ChemPilot could become a capable discovery contractor whose learning belongs to each customer. CuspAI’s reported funding above $650 million makes me ask what ChemPilot can uniquely accumulate, rather than assume fundraising settles the competition. I see no documented privacy violation, concealed leadership, or dismissive claim that the operating complexity is trivial; those red flags do not drive this pass.

I would pass with my own money today while keeping the team close. My one verifiable flip condition is a repeat paid order from a named defense customer for a ChemPilot-designed, experimentally qualified material, with contractual rights allowing ChemPilot to reuse the resulting learning. That would show a customer crossing from research interest into dependence—and establish what remains inside the company after the project ends.
