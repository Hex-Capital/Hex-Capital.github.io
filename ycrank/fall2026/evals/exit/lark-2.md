# Lark -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic sale to a distributor ERP or supply-planning vendor. A buyer could add purchasing automation that combines data across ERP, CRM, email, and files and writes approved orders back into the ERP. Interline Hardware reported a 20% reduction in inventory value and a drop in weekly reorder work from 70 to 10 team hours. Lark separately described the saving as 60 hours per month, so the labor benefit needs verification. The reported inventory reduction gives a buyer a reason to investigate whether it could deliver similar results across its customer base. Growth investments in Conexiom and Blue Ridge show investor interest in adjacent workflows. The memo supplies no acquisition prices or buyer interest in Lark. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

A milestone that would strengthen the sale case is repeatable paid deployment across multiple ERP systems, with customer-verified savings and measured implementation costs. Interline reportedly received its first recommendations in under a month, and Lark reports that most setups take one to two months. Those timelines make further deployments plausible. The memo leaves paid conversion, implementation staffing, and the capital needed to scale unverified. It also leaves IP and integration rights, customer consent requirements, and holder participation terms unverified. There is no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. The illustrative sale cases assume an all-cash close within that period, $5 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, and an eligible holder cashing out its full stake pro rata with no preference or escrow reducing the payout.

| Scenario | Distributable equity | Cash to holder |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------|
| Modest sale at $40 million enterprise value | $35 million | $1.05 million (2.1×) |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | $95 million | $2.85 million (5.7×) |
| Remains private and reinvests cash, with no share sale or distribution | No equity proceeds within eight years | $0 |

The holder needs about $83.3 million in distributable equity, or $88.3 million in enterprise value under the assumed deductions. At 2% ownership, even the strong sale yields $1.9 million. The memo provides no revenue, transaction price, or quantified buyer economics to support either sale value. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Epicor has already expanded native workflow automation in Prophet 21, an ERP Lark integrates with. If native tools cover enough of the purchasing workflow, an ERP vendor could build the remaining features and avoid acquiring Lark. Customer-specific mapping also raises the possibility that each deployment requires substantial services work, reducing the value of rolling Lark out across a buyer’s customer base. The memo establishes overlap and implementation risk. It leaves open whether native automation can match Lark’s cross-system workflow or whether a credible sale would fall short of the holder’s hurdle. Neither Pass gate is established. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a distributor ERP or supply-planning vendor justify an acquisition price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? A buyer’s build-versus-buy analysis grounded in customer-verified results and deployment costs across multiple paid installations would answer it. A credible range clearing the hurdle would move the action to Reach out.
