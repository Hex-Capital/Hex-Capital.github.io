# Glen -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what broke. Nikos Dritsakos has shipped Glen, previously sold SalesBop for $500,000, and worked at Composio. Those facts establish competence. They do not establish the more important sequence: a named obstacle, his workaround, and the observable change it caused. I cannot substitute résumé evidence for resourcefulness. On the controlling founder test, Glen receives the exact missing-evidence default.

The strongest case for investing is the amount one solo founder has already assembled. Glen is in production, captures work across agent sessions and workplace systems, supplies context to prompts, and distills recurring methods into skills. Three named organizations—Amorphic Labs, Zaplar, and Composio—provide testimonials. This is substantially better than an architecture diagram or a plan to begin after funding. But the dossier gives no corpus size, retrieval benchmark, workflow frequency, or independently measured improvement. It proves a functioning artifact, not yet a concrete proof spike.

The user evidence has the same shape. Wave-based onboarding and founder-led calls are the right way to start; they let Dritsakos learn personally rather than delegate sales. Yet a testimonial does not reveal whether anyone comes back tomorrow. The dossier supplies no payment, retention, repeated usage, referrals, or example of a user forcing Glen into an unplanned workflow. This matters especially because Dust reports 3,000 organizations, 51,000 monthly active users, and 300,000 deployed agents. In an overlapping market, “we also built organizational context” is not the proof. The proof would be a narrow group that cannot work without Glen.

There is nevertheless a plausible organic origin. Dritsakos worked inside Composio and previously built an AI sales-coaching company, so fragmented agent and organizational context is closer to a problem encountered through work than a generic “AI for enterprise” brainstorm. Glen also accepts real schleps: Slack and GitHub integrations, permissions, private sessions, stale memories, source verification, and sensitive data passing through OpenAI, Vercel, Neon, and Inngest. But the origin story remains inferred, and there is no dated example of customer resistance causing him to revise the product. The mission may be persistent; the running-back evidence is absent.

My strongest objection to passing is that the public dossier may simply lag the company. A repeat founder with a prior exit, direct agent-tooling experience, a production product, and three named users could already possess the missing usage data privately. That is a real possibility, but it is not a known-exception override: the available evidence shows neither unmistakable pull nor measured technical performance strong enough to replace the failed resourcefulness gate. My base case is that Glen either narrows within 36 months into a specific developer-facing context or skill-capture workflow, or remains below $2 million ARR while broader platforms absorb the general feature set. One fact flips me: a named customer using Glen at least weekly for eight consecutive weeks, together with a specific failure that Dritsakos personally turned into a shipped product change during that period.
