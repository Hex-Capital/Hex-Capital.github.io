## The Idea

- **Problem:** Factory, warehouse, and fleet planners use spreadsheets or packaged planning software and may have to rebuild production, staffing, or delivery plans by hand after equipment failures, delays, or order changes ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/W3c-opera-the-ai-decision-engine-for-factories-warehouses-and-fleets)).
- **Approach:** OpEra connects to warehouse management systems, ERPs, and Excel, builds a mathematical model of site-specific resources and rules, and reruns the optimizer when a planner enters a new constraint in plain language ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/W3c-opera-the-ai-decision-engine-for-factories-warehouses-and-fleets)).
- **Differentiation:** OpEra builds and deploys site-specific models across planning, scheduling, allocation, and routing, while Timefold sells routing and workforce-scheduling APIs, Kardinal specializes in last-mile delivery, and Imperia packages demand, procurement, and production planning into SaaS modules ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/W3c-opera-the-ai-decision-engine-for-factories-warehouses-and-fleets), [Timefold](https://timefold.ai/blog/timefold-raises-series-a), [Kardinal](https://kardinal.ai/kardinal-raises-e10m-to-optimize-last-mile-logistics-in-europe/), [Burda Principal Investments](https://www.burdaprincipalinvestments.com/2025/02/18/bpi-imperia-series-a-european-expansion/)).
- **Business Model:** Website not accessible at time of research. [Inferred]: OpEra’s published sequence of backtesting, piloting, integration, and ongoing monitoring could support implementation fees and recurring support fees ([OpEra website](https://operatechnologies.com/), via search snippet).
- **TAM/SAM:** Coherent Market Insights estimates the broader global supply-chain planning software market at $13.30B in 2026, with 11.8% CAGR through 2033 ([Coherent Market Insights](https://www.coherentmarketinsights.com/blog/information-and-communication-technology/supply-chain-planning-software-market-and-spreadsheets-cost-4404), Nov 2026).
- **GTM / Distribution:** The founders solicit introductions to COOs, supply-chain VPs, plant managers, and logistics heads, with a booking link and an offer to deploy at one site ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/W3c-opera-the-ai-decision-engine-for-factories-warehouses-and-fleets), Oct 2026).

## Defensibility

- **Moat today:** OpEra builds site-specific models around customer equipment, staffing, capacity, and rules and integrates those models with existing operational systems ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/W3c-opera-the-ai-decision-engine-for-factories-warehouses-and-fleets)).
- **Future moat:** [Inferred]: Embedded integrations and accumulated site rules could raise switching costs once planners rely on the system in production, with retention and replacement costs still unverified ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/W3c-opera-the-ai-decision-engine-for-factories-warehouses-and-fleets)).
- **Market structure:** No structural barrier identified at this stage.
- **Commoditization risk:** [Inferred]: Timefold’s routing and scheduling APIs give internal engineering teams a starting point for overlapping workflows, with site-specific rules and integrations still requiring implementation ([Timefold](https://timefold.ai/blog/timefold-raises-series-a), [Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/W3c-opera-the-ai-decision-engine-for-factories-warehouses-and-fleets)).

## Key Risks

- **Site integration:** [Inferred]: Customer-specific data mappings and rules can lengthen deployments across sites, with historical backtesting and a live-data pilot available before rollout ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/W3c-opera-the-ai-decision-engine-for-factories-warehouses-and-fleets), [OpEra website](https://operatechnologies.com/), via search snippet).
- **Constraint interpretation:** [Inferred]: A planner’s ambiguous instruction can produce a mathematically feasible schedule that violates an operating rule if the system encodes the instruction incorrectly ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/W3c-opera-the-ai-decision-engine-for-factories-warehouses-and-fleets)).
- **Production validation:** The launch reports outcomes on real datasets, including the founders’ MIT retailer project, and leaves customer identities, deployment volumes, and ongoing production performance undisclosed ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/W3c-opera-the-ai-decision-engine-for-factories-warehouses-and-fleets)).
- **Name collision:** [Inferred]: OpEra shares its spoken name with Opera’s web browser, creating potential ambiguity in branded search and attribution of social or download metrics ([OpEra’s YC directory](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/opera), [Opera browser](https://www.opera.com/)).
