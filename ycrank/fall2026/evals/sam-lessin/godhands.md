# GodHands -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

GodHands could capture something valuable: the exceptions experienced employees know but never write down. The investment question is who owns that value once captured. Customers own the underlying workflows; GodHands must earn a durable place maintaining and applying that knowledge. The proposed post-money, competing offers, and whether my check would actually be scarce are all undisclosed. I use my missing-terms default. YC membership establishes neither cheap entry nor an auction.

The strongest evidence is the support leader’s claim that GodHands documented previously undocumented escalations within two weeks. That is an observed product result, which deserves more weight than its 504 LinkedIn followers. But the anonymous testimonial, available through an indexed website excerpt, establishes neither payment nor repeat dependence. The desktop application might turn privileged workflow access into a useful knowledge asset; the dossier does not yet show that this access causes purchases. Earlier engineering-memory design partners cannot establish conversion for the broader operations product.

The bull case has substance. Sai Vamshi Batchu and Aditya Bommakanti worked together at Graviton, experienced the handover problem, and bring quantitative research, low-latency systems experience, and implemented NeurIPS research. GodHands fits my interest in bridges between systems that struggle to communicate: employees’ tacit judgment on one side, executable organizational knowledge on the other. Those engineering receipts earn some founder credit. They do not establish that the team has operated support organizations or deployed dependable agents inside them. Device-side redaction and application controls address a real adoption obstacle, but advertised safeguards do not establish employee trust.

Competition makes the ownership question consequential. Scribe reports $100 million ARR and already markets workflow context and agents; Mimica also learns from observed work. Their scale demonstrates demand for the category, without establishing demand for GodHands. Its emphasis on decisions and exceptions could matter, but the discrepancy between execution marketing and indexed read-only agent descriptions leaves the delivered capability unclear. This activates my concern about an AI application whose differentiation competitors can absorb. There is no evidence that the founders assume model errors disappear, so I would not attribute that stronger red flag to them.

What would my money prove? The dossier supplies no funded hypothesis, deadline, or pass/fail threshold, so the venture-moment score stays at its default. Revenue, margins, burn, and runway are also undisclosed; I cannot underwrite a route that survives deleting the next financing. My exceptions permit missing gate evidence to be outweighed by demonstrated conversion, retention, cash economics, or exceptional operating access. An anonymous documentation result does not reach that bar. I pass on the available evidence. My verifiable flip condition is three independent support customers renewing after 90 days at prices covering capture, inference, human review, and support costs. That would demonstrate that GodHands’ access creates an enduring economic relationship and give me a reason to reopen the financing gates.
