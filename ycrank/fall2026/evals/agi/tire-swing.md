# Tire Swing -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest path. Tire Swing says it extracts income and asset data, applies eligibility rules, and prepares source-linked certification packets for staff approval. The memo describes a manual workflow spread across calls, emails, spreadsheets, and office visits. If automation cuts total preparation time at equivalent accuracy, low-volume affordable housing operators might be able to buy certification preparation they previously had to handle in-house. That is a scenario assumption. The memo gives no like-for-like labor measurement or evidence identifying buyers newly able to pay. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The founders report dozens of properties live across eight states. The subscription terms establish a billing model, while payment and renewal behavior remain unknown. The documented pivot from healthcare compliance to a launched housing product shows execution, and the founders’ experience in digital forensics and rules-based software is relevant to the workflow. At each added property, incremental subscription revenue would need to cover OCR and inference, onboarding, support, and rule maintenance. Shared rules could reduce the cost of expansion, while property-specific configurations could erode that benefit. The memo provides no measured inputs for that comparison. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Tire Swing may save time for existing buyers without opening a new market. Staff still approve each certification, and review and exception handling could absorb much of the preparation savings. With cheap, capable models, Roost already markets overlapping source-linked income calculations, while Yardi bundles certification with property accounting. Tire Swing’s reported rule library and support for existing forms offer a plausible reason to stay, but the memo contains no buyer preference under functional parity or measured network benefit. It also identifies no scarce input Tire Swing controls for a separate Path A case. The evidence leaves both Path B conditions unproven, which blocks outreach and leaves a feasible test for Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid pilot with low-volume operators that previously could not justify buying certification preparation show that they repeatedly choose Tire Swing over a comparable workflow using equally capable models? Measure staff time per correctly approved file against the manual baseline, including review, retries, and exceptions. Then test repeat purchases at a price that covers incremental delivery costs and record what buyers value enough to stay. A positive result would support Reach out if it also raises the score to 65 or above with at least medium confidence. Evidence that the savings open no new paid use, or that buyers readily switch under parity, would move the action to Pass.
