# Nex -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I start with three blanks: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and what this check uniquely finances. YC and disclosed investors including Dharmesh Shah and Girish Mathrubootham suggest Nex is not invisible to capital, but they do not establish an auction. Nor does the dossier establish a cheap first check. Financing silence earns no benefit of the doubt when price is evidence: I cannot tell whether I am buying discarded uncertainty or paying YC consensus pricing.

The founders are the strongest part of the case. Nazz Mohammad shipped CRM product at HubSpot and built a ChatGPT plugin used by 48,000 people; Francisco Dias was HubSpot CRM’s fourth engineer and later worked on its AI infrastructure. That is real outsider-insider potential across GTM operations and programmable automation. The team has also built rather than merely narrated: WUPHF reached roughly 1,200 GitHub stars, 94 forks, and a 260-point Show HN discussion, while Nex reports a live context graph, granular RBAC, SOC 2 Type 1, and more than 1,000 integrations.

But attention is not yet a dark pool. GitHub stars, Product Hunt points, LinkedIn followers, and Hacker News comments demonstrate developer curiosity; none demonstrates that an owned audience, proprietary dataset, or embedded workflow caused a customer to purchase or remain. The discontinued CRM reportedly had revenue and enterprise proofs of concept, but no amount, conversion rate, or continuity into the current product is disclosed. Nex has moved from AI-native CRM to context infrastructure to “Claude Cowork for GTM” within six months. That may be intelligent searching, but it also means the customer-specific graph and decision history are still prospective switching costs rather than observed ones.

The strongest bull case is that unusually relevant builders are iterating toward the correct packaging: a $100-per-seat product, code-backed workflows, enterprise permissioning, and open-source distribution could turn HubSpot-earned access into a proprietary operating layer. I do not dismiss that. I reject the present underwriting because the known exception for a generic AI application requires hard operating receipts—conversion, retention, or cash economics—not better architecture and public engagement. Clay already reports $100 million ARR, while Gumloop and Relevance AI possess overlapping workflow runtimes and substantially more capital. If model capability makes natural-language workflow generation easier, it helps those incumbents too.

The capital plan is the decisive absence. There is no named hypothesis, dated milestone, or pass/fail metric for what the pre-seed round will reveal. “More integrations, more workflows, more GTM automation” is a roadmap, not a finite venture experiment. At the same time, the $100 seat price supplies no current revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or route to break-even without another round. Delete the Series A and I cannot yet identify the controllable cash metric that keeps Nex alive.

I am passing today. My verifiable flip condition is specific: show that at least 20 paying teams generated $20,000 or more in aggregate monthly recurring revenue from workflows retained for three consecutive months, and pair that with round terms plus a seed-funded path to break-even. That would convert the context graph from a plausible product property into owned workflow that causes purchases—and tell me whether scarce capital is funding a truth-revealing experiment rather than another AI capital factory.
