# Streamline Systems -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Streamline Systems has a reported $500,000 YC investment dated September 1, 2026. That tells me how much capital arrived, not what price I would pay or whether my check enables something otherwise unfundable. Proposed post-money, competing term sheets, and remaining financing need are undisclosed. I cannot call this an auction merely because it is a YC company. I also cannot call it a scarce-capital opportunity. With my own money, that distinction matters.

The interesting asset is flying hardware. Streamline reports satellite-independent autonomous flight, GPS handoff, and integration with ArduPilot, INAV, and PX4. Its recruitment material reports signed LOIs and scheduled government trials. That is a credible starting point for a venture moment: establish whether a drop-in module meets a buyer’s navigation requirements under interference. But the dossier does not connect round proceeds to a specified accuracy, mission duration, acceptance deadline, or purchasing decision. Scheduled trials are affirmative customer interest; they do not establish what truth this particular check buys.

The strongest argument against passing is that Streamline may already have crossed the difficult boundary between an engineering project and something military operators want to test. A functioning prototype plus signed LOIs can be highly informative at pre-seed. Government testing could also begin accumulating qualification evidence that competitors cannot obtain simply by copying a public specification. This fits my pattern of accepting physical complexity when a team connects difficult operating systems. The unresolved issue is ownership: the dossier establishes neither exclusive customer access nor a proprietary workflow that caused those LOIs. Autopilot compatibility makes the module easier to adopt; it does not, by itself, create a dark pool.

Poraj’s prior content agency using Premiere Pro automation is a modest operating receipt, beyond a university credential or software job title. Jessett’s RAF reserve affiliation adds military exposure. Together they suggest a plausible bridge between software development and defense users, but the record does not establish prior navigation deployments or procurement execution. Their reported flight demonstration strengthens the bull case without proving repeatable production. The advertised electrical and mechanical engineering hires still need to harden and productize the hardware.

Delete the next financing from the plan. What pays for that work? Module pricing, production cost, gross margin, burn, and runway are undisclosed, so I retain the economics default rather than inventing either profitability or a financing dependency. Advanced Navigation and ANELLO already supply alternative navigation architectures; Streamline needs a buyer-relevant performance or cost advantage, not simply exposure to GPS denial. I checked the exception case: demonstrated paid conversion, retention, cash economics, or unusually strong external validation could justify underwriting around missing financing evidence. Company-reported LOIs and future trials are meaningful, but they do not yet supply that override. Neither YC membership nor two co-founders establishes the auction or decision-making red flags.

I pass today. Streamline Systems has enough substance to merit attention, but the documented opportunity does not yet establish my two primary gates: an attractive price and a defined experiment purchased by the capital. One verifiable flip condition would reopen underwriting: a signed financing package below $10 million post-money that funds a dated customer acceptance test, with measurable navigation thresholds, a purchase commitment conditional on passing, and production costs supporting that order. That would connect the check, the technical result, and the cash.
