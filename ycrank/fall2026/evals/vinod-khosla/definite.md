# Definite -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Definite’s consequential possibility is making reliable financial expertise cheap enough to run continuously across a business. Reconstructing the same relationships for every reconciliation and report is wasted work. A shared financial model could eliminate that repetition. But the dossier supplies no baseline labor cost, population served, or measured reduction in work. I cannot price an extraordinary outcome from the phrase “back-office agents.” The consequence remains unquantified; that is missing evidence, not proof of a small opportunity.

The architecture gives me a reason to investigate. Read-only connectors establish context, deterministic rules calculate results, and models propose mappings and explanations. Source-to-cell lineage and historical snapshots address a real requirement: finance teams must explain their answers. However, Workato already offers connectivity, semantic context and agent orchestration. Definite has not demonstrated a discontinuity against that incumbent constraint. Its earlier FinBalance claim—beating GPT-5 at approximately 50 times lower cost using DeepSeek V3 and verification—concerns the regulatory-reporting product. Without a reproducible method or evidence that the advantage carries into today’s product, it cannot establish current technical superiority.

The strongest evidence is the founders’ response to rejection. Earlier compliance-product LOIs failed to become purchase commitments, and deals failed after demos; the team moved to contract-first selling. That is an observed revision of a belief. BitGo controls, Optiver trading systems, Boosted.ai financial agents and Meta machine learning also provide relevant technical exposure. These facts make the bull case uncomfortable to dismiss: capable founders who learn from failed sales could turn customer-specific mappings into reusable financial infrastructure. But there is no documented recruitment of complementary owners for accounting correctness or implementation risk, so I give credit for learning without inventing a talent advantage.

My execution-heavy fintech investments leave room for a company like this. A scientific breakthrough is not compulsory if a small team can rebuild consequential financial infrastructure. Definite’s retained rules and embedded mappings could support that route. The unresolved question is whether each deployment makes the next one cheaper, or requires another round of bespoke engineering. The dossier does not establish the proprietary learning loop I look for, but it also does not justify calling this merely an interface wrapper. My exceptions require an exceptional instigator and a demonstrated giant consequence, or unusually compelling technical progress. Relevant internships and a historical benchmark do not establish that override here.

I would pass with my own money today. Monthly contracts, no deployment fee and a two-month opt-out expose the team to customer judgment, but reveal neither pricing nor delivery economics. There is also no documented, costed experiment with a pass/fail threshold against the largest kill risk: reusable, correct financial modeling across customers. Those omissions receive the absent-evidence scores; three founders and undisclosed revenue are ordinary pre-seed conditions. My one verifiable flip condition is a prospective five-customer replication test: freeze the core ontology before deployment, demonstrate at least 10 times lower total reconciliation labor—including setup and review—against each customer’s existing process, with independently checked accounting correctness and positive contribution margin at the contracted price. That would connect consequence, capability and economics in one result.
