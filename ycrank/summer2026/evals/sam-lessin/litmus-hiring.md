# Litmus -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I first write down three numbers: post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and checks already committed. Litmus supplies none of them. YC affiliation and a public launch do not prove an auction, but neither does financing silence establish that my money would be the scarce first check. The company therefore receives the low prior on terms; $60K ARR cannot rehabilitate an unknown strike price because traction and price answer different questions.

The product has a plausible venture moment, but the round does not name it. Litmus could test whether repository-derived work trials predict quality of hire better than standardized screens, with a dated cohort, renewal threshold, and employer outcome. Instead, the dossier describes founder-led demos, free assessment builds, integrations, and custom contracts without specifying what the capital funds or what finite result changes the company’s value. That is operating activity, not yet a truth-revealing experiment.

The strongest evidence is the workflow itself. More than 3,000 completed assessments and $60K ARR show that employer context is causing actual use and purchases, not merely producing followers or integrations. Neo Scholars and its portfolio use a Litmus-built hiring system, while Autumn AI and Sleuth appear as customers. Repositories, tickets, candidate activity, and grading outcomes could become a proprietary data pool joining two mistrustful systems: employers want job-realistic evidence, while candidates want to work with the tools they actually use. The material limitation is that Litmus has not published retention, quality-of-hire outcomes, or evidence that its 3,000-assessment corpus improves validity.

The bull case against my caution stings. This is a three-person pre-seed team with real revenue, substantial assessment volume, deterministic sandbox tests, and a product adapted to AI-assisted engineering before larger vendors fully abandon standardized screening. Hard conversion can override missing seed-gate evidence. But the exception threshold requires operating proof strong enough to make price secondary. Here, configurable LLM graders have no public accuracy or demographic audit, candidate recording creates consent friction, and incumbent feature convergence is already visible across HackerRank, CodeSignal, and Karat. Litmus may have found demand without yet proving a defensible outcome advantage.

Deleting the next financing exposes the remaining problem. $60K ARR is a controllable cash signal, but there is no gross margin, burn, runway, contract retention, or cost-to-build evidence. Custom assessment creation plus Anthropic, OpenAI, and sandbox costs could describe efficient software or a labor-intensive recruiting service; the dossier does not distinguish them. My 36-month base case is that Litmus reaches roughly $1–3 million ARR as a useful contextual-assessment vendor but remains strategically vulnerable unless its outcome data becomes the product rather than another feature set.

The founders have lived adjacent sides of the bridge: Elena Zhao worked as an engineer at Meta and Two Sigma, while Shaivi Rau has startup, venture, and hiring exposure. Together they have shipped Litmus far enough to produce revenue and thousands of assessments, but the dossier shows no prior hiring system, scaled recruiting operation, or earlier product exit. I am neutral, not passing: the owned workflow is real, and the metrics are better than theater, but I will not fund an unspecified experiment at an unspecified price. One verifiable flip condition is a financing plan showing a sub-$10 million post-money round dedicated to a dated, independently measured study linking Litmus scores to quality of hire and customer renewal.
