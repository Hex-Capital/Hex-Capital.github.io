# Clean -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Clean’s strongest potential exit is a strategic acquisition by a sales intelligence platform such as Apollo or 6sense. The memo records a November launch and describes API access to buying-window signals drawn from dated operational records. In a customer testimonial, Vivameda reported $60,000 in signed contracts over two weeks and a rise in reply rates from roughly 4% to 19%. That is early evidence of value to a sales team. The acquisition inference is that a platform could sell those signals to its existing customers if owning Clean offered an advantage over building or licensing equivalent coverage. The value of that advantage to a buyer remains unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The seed-stage milestone is repeatable improvement in qualified opportunities across multiple paying customers through renewal. Clean reported over $200,000 in ARR before the November launch, so the revenue attributable to the current product remains unclear. The memo records a first investor check and the CEO’s account of a roughly $1.2 million round, with final proceeds undisclosed. The milestone would require runway to maintain data coverage and track customer cohorts through renewal. Available runway and additional capital needs remain unclear. Clean reports owning some datasets and licensing others. The owned assets support a plausible acquisition structure, while continued access to licensed data after closing remains unverified. No independent liquidity route is evidenced. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3%, with no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale prices and deductions below are scenario assumptions. Both sale cases assume the holder is eligible, the waterfall pays pro rata after the stated deductions, and the full cash payout arrives within eight years.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $35M | $1.05M, or 2.1× |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M | $2.7M, or 5.4×. If only 75% of that payout arrives within eight years, realized cash falls to about $2.03M, or 4.05×. |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or distribution within eight years | $0, or 0× |

The memo establishes neither a buyer-supported price range nor holder terms, so the proceeds score stays at the absent-evidence default. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A platform may be able to reproduce or source enough of Clean’s coverage to make an acquisition unnecessary. Salesmotion already aggregates filings and other account signals, Apollo bundles AI account research with outreach, and the memo identifies no structural barrier to replication. Clean’s own aircraft example also shows outreach beginning after a supplier had already been selected, which weakens the case that its timing advantage is repeatable. These risks justify Watch. Neither a structural closing barrier nor a credible price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle is established. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Could owning Clean’s buying-window signals create enough incremental profit for a strategic buyer to justify a price above the holder’s cash hurdle? A buyer-side comparison of acquisition, in-house development, and licensing, grounded in repeatable customer results, would answer it. Move to Reach out if that analysis supports a price that clears the hurdle after deal adjustments. Move to Pass if it establishes a credible price ceiling below the hurdle even under favorable supported terms.
