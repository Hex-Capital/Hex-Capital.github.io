# Zomma -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Jihyun Kim helped launch a $1B multi-strategy hedge fund, while Zomma is building in the generic AI-agent category I distrust most. That high-founder-conviction, low-category-fit pairing is Zomma’s signature: unusually credible operators wrapping lived investment-operations pain in a consensus computer-use product. AI agents are uninvestible—provocative overstatement, obviously—but Zomma must prove the asset is proprietary workflow knowledge rather than rented model capability.

**Drop.io** is the high rung for asymmetric conviction: I built against free file-sharing incumbents because the underlying behavior looked durable before the market agreed. Zomma approaches that rung through Kim’s Arrowpoint, Endowus, and Goldman Sachs history, reinforced by Varun Arumugam’s verification work at Apple and Cashel Fitzgerald’s vision-model experience. The analogy is load-bearing because Zomma’s founders have operated both sides of the workflow. Zomma diverges sharply from Drop.io because no founder has visible category writing, public technical artifacts, or evidence of pursuing this exact thesis for three years before YC.

**OpenAI**-of-[vertical] is the low rung because foundation-model capability gets repackaged into a consensus seed round with no proprietary asset. That failure mechanism appears directly in Zomma: Anthropic and OpenAI already expose computer use, while Zalos and Eloquent AI offer overlapping screen-operating agents in finance. Zomma’s approval-gated investment-operations focus is narrower than generic agent infrastructure, but Zomma has no exclusive dataset, patent, or retention evidence establishing a sleeve I already want to own. This is AI SaaS until proven otherwise, not Solana at $0.05.

**Zalos** has raised $3.6M, Eloquent AI has raised $7.4M, and Stacks has raised $35M around adjacent finance-agent narratives. Over the next 18 months, that capital turns Zomma’s supposedly narrow category into a factory-model auction for customers, talent, and seed allocation; Zomma then needs either cheaper entry pricing or an illegible asset competitors cannot price. Zomma discloses no round price, investor dynamics, or visible tier-one pass—no evidence, applying the absent-evidence default. Without terms, I assume YC-plus-AI consensus pricing rather than value-seed mispricing.

No public revenue, pricing, or billing metric establishes whether Zomma’s five-person forward-deployment model becomes default-alive or founder-draining. Zomma could flip this criterion into invest territory if implementation fees finance workflow mapping, contracts carry meaningful annual prepayment, and each deployment creates reusable reconciliation primitives rather than permanent services labor. Until Zomma demonstrates that structure, negotiated enterprise contracts plus regulated integrations look like multiple expensive regatta tacks inside a standard SaaS cap table.

**John Fish / Bookshelved** had owned distribution before the financing instrument existed; the person and audience were already assets on the cap table. Zomma has Kim’s 3K LinkedIn followers, Fitzgerald’s 1K, a NY Tech Week workshop described as having a long waitlist, and only 188 company followers. Those are sourcing surfaces, not an owned community. Zomma therefore has no creator-style downside cushion, no 10K-user organic channel, and no founder-brand mechanism that compounds independently of direct enterprise sales.

**Eloquent AI** already reports $0.5M ARR in regulated financial operations, which makes the likely destination legible. Within 36 months, I expect Zomma to pivot from broad computer-use agents into a forward-deployed reconciliation and exception-management service for wealth managers, reaching $2M-$5M ARR while remaining services-heavy rather than becoming a horizontal agent platform. Zomma’s domain-native founders should find a viable tack; Zomma’s weak category differentiation should prevent the original software story from surviving intact.

**Ro** is the strongest objection because regulated operational complexity can turn apparent capital intensity into the moat: Kim has lived hedge-fund workflows, Zomma already claims usage by hedge funds and asset managers, and human approval plus vaulted credentials could become the trusted prescription rail equivalent for investment operations. The objection stings because Zomma may be selling accountability, not clicks. Ro’s exception still does not apply yet: Zomma has unnamed customers, no revenue signal, SOC 2 still in progress, and three funded substitutes. I would flip to Invest if Zomma showed three named asset managers renewing at $100K-plus ACV while raising at or below a $15M post-money valuation.
