# Topos -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I want a biologist to open an agent’s proposed experiment and see which conclusions follow from explicit assumptions, which depend on uncertain evidence, and which still require a pipette. Topos is pointing toward that world. But agents verifying one another can also produce a very elaborate agreement. The decisive question is what can tell every agent in the swarm that it is wrong. The dossier supplies no independent checker specification or biological verification result. I can imagine the destination; I cannot yet identify the little artifact showing that Topos has reached it.

There are two separate crossings here. First, turn biological claims into statements a checker can evaluate without losing what matters. Then establish whether the assumptions describe living systems. A flawless proof built on a mistaken biological premise still sends someone toward the wrong experiment. Harmonic’s reported 96.8% result on VERINA code-verification problems demonstrates progress in an adjacent domain, but it does not cross either bridge for Topos. With no documented intermediate milestones or present biological artifact, my future-mapping score stays at its missing-evidence default.

The founders are the strongest reason to keep looking. Ryan Min June Shin and Sohum Sukhatankar have built together: the Felt poker application and their jointly credited September 2026 prime-progression project are more informative than their Harvard affiliations. Sukhatankar’s seizure-localization research at Boston Children’s Hospital also puts some clinical-data experience beside the mathematical computing. These are concrete reasons to believe the team can make things and collaborate. They do not yet establish a longstanding obsession with biological formalization, recruitment of believers, or command of the commercial problem. I give the founder dimension credit above its default, without turning mathematical ability into proof of the entire business.

Why does a researcher lose time today, and who benefits from that arrangement? The dossier’s proposed answer—expert review and experiments are expensive—is inferred, without a measured delay, named gatekeeper, or identified rent. Some verification work could return researchers’ time; some experimental work is the price of learning what nature actually does. Topos has not shown where it separates those costs. Nor is there documented repeat product use to illuminate the distinction. Fall 2026 YC participation is not a completed user action, and zero job postings tells me nothing useful about demand. The economics and behavior dimensions therefore receive their defaults; I am not deducting points for missing revenue.

The strongest argument against passing is that a small mathematical team could create the checking layer that scientific-agent companies eventually need. That fits my unstated preference for concealed infrastructure beneath a visible wave. It also resembles the exception I made for **CosmicBrain AI**: early technical founders pursuing an enabling technology before revenue. The material difference is that its functioning prototype and signed industrial proofs of concept supplied a bridge from founder ability to application. Topos’s disclosed artifacts establish founder ability, but not that bridge. Edison’s existing scientific workflows make an independent verification layer potentially useful; they do not establish that Topos controls one. No proprietary checker, formalization corpus, or other scarce asset is demonstrated, so infrastructure remains a possibility rather than earned score.

I would pass with my own money on this evidence. I see no documented leadership shell game, severe privacy issue, or dependence on one closed model supplier; those red flags are unestablished, not reasons for rejection. My hesitation is narrower: the company’s central promise lacks an observable correctness boundary. One verifiable flip condition would bring me back: an independently reproducible, blinded biology pilot in which Topos exposes its assumptions and checker, catches a consequential error missed by an unchecked agent workflow, and has that correction confirmed experimentally. That would give me something concrete to work backward from.
