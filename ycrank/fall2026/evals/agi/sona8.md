# Sona8 -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Sona8’s strongest path is **New Economies**. The manual baseline is consultant and employee time spent on sampled stakeholder interviews, plus analyst time to turn interviews and existing documentation into a usable workflow map. Sona8 uses adaptive interviews and task demonstrations to automate parts of that work. In a market-opening scenario, the savings would make it economical to refresh site-level maps between transformation projects, when another round of manual discovery would exceed the buyer’s budget. A customer testimonial describes s.Oliver’s CEO office using Sona8 to map processes and identify differences in teams’ planning needs. That use supports the discovery mechanism. The change in cost per usable map and the existence of a previously excluded use remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Sona8 reports a signed engagement with an unnamed top-three consultancy and more than 10,000 completed interviews. These are early commitment and usage signals. Pricing, repeat orders, and purchases for newly viable uses remain unverified. The founders have relevant experience in manual discovery and enterprise AI, and the team shipped voice interviewing followed by workflow mapping with task capture. For scale, the natural unit is a buyer-accepted site map or refresh. Against the manual baseline, its cost would include interview delivery, setup, analyst review, and rework for missing coverage or exceptions. Sona8 describes a platform fee plus usage charges. Contribution at that pricing remains unmeasured. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The documented deployments fit existing transformation work. Commercial voice APIs and kawaru.ai’s interview-generated maps and MCP access make substitution credible. A customer’s accumulated workflow history could give it a reason to stay if teams reuse that history across projects, but the memo shows no preference, retention, or premium under functional parity. The data policy restricts interview content to serving the customer that supplied it, and there is no demonstrated network effect or independently supported scarce complement. The evidence leaves both New Economies gates open to testing. It establishes neither a newly viable market nor actual switching at parity. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

### Decisive diligence question

Will a buyer pay Sona8 for a second site-level refresh it previously skipped on cost, after trying a functionally comparable alternative? A paid repeat rollout would move the action to **Reach out** if it documents the manual baseline, shows a materially lower cost per accepted map at comparable coverage and accuracy, and identifies why the buyer chose Sona8 at a price that covers delivery and review. Evidence that the use was already economically viable or that the buyer switches readily at parity would move the action to **Pass**.
