# Ooak Data -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Seven-figure contracts with the two largest AI labs make **Ooak Data** a schlep-heavy data-acquisition machine with an unusually weak organic-idea story. **Ooak Data** has persuaded companies to surrender emails, documents, financial figures, and collaboration-tool histories, then built an auditable anonymization pipeline around the resulting mess. That is difficult, unpleasant work. But Pierre-Louis Vouteau came from distribution and quick commerce, Grégoire Lamy from product and data operations, and Thomas Aubry from applied ML; the dossier never says any of them personally needed this product. The highest signal is appetite for the schlep. The lowest is evidence that the idea grew from the founders’ own pain.

**Coinbase** is the high rung for schlep blindness because compliance was not incidental to the product; willingness to endure it became the asset. **Ooak Data** has the same structural possibility. Regional storage, deletion rights, NDAs, automated anonymization, and acquisition of more than 300 company ecosystems are precisely the sort of chores clever founders unconsciously avoid. **Ooak Data** diverges from Coinbase because it may pay up to $300,000 per ecosystem, while Scale AI, Surge AI, Mercor, and Snorkel can attack adjacent environments with larger networks or capital. A schlep is valuable when doing it compounds. It is merely expensive when every new unit must be bought again.

A polished AI-wrapper founded by people who spotted a market opportunity is the low rung for organicity: the founders are standing outside the problem, looking at it through a spreadsheet. **Ooak Data** is not that bad—the founders operated data-intensive companies, Pierre-Louis started alone, and Ooak Data survived a pivot while signing initial clients—but the dossier contains no Dropbox-style forgotten USB stick or Stripe-style commerce frustration. Airbnb’s known exception does not apply: Airbnb had cereal boxes proving animal resourcefulness despite a wrong-seeming idea, whereas Ooak Data has impressive contracts without evidence that the founders built the product for themselves. I therefore apply the absent-evidence penalty nearly intact.

Seven-figure AI-lab contracts are tracks left by animals. **Ooak Data** had to source sensitive workflows, persuade suppliers to share them, satisfy frontier-lab buyers, and pivot before YC; over 18 months, those abilities can compound into faster ecosystem acquisition and increasingly reusable environments. The danger is that six open roles across sales, marketing, US expansion, operations, software, and ML could move **Ooak Data** toward manager mode before the machine is repeatable. The contracts lift the animal score well above default, but without a named unscalable stunt or evidence that Pierre-Louis personally closed hostile suppliers, they do not put Ooak Data beside Airbnb’s cereal boxes.

Thomas Aubry’s `officemd` repository has 23 stars, while Pierre-Louis Vouteau and Grégoire Lamy have no verified public repositories. **Ooak Data** would move up an entire tier on hacker genealogy if the anonymization system were shown to preserve workflow causality across emails, documents, and financial data better than frontier models or Snorkel’s simulations, with code or benchmark results attributable to the founders. Instead, **Ooak Data** publicly exposes 480 Mercor tasks rather than results from its own acquired datasets. The technical credentials are real—Samsung applied ML and PayLead data leadership—but the unstated Stripe and Reddit pattern is only faintly present. Hackers usually leave artifacts, not merely job titles.

Pierre-Louis beginning alone and adding Grégoire roughly a year before YC, then Thomas later, resembles the opposite of the **Twitch** pattern on cofounder configuration. Kiko’s founders had already built, failed, and worked through pain together before Twitch; **Ooak Data** has three complementary founders but no shared employer, university, or prior project. The functional coverage is sensible—distribution, operations, and ML—but **Ooak Data** has not shown that the relationships survive disagreement or failure. Three founders assembled sequentially are better than a solo founder, but they are not yet the strong pair or trio my best outcomes usually contain.

Two unnamed AI-lab contracts lead me to predict that within 36 months **Ooak Data** reaches $15–30 million in annual recurring revenue, with most revenue still coming from fewer than five frontier-model customers and the product increasingly resembling a managed workflow-data refinery rather than a self-serve environment library. **Ooak Data** can reach that range because sensitive-data procurement and anonymization are sales-and-operations bottlenecks that labs would rather outsource. The same high-schlep, low-organicity signature means Ooak Data is more likely to become a valuable supplier than a new software default like Stripe.

Scale AI, Surge AI, Mercor, and Snorkel make the bear case sting: **Ooak Data** could spend heavily acquiring 300 ecosystems, discover that anonymization destroys the fidelity labs pay for, and then lose either of its two concentrated buyers to internal data programs. **Ooak Data** also lacks the unstated hacker-genealogy signal, the founders did not build together for years, and six simultaneous hires risk manager mode. The fact that two leading labs have already signed seven-figure contracts wins narrowly because customers facing the problem have paid before the library is complete; Loopt’s force-of-personality exception is unnecessary when revenue itself is the artifact. I would flip to Pass if neither named lab renews or expands within 12 months, or if Ooak Data cannot publish evidence that environments derived from its own acquired workflows outperform expert-authored simulations.
