# Phone Harness -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest exit path is a strategic sale to a device-testing or agent-infrastructure platform. Phone Harness describes a common interface for personal and hosted phones, with dedicated cloud iPhones that preserve apps and logins across runs. A buyer could use that capability to add native mobile workflows to its existing product. The memo identifies BrowserStack as an example of a company already selling real-device infrastructure. Buyer interest, measurable workflow savings, and acquisition economics remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The memo records 5,100 GitHub stars, 522 forks, a November cloud launch, and YC backing. The company advertises dedicated iPhones at $80 per month, while realized revenue and paying-customer counts are undisclosed. The beta and developer interest make a paid production test plausible. The milestone is repeat paid use of persistent iPhones for permitted workflows, with measured customer savings and fleet costs. The capital needed to reach it remains unverified. Platform and app permissions constrain eligible workflows. Transfer rights for the hosted operations and holder participation terms remain unknown. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3% pro rata. The sale prices below are scenario assumptions. Both sale cases assume an eligible holder receives cash by year eight on its full stake, with no other claims diverting proceeds after the stated deductions.

| Scenario | Cash to holder |
|----------|----------------|
| Modest sale | $40 million enterprise value less $5 million in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $1.05 million, or 2.1×. |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value less $10 million in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $2.7 million, or 5.4×. Combined deductions above about $16.7 million would put the payout below target, as would selling only 80% of the stake at the stated price. |
| Prolonged private operation | Reinvested cash and no eligible share purchase or distribution yield zero realized proceeds within eight years. |

Published pricing establishes a way to charge for the service. The memo leaves paid usage, fleet margins, buyer willingness to pay, and the actual waterfall unverified, so plausible proceeds remain at the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could fork the MIT-licensed local harness or use an existing provider. Mobilerun already bundles hosted phones with an agent runner, and PhoneUse offers hosted iPhones. Phone Harness’s local iPhone control depends on Apple Mirroring, and its cloud terms exclude workflows on services that prohibit automated access. Those constraints weaken the case for paying a premium for the hosted operation. The evidence leaves open whether permitted, recurring workloads can create enough value to justify a purchase. It establishes neither a structural closing block nor materially inadequate proceeds under the strongest credible route, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can recurring paid use of persistent cloud iPhones for permitted workflows create enough value for a strategic buyer to support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? Paid cohort retention, measured customer savings, and fleet margins would show whether the hosted operation adds value beyond the forkable code. A credible buyer-side build-versus-buy assessment that ties those results to a price range clearing the hurdle after deal adjustments would move the action to Reach out.
