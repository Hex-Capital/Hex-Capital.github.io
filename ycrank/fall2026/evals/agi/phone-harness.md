# Phone Harness -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is **scarce complements**. As agents take on more work in native apps, reliable access to real iPhones could become more valuable for workflows that browser automation cannot reach. Phone Harness shipped a local harness and launched a cloud beta that offers dedicated iPhones. The company describes developers falling back to manual phone use for native-only steps. The memo establishes that Phone Harness offers device access and leaves open whether a recurring workflow makes that access scarce. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

The shared local and cloud interface lets teams reuse commands, and the company says saved cloud phones retain apps and logins. That continuity could make switching costly for recurring workloads, though the memo records no renewals or customer preference after a comparison. The reported 5.1K GitHub stars and 522 forks show developer interest. Paid cloud adoption remains unverified. Shawn Pana’s prior delivery of `smux` and the local-to-cloud rollout show relevant execution, with fleet reliability still untested in the memo. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Founders & Team*)

A dedicated iPhone is the expansion unit. At the listed annual rate of $64 per month, an assumed 80% paid occupancy yields about $51 per installed phone per month. Hardware amortization, hosting, replacements and support would need to fit below that amount for positive contribution. Occupancy, costs and the number of teams willing to rent fleets remain unverified. (*The Idea*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

PhoneUse rents real iPhones, Mobilerun offers hosted phones with a task runner, and developers can fork the MIT-licensed harness. Apple controls local mirroring, and third-party app rules can exclude workflows the product can technically perform. Those facts weaken the case that Phone Harness controls a scarce resource. The memo leaves open whether a substitute can meet the same reliability and device-state requirements on a recurring, permitted workflow, so the path remains on Watch. The New Economies path is also unproven. Manual phone work is the stated baseline, but the memo gives no comparative cost per successful run, evidence of a previously uneconomic use becoming viable, or evidence that its buyer would still pay Phone Harness under model parity. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent team renew a dedicated iPhone deployment at a price that covers its fully loaded fleet costs after testing a competing service on the same recurring, permitted native-app workflow? A paid renewal with run logs showing that Phone Harness reliably meets a requirement the alternative misses would move the action to Reach out. Ready substitution at comparable reliability and cost would move it to Pass.
