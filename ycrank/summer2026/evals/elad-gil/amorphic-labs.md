# Amorphic Labs -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Computer-use APIs released in 2024–2025 now let software vendors automate prospect-specific demos; whether Amorphic Labs becomes large depends on one variable: qualified pipeline generated per minute of human review. The dossier establishes that sales teams spend hours preparing demos, but supplies no conversion lift, preparation-time reduction, usable-video rate, or threshold at which automation becomes economically compelling. More importantly, the terms acknowledge inaccurate or incomplete actions and require vendor review. The market thesis is specific, but its controlling variable remains unproven.

The technology shift is real. Visually operating arbitrary interfaces avoids building an API integration for every application and could expand personalized demos from strategic accounts to the long tail of outbound prospects. Yet feasibility is not economics. There is no quantified reduction in production cost or delivery time, and no evidence customers changed behavior after adopting the product. A $6.0 billion sales-enablement market growing at 17.3% does not establish the size of the narrower agent-generated-demo category.

Amorphic’s strongest feature is workflow depth. The agent researches a prospect, seeds an application with relevant data, performs the workflow, records it, edits the footage, generates narration, and produces the shareable result. Better computer-use models should improve this system rather than immediately erase it. The company also handles credentials, browser state, and application actions, giving it more operational surface area than a video formatter. But app-specific workflow maps, correction histories, and execution traces are only inferred future assets; no reliability advantage, feedback loop, or cross-customer learning has been demonstrated.

The strongest argument for a talent-and-technology exception is Frank Li’s experience as an OpenAI MTS and project lead, supported by ML-infrastructure work at NVIDIA, alongside Dylan Kelly’s AI research. This is a credible team for browser control and personalization. It is not yet a scarce underlying technical asset: the enabling computer-use APIs are public, and the dossier identifies no proprietary model or benchmark advantage. Nor does founder pedigree prove the desperation or second-time startup learning relevant to my founder criterion.

Market crystallization is already unfavorable. Consensus, Walnut, and Reprise have raised approximately $143 million, $56 million, and $82 million, respectively, and all are moving toward AI-assisted personalized product experiences. Those incumbents can add application operation and narration through the same public model layer. Meanwhile Amorphic discloses no customers, repeat usage, or revenue, and bearer-link confidentiality plus access to cookies, tokens, passwords, and prospect data may raise the adoption threshold for enterprise buyers. The inferred data moat also fails the immediate reproduction-cost test.

My 36-month base case is that Amorphic pivots from personalized demo videos into broader AI sales-engineering automation—demo setup, technical validation, and follow-up—and remains below $2 million ARR rather than defining a standalone category. I pass today because the market and product-pull gates fail, despite a technically credible workflow and team. I would reverse the decision if three named B2B software customers each renewed after six months while generating at least 100 prospect-specific demos monthly with less than five minutes of human review per video.
