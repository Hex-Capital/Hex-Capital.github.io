# Axiomat -- Paul Graham Evaluation

What happened when these founders got stuck? Axiomat’s dossier doesn’t tell me. Ethan Kharitonov worked on formal methods and AI verification at AWS. That is relevant experience. It doesn’t establish what he did when an approach failed, how quickly he found another route, or whether the result worked for a user. I would pass on the evidence supplied. The missing fact is founder behavior under resistance, which matters more to me than whether verification is an attractive category.

The technical question is unusually important here. Axiomat says it translates documentation and schemas into a logical business model, then verifies queries against it. A proof can establish that a query follows the model while the model encodes the wrong definition of revenue. The difficult part may therefore be discovering what the customer actually means. Jack Naimer’s co-authored BioReason research repository supplies some inspectable evidence of prior technical work; its 406 stars are neither Axiomat users nor verification results. I give limited technical credit for that artifact and the specified architecture. There is no supplied benchmark showing which errors Axiomat catches, which assumptions it requires, or when it refuses to answer.

The strongest case for investing is that these founders may already understand the difficult part. Kharitonov has directly relevant AWS experience, Naimer has AI research experience, and Elad Perlman built utility control software at Survalent. There could be a good company inside the unpleasant work of reconciling schemas, contradictory documentation, and business definitions. But the dossier does not connect Axiomat’s origin to a particular failure the founders encountered themselves. Nor does it document a rejected hypothesis and a shipped revision. I leave those dimensions at their missing-evidence defaults rather than treating relevant employment as proof of the whole founder story.

Who needs the crude version today? “Employees asking questions of company data” is too broad to answer that. Axiomat has demo bookings and email outreach, but no identified user niche returning to the product, paying for it, or demanding a particular workflow. Fall 2026 YC participation supplies no such evidence. Enterprise contracts are plausible, but pricing and revenue are unknown. The infrastructure pattern I like involves developers already using a new primitive, or an unusually concrete technical result before the round. Neither is established here. AWS’s existing Automated Reasoning checks also mean a buyer can encounter verification through an incumbent, although the dossier does not establish equivalent query coverage.

I would not infer the familiar founder red flags from this silence. There is no evidence of waiting for a partner, delegating away the product, or ignoring contrary feedback; the undisclosed cofounder history is simply undisclosed. But the exceptions that could overcome my resourcefulness gate require a working prototype, independently validated proof, or unmistakable user pull. Axiomat’s absolute correctness claim supplies none of those. My flip condition is one documented customer episode: an incorrect business answer blocks adoption, the founders ship a bounded verification fix within two weeks, and that customer then uses it weekly for a month. That would show the people, the proof, and the need in one observable sequence. Today I cannot justify writing the check.
