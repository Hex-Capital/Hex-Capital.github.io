# Caladh -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition is Caladh’s strongest supported route to holder liquidity. Lockheed Martin’s purchase of Ultra Maritime and Motorola’s purchase of D-Fend show that strategic buyers have acquired adjacent maritime sensing and counter-drone capabilities. Caladh says its products can add drone disruption and asset concealment alongside third-party detection. A buyer with an installed detection business could acquire Caladh to offer those capabilities through its existing customer relationships. That rationale depends on Caladh demonstrating field performance and giving a buyer an economic reason to own the products. The memo documents neither company-specific field results nor acquisition interest. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

A successful paid pilot at an operating port or subsea facility that converts to a repeat order would give a buyer evidence of both performance and customer willingness to pay. Kita’s undersea systems experience and Caladh’s confirmed YC backing make a pilot plausible at seed. The memo records pilot solicitation and conflicting funding figures, leaving the pilot budget and runway unverified. Ownership and transferability of the relevant IP, closing permissions, and holder participation rights also remain unverified. The memo supports no independent route to holder liquidity. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale scenarios assume an eligible holder sells its full stake in an all-cash deal that closes within eight years. Enterprise value is reduced by net debt and other deal deductions to reach equity value. The scenarios assume no such deductions or senior preference claims, so the holder receives 3% of the equity proceeds in cash at closing.

- **Modest sale.** An illustrative $50 million enterprise value yields $1.5 million in cash, or 3×.
- **Strong sale.** An illustrative $100 million enterprise value yields $3 million in cash, or 6×. With $15 million in net debt and other deal deductions, the same sale yields $2.55 million, or 5.1×, before any preference leakage.
- **Prolonged private.** If Caladh reinvests cash and there is no eligible share sale or funded payout within eight years, the holder realizes $0.

The acquisition prices are sensitivity assumptions. The adjacent deals establish strategic activity, while the memo gives no Caladh revenue, buyer economics, or transaction terms that would support a realizable price range. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An incumbent could keep selling its own detection products and partner for additional capabilities if customers demand them. Ultra Maritime has already demonstrated underwater drone detection and classification in a port-protection exercise, and Caladh says its products can integrate with third-party detection. That makes partnership a credible alternative to acquisition. The Coast Guard inquiry indicates end-user interest, but the memo shows neither funded procurement for Caladh nor a field result that would make ownership valuable to a buyer. This supports Watch. The evidence does not establish a structural block or a proceeds ceiling that would trigger Pass. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What result from a paid operating-site pilot would make a named strategic buyer willing to acquire Caladh at a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A buyer-backed success criterion tied to a credible price range that still clears the hurdle after deal deductions and senior claims would move the action to Reach out. Without that evidence, the action stays Watch.
