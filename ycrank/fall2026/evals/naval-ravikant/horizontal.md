# horizontal. -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The most important number in horizontal.’s dossier is the claim that Niklas Marxer brought agentic AI into more than 150 industrial companies. If those were substantial deployments he personally delivered, he may know precisely where factory automation breaks. But the claim is self-reported, with no named implementations or outcomes. Jakob Hermeler’s October 2026 quadrotor collision-recovery paper supplies a concrete research artifact. It establishes technical ability in an adjacent domain; it does not establish reliable execution inside production systems. There is enough specific knowledge here to pay attention, but not enough corroboration to underwrite the strongest founder case.

Horizontal’s decisive question is what remains after someone copies the software. Its agents propose to work inside existing systems across production planning, materials, quality, and order releases. That access could accumulate valuable operational knowledge. The dossier establishes integration requirements, however, without demonstrating exclusive workflow histories, transferable models, or replacement costs. Siemens’ June 2026 orchestration launch and Pelico’s ERP/MES-connected agents make the distinction consequential: connecting systems is already competitive territory. Each deployment must leave horizontal. with an asset, or the company risks repeatedly selling engineering labor.

The expansion path is more credible than the moat. A plant manager can begin with one process, then extend the same system access into adjacent coordination tasks involving logistics and quality. That gives the founders room to maneuver. The $20.7 billion MES spending estimate establishes substantial adjacent software budgets, although it cannot be treated as horizontal.’s addressable market. This fits my preference for attacking consequential infrastructure bottlenecks. It does not yet fit the less visible pattern I favor: participants creating useful value for other participants. No evidence shows that one manufacturer improves another manufacturer’s product or acquisition.

The strongest argument against passing is that industrial learning often becomes visible only after installation. These founders combine mechanical engineering, industrial AI exposure, and robotics research; their incremental deployment approach could turn messy plant procedures into reusable execution knowledge. The company also says it works with international manufacturers. But those relationships are unnamed, and neither deployment outcomes nor reuse are documented. I can make exceptions for an exceptional demonstrated founder, an unusually efficient working prototype, or an emerging user network before monetization. This dossier does not establish those override conditions. There is also no documented integrity problem or financing pressure; the concern is the business mechanism.

I would pass with my own money today. The 485 LinkedIn followers and incubator participation establish visibility, while public pricing, realized revenue, and contribution economics remain unknown. Those are uncertainties, not evidence of failure. My flip condition is one independently referenceable, paid multi-plant deployment showing that a subsequent plant requires materially fewer engineering hours because horizontal. owns and reuses validated execution knowledge from the first. That would begin demonstrating that customer work compounds into a product asset.
