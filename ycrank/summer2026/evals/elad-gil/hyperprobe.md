# HyperProbe -- Elad Gil Evaluation

HyperProbe’s founders previously built Transporter.city and spent years operating HyperTest, but HyperProbe has no disclosed revenue for the current product. That pairing is the analytical signature: HyperProbe is a proven-shipping-founder bet with an unproven usage curve. Shailendra Singh and Karan Raina understand production-traffic tooling; the question is whether HyperProbe is *just working* or whether two testimonials are a false signal.

**Perplexity** is the high rung for iterative shipping because Aravind repeatedly returned with the prior week’s discussion built. HyperProbe sits below that but above a credential-only founder profile: Singh and Raina jointly shipped Transporter.city, founded HyperTest in 2019, raised $1.5 million, and reached more than 100 product teams. HyperProbe diverges from Perplexity because the dossier shows prior-product persistence, not dated evidence that HyperProbe’s founders are shipping customer-requested artifacts between conversations.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism: activity without evidence of durable economic leadership can conceal structurally inferior outcomes. HyperProbe has a CheQ Digital production-mismatch testimonial and a Housing.com same-hour race-condition fix, but HyperProbe publishes no revenue, retention, deployment count, or growth rate. For an AI-adjacent developer product, “Free to Start” plus two company-selected stories is closer to pilot tourism than Harvey’s “$3B → $5B → $8B” pattern.

Ninety percent AI-tool adoption among software-development professionals in 2025 creates a credible recent catalyst, while 46% developer distrust creates the need for runtime verification. Over 18 months, HyperProbe can compound if every coding agent becomes more useful after gaining safe access to unlogged production state; each successful diagnosis then expands probe usage and organizational deployment. HyperProbe’s catalyst nevertheless remains inferred rather than demonstrated through a post-catalyst buying curve, so the market has re-opened technologically but not yet commercially.

Dynatrace acquired Rookout and Datadog acquired Ozcode, showing that live debugging may be an observability feature rather than an independent distribution layer. HyperProbe’s index score flips upward if coding agents—not observability suites—become the control plane and HyperProbe becomes the standard runtime-evidence API every agent must touch. Today HyperProbe must install an in-process SDK, pass security review, and compete against vendors already inside production contracts, so HyperProbe is fighting for distribution rather than automatically indexing the growth of coding agents.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow infrastructure on top of a validated hard layer can avoid science risk. HyperProbe assumes existing JVM and Node instrumentation, then adds non-blocking probes, local PII redaction, approval controls, and a 0.5% CPU cutoff; HyperProbe does not require new model architecture or new systems science. The remaining miracle is commercial—convincing enterprises to permit agent-mediated production inspection—which is substantial but singular. The known Color exception therefore applies structurally, unlike the multi-miracle science projects I avoid.

**Lightrun’s** $26 million of funding and the Rookout and Ozcode acquisitions suggest my 36-month forecast: HyperProbe reaches roughly $3–7 million ARR and is acquired by Sentry for under $100 million as a production-debugging capability rather than becoming a standalone category leader. HyperProbe’s strong shipping history can produce a valuable product, but HyperProbe’s weak current-product revenue evidence and lack of an index-like distribution position make a strategic feature acquisition more likely than a Stripe-scale independent outcome.

HyperTest’s 100-plus product teams form the strongest bull case against that forecast: HyperProbe could convert an existing developer audience into the default runtime context layer for coding agents, while the CheQ Digital and Housing.com outcomes show concrete user value. The Anduril and Color known exceptions say experienced operators can overcome incumbent and security friction when they isolate one miracle, and HyperProbe arguably does. The evidence that still wins is attribution: nearly every scale metric belongs to HyperTest, while HyperProbe has no paid pricing, moat, retention, or disclosed usage curve. I would flip to Invest if HyperProbe reaches $1 million ARR with greater than 20% month-over-month growth across at least 25 paying organizations without relying on bundled observability distribution.
