# Archangel Health -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Archangel Health changed from helping surgery centers with CMS TEAM in April 2026 to building medical AI infrastructure by October. That could be the most important fact in the dossier. What broke, and what did the founders do about it? The two descriptions establish a change of product, but neither explains the observation that caused it or how quickly the founders responded. I can't distinguish a useful correction from a change of destination. Nor is there a documented obstacle followed by a founder-built workaround. Those are missing observations, not evidence of passive founders.

The current product addresses a concrete problem. An agent that knows which medication to prescribe but fails to enter the order hasn't done the job. Sandboxed EHR environments with physician grading could expose that difference. Archangel's indexed product description supplies some technical substance, and its [YC profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/archangel-health) says frontier labs and medical AI companies already use the infrastructure. But there is no inspectable task, measured result, or customer account establishing what the system actually does. Stanford's reported 46% completion on harder PhysicianBench tasks demonstrates the difficulty of the problem; it doesn't demonstrate Archangel's solution.

The strongest argument for investing is that the founders may already have found a small group of users who badly need this. Frontier labs developing clinical agents are a much sharper niche than “healthcare AI.” Patel's work at SimCare and Cedars-Sinai gives him relevant exposure, while Bhatia's prior business reportedly reached $70,000 in annual revenue. These are substantive reasons to take the team seriously. Archangel also resembles the developer infrastructure I like: a reusable environment in which other people build things. Yet the reported usage doesn't establish repeat dependence, and the claimed physician network doesn't establish a difficult substrate already assembled. The working-product and unmistakable-pull exceptions therefore remain unproven.

There is real schlep here: recruiting physicians, resolving disagreements between graders, handling consent, and making EHR tasks behave realistically. But the dossier doesn't connect Archangel's origin to a specific problem either founder personally encountered. Nor does it show which part of that work customers cannot readily replace with public benchmarking infrastructure or services from Centaur and iMerit. The advertised $25–60+ per case is contributor compensation, not customer pricing. It tells me there is labor to pay for, without telling me whether each engagement produces a reusable asset. A bespoke evaluation business becomes more interesting when successive customers make the product better and cheaper to deliver.

I would pass on Archangel Health today. I see no documented dependence on a major partner, premature delegation, or refusal to accept contrary evidence; being Berkeley students establishes none of those red flags. The decisive gap is affirmative evidence of resourcefulness, and the operational gate fails there. My flip condition is one customer-verified deployment record showing a blocked clinical workflow, the founders' shipped workaround within weeks, and subsequent repeat use of the resulting environment. That single sequence would connect the people, the artifact, and the need. The current dossier leaves those connections open.
