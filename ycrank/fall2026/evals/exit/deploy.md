# Deploy -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic sale to a warehouse automation vendor seeking to add mixed-item returns, kitting, and packing to its existing offering. Deploy’s retrofit workcell and RoboJudge could give that buyer a way to automate more workflows at existing customer sites. The acquisition rationale depends on whether intervention data can reduce remote operator time across sites. That benefit remains unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The memo records a trade-show demonstration in which Deploy packed more than 800 pens after about 30 minutes of fine-tuning data collection. Deploy advertises pricing about 50% below labor cost. A paid rollout across sites could test whether stations meet agreed throughput on varied item mixes and generate positive contribution after hardware, deployment, and remote staffing costs. The demo and the founders’ deployment and robotics experience support attempting that test. Paying-customer count, production reliability, and the capital and time required for rollout remain unknown. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at $10 million post-money, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at a full pro rata sale. The sale cases below are illustrative sensitivities. Each assumes $10 million in net debt and other deal adjustments, no further senior claims or preference payments, and an eligible holder selling its entire stake for cash by year eight.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Holder cash |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $50 million enterprise value less $10 million leaves $40 million | $1.2 million (2.4×) |
| Strong sale | $110 million enterprise value less $10 million leaves $100 million | $3 million (6×). Selling 80% of the stake would yield $2.4 million (4.8×). |
| Prolonged private | The company reinvests available cash and makes no distribution or share purchase | $0 realized within eight years |

The memo gives no transaction prices or quantified buyer economics to support either sale value. Transfer rights for the software, intervention data, and customer contracts are unverified, as is holder eligibility. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Plus One already routes failed picks to remote operators. An incumbent could extend that capability to Deploy’s target workflows, especially if Deploy needs frequent interventions or must retrain each site from scratch. That would weaken both the reason to acquire Deploy and the price a buyer would pay. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the memo leaves both the incumbent’s ability to substitute and the strongest attainable sale price unresolved. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

How much would owning Deploy be worth to a warehouse automation vendor once its workcells are proven across paid customer sites? A paid rollout that meets agreed throughput and records remote labor, hardware, and deployment costs would give a prospective buyer a basis to quantify the benefit of acquiring Deploy. Move to Reach out if those buyer economics support clearing the holder’s $2.5 million cash objective after the waterfall. Move to Pass if the strongest credible route leaves the holder materially below that objective.
