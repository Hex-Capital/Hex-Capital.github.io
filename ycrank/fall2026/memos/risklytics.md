## The Idea

- **Problem:** Robotics and autonomous-system companies need insurance for customer deployments, but conventional policies can exclude AI-related losses, including exposures falling between general liability and professional liability coverage ([Gold interview, July 2026](https://datainnovation.org/2026/07/5-qs-with-samuel-gold-founder-of-risklytics/)).
- **Approach:** Software drafts applications from company descriptions and public records, customers confirm the answers, and licensed brokers approach insurers and review policy wording before binding ([Risklytics website](https://www.risklytics.ai/)).
- **Differentiation:** Risklytics emphasizes physical autonomy and AI exclusions, whereas RiskCube emphasizes startup insurance procurement and certificates, and Koop combines insurance with security and compliance workflows ([Risklytics](https://www.risklytics.ai/about/), [RiskCube](https://riskcube.com/), [Koop](https://www.koop.ai/)).
- **Business Model:** Carrier-paid commissions, usually 10–20% of premiums according to the founders, with no additional brokerage fee and individually underwritten premiums rather than published rates ([founder launch, August 2026](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49451495), [pricing page](https://www.risklytics.ai/pricing/)).
- **TAM/SAM:** No public TAM/SAM data found for this specific segment; adjacent global AI insurance premiums could reach $4.8 billion by 2032 at approximately 80% CAGR, a premium-market forecast rather than brokerage revenue ([Deloitte, August 2025](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/multimedia/videos/ai-insurance-market-potential.html)).
- **GTM / Distribution:** Gold describes partnerships with wholesalers and managing general agents, plus distribution through YC and Harvard startup networks ([Center for Data Innovation, July 2026](https://datainnovation.org/2026/07/5-qs-with-samuel-gold-founder-of-risklytics/)).

## Defensibility

- **Moat today:** Public assets include producer licensing and a compiled AI-exclusion tracker covering 736 filings across 49 states and 33 insurers; these establish operational capabilities without demonstrating exclusivity ([licensing](https://www.risklytics.ai/about/), [tracker, September 2026 dataset](https://www.risklytics.ai/ai-exclusions/)).
- **Future moat:** [Inferred]: Placement outcomes, renewals, and carrier relationships could improve matching over time, but public evidence does not establish proprietary loss data or customer switching costs.
- **Market structure:** [Inferred]: No structural barrier identified at this stage, because existing licensed brokers can pursue the same carriers and review the same policy forms.
- **Commoditization risk:** [Inferred]: Application automation and policy comparisons are reproducible features, with RiskCube already advertising digital purchasing, exclusion reviews, and policy management ([RiskCube](https://riskcube.com/)).

## Key Risks

- **Carrier capacity dependency:** Risklytics brokers coverage subject to third-party underwriting and availability; Gold describes wholesaler and MGA partnerships as its response to carrier-access challenges ([company disclosures](https://www.risklytics.ai/about/), [interview](https://datainnovation.org/2026/07/5-qs-with-samuel-gold-founder-of-risklytics/)).
- **Policy interpretation:** Founders report differing underwriter interpretations of whether robot motor commands constitute generative-AI output, with policy review and explicit coverage wording used to address ambiguity ([founder launch](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49451495)).
- **Geographic licensing:** State-dependent producer authorization constrains placement availability; the company publishes licenses and asks customers to confirm their state ([Risklytics licensing disclosure](https://www.risklytics.ai/about/)).
- **Name disambiguation:** Older results concern German Risklytics GmbH and Oliver Demetz, whose records were excluded from this dossier ([FLF, 2022, via search snippet](https://www.kreditwesen.de/mimas/system/files/content/inserts/2022/flf2-2022_dsd_demetz.pdf)).
