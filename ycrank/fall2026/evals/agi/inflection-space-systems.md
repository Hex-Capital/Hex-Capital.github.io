# Inflection Space Systems -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Inflection’s strongest commercial case is selling a standardized spacecraft bus in batches to constellation operators facing long lead times for bespoke builds. Exponent’s common parts and single configuration could make production more repeatable. The memo identifies spacecraft manufacturing capacity as a constraint for constellation operators and leaves the link to a specific AI workload unestablished. It also describes fleet software for simulation and testing and planned industrial robots, with no demonstrated AI-driven cost reduction that would make a previously uneconomic mission viable. Both AGI paths score zero, which sets the action to Pass. (The Idea; Defensibility)

Inflection reports building more than half of the spacecraft stack in-house, including flight and ground software. That gives it some control over integration and testing. Whether repeated builds improve yields or cycle times remains unverified, and Apex and Muon already offer repeat-production alternatives with flown spacecraft. The founders’ spacecraft engineering, integration, and test automation experience supports the execution score. Inflection reports hardware fabrication and testing underway and a Q1 2028 launch booking, with flight qualification still ahead. (Defensibility; Founders & Team; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

The launch booking is a delivery milestone. Customer demand stays at the default because the memo identifies no signed payload customer, funded trial, or spacecraft order. Scale economics also stays at the default. The published $700,000 unit price for a 100-unit order sets a revenue assumption, while labor, yield, rework, and tooling costs per qualified spacecraft remain unknown. These gaps limit confidence in the commercial case. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Facts; Key Risks)

### Countercase

The strongest case for outreach is that Inflection could use its in-house testing software and standardized hardware to automate enough engineering work to bring a previously excluded constellation mission within budget. The published volume discounts and the CTO’s test automation experience make that scenario worth testing. Its AGI fit depends on AI driving the savings and on those savings crossing a buyer’s affordability threshold. The memo establishes neither link, so the Pass gate holds. (The Idea; Defensibility; Founders & Team)

### Decisive diligence question

Can AI-assisted integration and testing bring the fully loaded cost of a qualified Exponent batch below the budget for a mission a buyer previously shelved on cost? A representative batch comparison that isolates AI-driven savings, includes rework and failed units, and is backed by that buyer’s conditional purchase commitment would move the action to Reach out.
