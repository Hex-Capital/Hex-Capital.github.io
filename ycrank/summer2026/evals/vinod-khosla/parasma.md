# Parasma -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Sean Cole’s 200,000-neuron Doom experiment makes Parasma a capital-light option on capital-hostile science: Parasma’s highest dimension is technical risk, while its lowest is required-capital fit. That pairing matters. Parasma is not yet building the biological computer; Parasma is attempting to become the learning layer on Cortical Labs’ CL1. If biological substrates eventually displace part of GPU computing, the economic mechanism that breaks is semiconductor-bound scaling—energy, fabrication, and accelerator scarcity. That consequence warrants tolerating a 90% failure probability. It does not yet warrant pretending a software tenant owns the building.

**Commonwealth Fusion** (2019) is the high rung for technical risk: several falsifiable miracles had to work, and the capital market’s refusal was part of the opportunity. Parasma shares that mechanism. Parasma must prove that living neurons—not its surrounding decoder—perform useful learning, tame biological noise and non-stationarity, and scale beyond demonstrations such as token prediction and Doom. Those are real miracles, not ordinary SaaS execution. Parasma diverges from Commonwealth because its central experiment runs on another company’s CL1 hardware, its public code is open source, and replication could not isolate neuronal learning from structured software signals. The science is high-rung; ownership of the science is not. That is the stack-wrapper red flag in its most interesting possible form.

**DoorDash** (2013) exposed the low-rung failure mechanism for required-capital fit: a potentially enormous outcome can still leave my capital depth irrelevant at entry. Parasma presently looks even smaller on this axis. Parasma describes algorithms and infrastructure, depends on Cortical Labs’ API, and has no disclosed fabrication program, proprietary wetware, or roadmap requiring more than $50M before a commercial milestone. The inferred licensing model may be excellent venture economics, but any competent generalist can finance software built on commercially accessible CL1 hardware. My unstated consumer and fintech exceptions prove I can fund capital-light companies; they do not turn check-size mismatch into an advantage.

Cortical Labs’ programmable CL1 determines whether Parasma’s consequence compounds over the next 18 months. If every experiment gives Parasma proprietary stimulation policies, biological-response data, and benchmarks transferable across substrates, Parasma can become the control plane for a new compute category. If the policies remain CL1-specific, Parasma becomes outsourced R&D for an $11M-funded hardware supplier while The Biological Computing Co., already funded with $25M, integrates the learning stack itself. Parasma’s maximum ambition could break GPU-centric compute economics, but no public TAM, energy advantage, accuracy advantage, or scaling curve yet connects the Doom demonstration to that outcome. Parasma’s consciousness note introduces governance risk, although bioethics is not currently the product’s selling point; that red flag is latent rather than decisive.

Sean Cole skipped a bachelor’s degree, earned an AI MSc with distinction, and implemented closed-loop learning on living neurons before founding Parasma. For Parasma’s founder-defiance score to reach the top rung, Cole would need to state and validate a position that the biological-computing field itself rejects—for example, that useful substrate learning can be separated from decoder learning and demonstrated under blinded ablation. Shipping Doom in roughly one week shows unusual agency, and moving from diffusion in spiking neural networks to wetware training is intellectually non-consensus. Parasma still operates inside an emerging thesis shared by Cortical Labs, TBC, and Intactis rather than contradicting a documented expert consensus. Credentials are not the signal; a falsified orthodoxy would be.

**Slash** (2024) is the relevant single-anchor parallel for learning rate because its documented pivot—not its fintech category—overrode weaker consequence fit. Parasma supplies no equivalent “we were wrong” moment. Cole’s path from spiking-neural-network research to Doom and then Parasma is fast execution along one line, not evidence of abandoning a cherished thesis after contradictory data. Parasma deserves stage-adjusted credit for publishing random- and zero-signal ablations, which creates the machinery for learning. It does not deserve credit for a pivot that has not happened: no evidence—applying the absent-evidence default.

The founder reported no product and no revenue on May 17, 2026. My falsifiable 36-month forecast is that by July 2029 Parasma will have pivoted from replacing AI compute to selling biological-compute benchmarking and control software, will remain below $1M ARR, and will still depend materially on Cortical Labs’ substrate. Parasma’s technical risk is large enough to generate valuable experimental tooling, while Parasma’s low capital ownership and open-source implementation make capture of the underlying compute layer unlikely. The most probable outcome is modest. The probability-weighted outcome is not the point; consequence over probability is.

The `doom-neuron` repository’s 250 GitHub stars and coverage from *Scientific American* and *The Guardian* make the opposing bull case sting. Parasma could exploit commoditized CL1 access exactly as software platforms have exploited standardized hardware: Cole’s rapid implementation becomes a learning-rate signal, repeated experiments create the proprietary dataset, and capital-light distribution becomes the **DoorDash** exception rather than a weakness. That case loses today because independent replication could not establish that living neurons learned, Parasma discloses no moat, and competitors controlling hardware or integrated stacks have already raised $11M and $25M. One condition would flip my verdict to Strong Invest: a third-party benchmark showing Parasma-trained neurons achieve at least a 10× energy-efficiency advantage over matched silicon on an unseen continual-learning task across CL1 and one independent biological substrate.
