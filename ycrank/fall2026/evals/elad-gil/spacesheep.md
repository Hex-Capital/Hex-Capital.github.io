# Spacesheep -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Coding agents are increasing output volume; teams now need somewhere to review that output together. Spacesheep becomes a large company if it can turn that review bottleneck into ownership of a recurring team workflow. The controlling variable is whether collaborators consistently complete reviews faster in Spacesheep than inside their existing workspace. The dossier identifies the problem, but supplies no comparative evidence that interactive documents solve it. Publishing more attractive output does not itself establish that market mechanism.

The strongest evidence is architectural. Multiple agents can publish into shared spaces, teammates can comment at specific locations, agents can make revisions, and versions and semantic search preserve context. That is a real operational loop. Better models could increase the usefulness of the material while Spacesheep retains the discussion and revision history. This earns meaningful workflow credit. It does not yet establish a data moat: the dossier provides no reproduction-cost estimate, and exportability makes exclusive ownership of customer content a weak assumption.

The market opening and the competitive threat come from the same change. Hosted Claude Artifacts arrived in June 2025; Notion subsequently introduced external agents and, by July 2026, interactive HTML blocks. Those developments make Spacesheep’s proposed workflow technically credible, but they also put overlapping functionality inside existing distribution. There is no quantified improvement in review time, delivery cost, or customer economics. At $10 per member per month for Team, Spacesheep needs enough recurring value to justify another workspace purchase. Published prices establish a monetization mechanism, not willingness to pay.

The bull case is stronger than the launch numbers suggest. Michael Makarov worked on internal deployment and the first CC Artifacts version at Anthropic, giving him direct knowledge of this problem. A neutral collaboration surface could also serve teams using several agents better than any single agent vendor. James Briggs’s named use for coding plans, explanations, and reviews provides some demand evidence. But 22 Chrome extension users and 180 Product Hunt upvotes on October 27 establish initial discovery, with little information about organizational adoption. Makarov’s relevant experience supports execution credibility; it does not document the costly commitment or prior startup learning required for additional founder-conviction credit.

I would pass on a personal check today. My pattern of investing across a technology discontinuity makes this category worth following, but does not establish that another independent workspace captures the value. Spacesheep already has more workflow substance than a simple output formatter; the unresolved issue is whether that workflow earns durable distribution against incumbent convergence. The research-stage exception does not apply: the dossier establishes application-building expertise, rather than scarce frontier research with a documented capital path. One verifiable flip condition would change my decision: a paid cohort of ten teams retaining weekly collaborative review usage for three months, with measured review-time reductions against their previous tools. That would directly test whether Spacesheep owns a valuable bottleneck.
