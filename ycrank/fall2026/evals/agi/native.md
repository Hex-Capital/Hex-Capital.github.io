# Native -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Native has a plausible **industry-enabled** path in helping small businesses sustain regular social campaigns when owner time is scarce and agency service is out of budget. Its listed $79 monthly plan covers one brand. Native uses website and brand context to rank ideas, generate creative, and publish organic posts and Meta ads with optional approval. That workflow could reduce the owner time per successful campaign enough to make regular publishing viable. The scenario assumes review and rework stay low. The memo gives no comparable baseline for owner time, agency cost, or publishing cadence. If similarly capable models become widely available, Native could still deliver value through brand history, approvals, scheduling, and publishing. Whether buyers will pay separately for that workflow remains open. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Native earns zero for **building blocks of superintelligence**. It uses AI to deliver marketing and supplies no input whose scarcity constrains an AI workload or buildout. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Native reports more than $2M ARR and roughly 2,200 paying customers. HAVN Storage reported 9,495 Facebook and Instagram impressions after adoption versus 2,322 in the prior comparison period. These signals support demand and real use, while leaving repeat demand among previously excluded businesses and conversion lift unverified. Native launched publicly in March 2026, and its founders previously shipped Wepost together. Reliable publishing and exception handling at scale remain unverified. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

Native’s approval, edit, and performance history could improve recommendations for individual brands and make switching harder. The opt-out and lack of demonstrated ranking lift limit that advantage. The CEO reports that acquisition cost fell from roughly NOK 1,800 to NOK 800 per new customer. Without retention and delivery costs, that figure gives no contribution estimate for the next subscriber. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Native may save time for customers who already market regularly, leaving the same buyers and uses in place. The paying-customer count does not show who gained access to sustained marketing, and HAVN’s impressions comparison does not establish a lower cost per successful campaign. Google already derives campaign assets from a website, and Meta offers overlapping AI creative tools on the paid channel Native serves. If buyers can sustain the same cadence at comparable cost with those tools, the industry-enabled path falls to zero and the action becomes Pass. The end-to-end workflow gives Native a specific mechanism to test, supporting Watch today. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can small businesses that previously could not sustain regular publishing do so with Native at a cost they will keep paying? A paid cohort that renews after sustaining its target cadence, with measured subscription, owner review, and rework costs below what it would incur using comparable AI and native platform tools at similar ad spend, would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that these buyers were already able to sustain that cadence at comparable cost would move it to Pass.
