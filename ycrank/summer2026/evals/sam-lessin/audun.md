# Audun -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first underwriting line is blank: no post-money valuation, no competing-term-sheet count, and no evidence that Audun is receiving a genuinely scarce first check. YC participation does not answer whether the round is cheap or auctioned. With my own money, I cannot treat financing silence as proof of non-consensus pricing—especially when at least three funded competitors already automate overlapping collection workflows.

The more important blank is what the capital flips. Audun describes reminders, negotiation, payment plans, payouts, and human escalation, but those are product functions, not a finite experiment. Is the hypothesis that automated negotiation produces higher recovery than scripted outreach without increasing disputes? What result, by what date, dramatically changes the value of the business? The dossier supplies neither metric nor deadline. A creditor walkthrough and a debtor portal “coming soon” indicate construction, not a named venture moment.

The bull case is real enough to sting. Norway replaced its Debt Collection Act in May 2026, creating a moment when creditors may reconsider old workflows. Jacob Hambro has operating and enterprise-facing experience, Peter Flo brings Midjourney and data-science exposure, and the four-person team could combine AI with supervised escalation for disputes and vulnerable debtors. If Audun embeds itself during that regulatory transition, case-level histories connecting channel, timing, negotiation terms, and repayment could become proprietary data rather than another generic model wrapper.

But none of that dark pool exists in the evidence today. There is no disclosed creditor deployment, paid pilot, recovered balance, repeat usage, licence, case volume, or customer action caused by an owned workflow. AgentCollect, Altur, and Domu already cover calls, texts, email, negotiation, or payment plans, so conversational automation is demonstrably buildable by multiple teams. The proprietary dataset is an inferred future by-product, while the current distribution mechanism is a rented direct-sales funnel ending in “book a walkthrough.”

Deleting the next financing makes the problem sharper. Audun discloses no pricing, recovery fee, gross margin, burn, runway, or controllable cash metric. Automated collections can look like software until human escalations, compliance review, licensing, and consequential model errors turn it back into a labor business. The proposed system joins creditors, regulated collection operations, and vulnerable debtors, yet no founder has disclosed debt-collection experience or shipped operating receipts on both sides of that bridge. Hambro’s Aviant work and Flo’s Midjourney experience show adjacent execution capacity, but they do not establish collection access or regulatory judgment.

I am passing because there is no hard exception evidence strong enough to override the missing price and experiment gates. The verifiable flip condition is a paid Norwegian creditor deployment showing at least 1,000 completed cases, higher net recovery than its prior process, stable human-escalation costs, and no material compliance breach. That would prove Audun owns a functioning workflow that causes payment and can generate proprietary outcome data; until then, I am being asked to finance a crowded AI application before either the deal or the base business has been underwritten.
