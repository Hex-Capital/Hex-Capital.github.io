# TryNearby -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is not whether TryNearby works. It plainly works. The missing fact is what broke while the founders were building it, what they did next, and how fast that changed the result. The dossier identifies a customer obstacle—restaurant owners cannot continually negotiate with creators—and shows a shipped workaround. But it does not identify a specific obstacle encountered by the team or the founder-built maneuver that overcame it. I cannot infer relentless resourcefulness from a successful product alone.

The users supply much stronger evidence. More than 120 restaurants pay $399 monthly after ten months, implying nearly $48,000 in monthly subscription revenue at list price. Restaurant count is reportedly growing 37% month over month, monthly retention exceeds 90%, and owner referrals generate roughly half of new customers. This is a narrow well: local restaurants repeatedly pay to avoid creator coordination, and satisfied owners recruit other owners. The 2,500 videos and 35 million views matter less than payment, retention, and referrals, but the reported restaurant revenue increases suggest the underlying job is economic rather than merely promotional.

There is also a real technical artifact. The iMessage agent handles more than 10,000 creator texts each month across onboarding, invitations, scheduling, reminders, rejection, and rescheduling. Thousands of participating creators generate preference, travel, and availability data. This is not a diagram for software the founders plan to build after funding. It is deployed machinery coordinating a messy marketplace. The proof is somewhat discounted because there is no independently measured reliability benchmark, but it matches the concrete pre-round proof spikes I tend to trust.

The origin is unusually credible even without a documented founding anecdote. Yousef Abdelfattah helped build FaZe Clan; Obaida Albaroudi and Ahmad Ibrahim previously built creator-campaign infrastructure together at Clipping Exe, including systems tracking 10 billion views. These founders did not arrive at “local creators for restaurants” by combining two fashionable nouns. They came from creator operations. The schlep is substantial: local supply must be recruited city by city, visits rescheduled, disclosures monitored, and results explained to restaurant owners. Their history earns credit for choosing this burden, though not for how they respond when it resists them.

The strongest case for investing is that the demand itself should override the missing founder anecdote. At pre-seed, 120 paying customers, high retention, rapid growth, and referral-led acquisition are far more informative than polished claims about character. No stated red flag is active: distribution does not depend on one unnamed partner, the founders are selling before hiring a sales organization, and two cofounders have already worked together. The concrete-proof pattern applies. The known-exception logic nearly applies too, because unmistakable behavior can sometimes replace a failed prior.

I am not quite willing to use that override. Every headline traction number is company-reported, the website was unavailable during research, outcome attribution is weak, and the current system has proved liquidity only in Southern California. Hyper and Tablevert show that automation alone is not a structural barrier, while TikTok and Instagram control much of the resulting distribution. Most importantly, there is no evidence yet of TryNearby changing route after contrary evidence—the running-back test remains unobserved. My 36-month base case is a useful regional business reaching roughly $3–7 million ARR but struggling to reproduce its density across cities. I would flip to Invest if the founders opened one market outside Southern California and, within 90 days, reached 25 paying restaurants with at least 85% month-three retention through a documented founder-built workaround to the initial creator-liquidity problem.
