# Hop Aero -- Elad Gil Evaluation

$1.25 million from the U.S. Air Force makes **Hop Aero** an execution-rich defense vehicle bet, not yet an index on defense logistics. **Hop Aero** scores highest on founder shipping: Matija Milenovic scaled Ukrainian loitering-munition production from 10 to more than 100 units monthly, while Jacob Balaj shipped across SpaceX, Virgin Orbit, Masten, and SpinLaunch. The lowest dimension is structural distribution: Rook must win procurement vehicle by vehicle against Inversion, Grid Aero, Sierra Space, and potentially SpaceX rather than becoming a rail every defense-logistics entrant automatically touches.

**Perplexity** is the high rung for iterative shipping because Aravind Srinivas repeatedly turned conversations into working artifacts within a week. **Hop Aero** belongs just below that rung: **Hop Aero** built and tested a prototype in under 30 days, completed a tethered flight, hot-fired a company-built engine and igniter, and previously shipped propulsion IP and scaled weapons production. The divergence is that Perplexity’s iterations reached users continuously; Hop Aero’s public artifacts establish speed but not a repeated operational feedback loop with military units.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism for index position: being a capable number two in a market whose economics accrue disproportionately to the default network. **Hop Aero** faces a version of that problem before a default has even emerged. Inversion has raised $54 million, Grid Aero $26 million, and Sierra Space more than $1.95 billion, while **Hop Aero** has disclosed no proprietary distribution channel, qualification moat, or recurring launch network. Rook is differentiated by its 250-kilogram, container-launched regional mission, but differentiation is not an index; Hop Aero still has to win share against several capitalized architectures.

The Air Force’s transition from Rocket Cargo research into a formal Point-to-Point Delivery prototype program is a real recent catalyst for **Hop Aero**, not TAM theater. Over the next 18 months, that catalyst can compound through funded demonstrations, program-office access, and procurement learning: every successful test could make **Hop Aero** more credible for the next contract. The limitation is concentration. A single Air Force program can also become enterprise-pilot tourism with longer timelines, shifting requirements, and site approvals, so the catalyst scores well below Anduril’s post-Ukraine demand shock.

$1.25 million is meaningful pre-seed buying behavior, but it is not yet a revenue curve for **Hop Aero**. I would move **Hop Aero** into the Harvey-like “just working” tier if the next two quarters showed a second named defense counterparty, follow-on funding from the Air Force, and contracted value compounding above 20% quarter over quarter. Without that evidence, the contract could be nonrecurring R&D revenue, and requests for introductions to Navy and PACOM suggest distribution remains prospective rather than repeatable.

**Color Genomics** worked because the hard scientific capability—commodity sequencing—already existed, leaving workflow and distribution as the primary miracle. **Hop Aero** is the inverse today: **Hop Aero** must scale from a tethered prototype to a reusable vehicle carrying 250 kilograms over 750 kilometers, survive hypersonic re-entry, autonomously land on unprepared terrain, secure launch-and-landing approvals, and clear defense procurement. Those are partially coupled engineering problems, but they remain multiple independent failure surfaces. Hop Aero is skating for the puck in defense logistics while still needing too many things about the puck to become technically and regulatorily true.

Inversion Space’s $54 million capitalization illustrates where I expect the market to consolidate: within 36 months, **Hop Aero** will not have demonstrated repeatable 250-kilogram, 750-kilometer Rook missions and will instead be acquired by **Anduril** for propulsion, avionics, and founder talent in a transaction below $75 million. **Hop Aero** can build valuable artifacts because Milenovic and Balaj have unusually strong shipping histories, but Hop Aero lacks the index position and capital base required to commercialize an independent rocket-cargo network before better-funded platforms absorb the category.

**Anduril** is the strongest objection to that forecast. Defense procurement reopened, experienced operators converted early contracts into compounding programs, and a seemingly capital-intensive hardware business became software infrastructure for modernization. **Hop Aero** has the same sharpest ingredients: direct defense experience, a $1.25 million Air Force contract, a 30-day prototype cycle, and a specific Point-to-Point Delivery program. The analogy breaks because Anduril’s founders had already shipped scaled systems and isolated the central miracle around software-led procurement, while Hop Aero has only a tethered small-scale flight against full-scale hypersonic, landing, regulatory, and production requirements. One condition flips my verdict to Invest: Hop Aero completes an end-to-end autonomous flight carrying at least 250 kilograms over 750 kilometers and pairs it with a second named defense program worth at least $5 million.
