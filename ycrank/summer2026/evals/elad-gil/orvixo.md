# Orvixo -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Orvixo’s factory-specific constraint model makes Orvixo a technically plausible workflow layer whose highest signal is single-miracle architecture and whose lowest is the complete absence of a revenue or usage curve. The underlying optimisation, ERP integration, and language-model capabilities already exist; Orvixo is not waiting for new physics. The bet is that natural-language configuration makes advanced planning usable without specialist customization. That is one product-and-distribution miracle, albeit a difficult one. What is missing is any indication that it is “just working”: no revenue, customer, pilot, repeat usage, or production-performance result. In the current AI cycle, a credible architecture without a visible curve is a prototype, not yet a company I underwrite with conviction.

**Color Genomics** is the high rung on single-miracle architecture: workflow and data infrastructure built on commodity sequencing rather than a bet on new chemistry. Orvixo shares that load-bearing mechanism by placing a configurable planning layer on top of existing ERP, MRP, shop-floor data, symbolic planners, and optimisation algorithms. Orvixo diverges where Color had deliberately bounded the hard layer: Orvixo must still reconcile incomplete factory data, encode interacting labour and tooling constraints, generate operationally safe schedules, and persuade planners to obey them. Those are coupled execution risks, but they are not independent scientific breakthroughs. Orvixo sits below Color and above capital-intensive Mistral-style foundation-model development on this axis: mostly a single miracle, not cleanly isolated yet.

**Lyft** is the low rung on visible velocity because its Series C case did not show a curve strong enough to overturn the structural concern that #2 economics in a network-effect market would remain inferior. Orvixo presents an even earlier version of that evidentiary problem: 86 LinkedIn followers, zero listed jobs, and no disclosed revenue, customer, pilot, deployment, or repeat usage. Those figures are not negative traction at pre-seed; they are simply no evidence, so I apply the 8/25 absent-evidence default. Orvixo may be conducting founder-led scheduling reviews, but a review is a false signal until a factory pays, reruns schedules, and expands the deployment. Harvey showed what “just working” looks like in dollars. Orvixo has not yet shown the first point on that curve.

The $1.744 billion APS segment growing at 20.4% annually is not itself Orvixo’s catalyst; TAM arithmetic does not re-open a market. The tier-flipping counterfactual is specific: since 2024, language models must have become reliable enough to translate planner language into auditable symbolic constraints, reducing months of Siemens Opcenter or Asprova customization to days. Orvixo cites research supporting that technical possibility, but Orvixo provides no deployment evidence showing that the shift changed factory purchasing behavior in the last 24 months. A named plant replacing spreadsheet overrides after a two-week implementation would move this dimension sharply upward. Without that, Orvixo is skating for a plausible puck in a steadily growing market, not exploiting a demonstrated Stripe-after-PayPal discontinuity.

Ethan Phillips brings Cambridge physics, Attio growth experience, and semiconductor analysis; Kolya Schwinge brings applied ML and a senior ML-engineering role at IFS. The relevant single-anchor parallel is **Perplexity**: Aravind Srinivas repeatedly returned a week later with the discussed artifact built. Orvixo’s founders have credible technical ingredients, and Kolya’s IFS experience is adjacent to the enterprise systems Orvixo must integrate, but the dossier names no independently shipped product, factory-planning deployment, or user-feedback iteration. Credentials establish capacity, not cadence. One sequence of dated releases tied to planner feedback would matter more than both Cambridge degrees because it would demonstrate that Orvixo can turn messy operating constraints into working software between customer conversations.

Siemens Opcenter APS, Asprova, Oden Technologies, and Sight Machine determine whether Orvixo compounds into an index or fights feature-by-feature for share. Over the next 18 months, Orvixo could become more valuable with every deployment if factory-specific constraint libraries shorten implementation for the next customer; that would create a workflow position analogous to an index on increasingly dynamic manufacturing. The opposite loop is equally plausible: every Orvixo factory remains bespoke, every integration consumes founder time, and incumbents add natural-language configuration to installed systems. Orvixo has no evidence that model histories are reusable or contractually controlled, so the compounding loop is hypothetical. Today Orvixo is closer to a vertical application competing against distribution-rich incumbents than to Stripe’s developer-channel default.

Orvixo’s 86 company followers and absence of any disclosed deployment lead to a falsifiable forecast: by July 2029, Orvixo will have narrowed from generalized autonomous factory planning to a scheduling copilot for UK automotive suppliers, with less than $2 million in ARR and human planners retaining final schedule authority. Orvixo’s technically credible single miracle should produce a usable product, but the missing velocity signal, bespoke integration burden, and incumbent distribution should prevent the broad “AI that runs factories” position from compounding quickly. The likely outcome is valuable vertical software or a services-heavy planning product, not an index on global manufacturing.

Kolya Schwinge’s IFS experience is the strongest bull case against that forecast: Orvixo may understand the incumbent integration surface better than the public dossier reveals, while the 20.4% market growth and post-2024 language-model-plus-symbolic-planning catalyst could make natural-language factory modeling the equivalent of Color’s workflow layer on commodity sequencing. That Known Exception genuinely stings because Orvixo does not require new science. The evidence that still wins is the proof gap: no named factory, validated schedule, implementation time, measured outcome, reusable constraint asset, or buying behavior. I would flip to Invest if Orvixo signs three named factories and compounds ARR above 20% month-over-month for six consecutive months while demonstrating that its second and third deployments require materially less configuration than its first.
