# Nitroduck -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest supported route is a strategic acquisition by a specialty-crop breeder or an agricultural technology platform. Pairwise’s licensing deal with Enza Zaden shows that a vegetable breeder will pay for access to editing tools. Nitroduck proposes a flat-fee service that fits into existing breeding workflows and estimates that developing a new trait costs $3 million. A buyer could have reason to own the service if it delivers repeatable savings across breeding programs and control of the technology is worth more than continued access through a service contract. The memo documents a public launch and reports a $500,000 convertible note, but provides no paid breeder pilot, measured savings, or acquisition pricing. The next milestone is a breeder-validated edited seed in a commercially relevant crop. The founders’ prior plant-engineering work supports their ability to attempt it. The milestone’s budget, timeline, and funding coverage remain open. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at $10 million post-money, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% pro rata ownership. The reported note’s conversion and priority terms are unknown. The sale cases below assume an eligible holder sells the full stake for cash within eight years, with debt and deal adjustments deducted from enterprise value and no further priority claims or holdbacks. The prices and deductions are illustrative assumptions. (Market & Traction)

- **Modest sale.** $40 million in enterprise value less $5 million in debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $1.05 million, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value less $10 million in debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $2.7 million, or 5.4×. If only half the stake can be sold on those terms, cash received falls to $1.35 million, or 2.7×.
- **Prolonged private operation.** If the company reinvests its cash and no eligible share purchase occurs, the holder realizes $0 within eight years. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route.

### Countercase

A breeder can already license editing tools, and Nitroduck’s proposed flat-fee model gives customers a way to buy the service without acquiring the company. Even a successful pilot could therefore support a service business while leaving the acquisition premium unproven. Plantik’s published patent application also raises a potential rights issue for a buyer, with overlap dependent on Nitroduck’s implementation. These risks leave ownership economics and deal feasibility unresolved. The evidence establishes neither a structural block nor a ceiling on credible sale proceeds, so the Pass gate remains open. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would the economics demonstrated in a paid breeder pilot justify a strategic buyer paying enough for Nitroduck’s transferable rights to deliver $2.5 million in cash to the benchmark holder? A breeder-validated pilot that quantifies savings in a commercial crop, paired with a buyer-backed indicative price range that clears the hurdle after deal adjustments and the holder waterfall, would move the action to Reach out.
