# Almanac -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The contested truth would be: a continuously reconstructed model of a company can become sufficiently accurate and trusted that employees delegate cross-tool work to it, rather than merely search with it. What would prove this wrong? Users consult the wiki but refuse to let the assistant act, or abandon it once incorrect actions accumulate. Almanac presents no shipped experiment, retention result, or independent accuracy evidence testing that proposition. “The AI that knows your company” is therefore a fashionable slogan surrounding a potentially important secret, not yet the secret itself.

The monopoly question is worse. Almanac targets company-wide knowledge rather than a bounded beachhead, while Glean already has 250 connectors and more than $100 million ARR, and Dust reports 300,000 agents across more than 3,000 organizations. The managed browser and iMessage interface are product features built partly on publicly available computer-use capabilities; they are not proprietary technology, network effects, scale economies, or brand. A company-specific wiki might eventually create switching costs, but resemblance to a moat is not a moat. Today this is competition, not value capture.

The founders are the strongest evidence. Kushagra Chitkara and Rohan Sharma overlapped at Harvard for roughly two years; the three active founders combine machine-learning research, data science, and production engineering; and they previously shipped CodeAlmanac to 791 GitHub stars and 71 forks. That establishes some shared execution and suggests the core work is internal. It does not establish Divit Sheth’s working history with the others, founder ownership, salaries, or economic exposure, so the foundation appears credible rather than exceptional.

The strongest bull case is the product transition itself. The team learned from a developer-facing knowledge tool, moved toward a larger company-wide problem, set a concrete $250-per-user monthly price, and arrived just as computer-use models made non-API workflows addressable. If the managed browser becomes the missing execution layer between fragmented company systems, the prior GitHub adoption could represent genuine product intuition rather than irrelevant legacy traction. But no current customer, revenue, repeated usage, or named buyer validates that transition. A three-day trial and an inferred self-service-to-enterprise motion do not explain how a high-trust, high-permission product clears security review or supports its acquisition cost.

Nor does a known exception rescue the case. There is no verified zero-to-one technical event, persistent transaction behavior, exclusive data, or independently validated result that overrides the failed secret and monopoly gates. The current pattern is the one I avoid: an AI application entering a celebrated market after heavily financed competitors have already converged on search, connectors, agents, and action-taking. My 36-month base case is that Almanac remains below $3 million ARR and pivots back toward developer knowledge tooling because that is where its only demonstrated adoption exists.

I pass. The team may be capable, but capable founders in a commodity market create a better competitor, not necessarily a great company. I would reopen the decision upon one verifiable condition: twenty companies in one narrowly defined segment retaining paid Almanac deployments for twelve months because recurring cross-tool actions perform materially better than Glean or Dust. Until then, this is founder promise without a proved secret, monopoly, or distribution system.
