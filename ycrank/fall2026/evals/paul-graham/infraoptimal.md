# InfraOptimal -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The question I want answered is what Cassidy and Kocheta did when something refused to work. InfraOptimal’s September research gives me an actual experiment: roughly 3–5.6% lower decoder time on an Apple M2 Pro, alongside a scoped Z3 equivalence proof. That is more useful than a compiler diagram. But it establishes a technical artifact, not yet the founder behavior I most want to fund. The dossier does not give me a named obstacle, the workaround these founders shipped, and how quickly it changed the situation. I cannot fill that gap with their résumés.

The technical work deserves credit on its own terms. Cassidy’s processor-validation background and Kocheta’s research-systems experience fit the work. Their willingness to publish experiments also fits my preference for technical founders who build before asking permission. But the measurement was an agent-assisted decoder experiment, not an autonomous inference-endpoint deployment. Maintainers warned that the change could regress performance on microcontrollers and DSPs. The proof establishes an integer identity, not whole-program correctness. InfraOptimal has demonstrated a piece of the mechanism. It has not demonstrated that the mechanism reliably improves customers’ workloads.

The strongest case for investing is that this is how a difficult infrastructure company might begin. Two founders who have known each other since fourth grade are already doing concrete systems work. The move from September’s EvoCompiler materials to dedicated endpoints could put that work next to a customer’s bill, where improvement becomes easy to value. This fits my unstated preference for programmable infrastructure backed by something built. Yet the relationship history establishes familiarity, not shared recovery from failure, and the product change has no documented customer observation behind it. I cannot score a change in positioning as evidence-driven learning. Their prior work establishes relevant ability; it does not establish that they personally encountered the problem that produced InfraOptimal.

Who needs this badly enough to keep using an imperfect version? The dossier names no such user. A console, coding-agent integration, and consultation bookings are ways to reach users, not evidence that users return. Capacity billing is likewise a proposed revenue mechanism, not reported revenue. Charging for idle minutes makes the distinction consequential: a faster kernel must improve the customer’s actual economics after utilization, capacity charges, and optimization overhead. AWS already offers agentic kernel-development tools, while Together and Baseten optimize inference themselves. InfraOptimal needs to discover a workload where its improvement is important enough to make someone switch and stay.

Those competitors do not trigger a pass by themselves. Nor do leaving school, having two founders, or lacking public revenue at pre-seed. The dossier does not establish dependence on a large partner, premature sales hiring, or a ceremonial launch strategy. But the exceptions that could overcome my missing founder evidence require a working product with unmistakable pull or unusually convincing validated proof. A modest, hardware-specific research result does not supply that override. My expectation is that within 36 months this team will narrow into workload-specific optimization tooling rather than establish a broad dedicated-endpoint business; the technical evidence is much clearer than the evidence for the proposed customer relationship.

I would pass today. My flip condition is one documented customer rollout in which a production constraint forces a founder-built change within weeks, followed by eight weeks of paid repeat use with measured net savings. That would show resourcefulness and demand through separate observations. InfraOptimal has supplied enough engineering substance to make that outcome plausible. It has not supplied the evidence that would make me write the check.
