# Tire Swing -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a leasing automation platform such as EliseAI. The memo places EliseAI in tenant prequalification and describes Tire Swing’s downstream workflow for checking eligibility, flagging missing evidence, and assembling certifications for staff approval. Tire Swing reports thousands of compliance checks. Owning that workflow could help a buyer sell a broader product and avoid building and maintaining program-specific rules. The economic benefit to a buyer remains unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The founders report live deployments at dozens of properties across eight states. A proposed 12-month milestone is paid expansion at multiple operators, with measured reductions in staff review time and stable certification accuracy. The reported deployments provide a starting point for that test, while the capital and runway needed remain unknown. A software acquisition is plausible because the product works with operators’ existing forms. Contract assignment, rights to transfer tenant records, and continuity of third-party processing remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale prices below are scenario assumptions. Both sale cases assume closing within eight years, $5 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, no senior preference payouts, and an eligible holder selling its entire stake for cash.

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Holder cash |
|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | $30M less $5M = $25M | $750K (1.5×) |
| Strong sale | $100M less $5M = $95M | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private operation with cash reinvested | No distribution or share sale | $0 (0×) |

If only 80% of the holder’s stake could be sold for cash within the horizon, the strong case would yield $2.28 million (4.56×). The memo documents subscription terms and leaves revenue scale, buyer pricing, and an independent liquidity route unevidenced. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A strategic buyer could build or source the missing certification features. Yardi already bundles certification with property accounting, ExactEstate includes document tampering checks, and Roost markets income calculations linked to source documents. That overlap weakens the case for paying a price that clears the holder’s hurdle. The evidence leaves the Pass gate untriggered because it establishes neither a ready substitute for the full workflow at the strongest prospective buyer nor a price ceiling below the hurdle. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a leasing platform’s build-versus-buy analysis support a price that yields at least $2.5 million in cash to the benchmark holder within eight years? Buyer-side economics grounded in measured results from paid portfolio deployments, with a price range that clears the hurdle after deal deductions and the expected cash sale fraction, would move the action from Watch to Reach out.
