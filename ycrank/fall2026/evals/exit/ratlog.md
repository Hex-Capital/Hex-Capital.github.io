# RATLOG -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to a European launch incumbent is RATLOG’s strongest potential route. Arianespace already serves constellation customers, giving it a reason to evaluate technology that could add reusable capacity or lower launch costs. RATLOG targets 90% fewer engine parts and reports a prototype hot-fire. The prototype gives a buyer something to evaluate, while the economic benefit at flight scale and any buyer interest remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The company targets a flight-ready engine in 2027, a first-stage test in 2028, and orbit in 2029 or 2030. The reported hot-fire took four months and cost under $30,000. The memo also records a €30,000 grant and YC backing. Those signals support early execution, while the capital required for scale-up remains unknown and launch-site selection is open. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, which requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale cases below assume closing within eight years, $10 million in combined net debt and transaction costs, no preference claims ahead of this holder, and eligibility to sell the entire stake for cash pro rata. The prices and deductions are illustrative. RATLOG’s reported $400 million in signed launch reservations leaves buyer pricing unsupported, with customer identities, deposits, and cancellation terms undisclosed. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Arianespace already launches constellation payloads, SpaceX sells rideshare capacity, and Blue Origin has demonstrated orbital booster recovery. An incumbent could keep using its own capacity while RATLOG raises additional capital, scales the engine, and secures a launch site. The memo’s conflicting entity details also leave ownership of the engine IP and the ability to transfer it unverified. These gaps warrant Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a credible sale price that would leave the holder materially short. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
