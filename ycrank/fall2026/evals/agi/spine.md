# Spine -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path B, New economies, is Spine’s strongest path. Spine offers to take payroll exceptions, benefits enrollment, and compliance filings off founders’ hands while working in their existing HR systems. It says specialists review AI drafts before submission. The seed-stage hypothesis is that automation cuts the labor needed to deliver outsourced HR at equivalent quality enough for startups that previously handled the work themselves to get it at zero employer fees. The memo establishes the workflow and pricing offer. It leaves labor per completed job, including review, retries, and exceptions, unmeasured. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Spine’s broker-of-record role gives it a way to earn carrier commissions for executing benefits work. With cheap frontier models, a customer could draft answers or filings itself and would still need someone to resolve exceptions, coordinate with carriers, and finish the work. That supports paying for execution. Whether customers would keep Spine’s appointment when another broker can deliver equivalent service remains open. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Spine claims 30+ startup customers and roughly 15% average healthcare cost savings. The memo also reports about 4.4× commission revenue growth over six months, from an undisclosed base. These are early demand signals. The customer mix, renewal behavior, and prior outsourcing choices are unknown. The memo records a U.S. seed round and a product launch. The founders previously built a company together, and Cokar’s legal training is relevant to compliance. Service quality across recurring payroll and filing cycles remains unverified. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

The expansion unit is one additional employer account. At zero employer fees, its contribution is carrier commissions less onboarding, inference, specialist time, and fulfillment across payroll, benefits, and compliance. As a scenario assumption, if smaller employers generate less commission while still requiring substantial exception handling, the accounts most likely to be newly served could be the hardest to serve profitably. The memo gives no account-level inputs to test that scenario. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Spine could be bundling familiar brokerage and HR services at an attractive price. Specialist review and exceptions may consume most of the labor that AI saves. Rippling already offers HR and compliance support without co-employment, and Bennie combines brokerage with benefits administration technology. With abundant capital and the same models, a competitor could staff the execution layer and pursue the same broker appointments. The memo identifies no exclusive access or persistent capacity constraint for a separate Path A case. These facts keep both Path B conditions unproven and block outreach. They leave room for a seed-stage test of labor savings and customer preference. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of startups that previously handled HR in-house because outsourcing was uneconomic be served profitably through a benefits renewal and still choose Spine over a broker using comparably capable models? A matched trial should verify equivalent payroll and filing quality, measure all human time per completed operation, reconcile carrier commissions to account-level costs, and record actual broker-of-record renewals when a comparable offer is available. Repeatable positive contribution driven by measured labor savings, together with renewals under parity, would support Reach out if the updated score clears 65 with at least medium confidence. Evidence that exceptions erase the savings or that employers readily switch under parity would trigger Pass.
