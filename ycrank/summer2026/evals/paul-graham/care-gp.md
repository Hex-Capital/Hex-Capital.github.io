# Care GP -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Care GP has processed more than 3 million documents despite Melvin Chen having no visible GitHub trail. That is Care GP’s signature: unusually deep schlep appetite paired with unusually weak hacker genealogy. Care GP looks less like two hackers following an obsession and more like a resourceful operator who found an ugly workflow, shipped into it, and kept selling until 400 clinic operators depended on the result. The first pattern is rare enough to matter. The second keeps me from mistaking operational success for Stripe-like founder evidence.

Coinbase is the high rung because compliance, unreliable counterparties, and frightening consequences made the schlep part of the product. Care GP shares that mechanism: Care GP must interpret faxes and scans, match multiple patient identifiers, integrate with Best Practice Premier, obey overlapping Australian privacy regimes, and tolerate a 5%–20% human edit rate. The divergence is that Brian Armstrong personally built the rejected wallet prototype, while Care GP’s dossier shows no comparable founder-authored artifact. Care GP is close to Coinbase on unpleasantness, but not yet on evidence that the founder himself is the animal doing the unpleasant work.

The polished AI-wrapper pass case fails because professional credentials substitute for shipped technical evidence. Care GP partly matches that low rung: Chen’s biography leads with Oxford, Goldman Sachs, Terra Firma and Samena Capital; Care GP provides no public repositories, programming history, open-source work, or named technical cofounder. But Care GP is not merely a deck attached to those credentials. Millions of processed documents and 85% pilot conversion prove someone built a working artifact. Care GP therefore escapes the bottom rung, though the absence of hacker tracks is a real red flag rather than missing pre-seed publicity.

Care GP reported 150 clinics, 20% weekly growth, zero churn and profitability before YC S26. Resourcefulness compounds because every clinic adds malformed documents, exception cases and corrections that Care GP must absorb without letting accuracy collapse; surviving that loop for 18 months would produce operational knowledge competitors cannot buy instantly. Interviewing more than 150 providers is respectable persistence, but it is not an Airbnb cereal-box moment. Care GP leaves substantial animal points unearned because the dossier never names an improvised stunt, rejection reversal, or unscalable founder operation that created the first customers.

Melvin Chen worked as a healthcare-technology investor, operator and commercial lead before Care GP, but Care GP’s origin appears to be observed pain rather than Chen’s own pain. For Care GP to flip into the Dropbox end of the organic-idea range, I would need evidence that Chen personally performed referral allocation, reconciled pathology results, or built Samantha to solve a workflow he himself repeatedly endured. Interviewing 150 providers can reveal a good market. It cannot retroactively make Care GP a tool the founder first built for himself.

Airbnb had three founders whose complementary abilities became visible through years of building and surviving together. Care GP lists Chen as founder and CEO, has a team size of 10, and supplies no public evidence of a cofounder or prior working relationship. Care GP therefore gets the ambiguity default rather than the solo-founder penalty: ten employees do not substitute for a strong pair, but the dossier does not prove Chen is operating alone. A named technical cofounder who had built with Chen for two years would move Care GP materially upward.

Care GP will reach A$12–20 million in ARR within 36 months, primarily from Australian multi-site clinics, but Care GP will remain a healthcare-operations vendor rather than become the general agent platform its language implies. The mechanism is schlep accumulation: 400 operators, 3 million documents, Australian data residency and ISO 27001 make Care GP increasingly useful inside a narrow institutional workflow. The missing hacker genealogy makes rapid technical expansion into a broad platform less likely than methodical domination of document allocation, calls and billing.

Heidi Health’s nearly US$100 million and Lyrebird Health’s A$12 million create the strongest bear case: Care GP could be squeezed when better-funded products absorb document handling, billing and communications, while a 5%–20% edit rate prevents autonomous operation. That objection stings because Care GP has no established structural barrier and depends on Best Practice Premier. But 85% pilot conversion, zero reported churn, profitability and largely organic demand say Care GP is already doing tedious work clinics value, not merely promising another AI assistant. I would flip to Pass if verified cohort data showed net revenue retention below 90% after twelve months or fewer than half of the reported 400 operators were paying customers.
