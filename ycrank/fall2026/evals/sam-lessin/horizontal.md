# horizontal. -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

horizontal. has a plausible industrial problem and founders with relevant experience. It does not yet have an underwritable deal in this dossier. Post-money valuation: unknown. Competing term sheets: unknown. What this check proves: unspecified. I cannot infer an auction from YC participation, but I also cannot infer cheap entry or scarce capital. Those missing facts matter because I am buying a particular experiment at a particular price, and neither is defined here.

The strongest reason to take the meeting is Niklas Marxer’s self-reported experience bringing agentic AI into more than **150 industrial companies**, alongside Jakob Hermeler’s robotics research and coauthored work on autonomous quadrotor collision recovery. This is more substantive than a collection of school names. The founders have experience connecting software to physical operations, which fits my preference for people who can translate between systems. I give that meaningful founder credit, qualified by the absence of attributable deployment outcomes. I do not also count those 150 companies as horizontal.’s customers or proprietary distribution.

The bull case is that factory coordination contains valuable, inaccessible operating knowledge. Start with one process, learn how a plant actually handles disruptions, and become trusted enough to execute the next process. That could turn difficult implementation work into embedded workflow ownership. horizontal.’s incremental deployment approach makes this case credible enough to investigate. But “works with international manufacturers” establishes neither repeat usage nor a purchase caused by owned access. The **485 LinkedIn followers** and UnternehmerTUM participation do not establish that mechanism either. Meanwhile, Siemens’ June 2026 orchestration launch and Pelico’s ERP/MES-connected agents mean access to factory workflows is already contested.

The economic question is who pays for the exceptions. Replanning production and releasing orders can create real value, but integration work, approval requirements, and human intervention could consume the contract’s contribution profit. The dossier supplies no pricing, revenue, margin, burn, or runway with which to resolve that question. That earns the missing-economics default; it does not establish an uneconomic business. Likewise, published reliability and auditability requirements are sensible, but they are not measured production performance. I will not underwrite margins that quietly assume operational errors disappear as usage grows.

I checked whether the evidence warrants an exception to my financing gates. There is no documented paid conversion and retention, acquisition offer, or prior-exit-backed financing structure that supplies such an override. The founders’ industrial experience is the strongest opposing argument, and it earns its credit in the founder dimension. It cannot establish the round’s price, its experiment, or customer economics. I pass on the evidence presented. One verifiable condition would reopen underwriting: a signed seed proposal below **$10 million post-money** funding a defined six-month, two-plant experiment that measures contribution profit after integration and human exception costs, with enough committed capital to complete it. That would make the proposed check concrete enough to evaluate.
