# OpenTag -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

OpenTag automates repetitive operational work inside Slack and Microsoft Teams, but the dossier never converts that inconvenience into a consequential cost pool. A $7.6 billion generic AI-agents market is not a consequence-of-success calculation. There is no baseline for labor hours displaced, expert services made radically cheaper, or organizations newly able to operate. The stated outcome is fresher documents and less repeated work—useful, but incremental until quantified otherwise.

The technical case is worse. Anthropic already offers the same tag-a-shared-agent interaction through Claude Tag; Dust and Lindy connect agents to company tools. OpenTag adds more than 80 models, Microsoft Teams, pooled credits, sandboxed execution, and workspace memory, but supplies no measured improvement in task completion, cost, reliability, or speed. Model routing is architecture only if it produces a result customers cannot get from the incumbent. Today it looks like integration around commoditizing models, precisely where vendor bundling can erase the company.

The strongest bull case is the team. Three Berkeley-trained founders worked together at Lilac Labs, another YC-backed company, and collectively cover infrastructure, security, enterprise software, and regulated environments. Ten teams adopted the product within two weeks of early access, with six organizations publicly displayed. That is meaningful pre-seed velocity. The product also has sensible controls—isolated workspaces, visible plans, audit logs, citations, and approvals before external actions—against the autonomous-error and procurement risks it creates.

But velocity is not yet learning rate. The dossier records no material belief revised, complementary outsider recruited, or experiment designed to kill the central thesis. SOC 2 Type II is in progress, yet the existential risk is not merely whether enterprises trust OpenTag. It is whether a standalone model-agnostic coworker remains valuable after Anthropic, Microsoft, or Slack bundles comparable routing, memory, and actions. No milestone, numerical threshold, budget, or stop condition attacks that risk.

The known **Block** exception does not rescue this case. Both began with small teams and potentially platform-shaped wedges into large existing workflows. The material difference is that OpenTag has neither quantified an American Express-scale value pool nor shown a wedge capable of escaping its incumbent’s distribution. Its $50 monthly plan and shared credits establish accessibility, not durable economics; external model and infrastructure dependencies remain undisclosed in gross-margin terms.

I pass. My one verifiable flip condition is a controlled customer benchmark showing that at least five paying organizations complete the same recurring workflows through OpenTag at ten times the end-to-end speed or one-tenth the cost of Claude Tag, while sustaining at least 70% gross margin. That would turn model routing from a feature claim into a discontinuity and make the ten-team launch signal worth underwriting.
