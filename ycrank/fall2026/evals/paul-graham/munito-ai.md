# Munito AI -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Alexey Eryshev has shipped something. Munito AI launched its free tier on October 7 and paid tiers on October 15. That is more useful evidence than a polished description of European AI sovereignty. But what happened when something proved difficult? The dossier contains no particular obstacle, founder-built workaround, and resulting change. Leading inference at Mistral establishes relevant experience. It does not answer the question that would make me write a personal check.

The product earns substantial credit independently of that missing founder evidence. Munito offers compatible APIs, an EU endpoint, and zero prompt/output retention by default. These give a developer something concrete to try. The self-service approach also fits my preference for programmable interfaces that technical users can adopt themselves. But the other half of that pattern is developers actually using the interface. Published integrations with Hermes, pi, and OpenClaw establish available distribution paths; they do not establish repeated use. Nor does the proposed self-improving inference stack yet supply a measured technical advantage.

The strongest case for investing is that Eryshev has already done this work inside a formidable competitor. He could know exactly which customers find existing inference services unnecessarily difficult to buy. A solo founder who ships a usable alternative could reach those customers before a larger organization changes its sales process. This is where a working-product exception deserves consideration. Yet the dossier supplies neither unmistakable customer pull nor independently validated technical superiority strong enough to replace the missing evidence of resourcefulness. The product is real enough to investigate; the exception is not established.

Munito’s immediate problem is finding the small group for whom these differences are indispensable. Mistral already offers regional endpoints and third-party models. At $0.11 per million input tokens and $0.28 per million output tokens for EU DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Munito has an offer, but published prices establish neither revenue nor a cost advantage. Buyers attracted by privacy may also require the audits and service guarantees that Munito does not currently provide as standard. Handling that procurement work could uncover a valuable neglected wedge. Today it remains an unresolved task. The dossier also does not establish that a particular collision with this problem caused Eryshev to start Munito.

I would pass at 41. There is no documented dependence on a large partner, premature delegation, or refusal to respond to contrary evidence. There is simply no documented product revision to score, and no returning customer to distinguish this capable implementation from an urgently wanted business. My single flip condition is a verifiable customer deployment showing that Eryshev encountered a specific residency or procurement blocker, shipped a workaround within weeks, and converted that customer into sustained paid production use. That sequence would connect the founder, the obstacle, and the need. Munito has demonstrated the ability to ship; I still need to see that loop close.
