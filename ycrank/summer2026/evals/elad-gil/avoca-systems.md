# Avoca Systems -- Elad Gil Evaluation

250+ live clinics with call abandonment falling from roughly 30% to 2% make **Avoca Systems** a single-miracle workflow company with one conspicuous weakness: the product is visibly working, but the buying curve is invisible. **Avoca Systems** does not need new radiology science or new patient behavior; it needs reliable clinical-rule execution and enterprise distribution. That high single-miracle placement paired with the low revenue-velocity placement is the signature: deployed infrastructure whose product evidence is ahead of its commercial evidence.

**Color Genomics** is the high rung for single-miracle architecture: workflow and data infrastructure built on commodity sequencing rather than a bet on new chemistry. **Avoca Systems** shares that load-bearing mechanism by encoding modality, contrast, preparation, equipment, staffing, and referrer rules on top of existing radiology systems. Six named integrations, including Epic, Oracle Health, and Visage, plus SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, and HIPAA compliance show that **Avoca Systems** has already built much of the unglamorous layer. The divergence from Color is clinical-booking liability: incorrect scheduling can delay care, while third-party RIS write-back and human escalation leave Avoca dependent on both model reliability and integration continuity. That is still one system-level miracle, not a science project, but it is less clean than Color’s architecture.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism in my Series C pass: usage alone did not demonstrate the curve required to reverse a structurally unfavorable position. **Avoca Systems** reports 250+ clinics, an 86% call-resolution rate, and greater than 80% eligible-call conversion, but **Avoca Systems** discloses no ARR, customer growth chronology, retention, or deployment expansion. Those are excellent product outcomes and potentially a false signal commercially: “live” locations can represent one negotiated network contract rather than hundreds of independent buying decisions. For an AI company, silence on the revenue curve remains a red flag even at pre-seed; Avoca sits above the Lyft-style low rung because real workflows are running, but well below Harvey’s “just working” evidence in dollars.

Radiologic-technologist vacancy rates rising from 6.2% in 2021 to 18.1% in 2024 provide **Avoca Systems** with a plausible catalyst, but not yet a fully demonstrated market re-opening. Labor scarcity makes automating calls, referrals, preparation, and follow-up more valuable, while contemporary voice and document models make the workflow newly tractable. **Avoca Systems** would move up a full tier on this dimension if network buyers explicitly attributed 2025–2026 purchases to staffing shortages or if deployments showed materially lower labor cost per booked scan. Without that causal evidence, the catalyst remains inferred rather than Anduril-after-Ukraine explicit; the $780 million scheduling-software TAM and 18.96% cloud CAGR are steady-state market facts, not reasons the window opened now.

Max Shand was Afterpay’s first employee and built Serenade to 347 artists before an acquisition; Campbell Mercer-Butcher previously served as a principal engineer and technical lead. Over the next 18 months, **Avoca Systems** can compound that shipping capacity through each network deployment: every encoded protocol, roster constraint, and RIS integration shortens the path for the next customer. **Avoca Systems** already shipping across 250+ clinics is stronger evidence than titles or GitHub stars. The missing Perplexity-style signal is iteration cadence—no dated sequence shows Mercer-Butcher or Shand building specific requested capabilities between customer conversations—so prior shipping and current deployment breadth earn substantial credit without proving the highest rung.

**Stripe** became an index on e-commerce because nearly every new internet merchant could enter through the same programmable rail. **Avoca Systems** could become an index on radiology-network consolidation if every acquired clinic eventually needs one access layer spanning calls, faxes, referrals, booking rules, reminders, and results. The 150-location network deployment shows that leverage beginning, but **Avoca Systems** is not yet a neutral rail: AbbaDox and OpenDoctor already sell radiology-specific AI scheduling, while Epic and Oracle Health control important integration surfaces. Avoca must win accounts against vendors that can bundle adjacent functions, so the position resembles a valuable vertical workflow layer more than Stripe’s category-wide default.

250+ clinics and six healthcare-system integrations support a 36-month forecast that **Avoca Systems** reaches $12–20 million ARR across more than 1,000 clinics by July 2029 while remaining focused on radiology rather than broadening into general healthcare access. **Avoca Systems** gets there by using measurable call-abandonment reduction—from roughly 30% to 2%—to land consolidated networks, then expanding across their locations and communications workflows. The forecast follows the signature: the technical miracle is substantially solved, so the determining variable is whether deployed usage converts into repeatable enterprise revenue.

**AbbaDox** already combines RIS, PACS, VoiceAI, FaxAI, scheduling, check-in, and follow-up, which is the strongest bear case against **Avoca Systems**: a bundled incumbent can commoditize Avoca’s features before Avoca establishes independent distribution. OpenDoctor adds a second radiology-specific AI scheduler, the cited market is only $780 million, and 250 clinics could conceal one services-heavy contract with weak margins. The evidence that currently wins is **Avoca Systems**’s production depth—clinical rules, real-time RIS write-back, six integrations, three compliance regimes, and reported patient outcomes—not a generic voice-agent demo. I would flip to Pass if, by July 2027, **Avoca Systems** cannot show either more than 20% quarterly ARR growth or paid expansion within existing networks; failure on both would prove the 250-clinic footprint was implementation activity rather than “just working” demand.
