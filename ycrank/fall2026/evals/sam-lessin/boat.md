# Boat -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Boat has a published price for compute and no disclosed price for its equity. The first is $0.036 an hour; the second determines whether I want this bet. Post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital are all unknown. I cannot infer an auction from YC membership or competitors’ fundraising. I also cannot underwrite a cheap, non-consensus investment from financing silence. My money stays in my pocket on this dossier.

The business deserves more attention than that decision might suggest. Nougaret reports building cloud coding agents before extracting their infrastructure into the current product. That is a useful bridge: the team has worked on the application consuming the computer and shipped the infrastructure supplying it. Separate VM kernels, nested virtualization, Chrome, Docker, and snapshots make that experience tangible. This fits my preference for founders who can translate between systems. It earns founder credit; it does not establish exclusive technology or privileged customer access.

The strongest argument against passing is that Boat has already persuaded people to pay. More than 1,000 active paying users, reported on October 24, and separately reported six-figure ARR are substantial pre-seed signals. OpenInspect’s support for Boat also puts the product inside an actual agent-development ecosystem. The figures are founder-reported, and their definitions need reconciliation, but dismissing them would be lazy. The unresolved issue is ownership of demand: X outreach, a Product Hunt launch, and an integration announcement do not establish that a proprietary audience, dataset, or embedded workflow caused those purchases. Customers buying useful compute is encouraging. Evidence that Boat controls their route to that compute would change the investment mechanism.

The $0.036 hourly price makes that distinction consequential. Boat’s comparison with $0.331 alternatives could drive adoption, but the comparison is vendor-authored and supplies no contribution economics. Google and Modal already offer overlapping sandbox capabilities. If agent execution becomes interchangeable, customers capture much of the benefit while infrastructure suppliers compete on price. Six-figure ARR earns some economic credit; without host utilization, gross margin, burn, or runway, I cannot tell whether more usage finances the company. The documented product and paying demand mean this is more than a speculative AI application. They do not yet establish the measured retention or cash economics that could justify overriding my financing gates.

What exact truth would my check reveal? The dossier names no proceeds-funded hypothesis, deadline, or pass/fail milestone. That is missing information, rather than evidence the founders lack a plan. Still, neither the real product nor early payments establishes the exceptional conversion, retained demand, or financing structure needed to override both missing gates. I pass on the investment while taking the operating progress seriously. My verifiable flip condition is a documented seed proposal below $10 million post-money that funds one dated, measurable test of whether retained customer usage can carry Boat to cash break-even without another round.
