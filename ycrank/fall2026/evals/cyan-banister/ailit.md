# Ailit -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can picture a freight operator buying charged batteries the way it buys diesel today, while the locomotive keeps earning its living. Ailit gives that future a concrete engineering sequence: feed the existing diesel-electric drivetrain from a battery wagon, exchange packs instead of waiting for charging, and retain diesel backup. Preserving the installed fleet makes the transition more believable. But the little artifact that tells me this world has started arriving is still missing: Ailit’s first full-scale prototype is under construction, with deployment targeted for summer 2027. This is a specified path toward a future, with its central transition still untested.

Emil Tagesson is the strongest reason to take that path seriously. Koenigsegg battery experience and reported responsibility for bringing Candela’s electric ferry through certification and production are relevant operating tuition. Batteries have to survive institutions as well as physics. That is substantially more persuasive than a degree and enthusiasm for electrification. The dossier does not establish a longstanding personal obsession with rail, earlier independent experiments, or how this team recruited its first technical believers. Christian Lind’s physics background adds technical context; it does not fill those gaps.

Why must the railroad buy the equipment to stop buying diesel? Ailit’s answer interests me: own the batteries and exchange sites, then sell energy at an advertised 20% discount without an upfront equipment purchase. That could return capital and procurement flexibility to operators. However, the dossier identifies a financing obstacle rather than an artificial restriction or a gatekeeper extracting a quantified rent. And that advertised saving transfers a considerable obligation onto Ailit. Electricity, spare packs, degradation, charging infrastructure and financing must all fit inside the price. The global $108/kWh battery-pack average does not establish those installed economics.

The strongest argument for writing an early check is that waiting could miss the difficult infrastructure company hiding inside an ordinary energy bill. Ailit fits my pattern of looking beneath a visible transition for its physical enabling layer. Its CTO has unusually pertinent experience, and diesel backup could make early adoption less disruptive. My exception for pre-revenue physical technology is relevant here; revenue is not a prerequisite. But that exception becomes compelling with a functioning prototype and concrete industrial commitments. The dossier establishes neither for Ailit. SEK10M in previous funding and the separately reported $500K YC investment finance the experiment; rail-publication coverage does not show a railroad changing its behavior.

The infrastructure position also needs earning. Ailit proposes integrated hardware and coordination software, but demonstrated control of a scarce technical asset or operating network is absent. Dayton-Phoenix already reports integration testing of a competing battery tender; pack exchange must therefore establish its own operational advantage. A missed charged pack could turn an energy-saving system into a departure problem. I see no documented severe privacy issue, concealed CEO, or claim that incumbents are trivial. Lind is identified as CEO; Banderet’s reported co-founder/adviser role merits clarification without being evidence of a governance failure. The decisive uncertainty is whether this team can make the whole physical system dependable and economical.

I would keep talking, but I would not write my personal check on this dossier. The founder evidence is ahead of the product evidence, and the proposed economics depend on their meeting. My flip condition is one independently verified, paid freight pilot demonstrating scheduled battery exchanges and delivered energy at least 20% below diesel after accounting for battery depreciation, infrastructure, financing and backup fuel. That would turn Ailit’s most interesting promise into an observable operating fact.
