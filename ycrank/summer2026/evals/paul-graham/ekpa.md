# Ekpa -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Ekpa’s support for 38 brokerages makes Ekpa a schlep-heavy product without evidence that its founders were drawn to it by an organic wound. That is the signature here: unusually real operational ugliness, unusually thin founder-problem fit. Anant Asthana built drug-discovery agents and Yuga Patel studied economics and had investment exposure, but neither is described as an obsessive trader who built Ekpa because existing tools failed him. The problem may be genuine. It does not yet read as theirs.

**Coinbase** (2012) is the high rung for schlep blindness: compliance was not incidental to the product but the work Brian Armstrong ran toward. Ekpa encounters a similar mechanism because position-specific instructions, exact share counts, delayed market data, and algorithmic recommendations create regulatory and auditability work that software founders prefer to ignore. But Ekpa diverges from Coinbase at the decisive point. Ekpa says it is not an investment adviser, uses read-only SnapTrade access, and leaves execution to users. Ekpa has found the schlep, but so far it is standing at the edge of it rather than eating it.

**Loopt** (2005) exposed what happens when an idea rides a plausible technological wave without enough usable demand; Sam Altman’s animality was the exception that justified the check. Ekpa’s origin is similarly described through the 2025–2026 rollout of brokerage-integrated AI agents, not through a specific episode in which Anant or Yuga desperately needed Ekpa themselves. Yuga’s investment experience and Anant’s quantitative coursework are adjacent credentials. They are not Drew Houston forgetting his USB stick. With no direct founder-pain evidence, Ekpa gets the absent-evidence default on organicity, and the Loopt exception does not rescue it because the dossier contains no Altman-like force-of-personality evidence.

Ekpa’s 2,400 completed analyses and $3.4M in reported managed assets show that Anant and Yuga shipped something before the world had validated it. That is better than a deck. Over the next 18 months, animal resourcefulness would compound if Ekpa’s founders personally handle frightened users, incorrect recommendations, broker connection failures, and compliance objections until those edge cases become product. But $3.4M of linked assets is not revenue, 14 Launch YC votes are not user love, and no unscalable acquisition stunt is named. Ekpa has left tracks as programmers, but not yet as animals.

No public GitHub repositories were found for either founder, despite Anant’s agentic drug-discovery work and Yuga’s MIT Driverless participation. Ekpa’s hacker score would flip upward if those projects revealed a non-trivial artifact, benchmark, or open-source trail predating the startup. The current evidence is credential-heavy: MIT CS, machine-learning coursework, an AbbVie automation internship, and a functioning Ekpa product. Hackers do leave trails. Ekpa has shipped enough to avoid the bottom rung, but not enough to establish the genealogy that made the Collisons instantly legible.

Both Ekpa founders attend MIT, giving Ekpa the right numerical shape but uncertain connective tissue. **Stripe** (2010) had two brothers who had already built Auctomatic together; the relationship itself had been stress-tested before the payments schlep began. Anant and Yuga offer plausible technical and financial complementarity, yet no earlier shared employer, project, or co-founder history was found. Ekpa earns credit for being a pair rather than a solo-founder bet, but proximity at MIT is not the same as evidence that the pair can survive three years of bad broker APIs and angry users.

Ekpa’s $3.4M of linked assets points toward a narrower and less glamorous destination than the current consumer pitch. By July 2029, I expect Ekpa to have pivoted from an everyday-trader application into auditable research-agent infrastructure for RIAs or small broker-dealers, with $1M–$3M ARR. The mechanism is the signature: Ekpa’s strongest asset is the regulatory and integration schlep, while its weakest is an organic connection to consumer trading. The founders are more likely to discover that professionals will pay to externalize that grind than that consumers will pay another subscription for suggested trades.

**Public** already combines portfolio agents, research, and execution, while Composer was acquired by SoFi in June 2026; those facts form the strongest case that Ekpa is early to an important interface rather than late to a commodity. Airbnb is the uncomfortable precedent: weak-looking evidence can hide animals, and Ekpa’s 2,400 analyses may be the first visible trace. But Airbnb had cereal boxes; Ekpa has an unexplained assets counter, no disclosed subscription price, no revenue, and no founder story showing why Public cannot simply bundle the feature. I remain neutral and would not write the check yet. Six-month retention above 60% among at least 100 paying Strategy Lab subscribers would flip my verdict to Invest.
