# Theo -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a plumber finishing a job while her website quietly finds the next customer: noticing an underserved neighborhood, publishing something useful, and learning which inquiries became paid work. Theo has a piece of that machine today—conversational website creation followed by weekly optimization. Getting to that future requires connecting discovery to actual business outcomes and making reliable publishing decisions without consuming the owner’s attention. The dossier establishes neither transition. My imagined destination cannot earn Theo points for a roadmap it has not demonstrated.

Miles Pines gives me a better reason to pay attention. He reports bootstrapping Endgrate to more than $100,000 in annual revenue, generating thousands of monthly organic clicks, and then doing SEO for business-owning friends. That sequence matters: he encountered the work before selling its automation. It fits my preference for an operating wound becoming a product specification. His Harvard and Citadel background adds technical credibility, but the commercial record remains self-reported, and the dossier supplies little dated evidence of deeper obsession or recruiting ability. Endgrate’s revenue belongs to his founder record; Theo’s revenue is undisclosed.

Why does a business owner need another website after already buying one? Theo’s answer appears to be that launch ends while the work of being discovered continues. That is a recognizable operating burden. At $69.99 monthly for Starter, recurring research and publishing could give an owner useful time back. But the dossier identifies no quantified agency rent, restrictive contract, or artificial gate that Theo removes. Its requirement to replace an externally hosted site also creates a new commitment, especially when native bookings and payments are absent. The agency returned remains a plausible benefit with limited demonstrated economics.

The strongest objection to passing is that people may already be getting enough value to make those limitations irrelevant. Theo reports 10,000-plus sites built by September 28, 2026; its Buy My Lunch example shows 929 Google clicks against 131 previously. Thirty-seven Trustpilot reviews averaging 4.7/5 add an outside signal. A founder with firsthand acquisition experience and a working product helping ordinary businesses deserves attention. Yet generated sites do not establish live businesses repeatedly entrusting Theo with their acquisition. The named traffic comparisons help, but they leave retention, payment, and customer economics unresolved. I give the artifact credit without converting its outputs into evidence of devoted behavior.

Theo also sits at the visible application layer, with Durable already offering website creation and SEO/GEO. Connected analytics might eventually become a useful asset, but cross-customer learning and control over a scarce dependency are unproven. Google remains central to the workflow. That dependence does not establish the closed-model red flag, and a named solo founder/CEO avoids the specific equal-control concern. My exceptions for missing revenue require another compelling engine—exceptional technical proof, longstanding founder obsession, or extraordinary user commitment. This dossier does not establish one strongly enough to override the weak future and infrastructure evidence.

I pass with my own money today. Theo could become a useful subscription business; that possibility is credible without making it a compelling fit for my check. **My flip condition is a verifiable six-month cohort of paying businesses that keeps Theo’s recurring publishing active and shows attributable customer inquiries sufficient to cover the subscription.** That would demonstrate owners repeatedly handing over consequential work and receiving economic control back.
