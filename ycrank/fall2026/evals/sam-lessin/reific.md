# Reific -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Reific’s proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and need for a first scarce check are all undisclosed. I cannot call this an auction simply because it is YC, and I cannot call it cheap because the founder is working alone. The more interesting underwriting question is whether Reific can own something frontier labs need to buy repeatedly. Licensing real longitudinal records and checking outcomes could create that asset. Accepting nonexclusive licenses, with no disclosed exclusive holdings or customer purchases, leaves ownership of the advantage unproven.

The technical distinction deserves attention. Releasing records on a clock tests decisions made before the answer becomes obvious; a completed history can conceal precisely that failure. The November 2 evaluation reports leading reward averages of 31.6%, 43.1%, and 29.7% across three task families. Those figures demonstrate difficulty, not the economic value of training against it. Reific reports improvement without publishing its magnitude, training compute, or held-out transfer results. The venture moment would be proving that training on these records produces transferable gains worth paying for. That is my proposed experiment; the dossier supplies no round-funded test with a metric and deadline.

The strongest argument against passing is that Reific may already have the difficult half of this business: relevant research ability and functioning infrastructure. The founder’s first-author EVEREST work concerns temporal forecasting, and the company reports running 64 simultaneous sandboxes without failures. That is more substantial than an academic credential attached to a landing page. It fits my pattern of backing people who translate between systems—in this case, messy operating records and executable training environments. But the demonstrated experience is concentrated on the research and engineering side. There is no documented operating access to data owners or purchasing labs, and sample-request forms establish neither.

What happens if no Series A arrives? Environment licensing and custom construction could support a small, real business, but Reific discloses no prices, revenue, margins, burn, or runway from which to establish that path. Record acquisition, answer-key verification, and model-based judging all require work; the dossier does not establish whether customers will pay enough to cover it. Meanwhile, Prime Intellect offers environments within a broader training stack, and Bespoke Labs builds overlapping infrastructure. My AI red flag here is supplier substitution and buyer internalization. A better model might increase demand for difficult environments while still leaving Reific unable to retain the resulting value.

I pass at present. This is not evidence of a bad company or an overheated financing; it is insufficient evidence for my particular check. The exceptions in my framework require demonstrated conversion and retention, external acquisition validation, or a proven founder paired with a financing structure that removes repeated fundraising. Reific supplies none of those overrides yet. My one verifiable condition for reopening underwriting is a paid lab renewal supported by measured held-out training gains and positive contribution margin on that contract. That would connect the research asset to a repeat purchase and cash economics. Today, the strongest evidence establishes that the founder can build the experiment.
