# Dydema -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest supported route is a strategic acquisition by a gaming peripheral or wearable hardware maker seeking to add programmable hands-free controls. The memo documents a research-cap demo that used three imagined movements to type, play chess, and answer email. Dydema reports that its pretrained decoder adapts to new users after brief calibration and outperformed per-user models in internal tests. If that advantage holds on cheaper sensors, a hardware maker could acquire the decoder to reduce setup friction. The memo contains no measured buyer benefit or acquisition price evidence. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The next milestone is a consumer-grade pilot that measures calibration time, command reliability, and repeat use on cheaper sensors. The prototype and reported $500,000 convertible note provide early execution signals. The memo leaves the pilot budget, runway, and hardware staffing needs unresolved. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. Both sale scenarios below assume closing within eight years, eligibility to sell the entire stake for cash at closing, and a pro rata waterfall with no preference diversion, escrow, or rollover. The prices and deductions are illustrative assumptions. Actual holder and deal terms are unknown.

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest strategic sale | $40 million less $5 million in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million | $1.05 million (2.1×) |
| Strong strategic sale | $100 million less the same $5 million leaves $95 million | $2.85 million (5.7×). At 2% ownership, $1.90 million (3.8×). |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share purchase or funded payout | $0 realized within eight years |

The strong scenario clears the hurdle at 3%, but the memo provides neither buyer economics nor comparable sale prices to support that outcome. The research API terms describe model-improvement use of submitted data and on-premises licensing. Acquisition diligence would still need to establish which decoder and data rights could transfer. The memo gives no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Emotiv and Neurosity already offer programmable EEG commands, so a buyer could use existing technology if Dydema’s calibration advantage proves small. The demo used a research cap, and Dydema is still working to improve speed and command count on cheaper sensors. That makes the case for buying Dydema at a price that clears the holder’s hurdle weak today. The memo establishes neither a structural block nor a credible price ceiling below the hurdle, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid pilot with a gaming peripheral or wearable hardware partner show that Dydema’s decoder creates enough value on consumer-grade sensors to justify a purchase above the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Measured gains in calibration time, command reliability, and repeat use over the partner’s available alternative, backed by a buyer economics case that supports at least $83.3 million in distributable equity, would move the action to Reach out. Technical gains without that economic evidence would keep it at Watch.
