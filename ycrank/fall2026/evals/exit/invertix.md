# Invertix -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a large renewable operator is the strongest path. TotalEnergies’ solar teams tested Invertix, and Altenia’s CEO reported that some work went from days to minutes. Invertix reports roughly 2 GW of agent coverage. The asset a buyer would acquire is the software that connects plant data to fault triage, repair coordination, and production verification. Ownership could give an operator control over how those workflows evolve across its fleet. The evidence establishes workflow value, while the incremental value of owning the software remains unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

A paid production rollout across multiple sites that measures labor savings, restoration time, and integration cost would make the acquisition case easier to price. The €1.7 million pre-seed and reported deployments make the test plausible. Engineer-led onboarding and undisclosed burn leave the cost and runway uncertain. A share purchase could keep the contracting entity in place and transfer control of Invertix’s software. Customer change-of-control terms, data rights, and model-provider terms remain unverified. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

With investor terms undisclosed, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The eight-year, 5× target is $2.5 million gross before investor taxes. It requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million and no modeled fees or senior claims, the 3% case needs $88.3 million in enterprise value. A $25 million sale at year eight yields $600,000 (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). These are scenario prices. Custom quotes and reported time savings leave a realizable sale price unsupported. (Key Facts, Market & Traction)

If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. Wind-down recovery is unquantifiable without asset values, obligations, and preferences. The memo establishes neither post-reinvestment cash for a sponsor buyout or company payout, eligible demand for a secondary, nor operating scale and reporting readiness for an IPO. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An operator could license Invertix through its existing integrations, retain approval over plant-control changes, and capture the demonstrated workflow savings. Metris and Areg.AI already offer overlapping agent workflows, and engineer-led integration could limit the savings from a fleet-wide rollout. The TotalEnergies test and Altenia testimonial speak directly to operating utility. Ownership economics and a realizable price remain open, so the evidence supports Watch. It establishes neither a structural block nor a price ceiling that triggers Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a paid multi-site rollout show enough incremental value from ownership over licensing to support an acquisition price that delivers $2.5 million to the holder within eight years? Buyer-validated savings net of integration costs and an acquisition valuation tied to those savings would answer it. A supported range that clears the hurdle at credible dilution and after senior claims would move the action to Reach out.
