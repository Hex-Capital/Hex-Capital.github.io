# STASIS -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

STASIS is developing consumer eyewear for screen-related eye fatigue. Its proposed product uses inward-facing sensors to estimate fatigue and trigger tint changes, break prompts, or screen adjustments. The eyewear supplies no identified input to a specific AI workload, and the memo identifies no AI deployment constrained by a resource STASIS could supply. The product could make fatigue management more automatic, but the memo identifies no buyer or use previously excluded by delivery cost and gives no evidence that AI lowers the cost per successful outcome enough to open one. Screen breaks and phone-based blink training already address parts of the same problem. (The Idea; Key Risks)

The integration of sensing and intervention gives STASIS a distinct product design today. Available inward-tracking components, published blink-tracking designs, and a software substitute for some symptoms limit the case for durable pricing. STASIS reports training on 550 billion eye-data points, with ownership, exclusivity, and the performance benefit unverified. Existing screen-break workarounds show that users act on the problem. Launch views and an open refundable reservation program leave reservation volume and paid conversion unknown. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks; Key Facts)

Prototype coverage and the founders’ earlier eye-sensing hardware work support seed-stage execution. The company’s reported accuracy above 90% lacks a published reference measure or validation protocol, and shipment is scheduled for March 2027. The $299 first-batch price includes prescription lenses, but the memo gives no manufacturing, fulfillment, or returns costs from which to assess contribution per unit. Those gaps limit confidence in real-world efficacy, paid demand, and margins. (Founders & Team; Market & Traction; Key Risks; The Idea)

### Countercase

Continuous sensing could make personalized, timely intervention practical for screen workers who struggle to sustain manual breaks. If the fatigue estimates hold up during ordinary use, the glasses could still deliver on-eye sensing and automatic tint even when buyers have access to equally capable models. The product design makes that scenario plausible. The memo leaves both the incremental benefit over low-cost software and the economic threshold for a previously excluded use unverified. Neither AGI path clears its evidence gate, so the action is Pass. (The Idea; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can STASIS make sustained, personalized fatigue management viable for screen workers who cannot achieve it with manual breaks or blink-training software? A paid field trial in that group would change the action to **Reach out** if it showed that sensor-triggered interventions produced sustained symptom relief at a viable cost per successful outcome compared with those alternatives. The next conversation would test whether that result can hold at the regular $399 price. (The Idea; Key Risks)
