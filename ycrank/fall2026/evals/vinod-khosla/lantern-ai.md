# Lantern AI -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Lantern AI has evidence that employers will buy its product. It has much less evidence that success radically changes the economics of hiring. Those are different underwriting questions. The reported $1M ARR matters, although allocation between products is undisclosed. But the cited $3.62B recruitment-software market does not quantify the human effort Lantern can eliminate or the expertise it can make universally available. I can imagine the consequential outcome: expert hiring judgment delivered at negligible marginal cost. The dossier does not yet establish that mechanism.

The customer results deserve attention. Hyatt reports three times more candidates screened weekly; Gammon reports 40% faster time-to-shortlist. These are useful operational improvements, supported by 100K+ cumulative interviews across the interview product’s history. They do not establish superior hiring decisions. Screening more people can multiply mistakes as readily as insight. Lantern’s “World People Model” and “Company Hiring Model” describe an architecture, but there is no auditable technical comparison against Gem or Juicebox showing a 5–10x capability advantage. Gem already shares recruiting context across agents, and Juicebox adapts sourcing from feedback. Shared memory alone will be difficult to defend.

The strongest argument against my pass is that Lantern already has the ingredients for a learning system: enterprise customers, substantial interview usage, and company-specific criteria and decisions. Crypto.com, Hyatt and Gammon are meaningful evidence of access to real workflows. My actual investing pattern includes execution businesses; scientific novelty is not compulsory. If every completed hire teaches Lantern something competitors cannot learn, the interview wedge could expand into valuable hiring infrastructure. But interview volume measures exposure, and recorded decisions measure preferences. Neither establishes access to subsequent employee performance or proves that the model improves from it. That missing causal link limits the option’s upside today.

Thomas Chan and Justin Tang earn their strongest credit for responding to customers. Requests for upstream discovery led the team to expand beyond interviewing into an AI Hiring Manager. That is an observed change in direction, supported by recruiting experience at Career Hackers and Chan’s prior recruiting ventures. It is better evidence of learning than a prestigious résumé. There is no comparable evidence of recruiting complementary people to own assessment validity or model differentiation. The exceptional-case override would require unusually strong evidence of an instigator pursuing an enormous existing value pool, or a mission whose consequence alone justifies extreme uncertainty. This dossier establishes relevant operators and commercial demand; it does not establish that override.

The largest kill risk is whether customer-specific “taste” predicts productive hires rather than reproducing historical preferences. Lantern advertises consistent scoring, while its terms disclaim accuracy and freedom from bias and require human review. Those disclaimers do not prove a defective product. They identify what must be tested. The dossier supplies no ranked experiment, budget, threshold or stop decision against that uncertainty, so I give the milestone dimension its absent-evidence default. Subscription plans and research credits likewise reveal charging mechanics without revealing inference expense, review labor or cost per successful hire. Economic parity remains unproven; permanent subsidy dependence is not established.

I pass on the current evidence. The AI red flag here is overlapping capabilities without demonstrated technical separation; the dossier does not justify declaring Lantern an off-the-shelf wrapper. My one verifiable flip condition is a prospective, independently audited customer study showing at least 5x lower fully loaded recruiting cost per hire than the customer’s existing workflow, with non-inferior six-month retention and job performance. That would connect Lantern’s customer access to a consequential economic result. Until then, I see a commercially credible recruiting company whose most important investment claim remains untested.
