# Palisade -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Fnu Prince’s professional red- and blue-team work since age 16 makes Palisade a specific-knowledge-heavy, network-light security bet: a rare kernel builder attached to a product whose next customer adds almost nothing for the previous one. **Stack Overflow** is the high rung for specific knowledge—developers building for developers after years of public artifacts and lived frustration. Palisade shares that founder-product-market fit: Prince encountered OS vulnerabilities professionally and Palisade already mitigates three named CVEs. Palisade diverges where it matters: no public repository, technical writing, customer deployment, or independently verified benchmark yet converts Prince’s biography into durable proof.

**Perplexity** is the low rung on user-added compounding: a single-sided product where one customer does not automatically improve another customer’s experience. Perplexity escaped that weakness through permissionless product distribution, Aravind Srinivas’s exceptional retrieval expertise, and pre-consensus AI timing. Palisade has the same network deficit without the same override. Palisade’s fleet telemetry might eventually improve its models, but the dossier identifies neither cross-customer learning nor a marketplace mechanism; proprietary policy data remains an inference, not a flywheel.

Palisade publicly requested introductions to security and infrastructure leads and offered founder-led demonstrations. That is labor leverage at the mouth of a code-leverage product. Over 18 months, Palisade can replicate kernel rules cheaply after installation, but every deployment will require security review, procurement, integration, rollback assurances, and permission to enforce rules on production kernels. Palisade therefore scales computation permissionlessly but distributes permissionedly—and Cisco’s Tetragon already offers open-source eBPF observability and enforcement before Palisade begins the sales conversation.

The $37.2 billion endpoint-security market, projected to reach $95.9 billion by 2033, gives Palisade genuine pivot-room across servers, cloud devices, GPU fleets, and on-device systems. Palisade earns a high market score because kernel security can expand into runtime policy, workload isolation, vulnerability management, or developer infrastructure. The counterfactual required for a top-tier score is demonstrated portability beyond the current Linux, eBPF, LSM-BPF, and sudo evidence; if Palisade cannot cross into Windows, macOS, or broader cloud workloads, the large TAM belongs to the category rather than to Palisade.

Fnu Prince left Caltech at 18 and reports professional security work beginning at 16, which reads as intelligence and energy. **Twitter** had an additional signal at seed: Ev Williams had already built Blogger, completed an exit, and shown how he treated partners through a full cycle. Palisade has no equivalent operating history, co-founder relationship, prior venture, or public body of work from which to judge integrity over ten years. Palisade receives the neutral integrity default—not suspicion, but not yet the evidence required to play long-term games with long-term people.

Cisco/Isovalent’s Tetragon already performs open-source eBPF enforcement, while Palisade has disclosed three CVE demonstrations and no paid deployments. By July 2029, Palisade will have pivoted from autonomous fleet-wide remediation into a Linux/eBPF policy-testing and rollback product and will remain below $3 million ARR. Palisade’s specific knowledge should produce useful technology; the missing network and permissionless distribution should prevent the useful technology from becoming the independent platform.

The $95.9 billion projected market supports a painful opposing case: Palisade could be an **Anchorage Digital** exception, where permission becomes the moat rather than the defect. Prince’s domain immersion, Palisade’s three working CVE demonstrations, and the catastrophic value of shortening remediation from weeks to minutes could make cautious enterprise adoption a source of proprietary telemetry and deep switching costs. The objection fails today because Palisade publishes no false-positive rate, rollback performance, customer deployment, or advantage over Tetragon’s existing enforcement layer. I would flip to Invest if Palisade reaches $1 million ARR across five named production fleets while publishing a sub-0.1% remediation-rollback rate; until then, I do not write the check.
