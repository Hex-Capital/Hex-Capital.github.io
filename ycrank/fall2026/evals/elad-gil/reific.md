# Reific -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Frontier labs are moving post-training into executable environments; Reific sells them a specific missing capability: learning to act before the full history is available. The controlling variable is **held-out reasoning improvement per training dollar relative to alternative environments**. If temporal records produce transferable gains that synthetic tasks miss, there is a reason for labs to keep buying. If they merely produce a harder benchmark, Reific has identified an evaluation gap without establishing a large training business.

The product makes that distinction testable. Records arrive on a clock, agents use tools, and answers are checked against recorded outcomes. The November 2 results—31.6%, 43.1%, and 29.7% of available reward across three task families—show substantial remaining difficulty. They do not establish that training closes the gap or improves performance elsewhere. Reific claims improvement but publishes neither its magnitude nor the compute required. That leaves the market’s central causal proposition partly specified and unproven. The reported 64 simultaneous sandboxes demonstrate delivery capability; they establish neither customer demand nor attractive delivery economics.

The strongest bull case is that temporal structure constitutes a genuinely different training input. Synthetic environments can generate enormous task volume while still failing to reproduce delayed evidence and consequential timing. Reific already describes longitudinal record acquisition, evidence-checked keys, cross-lab judges, and numeric checks. Its founder’s first-author EVEREST research provides relevant technical grounding. This fits my pattern of backing infrastructure across a technology transition: better models could increase demand for harder environments, while reusable grading and execution infrastructure retain value. But the research-stage exception requires unusually strong technical evidence and a credible capital path. One relevant publication and a working environment system do not yet establish that exception.

Market structure makes proof particularly important. Bespoke Labs has raised $40 million, Patronus has raised $70 million, and Prime Intellect combines environments with compute and training infrastructure. Prime Intellect’s reported revenue belongs to that broader business; it cannot establish demand for Reific’s specialty. Nonexclusive record licenses also give competitors a plausible replication path. I would ask what reproducing each useful environment actually costs in money, time, and access. The dossier supplies no answer. Custom construction could support an early business, but without reuse and delivery-cost evidence I cannot underwrite expanding software margins. The July 2026 shift toward executable post-training is a real opening; it does not settle who captures the economics.

I would pass on a personal check today. The sample-request funnel provides no documented repeat usage, so demand receives the absent-evidence default rather than an adverse judgment. Likewise, academic distinction establishes relevant expertise but supplies no evidence of prior startup learning or costly personal commitment. My verifiable flip condition is **one frontier-lab customer renewing a paid deployment after a controlled, compute-matched comparison demonstrates superior transfer to unseen temporal tasks against its best available alternative**. That would connect Reific’s distinctive technical mechanism to repeat procurement—the missing link that currently matters most.
