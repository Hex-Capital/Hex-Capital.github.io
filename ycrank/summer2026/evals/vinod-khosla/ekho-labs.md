# Ekho Labs -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The downside is one check. The disclosed upside is a better freight-decision layer, not yet a rewrite of freight economics. The dossier quantifies a $5.02 billion supply-chain-risk software market, but not the emergency-freight cost pool, population affected, or percentage of global freight spend Ekho Labs could eliminate. A 12-day warning may be valuable. It is not evidence of a 100x societal consequence.

The strongest fact is technical: Ekho combines more than 10,000 sources and reports a 12-day median warning lead time, including seven confirmed Hormuz forecasts and one ceasefire call 21 days early. But the benchmark is self-authored. There is no prospective test, false-positive rate, false-negative rate, or comparison against Everstream, carrier alerts, or an incumbent TMS. Data integration is difficult execution; it is not automatically a 5–10x discontinuity.

The bull case is real. Alina Park previously built multilingual geolocation, risk-detection, and enterprise financial-intelligence systems. Enterprise customers are referenced, ZEBOX supplies proximity to CMA CGM’s ecosystem, and shipment outcomes could eventually create a proprietary learning loop. This is the execution-heavy software pattern I can fund even without scientific invention—if the wedge opens into a giant value pool and usage produces proprietary compounding data.

Today, neither condition is proven. No customer count, revenue, proprietary corpus, or structural barrier is disclosed. Everstream already claimed more than 200 enterprise customers and offers predictive and prescriptive analytics; Altana, Interos, and Prewave have collectively raised hundreds of millions. If Ekho’s differentiation is narrower routing recommendations assembled from licensed feeds, the likely outcome is a feature battle against better-capitalized platforms. That is the standard AI red flag: integration and interface labor presented before novel capability has been established.

The largest kill risk is forecast validity, yet the dossier gives no falsifiable milestone with a sample size, accuracy threshold, cost, timing, or stop condition. Capital should first purchase an answer to that question, not more feeds or enterprise onboarding. Founder-market fit earns Park some credit, but there is no documented belief revision or evidence that she has recruited owners for forecasting science, ocean operations, and enterprise deployment as distinct risks.

I pass. The exception case does not clear: the underlying freight pool may be enormous, but Ekho has not quantified the portion it can alter or shown that its narrow wedge can expand into infrastructure. One verifiable result flips me: an independently evaluated prospective trial across at least 100 disruptions showing a median lead of 10 or more days, under 10% false positives, and at least 5x lower emergency-freight cost than customers’ existing TMS or Everstream workflow.
