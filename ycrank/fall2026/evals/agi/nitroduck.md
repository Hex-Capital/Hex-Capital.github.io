# Nitroduck -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Nitroduck proposes a flat-fee service that returns treated pollen for breeders to use in their existing crosses. The company aims to produce edited seed while bypassing a separate plant-regeneration step. It estimates that a new variety takes 8–16 years to develop and a new trait costs $3 million. Producing edited seed in a commercially relevant specialty crop remains a stated milestone (The Idea, Key Risks).

**Building blocks of superintelligence** scores zero because the constraint described in the memo is crop-specific tissue culture in plant breeding. The memo identifies no AI workload that depends on Nitroduck’s service or scarce input whose demand rises with AI deployment (The Idea).

**Industry enabled by superintelligence** scores zero because the proposed cost lever is a change in how crop edits are delivered. The memo identifies no role for AI in lowering the cost per successful edit or making a previously uneconomic breeding project viable. Both zero scores trigger Pass (The Idea, Key Risks).

The proposed mail-in workflow could distinguish the service, while competing editing tools and a third party’s published pollen-editing patent application leave durable value capture unproven. The memo reports a public launch and a competing breeder license, but gives no Nitroduck buyer commitment or paid trial. The founders’ award-winning duckweed engineering project demonstrates relevant prior delivery. Undisclosed editing efficiency and pricing leave the cost of delivering a successful edit unresolved (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks).

### Countercase

If treated pollen reliably produces edited seed in an existing breeding program, bypassing regeneration could make some specialty-crop projects feasible that breeders would otherwise skip. Pairwise’s license with a vegetable breeder shows that a buyer has adopted external editing tools. Nitroduck still needs to demonstrate the result in a commercial crop, and even that result would leave the AGI gate unmet unless AI materially changes the economics of the delivered edit (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks).

### Decisive diligence question

Can a breeder pilot show that AI lowers the fully loaded cost of producing verified edited seed enough to make a specialty-crop project viable that the breeder would otherwise skip? A successful result in the breeder’s existing workflow, with costs compared against the best available non-AI route and a breeder commitment to pay at the demonstrated economics, would change the action to Reach out to test repeatability. A seed demonstration alone would leave the action at Pass.
