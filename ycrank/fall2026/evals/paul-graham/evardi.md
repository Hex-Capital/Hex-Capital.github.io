# Evardi -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Shah has worked inside Kenya Power’s grid operations. That matters more to me than the phrase “agents that engineer the grid edge.” She has been somewhere this problem actually occurs. But the dossier stops before the founder story I most want: what broke at a utility, what she built around it, and how quickly the situation changed. Evardi’s product addresses a recognizable obstacle—missing transformer-level visibility—but describing a workaround is different from documenting the founders executing one under pressure. Resourcefulness remains unproven, rather than disproven.

The strongest reason to take Evardi seriously is the ugly work beneath the interface. Combining GIS, SCADA, billing and meter records means dealing with incompatible representations of the same physical network. Shah’s utility and integration experience gives her a plausible reason to understand that burden. Borele brings energy-engineering experience. This could be an opportunity hidden by schlep blindness: the useful product may emerge from repairing enough utility data that recommendations become trustworthy. The founders also knew each other from freshman year, which answers some of the concern about a team assembled for an accelerator application ([Berkeley SCET](https://scet.berkeley.edu/study-abroad-to-global-solutions-evardi-energy-grows-from-scets-global-entrepreneurship-and-innovation-program/)).

Evardi has supplied more technical substance than a diagram. Its published simulations report a 61% inspection hit rate against 6% from billing flags, and 42% fewer unsafe switching actions in storm drills. Those are specific, testable claims. They fit my preference for industrial companies that turn ambition into numerical evidence. But the studies use public data; they do not establish that Evardi can reconstruct a particular utility’s imperfect network and improve its operators’ decisions. The architecture and simulated results deserve partial credit, not the credit an independently validated field result would earn ([case studies](https://evardi.ai/case-studies)).

The strongest argument against passing is the claimed deployment footprint: utilities serving 10 million customers and operating 180,000 miles of line. If engineers inside those organizations repeatedly depend on Evardi, I may be overlooking substantial demand. But those figures measure the utilities’ scale. They leave unanswered whether five engineers return every week or one innovation team attended a demonstration. Utility engineers are a specific user niche, and reported deployments earn some credit. They do not yet establish a deep well of need. My exceptions allow working products or unmistakable user behavior to substitute for uncertain founder evidence; this dossier does not demonstrate either strongly enough to override the resourcefulness gate.

There is a plausible business here: begin with one inspection or switching workflow, earn trust, and expand through the network model. Neara and Grid4C make the ability to earn that trust more important than the category label. No public revenue is unsurprising at this stage; the consequential uncertainty is whether Evardi’s integrations produce recurring operational value that supports software licensing. The invitation to bring a feeder map and a year of meter data is a concrete starting point. Nothing establishes dependence on a large distribution partner. Likewise, January’s mini-grid demand-prediction product and November’s utility-engineering product show movement, but without the observation that prompted it, I cannot score that movement as evidence-driven learning.

I would pass on this evidence. Evardi has credible proximity to the problem and promising technical claims, but my people-first gate remains unmet. One verified live rollout would change my decision if it documented a utility-data blocker, a founder-built fix shipped within weeks, and engineers subsequently returning to use the resulting workflow. That would connect the founders’ behavior to the product’s usefulness. Today those pieces are still separate.
