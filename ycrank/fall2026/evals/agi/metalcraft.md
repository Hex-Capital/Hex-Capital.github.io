# Metalcraft -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Metalcraft’s strongest commercial case is licensing biology evaluation tasksets to life-science model developers. The memo documents a public catalog of six tasksets with sample cases and an access request flow. Metalcraft reports model scores by taskset. Licensing is an inference from that access flow, and the memo contains no buyer commitments, paid deployments, or revenue evidence. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Building blocks of superintelligence.** The relevant workload is evaluating life-science models on research decisions under uncertainty. Existing benchmarks can isolate individual skills, but a gap in coverage does not establish a shortage of evaluation capacity. The memo identifies overlapping internal and third-party benchmarks and gives no evidence that buyers face development delays or that comparable evaluation coverage is slow to add. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Industry enabled by superintelligence.** The tasksets measure model performance. The memo identifies no previously excluded research buyer, use, or frequency and provides no evidence that AI lowers the fully loaded cost per successful research outcome enough to make one viable. Both thesis paths score zero, which sets the action to Pass. (The Idea, Key Risks)

In-house benchmark development creates a concrete substitution risk, and exclusive data rights remain hypothetical. Customer demand and scale economics take their absent-evidence defaults because the memo documents an access request flow but no buyer behavior or pricing and delivery-cost data. The published catalog and Marshall Kiely’s computational biology and applied AI experience support seed-stage execution credit. Commercial qualification remains unproven. (Defensibility, Key Risks, The Idea, Key Facts, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

### Countercase

As life-science models improve, developers may need more representative evaluations to make release decisions. Metalcraft has shipped tasksets across distinct research tasks, and the memo reports a performance gap between a domain-specific model and a general model on an existing benchmark. That makes specialized evaluation commercially plausible. The memo gives no evidence that a buyer hit a release bottleneck with available benchmarks or that Metalcraft’s scores predict performance in live research. The countercase leaves the Pass gate in place. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)
