# Asim -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** If cheaper intelligence increases the number of robot models and releases developers want to deploy, developers may need more physical testing to catch failures that emerge when objects or operating conditions change. Asim says it runs repeatable trials that vary shape, weight, friction, and material, and supplements them with simulation. The potential bottleneck is repeatable, deployment-relevant evidence from real hardware. The memo supports the failure mechanism and leaves Asim’s incremental detection and testing throughput unmeasured. (The Idea; Key Risks)

Asim says it keeps some test scenarios private and shares the results with developers. That could preserve the value of an independent test across model releases. A lasting advantage would require evidence that Asim can keep developing hard-to-reproduce tests and has rights to reuse relevant results. The memo records launch attention and gives no evidence of buyer commitments. The founders’ robotics, safety, and benchmarking experience, the earlier Vigie prototype, and the November benchmarking launch support a seed-stage execution case. Delivery on a customer’s robot remains unverified. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Founders & Team; Key Facts)

**Scale scenario.** Assuming Asim charges per evaluation, annual revenue would depend on the number of paying developers, evaluations per developer, and price per evaluation. The next job must cover scoping, hardware time, supervision, scene resets, simulation, and reporting. Reusing test setups across releases could improve contribution if customization stays manageable. The memo gives no measured price, labor per job, or repeat frequency. Path B is less supported because the memo identifies neither a newly viable buyer or use nor a measured reduction in delivery labor at equivalent quality. (The Idea; Key Risks; Key Facts)

### Countercase

With abundant capital, rival labs could build comparable physical testing capacity, and developers could bring common tests in-house using the baseline apparatus NIST plans to distribute. Robocurve already publishes physical evaluations, and Asim’s assessments are scoped to a specific system, task, and environment. If buyers can reproduce the useful findings at comparable effort, Asim’s private scenarios offer little protection. The memo provides no head-to-head result that establishes substitution for Asim’s customized tests, so the Path A override remains untriggered. The missing evidence on bottleneck relief and durable control keeps the action at Watch. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Asim earn a paid repeat evaluation by finding deployment-relevant failures that a robotics developer’s existing workflow misses at comparable cost? A paid pilot on the developer’s hardware could answer this through a matched comparison, measured supervision and reset time, and buyer-confirmed findings. A paid follow-on tied to those findings, with terms allowing Asim to reuse the test methods, would support Reach out if the result clears the score and confidence gates. Comparable findings at comparable effort through readily available alternatives would move the current case to Pass.
