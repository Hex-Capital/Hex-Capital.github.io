# screenpipe -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is still an adjective, not a calculation. screenpipe could give agents a persistent memory of everything a worker has seen, said, and heard, but the dossier never translates that capability into hours eliminated, expert services automated, or a cost pool reduced by 10x. An $11.2 billion productivity-tools TAM describes existing spending; it does not establish a 100x societal consequence. This may become useful infrastructure, but usefulness is not yet a Black-Swan outcome.

The technical work is more substantial than the typical AI wrapper. A 20.9K-star repository with 12,176 commits spans cross-platform capture, accessibility extraction, transcription, encryption, search, MCP, and permissioned agents. That is real implementation depth. Yet against Microsoft Recall, Granola, and Otter, the evidence establishes broader scope—not a measured 5–10x discontinuity in agent accuracy, workflow completion, latency, or cost. Microsoft can bundle overlapping functionality, while much of the stack rests on available OS APIs, SQLite, Whisper, OCR, and foundation models.

The bull case stings. Two hundred and two subscribers and $3,480 MRR existed by February 2025; usage subsequently grew sixfold over two months, revenue reportedly rose 20–30% week over week, and the project attracted 2.1K forks without paid acquisition. This resembles the known-exception pattern at Block: a tiny team can begin with an unremarkable tool and evolve into infrastructure around a large workflow. The material difference is that screenpipe has not yet demonstrated the platform expansion or value pool needed for that override. Its 320 weekly active users against 300K claimed users also suggests that attention and durable utility may be very different quantities.

The largest kill risk is not whether Beaumont can ship software. His infrastructure, machine-learning, computer-use, and satellite-intelligence experience, plus years maintaining a personal “second brain,” establish founder-market fit. The risk is whether continuous capture can survive enterprise privacy, consent, reliability, and incumbent-distribution constraints. The dossier provides mitigations—local storage, exclusions, encryption, retention controls—but no decisive experiment, pass/fail threshold, or spending sequence. Hiring a Head of Virality before proving enterprise trust would invert the risk-removal graph.

The current $21 and $42-per-seat pricing proves that some customers will pay without subsidy, but it does not demonstrate economic parity with the human work supposedly displaced. My 36-month base case is a developer-heavy product reaching $3–8 million ARR, not a universal context layer: strong open-source interest and organic growth sustain a business, while privacy friction and operating-system bundling constrain broad enterprise deployment.

I pass today. The one condition that flips me is independently measured evidence from at least ten 100-seat enterprise deployments showing that screenpipe-powered agents complete a named workflow at five times the incumbent throughput while satisfying those customers’ privacy and retention requirements. That would convert impressive engineering activity into a technical discontinuity attached to an expensive, defensible workflow.
