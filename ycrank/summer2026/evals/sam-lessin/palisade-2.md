# Palisade -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I start with three blanks: no proposed post-money valuation, no competing-term-sheet count, and no evidence that my check would be genuinely scarce. That does not prove Palisade is an auctioned YC deal, but it means I cannot underwrite the deal itself. More importantly, the dossier never says what the round buys beyond generic company-building. “Find customers” is not a venture moment. A valid experiment would specify a fleet, a deadline, an acceptable false-block rate, and whether safe automated remediation converts that operator into a paying customer.

The product is more substantive than the financing story. A solo founder reports a live LSM-BPF control plane, syscall-level decisions, fleet-wide kernel enforcement, and detection of three named Linux privilege-escalation vulnerabilities. Prince’s claimed red-team, blue-team, ML, and operating-systems experience since age 16 fits the technical bridge unusually well for pre-seed. This is not merely an AI interface pasted over a security workflow; the kernel mechanism exists. But the efficacy evidence is founder-published, the website was inaccessible, and there is no independent benchmark, deployment, or public repository establishing that Palisade can remediate rather than simply demonstrate.

The bull case is that a technically credible outsider has built the narrow capability that Oligo, Upwind, and Aqua—despite raising $80 million, $180 million, and $325 million respectively—have not made their center of gravity. If Palisade safely closes the loop from telemetry to mitigation, the incumbents’ breadth may become a liability. The market is also moving toward automated runtime response, with Sysdig reporting materially greater use of automatic process termination in 2026. That combination could turn a small Linux wedge into strategically valuable infrastructure.

The objection that stops me is control. Kernel-enforced remediation can create the failure it is supposed to prevent, and the dossier provides no rollback system, approval boundary, policy-testing layer, or measured false-positive rate. AI is currently attached to a dangerous action without evidence that the base workflow works commercially or operationally. Three detected CVEs do not establish that an enterprise will permit autonomous rules across thousands of endpoints. Nor does Palisade yet own a dark pool: 95 LinkedIn followers, a YC launch post, and requests for introductions have caused no documented pilot, purchase, referral, or retained usage.

Delete the next financing and the model disappears. There is no pricing, revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or controllable cash metric; even the per-endpoint SaaS model is inferred. The funded competitors validate demand but also make telemetry reproducible and distribution expensive. Palisade could become a useful feature or acquihire before it becomes a standalone business, especially while disclosed coverage remains Linux-focused.

I am passing today. The technical artifact earns another meeting, not a check, because both the deal and the truth-revealing use of capital are unspecified. My flip condition is one named enterprise converting a 90-day deployment across at least 1,000 endpoints into a paid annual contract after zero rollback-triggering false blocks. That single result would establish purchase causality, remediation safety, and the beginning of proprietary fleet data rather than another security demonstration.
