# Dualizer -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by an industrial simulation or automation vendor is the strongest private exit path. The memo documents a public demo and reports several active industry pilots with undisclosed payment terms. Dualizer says its in-house solver works with an engineering assistant to turn plant drawings into model changes and calibrate simulations against operating data. An incumbent could gain control of the solver and its roadmap by acquiring Dualizer, then embed the workflow across its product suite. Ownership would be worth paying for if the workflow materially shortened deployment across plants and licensing or building a comparable tool could not deliver the same benefit. The memo records Emerson’s purchase of the remaining AspenTech shares, which shows strategic demand for process simulation assets but supplies no usable price benchmark for Dualizer. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A paid rollout across multiple plants with measured deployment time and calibration accuracy would test whether the workflow is repeatable. Active pilots give Dualizer a way to run that test, and the documented $100,000 competition prize is a concrete funding signal. The memo leaves burn, runway, and rollout costs unknown. It also leaves solver IP ownership, plant-data transfer rights, and the investor’s share terms unverified. There is no independently supported fallback that can pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts, Defensibility)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** A $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

| Scenario | Timing and sale assumptions | Holder cash and gross multiple |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | Year 5, $40 million enterprise value | $1.2 million, 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7, $100 million enterprise value | $3 million, 6× at 3% ownership. At 2%, $2 million, 4×. |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested | $0 realized, 0×, with shares still held |

Both sale scenarios assume zero net debt and other price adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. They assume pro rata allocation with no preference leakage, all cash at closing, and zero stock, escrow, earnout, rollover, or compensation. These are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no buyer economics or applicable transaction terms to support either sale price or waterfall. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An incumbent could add conversational automation to existing HYSYS models through a ProcAgent-style integration, avoiding the cost of migration or acquisition. MaximaLabs already offers a deterministic solver with plant calibration, further weakening the ownership case. Dualizer’s published calibration demo used synthetic histories, its earlier tests had generalization problems, and site-specific customization could make each rollout costly. Together, those facts leave open whether buying Dualizer delivers enough incremental value to clear the proceeds hurdle. The memo does not establish that the substitutes match Dualizer’s full workflow or impose a structural price ceiling, so the Pass override does not apply. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a paid multi-plant rollout show enough incremental value from owning Dualizer to justify a price that leaves $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds? Customer-verified deployment and calibration results against HYSYS with conversational automation, tied to an incumbent’s economics for buying versus building or licensing, would move the action to Reach out if they supported that hurdle.
