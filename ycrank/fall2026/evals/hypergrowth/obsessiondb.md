# ObsessionDB -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**Path B. Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** The first buyer is a data team serving a live API with recurring latency and infrastructure cost pressure. Osmosis reports moving its API from Tinybird and cutting annual operating costs from about $60,000 to $30,000. It recorded 13 million requests in a 30-day period after migration, an early signal of recurring production use. ObsessionDB reports other paying production customers and a separate migration from Elasticsearch. The memo gives no cohort history or example of paid workload expansion. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The growth hypothesis is that cost audits can win similar buyers, a reusable migration can get the first API live, and separate compute groups can turn additional workloads into incremental revenue. The memo documents the sales and migration offers but leaves lead origin, conversion, and founder effort open. In a second geography, the same ClickHouse interfaces could serve the same job, assuming the deployment meets local hosting and buyer security requirements. Customer-cloud deployment gives buyers control over networking and encryption keys. Cross-market demand and rollout effort remain unverified. (The Idea, Key Risks)

For a managed account, incremental compute and storage revenue would need to cover added compute, object storage, cache capacity, and support. Each new account also adds sales and migration effort. Customer-cloud deployment could shift infrastructure spending to the buyer while ObsessionDB still provides operations and support. Osmosis’s savings establish buyer value. ObsessionDB’s contribution on the next workload remains unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

ObsessionDB may be able to win individual migrations without making them repeatable. Some workloads need changes to storage or coordination workflows, cache misses add latency, and ClickHouse Cloud already offers isolated compute over shared storage. If each win requires founder-led tuning and ongoing support to sustain the buyer’s savings, delivery effort could rise in step with revenue. The memo leaves that cost relationship unresolved, so the structural Pass gate does not apply. A measured rollout would be more informative than a conversation today. (The Idea, Key Risks)
