# Rodeo Robotics -- Paul Graham Evaluation

What happened the first time Rodeo Robotics encountered an oil filter its robot couldn't reach? That is the fact I want. The dossier identifies vehicle variation and unreliable service information, then describes human assistance and teleoperation as the response. This is a sensible approach. But it supplies no particular failure, founder-built workaround, or elapsed time. I cannot yet distinguish founders who have solved an awkward physical problem from founders who have correctly described one.

Hazard spent four years at Applied Intuition, and Duke led neural simulation there. They met at work, which gives their relationship more substance than a team assembled for an application. This is the strongest argument against passing: two people with relevant technical experience have chosen a narrow task and accepted the messy work of doing it with humans before automating everything. Instrumented tools could let them deliver maintenance while collecting demonstrations. Each job could produce both a service fee and information about what to automate next. But the dossier establishes the proposed mechanism, not that this learning loop works ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/rodeo-robotics)).

The distinction matters because the difficult part may be hidden inside the assistance. A robot that needs a technician throughout an oil change has not necessarily freed any scarce labor. Rodeo needs to show completed jobs, technician minutes per job, and what happens when the vehicle changes. No measured task result or intervention rate is supplied. My pattern of backing technical teams outside familiar software markets depends on concrete proof; their previous employers cannot substitute for it. My exceptions likewise require a working product, unmistakable pull, or validated technical performance. None is established here.

Fleet depots are a sufficiently specific place to begin. The $599.7 billion aftermarket estimate adds little to that insight. PitPro's tire automation and UVeye's inspections show adjacent activity, but neither establishes demand for Rodeo's oil changes. The reported $500,000 financing and October 13 launch coverage also do not tell us whether a depot has asked the founders to return. I give demand the untested default. Nor does accepting human assistance establish an evidence-driven change of route: there is no rejected hypothesis or dated product revision. Their automotive software experience is relevant, but the dossier does not connect this idea to a maintenance problem either founder personally encountered.

I would pass on Rodeo Robotics today. I see no documented dependence on a large partner, premature sales hiring, or refusal to confront contrary evidence. This is an evidence gap in my central founder test, rather than an active red flag. The opportunity contains plenty of schlep; I need to see these founders move through it. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: a documented 30-day trial at a named depot showing a specific maintenance failure, the workaround the founders shipped and how quickly, followed by the depot repeatedly using the revised system with fewer technician minutes per completed job. That would connect resourcefulness to something a user actually wants.
