# Ageospatial -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a commercial building carrying an updating record that changes its insurance when the building changes. Ageospatial’s November 2025 demonstration combining Gemini 3, Copernicus flood data and Overture building footprints is a small, concrete step toward that world. But connecting sources must lead to reliable reconciliation, then to decisions an insurer trusts enough to act on. The dossier demonstrates the first connection; it does not establish the remaining transitions. I give the working integration some credit without treating my imagined destination as the company’s accomplishment.

Maaz Sheikh is the strongest reason to keep looking. The independently documented January 2024 Geo+ launch, subsequent GeoForge product and Swiss cooperation award show someone building geospatial tools before this insurance launch. His geomatics training and reported seven years serving insurance, energy and government give that persistence a relevant direction. I put the historical 1,000 Geo+ users here as evidence that his earlier building reached people, not into current insurance traction. This is substantially better than a résumé followed by a fashionable pivot, although recruitment and commercial execution are less established.

Why are properties going uninspected? Who pays for that ignorance? Ageospatial identifies dependence on owner and broker submissions, with company estimates of 97% of commercial properties never inspected and $36 billion in annual premium leakage. That is a recognizable information failure. The immediate beneficiary, however, appears to be the carrier collecting more accurately priced premiums. The dossier does not demonstrate that owners gain control, avoid unnecessary inspections or receive credit for reducing hazards. Repairing insurer economics is useful; the individual agency I particularly value remains unproven.

The strongest argument against withholding a check is that this founder has already crossed into customer commitment. Ageospatial reports signing its largest insurance customer in October 2026, and Sheikh reports increasing revenue. Those claims matter at pre-seed, even without disclosed amounts. AXA and Die Mobiliar logos do not establish payment or repeated use, and 18 upcoming demos remain sales activity. Ageospatial could fit the exception that led me to back pre-revenue **CosmicBrain AI**: persistent technical building aimed at a larger physical future. The material difference is that the latter dossier established a functioning prototype and signed industrial proofs of concept; this one leaves the insurer’s actual deployment and consequential behavior largely undescribed.

Ageospatial also fits my quieter pattern of liking mundane operational repair beneath an attractive surface. An enduring asset record could become useful infrastructure. Yet accumulating records is not evidence of controlling a scarce asset: reuse rights, exclusive inputs and measured learning advantages remain unverified. Moody’s already combines CAPE intelligence with catastrophe models, while Betterview offers monitoring and property flags. Engineer-led deployment might uncover valuable operating complexity, but it might also leave Sheikh selling custom implementation work. Neither possibility earns infrastructure credit before the dependency is demonstrated.

I would hold my personal check, with interest in the founder rather than conviction in the layer. The old co-founder reference and current solo-founder description warrant clarification, but do not establish a leadership shell game; likewise, the dossier establishes neither severe privacy abuse nor dependence on one closed model supplier. My hesitation comes from the gap between a credible builder and a demonstrated enabling asset. **One verifiable flip condition:** a referenceable carrier deployment audit showing that Ageospatial’s reusable asset-history layer repeatedly changes underwriting decisions at a positive delivery margin. That would turn the central promise into something I could underwrite.
