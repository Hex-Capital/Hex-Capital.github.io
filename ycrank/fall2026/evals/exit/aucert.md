# Aucert -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is Aucert’s strongest private-company path. A QA automation vendor could buy Aucert to integrate mobile functional, design, accessibility, and store-policy checks into its release workflow. The memo documents a public launch and reports a private beta. Aucert describes agents that explore builds and retain customer-approved expectations in an organization-scoped knowledge graph. If that system repeatedly catches costly release defects that existing tools miss, ownership could give a buyer control over how the capability is integrated and developed across its product. The memo provides no measured production benefit or evidence that a buyer values that control above a license. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

The operating milestone is a production pilot across successive releases that measures incremental release-blocking defects caught and the cost of false positives against the customer’s existing workflow. The beta and the founders’ mobile QA and automated testing experience make that test plausible. The memo lists a reported $500,000 convertible note and a $125,000 seed entry, with no confirmed runway or pilot budget. A sale could center on the software, with IP ownership and rights to transfer customer-specific app maps still unverified. The memo supplies no independently supported fallback that could pay this holder. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Defensibility*, *Key Facts*)

**Proceeds bridge.** The investor’s terms and any achievable acquisition price are undisclosed. The illustrative seed case uses one $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3% and no follow-on. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash by year eight, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale scenarios assume zero applicable net debt or other price adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. They assume a pro rata waterfall with no preference adjustment and all consideration paid in cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation each contribute $0. The values are scenario inputs. Actual note conversion and priority are unknown. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A QA vendor could license Aucert or extend its own tooling. The memo describes overlapping exploratory and regression capabilities from Momentic and QA Wolf, and Google Play already covers some Android accessibility and stability checks. Aucert’s own materials differ on which checks are live. If a license or internal build delivers comparable results, the buyer has little reason to pay an acquisition premium. The overlap is concrete, but the memo leaves the adequacy of those substitutes and their effect on price unresolved. The structural Pass override is therefore unproven, while the missing ownership economics and price support warrant Watch. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a prospective QA acquirer use results from successive production releases to show that owning Aucert delivers enough incremental value over licensing or building to support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds under the modeled 3% case? A buyer-backed analysis that quantifies avoided release costs and supports that price would move the action to Reach out. A pilot that shows product value alone would keep it at Watch.
