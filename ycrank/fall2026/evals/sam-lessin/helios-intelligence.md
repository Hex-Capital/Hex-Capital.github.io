# Helios Intelligence -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Helios Intelligence has disclosed YC and Formation backing, but no post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or round-funded experiment. I cannot tell whether my check buys an overlooked belief or joins an already financed scientific ambition. That is missing deal evidence, not evidence of an auction. The distinction matters: I am passing on the investment presented here without concluding that the underlying science is unattractive.

The founders are the strongest reason to hesitate before passing. Omar Abudayyeh brings diagnostic-company formation and reported acquisitions; Jonathan Gootenberg brings Sherlock and Tome experience. Their jointly authored May 2026 mAge research connects proteomics, wearables, and mortality outcomes, reporting a 21% reduction in baseline prediction error. These are substantive receipts beyond academic titles. Helios fits my unstated preference for people who can translate between difficult systems—here, molecular biology and computational interpretation. The missing side is demonstrated consumer operation: their research does not establish that home collection reliably delivers the proposed 1,000-plus-biomarker panel or that customers benefit enough to repeat it.

The strongest bull case is the sequencing. Roon is already available, with subscriptions advertised from $9.16 per tin, and the company intends consumer commerce to help fund biological data collection. If that commerce generates cash and recruits consenting repeat testers economically, Helios could build an owned distribution channel and longitudinal dataset together. That would make the supplement business strategically useful. But availability establishes neither purchases nor that causal loop. There are no disclosed conversion, repeat-purchase, dataset-exclusivity, or customer-outcome figures. The dark pool remains a proposed asset.

Delete the next financing from the model. What pays for sample collection, laboratory processing, failed samples, interpretation, and customer support? Helios supplies neither test pricing nor margins, burn, or runway, so I use the missing-economics default rather than assuming this cannot work. Function’s 160-plus tests and SiPhox’s home collection show that consumers have alternatives; Helios must establish why its additional molecular breadth changes a purchase or health decision. Biomarker count alone cannot answer that. Nor does the related mAge result validate the commercial assay. The AI red flag would become active if predictive promises substituted for those operating results; the dossier does not establish that they have.

There is a legitimate venture moment available: determine whether this home-collected panel can deliver reproducible measurements that support a useful, repeatable paid service. The dossier does not tie round proceeds to a dated, measurable test of that hypothesis, so I cannot award credit for designing the experiment myself. I also checked the exceptions to my usual gates: prior founder exits are present, but documented conversion, retention, profitable operations, and an explicit financing structure that avoids repeated raises are absent. Those exceptions do not carry this deal. **My verifiable condition for reopening diligence is an independently audited paid-repeat cohort producing positive contribution cash flow after collection, laboratory processing, failed samples, and support.** That would turn the proposed commerce-to-data mechanism into operating evidence. Today, I keep my money.
