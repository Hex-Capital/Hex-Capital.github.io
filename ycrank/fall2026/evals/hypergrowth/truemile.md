# TrueMile -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

TrueMile’s strongest path is to win recurring truckload freight on dedicated lanes, add capacity through leased-on owner-operators, and use fleet-wide planning to pair outbound loads with return freight. Assuming strong product-market fit, denser lanes could reduce empty miles, while automated broker calls and dispatch coordination could let each dispatcher handle more trucks. TrueMile reports 10% more monthly miles per truck than the industry average and 9× as many trucks manageable per dispatcher. Its documented sales path runs through shipper inquiries and calls, with individually discussed lease-on terms for operators. (The Idea; Market & Traction)

Dedicated lanes create a credible recurring job. A retained shipper could book more loads, but the memo documents neither repeat paid volume nor terms that establish how TrueMile would monetize that expansion. The company reports $57K in freight booked over three weeks. The memo does not establish how much was delivered, billed, or collected, or whether realized revenue grew across comparable periods. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Facts)

Under the memo’s inferred carrier model, contribution per completed load would start with freight revenue and subtract the operator payout, any empty-mile costs TrueMile bears, agent costs, human approval and exception handling, and attributable sales, onboarding, safety, and compliance costs. Better backhaul matching and dispatcher productivity could improve contribution. The memo gives no carrier payout terms or representative load costs to quantify that improvement. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Countercase

The routing solver and dispatch agents could transfer to another market, but the current product is a carrier service built around U.S. lease-on operations. Serving comparable shippers abroad would require adapting carrier compliance and operating procedures and assembling a local operator and freight network. The memo supports no near-term path to doing that with bounded changes. This triggers the structural downside gate even assuming strong fit on the initial lanes. Required human approval and signature on every load also limit operating leverage, though the memo leaves their incremental cost uncertain. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can TrueMile use its current solver and dispatch workflow to serve a recurring, paid lane in a second country through a local carrier partner, with bounded compliance and onboarding changes? A completed pilot with repeat paid loads and a documented account of the changes and manual work would move the action to Watch if it demonstrated a repeatable rollout.
