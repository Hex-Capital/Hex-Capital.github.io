# Foreshadow Industries -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A naval systems integrator could buy Zulu and its retrofit integration work to reduce the time and cost of qualifying autonomy across existing vessels. Foreshadow reports integrations on three military vessels. The ownership case depends on whether the integration work is reusable and whether owning it would let a buyer qualify and adapt future retrofits faster than building internally or licensing a competing system. The memo provides no measured savings or evidence of buyer willingness to pay. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Foreshadow also reports repeated overnight demonstrations, including simultaneous operation on two vessels. The Navy’s move from at-sea tests to procurement in a separate vessel program makes production qualification a relevant milestone. A funded qualification on another vessel class would test whether Foreshadow can repeat the integration with materially less effort. The memo records YC backing of an undisclosed amount and gives no evidence of a funded qualification plan or runway to reach it. IP ownership and transfer rights are unverified. The memo supplies no independent path to cash for this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

With no investment terms or usable acquisition pricing in the memo, the bridge uses the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3%, with no follow-on. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The threshold is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. Both sale scenarios assume an all-cash closing, zero applicable net debt or other price adjustments, and a simple pro rata allocation with no preference deductions. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. The prices and dates are scenario inputs.

- **Modest sale, year 5.** $40 million of enterprise value yields $40 million of distributable equity and $1.2 million of holder cash, or 2.4× total checks.
- **Strong sale, year 7.** $100 million of enterprise value yields $100 million of distributable equity and $3 million of holder cash, or 6.0× total checks. At 2% ownership, the same sale yields $2 million, or 4.0×.
- **Prolonged private, through year 8.** Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public-market scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The holder retains the shares and realizes $0, or 0.0× total checks.

### Countercase

Sea Machines already sells vessel retrofit systems and is eligible for follow-on Navy procurement. An integrator could license an existing system, including Zulu if available on ordinary terms, and capture enough of the benefit to make an acquisition uneconomic. Foreshadow’s reported demonstrations are maritime, and the memo contains no comparative qualification costs or evidence of reuse across vehicle classes. This is a credible substitute, but the memo does not establish equivalent performance or a binding price ceiling below the hurdle. The structural Pass override does not trigger. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a naval systems integrator value owning Zulu above the modeled $83.3 million equity hurdle after seeing the cost and time required to qualify a retrofit on a second vessel class? A buyer assessment grounded in measured qualification results and a comparison with building or licensing would move the action to **Reach out** if it supported that price. Evidence that equivalent licensed capability caps the price materially below the hurdle would move it to **Pass**.
