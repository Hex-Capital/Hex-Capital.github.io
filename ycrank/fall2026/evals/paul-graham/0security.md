# 0.security -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The most useful fact about 0.security is that Doruk has been credited with finding a real vulnerability in JSONata. Someone responsible for the affected software acknowledged the work. But finding a vulnerability and overcoming a startup’s hardest obstacle are different tests. The dossier never shows what blocked these founders, what they changed, or how quickly the change worked. I would leave resourcefulness unproven rather than infer it from Doruk’s cyber-defense employment or either founder’s reported exit.

There is nevertheless something here to inspect. The public harness is working software, and the advisory supplies external evidence of technical ability. That fits my pattern of backing technical founders who build a useful primitive before asking permission. It earns substantial technical credit. It does not establish that the platform reliably verifies attack paths and produces correct fixes across unfamiliar repositories. The Spotify engineer’s report of scanning almost 30,000 files in under a minute measures throughput; it does not measure how many consequential vulnerabilities were found or correctly repaired.

The strongest argument for investing is that these two founders may already be doing the difficult work while their public numbers lag behind. A harness with 526 stars and 58 forks, reported work with EY, and a named engineer describing actual use make that plausible. The missing distinction is between trying a security tool and depending on it. None establishes repeated use, payment, or a small group that would be distressed if 0.security disappeared. The $30 monthly subscription and free $50 usage allowance make experimentation accessible, but published prices tell me nothing about conversion or whether usage pays for its compute.

Doruk’s work at Migros gives the problem a credible connection to his experience. It does not document the particular frustration that caused him to build this product. Own-cloud deployment, air-gapped models, and audit logs identify genuine enterprise schlep; the dossier does not show a customer installation in which the founders actually overcame it. Nor does it show an observation that forced a product revision. These are unanswered questions, not evidence of passivity. They matter because Aikido already offers overlapping workflows: the team needs a narrow job it performs unusually well, and customer resistance is often how founders discover that job.

I would pass on writing a personal check today. There is no documented dependence on a major partner or ceremonial launch, and I would not manufacture a cofounder problem from the limited relationship history. I also considered the exception for a working product or independently validated proof overriding missing founder evidence. The harness and vulnerability credit make that exception worth considering, but they do not yet demonstrate exceptional product performance or unmistakable user pull strong enough to replace the resourcefulness test. My flip condition is one documented customer sequence: a security team rejects a finding or deployment, the founders ship a workaround within two weeks, and that same team subsequently pays and uses it weekly for six weeks. That would connect technical ability to learning under resistance.
