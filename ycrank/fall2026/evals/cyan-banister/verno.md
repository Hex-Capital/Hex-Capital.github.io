# Verno -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I want an engineer to change a plant specification once and have every affected drawing, equipment list, and datasheet agree before anything gets ordered. Verno is working on an unglamorous obstacle to that world: changes still travel through documents by hand. Its proposed sequence—native CAD edits, cross-document checks, engineer acceptance—is sensible. But reliable reconciliation must precede trustworthy propagation across an entire project. The dossier establishes work on the first transition; the larger future remains my hypothesis.

Why does an engineer have to become a human synchronization service? Because separate deliverables can describe the same equipment without staying consistent when its specifications change. Verno identifies the institutional failure and its consequences: reconciliation labor, incorrect orders, and schedule delays. Returning that time to engineers appeals to me. However, the dossier does not quantify the burden or identify a gatekeeper collecting a rent. This is useful workflow repair, with less evidence of the deeper transfer of economic control I look for.

The founders have relevant operating tuition. Oliver Wesche’s engineering-document automation experience and Ujjawal Madan’s document-extraction work connect directly to the problem. Recruiting Rohit Shinde, with more than a decade in petrochemicals and EPC consulting, adds knowledge that model engineering alone cannot supply. Their reported years working together also matter. These are affirmative team signals, but the [founder account](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Wil-verno-ai-that-edits-and-checks-the-engineering-documents-behind-every-industrial-plant) does not establish a pre-company obsession through dated experiments or independently built artifacts. I see a credible team; I cannot yet see the first-believer act.

The strongest argument against passing is that an EPC reportedly entrusted Verno with actual project drawings by July 2026, followed by reported live customer deployments in November. Sharing those drawings and introducing software into engineering work costs organizational effort and trust. That is more meaningful than a pitch competition. My pre-revenue exception applies here: revenue is unnecessary when a working artifact and exceptional founder evidence make the destination convincing. But these deployment claims remain company-reported, without repeated-use detail or measured outcomes. They establish an early foothold, rather than demonstrating that engineers now depend on the product.

Verno also touches my pattern of backing concealed infrastructure beneath a visible application. Native-file editing could become a valuable connection between engineering intent and physical execution. The missing piece is demonstrated control over a scarce dependency. Connectors and project rules are described as a possible future moat; the dossier identifies none today. [Autodesk already provides authoring and configurable validation](https://www.autodesk.com/products/autocad/included-toolsets/autocad-plant-3d). The decisive question is whether Verno accumulates difficult engineering knowledge that customers cannot readily obtain elsewhere. Its [privacy disclosures](https://www.verno-ai.com/privacy)—customer approval, encryption, and contractual protections for US processing through Bedrock—address my consent concern sufficiently to avoid treating privacy as an established red flag. They do not establish model-provider switching power.

I pass with my own money today. Verno has credible people repairing a concrete industrial wound, but its strongest evidence is team relevance, while the controlled enabling asset remains unproven. Named CEO and CTO roles provide some accountability; ownership and tiebreaker arrangements are unknown, rather than evidence of a governance violation. My one verifiable flip condition is an independently referenceable EPC deployment showing sustained weekly use across successive project revisions, with audited reductions in reconciliation labor and escaped inconsistencies against its prior workflow. That would show that Verno has become part of how engineering work gets done.
