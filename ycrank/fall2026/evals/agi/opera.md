# OpEra -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

OpEra helps factory, warehouse, and fleet planners rebuild schedules and routes after disruptions. Its described product connects to operational systems, models site-specific rules, and reruns a mathematical optimizer when a planner enters a constraint in plain language. The memo identifies no AI workload that depends on an input OpEra supplies, so the building-block path scores zero. It also describes buyers who already replan manually or with existing software, without identifying a buyer or replanning frequency that only becomes economically viable with OpEra. That leaves the industry-enabled path at zero and triggers Pass. (The Idea)

Site-specific models and integrations could create switching costs once embedded in daily operations. Retention is unverified, and Timefold’s APIs give internal teams a starting point for overlapping workflows. The reported spreadsheet and manual workarounds support demand for the workflow. The memo documents a one-site deployment offer but gives no buyer-confirmed commitment, paid deployment, or repeat use. The founders’ operations research experience and joint retailer routing project support their ability to build the optimizer. They report 38% lower delivery costs on retailer data, while dependable live handling of ambiguous constraints remains unverified. Pricing is inferred, and there is no evidence on implementation or ongoing support costs to underwrite the next site. The production and payment gaps keep evidence confidence low. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

OpEra could make replanning after every disruption viable at a site that currently absorbs smaller disruptions because manual replanning takes too much staff time. The company-reported 54% reduction in unfulfilled marketplace orders suggests that better planning could have material operational value. Under comparable model capability, a buyer would still need a maintained site model and working integrations. The result leaves the key threshold open because it does not establish that a buyer began replanning in situations it previously skipped or that AI lowered the fully loaded cost enough to sustain that change. The Pass gate still applies. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a live pilot show that a site routinely acts on feasible replans for disruptions it previously chose to absorb? A buyer-confirmed comparison showing that cost per usable replan, including integration, planner review, and error correction, fell below the site’s adoption threshold would change the action to Reach out.
