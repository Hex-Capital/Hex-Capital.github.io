# Helios Intelligence -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with consumers who want to track health changes between conventional blood tests, Helios has a plausible growth path through direct online kit sales and repeat testing. The buyer’s job plausibly recurs across markets, and Helios’s proposed at-home collection and digital reporting could transfer. Whether the same assay and fulfillment workflow can serve buyers across markets is unresolved, which limits portability credit. The memo records an October waitlist and describes planned online ordering and web and mobile results. The waitlist establishes an entry point, while purchase, kit activation, and delivery of first results remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Helios reports using repeat draws and linked health records to train its model. A growing record could give consumers a reason to retest as their results become more informative over time. That is a retention mechanism, with paid retesting, billing cadence, and room for existing customers to spend more still unverified. Helios Test’s paid launch status, realized sales, and commercial growth are unknown. Roon’s listed subscription applies to a separate product. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The relevant cost bridge is revenue per completed test less the kit, outbound and return shipping, lab processing, report delivery, attributable acquisition and support, and any recollection costs. These are scenario cost drivers inferred from the proposed workflow. Helios’s price and operating costs are undisclosed. The memo’s $50 starting price for a third-party profiling service gives context for one possible input, while Helios’s supplier terms and per-test contribution remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Sample handling could constrain growth even if consumers want to retest. The memo describes research in which delayed processing preserved agreement with venous draws for only some analytes, and Helios’s own assay validation remains undisclosed. If routine shipping requires market-specific processing or frequent recollection, the same kit could become expensive to serve or difficult to roll out. The structural downside gate clears on the available evidence because those requirements for Helios remain unestablished. (Key Risks)
