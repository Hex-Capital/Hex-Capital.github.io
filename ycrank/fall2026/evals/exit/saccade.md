# Saccade -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Saccade’s strongest path. A diagnostics company such as Roche could pay to own Saccade’s eye-tracking assessment and integrate it into an earlier referral pathway for patients who later qualify for blood testing. The memo documents a working headset attachment and Roche’s blood test for symptomatic patients. Saccade describes its attachment as patent-pending and its scoring model as proprietary. The buyer benefit is an inference. Ownership could be worth a premium if exclusive control of the model and rights to longitudinal data materially improves referral decisions and gives the buyer an advantage it cannot secure through a license. The memo supplies no measured buyer benefit, acquisition pricing, or evidence that the core IP and study data can transfer. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is a prespecified study readout showing that the eye-movement assessment adds useful information about biomarker status in asymptomatic participants beyond the buyer’s existing assessment pathway. Saccade reports clinic testing, an ongoing IRB-approved study, and clinical collaborations. It cites an enrollment figure of 2,000 and gives no completed count. The reported backing figures of $125,000 and $500,000 leave available capital uncertain, and the memo provides no study budget or burn rate. The hardware and reported study activity support a path to the readout, with funding and timing still open. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Illustrative proceeds.** With actual holder terms unavailable, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash target within eight years. The target requires $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. With an illustrative $5 million of net debt, the 3% holder needs about $88.3 million in enterprise value before any fees or senior claims. A $25 million sale in year eight yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million gross (5.7×), assuming pro rata distribution before investor taxes. At 2% ownership, even the $100 million sale yields $1.9 million (3.8×). These are hurdle sensitivities. The memo supplies no supported sale-price range or company-specific net debt, fees, or senior claim amounts. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Sponsor buyout and company-funded payouts lack evidence of recurring cash generation or distributable cash. A secondary lacks evidence of eligible share demand or terms, and an IPO lacks evidence of operating scale or readiness. If Acuity growth plateaus, buyer interest fails to emerge, public scale remains out of reach, and Saccade reinvests all available cash for eight years without an eligible share sale or payout, the holder realizes $0 and retains the shares. Wind-down proceeds would be limited to the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantifiable. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A diagnostics buyer could license a comparable assessment or wait for more evidence. ViewMind already combines VR, eye tracking, and machine learning and has funding to evaluate its system against amyloid and tau burden in people with mild cognitive impairment. Saccade’s terms acknowledge that its assessment remains unvalidated for individual risk prediction, and the treatment pathway described in the memo requires early symptomatic disease and amyloid confirmation. Those facts weaken the case that earlier detection would generate enough actionable referrals to justify buying Saccade. They leave the acquisition case unresolved. The memo establishes neither a structural block nor a supported sale-price ceiling that makes holder proceeds materially inadequate, so the Pass gate does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

After a prespecified study readout in asymptomatic participants, would a diagnostics buyer’s own economics support paying enough to own Saccade for the holder to clear the eight-year cash target? A buyer-backed valuation range tied to measured incremental referral value and transferable rights would move the action to Reach out if it clears the hurdle under credible shareholder terms. A supported upper bound materially below the hurdle would move it to Pass. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
