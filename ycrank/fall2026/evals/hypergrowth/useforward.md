# Forward -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path C — Marketplace density and repeat transactions.** Commercial real estate debt advisors repeatedly need to find lenders willing to finance a deal under current mandates. Forward packages the advisor’s documents, matches the deal with lenders, and coordinates submissions and terms. The lender pays 25 basis points when a loan funds. If a denser lender network makes suitable matches more likely, advisors have a reason to bring their next deal, and lenders gain a recurring source of deals within their mandates. *(The Idea, Defensibility)*

Capital Partners CEO Kevin Heisser describes introductions to unfamiliar lenders and help securing term sheets. Forward reports that its first Southern California advisor went exclusive after one transaction and that 100 U.S. funds signed fee agreements. At pre-seed, the testimonial and exclusivity are concrete signals of advisor value. Cold calls and LinkedIn outreach give Forward a way to recruit more participants, but repeat funded use through that channel remains unverified. The iPartners origination partnership offers a possible route to another market. Portability would depend on finding lenders with mandates for deals in that geography and reusing the workflow across local documentation and compliance requirements. *(The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)*

**Illustrative economics.** At the stated fee, a hypothetical US$10 million loan that funds and pays the fee would bring in US$25,000 before costs. Contribution depends on covering advisor and lender acquisition, mandate upkeep, deal review, and coordination across both funded and unfunded submissions. The reported processed and originated volumes leave the funded share unknown. The reported annualized and contracted revenue figures leave currency and cash collections unspecified. *(The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)*

### Countercase

Forward could scale like a labor-intensive brokerage. The rollout concentrated deal supply in a single exclusive advisor, and signed lender agreements leave open how many lenders will actively review and fund deals. If each new advisor requires bespoke onboarding and each submission requires substantial manual follow-up, operating effort could rise with deal volume while fees arrive only on funded loans. The memo establishes concentration and closing risk. The proportional-cost Pass gate remains unresolved because there is no rollout-level cost evidence. *(Market & Traction, Key Risks)*

### Decisive diligence question

Can Forward use the same onboarding and matching workflow in a second geography to turn independently acquired advisors into repeat sources of funded loans? A pilot showing repeat funded deals from multiple advisors, collected fees, and measured acquisition and handling costs across all submissions would answer it. Move to **Reach out** if the pilot shows repeat paid use and a credible path to lower cost per funded deal. Keep **Watch** while that evidence is pending. Move to **Pass** if the pilot shows that each rollout requires bespoke work and costs rise in proportion to volume without a credible way to improve them.
