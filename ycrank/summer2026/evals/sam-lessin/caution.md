# Caution -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first problem is not security; it is underwriting. The dossier gives me no proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is Caution’s first scarce check. For a Summer 2026 pre-seed company, that silence is ordinary, but it still leaves my primary gate unanswered. I cannot tell whether I am buying an unfashionable technical truth cheaply or paying YC-auction pricing for a confidential-computing market forecast to reach $153.8 billion.

The underlying mechanism is real. AWS Nitro can attest to a running binary without proving that reviewed source produced it; Caution connects source, build inputs, configuration, kernel, and toolchain to the live enclave through reproducible builds and independent hash verification. Production deployments reportedly span five workload categories, and TrustedStake publicly identified itself as an early user. That is materially stronger than followers, a hackathon bounty, or generic pilot language. It shows that teams will place actual workloads inside the workflow, although the dossier does not establish paid conversion, retention, or whether Caution owns the customer relationship rather than renting access through direct outreach.

The founders are the best evidence here. Anton Livaja shipped security infrastructure at Ledn and contributed to OpenZeppelin audits; Lance Vick worked across Pebble, Fitbit, BitGo, Unit 410, and Turnkey; together they operated Distrust before building Caution. They understand both the software operator who must deploy sensitive workloads and the security reviewer who must distrust what is running. This is an outsider-insider translation layer with operating receipts, not résumé cosplay.

The bull case is that those relationships already constitute a compact dark pool: Distrust’s security work creates access to precisely the crypto, lending, hardware, post-quantum, and private-AI teams that need source-to-runtime assurance. The production deployments could therefore be the beginning of a high-trust managed platform. But the evidence stops before the economic action I care about. No customer payment, contract value, renewal, gross margin, burn, or runway is disclosed, while the complete platform is available under AGPLv3 and AWS controls the present trust root. OPAQUE, Edgeless Systems, and enclaive are already financed around overlapping layers. Technical credibility does not by itself explain who pays Caution rather than self-hosting or buying from an incumbent.

The other missing piece is the venture moment. “Hire engineers and support more hardware” would be a capital factory, not a test. The round needs to flip one card—for example, whether independent verification converts a defined number of current production deployments into paid managed contracts by a fixed date. Without a metric and deadline, I cannot separate a useful security consultancy-plus-hosting business from a convex software company. My 36-month base case is a credible niche operator below $3 million ARR, constrained less by engineering than by monetizing an open stack inside cloud-controlled trust infrastructure.

I am passing today despite unusually strong founder-market fit. One verifiable condition flips me: a sub-$10 million post-money round explicitly funding conversion of the current production deployments into at least $500,000 ARR at disclosed positive gross margin within 12 months. That would simultaneously prove scarce pricing, a finite experiment, causal customer conversion, and a plausible path that does not require a Series A.
