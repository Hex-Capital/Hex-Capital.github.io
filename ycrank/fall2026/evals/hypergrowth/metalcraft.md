# Metalcraft -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path B, repeatable sales with account expansion, is the strongest candidate. Metalcraft has a public catalog of six tasksets with samples and a booking link for full access. Its published success rates and mean rewards describe model performance on those tasks. The memo records a recent pivot and gives no buyer activation, paid usage, or revenue evidence for the current product. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

The likely buyer is a life-science AI team that needs to evaluate model changes across releases. If those teams can find a relevant sample, license an existing taskset, and integrate it through a standard handoff, the same catalog could serve more buyers and existing accounts could add tasksets. In a second geography, that scenario assumes the same evaluation needs and task content, with any changes limited to licensing, procurement, or support. Contribution would depend on how much integration and support each new buyer needs and how much expert validation each added taskset requires. The catalog establishes a route to request access. Paid activation, expansion, and delivery effort remain untested in the memo. (*The Idea*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Model developers can build overlapping evaluations in-house, as OpenAI did. If an off-the-shelf score has limited value in live research, buyers may only pay for benchmarks tailored to their workflows. That would make expert design and validation costs rise with each account. The memo establishes in-house substitution as a credible alternative, while its concerns about workflow validity and bespoke delivery remain untested for Metalcraft. The structural Pass gate is unmet. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)
