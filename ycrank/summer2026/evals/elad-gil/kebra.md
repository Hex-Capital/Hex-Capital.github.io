# Kebra -- Elad Gil Evaluation

OpenAI’s Realtime API became generally available in August 2025, giving Kebra a credible single-miracle architecture but no visible revenue curve. Kebra is a technically executable workflow product whose lowest signal is whether anyone is actually buying: the 30-minute demo exists, yet the dossier contains no revenue, usage, retention, or named customer. That pairing usually means the product can work while the company still does not.

**Color Genomics** is the high rung on single-miracle architecture: workflow and data infrastructure built atop sequencing that already worked. Kebra shares that mechanism by using available audio models to structure field notes and trigger billing, parts, and warranty actions; Kebra does not require a speech-recognition breakthrough. Kebra diverges from Color because reliable execution spans ServiceTitan, QuickBooks, warranty portals, suppliers, SMS, and California Penal Code §632 consent—more operational dependencies, but still primarily one product-and-distribution miracle rather than new science.

**Lyft** exposed how apparent activity can become a false signal when structural economics remain inferior; Kebra is one rung lower because Kebra does not yet disclose even the activity. YC participation and a free walkthrough are not buying behavior, and zero active job postings reveal nothing about demand. For an AI product in 2026, no revenue, repeat usage, signed LOI, or named operator means I apply the 8/25 absent-evidence default: Kebra is not yet “just working.”

OpenAI’s August 2025 production release did reopen recording-driven workflows, but the catalyst compounds ambiguously over the next 18 months. Better realtime audio lowers Kebra’s cost and improves extraction, while simultaneously lowering the barrier for ServiceTitan—which reported $961.0M FY2026 revenue—to reproduce Kebra’s functionality. Kebra benefits from the catalyst only if integration speed and workflow depth compound faster than model access commoditizes capture; otherwise Kebra is skating for a puck already controlled by the system of record.

Luca Hadife built multimodal AI systems at Microsoft, and Mohamed Hosny taught signal processing and computer architecture at Berkeley, but Kebra’s founder evidence remains credential-heavy. Kebra would flip this dimension into the invest range if Hadife and Hosny could show weekly releases driven by technician feedback, particularly iterations such as the automated SMS request for a missing equipment nameplate. Without prior independently shipped products, repeat prototype usage, or documented between-meeting iteration, the Perplexity pattern is not yet established.

ServiceTitan, QuickBooks, warranty portals, and parts suppliers could make Kebra an index on field-service administrative volume in the way **Stripe** became an index on e-commerce. Kebra touches several mandatory post-job workflows, which is better than a thin transcription application. Kebra nevertheless sits beside—not beneath—the system of record: ServiceTitan can restrict access, change interfaces, or bundle overlapping AI automation, leaving Kebra fighting for distribution rather than automatically compounding with the category.

ServiceTitan’s $961.0M FY2026 revenue validates the budget but also shapes my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Kebra will have pivoted from broad “AI-native field service” into warranty-claims automation and reached $1M–$3M ARR. Kebra’s single-miracle product architecture should produce useful software, while weak initial distribution and incumbent convergence should force Kebra toward the narrow workflow where missing serial numbers, photographs, and documentation have directly measurable reimbursement value.

Rilla’s estimated $70M ARR is the strongest bull objection: recording field interactions can clearly become a large business, and Kebra’s Microsoft multimodal experience plus Berkeley signal-processing depth may let Kebra move from insight to action while Rilla and Siro remain coaching products. The objection stings because **Decagon** shows that workflow infrastructure in an open AI category can scale quickly. Kebra’s dossier still loses on actual buying behavior and incumbent control—ServiceTitan already spans the back office and technician workflow. Six consecutive months above 20% revenue growth, supported by at least five named field-service operators with repeat usage, would flip my verdict.
