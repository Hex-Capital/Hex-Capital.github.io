# ProvenMetal -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I start with the missing numbers: the dossier reports $500,000 of YC funding, but no post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is a genuinely scarce first check. A small round is not necessarily a cheap round. Without terms, I cannot tell whether I am financing an unfashionable belief or buying into a Demo Day auction, so the deal fails my first underwriting gate by omission rather than by demonstrated excess.

The stronger story is the founders. Johnny Doyle and Will Carkner previously shipped Syncra hardware into a 17-day office pilot that collected more than 20 million datapoints, and Carkner has built PCB-testing systems at Lumindt. They have therefore operated on both sides of the bridge: designing and deploying hardware, then confronting the testing and sourcing infrastructure that ProvenMetal proposes to reorganize. Five LOIs after more than 30 industry conversations are meaningful at pre-seed, but the June concept involved AI-enabled X-ray inspection while the August offering is turnkey PCBA through Network PCB. I cannot assume those customers followed the pivot.

The KiCad plugin is the most interesting asset because it moves ProvenMetal inside the engineer’s workflow, checks live inventory and lead times, and preserves sourcing records. That could become the proprietary BOM, revision, supplier-performance, and inspection dataset that compounds with every order. Today, however, there are no installation, repeat-use, purchase-conversion, or retention figures. Network PCB can accept the same customer directly and advertises a three-day turnkey service against ProvenMetal’s five-day fastest turn. Until the integration demonstrably causes purchases, ProvenMetal is a program-management layer sitting on a supplier that can disintermediate it.

This does fit one of my unstated patterns: software translating between hardware teams and a fragmented physical production system. It also triggers the corresponding red flag. The dashboard offers five LOIs, 501 LinkedIn followers, and a plugin release, but no controllable cash metric, gross margin, burn, or route to break-even without another financing. The custom-quote model may produce attractive contribution economics, but resemblance to a profitable broker is not evidence that this one is profitable.

The bull case stings: domestic supply-chain pressure is increasing, the team has unusually relevant operating receipts, and an embedded design tool could turn a commoditized fabrication network into proprietary workflow and data. But none of the known exceptions applies yet. There is no viral adoption, paid enterprise conversion, retention, or rejected acquisition offer strong enough to override missing price and capital-plan evidence. Worse, the round proceeds do not name one dated experiment—plugin-generated paid orders, repeat-board volume, or inspection yield—that would reveal a dramatically more valuable business.

I pass today. The single flip condition is verifiable: show that at least 20 KiCad-originated customers placed paid orders, with 50% ordering a second revision within six months, while disclosing positive contribution margin and the round’s post-money valuation. That would establish an owned workflow causing purchases, a finite venture moment, and a business that might survive if Series A never arrives.
