# Gutgutgoose -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032 and probiotics stop looking like standardized bottles. They become closed-loop microbial programs: measure the gut, model the ecological competition, introduce selected organisms, verify what persisted, and adapt. Gutgutgoose has assembled a small leak from that future—sequencing 300-plus strains, compounding 8–20, and retesting after 90 days. But the bridge is incomplete. The dossier does not show that stool detection proves mucosal colonization, that colonization improves a prespecified health outcome, or that the loop survives supplement-claims scrutiny.

The most persuasive artifact is not the 120-person waitlist. It is seven people paying after an endometriosis event, with five referrals, for something unusually expensive and intimate. Complete costs $549 per cycle; Starter costs $279. Customers must mail stool, ingest a personalized formulation, and submit another sample months later. That is real friction, money, and biological disclosure. The alpha cohort finished and the beta was nearly full, but no repeat-cycle behavior is reported. I see willingness to try the ritual, not yet evidence that the ritual works or becomes habitual.

Anis Mihrshahi built the mechanistic algorithm while studying AI and data science. Leon Mojarrabi brings four years of Go1 GTM experience and claims the far more valuable operating credential: building a pharmacy reportedly producing $4 million annually. Childhood friendship may make the pair resilient, and their skills are complementary. What is missing is the origin wound. I cannot trace this company to years of microbiome experiments, a pharmacy failure that demanded personalization, or dated pre-company artifacts proving either founder was independently obsessed before precision biotics became fashionable. The dossier also leaves ultimate decision authority and founder control unspecified; given two longtime co-founders, I would verify the tiebreaker before wiring money.

There may be a compounding machine here. The March 2026 patent application covers profiling, mechanistic modeling, optimization, and iterative verification; every successful renewal could generate a valuable baseline-intervention-outcome record. The bull case stings because Florē’s 40,000-plus formulations and Viome’s million-plus microbiomes are mostly evidence of scale, not necessarily evidence of experimentally verified engraftment. Gutgutgoose could own the feedback loop competitors neglected. Today, however, that is a prospective moat built from an initial cohort, while the company sells a consumer supplement rather than a demonstrated enabling layer used across clinics, therapeutics, or manufacturers.

The hidden system is also less satisfying than the biology. Gutgutgoose identifies a genuine product failure—fixed formulations ignore the recipient’s microbial environment—but it does not identify who captures an artificial rent, which institutional rule sustains it, or whose occupational agency is restored. Worse, “guaranteed to colonize” sits beside unpublished 16× colonization and 31× persistence claims, a published stool-versus-mucosa measurement problem, and no visible architecture for consent, genomic-style data custody, deletion, or secondary use. Sensitive biological data without explicit limiting architecture is a gate, not paperwork.

I am neutral and would not write the check today. My 36-month forecast is that Gutgutgoose shifts most revenue toward clinician and compounding-pharmacy partnerships and reaches roughly $1–3 million ARR by August 2029, rather than becoming a broad direct-to-consumer subscription brand. One result flips me: an independently run, preregistered study of at least 100 participants showing that its personalized strains colonize mucosa and improve a prespecified symptom endpoint against placebo.
