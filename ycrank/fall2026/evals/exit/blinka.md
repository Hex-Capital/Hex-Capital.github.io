# Blinka -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a business banking platform is Blinka’s strongest route to holder liquidity. Blinka’s disclosed product combines remote USD account opening with local Latin American payouts. Mercury illustrates the potential buyer rationale. If it chose to broaden its U.S. operations requirement, acquiring Blinka could give it a way to serve U.S.-incorporated businesses that operate abroad. Buyer interest and the economic benefit of acquiring that capability remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The memo reports more than 400 businesses onboarded in the first two months, and the CEO reported more than $18 million in cumulative payment volume by November 4. That early activity supports a concrete milestone: show that successive customer cohorts keep using local payouts and generate positive contribution after partner costs. The current product’s retention, margins, and capital available to reach that milestone are unknown. The disclosed funding predates the current banking product. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. For illustration, assume an all-cash sale within that window, $5 million in combined debt and deal adjustments, no senior claims, and eligibility to sell the full 3% stake. A modest $40 million enterprise value leaves $35 million for equity and pays the holder $1.05 million (2.1×). A strong $100 million enterprise value leaves $95 million and pays $2.85 million (5.7×). If the holder can sell only 75% of the stake in the strong case, cash falls to about $2.14 million (4.28×). These prices and terms are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no basis for underwriting either sale price or confirming the waterfall. In the prolonged-private case, continued reinvestment yields zero realized cash. The memo gives no evidence of a funded payout or an eligible secondary sale. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Slash and Jeeves already offer overlapping capabilities, and Blinka relies on outside providers for deposits, cards, and settlement. A buyer could build a similar offering with partners, while Mercury could retain its existing eligibility policy. A sale would also depend on keeping or replacing Blinka’s core providers, whose change-of-control terms are unknown. These facts weaken the acquisition case. The memo establishes neither a structural block to closing nor a credible proceeds ceiling below the holder’s objective, so neither Pass gate is triggered. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a strategic buyer value Blinka’s recurring Latin American payout business highly enough to leave at least $83.3 million in distributable equity value within eight years? Evidence of repeat payout use and durable contribution profit after partner costs, tied to a credible buyer’s valuation range, would move the action to Reach out if it supported clearing that hurdle.
