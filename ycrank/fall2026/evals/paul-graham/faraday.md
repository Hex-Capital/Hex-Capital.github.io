# Faraday -- Paul Graham Evaluation

What happened when Faraday’s founders first tried to connect a factory’s systems and something broke? That is the question I would start with. The dossier gives me relevant experience: Oscar Rangel built factory systems at Worthington Steel and shipped medical devices at Stryker; Datta Kaligotla worked in enterprise engineering and at Handshake AI. But it contains no specific obstacle, founder-built workaround, or elapsed time. I cannot infer resourcefulness from their employers. This leaves my most important criterion at its missing-evidence default.

The benchmark is the most interesting thing they have made public. Faraday reports 96.2 against GPT-5.5’s baseline of 71.7, with 2.6 times lower run cost, across 74 synthetic episodes and 33 scenario families ([benchmark](https://faradaycompute.com/faraday-industrial-benchmark)). Publishing an inspectable benchmark gives the technical claim some substance. The limitation is that Faraday constructed the test as well as the harness. Those numbers earn provisional technical credit; they do not establish that the system survives an unfamiliar manufacturer’s records, permissions, and exceptions. The decisive experiment would expose it to problems the founders did not choose.

The strongest argument for investing is that these founders may know exactly where that experiment belongs. Rangel’s factory experience and Kaligotla’s enterprise background could make them unusually effective at the ugly integration work. Mapping approvals, dependencies, and inconsistent records is a plausible place for schlep blindness. But the dossier never connects a particular frustration from their prior work to Faraday’s first product. Nor does it document a rejected hypothesis and a subsequent change. I give the team credit for relevant experience without turning it into an invented origin story or pivot history.

Who wants the crude version badly enough to keep using it? Faraday’s Fabtech conversations and walkthroughs using prospects’ records are sensible ways to discover that user ([company posts](https://www.linkedin.com/company/faraday-compute)). There is no documented repeat usage, payment, or named customer commitment yet. The subscription and pilot agreements describe how Faraday could charge, but reveal no conversion or delivery economics. Against that uncertainty, the scope matters: procurement alone supports a focused competitor in Didero, while Faraday proposes coordination from back office to shop floor. One indispensable workflow would teach the founders more than nominal coverage of the whole factory. Missing customer evidence receives the default score; the $500,000 financing and 57 followers add no demand evidence.

Faraday partly matches my pattern of technical founders building programmable infrastructure, but the accompanying proof spike remains confined to its own synthetic suite. I checked the exceptions that could justify investing despite the missing founder evidence: there is no demonstrated rapid correction around a working customer product, unmistakable user pull, or independently validated result strong enough to override the gate. Equally, I see no affirmative evidence of partner dependence, premature delegation, or founder rigidity; the undisclosed cofounder history is an unanswered question. I would pass today. My single flip condition is a documented deployment in which a named manufacturer’s integration blocker leads to a founder-built workaround within weeks, followed by weekly use of that workflow for eight consecutive weeks. That would show both how these founders behave under resistance and whether anyone needs what emerges.
