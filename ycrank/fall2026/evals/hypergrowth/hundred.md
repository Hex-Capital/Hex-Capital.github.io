# Hundred -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit at its initial factories, Hundred could bring the same benchtop station to electronics manufacturers in other markets for the recurring job of inspecting incoming parts. Hundred says its station controls lighting, scans whole shipping trays, and calibrates from one known-good part. Those features make the core inspection process plausibly reusable. How much work each factory would need to qualify its trays, part tolerances, and receiving workflow remains uncertain. The documented acquisition path runs from the YC launch and website inquiry form to direct work with the founders on defect priorities. Single-reference calibration could simplify setup, but the memo leaves independent purchase and production setup unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Receiving inspection recurs as shipments arrive, and Hundred’s documented per-part photo history could support continued use if factories rely on it for failure investigations. A station sale or lease is an inferred pricing scenario, and recurring billing and paid expansion within accounts remain unverified. The memo records a November 3 public launch by a three-person team. Funding and launch attention leave realized sales and commercial growth unknown. (Defensibility, The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under a per-station sale scenario, incremental contribution would be the sale price less hardware, fulfillment, attributable sales and setup labor, and support. A lease would also depend on how long each station earns revenue relative to its upfront cost and ongoing service costs. The memo gives no price or measured costs to establish positive contribution under either scenario. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Reflective finishes and acceptable variation from the reference part could force repeated tuning, while slow scanning or quarantine could create a receiving bottleneck. If those risks materialize, qualification and support could grow with each installation, and factories with installed cameras could favor a retrofit. The structural downside gate stays open because these are plausible failure modes and the memo leaves production accuracy, sustained throughput, and required site work unverified. (Key Risks, Defensibility)

### Decisive diligence question

Can independent factories in two markets buy and qualify the same station for paid production with minimal founder help and positive contribution per installation? Paid rollouts that document purchase and setup effort, accuracy and throughput at normal receiving volumes, and fully loaded incremental costs would answer it. Repeatable results with enough paid uptake to reach the investment score threshold at Medium confidence or higher would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that unavoidable site work makes faster growth uneconomic would move it to Pass.
