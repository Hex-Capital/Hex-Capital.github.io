# Most Robotic -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032 and I can imagine robots learning unfamiliar physical tasks quickly enough to become ordinary labor infrastructure. But Most Robotic has not told me which machines are working, for whom, or what sequence gets us there. “Research lab for physical AI” names a fashionable present category, not a future behavior. NVIDIA’s May 2026 Cosmos 3 release is evidence that development cycles may collapse from months to days; it is not evidence that this team controls what becomes scarce next.

The founders are the strongest part of the file. MIT computer science, robotics research, and collective experience at NASA JPL, SpaceX, and AWS are unusually relevant ingredients for two people at pre-seed. Friends since middle school may also carry real accumulated trust. But the dossier cannot attribute those credentials individually, name the accountable leader, or show a dated pre-company build, independently pursued experiment, or recruiting act. I see technical proximity, not yet the first-believer proof that distinguishes obsession from an excellent résumé.

The opposing case genuinely stings: this is exactly the kind of team that may be hiding because the artifact matters more than the launch. **CosmicBrain AI** was also pre-revenue physical AI; the shared mechanism is technically serious founders attacking the layer beneath future robotic labor. The material difference is that CosmicBrain had a functioning prototype, signed industrial proofs of concept, and a founder’s documented lifelong robotics record. Most Robotic currently supplies none of those overrides.

I also cannot find the irritating system underneath the research. No customer, task, fee, delay, labor constraint, or incumbent workflow is identified, so I cannot tell whose agency returns if the technology succeeds. There is no working artifact, benchmark, deployment, dataset, or repeated user behavior. With no public product mechanism or monetization path, even a mundane experiment that reveals demand is absent.

The competitive geometry makes that silence expensive. Skild AI has raised $2.215 billion and reportedly produced roughly $30 million in 2025 live revenue; FieldAI has raised $405 million and reports deployments across hundreds of industrial environments; Genesis AI has disclosed its model, data engine, and robotic hand. Meanwhile, NVIDIA is opening world models and action-generation components. Most Robotic may sit beneath today’s hype, but it has not shown a controlled bottleneck—data, hardware, embodiment, or distribution—that prevents it from being squeezed between open infrastructure and vastly funded laboratories.

My 36-month base case is a pivot into one narrow industrial robotics task rather than survival as a general physical-AI research lab. I pass today. The single condition that flips me is a reproducible demonstration on a named physical task, used repeatedly by at least one external industrial customer, with evidence that the resulting proprietary interaction data improves performance beyond NVIDIA’s open stack. That one artifact would simultaneously reveal the future leak, the founders’ operating obsession, and the enabling asset they actually own.
