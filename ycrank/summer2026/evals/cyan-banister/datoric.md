# Datoric -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Nikhil Reddy automated 15,000 hours of annotation work by bypassing QA and authentication controls before building Datoric. That makes Datoric founder-authentic in a way most AI-data businesses are not: Reddy touched the fraud surface from inside it. Datoric’s weakest axis is the opposite one—nearly seven figures of 30-day revenue in a market occupied by Scale AI, Turing, Sama, and Toloka is commercially remarkable, but neither magically weird nor hidden. Datoric is an insider’s operational insight entering an increasingly legible category.

Crusoe is the high rung because Chase Lochmiller possessed the social license to walk onto oil fields before asking investors to understand stranded energy. Datoric shares that lived mechanism: Reddy did not discover annotation integrity in a market report; he personally found and exploited the controls Datoric now promises to secure. That matches my unstated preference for environmental evidence the founder did not manufacture for a pitch. Datoric diverges from Crusoe because Reddy’s contact was adversarial platform participation, not years embedded among the customers buying the product, and the March 2026 Arzule launch makes the depth of commitment harder to distinguish from exceptional pivot speed.

HQ Trivia is the low rung on durable non-consensus insight: an obvious, intoxicating artifact can override another broken dimension without revealing a hidden market truth. Datoric sits above that rung because sample-level consent, provenance, residential-video privacy, and $6-per-approved-hour contributor operations are ugly problems fashionable AI founders prefer not to touch. Datoric still cannot claim the HQ Trivia known exception—request-based Hugging Face access offers no explosive consumer experience capable of overriding the consensus problem. Nearly seven figures in one month and 300,000 reported active contributors make Datoric visible enough that I would expect price discovery, not investor disbelief.

Nikhil Reddy is the named CEO and Jeffrey Lin the named CTO, a division that should compound over the next 18 months as Datoric adds customers, contributors, and fraud controls. Datoric has already survived one product change together after building Arzule, suggesting the titles reflect actual working history rather than Demo Day choreography. Datoric has disclosed no founder-voting arrangement, vesting protocol, or explicit tiebreaker, however; “CEO” is evidence, but it is not the answer to what happens when Reddy and Lin disagree about accepting a concentrated seven-figure contract.

Egocentric recordings inside contributors’ homes force Datoric to manage consent, PII redaction, licensing, and permitted-use records at the sample level. Datoric would move into my frontier-regulated tier if those rights created exclusive collection permission, proprietary capture hardware, or a compliance approval competitors could not reproduce. The dossier instead calls isolation, provenance, and consent replicable operating processes, while Scale AI and Toloka already possess funded contributor networks. Datoric is unsexy and regulation-adjacent, but Datoric is not yet an Anduril-shaped technical or regulatory moat.

Flock Safety could be encountered through a Wi-Fi network and then photographed operating in a neighborhood; the product announced itself in the physical world. Datoric’s 250,000 computer-use traces, 30-plus voice languages, and 80 egocentric-video task categories are real artifacts made by real people, which gives Datoric more texture than an API wrapper. Datoric remains one layer removed from the experience: access is request-based, buyers receive datasets, and a normal person cannot install or observe the provenance machinery within 48 hours. I can touch the ingredients, but not yet the magic.

Nearly seven figures of current-product revenue in 30 days supports a specific forecast: by July 2029, Scale AI will acquire Datoric for $250–500 million after Datoric reaches $25–40 million in annualized revenue, rather than Datoric becoming the independent category leader. Datoric’s founder insight should produce better fraud controls and rights-clean multimodal supply; Datoric’s low non-consensus placement and replicable operating moat should make that supply strategically valuable to an incumbent before it becomes structurally defensible on its own.

Scale AI’s $1.5 billion annualized run rate makes the bear mechanism sting: Datoric may be temporary labor arbitrage wearing a provenance necklace, with 300,000 contributors multiplying fraud and privacy exposure faster than reliability data compounds. Datoric’s March 2026 pivot, undisclosed customer names, unknown concentration, and company-reported revenue activate my red flags around cadence and replaceability. The evidence that narrowly wins is operational rather than narrative—nearly seven figures from Datoric’s current product, 250,000 computer-use traces, and a founder who personally understands how annotation controls fail. If Datoric cannot show at least two named customers renewing 12-month engagements by July 2027, I flip from Invest to Pass.
