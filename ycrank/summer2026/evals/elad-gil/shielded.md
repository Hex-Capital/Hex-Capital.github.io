# Shielded -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Shielded’s claimed 48-hour ERP deployment makes Shielded a single-miracle workflow bet whose weakest signal is whether anyone is actually buying repeatedly. The technical premise is credible: combine ERP records, commodity prices, and regulatory updates into margin alerts and response options. The commercial evidence is not yet “just working.” Three unnamed customer examples, including a restaurant chain auditing 200 SKUs, establish use-case reality but disclose neither revenue nor repeat usage.

**Color Genomics** is the high rung on single-miracle architecture: workflow and data infrastructure built on commodity sequencing, without requiring new science. Shielded shares that load-bearing mechanism because Shielded relies on existing ERP APIs, public tariff sources, and established commodity data rather than new technical breakthroughs. Shielded diverges from Color because incorrect supplier origins or regulatory interpretations can corrupt the output, while no integration-depth, proprietary-data, retention, or security evidence shows that Shielded has converted feasible software into a durable system of record.

**Lyft’s** low-rung failure mechanism was category adoption without company-specific evidence that the economics would accrue to the number-two platform. Shielded shows the analogous revenue problem: supply-chain volatility is real, but Shielded reports no revenue, growth rate, contract value, retention, or named customer. The three use cases and Susa Ventures backing move Shielded above a pure narrative, but not into a Decagon- or Harvey-like curve; no revenue or usage curve evidence means I apply the absent-evidence default.

Repeated 2026 tariff actions give Shielded a real, recent catalyst rather than a generic $5.85 billion TAM slide. Over the next 18 months, that catalyst can compound for Shielded because every rate change creates another finance workflow, another urgency event, and another opportunity to replace spreadsheet analysis. The limitation is durability: tariffs may accelerate purchasing now, but Interos already sells tariff-cost and product-revenue impact analysis, so Shielded has evidence of a reopened buying window without evidence that the window uniquely belongs to Shielded.

James Simon’s MIT computer-science-and-economics training, Balyasny commodities experience, and interviews with hundreds of finance and procurement teams establish founder-market fit, but they do not establish a Perplexity-style shipping cadence. Shielded’s customized demos and live deployments suggest Simon can build, yet Shielded would move into the invest tier on this dimension only if successive customer deployments showed named product iterations driven by user feedback—for example, three documented releases across tariff classification, commodity hedging, and procurement actions within one quarter. Credentials describe capacity; artifacts describe velocity.

Interos’s $192.35 million of funding and $39.7 million of 2024 revenue show why Shielded is not yet an index on supply-chain volatility. **Stripe** became an index on e-commerce because every new internet business could enter through the same developer rail; Shielded currently requires founder-led demos, prospect-specific data, negotiated onboarding, and even a $1,000 referral payment. Shielded could become the margin-intelligence layer every ERP touches, but the dossier currently describes a feature wedge competing against Interos, Altana, Prewave, and Everstream—not a distribution position that automatically compounds with the category.

The $5.85 billion market growing at 14.21% supports a narrow forecast: by July 2029, Shielded will have focused on tariff and commodity-margin intelligence for mid-market manufacturers and reached roughly $1 million to $3 million ARR, rather than becoming a broad supply-chain-risk platform. Shielded can ship the product because the single miracle is already available, but Shielded will be pushed toward a narrower wedge by incumbent data graphs, security requirements, and integration-heavy sales. That produces a useful vertical workflow business before it produces an index on global supply chains.

Susa Ventures backing, three customer use cases, 48-hour deployment, and Simon’s commodities background form the strongest bull case: Shielded may be a **Color Genomics**-style known exception, using workflow software to monetize a hard external-data layer while better-funded competitors remain slow and services-heavy. The bull mechanism stings because repeated tariffs could make Shielded “just work” before incumbents can repackage their platforms. The dossier still loses on the signal I will not waive: unnamed examples without revenue or repeat usage can be pilot tourism, and Interos already claims the core impact workflow. I would flip to Invest if Shielded verifies three named paying enterprises, each renewing or expanding, alongside greater than 20% month-over-month revenue growth for three consecutive months.
