# Sintropix -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B. Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** Sintropix’s initial buyer is the finance lead at a multi-entity company that reconciles local books for each group close. The founders reported 10 ERP customers across Chile, Mexico, Brazil, and the U.S. That gives the recurring job some geographic breadth, while willingness to pay and filing coverage by market remain unclear. The CEO reported that managed-service customers moved their close from day 20 to day 1. That is an early signal of value in the workflow, with ERP-only results and buyer commitments still unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The memo documents direct demos and a public launch as acquisition routes. The founders reported four referrals for the earlier manual service. The route to the next thousand buyers would be a repeatable demo-to-paid-close process that reuses data integrations and ledger setup, then grows monthly fees as customers add entities and integrations. That is an inference from the product design and reported pricing. The memo provides no ERP conversion records, repeat-close cohorts, or realized account expansion. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The strongest countercase is that each new account brings a migration and substantial human review, while each new country adds integration work and ongoing filing maintenance. In a Mexico rollout from Chile, the scenario assumes the shared ledger and consolidation workflow carry over. Local data connections and filing rules would still need validation. The monthly fee would need to cover sales and migration effort, given the reported absence of an implementation charge, plus ongoing exception review, support, and local filing maintenance. Managed services were reported available in Chile and in beta in Mexico, and Brazil’s changing electronic-document requirements show a concrete source of recurring localization work. The memo gives no measured rollout costs or review burden, so the structural Pass gate remains untriggered. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid cohort of independent multi-entity customers in Mexico move from direct demo through onboarding, recurring closes, and its first applicable filing cycle using the same core ERP workflow as in Chile? Move to **Reach out** if invoices, usage records, and logged sales, migration, review, and support effort show reusable onboarding and positive contribution after accounting for country-specific filing maintenance. Move to **Pass** if the rollout shows that each added account or entity requires proportional bespoke work with no supported way to reduce it.
