# Audun -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: conversational AI now lets creditors automate negotiation across voice and messaging, but Audun becomes large only if it produces materially higher net recoveries at lower handling cost without creating regulatory or reputational liabilities. The dossier never states that governing variable, its required threshold, or present evidence against it. A $5.98 billion software market growing 9.72% is context, not a causal thesis.

The technology shift is real but already widely available. Commercial agents can respond in under a second and preserve context across voice, text, and WhatsApp. Audun applies that capability to reminders, negotiation, payment plans, payouts, interaction history, and human escalation. This could change service economics, particularly if humans handle only disputes and vulnerable debtors. But no recovery-rate improvement, cost-per-account reduction, gross margin, or human-escalation rate is disclosed. AgentCollect, Altur, and Domu provide overlapping automation, so the enabling technology expands adoption without yet explaining why Audun captures the value.

The strongest part of the company is its attempt to own the collection workflow rather than package model output. Payment scheduling, case history, debtor-channel continuity, and escalation are meaningful operational state. Better models should improve negotiation inside that system. Yet the debtor portal is still “coming soon,” and there is no evidence of creditor-system integrations, deployed cases, outcome feedback, or permissions that would make the workflow difficult to replace. The inferred data moat is especially weak: with no disclosed case volume, a funded competitor could reproduce the proposed timing-and-outcome dataset simply by winning distribution first.

There is also no product-market pull to arbitrate among these similar systems. Audun was incorporated in June 2026, offers walkthroughs, and joined YC, but the dossier identifies no creditor customer, pilot usage, LOI, revenue, or capacity constraint. Pre-seed status explains the absence; it does not convert absence into evidence. My base case is that by August 2029 Audun remains below €1 million ARR and has shifted from autonomous third-party collection toward workflow software for already-licensed Nordic agencies.

The bull case is credible enough to matter. A four-person team combining Midjourney AI experience, data science, McKinsey operating backgrounds, and prior work around Aviant could execute enterprise sales and model-driven operations. Norway’s May 2026 Debt Collection Act may also create a replacement cycle, while human escalation could make automation compatible with debtor protections. But this is not a known-exception case: the team has not shown frontier research comparable to a scarce model laboratory, and the category does not require a capital-intensive technical breakthrough. Licensing uncertainty and GDPR restrictions instead increase the number of things that must work simultaneously.

I would pass today. Audun exhibits the unstated pattern of entering a technology cluster early, but not the stronger pattern of owning a differentiated layer after that cluster begins crystallizing. The verifiable flip condition is a six-month cohort across at least three licensed creditors showing a 20% or greater increase in net recoveries after Audun’s fees and human-handling costs, without increased upheld complaints. That result would identify the controlling variable, demonstrate pull, and show that the regulatory workflow is an asset rather than overhead.
