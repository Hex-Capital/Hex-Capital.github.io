# Parasma -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The strongest fact is that Sean Cole did not begin with a market diagram. He wrote the code connecting roughly 200,000 cultured neurons to Doom, closed the stimulation-and-feedback loop, and produced a hybrid system that reportedly outperformed the learning algorithm alone ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-human-neurons-on-a-chip-learned-to-play-doom/), Mar 2026). That is unusually concrete pre-company proof. It makes Cole look like a fierce nerd, not someone waiting for funding or institutional permission to begin.

But the dossier does not reveal the obstacle sequence I care about most. Parasma names noise, non-stationarity, limited observability, stimulation policy, and cell degradation as blockers, yet it does not say which one broke the experiment, what Cole built around it, or how quickly the result changed ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/parasma/jobs/Vg7GVVl-founding-scientist), Aug 2026; [Parasma research note](https://parasma.com/research/consciousness-and-suffering), Jun 2026). Advertising for an 11-plus-year founding scientist at a $1 million salary may be a clever way to recruit the missing half of the company. It may also mean the hardest remaining problems have been converted into a hiring requirement. The public evidence cannot distinguish those stories, so I cannot infer resourcefulness from the job description.

The Doom experiment proves that neurons can participate in a learning loop. It does not yet prove Parasma’s larger claim that they are useful AI compute. There is no disclosed task accuracy, latency, energy, cost, or substrate-lifetime benchmark for token prediction. The source code being public under GPL-3.0 is good for attracting technical scrutiny, but it means the moat cannot merely be the implementation ([GitHub](https://github.com/SeanCole02/doom-neuron), Aug 2026). It must become accumulated experimental knowledge: stimulation policies, response data, or reproducible advantages that competitors cannot obtain as quickly.

Demand is the missing half of the proof. The repository’s 255 stars and 29 forks show curiosity, while coverage in Scientific American and The Guardian shows that the demonstration is legible to outsiders ([GitHub](https://github.com/SeanCole02/doom-neuron), Aug 2026; [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/games/2026/mar/16/petri-dish-brain-cells-playing-doom-cortical-labs), Mar 2026). Neither tells me who needs the crude product now. Parasma has no published pricing, revenue, recurring experiment usage, or commercial deployment. Its plausible distribution route depends partly on Cortical Labs, which already sells biological-computer hardware and API access. “Partner” is not yet a growth mechanism, especially when that partner could absorb the training layer itself.

The bull case is real. Cole encountered this substrate through spiking-neural-network research, built the Doom interface himself, and obtained a result interesting enough to attract researchers and developers before Parasma existed. This resembles the mechanism behind the Bindwell exception: a technically ambitious biological claim made credible by an artifact built before permission. The material difference is that Bindwell supplied a precise benchmark and laboratory validation, while Parasma has not disclosed a benchmark connecting its neurons to a commercially relevant advantage. Meanwhile, Cortical Labs, The Biological Computing Company, and Intactis Bio have already raised capital around hardware, adapters, or cloud access, so merely demonstrating biological computation will not establish Parasma’s wedge ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/naveenrao/2026/07/16/neurons-playing-tetris-intactis-bio--joins-the-biocomputing-industry/), Jul 2026; [Primary Venture Partners](https://www.primary.vc/articles/the-biological-computing-company-raises-25m-seed), Feb 2026).

My 36-month base case is that Parasma pivots from general AI compute to biological-computing research tooling and remains below $1 million ARR. Researchers can value programmable access long before production AI developers can justify biological infrastructure. I would not write the check today, because the operational gate fails on documented founder response and user pull despite the excellent technical artifact. One condition would flip me: three independent external teams using Parasma weekly for eight consecutive weeks while reproducing a preregistered benchmark in which the neuronal loop materially beats an algorithm-only control on performance or energy.
