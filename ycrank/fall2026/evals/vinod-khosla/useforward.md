# Forward -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Forward reports $300M+ of processed deals. How much capital actually reached borrowers, and what did it cost to get there? That distinction determines whether this becomes financial infrastructure or a more efficient intermediary. Structuring borrower information and matching lender mandates could reduce wasted analyst work and capital-search friction. But processed volume supplies neither a baseline cost nor a measured reduction. The dossier does not yet establish an orders-of-magnitude consequence. I would pass with my own money.

The strongest objection is that these founders are already doing something customers value. Investor-reported $180K annualised revenue within two months, founder-reported onboarding of 50+ US funds, and announced relationships with Fidelity Bank, Hudson Cove, and iPartners are meaningful pre-seed signals. The currency and revenue composition remain unspecified, but that does not erase the commercial evidence. My actual investment pattern includes execution-heavy financial businesses and marketplaces; scientific novelty is not an absolute requirement. The exception requires a credible mechanism by which a narrow entry point becomes much larger infrastructure. Forward has demonstrated access to participants. It has not established that completed transactions improve its matching advantage or make subsequent transactions substantially cheaper.

The team gives that possibility some credibility. Yoon worked in private credit at Kain Lawyers. Janjis worked on $1B+ in credit diligence at Rixon Capital and Longreach Alternatives. Luo previously served as an AI company’s CTO. Bringing credit experience and technical leadership together earns limited affirmative credit for complementary team construction. Yoon and Janjis also previously operated a tutoring business together, providing evidence of a working relationship. None of this establishes rapid belief revision or unusually difficult recruiting. I would not infer those qualities from educational credentials, dropouts, or outbound activity.

The technical question remains unanswered. Forward structures deals and matches mandates, while Finitive already operates a private credit marketplace and F2 and Heron automate adjacent financial workflows. There is no measured benchmark showing that Forward overcomes a binding incumbent constraint by 5–10x. That earns the absent-evidence default, rather than a finding that the product is merely a wrapper. Likewise, $300M processed does not reveal a decisive experiment against the largest kill risk: whether matching produces suitable, funded transactions with sufficiently little human intervention. No disclosed test specifies a threshold, cost, or stop condition. The licensing restrictions in Forward’s terms make the permitted transaction workflow part of that experiment; they do not establish misconduct.

I also cannot calculate economic parity from annualised revenue without pricing, servicing labor, conversion rates, or transaction costs. There is no evidence of permanent subsidy dependence, so this receives the missing-evidence default. My concern is that Forward could grow revenue through relationship work while leaving the underlying cost curve largely intact. The dossier does not show the advertising, exit-first, or demonstrated resistance-to-evidence red flags that would justify an active-mismatch verdict. One verifiable result would reopen my decision: a prospectively tracked, independently audited comparison showing at least 10x lower fully loaded origination cost per funded loan than the existing manual workflow across comparable transactions, including human intervention and compliance costs. That would turn the central claim into evidence.
