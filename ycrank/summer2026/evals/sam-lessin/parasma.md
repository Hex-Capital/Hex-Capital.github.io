# Parasma -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I need—post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and check size—are absent. I will not translate YC admission into scarcity, nor assume this is an auction. But financing silence earns only the low prior. More troubling, the only visible capital action is advertising a Founding Scientist role at a $1 million salary. That names an expense, not a venture moment: no dated experiment, target accuracy, energy threshold, substrate lifetime, or result that would dramatically reprice the company.

Sean Cole is the reason not to dismiss Parasma as science fiction. He built the code connecting approximately 200,000 cultured neurons to Doom, and Scientific American reported that the hybrid system outperformed the learning algorithm alone. His MSc work in diffusion and spiking neural networks plus direct access to Cortical Labs puts him on both sides of the interface: he understands the software abstraction and has operated the biological substrate. This is shipped technical work, not résumé theater.

The translation layer is real, but ownership is not. Noise, non-stationarity, stimulation policy, observability, and feedback design could generate a proprietary response dataset over repeated experiments. Today, however, no such dataset or patent is disclosed, the Doom implementation is GPL-3.0, and Cortical Labs, The Biological Computing Company, and Intactis Bio control funded hardware or cloud platforms. Parasma may be teaching someone else’s substrate how to learn while the substrate owner captures the customer and the data. That is a dangerous place in the stack.

The 255 GitHub stars, 29 forks, approximately 3,000 X followers, and coverage in The Guardian and Scientific American show curiosity, not economic behavior. There is no purchase, pilot, paid access, repeat prototype usage, or named customer. This is precisely where technical attention becomes metric theater if management cannot identify one controllable cash driver. The dossier also supplies no pricing, revenue, gross margin, burn, or route to profitability; deleting the next financing from the model leaves a $1 million hire and no business.

The strongest bull case is that the Doom experiment is unusually concrete pre-seed technical proof in a category where hardware access only recently became available. A solo founder who has already made living neurons outperform a software-only learner could accumulate the stimulation policies and biological-response data that later become the defensible product. But the known-exception bar requires hard external validation—enterprise demand, retained use, or economically meaningful conversion—not press, repository engagement, or the possibility of future proprietary data. Parasma has not crossed it.

My 36-month base case is that Parasma pivots from general AI compute to stimulation-policy tooling for biological-computing labs and remains below $1 million ARR. I am passing because the highest-quality evidence proves Cole can build the bridge, while the weakest evidence concerns whether anyone will pay to cross it. One result flips me: a named hardware or pharmaceutical customer paying for repeated use of Parasma’s training layer under terms that leave Parasma owning the resulting response data.
