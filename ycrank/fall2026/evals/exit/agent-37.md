# Agent 37 -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A cloud or agent platform could buy Agent 37 to add persistent, per-customer deployments and bring embedded reseller workloads onto its platform. The Cloud launch and published usage pricing are observable. Boon reported moving its agent infrastructure onto Agent 37, integrating provisioning and scheduled runs into its own app, and cutting monthly cost per agent from $85 to $4.94. That supports the customer value of the service. The ownership case is an inference that buying the provisioning stack and reseller relationships could bring those workloads onto a buyer’s platform faster and retain them more reliably than building or contracting for the same capability. The memo leaves that advantage unquantified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

ETEC+ reported launching in six days and moving more than 30 existing clients to its branded service. That supports a test of whether paid reseller deployments can repeat and retain at positive margins. Agent 37 claims it powers agents for more than 250 startups, and the founder reports average Cloud spending of $0.37 per sandbox per month. Paid retention, margins, and the capital and runway needed to reach the milestone remain unverified. Funding figures conflict. IP ownership, contract assignability, and holder rights are undocumented. The memo supplies no independently supported fallback that would pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Defensibility)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** With no applicable check terms supplied, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross holder cash, or 5×. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity consideration at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, fees, and other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. They assume a simple pro rata waterfall with no preference deductions. All consideration is cash at closing, with $0 each in stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation. Prices and timing are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no usable acquisition valuation evidence.

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Holder cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $40M → $40M | $1.2M | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100M → $100M | $3.0M | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No sale or distribution | $0, shares still held | 0× realized |

In the prolonged-private case, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. Even the modeled strong sale falls short at 2% ownership, yielding $2 million, or 4×. The decisive uncertainty is whether ownership could justify a price that clears the hurdle after dilution and any senior claims.

### Countercase

Boon obtained its reported savings as a customer, giving a prospective buyer a reason to consider a commercial agreement. AWS already offers isolated agent sessions, and Agent 37 attributes its low costs to rented servers, shared host resources, and workload optimization. Those facts make building or partnering credible substitutes. They leave open whether either substitute can match the full persistent reseller workflow at comparable cost. The memo therefore supports Watch. It establishes neither a structural price cap nor that a prolonged-private outcome with no route to cash is probable. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid, retained reseller cohort show enough incremental value from owning Agent 37’s stack and customer relationships, compared with building or licensing, to support roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity consideration at 3% ownership? Move to Reach out if production results on margins, retention, and migration effort support buyer economics near or above that hurdle. Move to Pass if a comparable build or license delivers the same benefit at a cost that caps the acquisition price materially below it.
