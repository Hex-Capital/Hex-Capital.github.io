# COACH -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032 and COACH has not shown me a different sales institution; it has shown me a much more observable one. Managers no longer drive 100 kilometers for a single ride-along because every field conversation can become coaching data. That is useful, but the causal chain stops there. The dossier attributes the opening to better models and AI tooling, while Rilla and Siro already provide substantially the same capture-and-coaching workflow. I see a present market working unusually well, not yet a leak from a future six years ahead.

The strongest evidence is behavioral. Reps have submitted 74,473 meetings across 200 companies—costly participation involving time, reputation, and sensitive customer conversations. COACH reports thousands of reps, five large French customers, 100% pilot conversion, zero churn, and €1 million ARR within eight months. Even discounting the unsupported 27%–32% conversion-lift claims, customers are paying and repeatedly putting the product inside real meetings. The 50-plus Google Play downloads look strange beside those figures, but an enterprise deployment model could explain that mismatch; the revenue and meeting corpus are far more informative.

The founders earned their way into this problem. Mathieu and Thomas previously operated a 44-rep field-sales company that generated €3.5 million in 14 months, so the product specification comes from living with the ride-along bottleneck. Mathieu had already built and closed Lucy, Nicolas brought a decade of software experience, and the brothers recruited their best friend as CTO. That gives me Tizz and credible Biz. It is not yet evidence of a long pre-market obsession, however, and the family-and-friend structure makes governance worth inspecting: Mathieu is identified as CEO, but I need to verify control, equity, and the contractual tiebreaker rather than infer accountability from titles.

The broken mechanism is concrete but only partially decoded. Physical observation is scarce and expensive, leaving managers blind to most conversations; COACH turns that intermittent supervisory labor into continuous analysis and gives each rep one skill to improve. Yet the dossier does not quantify the managerial rent, show who captures it today, or demonstrate that reps gain occupational control rather than merely becoming more observable to management. The same corpus that makes the product useful also activates my privacy gate. California requires all-party consent, and the policy promises explicit consent and deletion after withdrawal, but a policy is not proof that consent is obtained clearly in every noisy doorstep or showroom interaction.

Here is the bull case that stings: a four-person team with direct operating experience reached meaningful revenue before YC, converted pilots, and accumulated a corpus that could become a customer-specific sales brain. Perhaps speed, workflow discipline, and proprietary outcome labels matter more than owning the underlying model. But today Anthropic handles both AI and transcription, no validation method is public, and two heavily financed competitors already occupy the same layer. This fits my unstated preference for an operating wound becoming software, but contradicts the other pattern I seek: control of the concealed infrastructure beneath the wave. COACH currently rents that infrastructure.

I am neutral, not because the company lacks momentum, but because the momentum proves a strong application before it proves a defining asset. The known exceptions do not rescue the gap: there is no extraordinary new social ritual, lifelong technical obsession, or controlled enabling constraint that overrides the privacy and commoditization risks. One verifiable condition flips me to invest: within twelve months, COACH must publish cohort-level evidence that its proprietary meeting-and-outcome data produces materially better rep improvement than Rilla or Siro while documenting auditable, meeting-level consent and deletion enforcement.
