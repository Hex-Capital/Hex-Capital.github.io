# Native -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Reach out  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

The memo documents a recurring job for small businesses without a marketing team. Native reads a business’s website, uses brand context to rank ideas, generates creative, and publishes organic posts and Meta ads. Assuming strong fit with those buyers, the same workflow could serve businesses across many markets, with language, creative conventions, and channel mix shaping where it works best. Native’s claim of paying customers in more than 20 countries corroborates that portability. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The free website-to-post preview lets owners reach first value with little help, and the CEO reports scaling acquisition through paid ads. The memo leaves open whether a typical owner can complete checkout, connect publishing accounts, and go live independently. Native launched publicly on March 16, 2026. By November, the CEO reported more than $2 million in ARR and about 2,200 customers. Reaching that scale in roughly eight months with six to seven people is strong early commercial evidence, although the revenue trajectory rests on a company-reported milestone. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

Monthly subscriptions fit the recurring publishing job. The $79 single-brand plan gives typical small businesses limited room to expand spend, while the $249 multi-brand plan and $499 agency plan create expansion paths for customers who add brands or clients. That supports recurring revenue, with limited evidence that upgrades can offset churn across the starting customer base. At the $79 list price, incremental monthly contribution depends on what remains after generation, publishing infrastructure, and attributable onboarding and support costs. The CEO’s reported reduction in paid acquisition cost from about NOK 1,800 to NOK 800 per customer gives Native a concrete lever for improving payback. Actual payback remains uncertain without cohort revenue and serving costs. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Native could acquire single-brand businesses cheaply enough to grow quickly at first, then lose them before recovering acquisition and serving costs. Its research measures reach and engagement, leaving sales conversion lift unmeasured. Meta is the current paid publishing channel and offers its own AI creative tools, which could weaken the reason to keep paying for Native. The structural downside gate clears because the memo documents a reusable preview and publishing workflow and reports falling acquisition costs. It leaves actual cohort payback unresolved. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Does a representative cohort of small businesses acquired through paid ads recover its acquisition cost from subscription contribution within its observed paid lifetime? Monthly cohort billings after churn and downgrades, less attributable generation, infrastructure, onboarding, and support costs, would answer it. Payback across representative cohorts would sustain Reach out. Cohorts that repeatedly churn before payback would move the action to Watch.
