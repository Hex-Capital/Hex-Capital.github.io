# Daqstra -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Darell Chua’s work on distributed data acquisition for cryogenic rocket testing makes Daqstra a high-shipping, low-catalyst infrastructure bet. Daqstra has already operated live LOX/IPA static-fire campaigns and connected NI, Siemens, Modbus, OPC-UA, and custom systems; those are real artifacts, not founder theater. The problem is timing: Daqstra identifies costly fragmentation, but not a technological or regulatory change in the last 24 months that suddenly makes vendor-independent test orchestration possible. Daqstra is skating well; I am less certain the puck has moved.

**Perplexity** is the high rung on founder shipping because Aravind repeatedly turned conversations into working product within a week. Daqstra approaches that rung through Chua and Anchit Kumar’s SAMDAS work, Kumar’s 10-plus Mojave campaigns, and Daqstra’s deployments across rocket-engine stands and active aerospace programs. Daqstra diverges because the dossier shows domain artifacts, not an observed cadence of shipping between customer or investor conversations; no public repositories were found, and the unexplained contraction from four identified co-founders in 2024–2025 to two on the YC roster also deserves diligence on ownership and decision authority.

**Lyft** is the low rung on market re-opening: Lyft participated in a real mobile-location catalyst but entered a network market where the opening had already produced a stronger incumbent. Daqstra risks the infrastructure version of that pattern. Nominal has disclosed at least $155 million in recent financing and reached a $1 billion valuation, while Sift has raised $67 million and overlaps Daqstra in telemetry analysis and test operations. Those financings show investor demand, not a customer-side catalyst. Without a new hardware architecture, procurement shift, or AI capability that changes buying behavior, Daqstra may be arriving after the category opened rather than reopening it.

The $100,000-plus annual-savings target in Daqstra’s palm-oil pilot is useful, and day-to-day use by aerospace programs is more informative than a generic pre-seed waitlist. Daqstra still discloses no revenue, contract value, conversion rate, retention, or growth curve, so I cannot distinguish “just working” from well-executed pilot tourism. The counterfactual is straightforward: three named production customers, more than $500,000 of contracted ARR, or greater than 20% month-over-month expansion would move Daqstra into the invest tier because the commercial curve would outweigh the crowded entry point.

NI, Siemens, Modbus, OPC-UA, and custom TCP integrations give Daqstra a plausible path to becoming an index on increasingly complex physical R&D. Over the next 18 months, every additional connector can shorten the next deployment, while stored configurations, abort logic, and historical run context can increase switching costs; that is how Daqstra compounds rather than merely sells another dashboard. The failure mode is equally mechanical: if each installation requires bespoke safety validation and founder-led integration, Daqstra accumulates services obligations while Nominal and Sift accumulate distribution. Daqstra has the architectural position of a horizontal rail, but not yet the repeatability proving it behaves like one.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow and data infrastructure on top of commodity sequencing avoided betting on new chemistry. Daqstra similarly builds above validated sensors, PLCs, data-acquisition hardware, and foundation models. Daqstra does not need new physics; Daqstra needs reliable integrations, safety assurance, and enterprise distribution. Human approval for AI actions reduces the control-risk miracle, although bidirectional aborts and commands still make trust materially harder than read-only observability. This is predominantly a single-miracle architecture—the orchestration layer becoming reliable enough to standardize—not a science project.

Nominal’s $1 billion valuation signals that physical-test infrastructure can support venture outcomes, but it also sets the competitive geometry for my forecast: by July 2029, Daqstra will reach $1 million–$3 million in ARR across roughly 10–20 aerospace and energy deployments, remain deployment-heavy, and trail Nominal and Sift rather than become the horizontal standard. Daqstra’s founder-market fit should win demanding niches; Daqstra’s missing catalyst and invisible revenue curve should prevent breakout velocity. That is a good operating business trajectory, not yet one of the dozen or two companies likely to matter most in ten years.

**Anduril** is the strongest objection to that forecast. Anduril combined deeply credible builders, workflow infrastructure on top of hard hardware, and a market competitors had misread; Daqstra’s live static-fire experience, daily aerospace usage, mixed-vendor integrations, and palm-oil pilot create the same bull mechanism. The analogy breaks where it matters: Ukraine reopened defense procurement, Anduril had compounding contracts, and Daqstra currently has neither an equivalent catalyst nor disclosed buying velocity. I would flip from Neutral to Invest if Daqstra verifies at least three production customers totaling $500,000-plus ARR with repeatable deployment across different hardware stacks. Until then, Daqstra is an impressive product and founder-market-fit signal without enough evidence that the market is “just working.”
