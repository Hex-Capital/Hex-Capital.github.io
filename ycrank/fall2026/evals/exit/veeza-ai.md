# Veeza AI -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Veeza’s strongest exit route is a strategic sale to a visa processor such as Atlys or iVisa. The memo documents a Cairo appointment tracker and Arabic- and English-language WhatsApp support. Atlys already publishes appointment availability in India, and iVisa offers appointment-based services. An acquirer could use Veeza’s localized workflow to enter Egypt faster, but the memo gives no indication of buyer interest or what that head start would be worth. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A plausible seed-stage milestone is an agency pilot that demonstrates repeatable paid completions and positive contribution after refunds and human review, first in Egypt and then in a second origin market. The founder reports reaching five figures in monthly cash revenue, and Keiki reports that usage tripled after WhatsApp integration. Those signals support testing the workflow through a partner. The capital and staffing needed to replicate it remain unknown. A sale of the software and operations is plausible, with continued portal access and any rights or consents needed to transfer sensitive records still unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The memo provides no buyer price or holder terms, so the sale figures below are illustrative assumptions. Both assume an eligible holder, a full sale of the stake, pro rata allocation after deal deductions, no additional senior preference, and cash paid within eight years.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Modest sale | $30M enterprise value less $5M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $25M in distributable equity | $750K (1.5×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M in distributable equity | $2.7M (5.4×). If only 75% of the stake is sold, $2.025M (4.05×). |

### Countercase

An incumbent could build a similar workflow using commercially available browser-control models and its existing review and support operations. Buying Veeza would still leave appointment supply under consular control, with scarce slots driving refunds and changing requirements creating review work. That limits what a buyer might pay for the software alone. The memo leaves both the achievable sale price and the economics of the localized operation unresolved. It establishes neither a structural closing block nor a price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What would an incumbent pay to acquire Veeza after seeing the results of a live agency pilot? Measured incremental profit from paid completions after refunds and review, paired with a buyer-indicated price range, would answer it. Move to Reach out if that range credibly clears the holder’s cash hurdle after deal deductions. Move to Pass if the strongest credible buyer economics put proceeds materially below it.
