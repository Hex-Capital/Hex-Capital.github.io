# Stratum Industries -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: April–May 2025 federal policy made AI procurement and permitting modernization more actionable, agencies now have permission to buy, and the controlling variable is whether Stratum can increase correctly adjudicated applications per officer-hour without increasing errors, appeals, or compliance burden. The dossier provides no deployment, baseline, or threshold for that variable. A $34.4 billion “smart government” TAM does not answer it.

The product is more substantive than a generic government chatbot. It reads applications, applies agency-selected rubrics, routes steps among models, requests missing information, and returns cited work for a human decision. Better models could improve this system rather than automatically erase it. But Stratum has not shown agency integrations, retained case state, evaluated outcomes, or a reviewed-case dataset. Cross-domain ambition—from patents and drug approvals to disability benefits—also multiplies the compliance and accuracy regimes before one workflow has been proven.

The strongest bull case is the combination of institutional timing and team composition. Vihaan was an early engineer at document-AI company Cadastral before its June 2026 acquisition, while Raj reports averaging 220% of quota at Deel. That is unusually relevant technical and enterprise-sales experience for two pre-seed founders. The Anduril exception is directionally instructive because both companies entered government during an institutional opening; the material difference is that Stratum faces three funded channel incumbents rather than a strategic vacuum, and it has not demonstrated a change in unit economics.

Competition makes the missing variable more consequential. GovWell already serves over 150 agencies and 5,000 processes after raising $34.5 million; Polimorphic and Clariti have each raised roughly $28 million. Stratum’s cross-domain positioning is not yet differentiation because there is no evidence that a shared adjudication engine transfers accurately across heterogeneous programs. This market is crystallizing, and broader scope without a wedge may increase implementation cost faster than distribution.

There is also no product-market pull yet: no revenue, named pilot, repeat usage, signed LOI, or capacity bottleneck. I do not treat that as unusual for Summer 2026 pre-seed, but I cannot use the public launch as evidence that agencies will survive procurement, security review, impact assessment, and appeals governance. My 36-month base case is that Stratum either verticalizes into one permitting or licensing category and reaches $1–3 million ARR, or remains below $1 million ARR; the current “entire U.S. government backlog” scope is unlikely to persist.

I would pass today because the primary market gate is unresolved, not because the team lacks ability. One verifiable result would flip the decision: a named agency paying Stratum to process at least 10,000 applications while cutting median review time by 50% or more, with no material increase in error or appeal rates. That would establish the controlling variable, product pull, and workflow durability simultaneously.
