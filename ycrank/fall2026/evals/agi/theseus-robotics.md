# Theseus Robotics -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Theseus has one supported AGI path. Learned manipulation could make overnight replenishment viable at existing grocery stores that cannot reliably staff a manual shift. The company reports that stores struggle to staff those shifts and describes a system that uses delivery lists and shelf layouts to restock, with remote operators handling exceptions. The memo records two robots deployed across two stores and launch footage from Zürich. Theseus reports peak rates of 400 units restocked and 40 units picked per hour. The deployments support in-store feasibility. The economic case assumes the robots can complete a shift with substantially fewer human labor minutes per correctly stocked unit, including remote interventions and store-staff rework. Even with equally capable models, grocers would still need hardware, store integration, and exception handling to get shelves stocked. Theseus earns zero for building blocks because its robots serve grocery operations and the memo identifies no scarce input they supply to an AI workload. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

In-house demonstrations and the ability to capture operator corrections give Theseus a plausible operating advantage. Its durability depends on whether those corrections improve performance across stores, which remains unmeasured in the memo. Two stores hosting robots provide evidence of trial demand. Theseus reports pilots for up to 10 more robots in 2027, with paid conversion and repeat demand unverified. The team’s learning-based control experience and prior live pick-and-place deployment support execution, while dependable overnight delivery remains open. The monthly fee model lacks the price, utilization, intervention labor, and service costs needed to estimate contribution from the next robot. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Theseus could end up selling a different way to staff the same shift. If unfamiliar products and shelf layouts require frequent remote interventions or store-staff cleanup, peak throughput could coexist with too little labor savings to enable an overnight shift a grocer previously could not run. Competing replenishment systems and commercially available robot bodies also give grocers credible alternatives. The in-store deployments support a concrete mechanism, and the unresolved shift-level economics trigger Watch. (*Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a grocer that previously could not staff overnight replenishment sustain it at an acceptable cost with Theseus? A paid pilot across representative shifts and product mixes would answer this by measuring correctly stocked units, remote operator time, downtime, and store-staff rework, then comparing the fully loaded cost per correctly stocked unit with the grocer’s manual baseline and budget. Sustained shift completion within that budget, followed by a paid continuation, would move the action to Reach out. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)
