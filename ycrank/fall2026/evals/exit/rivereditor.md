# River -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is River’s strongest path. A productivity platform could pay to own River’s native editors and cross-file agent if integrating them would win or retain customers faster than building comparable tools or partnering. The memo documents a desktop launch and published pricing, and describes a workspace that combines documents, spreadsheets, slides and a browser with multiple model providers. River reports more than 100,000 creators. The memo provides no paid-team retention, measured workflow gains, or buyer interest. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

The operating milestone is repeat paid use by teams completing complex projects across files and apps, with a measurable advantage over an incumbent suite. River’s launch supports its ability to put the product in users’ hands. The reported $500,000 YC note gives a financing signal, while burn, runway, and the cost of reaching that milestone remain unknown. IP ownership and the transfer terms for model and connected-app agreements are also unverified. The memo supplies no independently supported fallback that could pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The reported note lacks the terms needed to model the holder’s ownership and payout, so the scenarios use the thesis’s illustrative $500,000 check at $10 million post-money, with 5% ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-on checks. Assume no net debt, fees, or working-capital adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. Assume a simple pro rata waterfall and full cash payment at closing, with zero stock, escrow, earnout, rollover, or compensation. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in gross holder cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. At 2% ownership the hurdle rises to $125 million, and at 4% it falls to $62.5 million. (Market & Traction)

| Scenario assumption | Timing | Gross holder cash | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale at $40 million enterprise value | Year 4 | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | Year 6 | $3.0 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private ownership with growth plateauing, buyers staying away, public scale out of reach, and cash reinvested | Through year 8 | $0, with shares retained | 0× realized |

The sale prices illustrate where proceeds cross the hurdle. The memo offers no River-specific valuation or buyer economics to support either price. At 2% ownership, even the modeled $100 million sale yields $2 million, or 4.0×.

### Countercase

Microsoft already offers cross-file execution and in-place editing under existing Microsoft 365 tenant controls. Anthropic and Genspark offer overlapping agent workflows, and Genspark also has native office tools. A buyer with comparable capabilities may have little reason to acquire River, especially if reported adoption fails to translate into retained, paying teams. This keeps the case below the outreach gate. The memo leaves open whether River’s multi-model workspace can deliver an ownership advantage in a distinct workflow, so the evidence does not trigger a structural Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production pilot show that owning River would give a productivity platform an economic advantage large enough to justify roughly $83 million in distributable equity value? Repeat team usage, measured gains against the platform’s existing tools, and a buyer-side estimate of the value of ownership over building or partnering would move the action to Reach out if they support that hurdle.
