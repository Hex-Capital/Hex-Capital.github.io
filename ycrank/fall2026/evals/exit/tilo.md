# Tilo -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest supported route is a strategic sale to an agent-sandbox provider such as E2B or Daytona. The memo records a public repository implementing shared app state, attributed edits, undo, and workspace permissions, and Tilo lists about 50 native apps on that model. A sandbox provider could use that integrated desktop to offer customers faster, cheaper multi-app automation. Tilo reports roughly 1/29 of the cost and 90× the speed on one same-model task. The comparison run failed, so the test leaves the savings on completed workflows unresolved. The memo establishes a reason a buyer might value the product, while the economics of owning it remain untested. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

A paid production pilot across several completed workflows is the next milestone. The browser demo and Rust SDK give Tilo a starting point for deployment and integrations. Early-alpha durability and third-party app coverage could lengthen the pilot, and the listed pre-seed round leaves runway and capital needs unclear. The memo provides no deal terms or evidence on IP transfer, required consents, or holder eligibility. It also provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The memo reports no Tilo revenue figure or buyer price evidence, so the sale prices below are illustrative sensitivities. Both sale cases assume $5 million in combined net debt and deal adjustments, a closing within eight years, an eligible holder selling its entire stake for cash at closing, and a pro rata waterfall with no preference reducing the holder’s share. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

- **Modest sale.** $45 million in enterprise value becomes $40 million in distributable equity, yielding $1.2 million in cash to the holder, or 2.4×.
- **Strong sale.** $105 million in enterprise value becomes $100 million in distributable equity, yielding $3 million in cash, or 6×. If the holder can sell only half its stake, cash falls to $1.5 million, or 3×.
- **Prolonged private operation.** If Tilo reinvests its cash and no eligible share purchase or distribution occurs within eight years, the holder realizes $0.

### Countercase

Sandbox providers could adopt open MCP interfaces and build the pieces customers value most. Windows customers could also keep their desktops as Microsoft adds agent capabilities or use Minicor to expose legacy workflows as APIs. Tilo’s public code gives a potential buyer a way to evaluate how much it could reproduce, and the single-task benchmark leaves the value of acquiring the full workspace unproven. These are credible substitution risks. The memo leaves open whether they can match Tilo’s integrated workflow and whether a strategic buyer could pay enough to clear the hurdle, so neither Pass gate is triggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production pilot with an agent-sandbox provider show repeatable savings on completed multi-app workflows that, at deployment volumes the provider can substantiate, support an acquisition price clearing the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Verified pilot results and a buyer-supported build-versus-buy analysis that substantiates that price would move the action to Reach out. If that analysis shows that even the strongest credible sale falls materially short after the waterfall, move to Pass.
