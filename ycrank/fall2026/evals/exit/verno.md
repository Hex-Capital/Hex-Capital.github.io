# Verno -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Verno’s strongest path. Autodesk is a plausible buyer because Plant 3D already validates P&IDs within a CAD project. Verno’s documented workflow extends change control across native DWG/DXF drawings and downstream files, with engineer approval and revision history. Owning that workflow could let Autodesk sell broader change control to its CAD customers and shorten the time needed to build cross-file write-back. Verno reports finding three conflicting design pressures for one tank in documents issued for construction. That illustrates a concrete use case, while the frequency and cost of such errors and the buyer’s economic benefit remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Verno reports an active pilot, and the memo also records reported live customer sites. A milestone that would strengthen the acquisition case is repeat paid use across multiple projects at two independent EPCs, with measured reconciliation time saved and cross-file edit error rates. The pilot gives Verno a place to start. The capital and runway needed to replicate it remain unclear, and compatibility with specific CAD setups still needs validation. IP ownership and customer-contract transfer rights remain unverified. The other routes lack evidence of cash generation for a buyout or payout, eligible share demand for a secondary, or operating scale and readiness for an IPO. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Using the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million gross before investor taxes. At 3%, it requires $83.3 million of distributable equity, or $88.3 million of enterprise value with $5 million of assumed net debt. At 2% and 4% ownership, the equity hurdles are $125 million and $62.5 million, respectively. With the same assumed debt, a $25 million sale yields $600,000 (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). These prices are scenario inputs. Verno’s realizable sale price and holder terms remain unknown. If adoption plateaus at limited deployments, buyer interest fails to materialize, public scale stays out of reach, and Verno reinvests all available cash with no secondary or payout, the holder realizes $0 within eight years and retains the shares.

### Countercase

Autodesk could extend Plant 3D or license Verno’s connectors if the workflow creates value but offers little advantage from ownership. Plant 3D’s existing validation gives Autodesk a starting point, and Verno’s case-by-case compatibility work could make new deployments slower and more expensive. The memo leaves open whether acquisition beats those options or can clear the proceeds hurdle. That warrants Watch. The evidence establishes neither a structural block nor a supported price ceiling that triggers Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

A buyer-side analysis grounded in repeat paid EPC deployments and measured savings would move the action to Reach out if it supported a price that could deliver $2.5 million gross within eight years at plausible retained ownership and after applicable claims.
