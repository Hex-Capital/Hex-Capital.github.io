# Hue -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Hue’s strongest path. An agent development or evaluation platform could buy Hue to integrate repeatable replay of production failures into its release-testing workflow. Hue says its software reconstructs app state at a selected checkpoint from recorded traces and gives each test run an isolated copy. Owning the replay engine could make sense if deep integration materially improves failure reproduction and gives the buyer control over a capability it cannot get on comparable terms through a license or internal build. Buyer interest and the value of ownership remain unverified. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*)

The memo reports 69,000 weekly SDK downloads, an early distribution signal. The founders report building related testing and replay infrastructure in prior roles. Those signals make a paid production comparison plausible at seed, with funding and runway still unverified. The memo describes restrictions on processing customer data and leaves IP ownership and rights to transfer customer-derived replay assets unresolved. Subscription terms establish a billing mechanism. The memo provides no usable evidence of recurring cash flow for a buyout or payout, demand for this holder’s shares, or a path to public resale. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

The illustrative $500,000 check starts at 5% ownership and falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× hurdle is $2.5 million in gross holder cash. Under a simple pro rata waterfall, that requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing and sale bridge | Cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | Year 5. $50M enterprise value becomes $50M in distributable equity cash. | $1.5M (3×) |
| Strong sale | Year 7. $100M enterprise value becomes $100M in distributable equity cash. | $3.0M (6×) |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. The investor retains the shares. | $0 (0×) |

Both sale scenarios assume zero net debt and other price adjustments, no preference diversion, and full cash payment at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. The sale values are scenarios. The memo supplies no usable price evidence to underwrite either one.

### Countercase

Ancha already offers commercial trace-based replay, and open-source ClawsBench offers stateful Gmail and Slack simulations with snapshot restoration. Hue also says incomplete traces can prevent faithful reconstruction. If a substitute reproduces the same costly failures, the buyer can get the benefit without acquiring Hue. The overlap makes that a credible risk. Whether substitutes match Hue on consequential production failures remains open, so the structural Pass gate stays untriggered. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production comparison show that Hue reproduces costly failures a credible substitute misses, with enough recurring economic value to justify buying Hue at a price that clears the holder’s hurdle? A platform buyer’s measured results and documented buy-versus-license economics supporting that price would move the action to **Reach out**.
