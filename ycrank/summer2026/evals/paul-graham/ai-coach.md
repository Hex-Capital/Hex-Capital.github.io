# COACH -- Paul Graham Evaluation

$500,000 ARR after five months makes COACH a commercial-animal company with unusually thin hacker genealogy. COACH has the tracks animals leave: the Perez brothers previously built Alsace Test to €3.5 million in 14 months, endured six pivots, sold door to door, and converted forward-deployed pilots into claimed recurring revenue. Nicolas Sebag has ten years of engineering experience, but COACH has no public repositories, technical artifact, or hacker-community trail predating the startup. The strongest fact is resourcefulness. The weakest is hacker lineage.

**Airbnb** is the high rung for animal resourcefulness because the cereal boxes turned rejection into revenue through an unscalable stunt. COACH shares the load-bearing mechanism: Mathieu and Thomas Perez personally worked alongside Réseau ACEH representatives, and COACH reportedly reached $500,000 ARR with 100% pilot conversion after only five months. COACH diverges because Airbnb’s stunt was concrete and independently legible, while COACH’s 120% monthly growth, zero churn, and conversion claims lack customer counts and cohort definitions. These founders look like animals, but the footprints have been photographed by the animals themselves.

**Loopt** occupies the low rung on hacker genealogy because weak product timing required Sam Altman’s force of personality—not a public pre-company technical artifact—to supply the override. COACH sits above that pattern: Sebag built iOS software at Accor and has a decade of engineering experience, so COACH is not an MBA plan to hire programmers later. But no COACH founder has a public repository, open-source contribution, programming prize, or Hacker News artifact. Sebag’s credentials establish competence; they do not establish the Collison-style hacker gene. The Loopt exception warns me not to reject animals merely because their GitHub pages are empty, but exceptions should remain exceptions.

Mathieu and Thomas Perez personally encountered field selling through Réseau ACEH, which makes COACH substantially more organic than a sales-enablement idea found in a market report. COACH would move to the very top of this criterion if the founders had built an early version to coach their own representatives inside Alsace Test and could show repeated internal use before selling it. The dossier does not establish that sequence. COACH comes from adjacent lived pain rather than a Dropbox-like moment where Drew Houston built exactly what he himself needed.

Recording customer-facing meetings creates COACH’s real schlep: consent, security review, device reliability, outcome attribution, and persuading field representatives to tolerate continuous observation. Over the next 18 months, COACH can compound if its forward-deployed pilots turn those annoyances into customer-specific recording datasets and renewal knowledge that a generic model cannot copy. If COACH treats compliance as paperwork and the model as the product, Rilla can reproduce nearly everything. The unpleasant deployment work—not the AI—is COACH’s prospective moat.

The Perez brothers resemble the Collisons on one narrow axis: they are siblings who already built a €3.5 million-revenue business together. That makes COACH’s core pair much stronger than three strangers assembled for an application. COACH’s third founder is less proven relationally because Thomas recruited Nicolas Sebag through LinkedIn in 2025, but the prior brother-founder history and Sebag’s complementary mobile engineering make the configuration unusually sensible. It is a strong pair with an attached technical founder, not yet a fully tested trio.

$500,000 ARR after five months points to COACH reaching $15–30 million ARR by July 2029, concentrated in home-services and other field-sales verticals, while remaining smaller than Rilla rather than becoming a general revenue platform. COACH’s commercial animals can keep selling and servicing difficult deployments faster than its limited hacker genealogy can produce a category-defining technical advantage. The likely result is a valuable, sales-led vertical business—not the next Google, but more than enough for an angel check.

**Rilla** is the bear case that stings: an estimated $70 million ARR, $78.9 million raised, and direct overlap make COACH look like a fast-selling clone entering after the answer is known. COACH also lacks public model documentation, consent controls, independently verified retention, and a technical artifact that would expose an unseen insight. The rebuttal is behavioral: founders who previously reached €3.5 million in 14 months, survived six pivots, went into the field, and then claimed $500,000 ARR in five months are displaying resourcefulness rather than manager mode. I would invest in COACH because animals can survive a crowded market, but I would flip to Pass if fewer than half of the customers underlying COACH’s reported ARR renew at the contracted price after twelve months.
