# Constor -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is Constor’s strongest path. The memo records TAG Software Group’s acquisition of Countfire, establishing that a buyer has paid to own an adjacent takeoff and estimating product. Constor’s potential asset is its HVAC and insulation workflow, which turns drawings into editable network quantities and 3D routing. TAG or a similar buyer could add that capability to an existing estimating suite. Ownership could be worth more than licensing if it secures control of a workflow and a transferable set of drawings and estimator corrections that materially improve takeoff performance. That benefit remains an inference. A2C Isolation reports cutting takeoffs from hours to minutes, but the memo supplies no comparison with a buyer’s existing tools and no acquisition price for Countfire. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A seed-stage milestone would be repeat paid use on live HVAC and insulation bids, with measured reductions in estimator time after corrections and verified quantity accuracy against an existing workflow. Constor reports five paying design partners, 12 paid preorders with unspecified overlap, and about 15 contractors bidding with the product. Those are plausible starting points for the test. The memo records a $500,000 YC convertible note, with runway, test costs, and note terms still open. Current terms allow internal training on submitted drawings, while continued use after a sale remains unverified. The other routes have weaker evidence. Reported launch profitability does not establish cash generation for a buyout or payout, and the memo gives no evidence of eligible secondary demand or readiness for a public listing and resale. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** Assume a $500,000 investment at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, no follow-ons, and a 5× gross cash target within eight years. The holder needs $2.5 million, requiring $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% hurdle is about $88.3 million in enterprise value before any additional fees or senior claims. At year eight, a $25 million sale yields $600,000 gross before investor taxes (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×), assuming simple pro rata distribution after that debt. Neither price is supported by buyer economics in the memo. Constor’s actual net debt and the note’s effect on dilution or repayment are unknown. If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

TAG already owns Countfire’s linked takeoff and pricing workflow. Beam AI already covers mechanical takeoffs, and Bobyard offers commercial plumbing takeoff. A buyer could extend its own product or license a specialist capability. Customer verification reduces the labor savings a buyer can underwrite, and limits on blueprint reuse could reduce the value of acquiring the training data. These are concrete reasons for Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported sale price that leaves the holder materially short, so the Pass gate does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would the incremental value of owning Constor’s HVAC and insulation workflow support a strategic sale above the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Move to **Reach out** if a paid production benchmark measures time saved after corrections at verified accuracy, and a plausible buyer’s integration economics support a price range that clears the hurdle after dilution and senior claims.
