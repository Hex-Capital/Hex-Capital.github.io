# Armorer Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an enterprise security platform is the strongest private exit path. The asset would be Armorer’s workflow for testing agent behavior, checking proposed fixes against customer workflows, and routing fixes for engineer approval. A platform could pay to own that workflow if integrating it into its suite materially cuts the time and cost of resolving security findings, and ownership captures more of that benefit than building or licensing the capability. That ownership advantage is an inference. (The Idea)

The memo records Check Point’s acquisition of Lakera, which establishes that a security platform has bought an adjacent capability. A Just Eat Takeaway engineering director credited Armorer with resolving a security problem, and SnapLogic executives discussed collaboration. The testimonial gives evidence of customer utility. The memo supplies no measured remediation savings, commercial terms, or acquisition interest in Armorer. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The operating milestone is a paid production deployment that measures time and engineering effort from finding to approved fix against the customer’s existing workflow. The enterprise launch and the founders’ prior work together on AI security support a plausible path to that test. The memo gives no test budget, runway, or evidence that the core IP and customer agreements could transfer in a sale. It also gives no separate basis to underwrite a sponsor buyout, secondary, public resale, or company-funded payout. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** With no disclosed check terms, use the illustrative $500,000 check at $10 million post-money, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-on checks, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. The sale scenarios assume zero applicable net debt and transaction costs, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. They assume a simple pro rata waterfall without senior claims or preference adjustments. All consideration is cash at closing, with $0 in stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation. The prices and timing are scenario assumptions. The memo provides neither the Lakera purchase price nor measurable buyer economics that would support a price for Armorer. The hurdle is about $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, and $62.5 million at 4%. The modeled strong sale clears at 3% and falls short at 2%. Armorer needs evidence that a buyer could justify a price above the applicable hurdle.

### Countercase

A platform could cover runtime controls with an existing bundle or open-source policy enforcement and license any incremental remediation capability it needs. Check Point already combines discovery, testing, and runtime controls. Armorer’s company-run comparison reported 98% accuracy and 1.9 ms median latency on 50 documents. That test leaves production performance and the value of its approval workflow unresolved. The substitutes weaken the case for ownership, but the memo does not establish that they can match the full remediation workflow or cap a buyer’s price below the hurdle. The modeled $0 prolonged-private outcome is a downside scenario. The evidence supports Watch, with no Pass override triggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production deployment show enough incremental value from owning Armorer’s remediation workflow for a security platform to justify a price that clears the investor’s hurdle? Move to Reach out if a customer-verified comparison measures the savings and a prospective buyer validates a credible path from those savings to at least $83.3 million of distributable equity within eight years at 3% ownership. Keep watching if the deployment establishes customer value while buyer economics remain unresolved.
