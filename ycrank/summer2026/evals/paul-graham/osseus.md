# Osseus -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Anish Mariathasan’s biomedical-engineering work and Ayush Patel’s self-driving-robot project make Osseus a schlep-heavy engineering company whose founders have not yet left animal tracks. Osseus is willing to connect CAD, electronics, code, tests, suppliers, permissions, and customer VPCs. That is unusually unpleasant work. But invited workspaces and a demo form are not the equivalent of revenue, repeat use, or some improbable stunt that proves the Osseus founders will force the product into existence.

**Coinbase** (2012) is the high rung because compliance was not incidental to the product; enduring the compliance grind was the mechanism that made Coinbase valuable. Osseus has a similar shape in mirrored permissions, custom connectors, customer-cloud deployment, and forward-deployed onboarding. Those chores may repel the AI-wrapper crowd. Osseus diverges from Coinbase at the decisive point: the dossier names no deployment where Anish or Ayush personally survived the integration grind, and no customer has yet paid them to keep surviving it.

**Kiko** (2005) exposed the low-rung failure mechanism: shipping software is insufficient if the founders’ behavior ultimately points toward a tidy exit instead of relentless expansion. Osseus describes a fairly elaborate working artifact, but Osseus shows no public repository, revenue improvisation, manual customer rescue, or pre-funding act that sounds slightly insane. Anish’s prior compliance-AI startup could contain such evidence, yet the dossier supplies none. Animals leave tracks. Here I am applying the absent-evidence default.

Anish Mariathasan’s physical-system and health-AI background gives Osseus proximity to the problem, while Ayush Patel’s Apple QA work and Raspberry Pi robot give Osseus additional exposure to fragmented engineering workflows. Proximity is not an organic idea. Over the next 18 months, firsthand pain would compound into better choices about which connector, revision edge, or failure record matters; borrowed pain would produce a broad graph that demos well and solves nobody’s urgent problem. The dossier never says either founder personally lost a robot iteration because design rationale was scattered, so Osseus sits only slightly above the default.

Ayush Patel completed two Apple internships and built a computer-vision robot, but Osseus has no public GitHub trail, competitive-programming result, or independently adopted technical artifact. The hacker score would flip a tier if Osseus showed that Ayush or Anish had built the cross-domain parser before fundraising and robotics engineers were already using it without hand-holding. Until then, Apple and Imperial are credentials. They are not yet the Dropbox demo on Hacker News.

**Stripe** (2010) had two brothers who had already built companies together; the relationship itself reduced the number of ways the startup could die. Osseus also has the right numerical configuration: Anish and Ayush are two technical founders who attended Imperial College London from 2022 through 2026. But Osseus provides no evidence that they built together during those four years or that their roles are complementary under pressure. I give Osseus credit for the pair, not yet for the bond.

CoLab’s $72 million Series C means Osseus is entering a validated category with a much better-financed peer, while the integration breadth will punish any attempt to remain horizontal. By July 2029, Osseus will have narrowed into a forward-deployed CAD-and-electrical change-review product for robotics teams and reached $1 million to $3 million in annual recurring revenue, rather than becoming the universal engineering record described today. Osseus’s appetite for schlep can win a niche; the missing animal evidence makes me doubt it can win seven systems simultaneously.

Osseus’s strongest bull case is that default-deny memory promotion, mirrored permissions, and customer-VPC deployment are already the unglamorous architecture serious robotics customers require. Airbnb (2009) warns against rejecting a wrong-seeming idea merely because conventional validation is absent. But Airbnb had cereal boxes: a specific act that revealed the founders. Osseus has architecture and biographies, not an equivalent act. I would flip to Invest if Osseus produced three named robotics customers that completed paid integrations and renewed after six months, because that would simultaneously prove resourcefulness, organic workflow knowledge, and willingness to endure the schlep.
