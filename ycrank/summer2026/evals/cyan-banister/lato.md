# LATO -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Tymek Staniszewski spent six years inside Verdane, yet LATO sells a demo-only software abstraction that a normal human cannot touch or independently test. That high founder-problem intimacy and low physical verifiability is LATO’s signature: an insider building a credible tool for his old job, not a magically weird artifact found in the wild. LATO passes my “Biz” test and probably the “Tiz” test through Tien Chu, but the “Rizz” remains trapped behind an enterprise demo form.

Anduril is the high rung for founders who lived adjacent to their problem before forming a company: Palmer Luckey arrived with years of hardware-making identity, not a market memo. Tymek’s six years in growth equity gives LATO the same load-bearing insider fluency; Blume Equity and FoodLabs suggest LATO can translate that fluency into buyer trust. LATO diverges because Tymek did not previously ship a research product at scale, while Tien’s constant-memory DNN work is technically credible but not specific evidence that either founder has solved expert interviewing or behavioral simulation before.

GameCrush is the low rung for physical-world verifiability because governance trouble could strip away the product DNA while investors lacked an external artifact that proved what had to survive. LATO exposes a related weakness: LATO’s value lives inside proprietary fund documents, recorded expert calls, and unreported simulations, with no public back-testing, accuracy measurement, or self-service product. A user can hear an interview recording, but an outsider cannot observe whether LATO’s simulated market participants are right. I cannot ride it like Uber or notice it on a Wi-Fi scan like Flock Safety.

AlphaSense already exceeds $600 million ARR, while Rogo has more than 25,000 daily users and Hebbia serves over 40% of the largest asset managers by AUM. Over 18 months, LATO’s primary-interview transcripts could compound into proprietary market memory, but only if LATO retains reuse rights and repeatedly interviews the same ecosystems. Today the opposite mechanism dominates: generally available voice APIs commoditize collection, established competitors own distribution, and LATO repositioned from Excel-model checking to interviews and simulation in roughly three months. That is adaptation, but it is not yet “magically weird”; it may simply be a crowded market wearing a new hat.

Tymek Staniszewski is explicitly LATO’s CEO, and Tien Chu is explicitly LATO’s CTO, so LATO begins above my absent-evidence governance default. The tier would flip upward if Tymek held documented tiebreaker authority, founder vesting, and an exit-conflict protocol; eleven years of friendship since high school is not a substitute for that conversation. Two founders can love each other and still discover that 50/50 is a hand grenade with a long fuse.

LATO’s SOC 2 and ISO 27001 work remains in progress, but LATO is not Mindbloom-style regulated healthcare or SpaceX-style frontier hardware. The single-anchor parallel is Vigil Labs: both enter trust-sensitive financial workflows, yet Vigil inhabits regulation as part of the moat while LATO currently treats security certification as procurement work. LATO could become unsexy in the productive sense if consent, expert attribution, confidential-data isolation, and simulation auditability harden into infrastructure; today it remains an AI application sitting above commodity models.

Blume Equity and FoodLabs are the evidence behind my 36-month forecast: LATO narrows around AI-conducted expert interviews for private-market diligence, reaches roughly $3 million to $7 million ARR, and is acquired by AlphaSense rather than becoming an independent research platform. LATO’s founder-market fit can produce a useful enterprise workflow, while LATO’s low physical verifiability and absent structural moat make the interview corpus more valuable as a feature inside an incumbent’s distribution machine.

Tien Chu studied computer science, mathematics, and philosophy, which makes the opposing case sting: LATO may be my DeepMind exception, a polymath paired with a buyer who lived the workflow, while two early customers seed a proprietary transcript-and-outcome flywheel before AlphaSense notices the category shift. LATO could use interviews as the consumer wrapper while quietly building a market-behavior data network underneath—“I’m never playing the game I seem to be playing.” The dossier still gives the acquisition case the edge: LATO publishes no dataset scale, retention, simulation accuracy, or present moat, and the product crossed several workflows in three months. I would flip to Invest if LATO reaches $2 million ARR from at least ten renewing investment firms within twelve months.
