# Agency Tool Company -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Jack Morrison and Davis Foster shipped hundreds of Scythe robots before its 2026 acquisition by ASI, while Agency Tool Company has disclosed no revenue curve beyond three launch partners. That pairing is Agency Tool Company’s signature: unusually strong demonstrated shipping attached to unusually weak commercial visibility. Agency Tool Company looks like workflow infrastructure built by founders who lived the failure mode, but I cannot yet distinguish “just working” from three sophisticated design partners being helpful.

**Perplexity** is the high rung on founder iteration: Aravind repeatedly built the discussed product within a week. Morrison and Foster give Agency Tool Company the closest available pre-seed analogue because they spent eight years together, deployed hundreds of autonomous machines, and built Agency Tool Company around tooling they personally lacked at Scythe. Agency Tool Company diverges from Perplexity because the dossier shows a shipped beta and prior operating history, not repeated product changes driven by current users; exceptional shipping capacity is demonstrated, but the present iteration cadence is not.

**Lyft** is the low rung on visible curve quality because its Series C case lacked a credible path to share inversion in a market where relative momentum determined the economics. Agency Tool Company presents an earlier and harsher information gap: Burro, Tempo Works, and Gather AI use Deploy, but Agency Tool Company reports neither revenue nor customer expansion, and Burro’s 750 machines are Burro’s footprint rather than Agency Tool Company deployment volume. Three named production users are more informative than a waitlist, yet without paid conversions, fleet penetration, or month-over-month usage, Agency Tool Company remains closer to false signal than to Harvey-style “just working.”

**$703.46 million** of robot fleet-management software in 2025, growing at 15.29% annually, is a category trend rather than a market re-opening. Agency Tool Company can compound over the next 18 months if physical-AI funding converts prototypes into persistent field fleets: more deployed robots create larger artifacts, intermittent connections, rollback risk, and pressure to replace SSH and spreadsheets. Agency Tool Company has founder timing after Scythe’s 2026 acquisition and production use across agriculture, construction, and logistics, but the dossier names no transformer-scale technical step-function, procurement change, or incumbent failure in the last 24 months. I see a favorable slope, not a Stripe-after-merchant-accounts catalyst.

**Burro’s 750-plus machines** show how Agency Tool Company could become an index on field robotics: every additional robot, model revision, and operating-system update increases deployment activity without Agency Tool Company needing to manufacture hardware. The counterfactual is decisive. If Agency Tool Company becomes the hardware-neutral release system used across Burro, Tempo Works, Gather AI, and subsequent heterogeneous fleets, the index score moves into the top tier; if Formant, InOrbit, or internal platform engineers absorb byte-delta deployment as a feature, Agency Tool Company remains a narrow tool fighting for share. The current three-partner footprint supports the first path but does not establish it.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow and data infrastructure on top of commodity sequencing avoided making new science the investment premise. Agency Tool Company similarly assumes Linux artifacts, compression, containers, and field robots already exist; Agency Tool Company’s remaining work is reliable rollout, integrations, and GTM rather than new physics. The Color known exception therefore applies partially, and the unstated workflow-on-top-of-the-hard-thing pattern fits strongly. Build being “coming soon” adds execution risk, while safety-critical targeting and rollback add reliability risk, but these are coupled product problems—not three independent miracles.

**Three launch partners** lead me to forecast that by July 2029 Agency Tool Company reaches $6 million to $10 million in ARR across at least 40 paying robot fleets, with deployment remaining the core product rather than a pivot. Agency Tool Company’s founders should be able to turn lived Scythe failure modes into a reliable product, while the missing revenue curve implies a slower commercial ramp than the strongest AI infrastructure companies. The mechanism is expansion inside deployed fleets: win one release workflow, accumulate configurations and failure histories, then become painful to remove as robot counts and artifact frequency rise.

**Formant’s $45.25 million raised**, InOrbit’s explicit OTA capability, and Balena’s general Linux-device platform form the strongest bear case: Agency Tool Company may be a feature masquerading as a platform in a $703.46 million market where customers already tolerate scripts. Agency Tool Company also triggers my AI-cycle red flag—no disclosed growth curve—and its three partners may never become meaningful contracts. The evidence that narrowly wins is Morrison and Foster’s prior exit plus actual use by three named operators across distinct industries, which is stronger than founder narrative and matches my workflow-infrastructure pattern. I would invest my own money at pre-seed, but if none of Burro, Tempo Works, or Gather AI converts into a paid, expanding deployment within two quarters, I flip to Pass.
