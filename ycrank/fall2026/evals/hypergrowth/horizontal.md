### Company case

Assuming strong fit with the initial manufacturer, Horizontal’s best growth path is to start with production replanning at one plant, then reuse the integration and approval workflow at other plants. Planning teams in the US and Europe plausibly face the same recurring job. Horizontal reports using existing plant-system interfaces and offering regional cloud and on-premises deployment. Those are credible starting points for reuse, with the amount of site-specific configuration and validation still uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The memo documents a founder-led demo tailored to a plant workflow. Starting with one workflow gives the team a repeatable sales pitch, while plant-specific setup and validation point to a high-touch path to first value. Daily use in production planning could support retention, and additional workflows or plants could increase account spend if contracts charge for that expansion. Recurring billing and expansion pricing remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The two-person team launched publicly on October 21, 2026. Horizontal reports testing in operating plants and working with international manufacturers, but the memo leaves payment status and commercial growth unknown. The advertised operating improvements lack customer names and measurement baselines. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under a negotiated-contract scenario, incremental contribution per added plant would be the revenue attributable to that deployment less sales effort, integration, validation, infrastructure and support. Reusing connectors and approval workflows could reduce the labor required at each successive plant. The memo provides too little pricing or delivery-effort evidence to estimate contribution. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Even with strong fit at the first plant, differences in interfaces, procedures and approval rules could make every deployment an engineering project. The plant-specific demos and openings for forward-deployed engineers make that a credible concern. The structural gate stays open because the memo leaves the extent of required rework and its effect on contribution unresolved. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
