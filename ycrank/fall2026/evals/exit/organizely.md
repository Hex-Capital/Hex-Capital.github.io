# Organizely -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A **strategic acquisition by a manufacturing ERP vendor** is the strongest path. A buyer could pay to own Organizely’s factory data model and the workflows that link production planning, equipment maintenance, and purchasing. Ownership could let the buyer integrate those workflows into its core product and roll them out across its customer base. That advantage depends on whether the combined workflows deliver repeatable gains and whether a buyer can reproduce them cheaply. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*)

The memo records a November factory ERP launch and a Shopify inventory app listing with two customer reviews. The company reports factory deployments across six states, with installation counts and customer identities undisclosed. Kinetic Labs, the founders’ prior business, reports a 22% higher operating margin and 10 hours saved per employee per week. That testimonial points to a benefit worth testing at unaffiliated plants. A cohort of paid plants that renews and reproduces measurable gains would strengthen the acquisition case. On-site setup and production data quality make rollout cost consequential, and the memo supplies no budget or runway for reaching that milestone. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *The Idea*)

**Proceeds bridge.** With investment terms undisclosed, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, zero follow-on checks, and a 5× gross cash objective within eight years. The holder needs $2.5 million before taxes. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The memo gives Shopify subscription prices and leaves factory ERP pricing undisclosed. Neither provides a basis for underwriting an acquisition price. (*The Idea*, *Key Facts*)

The sale scenarios below are illustrative. Each assumes zero net debt, seller fees, and senior claims, with simple pro rata allocation. Under those assumptions, enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration, paid entirely in cash at closing. Stock consideration, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. Actual holder rights and asset transfer terms are unverified.

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value and distributable equity | Holder cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $40 million | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100 million | $3.0 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No sale | $0 | 0× |

The strong sale clears the hurdle at 3% ownership. At 2%, it pays $2 million, or 4×. In the prolonged-private case, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and the company reinvests its cash. The investor retains the shares. The memo supplies no independent fallback supported by holder demand, buyout economics, or distributable cash.

### Countercase

An ERP vendor could build or license enough of the workflow to capture the same benefit. Sutton already offers overlapping manufacturing ERP functions, and Bizmark automates procurement and production planning using existing systems and spreadsheets. The founder-affiliated result leaves open whether Organizely’s linked workflows deliver enough incremental value at independent plants to justify an acquisition premium. The memo establishes competitive overlap and leaves substitute adequacy and buyer economics unresolved. That supports Watch. The structural Pass override remains untriggered. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can paid, unaffiliated factories renew after achieving repeatable gains from Organizely’s linked workflows at a documented rollout cost? Verified results across several such plants would move the action to **Reach out** if the gains and rollout economics support a credible case for an ERP vendor to pay the price implied by the proceeds hurdle.
