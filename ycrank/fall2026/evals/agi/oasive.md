# Oasive -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Oasive launched its municipal product on November 4. It brings audited financials, debt terms, local news, and timestamped public-meeting summaries into a source-linked research workflow. The memo describes analysts spending hours gathering this material, and Oasive says it uses a Google model to transcribe meetings. **Scenario assumption:** If analysts can verify the output and handle exceptions in materially less time at equivalent credit-review quality, a small municipal desk could afford to monitor borrowers it previously left uncovered or reviewed only occasionally. The labor savings and the newly viable use are both unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Howland reports coverage of more than 8,600 school districts across 43 states. A maintained history of comparable financials and timestamped credit events could give desks a reason to pay under model parity if it saves them the work of assembling and maintaining that history. Buyer preference at parity is untested. The manual research burden is an early demand signal. The company-reported 15 institutional desks used the earlier Macro & Rates and Agency MBS products, so municipal willingness to pay remains open. (Defensibility, The Idea, Market & Traction)

The Fall 2026 launch makes this a plausible U.S. seed case. Howland shipped the municipal workflow as a solo founder with fixed-income and AI product experience. Maintaining credit-data quality as coverage grows is the next execution test. The scale scenario is to reuse borrower coverage across desks. Contribution from the next desk would be its realized team fee less incremental inference, data, onboarding, support, and any Oasive-side quality-control costs. Custom pricing leaves realized fees unknown, and the cost inputs are unmeasured. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Oasive may make existing analysts faster while leaving the set of economically viable credit reviews unchanged. Its terms say automated output reaches users without company review, so analyst verification and exception handling could absorb the time saved. With comparable models and ample capital, incumbent municipal data providers could plausibly add meeting coverage to their existing products. Oasive depends on public recordings that can disappear and licensed data that providers can suspend. That weakens the case for durable control and leaves Path A without a separately supported bottleneck. The memo establishes neither that verification erases the savings nor that buyers would readily switch under parity. Both Path B conditions remain unresolved, which supports Watch at seed. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent municipal desks repeatedly pay a viable team price to monitor borrowers they previously left uncovered when they can also use a credible alternative with comparable model capabilities? A paid head-to-head pilot should compare both options with the desks’ manual workflow at the same credit-review standard, counting analyst verification, retries, and exceptions. Material labor savings and repeat purchases of Oasive despite the alternative would justify rescoring for Reach out. If verification erases the savings or desks readily switch at parity, Pass.
