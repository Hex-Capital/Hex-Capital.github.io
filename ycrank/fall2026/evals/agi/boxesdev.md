# Boxes.dev -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** As cheaper coding agents make parallel work more practical, the potential bottleneck is getting each agent a ready-to-run copy of a team’s development environment. The memo describes local worktrees sharing CPU, memory, and ports and losing continuity when a laptop sleeps. Boxes.dev says it snapshots a configured Linux environment and forks isolated machines that run customers’ own Claude Code or Codex. A seed-stage benchmark could establish whether this materially reduces setup and recovery time at the same rate of successful task completion. The memo provides no such comparison. Path B remains unproven because the memo does not establish a newly viable class or frequency of paid work after accounting for human review and retries. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

The memo records 10,611 standalone CLI downloads in one week, a signal of interest. The founders report one customer running more than 200 agents across more than 100 machines for a build. Payment and repeat use are unknown. The San Francisco YC company is a plausible seed fit. The founders previously built engineering systems together at Gem and have publicly shipped Boxes.dev. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Founders & Team*)

**Scale scenario.** Seats and awake box-hours are identifiable expansion units. At the listed $0.40 overage price, each additional billed hour contributes only if its incremental compute, storage, networking, and support costs sum to less than $0.40. Actual costs, utilization, and repeat paid expansion are unknown. (*The Idea*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

With ample capital and access to the same models, rivals could reproduce the workflow. Replicas already runs native agents in isolated VMs, Coder offers customer-hosted workspaces, and OpenAI’s acquisition of Ona creates a path to bundle workspaces into Codex. Versioned templates could make switching costly, but the memo identifies no structural barrier or measured retention advantage. Anthropic’s hosting and authentication terms also constrain one route to Claude Code. These facts leave the control gate unresolved. A seed-stage comparison could still establish that buyers value Boxes.dev’s environment replication enough to keep paying for it. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent engineering teams renew at a price that covers delivery costs after comparing Boxes.dev with their best alternative on the same repositories, agents, and representative tasks? A paid trial that shows materially less setup and recovery time at comparable task success, followed by renewals attributable to that advantage, would support Reach out if the updated score clears 65 with at least medium confidence.
