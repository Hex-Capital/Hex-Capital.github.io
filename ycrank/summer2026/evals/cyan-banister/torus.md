# Torus -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. An engineering specification changes, and every dependent drawing, datasheet, approval, and procurement instruction updates as one traceable system before anyone pours concrete or orders equipment. Torus has found a credible leak from that future: a working product extracts parameters, maps relationships, performs more than 50 consistency checks, and drafts the resulting workflow. But the dossier does not map the intervening transitions from today’s document checker to that living engineering graph. It names multimodal models and rising AEC adoption, not the proprietary asset or sequence of unlocks that closes the distance.

The present wound is concrete but only partially measured. Cross-document inconsistency can create a $500,000 rework order, while engineers currently reconcile thousands of specifications, drawings, and communications. Torus could return time and decision control to those engineers by exposing a mismatch before it becomes steel, labor, and delay. Yet the $500,000 figure is illustrative, not a documented customer loss avoided, and the dossier never identifies who captures the existing rent or quantifies the review labor being displaced. This is recognizable friction, not yet decoded system economics.

Marcus Lima and Rahul Thayil are the strongest part of the bet. Lima studied energy systems, worked in consulting, and previously built direct-air-capture company Heimdal through $9.7 million of funding. Thayil built AI risk-intelligence company Orion through Techstars and a $3.5 million round. That is complementary operating tuition in capital infrastructure and enterprise AI, not two people who discovered industrial software during YC. A five-person team, three active engineering searches, and deployments with major EPCs also show an ability to recruit and enter difficult institutions. What remains missing is the dated pre-Torus artifact—the obsessive prototype, customer wound, or parameter system they built before deciding there was a company here.

The bull case stings because multiple Fortune 500 companies and major EPCs are already working with Torus on data-center and energy infrastructure. At pre-seed, getting sensitive project documents into a functioning system can be more informative than small reported revenue. It suggests users are spending political capital and integration effort, not merely joining a waitlist. But the claims have no named customers, contract values, deployment counts, repeat-use cohorts, or documented catches. Founder-reported deployments therefore reach the ceiling for unverified behavior; the known pre-revenue exception does not activate without signed proof, a testable artifact, or unusually deep evidence that users have reorganized work around the product.

The unstated-pattern fit is partial. This is exactly the kind of unglamorous workflow repair I like, and isolated tenants plus sovereign deployments acknowledge that industrial customers will not casually surrender their documents. It is not yet the concealed enabling layer beneath the visible AI wave. Torus discloses no controlled corpus, proprietary technical bottleneck, retention advantage, or structural barrier, while Trunk Tools, Transcend, Augmenta, and SWAPP have collectively raised more than $147 million across adjacent document and design workflows. The parameter graph might become infrastructure; today that is an inferred future moat, not something Torus controls.

I am Neutral rather than writing the check. No leadership shell game is visible—Lima is clearly identified as CEO—and there is no evidence the founders dismiss incumbent complexity. The live red flag is data custody: SOC 2 Type I and II remain in progress while the product ingests safety- and cost-sensitive engineering packages. My base case is a useful enterprise quality-assurance product whose repeat-founder team can sell pilots, but not yet a defining engineering substrate. I would flip to Invest if one named EPC documents a paid deployment across multiple active projects that catches at least $1 million of otherwise-issued rework and then renews or expands after twelve months.
