# Reific -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Reific’s strongest path. A frontier lab or AI training platform could buy Reific to secure a recurring supply of hard-to-source operating records, along with the timed replay and grading system that turns those records into training tasks. The memo documents Reific’s public launch and Mercor’s acquisition of Deeptune for enterprise software simulations, an adjacent purchase with no disclosed price. Reific reports that one open model’s full-solve rate on unseen tasks rose from 25.6% to 39.5%. That result gives a buyer a reason to test the product. The ownership case depends on whether exclusive access to fresh records produces repeatable gains that the buyer cannot obtain through a license or its own data. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A paid production pilot that reproduces the gain on fresh records is a plausible seed milestone. The public launch gives Reific a starting point, while the reported $125,000 seed entry leaves pilot funding and runway unclear. The memo describes supplier licensing and privacy checks, with assignment rights, signed exclusivity, and investor preferences unverified. It supplies no usable sale-price evidence or separately supported fallback that would pay this holder. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** Use the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million gross cash, or 5×. Under a simple pro rata waterfall, the hurdle is $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%, $125 million at 2%, and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, no other price adjustments or preference deductions, and all equity consideration paid in cash at close. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. The prices and timing are scenario assumptions.

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Holder cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|-----------|--------|------------------------------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| Modest sale | Year 4 | $40M to $40M | $1.2M | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100M to $100M | $3.0M | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No sale or payout | $0, shares retained | 0× realized |

The strong sale clears the hurdle at 3% and yields $2.0 million, or 4×, at 2%. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and Reific reinvests its cash. The memo gives no basis to underwrite the strong sale price today.

### Countercase

A lab with its own records could license Reific’s environments or use open tooling to build timed tasks. Reific’s supplier intake asks about exclusivity, and the memo documents no secured exclusive rights. Reific also reports that a test model reconstructed personal details from de-identified records, adding friction to the release of fresh data. If a license or internal build delivers comparable gains, the buyer’s reason to pay an acquisition premium falls away. The memo leaves that substitution test unresolved, so the structural Pass override does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production pilot show that owning Reific’s exclusive, transferable rights to a recurring record stream creates enough incremental value over licensing or building internally to support the sale hurdle? Move to Reach out if signed supplier terms establish those rights and buyer-validated pilot economics support roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. Stay at Watch while that evidence is pending. Move to Pass if the pilot shows that a readily available substitute delivers comparable results and caps buyer economics below the hurdle.
