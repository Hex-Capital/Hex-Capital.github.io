# Keres -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with its initial military buyer, Keres has a plausible path to buyers abroad. A UK purchase of a competing interceptor shows that another government pays to address the same recurring defense need. The core Garmur platform could be reusable across markets, but the memo leaves the scope of buyer-specific changes uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The U.S. defense accelerator award establishes a route to acquisition officials. The likely path to a first purchase still involves buyer-led evaluation and procurement. The memo gives no usable evidence of realized sales, paying-customer growth, or confirmed pre-revenue status. It also gives no evidence of recurring billing or repeat spending by existing buyers. Initial traction and retention economics therefore use their absent-evidence defaults. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

Keres claims Garmur can return for reuse. Successful reuse could improve the buyer’s economics, and the memo documents no completed demonstration of that claim. Under the inferred hardware-sales model, Keres’s incremental contribution would be the realized sale price less the costs of building, selling, delivering, and supporting the next unit, including any capacity increase required to supply it. The memo gives no usable inputs for that calculation, so cost economics uses its absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Even with strong initial fit, repeated buyer-specific evaluations and deployment work could make each sale slow and expensive. The accelerator award has yet to show a verified conversion to procurement, and the memo gives no basis for judging whether contribution improves as volume grows. The structural downside gate remains open because the memo does not establish that substantial market-specific rebuilding or uneconomic per-buyer work is unavoidable. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a defense buyer in a second market complete a paid purchase of substantially the same product with bounded buyer-specific work? A completed delivery with documented purchase terms, implementation effort, and attributable costs would answer it. Evidence that substantial rebuilding is unavoidable would move the action to Pass. A repeatable sale would justify rescoring, with Reach out contingent on clearing the score and confidence thresholds.
