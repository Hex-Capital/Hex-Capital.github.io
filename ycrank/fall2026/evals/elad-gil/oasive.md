# Oasive -- Elad Gil Evaluation

AI-ready financial data is making automated research easier to build; municipal credit desks become an attractive market if Oasive can turn fragmented borrower documents into research analysts trust enough to stop assembling themselves. The controlling variable is **analyst time eliminated after verification**. Source-linked numbers help, but the dossier establishes neither the time saved nor the accuracy threshold at which a desk changes its process. That leaves the central market proposition unproven.

The strongest argument for investing is that Oasive already reports use across **15 institutional desks**, including large asset managers, banks, and corporate treasury teams. That is meaningful access for a company this early. It could also create distribution: sell municipal research through relationships established by MBS and rates products. However, the September 24, 2026 announcement concerns those existing products; municipal research still has an early-access form. I give the desk count credit as company-reported adoption, with a discount for undisclosed payment and repeat usage. The **$200/user/month** rates offering and advertised paid MBS pilots establish commercial packaging, not realized revenue.

The economics could move in either direction. DPC DATA’s **March 2026 AI-ready municipal datasets** lower the difficulty of building automated research, but they also lower it for competing vendors and institutional engineering teams. Oasive has not documented a reduction in research cost, an improvement in coverage, or a margin advantage attributable to its technology. Existing suppliers already cover standardized financials, issuer news, and AI-generated credit memos. This market is crystallizing around established data access and analyst workflows; aggregation alone gives me little reason to expect a new company to capture the economics.

Oasive’s sector-specific pages and links from numerical claims to sources provide useful product substance. Stronger models could improve extraction and coverage. The dossier does not establish that those improvements compound into retained analyst corrections, approval history, integrations, or responsibility for completed credit work. Municipal data exclusivity is also undocumented; the advertised proprietary models concern MBS. This activates my concern about reproducible data products, although the evidence does not establish that Oasive is already being commoditized. Howland’s Wells Fargo, Google Treasury, and AI experience makes the bull case more credible, but her previous venture supplies no documented startup lessons or costly commitment that would raise the founder criterion above its default.

I would pass with my own money today. The substantive opposing case is that an experienced fixed-income operator has already reached institutional desks and can use that channel to make municipal research a recurring purchase. That possibility deserves attention. It does not yet satisfy my market gate, and the dossier supplies neither frontier-research evidence nor a demonstrated economic breakthrough supporting an exception. **My flip condition is a documented municipal deployment cohort in which at least five institutional desks renew paid contracts after six months and measured analyst time, including verification, falls by at least 50%.** That would connect distribution, customer value, and recurring demand around the variable that matters.
