# Graphify Labs -- Paul Graham Evaluation

98,805 GitHub stars and 4M-plus PyPI downloads make Graphify Labs a hacker artifact with distribution, but not yet a schlep business. That is Graphify Labs’s signature: overwhelmingly strong hacker genealogy paired with a weakly demonstrated appetite for unpleasant enterprise work. Safi Shamsi did not arrive with a market slide; he arrived with 36 tree-sitter grammars, support for 17 coding assistants, and production use at Rootly, Geotab, and Tweddle Group. The code has passed the hacker test. The enterprise business has not yet passed the Coinbase test.

**Stripe** (2010) is the high rung for hacker genealogy: Patrick Collison’s Lisp work and Auctomatic preceded the payments API, so Stripe was another artifact in a long trail of hacking. Graphify Labs sits just below that rung. Safi Shamsi has knowledge-graph research, graph-based disinformation work at Valent Projects, and a 98,805-star repository; Graphify Labs therefore has more technical evidence than nearly any pre-seed dossier. The divergence is that Stripe’s genealogy included multiple companies and years of commerce hacking, while Graphify Labs supplies one astonishing artifact but little evidence of a longer public trail beyond Safi’s research.

**Kiko** (2005) is the low rung for schlep: a clever pure-software calendar whose difficulty did not force a durable operational advantage, and whose founders became more interesting only after failure. Graphify Labs currently shows some of that pattern. Parsing 36 languages is hard, but Graphify Labs’s Apache-2.0 core is inspectable and reusable, while formal verification, Jira integration, SSO, RBAC, and self-hosting remain in early access. Those enterprise chores could become the schlep, but Graphify Labs has not yet shown founders personally enduring security reviews, broken customer graphs, procurement, or deployment inside hostile legacy repositories. A schlep described on a pricing page is not yet a schlep survived.

4M-plus PyPI downloads in under four months compound Graphify Labs’s resourcefulness because each new user can generate integrations, bug reports, and contributor pull requests without paid acquisition. Graphify Labs already has 71 contributors, 9,579 forks, and engineers using it in production. Over the next 18 months, that loop either turns Safi Shamsi into the maintainer every coding agent depends on or turns the repository into a famous free library with no company attached. The speed and scale of the artifact pass my animal test decisively. This is the same hierarchy that made **Loopt** (2005) a known exception: an unusually forceful founder can outweigh an immature business, though not forever.

Safi Shamsi’s MSc thesis on knowledge-graph-powered RAG places Graphify Labs near the problem, but Graphify Labs provides no evidence that Safi personally suffered repeated code-search failures and built the engine for his own daily use. I therefore apply the organic-idea absent-evidence default. The score would jump a tier if Graphify Labs could show that the first version began as Safi’s tool at Valent Projects, or that Safi maintained a large codebase whose failures directly produced the provenance model. Academic and employment proximity prove expertise. They do not prove the Dropbox-style forgotten USB stick.

Graphify Labs lists two people, but Graphify Labs publicly names only Safi Shamsi and provides no evidence about the other co-founder or their prior working relationship. **Airbnb** (2009) had three founders whose complementary abilities became visible through the cereal-box stunt and the work of photographing hosts’ apartments. Graphify Labs gets the structural benefit of a pair, but not the relationship signal: no shared project, years together, or division of technical and operational responsibility is documented. The missing biography is not fatal at pre-seed, though it leaves one of my explicit red flags unresolved rather than cleared.

Graphify Labs will reach roughly $8M–$15M in ARR within 36 months as an independent enterprise code-verification business, with the free graph engine remaining the distribution layer. Graphify Labs can get there if even a small fraction of 4M-plus downloads convert into self-hosted deployments with RBAC, Jira integration, and auditable provenance. The mechanism is not the graph alone. It is the transition from “find relevant code” to “prove why an autonomous agent’s change is safe,” where verification histories and organization-specific graphs become harder to replace than an Apache-licensed parser.

Sourcegraph’s $223M, Qodo’s $120M, and Greptile’s $30M make the strongest bear case: Graphify Labs may have accidentally built free customer acquisition for better-capitalized vendors. Graphify Labs has no paid tier, no disclosed revenue, no proprietary source-code barrier, and ambiguous or inferred graph edges can undermine the verification promise precisely where enterprises require certainty. That objection stings, but 98,805 stars in four months, 4M-plus downloads, and production use at three named companies are animal tracks, not a glossy AI-wrapper pitch. I would invest because the founder signal wins today. If Graphify Labs cannot sign at least three enterprises worth $100,000 or more in annual recurring revenue each within 12 months, I would flip to Pass: that would show the schlep is being admired rather than undertaken.
