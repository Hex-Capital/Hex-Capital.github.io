# Audun -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is too small and morally misdirected. Audun may reduce collection labor and make repayment conversations less scripted, but the dossier quantifies only a $5.98 billion software market—not the labor cost displaced, the population helped, or an order-of-magnitude reduction in debtor harm. This is workflow automation inside debt collection, a category I explicitly avoid funding with my own money. A large TAM adjective cannot turn an incremental ambition into a societal option.

Technically, the product is reminders, multichannel negotiation, payment plans, recordkeeping, and human escalation. AgentCollect, Altur, and Domu already offer overlapping calls, messages, negotiations, or plans. Audun supplies no benchmark for recovery rate, cost per account, complaint rate, or response latency; no proprietary architecture; and no case volume feeding the inferred optimization loop. This looks like implementation competition around available AI, not a 5–10x discontinuity against a binding incumbent constraint.

The strongest bull case is the team. Peter Flo brings Midjourney and machine-learning experience; Jacob Hambro and Magnus Njålstad Vonen bring McKinsey-style enterprise operating capacity; Henrik Brun Fevang adds data-science experience. Four people spanning technical work and sales could evolve an incremental collection wedge into broader financial infrastructure, while Norway’s new Debt Collection Act and GDPR controls might reward a system built for auditability and human escalation. If usage created a proprietary map from debtor circumstances and negotiation terms to repayment outcomes, the mundane wedge could become a learning platform.

But none of that has happened. The debtor portal is still “coming soon,” revenue is undisclosed, no customer or repeated usage is identified, and no founder has disclosed debt-collection experience. More importantly, the team has not shown belief revision, a difficult risk-specific recruit, or evidence that it understands debtor vulnerability beyond product copy. Strong résumés are not the same as a demonstrated learning rate.

The largest kill risk is regulatory permission and conduct, not model fluency. Audun was registered June 8, 2026, but its Finanstilsynet licence could not be verified; the replacement Norwegian statute has no stated commencement date; and automated payment decisions may trigger GDPR restrictions. Yet the dossier gives no licence milestone, supervised pilot, pass/fail threshold, experiment cost, or stop branch. Capital would therefore fund product and direct sales before proving the right to operate. The unit economics are equally untested: no pricing, recovery fee, servicing cost, human-escalation rate, or comparison with incumbent collection economics is disclosed.

My 36-month base case is that Audun either closes or pivots from third-party debt collection into creditor-controlled accounts-receivable automation for Nordic businesses, remaining below $3 million ARR. The one condition that flips my decision is a regulator-approved pilot with a named creditor demonstrating at least a 5x reduction in fully loaded collection cost while improving repayment rates and reducing verified hardship complaints. Until then, this is a capable team applying commodity automation to a category I do not want to accelerate. Strong pass.
