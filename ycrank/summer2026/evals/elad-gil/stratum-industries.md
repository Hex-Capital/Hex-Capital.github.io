# Stratum Industries -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The FDA’s December 2025 deployment of agentic AI across its workforce makes **Stratum Industries** a catalyst-rich, curve-free government workflow bet: market re-opening is Stratum Industries’s strongest dimension, while revenue velocity is its weakest. Federal generative-AI use cases increased ninefold from 2023 to 2024, so the window is real; no customer, pilot, processing volume, or revenue is disclosed, so whether Stratum Industries is “just working” remains unknown.

**Anduril** is the high rung for catalyst-driven market re-opening: Ukraine changed defense procurement, and Anduril had the positioning to capture that change. Stratum Industries shares the load-bearing mechanism—a previously resistant government market adopting AI after a specific institutional shift—but diverges because the FDA’s deployment validates agency appetite, not Stratum Industries’s wedge. Anduril already had contracts and founders with Palantir and Oculus distribution context; Stratum Industries has no disclosed agency deployment, and neither Raj nor Vihaan Jagiasi has government procurement experience.

**Mistral** entered with the missing-revenue-curve failure mechanism I normally reject, but it qualified as a Known Exception because the post-ChatGPT catalyst had already occurred and its founders had shipped Llama at Meta. Stratum Industries also discloses no revenue or usage curve, but Stratum Industries lacks Mistral’s category-defining technical pedigree or an equivalent exception: a YC launch and requests for introductions to adjudication leaders are not buying behavior. In AI, silence on growth is a negative signal even at pre-seed when direct competitor ClearOS already markets nearly the same intake-and-adjudication workflow.

Vihaan Jagiasi was founding engineer number three at Cadastral before its acquisition by Legora, while Raj Jagiasi averaged 220% of quota at Deel and ranked among more than 1,000 representatives. That pairing can compound over the next 18 months: Vihaan can compress agency feedback into product iterations while Raj navigates founder-led sales, giving Stratum Industries more shipping credibility than a credential-only founding pair. Stratum Industries has not disclosed a prototype iteration, repeat user, or agency feedback loop, however, so the Perplexity-style “show up next week with it built” signal remains inferred rather than demonstrated.

Salesforce already offers FedRAMP-authorized public-sector agents, which means Stratum Industries currently fights for share rather than functioning as an index on government AI adoption. The index score flips into the Invest range if Stratum Industries becomes the neutral adjudication layer spanning multiple agency systems—owning rubrics, cited decision histories, and outcome audits—rather than an application inside an incumbent stack. Stratum Industries has disclosed none of those accumulated assets, while GovWell already serves more than 130 municipalities and counties across 34 states.

**Color Genomics** worked because the hard capability—commodity sequencing—already existed, leaving workflow and distribution as the single miracle. Stratum Industries similarly builds workflow above validated foundation models, but consequential adjudication adds independent dependencies: model accuracy, sensitive-data security, agency procurement, and defensible integration. Human approval and cited summaries reduce the accuracy risk, yet Stratum Industries discloses no security certification or deployment architecture, making the architecture closer to three coupled miracles than one.

ClearOS’s direct overlap makes my 36-month forecast specific: by July 2029, **Stratum Industries** will have narrowed from “all government backlogs” to one state or municipal permitting or licensing vertical and reached $1 million–$3 million ARR, but Stratum Industries will not yet be a horizontal federal adjudication platform or an acquisition target for Salesforce. Long procurement cycles force focus, and the strongest plausible asset is a narrow corpus of agency rubrics and audited decisions rather than a universal agent.

GovWell’s $34.5 million raised and 130-plus government customers form the strongest bull case: government software is re-opening, Raj has exceptional enterprise-sales performance, and Vihaan has already shipped inside an acquired startup. The Anduril exception could apply if Stratum Industries converts the catalyst into contracts faster than incumbents can adapt. The evidence that wins today is the absence of even one named pilot alongside direct ClearOS and Salesforce overlap; I would not write the check yet. Three paid agencies, repeat case volume, and greater than 20% monthly revenue growth sustained for four months would flip Stratum Industries to Invest.
