# Mirrors -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Mirrors has a reported $500,000 YC investment, but I cannot price my check from that number. Proposed post-money: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. Whether my money would enable something otherwise unfundable: undisclosed. Those are missing facts, not evidence of an auction. They also leave my two largest underwriting questions unanswered: what am I buying, and what specific truth does this round pay to discover?

The product addresses a real operating problem. Engineers accumulate prompt exceptions after inspecting failed agent traces; Mirrors makes those corrections conditional, reviewable, and deliverable at runtime. That could turn recurring engineering labor into a useful business. But the [SDK documentation](https://mirrors.dev/docs/sdk/reference) distinguishes an accepted server decision from context actually being applied or the agent succeeding. That distinction is the economic question. Does inserting Mirrors produce enough additional successful work to justify another vendor in the execution path, including its documented 1,000-millisecond context-wait budget? The dossier contains no funded experiment with a deadline and measurable answer. I will not invent one and credit the company for it.

The strongest bull case is that the review workflow becomes more valuable as customers accumulate corrections. Previewing matches, approving guidance, and controlling rollout could embed Mirrors inside engineering operations even when the underlying models improve. This fits my interest in translation layers: engineers retain control while agents receive situational instructions. Aryan Singhal also brings relevant substance—ML research, language-model evaluation work, and a previously built GPU-optimization project, Profine. That earns some founder credit beyond academic titles. What remains unproven is the other side of the bridge: operating customer-facing agents and getting engineering organizations to purchase this workflow.

The [711,933 agent sessions](https://mirrors.dev/) are where I stop the bull case from outrunning the evidence. There is no measurement period, customer breakdown, or allocation between the older replay product and current context-delivery product. That is an activity counter, not demonstrated purchase causality. Customer-specific guidance might become a proprietary dark pool, but the dossier does not establish that customers retain it, depend on it, or pay because of it. Meanwhile, framework vendors and internal platform teams could reproduce conditional delivery. My AI substitution concern therefore applies, although Mirrors’ reviewed corrections and disabling controls give me no basis to accuse it of assuming agent errors will disappear.

I checked the reasons I sometimes override missing financing gates: exceptional paid conversion and retention, hard external demand, or an established operator paired with a financing structure that avoids repeated fundraising. Mirrors supplies none of those receipts yet. Its custom pricing establishes a sales conversation, not revenue or cash economics; missing margins and burn receive the stated default rather than an invented penalty. I also cannot infer an equal-founder governance problem from a two-person team. The decisive issue is simpler: useful technical work has not yet established owned purchasing access or a business that survives without the next check.

I pass on the evidence available. One verifiable condition would reopen underwriting: collected recurring cash from renewing production customers covers Mirrors’ operating burn on its existing capital. That would demonstrate customers repeatedly value the workflow and give the company financing independence. Today, the dossier establishes a credible product direction and relevant builder experience. It does not establish why my personal capital belongs here.
