# Raven -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Raven’s strongest exit route is a strategic sale to a handset maker seeking to add cross-app task delegation to its phones. The prototype demonstrates why a buyer might care. Users can hand off tasks such as flight search or calendar changes from the phone. A handset maker could buy Raven to accelerate that work if the product delivers measurable gains over its existing assistant. The memo shows no measured buyer benefit, buyer interest, or comparable acquisition price. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The prototype won a People’s Choice award with $50,000 in API credits. Raven reports that early adopters received test handsets and that more than 1,000 people contacted the company after its prototype debut. Published pre-order terms make the $199 payment refundable before shipment. A production pilot measuring repeat use, task completion, and time saved is a plausible next milestone. Its cost and timing remain open because Raven has yet to publish final hardware specifications or a commercial ship date. The memo also leaves IP ownership, contract assignability, and investor participation rights unverified. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. Both sale scenarios assume an eligible holder sells its full stake for cash within eight years, $10 million in combined debt and deal deductions, and a pro rata distribution with no senior preferences.

- **Modest sale.** $50 million in enterprise value leaves $40 million in distributable equity and $1.2 million in holder cash, or 2.4×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value leaves $90 million in distributable equity and $2.7 million in holder cash, or 5.4×.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with reinvested cash and no eligible share sale or payout yields $0 in realized holder proceeds.

The sale prices are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no basis for underwriting either price or an independent liquidity route. Watch fits the specific gap in buyer economics. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A handset maker can meet much of the same need through its current products. Galaxy S26 already delegates supported multi-step tasks, and Phone Harness gives agents control of existing phones. Raven has no demonstrated retention or workflow advantage that would make buying it preferable to extending those capabilities. That substitution risk weakens the strategic case. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported sale-price range that would leave the holder materially short, so the mandatory Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a handset maker’s business case, grounded in a production pilot against its existing assistant, justify enough cash at closing to clear the holder’s $2.5 million hurdle? A buyer’s valuation range tied to sustained workflow gains would move the action to Reach out if it supports at least $83.3 million in distributable equity under the benchmark assumptions.
