# Veeza AI -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a visa processor is the strongest path. Veeza’s published appointment tracker and its Cairo booking workflow with Arabic and English WhatsApp support could give a processor a way to serve Egyptian applicants and reduce manual work. Ownership could justify a premium if Veeza’s workflow adapts to portal changes and completes paid applications at a lower cost than the processor could achieve by building or partnering. That advantage remains an inference. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*)

The founder reports five figures in monthly cash revenue in USD, and the integration partner reports that usage tripled after WhatsApp integration. Those signals support testing whether Veeza can sustain a lower cost per completed paid application across multiple visa centres after human review, support, and refunds. The memo leaves margins, burn, runway, and the capital needed for that test unknown. It also leaves IP ownership, applicant data transfer rights, and continuity of portal access under new ownership unverified. No independent fallback has usable support for paying this holder. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** The memo supplies no investment terms or usable acquisition pricing evidence. Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× gross cash objective requires $2.5 million to the investor, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%.

For both sale scenarios, assume zero net debt and no other price adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. Assume a simple pro rata waterfall with no preference adjustments. All consideration is cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and separate compensation are each $0. Prices and timing are scenario assumptions.

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value → distributable equity | Investor cash | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $40M → $40M | $1.2M | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100M → $100M | $3.0M | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No transaction or payout | $0 | 0× |

The strong sale clears the hurdle at 3% ownership and yields $2.0 million at 2%. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and Veeza reinvests its cash. The investor still holds the shares.

### Countercase

A processor could build with commercially available browser automation and its existing review operations, or get local coverage through a partnership. Atlys already publishes appointment availability in India, and consulates and visa centres control the slots that Veeza can book. Scarcity can delay fulfillment and trigger full refunds, limiting the value a buyer could capture from better automation. These facts make a substitute plausible, but the memo does not establish equivalent performance in Egypt, a structural price ceiling, or a probable prolonged-private outcome. The Pass override does not apply. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)
