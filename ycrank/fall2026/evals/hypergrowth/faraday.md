# Faraday -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales with account expansion.** The initial buyer is an operations team at a manufacturer or distributor that repeatedly reconciles orders, inventory, and exceptions across disconnected systems. Faraday offers demos on prospects’ own records and has solicited introductions to industrial operators. The company reports one unnamed customer deployment. Whether that customer came through the demo process, paid, or kept using the product is unknown. That is a concrete implementation signal at this stage. Buyer commitment and product-market fit remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Growth scenario.** A prospect-data demo could lead to a paid first workflow, then expand into quoting, inventory, or purchasing on the same runtime. The scenario assumes that connectors and workflow templates carry over across accounts, and that employee corrections reduce ongoing intervention. The same buyer problem plausibly recurs abroad, but the memo provides no second-market buyer or localization test. Serving that buyer would test whether Faraday can reuse its connectors and adapt local workflow rules and authorization controls within the same product. There is no evidence of repeat use or account expansion yet. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Faraday could scale as an integration-heavy services business. The company reports that lengthy remote-desktop exports blocked its disclosed deployment until it switched to a third-party sandbox. Its reported benchmark accuracy rose from 38% to 94% with the same underlying model, while live reliability still requires validation against each customer’s rules and exceptions. A new account can add integration and validation labor. Each additional workload can add compute, monitoring, and exception handling, and write-enabled workflows require separate authorization and agreed controls. The deployment blocker weighs against repeatable onboarding. The memo gives no rollout cost breakdown and does not establish that these costs grow proportionally with volume, so the structural Pass gate is unmet. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent manufacturer or distributor in a second geography go from a prospect-data demo to paid, recurring use of the same workflow through a reusable rollout? A paid order, usage across the buyer’s normal operating cycles, and a rollout record showing connector reuse and the time spent on integration, validation, and support would answer it. A successful rollout with bounded delivery effort would move the action to Reach out. If the test remains inconclusive, stay on Watch.
