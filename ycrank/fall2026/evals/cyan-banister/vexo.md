# Vexo -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move six years forward: my assistant remembers a promise I made over lunch and helps me keep it without making me reconstruct the conversation. Vexo has a small artifact from that world: searchable conversational memory exposed to authorized agents through MCP. But getting there requires reliable capture, useful retrieval, and permissioned execution. The documented interface cannot write, send, or delete, while spoken-request execution remains “in development.” That distinction matters. Vexo has evidence for the memory connection; “acts before you ask” still describes the destination.

The founders make me take that destination seriously. Malik’s Tesla and K-Scale firmware experience, Kiani’s mechatronics background, and Narra’s prior wearable-AI work fit the physical job. Malik’s public Rust sensor driver is something I can inspect. Malik and Narra also built Offline together before this company, giving me evidence of a working relationship beyond matching résumés. Those are meaningful first-believer signals. They do not yet establish a sustained obsession with conversational memory. The earlier health-ring description makes me curious about how the idea evolved; an undisclosed transition date does not establish an opportunistic pivot.

Why must I keep typing my life into every assistant? That is an irritating system worth repairing, and it fits my pattern of finding opportunity in ordinary operating friction. But Vexo has not identified a gatekeeper collecting a measurable rent or demonstrated how much control its users recover. Its memory interface also resembles the concealed infrastructure I like beneath a consumer product. The missing piece is a scarce asset it controls: accumulated history might create one, but switching costs are inferred, and Omi already publishes hardware, software, APIs, and an MCP interface. Connecting memory to agents establishes a useful stack position; it does not yet establish ownership of the bottleneck.

The strongest argument against passing is that this particular team could make continuous capture comfortable enough to become habitual. Embedded engineering matters when something must live on a wrist all day. I would not demand revenue before believing that: **CosmicBrain AI** justified a pre-revenue hardware exception through a functioning prototype, signed industrial proofs of concept, and a founder’s longstanding robotics record. Vexo shares relevant technical experience, but the dossier does not establish comparable external commitment. A free app with one rating and a $199 preorder offer do not show that people repeatedly wear the bracelet, retrieve memories, or change their behavior. December 2 shipping is an estimate, not delivered-user evidence.

Then there is everyone else at lunch. Local transcription, account deletion, and revocable sharing are substantive safeguards, but they do not establish how bystanders consent to capture. I cannot conclude that Vexo has severe privacy failures; I also cannot treat account-owner controls as proof that this gate is cleared. The dossier identifies a CEO, CTO, and CPO and supplies no affirmative governance red flag. My pass rests on the distance between a technically credible memory product and a trusted habit worth wearing. One result would change my decision: a documented 60-day pilot in which 20 non-team users repeatedly complete useful, permissioned actions from captured conversations, with independently verified bystander-consent handling. That would show the future arriving through behavior.
