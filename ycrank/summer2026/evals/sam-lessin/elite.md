# Elite -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I first need three numbers: post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and the size of the first scarce check. Elite supplies none. That does not prove a Demo Day auction, but it removes the evidence I need to distinguish a cheap non-consensus bet from another fashionable healthcare-AI allocation. The company is entering a category where Assort Health, Hyro, and Syllable have already raised more than $81 million each. The thesis may still be right; it is plainly no longer undiscovered.

The more important missing sentence is what the round proves. Protocols for 18 specialties and write-back to more than 19 EHRs are meaningful implementation work, but “build integrations and sell to practices” is a program, not a finite experiment. I would want one specialty, one workflow, one measurable result, and one date—for example, whether Elite can resolve a defined share of refill requests without a clinically material error by March 2027. Without that card flip, the capital finances simultaneous product, safety, integration, and distribution risks.

The strongest bull case is hiding in the product architecture. Outcome-based pricing aligns Elite with completed work rather than minutes or seats; audit logs, escalation, BAAs, and EHR write-back suggest the team understands that healthcare automation must execute actions, not merely produce convincing speech. Vihaan Jagiasi also shipped as an early engineer at Cadastral before its acquisition, while Raj Jagiasi brings GTM experience from Deel. A two-person team assembling this breadth at pre-seed could be moving faster than the sparse public traction record shows.

But the dossier documents no practice customer, paid interaction, repeat usage, proprietary dataset, or conversion caused by an owned channel. A public voice demo and booking form are rented-distribution beginnings, not a dark pool. The founders also moved from a government application-review product launched July 29, 2026, to a healthcare HIPAA notice effective August 22. That speed can be productive, but without healthcare-operating receipts it makes the 18-specialty breadth look more like horizontal model capability than accumulated workflow ownership.

Delete the next financing and the model becomes impossible to underwrite. Per-resolved-interaction pricing could support attractive economics, but Elite provides no price, resolution cost, gross margin, burn, runway, or customer volume. Clinical triage and EHR actions create tail-risk costs that may not appear in a demo, while better-funded competitors can subsidize integrations and compliance. The AI is not yet an option sitting on top of a working base business; publicly, it is the business.

I am passing at this evidence level. The verifiable flip condition is a signed, paid deployment in one specialty showing at least 90 days of retained usage, positive contribution margin per resolved interaction, and a measured reduction in unanswered calls or staff workload without a material safety incident. That would convert the integration breadth from an impressive product claim into proprietary workflow evidence—and reveal whether this is a real operating system or another agent layer waiting for the next model release.
