# Agent FM -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: coding agents are becoming parallel, long-running workers; engineers now need supervision rather than another code generator; and the controlling variable is whether simultaneous sessions per engineer rise enough that a neutral command layer becomes necessary rather than merely convenient. OpenAI reported Codex usage doubling from mid-December 2025 to February 2026 and exceeding one million developers, so the underlying wave is real. Agent FM has not established the threshold, however: there is no evidence that developers running two, five, or ten agents will pay for ambient supervision, or that supervision becomes a separate market rather than a feature of the agent platforms.

The strongest bull case is cross-vendor coverage. Agent FM passively observes Claude Code, Codex, and OpenCode without proxying them, then consolidates blockers, decisions, token usage, and tool errors into audio and chat. That architecture may avoid some compatibility problems that caused Omnara to archive an earlier Claude Code wrapper. It also fits the pattern of buying coverage across a discontinuity before the winning layer is obvious. If developers continuously mix models and agent tools, no single upstream vendor has the same incentive to provide a neutral operating surface.

The counterfactual is harsher. OpenAI’s Codex app already manages parallel agents and displays progress and decisions; Conductor adds worktrees, review, and merging; Omnara combines remote access with voice. Those products control actions and workflow state, while Agent FM currently narrates and observes them. Better models make its summaries cheaper, but they do not deepen its ownership of the work. An upstream vendor can reproduce audio alerts and session dashboards without recreating a difficult dataset, integration graph, or operational feedback loop. The inferred corpus of cross-session failures is not a moat until its scale, reproduction cost, and measurable learning effect exist.

Demand evidence does not resolve this. The company has 809 LinkedIn followers, macOS and Windows releases, and a self-serve free tier, but no disclosed active-user count, repeat usage, revenue, named team deployment, or paid conversion. Starter at $48 annually and Pro at $144 annually are waitlisted rather than demonstrated price discovery. These are distribution inputs, not product-market pull. At those prices, Agent FM also needs either very large individual volume or a credible move into enterprise engineering operations; session-context transmission to Gemini or OpenAI makes that enterprise path materially harder despite planned Privacy Mode and SSO/SAML.

The founders have relevant proximity to the problem: Aniket Deshpande worked as a Meta software engineer, and the team says it personally experienced the pain of juggling parallel sessions. That supports product intuition but does not establish rare model-building talent, second-time learning, or costly commitment. None of the known exceptions applies. This is not frontier research with scarce technical supply, nor a physical market whose unit economics have been reconstructed. It is an application-layer product in a rapidly crystallizing market where incumbents already possess distribution and adjacent functionality.

I would pass today. The likely outcome is either a pivot toward team-level agent reliability and governance or absorption of the ambient-supervision feature into Codex, Claude Code, or a broader workspace. The single flip condition is verifiable: demonstrate at least 20 paying engineering teams with eight consecutive weeks of weekly usage across two or more agent vendors, where the dashboard or alerts measurably reduce blocked-agent time. That would show Agent FM is becoming a neutral operational workflow rather than an audio interface over session logs.
