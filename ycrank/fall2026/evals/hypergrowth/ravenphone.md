# Raven -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Product-led adoption** is the strongest hypothesis. The initial buyer is a professional who repeatedly switches among email, calendars, and calls to get work done. If Raven reliably completes those tasks from one interface, a product demo could bring the next thousand buyers to direct checkout, and connecting their existing accounts could get them to first value through the same setup. Continued delegation could support paid renewal. Referrals and paid expansion remain untested. (The Idea)

Raven opened direct pre-orders and a free waitlist. It reports more than 1,000 inbound contacts and delivery of its first test handsets. The memo gives no conversion from those channels to paid activation or any evidence of recurring use. The listed $199 pre-order is refundable and covers the first three months after activation. The projected $99–$199 monthly price is unvalidated. Those are useful early signals of interest, with willingness to switch phones and keep paying still open. (The Idea; Market & Traction)

The same app workflow could recur abroad. A second-market rollout would test whether Raven can reuse account setup and agent workflows while adapting cellular service, device fulfillment and returns, and any local language or privacy requirements. Under the proposed membership model, each activation could tie up capital in a handset before renewal, while cellular service, inference, support, and returns add costs as volume grows. The memo provides neither a cross-market buyer test nor measured per-customer costs. (The Idea; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Existing phones already handle some of the same agent tasks, which may leave buyers little reason to switch handsets. Raven is still working with hardware partners and has published neither final specifications nor a commercial ship date. If users can get enough value from their current phones, Raven could incur device and service costs for customers who never renew. The memo leaves both buyer behavior and per-customer costs unresolved, so the structural Pass gate is unmet. (Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a small cohort of independent buyers in a second market come through direct checkout, activate the same recurring work tasks with the same onboarding, and renew at the first post-introductory bill? Observed renewals through that route, with a basic breakdown of per-device and per-subscriber costs, would move the action to Reach out.
