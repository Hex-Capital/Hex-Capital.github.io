# Adalat AI -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Adalat AI has reached a consequential bottleneck: the production of reliable court records. One magistrate reports handling five to six witnesses daily instead of two to three. If that improvement survives independent measurement, the benefit extends beyond cheaper transcription to people spending less time waiting for justice. But faster witness recording does not establish faster case resolution. The dossier quantifies a local capacity gain, not an orders-of-magnitude change in the cost or availability of justice. That distinction limits the consequence score.

The strongest argument for investing is that institutional adoption has already happened. Oxford documents over 4,000 courts across nine states as of March 2026; the company subsequently reports 6,300+ courtrooms across 11 states. Kerala required primary use for witness depositions beginning November 1, 2025. Those are substantial implementation achievements. They fit my revealed preference for teams that navigate difficult institutional permissions and combine disciplines around an expensive workflow. Saxena brings courtroom experience; Bhattacharya brings multilingual NLP experience. Their February 2024 collaboration produced an MVP for court demonstrations. That is purposeful team assembly, although the dossier supplies neither an observed material belief revision nor evidence of unusually difficult recruits won.

Adoption does not yet establish the technical discontinuity. Adalat claims support for over 15 Indian languages and 90%+ recognition accuracy, but supplies no comparable courtroom benchmark against TERES, another transcription system, or manual dictation. The relevant denominator is the cost and time required to produce a **correct, signed deposition**, including corrections. Kerala’s endorsement requirement makes that distinction operationally important. There is insufficient evidence to call the product a generic wrapper; there is also insufficient evidence to award it a measured technical breakthrough.

The September 8, 2026 infrastructure investigation exposes a separate survival risk. Credential-enabled access reached production-cluster administration. Disabling the affected host, rotating credentials, and restricting access are concrete corrective actions, deserving some credit for risk removal. They do not establish that the largest remaining risk has been retired. The dossier provides no costed experiment, numerical acceptance threshold, or independent retest. I would want the next dollars to buy evidence about production security and material transcription errors before underwriting further expansion. The reported team size of 80 does not itself prove premature scaling.

The economic issue is more fundamental than missing revenue. Adalat operates as a nonprofit, received a $500,000 grant, and has documented pro bono court agreements. Free delivery establishes affordability to courts; it does not establish low underlying cost. There is no disclosed cost per completed deposition or path for an angel investor to receive financial returns. Equally, grant funding does not prove permanent subsidy dependence, so I retain the absent-evidence economics score. My exceptions for backing companies before a business model exists require an extraordinary consequence or demonstrated technical trajectory strong enough to override that uncertainty. Adalat’s institutional reach makes that argument credible, but its present evidence does not complete it.

I pass with my own investment capital. The strongest objection is serious: court access, complementary founders, and an administrative mandate may provide the foundation for making scarce judicial capacity substantially more abundant. That could justify philanthropic support today. It does not yet demonstrate the technological and economic leverage I am buying as an investor. One verifiable condition would reopen my decision: an independently audited, court-funded deployment demonstrating at least 5x lower fully loaded cost per signed deposition than manual processing, with no increase in material errors.
