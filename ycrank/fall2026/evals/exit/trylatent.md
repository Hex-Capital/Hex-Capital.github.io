# Latent -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Latent’s strongest path. An inference platform or AI security vendor could buy its in-process vLLM monitoring and deployment-specific calibration to catch more rule violations in customers’ production traffic. The memo records Cisco’s acquisition of Galileo, which establishes precedent for buying a runtime guardrail product. Latent reports stopping 99% of rule-breaking agents versus 69% for a frontier judge in its sandbox. The production benefit and the economics of owning Latent’s product remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The self-serve launch and distributed SDK give Latent a starting point for a paid production pilot. The memo establishes neither paid production adoption nor runway. The reported $125,000 financing is unconfirmed, and ownership of the core IP and transfer rights for customer contracts and calibration data remain unverified. The memo also supplies no evidence of buyout cash flow, eligible secondary demand, IPO readiness, or distributable cash for dividends or buybacks. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target requires $2.5 million gross, or $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership. The hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. With $5 million of illustrative net debt and a simple pro rata allocation before taxes, a $25 million enterprise-value sale yields $600,000 (1.2×), while a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). At 2% ownership, the same $100 million sale yields $1.9 million (3.8×). The memo supplies no supported sale price, actual shareholder terms, or basis to estimate a wind-down residual.

If growth plateaus, buyer interest fails to emerge, public scale remains out of reach, and Latent continues reinvesting all available cash, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. That is a downside scenario. The memo supplies no evidence of a funded payout or eligible share purchase that would change it.

### Countercase

Goodfire already distributes activation-based monitoring through Baseten, showing that a platform can obtain similar functionality through a partnership. Latent lets customers export labeled examples, which may reduce the cost of switching. Together, these weaken the case for buying Latent outright, especially while its reported advantage comes from a sandbox comparison with a frontier judge. The Pass gate remains open because the memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a supported ceiling on sale proceeds. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid vLLM production pilot show enough incremental value over a platform buyer’s best licensing or build option to justify an acquisition that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? A buyer-validated comparison of prevented violations at comparable false-hold rates, latency, and cost would move the action to **Reach out** if the resulting acquisition economics support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership within eight years.
