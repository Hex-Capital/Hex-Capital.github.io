# Evardi -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Evardi targets the part of the grid where it says 94% of outage minutes originate. That is a consequential place to look. Transformer-level visibility could let utilities find overloaded equipment and prevent bad switching decisions without instrumenting every asset. But 94% describes where the problem sits; it does not quantify how much Evardi can eliminate. The dossier supplies no measured reduction in outage minutes, infrastructure spending, or engineering cost. I can see the mechanism for useful infrastructure software. The orders-of-magnitude consequence remains unproven.

The strongest argument for investing is that Evardi could extract substantially more performance from infrastructure utilities already own. That fits my preference for technologies compatible with installed systems and for scarce engineering workflows that can become repeatable platforms. Its reported footprint spans utilities serving 10M+ customers and operating 180,000+ miles of line. Its simulated inspection hit rate is 61%, versus 6% using billing flags—roughly tenfold. If that advantage survives deployment, waiting for substantial revenue could mean waiting until the important uncertainty has already disappeared. The footprint, however, measures the utilities’ scale; it does not establish how extensively they use or pay for Evardi.

The technical evidence deserves credit within its actual boundaries. Combining GIS, SCADA, billing and meter records into a power-flow model gives the agents an engineering constraint beyond plausible language. Checking against feeder-head measurements is useful. But aggregate agreement can still conceal incorrect transformer-level allocations. The public-data simulations also report catching 60% of switching errors and reducing unsafe storm-drill actions by 42%; neither establishes live operational reliability. The tenfold inspection result benchmarks an incumbent practice, not demonstrated superiority over Neara or Bidgely/Grid4C. Those competitors have adjacent capabilities. I would not classify Evardi as a generic model wrapper, but neither would I grant it a technical moat from workflow integration alone.

Shah’s Kenya Power operations experience and Autodesk utility-integration work matter more than founder follower counts. Borele brings chemical engineering and prior energy ventures, giving the team complementary exposure rather than identical software résumés. The move from mini-grid demand prediction in January 2026 to utility engineering could reflect useful learning. There is no documented evidence explaining what observation caused that change, however, and no demonstrated recruitment of a specialist to own model reliability or utility security. I cannot turn a changed product description into proof of fast belief revision.

The next dollar should test whether imperfect utility records produce sufficiently accurate local recommendations. Evardi has simulations and operator approval, but the dossier provides no ranked kill-risk experiment, budget, numerical deployment threshold, or stop condition. That is missing process evidence, not evidence of reckless spending. Economics are similarly undisclosed: negotiated licensing is inferred, with no implementation cost, engineering-support burden, or savings per utility. Software avoids a manufacturing scale-up, but bespoke data reconciliation could still consume the margin. There is no evidence of permanent subsidy dependence; there is also no demonstrated route to economical delivery.

I pass with my own money today. Evardi shows neither the acquisition-first behavior nor the documented spending-before-validation red flags that would justify an active rejection. My exceptions for an extraordinary instigator or an overwhelming quantified consequence do not yet override the missing evidence here; domain exposure and utility footprint alone do not establish either. My decision flips if a utility-audited live deployment demonstrates a tenfold improvement in outage minutes avoided per dollar against its existing workflow, counting integration, human review and operating costs. That single result would connect Evardi’s promising technical wedge to the consequence and economics I actually want to purchase.
