# Central Blue -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B, repeatable direct sales with account expansion,** is the strongest case. The first buyer is a spacecraft operator whose electric propulsion is constrained by stored power during eclipse. Central Blue’s mission intake asks for power requirements, location, and timing. The company proposes delivering power to dedicated receivers and using CORE to coordinate handoffs. It reports a nearly complete Seattle prototype and a planned ground propulsion demonstration with Wave Motion Launch Technologies. That named test is an early signal of fit. Buyer funding, completed power delivery, and paid use remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

If operators can use a common receiver integration and buy power repeatedly across eclipse windows, direct sales could lead to additional delivery windows and then more spacecraft in the same fleet. A buyer in a second geography could use substantially the same product if its spacecraft can use the same receiver interface and the network can serve its orbit and delivery windows. Each new spacecraft would still require receiver integration and validation. Each delivery would consume power and a scheduling slot. Spare capacity could let revenue grow faster than delivery costs, while insufficient coverage or capacity could require another capital-intensive node. The memo gives no measured integration effort or delivery costs. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Dedicated receivers could turn each sale into a spacecraft-specific engineering project. Star Catcher’s use of standard solar panels sharpens the risk that buyers will prefer a supplier that can work with hardware they already carry. Even a successful ground demonstration would leave open how much integration each buyer needs and how much orbital capacity each contract consumes. Those risks warrant Watch. The memo does not establish a structural ceiling on buyer demand or show that delivery costs rise in proportion to revenue. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent operator outside the U.S. fund an orbital propulsion pilot that reuses the receiver integration, delivers useful power across multiple eclipse windows, and records the engineering effort and node capacity consumed? A successful pilot with those records would move the action to **Reach out**.
