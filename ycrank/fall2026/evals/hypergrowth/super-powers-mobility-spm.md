# Super Powers Mobility (SPM) -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with its initial buyers, SPM’s best growth path is to reuse its modular battery packs and power electronics across vehicle programs, then sell additional units as those programs enter fleet production. The memo describes a recurring job for vehicle builders and defense primes that lack in-house electrical integration expertise. The same job plausibly recurs abroad, and the pack and power-export architecture offers a reusable core. How much vehicle integration, qualification, and sourcing work transfers remains uncertain. SPM quotes orders from single units through fleets and contracts separately for custom engineering. That gives buyers a repeatable way to request a quote, with human-led selling and integration still central to the documented motion. Fleet reorders could expand spend within an account, but realized repeat purchases and recurring billing are unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

SPM reports $421,815 in realized revenue in one month for the current products. That is a meaningful early commercialization signal for a four-person team given the August sales start and September pivot dates reported in the memo. The launch separately describes more than $400,000 in contracted revenue in one month. Whether the figures overlap, which customers contributed, and whether sales repeated are unresolved. Pipeline, production bookings, and prior vehicle preorders add no realized revenue credit. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The incremental contribution scenario is quoted fleet-order revenue less cells, build and test labor, attributable selling and integration work, and any production capacity added for the order. Reusing a pack design across orders could spread engineering effort, and automation could reduce labor per unit. The memo supplies no order-level prices or costs to establish positive contribution at fleet volumes. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

SPM could remain a custom supplier whose growth depends on primes winning programs, engineers integrating and validating each vehicle, and Pittsburgh production capacity expanding with orders. U.S. defense sourcing requirements may also limit cell options for some programs. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo does not establish that each market requires a bespoke rebuild or that required labor and capacity make faster growth economically infeasible. The modular design and fleet-order offering leave a concrete path to test. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a buyer in a second market place a paid fleet reorder after deploying the same core pack and power-export system with bounded integration and qualification work? A delivered reorder, with engineering hours and order-level costs showing positive incremental contribution at a repeatable price, would support rescoring for Reach out if the score reaches 65 and confidence reaches Medium or High. Evidence that each rollout requires unavoidable bespoke work or capacity commitments that make faster growth uneconomic would trigger Pass.
