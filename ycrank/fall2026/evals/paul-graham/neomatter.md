# Neomatter -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Two hundred legal-team interviews should leave the founders with some very specific stories. Which lawyer had a problem the existing tools couldn't solve? What did the founders build when the first approach failed? Neomatter's dossier records the interviews, but no such episode. That is the missing fact I care about most. Wagner and Mainwaring have worked together at DAHLIA and on autonomous-agent research at Acadia, which gives their partnership substance. It doesn't yet show how they behave when blocked. Resourcefulness remains at my absent-evidence default.

The product earns more credit. Neomatter describes intake from Gmail, Outlook, Slack and Teams, followed by analysis against company playbooks and agreements, with lawyer approval before execution. Founder demonstrations and company-reported SOC 2 Type I completion suggest work beyond an application idea ([launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Vnk-neomatter-the-operating-system-for-in-house-legal-teams), [trust center](https://neomatter.com/trust)). But the dossier supplies no independently observed completed workflow, accuracy result or deployment benchmark. I give the technical dimension intermediate credit, discounted for company-reported evidence. This also falls short of the concrete proof spikes I look for in unfamiliar sectors. There is no evidence yet of developers appropriating a new primitive for unexpected uses.

The strongest argument for investing is that these founders have relevant agent experience, have already done considerable customer discovery, and are tackling the unpleasant work between a legal request and its resolution. Integrations, privileged information and approval rules could conceal a useful wedge. Two hundred conversations might have exposed one that competitors missed. But we aren't told what that discovery was. Their research establishes technical preparation, not a personal collision with legal work; there is also no documented hypothesis rejected and product changed in response. Those two dimensions stay at their defaults. The opportunity becomes much more interesting if one interview produced a precise problem and a customer who kept returning.

Who wants this now? Interview participation doesn't answer that. Neither do 9,830 launch views. Neomatter's named-user subscriptions and default twelve-month contracts describe how customers would pay, without establishing that anyone does ([terms](https://neomatter.com/terms)). Demand therefore stays at its absent-evidence default. Wordsmith already advertises overlapping intake and execution. That makes the particular user's reason for choosing Neomatter consequential. It doesn't make the market unwinnable. Nor does having a launch establish launch dependence, and the founders' prior shared work weighs against the hastily assembled cofounder red flag. I see missing investment evidence rather than demonstrated bad behavior.

I would pass on the evidence here. The known exceptions don't supply an override: the dossier establishes neither an impressive working prototype under independent observation, unmistakable user pull, nor validated technical results strong enough to replace the missing founder evidence. Neomatter fails my resourcefulness gate before the market argument becomes decisive. My condition for reopening the decision is one documented customer episode: a named legal team hits a deployment blocker, the founders ship a workaround within two weeks, and that team then uses the resulting workflow weekly for a month. That would connect founder action to something users actually need.
