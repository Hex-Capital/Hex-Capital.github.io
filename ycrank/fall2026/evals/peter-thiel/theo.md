# Theo -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Theo’s strongest possible secret is that small businesses will delegate continuing website improvement to software if the software produces customers rather than merely pages. That proposition is testable: compare qualified leads against an ordinary builder over a sustained period at comparable cost. But the dossier does not establish which prevailing belief Theo overturns. Agencies already sell continuing optimization; Durable already combines website creation with SEO/GEO. Automating an expensive service can create value without creating a company that captures much of it.

The decisive problem is ownership of the market. Theo addresses small businesses broadly, with no demonstrated dominance in a bounded segment and no established proprietary technology, network effect, or scale advantage. Requiring customers to host replacement sites creates some switching friction, but friction is not automatically a monopoly mechanism. It also makes adoption harder, particularly when Theo lacks native bookings, payments, and member databases. The incumbent-overlap red flag is present in the product evidence; the hypothetical cross-customer performance dataset remains an inference.

The strongest objection to passing is that Miles Pines has already found demand through doing the work. He reports generating thousands of organic clicks for Endgrate, bootstrapping that previous business to more than $100,000 in annual revenue, and performing SEO for business-owning friends. This fits my revealed preference for founders building from an operating problem they have experienced. Theo also reports concrete customer outcomes: Buy My Lunch increased Google clicks from 131 to 929, while 118 118 Money increased monthly organic visits from 50,000 to 118,000. Those results make the product harder to dismiss as an interchangeable demonstration. They do not establish exclusive knowledge or a durable advantage over competing optimization products.

Would that customer behavior justify an exception to the monopoly requirement? I would override competitive concerns for independently demonstrated technical discontinuity or behavior that directly establishes durable value capture. These company-reported traffic comparisons establish neither: attribution, comparison periods, and retention remain unresolved. Separately, Pines’s technical background supports his ability to build, but it does not document full-time status, ownership, or economic exposure. Solo founding eliminates the co-founder relationship question; it does not answer the remaining alignment questions. I use the default there rather than invent a founding defect.

Theo has a coherent revenue engine and a visible acquisition funnel. Starter costs $69.99 monthly and includes ongoing SEO; approximately 119,100 Starter subscriptions would produce $100 million in annual recurring revenue before discounts or churn. That is arithmetic, not a forecast supported by customer expansion or margins. Approximately 4,000 free audits each month provide actual channel activity, stronger evidence than a proposed partnership strategy. Yet the 10,000-plus sites built are not identified as paying subscriptions, and migrating a site and connecting Google accounts impose delivery work against a modest contract value. Neither paid conversion nor acquisition payback is established.

I pass on investing my own money. Theo may build a useful subscription business, but the evidence presently supports customer usefulness more strongly than lasting market ownership. My flip condition for reopening diligence is an independently auditable, controlled trial within one named SMB vertical showing that Theo’s proprietary optimization produces at least three times the incremental qualified leads of Durable at comparable spend, with that advantage sustained for twelve months. That would supply evidence of a specific technological advantage where the current dossier supplies overlapping features.
