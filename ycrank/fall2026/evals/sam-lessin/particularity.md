# Particularity -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Particularity proposes to turn a changing website into an actionable government investigation. That could be valuable. But timestamping an interaction, proving a statutory violation, and getting an agency to buy the resulting work are three different accomplishments. The dossier describes the first and proposes the second; it does not document the third. Meanwhile, post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and round proceeds are unknown. I cannot establish either a cheap entry or what specific truth my check would reveal.

The strongest argument for this team is the bridge it can build. Kumaravel worked on consumer protection at the Senate Banking Committee; Bannon worked on security and compliance infrastructure at AWS and has published the Sailor frontend framework. That combination fits my preference for people who can translate between institutions and technology. It earns some founder credit beyond credentials alone. But legislative experience does not establish agency procurement access, and infrastructure engineering does not establish that investigators have accepted their evidence. The operating receipts cover only part of the bridge.

The product has a more interesting wedge than generic compliance monitoring. Particularity targets the regulator sourcing cases, while the cited competitors primarily serve organizations managing their own compliance. Checkout testing and cancellation investigations are concrete workflows where automation could increase investigative capacity. Here is the bull case that matters: the founders understand enough of both systems to make an investigator materially more productive, and each deployment accumulates useful agency-specific knowledge. Yet the dossier explicitly labels that future workflow advantage as inferred. Soliciting introductions to attorneys general and city departments is prospecting; it does not demonstrate owned access causing a purchase.

The economic question is how much human work survives each automated investigation. Hashes protect record integrity; they do not establish that the agent interpreted the interaction correctly. If investigators must reconstruct every finding, Particularity could automate collection while leaving the expensive judgment untouched. Existing agency mystery-shopping and recording tools make that distinction consequential. This activates my concern about AI economics depending on errors becoming negligible, although the dossier does not establish that the founders actually make that assumption. Subscription pricing is inferred, and margins, burn, runway, and a funded break-even plan are absent, so the economics receive the stated default.

I pass on this evidence. The October 15 launch is recent; missing revenue is not proof of failed selling, and YC participation is not proof of an auction. But neither establishes my investment gates. My exceptions to those gates require documented conversion, retention, cash economics, or unusually strong operating access. Particularity has relevant founder experience without those overriding receipts. One verifiable event would reopen underwriting: a named agency renews a paid deployment because its investigations produced usable cases, with documented positive contribution profit after human review. That would connect the technical bridge to a business someone demonstrably needs.
