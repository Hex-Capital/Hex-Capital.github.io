# Mentlio -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The founders have built close enough to the problem to earn attention, but not yet enough to prove specific knowledge of the buyer. Ashank Shah and Ahmet Demirbas bring ML research, founding-engineer experience, and a token-compression project; Mentlio’s router also retained 98.93% of Fable 5’s accuracy while cutting cost 24.90% on Terminal-Bench 2.1. That is shipped technical work, not résumé theater. The missing action is selling into engineering organizations: three months before launch, the founders were building Adjoint, a Unity plugin, and the dossier shows no production deployment, repeat usage, or paid customer for the current product.

The router is useful, but usefulness is not durability. Local processing protects customer code and prompts, while routing, caching, context optimization, and log compression can reduce a real expense. Yet a competitor copying Mentlio’s code inherits essentially the same architecture. The claimed proprietary ML has one disclosed benchmark, no unavailable dataset, no longitudinal customer outcomes, and no evidence that deployments already create a compounding model advantage. Jellyfish and DX already connect AI spend to engineering output; Mentlio’s boundary is local optimization, not a structural barrier.

The market can become large because AI spend per developer reportedly rose from roughly $5 to $81 per month in ten months, and engineering intelligence has produced a $1 billion acquisition in DX. Mentlio also has a coherent initial buyer—engineering leaders at organizations with at least 15 developers—and can expand from token control into delivery and quality management. But that expansion is also where incumbent products live. A broad parent market is not automatically room to maneuver when the shared capability is a replicable integration layer across Claude, Codex, Cursor, GitHub, Jira, and Slack.

The strongest bull case is that pre-seed evidence should be technical before it is commercial: the founders identified a new cost inflection, built a working local router, and attracted 13,000 launch-post views without established distribution. The closest known exception is Notion: an early prototype can justify investing before a user loop exists. The material difference is that Notion’s founders had developed an unusual product language over years; Mentlio arrived through a three-month pivot into a category already occupied by well-funded engineering-analytics vendors. One benchmark does not yet override that distinction.

There is no disclosed integrity violation, artificial deadline, reassurance-seeking behavior, or other founder red flag. The deeper mismatch is structural: each customer currently requires founder outreach, a free audit, integrations, and a demo, while customers do not create utility or distribution for one another. There is also no public price, payer, recurring revenue, or completed transaction. I pass today. The verifiable flip condition is five paying organizations with at least 50 engineers each, accompanied by cross-customer routing results showing that accumulated deployment data improves savings or quality beyond the published 24.90% benchmark. That would turn copyable software into a compounding asset.
