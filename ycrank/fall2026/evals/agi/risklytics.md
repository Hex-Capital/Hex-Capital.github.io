# Risklytics -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest path for this plausible seed-stage investment. Risklytics uses AI to draft applications from company materials, then a licensed broker shops the risk and reviews exclusions before binding. The new transaction would be placing deployment-required coverage for robotics and autonomy accounts that generalist brokers could not serve economically. The workflow plausibly reduces intake labor. Whether it lowers total labor per successful placement enough to open that segment remains unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The founders report that three previously denied businesses obtained coverage in one week. That is an early demand signal, with premiums, commissions, renewals, and repeat placements unverified. The founders also brought prior insurance risk-modeling experience, reached beta with their wildfire product, and built and launched the brokerage intake after pivoting. That demonstrates early execution. Repeatable delivery through the licensed brokerage workflow remains unproven. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

The expansion unit is one additional bound policy. **Illustrative scenario:** At an assumed $10,000 annual premium and the founders’ stated typical commission of 10–20%, a policy would generate $1,000–$2,000 in gross brokerage commission before any wholesale split, inference, licensed review, rework, failed submissions, and servicing. The memo has no measured contribution per policy or estimate of reachable buyers within the licensed footprint. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The reported denials may reflect carrier appetite or policy wording. Cheaper intake could leave both constraints intact, and a launch-day tester reported a missed coverage selection that illustrates how review can eat into labor savings. With equal models, buyers could still value licensed placement and exclusion review, but Alton Risk already places overlapping robotics coverage, and Risklytics’ public tracker can inform rivals. The memo supplies no like-for-like evidence that buyers prefer Risklytics under parity. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Even with abundant competing capital, carrier willingness to cover AI exposures may remain scarce. Insurers control underwriting, and the memo identifies no preferential capacity rights for Risklytics, leaving Path A unsupported. Both Path B gates remain unresolved. A representative seed-stage trial can test them, which supports Watch. Confidence is low because the decisive placement economics and buyer preference are unmeasured. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Risklytics repeatedly bind paid, deployment-ready coverage for previously denied or uneconomic robotics accounts at lower total human labor per successful placement, while buyers still choose it over a specialist with equivalently capable models? A matched trial should compare premiums, limits, and exclusions, count review, retries, and failed submissions, and verify positive contribution after placement and servicing costs. A result that clears both gates and raises the case to the outreach threshold with at least medium confidence would change the action to Reach out. A credible result showing no market-opening labor savings or easy buyer substitution would change it to Pass.
