# Micora -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with remote industrial operators, Micora has a plausible route to serving the same power needs across markets. Stackable solar blocks and containerized batteries give it a reusable core. Terrain, load, and backup needs still shape each deployment, and the extent of adaptation across markets is uncertain. The web configurator standardizes intake, while final pricing follows site review and Micora handles design through commissioning. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The offered 12- and 24-month leases support recurring revenue during the contract term. An existing customer could lease more capacity or add sites, but each addition would require another site review. Micora reports projects underway at Pacific island and manufacturing sites. The memo leaves payment status and commercial growth unknown, so initial traction receives the default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

For a sale, compare the site price with equipment, freight, sales and design labor, surveying, installation, and commissioning. For a lease, compare contracted payments with those costs plus financing, retrieval, and idle time between deployments. The favorable scenario assumes robotic placement cuts crew hours and portable hardware can be redeployed quickly enough to keep leased equipment earning. The memo gives no measured inputs to establish either effect. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Micora could remain a project-by-project installer even with strong demand. Site-specific design, backup sizing, and commissioning could keep labor tied to each project, while leases could tie up more capital with every deployment. The structural gate stays open because modular hardware, robotic placement, and equipment reuse offer concrete ways to reduce that burden, with field economics still unverified. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Micora repeat a paid industrial deployment in a second market using the same core kit, with limited redesign and positive incremental contribution? A commissioned site, a paid invoice, and a breakdown of freight, labor, and equipment costs would test that path. A successful repeat would warrant rescoring. Evidence that unavoidable site work or capacity costs make repeat deployments uneconomic even after automation savings would change Watch to Pass.
