# Mnemonic Labs -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Building blocks of superintelligence.** Mnemonic is training a foundation model on market activity to discover and execute trades. The memo identifies no AI workload whose deployment is constrained by access to a financial model or signal, and no evidence that growing AI deployment would make either input scarce. This path scores zero. (The Idea, Defensibility)

**Industry enabled by superintelligence.** Established quant desks already use statistical and neural models to find strategies. Mnemonic aims to automate more of the research and execution loop, but the memo identifies no specific strategy class, buyer, or trading cadence that conventional delivery makes uneconomic and Mnemonic could plausibly unlock. The company reports building a pipeline and architecture in under six weeks and using over 25 TB of tokens in its launch training run. The memo records a public launch and audience attention, with no disclosed live returns, execution costs, or reduction in researcher time. This path also scores zero. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Commercial diligence.** Proprietary trading is the strongest commercial scenario in the memo and remains an inference. Published market-event pretraining methods and competitors’ reported use of alternative workflows in live markets weigh against durable capture. A proprietary feedback loop from orders and fills remains hypothetical. Launch attention establishes visibility, while the memo supplies no buyer trial, commitment, or revenue signal, so customer demand stays at its default. The founders’ quant experience and the company-reported training run support seed-stage execution credit. Scale economics stays at its default because the memo gives no basis for estimating the contribution of the next deployed strategy. A meaningful comparison would deduct fees, slippage, market impact, data, compute, supervision, and the cost of trading capital from live gross P&L for both Mnemonic and a conventional quant workflow at comparable risk. With both thesis paths at zero, the decision gate is Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

If researcher time makes short-lived signals too costly to investigate, a model that discovers and executes strategies with less supervision could make those signals tradable. The memo describes research showing that market-event pretraining can reproduce features of order flow in held-out markets, and it records competitors’ reported live use of AI trading workflows. Even with comparable models available, order execution and risk management would still have to be delivered. This is a plausible scenario, but the memo supplies no specific excluded use or evidence that Mnemonic crosses its economic threshold, so the Pass gate still applies. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
