# Derya -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Mert Turna built ship-operations software used on more than 30 vessels, while Derya publishes no two-to-five-year architectural roadmap. That pairing is Derya’s signature: unusually credible freight operators pursuing an indefinite plan. Derya can become a good business through founder competence; Derya has not yet explained why it becomes an inevitable monopoly. Execution without a secret is mimetic energy applied efficiently.

Anduril is the high rung for founder lineage: Palmer Luckey had already shipped technically difficult hardware to a billion-dollar exit before attacking an adjacent institutional failure. Derya shares the load-bearing mechanism at smaller scale. Turna built trucking operations and software covering $200 million of fleet value; Oguzhan Karaca worked inside CEVA/CMA CGM and previously built a six-figure-ARR business. Derya diverges where the analogy matters most: neither founder has shipped at Oculus scale, and Derya’s credentials do not independently prove the capacity to create a new category.

Quora is the low rung for definite planning: formidable founder pedigree overrode an inherently indefinite consumer roadmap. The same failure mechanism appears more starkly at Derya. Same-business-day quotes, AI carrier matching, and financing above $20,000 are present capabilities, not a definite destination. Derya names no committed trade lane, automation threshold, proprietary infrastructure milestone, or five-year market sequence. “Operate the forwarding office with agents” remains a direction in which to iterate. A definite optimist says what must become true and when.

Flexport, Forto, Nuvocargo, and Zencargo already combine freight execution with software, so Derya’s “AI-native logistics office” does not yet contain a contrarian secret with a named incumbent failure mode. Derya’s decision to operate the forwarding function rather than sell software is more substantive than the median “AI for X” pitch, but the claimed advantage—matching suppliers and carriers within minutes—will decay as models diffuse. Over 18 months, proprietary shipment data could compound; without exclusive volume or a structural explanation for why Flexport cannot adopt similar automation, faster quoting compounds into competition rather than power.

More than $3 million of managed transactions could become the seed of a monopoly, but Derya has not shown that transaction volume produces exclusive data, superior pricing, or customer captivity. The counterfactual is precise: Derya would move into the Invest tier if one narrow trade lane produced an exclusive carrier network whose pricing and reliability data enabled sustained premium margins that larger forwarders could not reproduce. Today, Derya relies on partner licenses and relationships without disclosed exclusivity. A network one can join is distribution; a network one cannot recreate is a moat.

Palantir turned regulated-customer complexity into accumulated integration depth and trust. Derya touches customs, carrier qualification, cargo-data security, and financing, so Derya does enter the neglected institutional terrain that most software investors avoid. The parallel stops because Derya’s partners appear to possess the licenses and local connections, while Derya assumes the booking and working-capital exposure. Regulation is only a moat when it excludes competitors. Here it may instead socialize differentiation while concentrating liability.

The $80,000 of contracted first-month revenue predicts commercial competence, not monopoly. By July 2029, Derya will be generating $8 million to $15 million in annual gross forwarding revenue and presenting embedded freight financing as its principal differentiation, while remaining a technology-enabled forwarder rather than a category-defining logistics platform. Derya’s founder quality should convert opportunities into shipments; Derya’s missing definite plan and nonexclusive network should prevent those shipments from converting into durable pricing power.

The $3.2 million first-month opportunity pipeline supplies the strongest objection: Derya may be finding demand unusually quickly, and the Asana exception shows that operator pedigree can override weak structural moats. Karaca’s six-figure prior business and Turna’s deployments across 30 vessels make that bull case real. It still fails because Asana’s override rested on Dustin Moskovitz having shipped Facebook to global scale, while Derya’s competitors already possess capital, software, and forwarding operations. I would not write the check today. I would flip to Invest if Derya documents by January 2028 a single lane exceeding $5 million in annualized booked revenue, 20% gross margin, and 70% repeat-customer volume supported by exclusive carrier capacity.
