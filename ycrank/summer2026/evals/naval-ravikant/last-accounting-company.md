# Last Accounting Company -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

$688.2 billion of global accounting services gives Last Accounting Company enormous pivot-room, while a proprietary ledger serving economically independent customers gives Last Accounting Company almost no network compounding. The signature is clear: deep financial-data builders applying code to a vast labor market, but building a managed service rather than a network. Big market, weak flywheel. Code can scale; service relationships do not compound automatically.

**Uber** is the high rung on market optionality: black cars became UberX because transportation contained many adjacent businesses inside one architecture. Last Accounting Company can move among bookkeeping, payroll, VAT, filings, reporting, audit controls, and agent infrastructure without leaving the $688.2 billion accounting market. Last Accounting Company diverges from Uber because Finnish controller Bloom Systems Oy targets Nordic businesses while supported jurisdictions and accountant certifications remain unspecified. A large theoretical market is not the same as permission to operate across it.

**Perplexity’s** low-rung weakness on network compounding is that one more user does not automatically improve the product for every prior user. Last Accounting Company has the same structural gap: each customer buys an accountant-reviewed close, not access to other customers, counterparties, or shared liquidity. Customer-specific transaction histories may improve Last Accounting Company’s agents, but that is an inferred data advantage, not a demonstrated network; no retention, accuracy, or data-scale evidence shows an Nth-customer flywheel.

**Bjørn** performs manual accounting processes while certified accountants approve the output, placing Last Accounting Company between code leverage and labor leverage. Over 18 months, proprietary agents and an MCP server could reduce marginal work as corrections become reusable automation. But Last Accounting Company currently acquires customers through direct messages and still inserts human approval into every workflow. Product and media are permissionless leverage; accountant hours and jurisdictional authority require permission. The architecture has code inside it, but code is not yet the architecture.

**Riki Karjalainen and Sami Laakkonen** built petabyte-scale financial-data infrastructure at Token Terminal, which is real specific knowledge rather than four logos arranged as a résumé. Last Accounting Company also benefits from Veikko Pere and Pietari Virkkunen encountering financial workflows through BCG, private equity, venture capital, and technology. The counterfactual for a top-tier score is narrower: Last Accounting Company would need evidence that the founders previously owned close, filing, or ledger correctness—not merely financial-data scale—or publish an artifact showing materially superior accounting accuracy. Data engineering transfers. Fiduciary correctness does not transfer automatically.

**Stack Overflow** worked because practicing developers built for developers and had years of public artifacts through which intelligence, energy, and integrity could be observed. Last Accounting Company’s four founders have known one another since middle school, and Last Accounting Company reached first-customer onboarding roughly ten weeks after the idea emerged; both are positive long-term-partner signals. The missing piece is legibility: no public repositories, prior ventures, or operating history shows how the founders behave when money, mistakes, and responsibility collide. Accounting is where integrity becomes a product feature.

**The proprietary ledger and MCP server** point to Last Accounting Company becoming infrastructure rather than remaining a full-stack accounting firm. My 36-month forecast: Last Accounting Company pivots toward agent workflow and ledger software sold to accounting firms or finance teams, reaches $3 million to $8 million in ARR, and retains human-reviewed managed accounting as a reference implementation rather than the primary business. Last Accounting Company’s broad market supplies the pivot-room; the absence of network effects pushes the business toward reusable code leverage.

**Balance’s** AI bookkeeping with human review makes the opposing case sting: Last Accounting Company may be entering a crowded, liability-heavy service category where Pilot, Bench, Truewind, and incumbents can copy the automation while distribution remains founder-led. The bull precedent is **Perplexity**—a single-sided product can waive the network criterion when permissionless distribution, specific knowledge, and category timing all clear emphatically—and **Anchorage Digital** shows that regulation can become a moat. Last Accounting Company has not earned either exception: no organic product-led adoption, accuracy data, retention evidence, or enumerated regulatory coverage appears in the dossier. I would flip to Invest if Last Accounting Company demonstrates that at least half of new customers arrive through inbound or referrals while accountant-review time per close falls by 80% without a higher error rate.
