# Computable -- Elad Gil Evaluation

One H100 cluster, one auction closing July 31, and no disclosed transactions make Computable a catalyst-timed financialization bet with no visible buying curve. Computable is skating toward a real puck—GPU reservation exposure is becoming tradable as Ornn pursues exchange-listed compute futures—but the market signal is currently much stronger than the company signal. I would not write a check from my own money today.

**Anduril** is the high rung for market re-opening: Ukraine changed procurement economics, and Anduril already had contracts positioned to capture the change. Computable shares the mechanism because AI infrastructure spending has created idle-capacity risk, price volatility, and demand for transferable reservations; Ornn’s July 2026 futures effort corroborates that catalyst. Computable diverges because the dossier labels financialization as inferred, while Anduril’s catalyst had translated into procurement. A $4.37 billion GPUaaS market growing at 16% is TAM math; Computable still needs evidence that buyers want weekly financial positions rather than cheaper rentals.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism: category demand can be real while the investable economics belong elsewhere. Computable’s 46-point Show HN launch and scheduled auction are attention signals, not the revenue velocity that made Harvey “just working.” Computable discloses no bids, clearing volume, repeat buyers, revenue, or named counterparties—no evidence, so I am applying the absent-evidence default. In an AI-adjacent market, silence on the curve is a red flag even at pre-seed because an auction exists specifically to produce measurable buying behavior.

Yuan Song’s inferred Jump Trading background and the unnamed co-founder’s claimed Coinbase experience could compound over 18 months into unusually fast market-structure iteration: contract design, quoting, collateral, settlement, and risk controls all benefit from those operating contexts. Computable has shipped a live auction rather than merely publishing a GPU-futures thesis, which earns credit. Computable has not shown the Perplexity pattern yet—no named prior product, public repository, user-feedback loop, or sequence of weekly improvements—so credentials cannot substitute for demonstrated shipping rhythm.

SF Compute already operates thousands of GPUs and supports resale orders, while Computable has disclosed one cluster and no additional suppliers. Computable’s index score flips upward if every buyer and supplier can use its weekly clearing price as the reference rate for compute, making Computable a basket of GPU demand in the Coinbase sense. Today the standing buyback quote creates balance-sheet exposure rather than distribution leverage; without multi-supplier depth, repeated clearing volume, and external adoption of the price history, Computable must win share against SF Compute, Ornn, Vast.ai, and Compute Exchange.

**Color Genomics** isolated one miracle by building workflow and data infrastructure on top of commodity sequencing. Computable does the opposite today: Computable needs heterogeneous GPU capacity to become contractually standard, a two-sided market to become liquid, standing buybacks to remain financeable, and future-dated positions to avoid disabling commodities or derivatives treatment. Those are independent miracles. The Jump Trading and Coinbase backgrounds improve the odds of navigating them, but neither founder precedent nor a Known Exception removes the multi-miracle dependency; unlike Mistral, Computable cannot point to a prior Llama-scale shipping artifact that warrants overriding the rule.

The July 31, 2026 auction will reveal whether Computable has a market or a mechanism looking for one. My 36-month forecast is that Computable pivots from transferable positions with standing buybacks into non-transferable enterprise GPU procurement and capacity brokerage, reaching $1–3 million ARR rather than becoming an independent compute exchange. Computable can monetize fragmented supply through brokerage before it can standardize compute, fund liquidity, and resolve derivative classification.

Jump Trading plus Coinbase is the strongest opposing case: Computable may be the rare founding pair capable of turning weekly H100 positions into a Coinbase-like market, and one auction is a reasonable pre-seed starting point rather than evidence of failure. The Color exception also helps the bull case—Computable could become workflow infrastructure above commodity hardware instead of underwriting the hardware itself. The dossier still gives Computable no named second founder, fee schedule, regulatory structure, supplier depth, reserve capacity, or completed volume, while SF Compute and Ornn have raised $55 million and $33 million respectively. I would flip to Invest if Computable clears auctions from at least three independent suppliers, produces more than $1 million in monthly traded volume for three consecutive months, and sustains a standing buyback without principal losses.
