# AnyAlpha -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by Kalshi is the strongest supported route. The memo documents a live integration that lets Kalshi users connect their accounts for automated orders. AnyAlpha reports more than 1,000 active traders and more than $1 million in weekly trading volume shortly after launch. Kalshi could have a reason to own the product if AnyAlpha brings it repeat trading activity that would otherwise go elsewhere or remain unexecuted. The reported volume leaves that incremental benefit unmeasured. The immediate milestone is a production test that measures repeat use and incremental exchange contribution. The live integration makes that test plausible, while its funding and the capital needed to scale remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The memo gives legacy app purchase prices and reported trading volume, but no current trading revenue, buyer economics, or comparable sale price to support an acquisition valuation. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

| Scenario | Assumed proceeds bridge | Cash to holder |
|----------|-------------------------|----------------|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $45M in distributable equity | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M in distributable equity | $2.70M (5.4×) |

The sale cases are illustrative. Both assume an eligible holder sells the entire stake for cash within eight years and receives 3% of distributable equity, with no preference claims, holdbacks, or contingent payments. At 80% participation, the strong case pays $2.16 million within the horizon. The ability to transfer the integration, carry over exchange permissions, and participate as a holder remains unverified. The memo supplies no evidence for an independent liquidity route. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Kalshi may capture the same economics through its public API by building the workflow itself or working with an alternative such as TurbineFi, which already offers plain-language strategy creation, backtesting, and cloud execution. AnyAlpha’s claimed first-and-only partnership has unverified exclusivity and duration, and changes to API permissions could interrupt live trading. That makes acquisition value uncertain. The memo establishes neither a structural block nor materially inadequate proceeds under the strongest credible route, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Does AnyAlpha generate enough durable, incremental contribution for Kalshi to justify an acquisition that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? A Kalshi-validated cohort test measuring repeat trading and incremental contribution after incentives and servicing costs, paired with a credible valuation bridge to at least $83.3 million in distributable equity, would move the action to **Reach out**.
