# Lantern AI -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

By 2032, a hiring manager might teach a system what a good colleague looks like once, then carry that understanding through discovery, interviews, and the eventual hiring decision. Lantern AI has a small, credible leak from that world: customers of its interviewer asked it to move upstream into finding candidates. That request matters more to me than the phrase “World People Model.” Someone encountered the product and wanted it to take another piece of the job. But preserving preferences, learning which preferences predict successful hires, and earning permission to act on that knowledge are three different transitions. The dossier establishes demand for the first. The rest remain a possible future.

The founders have earned more attention than a generic recruiting-agent pitch would deserve. Thomas Chan reports two previous recruiting businesses with “2M+” in sales, although the currency is unspecified, and both founders worked at Career Hackers. There is operating tuition here. There is also commercial evidence: Lantern reports crossing $1M ARR in October 2026, names Crypto.com, Hyatt, and Gammon as customers, and reports 100,000 cumulative interviews. Hyatt’s testimonial describes screening three times as many candidates weekly. Those are people entrusting an actual workflow to software. I give that substantial weight while keeping the interviewer’s accomplishments separate from proof that the newer sourcing platform works. The biography establishes sector commitment, but supplies little dated evidence of independently pursued experiments or unusual recruiting ability.

Why does hiring judgment disappear between people in the first place? A manager’s criteria pass through recruiters, job descriptions, screening tools, and interview notes; each translation can lose something. Lantern’s shared hiring model addresses that specific failure. Yet the agency returned is principally the employer’s ability to preserve its preferences. The dossier identifies neither a quantified intermediary rent nor a restriction preventing individuals from working on their own terms. And “turn your taste into hires” deserves a second question: whose taste, with what opportunity to correct it? Consistent scoring can consistently preserve a bad preference. Human review is useful, but the accuracy and bias disclaimers leave assessment quality unresolved.

The strongest case against my hesitation is that Lantern already has the commercial beachhead from which a much harder company could grow. Its interview business could finance a persistent learning system spanning hiring decisions. That fits my pattern of finding ambitious infrastructure inside ordinary workflow repair. My pre-revenue exception for **CosmicBrain AI** rested on a functioning prototype, industrial commitments, and a lifelong technical obsession; Lantern has stronger reported revenue, but less evidence of the distinctive technical destination that would justify overlooking an incomplete future map. Revenue answers whether someone pays. It does not yet answer what becomes possible because this particular company exists.

Today, the disclosed asset is customer-specific criteria, feedback, and decision history. That is useful context, but Gem already shares recruiting history across agents, and Juicebox adapts sourcing to feedback. Lantern has not demonstrated control over a scarce dependency beneath those products. Nor does the dossier establish dependence on one closed model supplier, founder-control deadlock, or misleading leadership titles; I would not invent those red flags. The concrete custody concern is that disconnecting Google access does not automatically delete stored communications and meeting records. Available deletion requests and retention controls temper that concern, but consent and custody need examination before these records become an expanding company memory.

I am neutral on investing my own money. Lantern AI has convincing evidence of a useful interview business and an interesting customer-led expansion. My expectation is that, within 36 months, interview screening will still generate more than half its subscription revenue; that is plainly testable and could be wrong. My flip condition is an independently audited customer trial showing that Lantern’s accumulated hiring context improves accepted-offer conversion by at least 20% relative to the same underlying models without that context. That would make the shared memory an observed advantage rather than an attractive architectural description.
