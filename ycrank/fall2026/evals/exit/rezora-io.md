# Rezora IO -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Rezora’s strongest exit path is a strategic sale to a brokerage software platform that could add outbound voice follow-up to its existing lead management. Luxury Presence already qualifies website leads by SMS, so Rezora’s real-estate call workflows could give a buyer a way to turn more of those leads into booked meetings. The memo records Yelp’s acquisition of Hatch as an adjacent transaction. Rezora’s founder reported 1,000 meetings booked on the current platform and attributed a customer’s $65,000 in closings to its use for CRM follow-up. Those claims show possible customer value. The incremental lift a buyer could capture remains uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A paid brokerage software integration that demonstrates repeatable booking lift on consented leads is the next operating milestone. The earlier paid custom-agent pilot and the current sales hiring effort support the plausibility of running that test. Current paid retention, test cost, and available runway remain unknown. A sale of the standalone software looks feasible in principle. IP and contract transfer rights, lead-data permissions, and holder participation would need verification. An independent liquidity route remains unsubstantiated. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

Using the shared $500,000 check at a $10M post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons, the holder needs $2.5M in gross cash within eight years. That requires about $83.3M in distributable equity at 3%. Both sale scenarios assume an all-cash close within eight years, full holder participation, and zero net debt, deal deductions, or senior claims, so enterprise value flows through to equity pro rata.

- **Modest sale.** At an assumed $5M ARR and 4× ARR, $20M of enterprise value yields $600,000 to the holder, or 1.2×.
- **Strong sale.** At an assumed $25M ARR and 4× ARR, $100M yields $3M, or 6×. A $10M net debt or deal adjustment would reduce that to $2.7M. Hatch’s reported $25M ARR provides a scale reference. Its purchase price and Rezora’s current platform revenue remain unknown. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share purchase yields $0 realized within eight years.

### Countercase

A buyer could build the basic calling and booking workflow or use an existing substitute. Rezora’s founder assembled the original pilot with commercial tools, Jobber already bundles inbound booking, and Avoca already books directly into ServiceTitan while Rezora lists that integration as forthcoming. Outbound consent requirements also limit the pool of leads a brokerage buyer could use. Together, these facts weaken the case for paying an acquisition premium. The Pass gate remains open because the memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported ceiling on proceeds from the strongest route. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a paid pilot with a brokerage software platform show enough incremental bookings from consented leads to support a buyer’s case for acquiring Rezora at a price that leaves at least $83.3M in distributable equity? Repeatable lift against the platform’s existing workflow, paired with buyer-side economics that support that price, would move the action to Reach out. A supported price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle would move it to Pass.
