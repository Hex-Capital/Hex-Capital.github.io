# Simulithic -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Simulithic’s strongest exit path is a strategic sale to a device-cloud testing platform such as BrowserStack. Simulithic documents a visitor-behavior SDK, workspace journey maps, and per-device comparisons of pull-request previews against a baseline. A buyer could use those assets to focus its device cloud on the journeys customers actually take. The acquisition rationale depends on whether that approach catches valuable regressions or saves engineering time beyond what the buyer’s own agents deliver. The memo provides no measured advantage or evidence of acquisition interest. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A proposed milestone is a 90-day test across three paying teams that measures regressions caught and engineering time saved against their current QA workflows, at comparable run cost. The founders report several live deployments, and the memo reports $500,000 in YC funding. Those signals make a limited test plausible, while paid retention, runway, and the cost of broader device coverage remain unknown. The GitHub App and CLI provide a plausible integration path. A sale would still depend on transferable IP and customer agreements, including any permissions needed to retain or transfer visitor-derived data. Simulithic documents consent controls and a data processing addendum, while transfer rights remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

With actual investor terms unavailable, the benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, dilution from 5% to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× objective is $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale scenarios below assume $5 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, no additional senior claims, and an eligible holder selling its entire stake for cash at closing within eight years. The prices are illustrative assumptions. The memo gives no realized revenue, acquisition comparables, or buyer economics that support either valuation. (Key Facts, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

BrowserStack already offers specialized testing agents on its device cloud, and TestSprite already discovers application flows. A buyer could use its existing tools or build the remaining functionality internally if Simulithic’s visitor-derived coverage adds little. Native-device limitations and rising run costs as test coverage expands make that countercase stronger. The memo establishes meaningful overlap, but leaves open whether existing tools match Simulithic’s coverage in production and what a buyer would pay for any measured advantage. The Pass gate remains untriggered because neither a structural block nor materially inadequate proceeds under the strongest credible route is established. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Simulithic demonstrate a repeatable advantage in paid production that a device-cloud platform values enough to buy at a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A head-to-head production test, paired with a prospective buyer’s estimate of the value of acquiring the capability versus building it, would answer that question. Move to Reach out if that evidence supports at least $2.5 million in holder cash within eight years after dilution and deal deductions. Stay on Watch while the economics are unresolved. Move to Pass if the strongest supported deal falls materially short.
