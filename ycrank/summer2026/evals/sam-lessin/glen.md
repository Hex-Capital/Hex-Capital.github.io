# Glen -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The founder is more investable than the round currently described. Nikos Dritsakos has shipped inside agent infrastructure at Composio, operated B2B software, and sold SalesBop for $500,000 after roughly 18 months. That is credible outsider-insider positioning: he understands both the machinery supplying agents and the organizational workflows consuming them. A solo founder also leaves no ambiguity about who makes the product and financing decisions.

The missing facts are the ones I use to underwrite my own check. There is no proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is genuinely scarce first capital. I therefore cannot distinguish a cheap non-consensus bet from another YC AI round whose uncertainty has already been auctioned away. The dossier also names no use of proceeds, deadline, or pass/fail experiment. “Build unified organizational context” is a product ambition, not a venture moment.

The strongest bull case is that Glen may already sit inside a valuable workflow. It is in production, onboards in waves, and has named testimonials from Amorphic Labs, Zaplar, and Composio. The product captures Slack, GitHub, tickets, calls, documents, and agent sessions, then turns repeated work into reusable skills. If customers repeatedly rely on those histories, the accumulated context could become a proprietary data asset with real switching costs. The founder’s 5,376 LinkedIn followers and waitlist may help open doors, but neither proves that owned access causes purchases.

The bear case still wins because the alleged dark pool has not been observed. Glen reports no revenue, paid conversion, retention, corpus growth, or repeat-use metric. Its own terms warn that recalled context may be inaccurate or outdated, while coverage omits private Slack channels and sessions. Those limitations attack the core product, not a peripheral feature: incomplete capture produces incomplete organizational memory. Meanwhile Dust reports 3,000 organizations, 51,000 monthly active users, and 300,000 deployed agents; Glean has reached $300 million ARR. MCP lowers integration costs for Glen, but it lowers them for every competitor too.

Delete the next financing and the model becomes even less comfortable. Stripe appears in the subprocessor list, but there is no disclosed pricing, revenue, gross margin, burn, or route to break-even. A one-person team could be capital-efficient, yet that is merely a cost observation. It does not establish that customers will pay enough for a sensitive-data product spanning several subprocessors, or that support and security costs remain manageable as deployments deepen.

I am passing at the current evidence level. The one verifiable flip condition is five named design partners converting to paid annual contracts, with at least 90% retained after six months, tied to one financed milestone and disclosed round terms below a $10 million post-money valuation. That would simultaneously show scarce pricing, a finite truth-revealing experiment, and workflow ownership that produces cash. Without it, Glen is a capable founder pursuing a real problem inside a crowded, rapidly commoditizing category—not yet a check I can underwrite.
