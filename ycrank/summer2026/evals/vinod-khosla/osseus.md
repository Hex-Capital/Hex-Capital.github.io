# Osseus -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is not yet large enough. Osseus points to a $58.36 billion engineering-software market, but that is a vendor-revenue statistic, not the cost of robotics failures, redesign cycles, engineering labor, or delayed deployment. The dependency graph could eventually make physical-product development radically cheaper, yet the dossier quantifies neither today’s bottleneck nor the improvement. I cannot price a 10%-likely breakthrough when the claimed consequence remains an adjective.

The product mechanism is more interesting than the market framing. Connecting CAD, ECAD, GitHub, Jira, tests, supplier constraints, and decisions could create a production learning loop that no isolated system owns. But Osseus provides no measured comparison against Trace.Space, Duro, or Altium–Valispace: no dependency-recall rate, time-to-impact-analysis result, or reduction in failed tests. Reviewable agent drafts are prudent, but they do not prove a 5–10x discontinuity. Today this could still be systems integration with an AI interface.

The founders are the strongest part of the option. Anish Mariathasan combines biomedical engineering, ML research, embedded medical devices, and a prior compliance-AI startup; Ayush Patel brings computing, Apple agentic-QA work, and a self-driving robot project. That is credible cross-domain range for a two-person pre-seed team. What is missing is the evidence I value more than credentials: a material belief revised after data or a difficult recruit brought in to own mechanical, electrical, or industrial-deployment risk. The move from a broad “research brain” to robotics could be learning or merely positioning drift; the dossier cannot distinguish them.

The largest kill risk is cross-domain reliability, yet there is no experiment, numerical threshold, cost, or stop condition attached to it. Security features such as permission mirroring, customer-managed keys, and VPC deployment address enterprise access risk, not whether the graph correctly predicts downstream consequences. Marketplace liquidity introduces another untested system before the core technical claim is proven. Capital should first answer one question: can Osseus reliably detect changes that experienced robotics engineers miss?

The strongest bull case is the known exception represented by Block: a tiny team can begin with an incremental-looking tool and expand into infrastructure around an enormous workflow. Osseus shares the platform-shaped wedge and small-team architecture. The material difference is evidence. Osseus has 418 LinkedIn followers and undisclosed onboarding, but no repeat production use, quantified customer constraint, or proof that its dependency record compounds into proprietary capability. My 36-month base case is a pivot toward narrower requirements traceability for one robotics vertical, with less than $1 million ARR, rather than a general intelligent-development platform.

I would not invest my own money today. The pricing tiers disclose a monetization format but no customer ROI or bottom-up path to beating incumbent engineering labor and tooling costs. My decision flips if three named robotics teams complete 90-day production deployments showing at least a 5x reduction in engineering-change validation time while maintaining at least 95% precision on affected dependencies. That result would convert a broad integration claim into a Black-Swan technical option.
