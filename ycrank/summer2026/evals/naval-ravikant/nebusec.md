# NebuSec -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

NebuSec’s founders have acquired specific knowledge through work that can be inspected. Zou’s CVE-2022-27666 exploit and Wu’s automated-exploit-generation repository are artifacts of understanding how systems fail. Tan’s program-analysis research and Yu’s audit experience fit the same problem. PingCAP independently confirms an investigation completed by October 23, 2026. That matters more to me than the degrees: somebody entrusted this team with an actual security incident.

The investment question is whether their discoveries accumulate into something a competitor cannot reproduce. NebuSec’s September benchmark reports 88% coverage across 76 known vulnerabilities, ahead of its reported results for competing tools. That supports investigating the product. It establishes little about durability. The dossier identifies no exclusive training rights, proprietary model, or customer asset that compounds across deployments. Public exploit releases demonstrate the founders’ ability, but making the research public also makes it available to competitors. I give the team substantial credit for knowing the problem and only the absent-evidence prior for a durable advantage.

The strongest bull case is that these researchers can turn expensive investigation into repeatable automated defense. Repository scanning, exploit verification, and patch generation share capabilities with audits and incident investigation; PingCAP supplies a concrete example of adjacent demand. The application-security market’s forecast of $42.09 billion by 2033 gives them room to maneuver, although it does not establish their obtainable market. The danger is that every difficult deployment continues to consume researcher attention while Semgrep and Aikido absorb the repeatable workflows. NebuSec could become a valuable specialist business without producing the economics I need from an angel investment.

I look for systems where participants contribute useful work and individual knowledge becomes scalable output. NebuSec’s roughly 1.7K repository stars show attention; they do not demonstrate customers improving detection or distribution for one another. The proposed exploit-data feedback loop remains an inference without established data rights. Fixed scan quotes and Developer, Business, and Enterprise plans identify a direct charging mechanism, but disclosed prices, collected revenue, and contribution margins are absent. I will not count the PingCAP engagement as proven payment. Nothing here establishes an integrity or financing-pressure red flag; the unresolved issue is how the business compounds.

My exceptions for exceptional founders and demonstrated prelaunch products justify keeping NebuSec under consideration despite immature commercial evidence. They provide no basis to assume the missing asset already exists. I am neutral today: unusually credible builders, a meaningful market, and an unproven mechanism for retaining the value they create. My one verifiable flip condition is a prospective customer study showing that an exclusively licensed corpus of prior deployment findings materially improves detection on unseen repositories versus the same system without that corpus. That would demonstrate their specific knowledge becoming an owned, compounding asset.
