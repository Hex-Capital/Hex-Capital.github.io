# Coasty -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Coasty’s 82.81% OSWorld Verified score makes Coasty an observable machine with an unobservable captain. Replayable trails, structured actions, and mid-task recovery give me something real to inspect; listing Prateek Jannu and Nitish Kovuru only as “Founder,” with no named CEO or tiebreaker, gives me nobody clearly holding the wheel. That high physical verifiability and low governance pairing is Coasty’s signature: the artifact is more legible than the human authority behind it.

**Niantic** is the high rung because I played Ingress and felt the product before Pokémon Go made the thesis obvious. Coasty shares that inspectability through screenshots, timestamped replays, and a published benchmark repository; Coasty is not selling a diagram. Coasty diverges where the analogy matters most: a normal person cannot experience a $0.03 grounding request the way she can walk through a Niantic world, and Coasty’s healthcare demonstration used mock portals rather than a live patient-record workflow. The machine is visible, but only from inside the engine room.

**HQ Trivia** is the low governance rung because two founders without durable tiebreaker authority turned an addictive artifact into a feud. Coasty names two Purdue-connected founders but no CEO, majority operator, vesting protocol, or conflict mechanism. Coasty cannot claim the HQ Trivia exception yet: “thousands of active users and paying customers” is encouraging, but Product Hunt reached only #44 with 84 upvotes, and the 700-person waitlist belonged to an earlier “AI employee” product. Virality once overrode my governance rule; ambiguous cross-product usage does not.

Nitish Kovuru previously built agentic sales automation, while Prateek Jannu worked on ML inference and fault-tolerant pipelines. That gives Coasty technical contact with the machinery, not lived contact with accounting, insurance, healthcare, or regulatory filings. Over 18 months, Coasty’s background should compound into better recovery logic and a useful failure corpus; the same distance from back-office operators may encode the wrong definition of a costly mistake. Coasty’s founders are of computer-use agents, but they appear to be visiting the regulated workflows those agents touch.

Adept has raised $415 million, Browserbase $67.5 million, and UiPath produced $1.43 billion in FY2025 revenue. Coasty is therefore not magically weird merely because screenshots beat selectors; computer-use agents are a crowded consensus category, and Coasty discloses no investor rejection or underpriced round. The counterfactual is crisp: Coasty moves up if sophisticated buyers and investors reject legacy-desktop automation as too operationally ugly while named customers keep renewing specifically because Coasty recovers from interface changes. Until then, Coasty looks technically sharp inside a fashionable market—more rebuttal than hard no, but not a Crusoe-shaped mispricing.

**Vigil Labs** is the useful single-anchor parallel: regulated finance becomes attractive when regulation itself compounds into a moat. Coasty points toward healthcare, compliance, tax, and insurance, but Coasty’s present asset is a software abstraction running commercially available multimodal models. The terms permitting customer content and session data to flow through Coasty’s AI and infrastructure providers may repel the buyers whose ugliness should create the moat. Regulated adjacency is not regulated ownership.

UiPath’s $1.43 billion revenue base makes my 36-month forecast concrete: by July 2029, Coasty will have pivoted from autonomous regulated back-office execution into computer-use testing and observability, reached $3 million–$8 million ARR, and been acquired by UiPath for under $100 million. Coasty’s benchmark performance and replayable trails are valuable components; Coasty’s missing authority signal and weak evidence of production-grade regulated deployment make an independent system of record less likely. I see a very good camera for watching agents fail, not yet the Anduril of back-office labor.

Thousands of active users and paying customers across four continents form the bull case that stings. Coasty already reports 82.81% verified performance, Prateek left Stanford to build, Nitish has adjacent agent experience, and the prior-product pivot could reflect speed rather than confusion; the **HQ Trivia** exception says explosive product love can outrun unresolved governance, while **DeepMind** says polymath technical compounding can outrun an imperfect origin story. Coasty loses that argument today because revenue, retention, and current-product usage remain unquantified, while the regulated demonstration used mock data and the earlier waitlist is not comparable. I do not write my own check yet. Three named regulated customers each renewing after more than 100,000 production actions with fewer than 0.1% uncorrected critical errors would flip my verdict.
