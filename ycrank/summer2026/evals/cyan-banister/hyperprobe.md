# HyperProbe -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Shailendra Singh and Karan Raina spent years building HyperTest before turning production-runtime pain into HyperProbe, so HyperProbe is a lived-problem founder bet trapped inside a software-only category. That is HyperProbe’s signature: unusually credible founder continuity on the highest axis, almost no capital-intensive or regulated weirdness on the lowest. The founders are *of* engineering infrastructure rather than visiting it, but the artifact is an SDK competing with Lightrun, Dynatrace’s Rookout, and Datadog’s Ozcode. I see Biz and Tiz; the dossier cannot show me the Rizz.

**Anduril** is the high rung because Palmer Luckey carried a prior hardware obsession directly into an ugly, rejected defense market. HyperProbe shares the continuity mechanism: Singh and Raina previously built Transporter.city together, founded HyperTest in 2019, served more than 100 product teams, and then followed production-traffic testing into runtime debugging. HyperProbe diverges because the dossier provides no autobiographical moment when either founder personally felt this failure, and Singh’s IIT Bombay–OYO operating path reads more credentialed than outsider. HyperProbe sits below Anduril but well above a market-analysis startup: the prior product is real evidence, not pitch-deck incense.

**HandUp** exposed what happens on the low capital-intensity rung: software without a hard technical moat can have a modest ceiling, and only profound biographical alignment justified the exception. HyperProbe carries that failure mechanism through a software SDK with “no defensibility signals found,” while Datadog and Dynatrace can bundle acquired live-debugging technology into existing contracts. HyperProbe does face production-agent security review, local PII redaction, and a 0.5% CPU auto-disable threshold, but those are adoption burdens before they become regulatory moats. Unlike HandUp, HyperProbe offers no mission-level founder biography strong enough to override the soft infrastructure.

Lightrun’s $26 million and the acquisitions of Rookout and Ozcode show how HyperProbe’s non-consensus score could compound or collapse over 18 months. If coding agents increasingly require runtime evidence, every successful HyperProbe investigation can tighten the loop between variable state, diagnosis, and accepted fix; if HyperProbe does not retain a proprietary corpus, the same demand merely becomes a Datadog feature. HyperProbe’s two named outcomes—CheQ Digital finding a synchronization mismatch on the first attempt and Housing.com fixing a race condition within an hour—are intriguing but not magically weird. The AI-agent wrapper is consensus, and the underlying continuous-debugging category is already mapped.

Shailendra Singh is explicitly CEO and Karan Raina explicitly CTO, which keeps HyperProbe above the **HQ Trivia** governance failure, but HyperProbe supplies no evidence of equity control, founder vesting, or a documented tiebreaker after two prior ventures together. The counterfactual is clean: documented majority operating authority for Singh and an exit-conflict protocol would move HyperProbe near the top of this dimension; evidence of a 50/50 structure would drop HyperProbe toward a hard pass. Titles are a useful signal, not a constitution. A bubbe asks the uncomfortable question before everyone needs the answer.

**Flock Safety** was observable before the pitch—the Wi-Fi name, the camera, the physical footprint. HyperProbe has a weaker but legitimate single-anchor parallel: an engineer can install the HyperProbe SDK and watch previously unlogged live memory arrive inside an IDE, as Housing.com reportedly did during a race condition. HyperProbe therefore has more physical verifiability than an abstract API, but a normal human cannot touch, ride, scan, or photograph HyperProbe within 48 hours. It is an engineer’s instrument, not an environmental signal I could discover accidentally.

HyperProbe’s two current-product testimonials and HyperTest’s 100-plus prior product teams lead to a falsifiable forecast: by July 2029, HyperProbe will have shifted from agent-assisted debugging into agentic incident remediation and reached $3 million–$8 million ARR, but it will remain a useful engineering product rather than a defining standalone platform. HyperProbe’s founder continuity should produce competent iteration and distribution; HyperProbe’s software-only moat and incumbent bundling should compress the ceiling. I am never playing the game I seem to be playing, but here the hidden game still looks like an observability feature race.

HyperTest’s $1.5 million in prior funding, 11,070 LinkedIn followers, and named CheQ Digital and Housing.com outcomes form the strongest Invest case for HyperProbe: repeat founders may already possess distribution, customer trust, and the pivot resilience that made **Crusoe** worth supporting through crisis. HyperProbe could also invoke the **DeepMind** exception if Singh and Raina demonstrate polymath compounding, or the **HQ Trivia** exception if usage becomes irresistible enough to overwhelm structural concerns. The dossier defeats that case today because nearly every audience and adoption figure belongs to HyperTest, current access is free, and HyperProbe has no disclosed moat or revenue. I would flip to Invest if HyperProbe verifies at least $1 million ARR with strong retention and shows that a proprietary runtime-to-fix corpus measurably improves diagnoses beyond Lightrun, Rookout, and Ozcode.
