# Fleety -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to a field-service software platform is Fleety’s strongest exit route. Jobber is a plausible buyer because it could combine Fleety’s booking checks and revenue-based technician matching with its existing crew routing. In one customer deployment, Fleety reported making about 92% of assignments and saving about $60,000 annually in overhead. That suggests customer value. The incremental benefit over a buyer’s native tools, and the value of owning the product, remain open. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Deployments reported in California and Florida and the founders’ dispatch experience make a paid pilot across several shops plausible. The milestone is to reproduce savings against native dispatch while measuring installation and support costs. Fleety says it goes on-site for every deployment, so the capital needed to repeat the result at scale is uncertain. ServiceTitan writeback and Fleety’s listed Jobber and Housecall Pro integrations make a product acquisition technically plausible. Continued API access after a sale and the transferability of customer contracts and data rights remain unverified. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route for this holder. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale scenarios assume an all-cash close by year eight, full participation by the holder, and a pro rata waterfall after net debt and deal costs, with no additional preference deductions. The prices and deductions are illustrative assumptions. Fleety’s disclosed subscription terms give no pricing or revenue figures to support a sale valuation. (The Idea, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Distributable equity | Gross cash to holder |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in net debt and deal costs = $35M | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in net debt and deal costs = $90M | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Cash reinvested through year eight, with no funded payout or eligible share purchase | $0 (0×) |

The strong case clears the objective by $200,000. Another $6.7 million in deductions before pro rata distribution would consume that cushion. A purchase of only half the holder’s stake at the same equity valuation would deliver $1.35 million.

### Countercase

ServiceTitan already offers revenue-based technician matching and controls access to the dispatch board where Fleety writes assignments. A platform could use or extend its native tools, especially if Fleety’s reported savings mostly come from automating assignments that those tools can already handle. On-site setup also raises the possibility that the gains depend on shop-specific work a buyer would have to replicate. That could weaken acquisition demand and price. The evidence establishes neither a closing barrier nor a ceiling on proceeds from the strongest route, so the Pass gate remains open and Watch is appropriate. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Fleety demonstrate repeatable gains over a prospective buyer’s native dispatch tools that support a price clearing the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Paid deployments comparing the two workflows, with measured savings and rollout costs, would let a buyer estimate the value of owning Fleety. Move from Watch to Reach out if that buyer-side analysis supports more than $83.3 million in distributable equity value under plausible deal terms.
