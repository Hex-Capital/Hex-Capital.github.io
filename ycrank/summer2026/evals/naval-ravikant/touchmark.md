# Touchmark -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Roman Yanushevskyi is an IOI gold medalist with Citadel quantitative-research and Lovable AI-engineering experience, while Touchmark has no customer, dataset, or network evidence. That is Touchmark’s signature: unusually strong raw specific knowledge wrapped in a single-sided infrastructure product where the next customer does not yet improve the product for the last. Intelligence can create the instrument. It cannot manufacture compounding. Touchmark looks more like a sharp feature searching for a platform than a platform already revealing itself.

**Replit** is the high rung for specific knowledge: Amjad Masad spent years building code environments before the financing narrative arrived. Touchmark shares the builder-level technical aptitude—Roman’s mathematics, computer science, quantitative research, and AI work are real—but Touchmark diverges where the analogy matters most. No public repository, prior pricing system, evaluation methodology, or shipped artifact shows that either Roman or Ilia Bolgov has lived this exact problem for years. Touchmark sits below Replit because credentials suggest capacity; artifacts prove obsession.

**Perplexity’s** low rung on direct network effects was survivable only because permissionless product distribution, Aravind Srinivas’s retrieval expertise, and pre-consensus AI timing overwhelmed the missing marketplace. The same absence appears more starkly in Touchmark: each AI vendor can consume Touchmark’s SDK independently, and no public mechanism makes one vendor’s participation automatically valuable to another. A proprietary calibration dataset could eventually create scale learning, but Touchmark reports no deployments, scored-output volume, or accepted billing adjustments. A hypothetical data flywheel is not a network.

Touchmark’s reported SDK is code leverage at production but labor leverage at distribution. Over 18 months, Touchmark can replicate evaluations and billing adjustments at near-zero marginal cost, yet the inferred founder-led outreach to AI-product teams means adoption still requires permission one enterprise integration at a time. Touchmark also misses the unstated builder-product pattern today: there is no open-source adoption, public documentation, GitHub community, or organic developer pull. Code is the product; sales appears to be the channel. That earns partial leverage credit, not the Stack Overflow form of permissionless compounding.

Metronome has raised $128 million, Orb $44 million, and Valmi already meters AI-agent outcomes; Touchmark therefore inhabits a potentially large AI-pricing market but has not established pivot-room. Touchmark would move up a full tier if output-quality evaluation became a reusable control layer spanning billing, model routing, procurement, and SLA enforcement. The current evidence is narrower: a “Coming soon” website, no TAM, and a billable quality score that incumbents could accept as another event. If the first idea fails, Touchmark may need to abandon the pricing layer before an adjacent platform emerges.

**Stack Overflow** passed the long-term-founder test because practicing developers had already built and written for developers. Touchmark offers a weaker but positive parallel: Ilia’s Revolut product experience is relevant to pricing systems, and Roman’s IOI gold, Citadel internships, and Lovable work demonstrate intelligence and energy. Touchmark provides no public co-founder history, prior venture, or evidence about how Ilia and Roman handle conflict, so integrity and ten-year partnership remain untested. Silence is neutral here. Pedigree is not partnership.

Touchmark’s website remained “Coming soon” in July 2026, and my 36-month forecast is that by July 2029 Touchmark will have pivoted from autonomous quality-priced billing into evaluation telemetry sold through Orb- or Metronome-style billing workflows, reaching $1 million to $3 million in ARR without becoming an independent pricing platform. Touchmark’s specific knowledge can produce useful measurement infrastructure; Touchmark’s missing network and incumbent-controlled distribution will keep that infrastructure subordinate.

Roman’s technical profile supplies the strongest bull case: Touchmark could define a new pricing primitive precisely when AI vendors are moving toward verified outcomes, then accumulate a proprietary output-to-payment dataset that Metronome and Orb cannot reconstruct. Perplexity is the known exception that stings—single-sided products can win when specific knowledge, permissionless distribution, and category timing align. Touchmark has only the first and perhaps the third; no methodology, customer deployment, dataset, or organic developer channel establishes the second. I would flip to Invest if Touchmark publicly demonstrated at least 50 independent SDK integrations and 10 million scored outputs producing accepted billing adjustments without founder-mediated deployment.
