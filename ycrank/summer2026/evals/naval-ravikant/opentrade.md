# OpenTrade -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The team has learned by shipping, but not yet by surviving the problem. Ojas Kandhare, Tario You, and Dylan Pak built together through 67 Labs, launched the iOS app on July 1, 2026, and moved across trade decks, fantasy leagues, swipeable briefs, and browser drills. Tario adds real AI research ability through Ai2, UW, and an ECCV 2026 paper; Dylan claims distribution experience behind 600M content views. These are action-revealed signals. They do not yet prove specific knowledge about investing behavior, financial transactions, or why users become better investors rather than merely more engaged players.

OpenTrade’s strongest evidence is rapid experimentation and attention capture: weekly active users rose 3× in three days, total users 5× in five days, and one launch animation received more than 100,000 X views. The missing denominators matter. Growth from ten users and growth from ten thousand users are different facts, and 3,411 LinkedIn followers are distribution, not product retention. The Market IQ scorecard’s 50% hit rate and nine-point calibration gap also prevent the AI layer from serving as technical proof. This looks like a capable consumer team discovering a product, not yet a company that has discovered durable demand.

If a competitor copies every screen and drill, little remains. Alinea, Blossom, Invstr, and StockGro already possess overlapping AI research, quizzes, social portfolios, and trade ideas; OpenTrade has disclosed no exclusive data, proprietary model, regulatory position, custody relationship, or accumulated user network. The inferred dataset connecting game decisions to market outcomes could become valuable, but a possible future dataset is not a present asset. Code is leverage only while it remains scarce. Here the implementation is likely the easiest part to reproduce.

The bull case is that the apparent product instability is actually fast search by three young founders who understand short-form consumer behavior better than incumbents. Relative growth, the 100,000-view launch, referral campaigns, and competitive formats could precede a social investing network that becomes legible only after density arrives. That is the strongest exception case. It fails today because users are playing against prompts and ChatGPT, not demonstrably improving the product or distribution for one another. No cohort, contribution, referral, or matching evidence shows value compounding across participants.

The stated $10.9 billion online-trading-platform market supplies room in theory, but OpenTrade currently neither executes trades nor advises users, and it has not identified the capability that carries a game into brokerage, portfolio infrastructure, or another large transaction layer. The free app has no disclosed subscription, referral fee, advertising model, or completed transaction. This is not fatal at pre-seed; it is simply zero evidence of direct value capture. The repeated movement among execution language, fantasy leagues, AI briefs, and training games is not a founder red flag by itself, but it becomes one if skeptical feedback keeps changing the product before any behavior is measured long enough to compound.

I would pass. OpenTrade presently matches none of the permissionless, user-contributed, or infrastructure patterns that would override weak monetization, and the dossier reveals no integrity or artificial-deadline issue that would force a stronger rejection. One condition flips the decision: a published retained cohort showing that user-created portfolios or research artifacts measurably improve another user’s retention or acquisition while producing an exclusive outcome dataset that grows with participation.
