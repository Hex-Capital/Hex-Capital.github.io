# Luca IQ -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is not yet large enough. Luca IQ targets a $23.94 billion tax-software market in which four legacy vendors reportedly handle 95% of U.S. firms, but the demonstrated outcome is 1,100 hours saved and a 57% reduction in administrative preparation time. That is valuable workflow compression. It is not evidence that Luca IQ can make tax expertise 10x cheaper, expand access by 100x, or displace a quantified societal cost pool. The dossier describes a better tax-production system, not a radically different economic system.

The product is more substantial than the fashionable AI-wrapper red flag. AI determines inputs, but a deterministic rules engine calculates the return, preserves a source-level audit trail, validates schemas, and transmits through IRS Modernized e-File. Luca IQ completed a customer tax season and passed IRS Assurance Testing in July 2026. Those are real artifacts. Still, the claimed 30-minute intake-to-review-ready time has no incumbent baseline, and the 57% administrative reduction is not a 5–10x discontinuity against UltraTax or another named system. April and Column Tax already provide authorized filing infrastructure, so API access alone is not a technical monopoly.

The best signal is dollars spent against regulatory risk. Federal 1040 authorization converted a potentially fatal claim into a verified capability before the company scaled distribution. Now the risk sequence becomes less disciplined: state coverage trails April, SOC 2 Type II remains unfinished, and continuous rule maintenance is unavoidable, yet the visible next hire is for outbound sales. I want explicit thresholds for state coverage, security certification, calculation error rates, and accepted-return economics before capital accelerates customer acquisition.

Angelo Policicchio supplies unusually relevant context through CPA, EY, and FBI forensic-accounting work; Akash Sadashivapeth complements that with engineering and data-science experience. This is credible founder-market fit, not proof of exceptional learning rate. The dossier documents neither a material belief revised after customer evidence nor a difficult recruit brought in to own compliance, security, or tax-rule risk. Two Harvard MBAs with complementary résumés can build a competent company. Résumés do not establish the adaptability required to build a defining one.

The bull case stings. Hundreds of firms applied for the pilot, one customer reports 1,100 hours saved, and the team crossed an IRS gate before YC Demo Day. Execution-heavy financial infrastructure can begin as an incremental wedge and evolve into a broader platform; Luca IQ’s calculation API could eventually replace the system of record rather than merely automate data entry. But that exception requires evidence of platform expansion or compounding proprietary data. Today there is no disclosed paid-customer count, revenue, retention, structural barrier, or advantage over well-funded filing engines. Filing histories and review corrections are only an inferred moat.

My 36-month base case is $3–8 million in ARR: a useful CPA infrastructure vendor, but not yet a 100x option. Fixed per-return pricing could support a sound business, yet no published price, gross margin, or all-in cost comparison shows that Luca IQ beats incumbent software plus human labor at scale. I would not invest my own money today. One condition flips the decision: a verified cohort showing at least 5x more accepted returns per labor-hour than UltraTax at a lower all-in cost across 50 paying firms.
