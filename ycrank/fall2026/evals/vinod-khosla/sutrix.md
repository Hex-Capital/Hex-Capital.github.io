# Sutrix -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Sutrix is attacking a genuine bottleneck: clinical researchers spend weeks assembling data before they can ask a scientific question. Making that expertise radically cheaper could expand who conducts research and which studies become economical. But the dossier establishes faster dataset preparation, without quantifying the research spending displaced, additional studies enabled, or population reached. I can see a useful business. The consequence large enough to justify purchasing a low-probability option remains unestablished.

The strongest evidence is deployment. Ten-plus paying institutions, 15-plus labs, and 50-plus studies are meaningful for two founders at this stage. The claimed 80% preparation-time reduction implies roughly 5x throughput for that task, assuming comparable work and quality. That deserves attention. It does not yet establish technical discontinuity: there is no disclosed benchmark method, error rate, or accounting for reviewer labor. Layer Health already reports more than 65% less abstraction time. Those figures come from different workflows and cannot establish Sutrix’s superiority. Source-linked fields and recorded reconciliation decisions make the product inspectable; their existence does not prove scientific correctness.

The bull case is stronger than “another healthcare AI interface.” Ritvik has conducted clinical research at HSS, Emory, and Georgia Tech; Supratik brings software experience and a prior Kairo exit. They have worked together for four years and released software previously used in delivering research engagements. That fits my pattern of scarce scientific workflows becoming repeatable production platforms. The founder-reported $150K-plus revenue shows customers will pay for delivered work. The unresolved mechanism is whether each additional study reuses software or purchases another substantial allocation of founder and reviewer labor. Accumulated reconciliation histories could support learning, but the dossier establishes neither reusable learning rights nor measured improvement across studies.

I would spend the next dollar testing correctness and transferability. Sutrix’s terms require customer verification, and its current FHIR connections are sandboxes; neither fact proves failure. They identify work still to validate. The dossier supplies no ranked kill risk, decisive experimental threshold, or experiment budget, so I give no milestone credit merely for having customers. Likewise, the founders’ complementary backgrounds establish relevant competence, but do not document belief revision or difficult recruiting. Missing evidence receives the default, without treating a two-person team as a weakness.

Economics could decide whether this becomes infrastructure or a research services business. Individually negotiated engagements and undisclosed service/software revenue allocation leave cost per accepted dataset unknown. There is no bottom-up comparison with human preparation after integration, model inference, and independent review. I also cannot conclude that Sutrix triggers my red flags around generic model replication or premature sales expansion; the dossier does not establish either. My exceptions for an extraordinary instigator or overwhelming consequence do not override the gaps here: the founders have credible execution evidence, but neither the exceptional behavioral record nor quantified societal upside needed for that override is documented.

I pass with my own money today. **One verifiable flip condition:** an independently audited prospective deployment across three institutions showing at least 10x lower fully loaded cost per accepted research dataset than manual preparation, with prespecified noninferior scientific accuracy and reviewer time included. That result would establish a mechanism for making research expertise dramatically cheaper. Current adoption earns a serious hearing; it does not yet establish that mechanism.
