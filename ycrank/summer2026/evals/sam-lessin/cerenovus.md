# Cerenovus -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Post-money valuation: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. Whether this is genuinely the first scarce check: undisclosed. YC participation does not prove an auction, but it does not answer the price question either. Since Cerenovus provides no financing terms or evidence that investors have dismissed the thesis, the most important gate remains at its absent-evidence default.

The product contains a plausible venture moment: give Cerenovus read-only access to fragmented enterprise systems and test whether its 26 investigative sweeps can reconstruct operations and uncover financially material leaks with source-level citations. But that is my formulation, not the company’s disclosed capital plan. There is no named use of proceeds, dated milestone, or pass/fail metric. “Build an enterprise knowledge company” is general ambition; “convert three live pilots into paid contracts after documenting recoverable dollars” would be an experiment.

The live pilots are the strongest evidence here. Customer-specific entities, permissions, reviewer decisions, and outcome records could become a compact proprietary workflow that improves with use. The bull case stings: Cerenovus may be entering through a services-heavy diagnostic, earning unusually broad data access, and converting each investigation into a reusable operating map before Glean or Workday treats this workflow as important. Yet the dossier reports no paid conversion, repeat usage, retention, or resulting customer action. Four hundred LinkedIn followers and Compendium’s 122 Product Hunt points establish visibility, not purchasing behavior; Compendium’s traction also cannot be transferred across a July 2026 shift from startup collaboration to enterprise diagnostics.

Deleting the next financing makes the economics disappear. Current pricing, revenue, gross margin, burn, and runway are all undisclosed. The prior product charged $40 per seat while including $40 per user in model credits, which supplies no useful evidence that the new product can support enterprise integration, permission management, human review, and investigation costs from operating cash. Meanwhile, Glean has more than $100 million in ARR and connects 275-plus tools, while Coworker, Hebbia, and Workday’s Sana overlap with the proposed company-model layer. Cerenovus must prove that impact-ranked findings produce cash value before those vendors absorb the feature.

The founders have shipped more than résumés alone imply: Waldorf and Moreland built Compendium, Waldorf worked on quantum-algorithm tooling at QuEra, and Baur has portfolio-management exposure. That earns intermediate credit. It does not establish the outsider-insider translation advantage I want. None has documented responsibility for an enterprise operating system, a scaled B2B deployment, or executive purchasing on the other side of this bridge. Three equal co-founders without a named final decision-maker also activates the accountability red flag.

The known-exception route would require hard external receipts—paid enterprise adoption, retained usage, measurable customer outcomes, or another unmistakable economic signal. “Pilot customers live” does not yet clear that bar. By August 2029, my base case is that Cerenovus has narrowed into a services-heavy private-equity operational-diligence product below $1 million ARR rather than becoming a broad enterprise knowledge platform. I pass today. My verifiable flip condition is a sub-$10 million post-money round explicitly tied to converting three current pilots into paid annual contracts by a fixed date, with at least one customer retained for six months.
