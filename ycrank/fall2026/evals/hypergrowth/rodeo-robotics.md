# Rodeo Robotics -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with repair shops, Rodeo’s most credible growth path is to deploy the same robot for a narrow set of routine jobs across many shops. Oil changes give that path a defined buyer and a recurring job that plausibly transfers across markets. Rodeo’s task-specific software and hardware could be reused, with adaptation for different vehicle mixes and service manuals still unmeasured. A robot that handles daily shop work and uses past jobs in diagnosis has a reason to stay in use. Recurring billing and same-customer expansion remain unestablished. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The memo documents a shop reservation waitlist and an inquiry inbox. Rodeo says early partners will help shape the product ahead of planned January 2027 deployments, with technicians supervising and stepping in. The waitlist is a reusable intake channel. Purchase, installation, and first use still lack a demonstrated repeatable flow. Rodeo’s reported 96% autonomous execution time covers initial tasks and is a technical signal. Paid production and revenue status are unknown, so Initial traction uses the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Vehicle variation and technician supervision could make each new shop expensive to serve even where demand is strong. In a paid deployment scenario, Rodeo’s contribution would be shop revenue less the hardware capacity, installation, maintenance, support, and any intervention labor Rodeo bears. Technician time borne by the shop would reduce its labor savings and willingness to pay. The reported autonomy figure leaves throughput, supervision burden, and contribution unresolved. The structural gate stays open because the memo establishes a need for supervision while leaving its intensity and economic impact uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Rodeo deploy the same configuration at multiple paying shops with different vehicle mixes and earn positive incremental contribution at normal utilization? The planned rollout could answer this with paid job volume, installation hours, technician minutes per job, and attributable hardware and support costs. Repeatable deployment and positive contribution would support Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 with at least Medium confidence. Persistent bespoke work or supervision that makes expansion uneconomic without a demonstrated improvement lever would move the action to Pass.
