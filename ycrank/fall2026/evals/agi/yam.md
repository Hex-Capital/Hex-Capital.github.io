# Yam -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Yam supports the industry-enabled path through a specific use: players can supply their own premise and role for a romance game in which an interacting cast responds to their choices. The authored model represented by Dorian requires creators to write branches in advance. Yam describes generating those branches during play and carrying character memories and relationships across turns. The seed-stage assumption is that this can make bespoke, replayable stories affordable for players whose premises would never justify a separately authored game. The live catalog supports the publishing workflow. The memo offers limited direct evidence on sustained story coherence or the cost of delivering it. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The building-blocks path scores zero. Yam uses external models to deliver a consumer game, and the memo identifies no scarce input Yam supplies to AI developers. Under model parity, its best case for keeping players is the combination of persistent story state, creator publishing, and a catalog spanning 112 fandoms. The catalog is visible today. A creator network effect remains an inference because the memo gives no player-to-creator conversion or retention data. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The memo records 6,528 tracked Android downloads and a public counter of 2.4 million plays for the top listing. Yam separately reports one million beta play hours and more than three hours of average daily playtime per daily active user. These signals support early demand for play. Repeat paid demand remains unverified. The team shipped the game and brings relevant consumer product and creator-tool experience. Listed prices establish what Yam charges, while realized revenue and per-player inference, moderation, and support costs are missing. That leaves scale economics at the default and supports medium confidence. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Yam’s strongest visible engagement signal is a fan-IP title that could be removed after a rights-holder request. Players may primarily want familiar characters, and other roleplay products already offer user-created characters or persistent worlds. If comparable models and existing tools can readily deliver an equivalent multi-character romance experience, the enabled path would also score zero and trigger Pass. The memo leaves that substitution question open, which supports Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Yam show that a cohort of players pays and renews for original, player-directed romance worlds at positive contribution after inference, moderation, support, and the free play used to convert that cohort? Observed renewals tied to those worlds and measured delivery costs would move the action to Reach out. Early usage without that evidence keeps it at Watch.
