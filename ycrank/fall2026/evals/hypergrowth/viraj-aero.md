# Viraj Aero -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Viraj Aero’s strongest path is to sell a standard 10 MW GENA module to data center developers that need on-site power and cooling water. Assuming strong fit at the first sites, the same turbine and water-recovery design could serve that job in other markets. The extent of site adaptation remains uncertain because each deployment needs gas access, air approvals, and integration with the facility. Viraj Aero’s published performance specifications still need continuous-duty validation at a data center. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Viraj Aero reports more than 1.3 GW of demand under letters of intent at its October 29 launch and says the first unit was under construction in November. The reported payment from an engine manufacturer has undisclosed scope and value, leaving realized GENA revenue and its growth rate unknown. The ongoing power and cooling workload could support repeat module purchases as customers expand, though the memo supplies no repeat orders or recurring billing terms. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, The Idea)

### Countercase

GENA could scale as a series of individually engineered power projects. Founder-led outreach and production-slot reservations give buyers a reusable way to start, while procurement and site integration likely require substantial human work through commissioning. Under an equipment-sale scenario, the next module’s sale price must cover manufacturing, delivery, attributable sales and site engineering, commissioning, support, and any added production capacity. The memo gives too little pricing or cost evidence to estimate that contribution. The structural downside gate remains open because the evidence does not establish that replication requires major redesign by market or that the cost of serving additional sites makes faster growth infeasible. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Viraj Aero commission a paid GENA deployment in a second market using the same core module and a repeatable installation plan, with positive incremental contribution? A customer acceptance record, the engineering changes between sites, and actual deployment costs would answer it. Successful replication would support rescoring for Reach out if the case reaches 65 points with at least Medium confidence. Evidence that each new market requires major redesign or that required deployment work makes faster growth uneconomic would move the action to Pass.
