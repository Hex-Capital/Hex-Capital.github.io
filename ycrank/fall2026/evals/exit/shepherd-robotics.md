# Shepherd Robotics -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest supported route. An industrial automation vendor could buy Shepherd to add supervised wiring and assembly for equipment that varies across sites. Shepherd describes adapting its dual-arm robots from technician demonstrations and running the resulting policies locally. Teradyne’s strategic investment in Bright Machines shows buyer interest in adjacent automation. The acquisition rationale depends on Shepherd’s system saving technician time after supervision and integration costs. The memo documents a public demo and reports company-measured bench results of 95% success on cable routing and 60% on cable insertion. Founder experience deploying factory robots supports a production test, while the pilot budget, timing, and available runway remain unverified. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*)

Using the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in cash, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. Both sale scenarios below assume an all-cash sale within eight years, an eligible holder selling their entire stake, and a pro rata waterfall with no preference leakage. The prices and deductions are illustrative assumptions.

- **Modest sale.** $40 million in enterprise value less $5 million in assumed debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $1.05 million, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value less $10 million in assumed debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $2.7 million, or 5.4×. At 2.5% final ownership, the same sale yields $2.25 million, or 4.5×.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested yields zero realized holder proceeds within eight years.

The memo provides no basis for estimating a realizable sale price, confirming transfer rights or holder eligibility, or underwriting a separate liquidity route. Those dimensions use their defaults. (*Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Site-specific adaptation and frequent technician intervention could absorb the labor savings a buyer would pay for. The reported 60% cable-insertion success rate and reliance on technician exception handling make that a concrete risk. Watney also reports operating robots in customer data centers, giving potential buyers another option. Together, this evidence weakens the rationale for acquiring Shepherd. It leaves both Pass gates untriggered because it establishes neither a structural block nor a supported proceeds ceiling below the hurdle. (*Key Risks*, *Market & Traction*)
