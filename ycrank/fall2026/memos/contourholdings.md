## The Idea

- **Problem:** Infrastructure developers require environmental reports, studies, and permit applications, while consulting firms face repetitive manual work that constrains delivery capacity; Contour targets these existing service providers ([Contour website](https://contourholdings.ai/); [YC profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/contourholdings)).
- **Approach:** Contour acquires environmental consulting firms, permanently holds them, and embeds engineers on-site to implement Cortex, whose agents produce reports, studies, and permit applications ([Contour website](https://contourholdings.ai/); [YC profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/contourholdings)).
- **Differentiation:** Contour combines firm ownership with internal automation, whereas Paces sells energy-development software and services and Transect provides environmental site-assessment software and expert guidance ([Contour](https://contourholdings.ai/); [Paces](https://www.paces.com/); [Transect](https://www.transect.com/)).
- **Business Model:** [Inferred]: Monetization would come through acquired consulting practices’ service fees, given its stated acquisition-and-hold strategy; public pricing and company revenue: No public data found ([Contour website](https://contourholdings.ai/)).
- **TAM/SAM:** The broader U.S. environmental consulting market was estimated at $27.0B in 2024, reaching $32.3B in 2030 at 3.0% CAGR, but this includes services beyond Contour’s stated permitting focus and is not a measured SAM ([KPMG, Summer 2025, citing IBISWorld](https://corporatefinance.kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2025/engineering-environmental-infrastructure.pdf)).
- **GTM / Distribution:** Contour’s website solicits environmental compliance practice owners considering succession or ownership changes, establishing an acquisition-sourcing channel ([Contour website](https://contourholdings.ai/)).
