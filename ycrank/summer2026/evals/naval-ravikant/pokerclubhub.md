# PokerClubHub -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

PokerClubHub has shipped an operating system for licensed private poker, but the dossier does not reveal who shipped it or what they learned through costly action. The April 14, 2026 Anjouan license, Fireblocks custody integration, and BMM-certified RNG are real artifacts. Yet with no founder names, biographies, prior products, or operating history, I cannot distinguish specific knowledge from competent outsourcing. Missing identity is uncertainty, not an integrity violation, but investing my own money requires knowing the principals.

The strongest case is that PokerClubHub has moved the hardest part of private-club poker onto the platform. ClubGG, PokerBROS, and PPPoker leave real-money settlement outside their nominally play-money products; PokerClubHub directly receives USDT and USDC, processes withdrawals, and pays hosts affiliate fees. Licensing, custody, and compliance operations survive copying the interface better than ordinary software does. But the Anjouan authorization expires April 13, 2027, Fireblocks is an external dependency, and PokerBROS already combines BMM certification with more than 500,000 downloads. The company owns an operational head start, not yet a compounding asset.

The club model could become permissionless distribution: hosts recruit players and supply games while the platform handles trust and settlement. That is the bull case, and it matters. But a network is measured by what participants contribute, not by drawing arrows between them. The dossier discloses no active clubs, players, transaction volume, repeat usage, referrals, or liquidity improvement. A launch post with roughly 4,000 views and a Two Plus Two thread with 133 views and one reply show exposure, not adoption. No Known Exception rescues the absence: there is no demonstrated behavior already compounding beneath an unfinished business model.

The market evidence is similarly bounded. A $3.9 billion global online-poker market projected to reach $6.9 billion by 2030 supports a real category, but it does not identify the adjacent market PokerClubHub can enter if licensed private clubs remain narrow. Jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction restrictions may shrink that room further. The disclosed wedge is not yet a platform for broader gaming settlement, compliance, or custody; those are possible pivots rather than evidenced demand.

The economics are opaque. Club owners apparently receive affiliate fees, but the dossier provides no retained rake, subscription price, take rate, paying club, completed transaction, or revenue. PokerClubHub may be assuming custody and regulatory risk before proving direct value capture. My flip condition is verifiable: disclose at least 25 active clubs, $1 million in monthly settled volume, a positive retained take rate, and three-month player cohorts showing that additional hosts or players measurably improve retention or acquisition. Until then, this is infrastructure-shaped execution without founder evidence, network evidence, or economic proof. I pass.
