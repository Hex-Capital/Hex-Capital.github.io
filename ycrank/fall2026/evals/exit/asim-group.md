# Asim -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest exit path is a strategic sale to a robotics manufacturer or deployment integrator. Asim describes repeatable physical trials tailored to a customer’s robot and operating conditions, with held-out scenarios for testing unfamiliar cases. A buyer could value owning that capability if it consistently catches costly failures that its internal tests miss. The memo records a November launch and YC backing, with conflicting funding estimates. It supplies no paid repeat engagement, measured buyer savings, or acquisition price evidence. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A paid evaluation repeated across two releases is a plausible seed-stage milestone. Asim already solicits scoped evaluations, and the team has relevant robotics and benchmarking experience. Supervision and scene resets are known cost drivers. Pilot costs and available runway remain unverified. Asim says it uses separate written service agreements. The memo provides no transaction-specific evidence on assignment or rights to retain and transfer customer results, and it supports no independent liquidity route. (The Idea, Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale scenarios below assume the holder is eligible to sell the full stake, all distributable equity pays pro rata after the stated deductions, and cash arrives within eight years. The prices and terms are illustrative assumptions.

- **Modest sale.** $40 million enterprise value less $5 million in assumed debt and deal deductions leaves $35 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $1.05 million, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million enterprise value less $10 million in assumed debt and deal deductions leaves $90 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $2.7 million, or 5.4×. If only 80% of that payout arrives in cash within eight years, realized proceeds fall to $2.16 million, or 4.32×.
- **Prolonged private.** If Asim keeps operating and reinvests available cash, with no eligible share purchase or funded payout, the holder realizes $0 within eight years.

### Countercase

A manufacturer could run common tests in-house using NIST’s planned baseline apparatus and buy specialized evaluations as needed. Asim’s physical trials can require supervision and resets, and the value of its held-out tests depends on whether the failures they surface matter in deployment. This weakens the case for paying an acquisition premium. The evidence leaves room for differentiated testing to prove valuable and establishes neither a structural closing block nor a ceiling on credible proceeds. The Pass gate stays open pending evidence. (Defensibility, The Idea, Key Risks)
