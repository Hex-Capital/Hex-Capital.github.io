# Perceptual Labs -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Perceptual Labs gives me a category to consider, but no transaction to underwrite. Post-money valuation: unknown. Competing term sheets: unknown. Whether my money would be the first scarce check: unknown. I cannot infer an auction from YC membership, or cheap entry from an inaccessible website. The round receives my absent-evidence default. The second gate is equally unresolved: “building the next generation of visual intelligence” identifies neither the hypothesis my check tests nor a dated result that would materially change the business’s value.

The interesting asset could be exclusive access to visual data or an embedded customer workflow. That would fit my preference for proprietary access that survives once the technology becomes widely available. But the dossier labels the dataset thesis as inferred; it verifies neither ownership nor a resulting customer action. Roboflow and TwelveLabs establish that capable adjacent suppliers exist. Their presence makes the question of who owns the customer’s data and workflow consequential, while Perceptual Labs’s undisclosed scope prevents any concrete substitution analysis. I have no basis to award distribution credit—or to declare this a generic AI wrapper.

Delete the next financing from the plan and there is still no operating model to inspect. Revenue, gross margin, burn, pricing, and runway are undisclosed, so economics receive the stated default rather than an invented assumption of capital intensity. The two-person team could build efficiently, but headcount does not establish that. Founder identities and operating histories are also unavailable. My pattern of backing people who bridge two systems requires evidence of access and execution on both sides; the dossier cannot establish either side here.

The strongest objection to passing is the research process itself. Both the website and YC profile were inaccessible after retries on October 29, 2026. That can hide an excellent product just as easily as an undifferentiated one. A two-person infrastructure company could have substantial technical work and private customer relationships without a public footprint. This substantially limits my confidence about company quality. It supplies no affirmative underwriting evidence, however. The known exceptions in my framework rely on documented conversion, retention, acquisition interest, or a prior exit paired with a concrete financing structure. None is established here. Nor does the dossier establish the auction pressure, financing dependency, or error-free automation assumptions that would constitute active red flags.

I pass at **28/100**, with low confidence about the underlying business and high confidence that this dossier cannot support my check. My verifiable flip condition for moving into active diligence is one customer-confirmed paid deployment showing that exclusive visual data or embedded workflow caused the purchase and that delivery produces positive contribution margin. That would establish a functioning economic asset worth investigating. Today, Perceptual Labs offers no documented mechanism connecting its visual-intelligence ambition to owned demand or cash.
