# Grep AI -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Grep AI’s founders have earned specific knowledge. Asver and Ríos worked together at Brex in 2020, encountered the verification backlog, and then operated compliance agents through Parcha beginning in 2023. That is a sequence of problem, action, and deployment—not an inference from prestigious employers. Their strongest qualification is having learned what happens when an automated decision meets an exception inside a financial institution ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/grep-ai), [Grep About](https://grep.ai/about)).

The investment question is whether that learning accumulates somewhere a competitor cannot reproduce. Converting repeated reasoning into code can improve execution economics, but competitors can implement the same optimization. Grep has something more concrete at IG: encoded review criteria and a workflow tested against live cases before governance approval. Those are deployment assets. The dossier does not establish exclusive rights, proprietary learning across deployments, or demonstrated switching costs. I give the existing procedures credit without assuming they constitute a durable moat ([IG case study](https://grep.ai/case-studies/ig)).

The strongest argument against my hesitation is IG’s reported result: screening automation increased from 10% to 30%, flagged-customer activation fell from two or three days to under ten minutes, and accuracy exceeded 99%. Airwallex, Worldpay, and Citizens Bank also appear among named customers. This deserves more attention than another agent demonstration. The founder-led exception to demanding an established network is relevant: these founders have shipped into the exact problem. But the accompanying evidence of capital efficiency is unresolved. Asver reported nearly $80,000 in monthly AI spending and loss-making runs before redesigning the harness; current margins remain undisclosed. Customer value is demonstrated more clearly than shareholder value capture.

Grep AI has room to maneuver. Compliance investigations can lead into onboarding, diligence, and broader document-heavy operations using the same instruction-to-procedure capability. n8n’s estimated $100 million ARR provides evidence that enterprises spend on adjacent automation, although it is competitor revenue, not Grep’s addressable revenue. The same market also brings substitution: SAP is distributing n8n through Joule Studio. Grep needs an advantage that survives both cheaper implementation and an incumbent owning the customer relationship ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/n8n/), [n8n/SAP](https://blog.n8n.io/n8n-sap/)).

The missing compounding mechanism matters to me. Hundreds of preview users, an open-source project, and referral credits establish access paths; they do not demonstrate that one customer makes another customer’s product better. Grep fits my interest in turning individual knowledge into reusable output, but the documented reuse occurs within a deployment. Permissionless contribution and customer-created distribution remain unproven. Likewise, $200 and $500 monthly plans establish a direct charging mechanism, while realized revenue and contribution margins remain unknown. Nothing supplied establishes an integrity or artificial-deadline veto; the concern is economic structure.

At 57, I am neutral and would keep my money uncommitted. Grep AI has unusually credible builders and meaningful operating evidence, but insufficient proof that its advantage compounds beyond their continued work. My flip condition is a controlled replication test showing that accumulated, contractually exclusive case knowledge materially improves held-out compliance decisions over an otherwise identical stack without that knowledge. That would establish something valuable remaining after a competitor copies the code.
