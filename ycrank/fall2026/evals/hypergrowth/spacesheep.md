# Spacesheep -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with teams that use multiple coding agents, Spacesheep has a portable recurring job. Teams can publish agent output through the same MCP connector or CLI and review it through hosted links. The workflow can plausibly travel across markets with modest changes for teams permitted to send coding-session content to a hosted service. The company documents scoped access and enterprise controls, though some buyers may still require approval. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The company documents self-serve signup and a path from agent setup to a first published space. Sharing that space brings teammates directly into the review workflow and could drive team adoption. Claude Code on the web adds a connector or allowlisting step, and the memo leaves independent payment and repeatable conversion unverified. The October launch drew measurable attention, and the company showed a lab dashboard and live camera stream. Those signals establish interest and illustrate usage. The memo leaves paid production use, realized revenue, and commercial growth unknown. (The Idea, Key Risks, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

If teams repeatedly use Spacesheep for reviews, accumulated comments, revisions, and searchable work could support renewals. The published monthly plans give retained customers ways to spend more through upgrades and $10-per-member Team seats. Expansion could offset churn, though the memo gives no evidence of that outcome. In a Team-plan scenario, each additional paid seat adds $10 per month before incremental hosting, storage, model inference, and attributable onboarding and support. Tiered model allowances and deployment limits give Spacesheep a way to charge more for heavier workloads. Contribution under typical usage remains unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility)

### Countercase

Claude Artifacts and ChatGPT Sites already let users publish inside the tools where they generate the work. If most teams primarily use one agent, native publishing may be sufficient, leaving Spacesheep’s shared review workflow valuable to a narrower set of buyers. Export to Drive or GitHub also makes it easier to leave. This weakens the case for recurring paid use. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo supports a common cross-agent workflow and establishes neither a need for substantial market-by-market rebuilding nor an unavoidable cost that makes scaling infeasible. (Defensibility, Key Risks, The Idea)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of teams that use multiple coding agents sign up, publish, and become recurring, contribution-positive Team customers through the standard self-serve flow? A cohort outside the free Team promotion that independently publishes, repeatedly reviews work across agents, and renews at positive incremental contribution would move the action to Reach out. If most teams stop after the first share or need sustained hands-on help to keep using the product, keep it on Watch.
