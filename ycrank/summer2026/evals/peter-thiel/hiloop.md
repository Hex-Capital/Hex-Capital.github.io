# hiloop -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Karan Brar led ML infrastructure at DynamoAI, while Thomas Boser shipped production ML systems at Discord, SoFi, Crosswise, and Reducto. hiloop is therefore an operator-built infrastructure wager whose highest signal is founder lineage and whose lowest is structural moat: credible builders optimizing 4,188 experiments on 50 B200 GPUs, but no owned atoms, regulatory barrier, or protected customer network. hiloop may build an important product; importance without durable power is consultancy disguised as infrastructure.

**Anduril** is the high rung for operator pedigree because Palmer Luckey had already shipped Oculus to a billion-dollar exit before attacking defense. hiloop shares the adjacent-domain mechanism: Brar and Boser worked together on ML, post-training, and platform systems at Reducto, so hiloop emerges from lived technical friction rather than YC tourism. The divergence is decisive: neither hiloop founder has a verified exit, scaled product, Olympiad-level signal, or Luckey-style achievement that independently de-risks the leap from competent tooling to category monopoly.

**Asana** exposes the low-rung failure mechanism on structural moat: pure software in a mimetic workflow market, with founder pedigree required to override the absence of atoms or regulation. hiloop exhibits the same structural weakness more sharply. The 50 B200 GPUs are rented inputs, not hiloop-owned capex; customer-cloud deployment lowers adoption friction but also prevents infrastructure ownership from becoming a barrier. hiloop has neither Asana’s Moskovitz exception nor a Palantir-like regulated customer relationship. “We wanted flying cars, instead we got 140 characters” applies even when the 140 characters are CUDA kernels.

**Prime Intellect’s** self-improving-agent platform and 6,000 reported customers reveal hiloop’s contrarian-secret problem. Over the next 18 months, experiment orchestration, lineage, snapshots, and brokered secrets will compound only if hiloop discovers results competitors structurally cannot reproduce; otherwise Together AI and OpenPipe can bundle the same control plane around compute they already sell. hiloop’s inferred secret—that autonomous agents should optimize data, SFT, prompts, tools, and harnesses together—is interesting, but hiloop has not named why Prime Intellect’s architecture or Together AI’s economics make that impossible. Competition is not validation. Competition is the absence of a secret.

**The 0.9016 validation bits-per-byte result**, beating the cited 0.9109 benchmark, gives hiloop a technical proof but not yet a pricing-power hypothesis. hiloop would move into an Invest tier if repeated customer campaigns created proprietary task-to-experiment data that delivered measurable gains unavailable without hiloop, producing multi-year workflow lock-in and premium enterprise pricing. Without that counterfactual, the private monorepo, SQL-queryable traces, and forkable snapshots remain reproducible features in a market where Together AI has announced $1.21B in financing and can subsidize orchestration to sell compute.

**SpaceX** is the single-anchor parallel for definite planning: it committed to a multi-year reusability architecture rather than promising to learn what rockets customers wanted. hiloop’s 4,188 experiments in two days and 12.54% NanoGPT runtime reduction show an unusually concrete operating system for search, but hiloop’s public plan ends at founder-led campaigns for teams with measurable objectives. No two-to-five-year architectural milestones, target workload, or non-pivot commitment appear. The machinery is definite; the destination remains indefinite.

**Together AI’s $1.21B financing** points to the most likely 36-month outcome: hiloop reaches roughly $2M–$6M ARR in specialized post-training campaigns and is acquired by Together AI for its experiment-control plane rather than becoming an independent monopoly. hiloop’s founder pedigree can produce technically valuable infrastructure, while hiloop’s absent atoms, regulatory moat, and proprietary dataset make absorption by a compute platform more likely than durable pricing power. In Tolkien’s terms, hiloop may forge a useful ring; Together already owns the territory.

**Asana’s known exception** is the strongest objection: exceptional operators can overcome software-only commoditization, and hiloop’s Reducto relationship, 4,188-run demonstration, customer-cloud deployment, and benchmark improvement could conceal a Stripe-like secret about autonomous research loops becoming the default interface to compute. hiloop might accumulate outcome data faster than Prime Intellect can integrate it, turning experiment lineage into a network of proprietary optimization priors. The objection fails today because hiloop has no public customer, revenue, retained dataset, or named incumbent blind spot, while Asana had Dustin Moskovitz and Stripe had a precise failure theory for legacy payments. I would flip to Invest if hiloop reaches $3M ARR with at least five unrelated customers whose measured optimization gains persist and materially deteriorate when hiloop is removed.
