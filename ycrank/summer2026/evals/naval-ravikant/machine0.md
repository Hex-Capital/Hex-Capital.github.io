# machine0 -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Barnaby Malet has specific knowledge earned through action. He researched cloud resource allocation, ran DevOps, infrastructure, and security as Upflow’s CTO/CPO, built the Director MCP gateway, and then pivoted after observing agent runs extend to six or eight hours. By August 2026, one person had shipped an SSH-ready cloud product supporting static IPs, snapshots, credential profiles, and machines up to eight H200 GPUs. PACIFIC and Geoffrey Négiar provide modest evidence that someone beyond the founder uses it. This is not résumé proximity; it is direct contact with the problem.

The problem is that machine0 currently turns that knowledge into a feature advantage, not a durable asset. Its VMs run on DigitalOcean. Modal added full-VM sandboxes in June 2026, while Daytona already offers persistent, snapshot-capable environments and reported $5 million in annualized revenue. Static endpoints, root access, and NixOS provisioning may win users today, but a competitor copying the control-plane code inherits the same underlying compute. No proprietary model, scarce data, hardware, exclusive supply, or accumulated user network survives that counterfactual.

The bull case is stronger than the score alone suggests. A solo founder has compressed a complicated infrastructure product into `machine0 new mybox`, reached 95 points and 36 comments on Hacker News, attracted an independent review, and attached transparent per-minute prices to real compute. Notion is the relevant exception: a capital-efficient founder and working product can deserve patience before a user loop becomes visible. The material difference is that machine0 already faces heavily financed competitors selling convergent infrastructure; early product taste must become a compounding asset before their distribution absorbs it.

The market is broad in category but narrow in demonstrated wedge. Persistent agent computers could grow into fleet orchestration, security, deployment, and managed infrastructure, and Modal’s reported $300 million-plus annualized revenue proves substantial adjacent spend. But the dossier does not yet show why machine0’s persistent-VM entry point causally owns those layers. Today, every new customer consumes rented compute without making the service more valuable or easier to distribute to the next customer. This is centralized resale, not permissionless leverage.

Direct pricing is better than business-model ambiguity: CPU instances begin at $0.013 per hour, GPUs at $0.836, storage at $0.078 per GB per month, and customers must make a $5 minimum top-up. But listed prices are not realized value capture. Neither PACIFIC nor Négiar is tied to paid usage, retention, gross profit, or expanding consumption. Supplier dependence also constrains the economics before scale supplies bargaining power.

I would pass today. I see no integrity or artificial-scarcity red flag; the failure mode is structural, not personal. The verifiable flip condition is six-month cohort evidence showing that retained agent fleets accumulate profiles, snapshots, or operational data that materially improve deployment or switching costs, alongside at least $1 million ARR without services-heavy onboarding. Until then, machine0 is a capable founder renting commodity leverage in a market where larger principals can copy the interface.
