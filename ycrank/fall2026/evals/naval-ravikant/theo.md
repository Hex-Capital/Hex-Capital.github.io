# Theo -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Miles Pines has earned a hearing through action. He reports acquiring thousands of monthly organic clicks for Endgrate, then doing the same work for friends before building Theo. The live product and 37 Trustpilot reviews provide evidence beyond a résumé. Harvard and Citadel matter less than having repeatedly encountered the customer’s problem. His reported $100,000-plus annual revenue belongs to Endgrate; it establishes operating experience, not Theo’s economics. I give the founder substantial credit, with some uncertainty around the independently unverified SEO history.

The decisive question is what Theo accumulates when it does the work. Connecting Search Console, finding keywords, and publishing weekly improvements can create customer value. The dossier identifies no exclusive data, proprietary model, or demonstrated switching cost that makes those improvements difficult to reproduce. Customer analytics could eventually support a learning advantage, but connecting accounts does not establish that advantage. Durable already combines website creation with SEO/GEO, and Wix has its own AI builder. If competitors reproduce Theo’s workflow, the evidence does not yet identify what remains scarce.

The strongest objection to withholding a check is that customers may care much more about results than scarcity. Theo reports 10,000-plus sites built by September 28, 2026, approximately 4,000 audits monthly, and a customer whose Google clicks increased from 131 to 929. Those signals deserve attention. An effective automated service could replace recurring agency work and give small business owners more output without hiring employees—a pattern I like. But sites built do not establish recurring use, and these customer outcomes do not show that one customer improves another customer’s experience. Theo currently demonstrates automation of individual work; the customer-created network remains unproven.

There is a credible commercial wedge: sell ongoing discovery improvements to businesses already needing a website. The estimated $5.3 billion website-builder market establishes meaningful category spend, while freelancers and agencies identify an existing alternative. The dossier does not establish a specific expansion into adjacent buyers or transactions, and Theo currently lacks native bookings and payments. Its $69.99 Starter and $129.99 Pro subscriptions put an explicit price on continuing optimization. That is useful business-model evidence, although published prices establish neither realized revenue nor contribution margins.

I would remain neutral and keep my money uncommitted. My exceptions for unfinished monetization require another unusually strong mechanism: an emerging network, exceptional founder evidence, or a distinctive prototype with demonstrated capital efficiency. Theo’s shipped product makes that objection serious, but the dossier does not establish enough to override the durability concern. There is no documented integrity problem, artificial financing deadline, or reassurance-seeking behavior; this is a structural reservation. My one verifiable flip condition is a controlled deployment study showing that a proprietary model trained on permissioned cross-customer outcomes consistently outperforms the same workflow without that accumulated learning. That would turn additional customers into a compounding asset.
