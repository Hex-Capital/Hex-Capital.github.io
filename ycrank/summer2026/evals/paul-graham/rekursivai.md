# rekursiv.ai -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Joshua V. Dillon created TensorFlow Probability, and Dan Kondratyuk authored an ICML Best Paper before rekursiv.ai made autonomous researchers run 984 experiments over 30 days. That combination defines rekursiv.ai: unusually strong hackers building a machine they themselves would use, but with little evidence yet of the external schlep that turns a research system into a company. rekursiv.ai looks more like the Apple I on Wozniak’s bench than an established business. That is both the attraction and the danger.

**Stripe** is the high rung for hacker genealogy because the Collisons combined technical taste with a tool born from their own commerce frustration. Dillon’s Veo prototype and Kondratyuk’s VideoPoet work put rekursiv.ai unusually close to that level; their public repositories have reached 504 and 1,200 stars, and rekursiv.ai has already produced working artifacts rather than slides. The divergence matters: Stripe’s code immediately removed pain for outside developers, while rekursiv.ai’s strongest evidence remains company-run ARC and Sudoku benchmarks. Great hackers can produce beautiful research systems that nobody buys.

**Kiko** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism on schlep: clever software without an accumulating unpleasant advantage can remain merely clever software. rekursiv.ai has real technical toil—235 ARC experiments, 684 Sudoku experiments, runs lasting up to 48 hours—but rekursiv.ai has not yet shown the customer integration, compliance, manual onboarding, or institutional resistance that made Coinbase’s grind valuable. Experiment duration is a nuisance. It is not yet a moat. Sakana’s open-source AI Scientist and rekursiv.ai’s own Apache-2.0 releases make the absence of a harder schlep more consequential.

984 experiments over 30 days are evidence of resourcefulness because the loop reportedly took eight system attempts to establish, and rekursiv.ai then pushed it through four days of Sudoku experiments to 100% accuracy across 99,768 puzzles. Over the next 18 months, that habit can compound: faster experiments produce better research agents, which produce faster experiments. But rekursiv.ai has not shown the Airbnb kind of animal track—no cereal boxes, manual customer rescue, or improvised revenue. rekursiv.ai has proved persistence inside the laboratory, not yet refusal to accept “no” from the world.

Joshua V. Dillon spent 13 years at Google Research and DeepMind, while Kondratyuk led Luma AI’s World Models group, so the idea behind rekursiv.ai is almost maximally organic. rekursiv.ai is automating the exact serial hypothesis-and-experiment workflow its founders inhabited. The counterfactual that would lower this dimension is evidence that outside researchers do not share the pain—that the system merely lets its creators run entertaining benchmarks faster. Repeat use by three independent ML laboratories would resolve that ambiguity and make the organic origin commercially informative rather than merely autobiographical.

**Twitch** benefited from Kan and Shear having already failed together at Kiko; adversity had tested the relationship before the second bet. Dillon and Kondratyuk worked together at both Google and Luma AI, and Kondratyuk publicly called Dillon a friend, giving rekursiv.ai a much stronger configuration than a pair assembled for an application. rekursiv.ai still lacks the Twitch-level evidence of surviving a failure together, but two technical founders with overlapping history are the configuration I want. There is no solo-founder or newly paired-cofounder red flag here.

$5M of financing and benchmark results as high as 75.5% on ARC-AGI-1 make rekursiv.ai likely to create valuable technology, but the high-hacker, low-schlep signature points toward absorption rather than an independent research platform. My 36-month forecast is that Google DeepMind acquires rekursiv.ai for its founders and research-loop technology before rekursiv.ai exceeds $3M ARR. The mechanism is straightforward: rekursiv.ai can demonstrate inventions faster than it can build proprietary distribution, while Google already understands both founders’ work and can deploy the system across a much larger research organization.

Sakana AI’s $412M, Periodic Labs’ $300M, Lila Sciences’ $550M, and Edison Scientific’s $70M form the strongest bear case: rekursiv.ai could be open-sourcing components into a capital war while reporting benchmarks that fall four to six points on semi-private ARC evaluation and fail to transfer cleanly from Sudoku. That commoditization mechanism stings because rekursiv.ai has no published revenue, only 14 GitHub-organization followers, and no identified moat. But capital is not hacker quality, and rekursiv.ai’s founders have already built TensorFlow Probability, Veo, and VideoPoet before producing nearly a thousand autonomous experiments. I would invest in the hackers, with the acquisition outcome as the floor. I would flip to Pass if, by July 2027, rekursiv.ai cannot show three independent research organizations each paying at least $100,000 annually and repeatedly running experiments through the system.
