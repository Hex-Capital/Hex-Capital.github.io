# Lantern AI -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an applicant tracking system (ATS) vendor is Lantern’s strongest exit path. Lantern’s agents share employer-approved hiring criteria and feedback across sourcing, screening, and interviews while working on top of an existing ATS. An acquirer could buy that capability to add workflows faster and sell more modules to its customers. The memo documents Ashby’s acquisition of AI interviewer technology and cites a Lantern customer who reported screening more than 1,000 candidates and hiring one engineer. Those observations support buyer interest in the category and real workflow use. A buyer’s economic benefit from owning Lantern remains unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Lantern reports $1M in contracted enterprise ARR and one account expanding from about $11K to $92K annually. A proposed 12–18 month milestone is renewal and paid expansion across several accounts, with measured recruiter time saved against native ATS tools. Existing contracts make that test plausible. Integration coverage varies by ATS, and the memo gives no burn or runway figures to assess how much capital Lantern would need to complete it. A software sale is plausible, with assignment of customer contracts and continued use of candidate and employer data still unverified. The memo provides no evidence of an independent route to holder liquidity. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

The benchmark is a $500,000 check at a $10M post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3% with no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5M within eight years, or about $83.3M in distributable equity value. The sale scenarios assume a full cash sale within that window, $5M in net debt and deal deductions, and an eligible holder receiving 3% of the remaining equity proceeds at closing, with no preference diversion or holdback. Revenue and multiples are scenario assumptions.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $5M ARR × 5 = $25M enterprise value, less $5M = $20M distributable equity | $600K (1.2×) |
| Strong sale | $15M ARR × 6 = $90M enterprise value, less $5M = $85M distributable equity | $2.55M (5.1×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operations with cash reinvested and no eligible share purchase or payout | $0 realized |

The strong case clears the hurdle narrowly. At 5× ARR on the same $15M revenue, the holder receives $2.1M. The memo leaves both that scale and the price a buyer would pay unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Ashby already has native interviewing technology, and Workday has announced recruiting automation and embedded candidate grading. An ATS vendor may be able to deliver enough of Lantern’s benefit through its own products, while variable integration coverage could make Lantern harder to absorb. The memo offers no comparative performance or migration data showing that Lantern’s shared hiring criteria create an advantage worth acquiring. This makes buyer willingness to pay the decisive gap. The evidence establishes neither a structural closing block nor a price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle, so the action is Watch. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Could an ATS vendor justify paying roughly $90M for Lantern based on the incremental value of owning its cross-workflow product? Measured gains over native tools across several renewing, expanding enterprise accounts, combined with buyer-side build-versus-buy economics supporting that price, would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that even the strongest credible buyer price leaves the holder materially below $2.5M would move it to Pass.
