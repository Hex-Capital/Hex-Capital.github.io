# Elite -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is presently too small and too vague. Elite points to a $13.7 billion conversational-healthcare market, but does not quantify front-office spending, patients affected by unanswered calls, or the cost eliminated per resolved interaction. Automating scheduling, referrals, refills, eligibility, intake, and billing could improve access. Nothing in the dossier shows an order-of-magnitude change in healthcare delivery rather than a more efficient call center.

The technical case is weaker. Protocols for 18 specialties and write-back to more than 19 EHRs demonstrate implementation breadth, not a 5–10x discontinuity. Assort already covers 23-plus specialties, Hyro has health-system deployments and explainability controls, and Syllable spans the same voice, SMS, and chat channels. Elite provides no measured resolution rate, safety benchmark, cost comparison, proprietary model, or learning advantage against these named incumbents. This is currently an integration product inside a heavily funded consensus market.

The strongest bull case is the speed of the two-person team. Between Stratum’s government-review launch on July 29, 2026 and Elite’s HIPAA notice on August 22, the brothers redirected their software into healthcare, assembled specialty protocols, exposed a voice demo, and claimed broad EHR connectivity. Vihaan was founding engineer number three at an acquired company and previously generated more than 1,000 campaign signups in 48 hours; Raj supplies GTM experience from Deel. Rapid construction and complementary commercial and technical roles matter. But a rapid pivot is not yet evidence of rapid belief revision: the dossier gives no customer observation that caused the change, and neither founder has documented healthcare-operations experience or a risk-specific clinical recruit.

The largest kill risk is not whether the agent can answer a telephone. It is whether it can perform refill, referral, triage, eligibility, and billing actions without creating clinically or financially dangerous errors. Elite discloses escalation, audit logs, encryption, HIPAA compliance, and BAAs, but no prospective test, pass/fail threshold, error taxonomy, clinical owner, or stop condition. Capital spent expanding integrations before measuring safety-adjusted autonomous resolution may scale the surface area of failure rather than remove risk.

Outcome-based pricing is directionally intelligent because Elite is paid only for completed work, but there is no public price, human-cost baseline, gross margin, inference cost, exception-handling burden, or implementation-cost curve. If practices save money only while the founders absorb integration and escalation labor, the apparent automation is subsidized services. The future dataset could become valuable, but no customer volume or proprietary dataset exists today; resemblance to a learning loop is not a learning loop.

I pass. My one flip condition is a blinded, independently auditable deployment across at least ten practices showing a 10x reduction in cost per correctly resolved interaction versus staffed front offices, with at least 90% autonomous resolution and fewer than one safety-critical routing or EHR-action error per 10,000 interactions. That result would convert Elite from another healthcare voice interface into a technical cost-collapse option.
