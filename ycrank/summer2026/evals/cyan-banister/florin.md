# Florin -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. A robotics factory, launch provider, or advanced-energy operator should not complete a generic form and wait a week for someone to force an unfamiliar machine into an old class code. Its changing physical operations should be continuously legible to insurance capital. Florin has found a real leak from that future: applicants are being delayed or declined because rate tables cannot describe them, while its system claims to combine permits, imagery, public records, and simulations into a bindable quote in under one minute. What is missing is the path between those points—regulatory authority, capacity, reinsurance, claims feedback, and proof that these external signals price losses rather than merely accelerate intake.

The broken rule is Florin’s strongest evidence. Existing carriers control access through class codes, incomplete-application loops, and week-long underwriting; Florin proposes returning purchasing speed and coverage access to frontier-technology companies. But “zero underwriters” is not itself the liberation. Underwriters do not create the underlying risk, and removing them before the model has loss data can transfer uncertainty onto policyholders or capital providers rather than eliminate a rent. The dossier identifies the gate and its delay, but not who economically captures that delay or whether Florin can safely assume the rejected risks.

This team can build difficult things. Shaurya worked on TPUs and encryption deployed across more than 100,000 hosts; Amol built perception and compute systems spanning Orchard Robotics, Tesla, NASA, and Boeing; Aydin reports helping grow TerraFirma’s realized revenue from $500,000 to $30 million and operating teleoperation across 50-plus vehicles. Their long personal relationships reduce recruiting risk, and Shaurya is explicitly identified as CEO, so I do not see the leadership shell game or disclosed equal-control problem that would stop me immediately. What I cannot find is pre-company insurance obsession. A banking-to-insurance pivot only weeks before launch makes this look discovered through the current AI wave, not independently pursued through years of actuarial or carrier pain.

The bull case stings: three unusually technical operators could use physical-world evidence to build the proprietary claims dataset that incumbent class codes lack, eventually becoming infrastructure beneath robotics, space, defense, and energy. That would fit my concealed-layer pattern beautifully. Today, however, the inputs are largely public, no moat is disclosed, four heavily funded competitors already automate commercial underwriting, and Florin has published no policy, customer, model-performance result, or capacity partner. The pre-revenue exception does not apply as it did for **CosmicBrain AI**: both attack physical-industry bottlenecks, but Florin lacks the documented prototype use, signed industrial proofs of concept, and lifelong founder obsession that made missing revenue tolerable there.

The privacy architecture also matters. Pricing coverage from social-media evidence and public records creates consent, discrimination, and data-custody questions inside a regulated decision. Florin discloses neither limiting governance nor even the carrier, producer-license, or capacity-provider structure behind its “full-stack carrier” claim. A working quote shown on a website is not costly user behavior; a YC launch and work-email form do not prove that anyone entrusted Florin with premiums, sensitive operating data, or an actual loss.

My 36-month forecast is that Florin becomes an MGA or underwriting-software provider to a licensed carrier, reaching roughly $1 million to $5 million in annual revenue, rather than operating as the promised zero-underwriter carrier. That could still become a good company, but I would not invest my own money today. One artifact flips me: a named, regulator-verifiable carrier or reinsurance partner reporting at least 25 policies bound without human overrides and a full year of disclosed claims performance.
