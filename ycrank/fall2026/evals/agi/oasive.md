# Oasive -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Oasive’s strongest case is that automation could make recurring credit monitoring economical for school districts that municipal bond desks currently review only sporadically. The memo describes analysts spending hours searching filings, board meetings, and local news. Oasive’s product materials show borrower pages that bring those records together with timestamped meeting summaries, source links, and watchlists. The market-opening hypothesis is that this workflow frees enough analyst time to cover more issuers routinely. The memo contains no measured comparison of the full cost per verified update, including automated processing, analyst review, corrections, and manual work when recordings are missing. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

Howland reports coverage of more than 8,600 school districts across 43 states. Under the assumption that rivals have equally capable models, buyers might still pay for a reliable borrower history and watchlists that reduce the work of tracking credit changes. That preference remains untested. The company also reports 15 desks using its earlier Macro & Rates and Agency MBS products. Municipal pricing is listed as custom, and the memo provides no paid municipal usage or renewal evidence. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *The Idea*)

Howland’s fixed-income and AI product experience, together with the municipal launch, supports seed-stage execution credit. The unit of expansion is another district monitored over time. Reusing a borrower record across desks could spread processing costs, but contribution at the municipal team price remains unmeasured once refreshes, missing-source exceptions, and onboarding are included. (*Founders & Team*, *Market & Traction*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Oasive may help existing analysts work faster while leaving the same issuers uneconomical to monitor. Its terms say automated output reaches users without company review, leaving verification with the analyst. CreditScope and DPC DATA already offer overlapping data or monitoring, while Oasive depends on public recordings and can lose access to licensed data at a provider’s direction. Those facts weaken the case that buyers would keep paying under model parity. The memo contains no representative cost comparison or observed switching that closes Path B. Watch is warranted while both conditions remain testable. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent municipal bond desk renew at a price that covers Oasive’s delivery costs for recurring monitoring of districts it previously skipped, after comparing Oasive with a credible alternative using equally capable models? A paid pilot should compare the full cost per verified update against the desk’s manual workflow on a representative set of districts, including missing recordings and analyst corrections. A renewal backed by viable delivery costs, newly recurring coverage, and a demonstrated preference for Oasive would move the action to **Reach out**. A representative trial showing that the coverage remains uneconomic or that the desk can switch and retain the same outcome would close Path B and move the action to **Pass**.
