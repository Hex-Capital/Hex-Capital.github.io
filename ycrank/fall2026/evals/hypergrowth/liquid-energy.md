# Liquid Energy -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with inference buyers, Liquid Energy’s best growth path is to distribute capacity through inference platforms and fulfill demand with a standard fleet of NanoDCs. The factory-built module bundles GPUs, solar panels, batteries, cooling, and connectivity. If platforms can route workloads remotely, a fleet at suitable sites could serve buyers across markets. The memo identifies a reusable design and leaves cross-market delivery and sustained field performance unverified. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

Liquid Energy takes direct capacity requests and reports agreements with unnamed inference platforms. It claims a four-week deployment and activation within 30 minutes of arrival. Those timings describe hardware rollout. The documented customer path still involves a capacity contract and company-managed deployment, with independent purchase and first use unverified. Ongoing inference workloads give customers a reason to keep buying capacity. Higher workloads could expand an account if its contract bills for more usage or capacity. The memo gives no billing terms or evidence of repeat purchases. (*The Idea*, *Key Facts*)

The memo records a November 5 launch and lists a five-person team. Liquid Energy reports 20 units on hand and manufacturing capability of 2 MW per month. The launch, inventory, and platform agreements leave paid production and pre-revenue status unresolved, so initial traction uses the rubric default. (*Liquid Energy*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Growth could require a roughly proportional increase in company-owned hardware once the fleet reaches capacity. At the company’s modeled commodity token pricing, each NanoDC would generate about $1,500 in annual compute revenue. In that scenario, the $1,500 must cover annualized hardware costs and the attributable costs of shipping, setup, connectivity, maintenance, and selling or distributing the capacity. Lower billable utilization would further reduce revenue at the same token price. Battery autonomy and link reliability are unverified in the field, and savings from repeated factory builds remain an inference. The structural gate stays open because the memo establishes a capacity dependency but leaves contribution under realistic operating conditions unresolved. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can one inference platform expand a paid production contract onto a second standard NanoDC at a different site and generate positive incremental contribution? Invoices, measured billable throughput across day and night, deployment effort, and attributable costs including annualized hardware would answer it. A profitable expansion using the same setup would support rescoring for Reach out. Persistent losses at realistic utilization, with no credible improvement lever, would change the action to Pass.
