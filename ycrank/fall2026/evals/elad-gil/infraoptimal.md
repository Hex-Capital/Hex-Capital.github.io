# InfraOptimal -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Agentic performance engineering is becoming feasible; teams serving open models are the buyer; sustained reductions in production inference cost determine whether InfraOptimal becomes a company or a capability inside an existing platform. The market does not need another dedicated endpoint. It needs an optimization advantage large enough to change purchasing behavior after accounting for latency, correctness, engineering effort, and idle capacity. The dossier identifies underutilization as the problem, but does not yet establish that economic advantage. I would pass on a personal check today.

The September 2026 compiler experiment gives me something concrete to evaluate: approximately 3–5.6% lower decoder time on an Apple M2 Pro. That is useful engineering evidence, with appropriately limited claims. It was agent-assisted, was not a current endpoint deployment, and could regress on other hardware. AWS’s June 2026 kernel-writing and profiling agents support the technical why-now, but also show that the enabling capability is reaching incumbents. InfraOptimal has demonstrated a possible optimization mechanism; it has not demonstrated a corresponding change in GPU serving economics. Its minute-based capacity billing, including idle time, makes that distinction consequential: faster execution does not automatically translate into a proportionately smaller customer bill.

The strongest part of InfraOptimal is the proposed operational loop. Agents propose transformations, check behavior, benchmark candidates, and retain accepted changes. Better coding models could expand the search while the product preserves workload measurements and accepted optimizations. That is a more substantial architecture than generating a kernel once. The scoped Z3 proof also shows attention to verification, although an integer identity is not whole-program correctness. I give the workflow affirmative credit, but cross-customer learning and durable ownership of production decisions remain unestablished.

The strongest objection to passing is that these founders may be building precisely the machinery that turns rapidly improving agents into reliable infrastructure. Cassidy’s processor validation and formal-methods background fits the verification problem; Kocheta’s research and robotics infrastructure experience fits experimentation. Leaving school to work full-time is documented commitment. My pattern of backing several layers of a technology transition makes this category interesting. But the market is already crystallizing around funded inference platforms: Baseten, Fireworks, and Together combine optimization with deployment and capacity. The dossier does not establish the rare frontier-research team and credible capital path that would justify overriding absent product pull, nor the changed physical unit economics needed to underwrite capacity exposure.

There is no public evidence of paying customers, repeat endpoint usage, or retention. I assign the prescribed missing-evidence score rather than interpreting silence as failed adoption; zero job postings says nothing useful about demand. The decisive gap is that InfraOptimal’s technical loop has not yet been connected to a customer’s buying decision. My one verifiable condition for revisiting the pass is a production customer renewing after 90 days with measured all-in cost per token at least 20% below its prior provider on matched hardware, workload, accuracy, and latency requirements, including idle charges and optimization overhead. That would connect the engineering to the market variable that matters.
