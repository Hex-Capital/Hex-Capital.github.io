# Understudy Labs -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Aamir Poonawalla spent ten years building Instacart’s ads, auction, experimentation, and logistics infrastructure, yet Understudy Labs has disclosed no defensibility signal. That pairing is Understudy Labs’s signature: unusually credible operators building an unusually reproducible software workflow. The founders can execute; the architecture does not yet explain why execution ends in monopoly rather than acquisition.

**Affirm** is the high rung for operator pedigree because Max Levchin had already built PayPal, not merely worked inside it. Understudy Labs has a legitimate but weaker version of that mechanism: Poonawalla operated consequential infrastructure for a decade, while Luis Manrique reports closing $1.9 million in ARR at Gumloop and shares an advertising-pacing patent with Poonawalla. Understudy Labs diverges from Affirm because neither founder has previously created and scaled the institution itself. This is strong lineage for pre-seed, but not the pedigree override that rescued Asana or Quora from weaker structural theses.

**Asana** is the low rung on the atoms-heavy axis: pure workflow software without regulatory or capex barriers, rescued only because Dustin Moskovitz had already shipped Facebook. Understudy Labs reproduces the underlying failure mechanism more cleanly than the exception. Understudy Labs sells software into unregulated production-LLM workflows; customers retain their traces, evals, routing rules, and weights, while Understudy Labs contractually forgoes cross-customer payload training. There are no atoms, certifications, government relationships, or scarce infrastructure. The moat is not merely absent—it has been partially exported to the customer.

Fireworks already reports more than $1 billion in annualized revenue, which exposes the weakness in Understudy Labs’s alleged secret. Understudy Labs does hold a plausible contrarian belief: bounded production tasks should be measured and migrated away from frontier APIs rather than permanently rented from them. Over eighteen months, trace capture, held-out evaluation, tuning, and gradual routing could compound into customer-specific knowledge. But Understudy Labs has not named an architectural reason Fireworks, Baseten, Together AI, Braintrust, or an internal ML group cannot reproduce that loop. “Open weights are now good enough” is timing; it is not yet a secret.

**Facebook** achieved pricing power because every additional participant strengthened a graph competitors could not copy. Understudy Labs has the inverse architecture: every customer owns the tuned weights and associated artifacts, and Understudy Labs cannot combine customer payloads into a shared-data advantage. Order-form pricing supplies no five-year pricing-power hypothesis, while Baseten’s $2.085 billion of financing and Together AI’s $1.33 billion make feature competition economically asymmetric. Understudy Labs may create meaningful switching friction inside each deployment, but one year of integration friction is a feature; ten years of pricing power is a company.

Understudy Labs’s held-out benchmarks, variance reporting, and gradual traffic routing demonstrate a definite operating procedure, not a definite founder plan. Understudy Labs could move this score into the investment range by committing to a two-to-five-year architectural destination—such as becoming the portable optimization layer across every inference provider—with named technical milestones that cannot be reached through ordinary customer-driven iteration. The present dossier instead describes private-preview design partnerships and an optimization process whose destination changes with each workload. That is disciplined iteration, but still iteration.

Baseten’s combination of post-training, embedded engineers, and managed inference makes it the natural buyer: by July 2029, Understudy Labs will be acquired by Baseten after reaching roughly $2 million to $5 million in ARR, with its trace-to-eval routing workflow absorbed into Baseten’s inference platform. Understudy Labs’s operator strength is sufficient to produce a useful product and enterprise contracts; Understudy Labs’s low structural-moat score makes an independent monopoly less likely than a strategic acquisition. The One Ring here belongs to the infrastructure platform, not the optimization plugin.

Luis Manrique’s $1.9 million ARR record, Poonawalla’s decade at Instacart, the private-preview design partners, and backing from Instacart co-founder Max Mullen form the strongest opposing case. Understudy Labs could be an **Asana**-style known exception: superior operators may turn an apparently consensus workflow into the default control plane, while customer-owned weights become a distribution advantage because enterprises distrust platform lock-in. The objection stings because portability can become a standard before it becomes a moat. But Understudy Labs currently has 9 GitHub stars, no disclosed revenue, no shared-data flywheel, and several capitalized platforms able to bundle the workflow. I would flip only if Understudy Labs reaches $10 million ARR with greater than 120% net revenue retention by July 2028; that would show the supposedly portable artifacts are creating pricing power rather than exporting it.
