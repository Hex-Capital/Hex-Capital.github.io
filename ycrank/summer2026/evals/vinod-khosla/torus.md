# Torus -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is plausible but unquantified. Torus shows how one inconsistency can create a $500K rework order, yet it does not establish the annual rework cost pool, the share addressable by document checking, or an order-of-magnitude change in infrastructure economics. A $10.1–$11.3 billion AEC software market is not the consequence; it is vendor spending. The missing number is how many billions of capital-project waste disappear if Torus works.

The product mechanism is more substantive than a generic chatbot. Extracting engineering parameters, mapping their relationships, running more than 50 consistency-check categories, and producing traceable RFIs could become a real system of record for engineering intent. But Torus supplies no measured accuracy, false-negative rate, review-time reduction, or rework benchmark against current engineering review. Without a 5–10x result, this remains an intelligent workflow product rather than a demonstrated technical discontinuity.

The strongest evidence is the team paired with early enterprise access. Marcus Lima has energy-systems training and previously built direct-air-capture company Heimdal, which raised $9.7 million; Rahul Thayil previously built AI risk-intelligence company Orion and raised $3.5 million. Torus also reports deployments with multiple Fortune 500 companies and major EPCs in data-center and energy infrastructure. That is unusually relevant pre-seed access, not follower-count theater.

The bull case is that repeat founders have found a platform-shaped wedge inside an expensive installed workflow. Enterprise software and execution-heavy businesses can justify investment without scientific novelty when a small team is attached to a giant value pool and can expand rapidly. But the dossier does not yet prove that exception: customers are unnamed, revenue is undisclosed, and there is no retention, deployment-scale, or proprietary-learning evidence. Meanwhile, four overlapping competitors have disclosed more than $147 million in funding. Torus may be differentiated, but it is not yet defensible.

Capital should now attack the largest kill risk: whether the system can catch consequential inconsistencies without creating unacceptable false confidence. Instead, the public milestones emphasize deployments, sovereign hosting, certifications in progress, and three hires paying as much as $250K. There is no stated experiment, threshold, cost, or stop condition for engineering accuracy. Pricing and implementation economics are also absent, so I cannot tell whether savings survive enterprise integration and human-review costs.

My 36-month base case is $2–5 million ARR as a services-heavy engineering-document QA product, not a category-defining engineering intelligence platform. I pass today. One condition flips the decision: a named EPC publishing results across at least three live projects showing a greater than 5x reduction in review or rework cost, with independently measured false-negative performance and savings exceeding ten times Torus’s fully loaded price.
