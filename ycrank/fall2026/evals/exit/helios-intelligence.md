# Helios Intelligence -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A diagnostics or repeat-testing company could pay for a validated at-home testing workflow and a longitudinal dataset that improves risk predictions. The memo records acquisitions of two diagnostics companies the founders co-founded. Helios reports training its model on repeat draws linked to health records, but the memo gives no dataset size, exclusivity, or measured predictive benefit. A buyer would have a reason to own the asset if exclusive access to the data and workflow delivered a measurable advantage over licensing a panel or building a comparable dataset. The memo establishes a plausible buyer class and asset, with buyer economics still unproven. (The Idea, Defensibility, Founders & Team)

The seed-stage milestone is a funded repeat-testing study that demonstrates reliable results after home collection and shipping, then measures whether repeat data improve risk prediction over a commercially available panel. The reported waitlist and disclosed financing entries provide early signals. Study funding, burn, runway, and the cost to reach that milestone remain unknown. The memo also leaves open whether a buyer could acquire the necessary assay, model, and data-use rights. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and zero follow-ons. The eight-year target is $2.5 million in gross cash before investor taxes. It requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. At year eight, illustrative sales at $25 million and $100 million of enterprise value would return $750,000 (1.5×) and $3 million (6×), respectively, before debt and other deductions. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, those returns fall to $600,000 (1.2×) and $2.85 million (5.7×), and the 3% hurdle rises to $88.3 million of enterprise value. The reported convertible note has undisclosed terms, leaving its debt, dilution, and priority effects unresolved. The memo supplies no usable acquisition price or buyer economics to support the strong-sale case. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

If test adoption plateaus, buyers stay away, public listing scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. The memo supplies no evidence for an eligible secondary, sponsor buyout, IPO resale, or funded payout. Wind-down proceeds would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo does not quantify. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

VO Health already uses a commercial platform to measure more than 1,000 proteins from a small self-collected sample. That gives a prospective buyer a credible licensing or build option for broad profiling. The memo also reports that delayed processing preserved agreement with venous draws for only some analytes in published research, while Helios’s own assay validation remains undisclosed. Together, these facts weaken the case for paying an acquisition premium. The Pass gate remains open because the commercial substitute addresses broad profiling and the value of Helios’s proposed linked dataset remains unresolved. The memo establishes neither a structural bar to a sale nor a supported price that leaves the holder materially short. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

What would a diagnostics buyer pay to own Helios’s testing workflow and longitudinal data? A documented buyer-side analysis grounded in repeat-testing results, with data-use rights that survive a sale and a price range that can clear the holder’s cash hurdle, would move the action to Reach out.
