## The Idea

- **Problem:** Wholesale buyers spend hours reconciling stock, customer orders, deliveries, and sales history across email, spreadsheets, and legacy ERP before deciding what to reorder ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/W5s-lark-ai-agents-for-distributors)).
- **Approach:** Lark connects to existing ERP, CRM, email, and files, uses inventory optimization and customer rules to rank shortages and draft orders, and writes approved transactions back to the ERP within customer-set limits ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/W5s-lark-ai-agents-for-distributors), [Lark product](https://larkagents.com/)).
- **Differentiation:** Lark combines purchasing and forecasting with sales and order entry, overlapping with Whitespace’s purchasing workflows, Proton’s sales and CRM workflows, Conexiom’s order entry and invoice processing, and Blue Ridge’s supply and inventory planning ([Lark](https://larkagents.com/), [Whitespace](https://www.whitespacehq.ai/), [Proton](https://www.proton.ai/), [Conexiom](https://conexiom.com/blog/conexiom-announces-130m-growth-investment-from-warburg-pincus/), [Blue Ridge](https://www.blueridgeglobal.com/news/blue-ridge-launches-next-generation-platform)).
- **Business Model:** [Inferred]: A recurring software fee could follow a configured pilot, with expansion as customers activate more agents or workflows ([Lark FAQ](https://larkagents.com/faq)).
- **TAM/SAM:** No public TAM/SAM data found for this specific segment.
- **GTM / Distribution:** Lark takes prospects from a booked call to a pilot and subsequent workflow expansion, and Michael Wang reports meeting customers in Toronto and prospecting at a National Fastener Distributors Association event ([Lark](https://larkagents.com/), [Lark LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/larkagents/), [Michael Wang LinkedIn, via search snippet](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michael-wang-40061923b)).

## Defensibility

- **Moat today:** [Inferred]: Replacing a deployed agent could require remapping items, vendors, and approval rules that Lark configures with each customer during the pilot ([Lark FAQ](https://larkagents.com/faq)).
- **Future moat:** [Inferred]: Customer-specific reorder history and approval rules could make recommendations harder to replace over time, with one named case study documenting the deployment mechanism ([Lark Interline case study](https://larkagents.com/customers/interline-hardware)).
- **Market structure:** No structural barrier identified at this stage.
- **Commoditization risk:** Epicor’s June 2026 Prophet 21 release added natural-language business-rule creation and expanded workflow automation inside an ERP Lark integrates with ([Epicor](https://www.epicor.com/en-us/blog/supply-chain-management/prophet-21-2026-1-is-now-available/), [Lark FAQ](https://larkagents.com/faq)).

## Key Risks

- **ERP substitution:** [Inferred]: Epicor can sell overlapping automation inside Prophet 21, while Lark connects across ERP, email, documents, and CRM ([Epicor](https://www.epicor.com/en-us/blog/supply-chain-management/prophet-21-2026-1-is-now-available/), [Lark FAQ](https://larkagents.com/faq)).
- **Implementation workload:** [Inferred]: Customer-specific mapping can lengthen deployments as workflows and systems multiply, with Lark reporting that most setups take one to two months and starting customers on a pilot ([Lark FAQ](https://larkagents.com/faq)).
- **Transaction accuracy:** [Inferred]: Incorrect SKU matches or reorder quantities can carry into ERP transactions, and Lark uses exception flags, customer-set approval limits, and action logs to control writes ([Lark product](https://larkagents.com/), [Lark FAQ](https://larkagents.com/faq)).
- **Name collision:** [Inferred]: An unrelated YC Summer 2025 software-testing company also uses Lark, creating search ambiguity that the distributor startup’s larkagents.com domain helps resolve ([Lark Fall 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/lark-2), [Lark Summer 2025](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/lark)).
