# RightNow -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

RightNow’s effective token price depends on which context is already sitting on which GPU. That makes session routing economically interesting—and the disclosed cache methodology decisive. The published rates describe the highest qualifying account, with hits provided best effort. I cannot underwrite a business from its best customer’s cache behavior. The proposed post-money, competing-term-sheet count, and current round’s use of proceeds are also undisclosed. Those are unanswered underwriting questions, not evidence that this is an auction. They leave me without either a cheap entry or a defined experiment that my check buys.

The founders have built something worth examining. Autokernel includes an implementation and correctness harness; Jaber’s OpenFang project supplies concrete evidence of shipping software for agent developers. Together with the founders’ inference research, those artifacts suggest a team working across developer workflows and GPU optimization, rather than merely listing relevant employers. That fits my preference for people who can translate between systems. It is RightNow’s strongest signal. It does not yet establish experience operating a reliable, profitable inference service at scale.

The strongest argument against passing is that repeated coding context creates a specific, recurring inefficiency. A two-person team that understands both agents and serving infrastructure could concentrate on that workload and build a useful business without matching every feature of Together AI or Fireworks. Autokernel’s approximately 1,600 stars show technical attention; OpenFang’s approximately 18,200 stars offer a potential route to developers. But those are separate projects, and the dossier establishes no conversion from either into paying RunInfra accounts. The July 1 and October 20 launches demonstrate distribution activity, not an owned channel that already causes purchases. Public MIT-licensed optimization code also leaves competitors free to reuse the work. The potential access advantage has not yet become a proprietary one.

Delete the next financing from RightNow’s plan. What pays the GPU bill? The $5 prepaid entry and $10/$29/$99 subscriptions establish an offer, but disclose neither realized revenue nor contribution margin, burn, or break-even requirements. I am not assuming those economics are bad. I am declining to assume that attractive displayed prices prove they work. Together AI already develops kernels and inference infrastructure, so optimization alone cannot carry the underwriting. RightNow needs a workload advantage that survives ordinary cache behavior and competitive pricing. Its benchmark disclosure is candid, but unavailable comparison data leaves that advantage unverified.

I pass at this evidence level. The usual AI application objection only partly applies: RightNow is addressing a real serving constraint, and its technical artifacts deserve more credit than a model-dependent product promise. But the exceptions I would consider require measured paid conversion, retention, cash economics, or unusually strong external validation; repository popularity supplies none of those by itself. There is also no evidence of fundraising pressure or founder decision paralysis, so I would not invent either red flag. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: a 90-day paid coding-workload cohort that renews while generating positive cash contribution after GPU and direct support costs, measured using representative cache rates. That would turn the central mechanism from a plausible optimization into an underwritable business; price would then determine whether I buy it.
