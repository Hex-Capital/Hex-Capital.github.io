# Native -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Native’s possible secret is that predicting which marketing idea deserves execution is more valuable than making content production cheaper. That is a testable proposition. Randomly assign comparable ideas to Norn’s rankings and a credible baseline, hold production quality constant, and measure customer outcomes. But the dossier identifies neither named peers rejecting this proposition nor a convincing experiment establishing it. The reported 5.5% engagement versus 2.9% for Briggs on Homes is encouraging; Native’s acknowledged selection and survivorship biases prevent it from establishing predictive superiority. There is a hypothesis worth testing, with limited evidence that it is a secret.

The more immediate investment problem is ownership. Even if prediction becomes the valuable layer, why does Native own it? Blaze already offers strategy, creation, publishing, and learning. Norn collects acceptance and performance signals, but the dossier establishes neither exclusive access nor a measured advantage from their accumulation. Customers can opt out of training. Small businesses constitute an audience; they do not yet constitute a bounded market Native can dominate. This is affirmative competitive overlap, and it fails my monopoly gate on the evidence available.

The strongest objection to passing is substantial: Native reports $2M ARR within six months and more than 2,200 paying customers. These are unusually informative pre-seed signals. Customers are purchasing a complete workflow, and their usage could supply precisely the feedback that improves prediction. Native might acquire a proprietary learning advantage through commercial execution before competitors recognize its importance. My exceptions permit demonstrated technical breakthroughs or persistent customer behavior to outweigh an initially unattractive structure. Here, purchasing establishes willingness to pay; it does not yet establish that accumulated feedback produces an advantage competitors cannot match. The exception does not remove the failed gate.

The founders deserve a stronger assessment than the market position. Bekken, Gråsberg, and Hauga built Wepost together before starting Native in January 2026. Their shared operating history is affirmative evidence of alignment, while their content, agency, and machine-learning backgrounds explain how this team could build the product internally. This partially matches my unstated preference for operator-led company formation around an existing problem, although no proprietary founder network is documented. The dossier supplies no affirmative evidence of convenience matching, outsourced core engineering, or investor-majority ownership. Full-time status and economic exposure remain unverified, so founding structure earns meaningful credit without receiving the highest band.

Native also has one recognizable revenue engine rather than several unrelated monetization proposals. Its reported ARR and customer count imply roughly $76 monthly revenue per customer, broadly consistent with the advertised $79 Pro plan, although the figures may cover different dates and customer mixes. That establishes commercial substance. It does not establish margins after generation and publishing costs or expansion economics sufficient for a power-law outcome. Self-service onboarding fits a subscription at this price; agency and enterprise sales require separate economic justification. Paying customers demonstrate delivery, but the dossier does not identify a repeatable acquisition channel, conversion rate, or acquisition cost.

I would pass with my own money. Native could become a substantial marketing business while competition prevents it from retaining exceptional value. My verifiable flip condition is a prospective controlled test showing that access to Native’s accumulated cross-customer feedback produces a material, persistent improvement in customer outcomes over an otherwise identical system without that history. That would connect the proposed secret to a proprietary mechanism. More generated posts or additional revenue alone would strengthen the business case without resolving the ownership question.
