# The Agentic Data Co. -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a speech-model developer is the strongest path. A buyer could pay to control YapRoom’s collection pipeline, the rights to its private speech corpus, and its audio training environments. The memo records a #2 end-of-turn result on TurnBench. The company says unnamed AI labs buy its data. Ownership could make sense if continuous access to specialized recordings and training environments improves production performance enough to justify the cost of acquiring the company over licensing data or building the capability in-house. The benchmark establishes a signal on conversational timing. The economics of ownership remain unverified. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

A funded production test could measure turn-taking errors and tool-task completion against the buyer’s best licensed alternative. YapRoom has already collected 1,602 conversations from 239 speakers, which makes a focused test plausible. Participant payouts alone run $20 per usable two-person hour before review. The memo’s funding figures differ, and it gives no burn or test budget. The company operates the app and collects speaker-level consent, giving a buyer a plausible core asset to acquire. Rights to use the private recordings after a change of control remain unverified. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

**Proceeds bridge.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, no follow-ons, and a 5× gross cash target within eight years. The $2.5 million target requires $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. At 3%, assuming $5 million of net debt, the enterprise-value hurdle is $88.3 million. Under the same assumptions, a $25 million sale in year eight pays the holder $600,000 gross (1.2×), and a $100 million sale pays $2.85 million gross (5.7×), pro rata before investor taxes. These are illustrative prices. The memo provides no usable acquisition price, actual holder terms, or figures for debt, fees, or senior claims. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

If licensing growth plateaus, acquisition interest fails to materialize, public-market scale stays out of reach, and the company reinvests all available cash, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. The memo establishes neither predictable cash generation for a sponsor buyout nor excess cash for dividends or buybacks. It also provides no eligible secondary demand or evidence of IPO readiness. A wind-down recovery cannot be estimated without assets, obligations, and preferences. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A lab could use the commercially available Open Yap 1K corpus, license full-duplex recordings from oto at a published entry price of $10 per audio hour, or license private data directly from the company. Those options weaken the case for buying the whole business, especially while the benchmark leaves tool-task performance and multilingual transfer unverified. The evidence supports a credible substitute for recording supply. It establishes neither an equivalent substitute for the combined capability nor a ceiling on acquisition proceeds, so the Pass gate remains open. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)
