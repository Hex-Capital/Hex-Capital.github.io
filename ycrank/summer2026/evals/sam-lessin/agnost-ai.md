# Agnost AI -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three deal facts are blank: proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. The earlier $250,000 from Entrepreneurs First and Transpose Platform establishes that Agnost AI has raised money, not that today’s round is cheap or non-consensus. I will not convert financing silence into favorable terms, particularly for a YC AI-infrastructure company operating in a category where LangChain, Braintrust, and Arize have collectively raised hundreds of millions.

The product evidence is substantially better than the deal evidence. Agnost processes more than 1 million events daily, reports work with teams at Google, Exa Labs, and Corgi Insurance, and previously processed roughly 2.5 million monthly calls through its MCP analytics product. The mechanism is real: conversations reveal frustration and abandonment that an HTTP response, trace, or prewritten eval can miss; Agnost then connects those failures to tool calls and reviewed code changes. This is an outsider-insider translation layer between product analytics and agent infrastructure, and the founders have shipped enough relevant systems to make that claim credible rather than decorative.

But events are not purchases. Google’s MCP Toolbox integration and the named-company usage suggest embedded workflow access, yet the dossier provides no paid-customer count, conversion rate, retention cohort, or evidence that the $49 and $499 plans generate revenue. The 2,885 company followers, 77 Hacker News points, and founders’ combined reach are distribution possibilities, not a proprietary dark pool that has demonstrably moved economic behavior. More than 1 million daily events could represent one enthusiastic free deployment as easily as a repeatable business.

The bull case stings: a two-person team has already built production infrastructure, attracted recognizable users, and entered an agent-observability market forecast to grow from $1.23 billion in 2025 to $8.62 billion by 2031. That is the kind of hard product usage that can override missing seed evidence. The known-exception path, however, requires measured paid conversion, retention, or another external proof strong enough to compensate for a bad or unknown deal. Agnost supplies none of those yet, while LangSmith, Braintrust, Arize, and Langfuse can all absorb conversation analytics into broader suites.

There is also no named venture moment. The dossier does not say what this round funds, which hypothesis it tests, when the test ends, or what result dramatically changes Agnost’s value. Pricing tiers show a plausible software model, but there is no revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or route to break-even without another financing. Delete the Series A and I cannot tell whether this becomes a durable product or an excellent feature waiting to be acquired by a suite.

I am passing today, not because the product is unserious, but because the capital proposition is missing around a technically credible product. One verifiable condition flips me: a sub-$10 million post-money financing explicitly tranched on Agnost reaching ten unaffiliated paying teams with six-month retention by February 2027. That would simultaneously establish scarce pricing, a finite truth-revealing experiment, and customer behavior stronger than event volume.
