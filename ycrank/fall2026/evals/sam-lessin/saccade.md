# Saccade -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Saccade has built an eye-tracking attachment; it has not established what my check buys. Proposed post-money, competing offers, and round proceeds are undisclosed. I cannot call this an auction because it is in YC, and I cannot call it cheap because it is early. More consequentially, the dossier names no funded experiment with a deadline and a pass/fail result. “Detect Alzheimer’s earlier” is an ambition. What specific uncertainty does this round eliminate?

The strongest case is the engineering. [UCSB documents](https://capstone.engineering.ucsb.edu/projects/saccade) custom cameras, illumination, chassis, and thermal management, with Bhardwaj participating in development. That is tangible execution, beyond a mechanical-engineering credential or a patent-pending label. It activates my preference for founders connecting difficult physical systems to neglected workflows. But the bridge needs two ends: the dossier establishes hardware work, while direct clinical operating experience and control of patient access remain unproven. Neurologist-designed tasks do not themselves establish that the founder can navigate clinical adoption.

The bull case gets sharper with Saccade’s [reported San Francisco clinic deployment](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Vff-saccade-world-s-earliest-test-for-alzheimer-s-biomarkers). A 15-minute assessment could give the team a practical way to collect eye recordings alongside confirmed pathology. If those clinics repeatedly use the device and supply defensible data rights, the hardware could become an entrance into a proprietary dataset. That possibility is worth taking seriously. Today, however, clinic names, assessment volumes, purchases, and data rights are undisclosed; the homepage recruits for future research. Deployment earns attention, but it does not yet demonstrate that owned access causes a purchase. The $7,500 competition award proves neither customer demand nor revenue.

The decisive uncertainty is whether Saccade measures something a buyer can act on. Its launch claims detection up to 15 years before symptoms, while its [current disclosure](https://saccade.health/) says diagnostic use and individual risk prediction are unvalidated. That gap matters economically: collecting an eye movement is different from selling a reliable interpretation. ViewMind already overlaps in eye-tracking assessment, and blood biomarkers provide another comparator. Even a valid asymptomatic risk signal would not itself establish treatment eligibility. The generic-AI red flag is therefore a question about where value survives: does Saccade own clinically useful evidence and access, or merely an implementation competitors can reproduce?

I would pass with my own money. Pricing, margins, burn, and a seed-funded route to break-even are missing, so I cannot underwrite a business that survives without another financing. Those omissions receive the stated defaults; I am not treating them as evidence of bad economics. I have checked the exception case: documented conversion, cash generation, proprietary access, or substantial operating receipts could justify proceeding despite incomplete financing evidence. The prototype and unnamed deployments do not yet provide that override. One verifiable development would reopen the decision: a signed agreement with a named clinic committing to purchase at a stated price if Saccade meets a prospectively specified biomarker-validation endpoint within 12 months. That would connect the engineering, the experiment, and an actual buyer.
