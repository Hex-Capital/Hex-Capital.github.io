# Hundred -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an industrial automation vendor is Hundred’s strongest supported exit route. Hundred says its benchtop station scans entire shipping trays at receiving and retains a photo of each part. That could give an acquirer a way to extend its inspection offering upstream of the production line. Rockwell Automation’s reported investment in Elementary shows strategic interest in adjacent inspection technology. The rationale for buying Hundred is inferred, and buyer interest and the value of owning its workflow remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The reported $125,000 YC seed investment, public launch, and team experience in supplier quality and software support a plausible next step. A paid receiving deployment should measure missed defects, false rejects, sustained throughput, and cost per tray against the factory’s current process. The memo gives no production results or pilot budget, so the capital and time needed to reach that milestone remain uncertain. IP transfer and holder participation are also unverified, and the memo is silent on an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Using the benchmark $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale scenarios below are illustrative assumptions. Both assume an eligible holder sells the entire stake for cash by year eight, with pro rata distribution after the stated deductions and no additional senior preference claims.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Holder cash |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $45M enterprise value less $5M in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $40M | $1.20M (2.4×) |
| Strong sale | $105M enterprise value less $10M in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $95M | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private | The business reinvests available cash, with no distribution or eligible share purchase | $0 realized |

The strong sale clears the objective under those assumptions. At 2.5% ownership, the same $95 million distribution yields $2.375 million and misses it. The memo supplies no buyer economics, acquisition prices, or share demand that would support either sale price. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A strategic buyer could extend an existing camera workflow and avoid acquiring a dedicated station. Overview AI already sells configurable inspection cameras, and Elementary adds inspection software to installed cameras. Hundred’s whole-tray approach could still win at receiving, but the memo supplies no production error rates or sustained throughput to show that it does. This supports Watch. The substitution risk leaves the economics unresolved, and the evidence does not establish a structural block or inadequate proceeds under the strongest credible route. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production deployment demonstrate enough incremental value over an installed-camera alternative to give a strategic buyer an economic reason to acquire Hundred at a price that could leave at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? Customer-verified results on defect escapes, false rejects, throughput, and cost, combined with a credible buyer’s build-versus-buy economics that support that hurdle, would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that the strongest credible buyer route falls materially short would move it to Pass.
