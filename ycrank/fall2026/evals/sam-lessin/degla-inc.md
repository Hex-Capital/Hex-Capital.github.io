# Degla Inc -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Degla Inc has demonstrated a voice-commanded aircraft mission. What would my check prove that the demonstration has not? The dossier supplies neither round terms nor a capital-funded experiment with a measurable result and deadline. Post-money valuation: unknown. Competing term sheets: unknown. Whether my capital is scarce: unknown. YC membership does not establish an auction, but it does not establish an attractive deal either. Both financing dimensions receive their missing-evidence defaults.

The strongest case for Degla is that Aziz Hanafi can actually connect AI research to flying hardware. His InstaDeep autonomous-systems work, MIT research, and the company-reported October 2026 VTOL demonstration support that argument. His prior UChallengeMe product reportedly exceeded 10,000 users before closure and sale of its codebase. Those are shipped receipts beyond a university credential. Degla fits my pattern of accepting physical complexity when software reorganizes an operating system. The missing half is documented experience running the public-safety or infrastructure operation being reorganized; demonstrating vehicle tracking does not establish that bridge.

The San Diego demonstration could become a valuable distribution wedge. FBI, CBP, and DIU representatives reportedly observed it, putting the product before relevant institutions. But attendance establishes neither a pilot nor procurement access, and 545 LinkedIn followers establish no purchasing behavior. There is no documented customer action attributable to an owned relationship, dataset, or embedded workflow. Meanwhile, Skydio already offers overlapping public-safety fleet operations and reports additional agency approvals. Degla needs a reason an operator chooses its system and keeps choosing it. Conversational control alone has not yet supplied that evidence.

Delete the next financing from the model. Degla’s inferred aircraft-plus-software business could support a real company, but the dossier contains no prices, margins, burn, or route to cash break-even. That earns the economics default, without assuming hardware must be uneconomic. Autonomous docking is scheduled for Q1 2027, while failure recovery and degraded-communications performance remain unmeasured publicly. The relevant AI red flag would be a business model that requires those errors to disappear; the dossier does not establish that assumption. It also supplies no evidence of auction pressure or equal-founder decision paralysis.

I pass at present. The bull case has substance: a founder has integrated software and aircraft and attracted relevant observers unusually early. But my known exceptions to price and AI concerns rely on demonstrated conversion, operating economics, or unusually hard external validation. Degla’s demonstration has not yet produced those receipts; its prior codebase sale also establishes no alternative financing structure or capital-use plan. One verifiable flip condition would be an executed financing-and-deployment package: a sub-$10 million post-money round funding one dated, paid agency deployment test, with contractual acceptance metrics and a credible budget to reach break-even without another raise. That would turn a promising flight into an underwritable venture moment.
