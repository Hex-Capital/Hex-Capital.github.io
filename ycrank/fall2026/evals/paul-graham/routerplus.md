# RouterPlus -- Paul Graham Evaluation

RouterPlus has shipped something useful. What I cannot yet see is what the founders did when building it became difficult. The strongest incident is a consumer application whose P95 latency fell from 4.8 seconds to under 4 seconds after a dedicated deployment. That establishes a concrete product result. It does not establish who solved the obstacle, what they tried first, or how quickly they found the workaround. Those details matter because I am buying the founders’ ability to solve the next problem.

There is a substantial case for writing the check. Lakshmi Narayana spent four years building Samsung’s ML-platform GPU infrastructure and was previously Trupeer’s first hire and CTO. Bishoyi worked on edge-AI deployment. RouterPlus now offers an API and reports a customer running 3 million agent conversations daily. These are relevant builders with a functioning product and a potentially demanding user. The latency result adds technical substance. But the conversation count describes the customer’s activity; the dossier does not establish how much passes through RouterPlus, whether the customer pays, or whether its usage is growing. I give the deployed software meaningful credit and the demand claim provisional credit.

The interesting business is the unpleasant work behind the API: evaluating a workload, finding capacity, testing it under load, and arranging a fallback. A founder-reported network of 40-plus providers could help the team do this manually while learning what to automate. That fits my preference for founders willing to do the schlep. Yet OpenRouter already offers customer-specific evaluations and dedicated deployments. RouterPlus needs customers to return because its procurement work reliably solves their particular problem. Prepaid token pricing supplies a way to collect money, but free BYOK inference and separately negotiated capacity contracts leave the actual capture of value unproven.

RouterPlus partly matches my unstated preference for programmable infrastructure with concrete proof before fundraising: the API exists, and a deployment has a measured outcome. Evidence that developers depend on this primitive remains thinner. The move from EVO autoresearch in May–June to EVO Router in August and RouterPlus in October shows successive releases, but no documented customer observation explains the route changes. Likewise, the founders’ backgrounds establish relevant experience without establishing that this particular problem arose from their own work. I would leave both questions open. The 10,000-plus prior-product repositories and 244,000 launch views cannot answer them.

I would pass today. The usual launch and partnership red flags are not established: RouterPlus has shipped, and its supplier network does not depend on one promised partner. Nor does undisclosed cofounder history establish a bad relationship. I considered whether the working-product exception should overcome the missing founder evidence. It could, with unmistakable customer pull; the reported volume is not sufficiently attributable here. **One verifiable flip condition:** a dated customer deployment record showing a founder overcoming a specific capacity failure within weeks, followed by that customer renewing a paid RouterPlus contract. That would connect resourcefulness to a user who actually needs the result.
