# Vorelios -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B, repeatable sales and account expansion,** is the strongest hypothesis. Vorelios identified aerospace aerodynamics as its initial market. The likely buyer is an aerodynamics engineering lead whose team repeatedly evaluates candidate geometries. Vorelios reports subsecond predictions from clean CAD and initial searches across thousands of candidates, with predictions within a few percent of an independent solver in those runs. Buyer activation, payment, and repeat use remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Growth scenario.** A team pays to screen more designs on one program, then brings the same workflow to other programs. If teams in another geography can supply the same CAD and operating inputs and accept the same solver checks for design exploration, Vorelios could sell substantially the same product there. Fast predictions could make additional searches cheap, but contribution depends on how much CAD cleanup, solver verification, support, and new training data each workload requires. The memo provides latency figures and leaves those costs unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Vorelios says its previous architecture failed on design families outside its training set, and the current accuracy gap remains open. If unfamiliar geometries routinely require manual cleanup, fresh simulation data, retraining, and project-specific validation, engineering effort could rise with each account despite fast predictions. The prior failure makes that risk concrete. The memo leaves its frequency and cost under the current architecture unresolved, so the structural Pass gate is unmet. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
