# Yam -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Yam’s strongest exit route is a strategic sale to a romance-game publisher or an AI roleplay platform. A buyer could use Yam’s persistent characters, branching stories, and creator publishing tools to add a romance product with an existing audience. The public catalog showed 112 fandoms and 2.4 million plays for its top listing. Yam separately reported 1 million cumulative beta play hours and more than three hours of daily playtime per daily active user. Those signals establish activity, while repeat paid use and buyer willingness to pay remain open. The memo reports no realized Yam revenue or comparable acquisitions. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Yam’s plan to develop original IP gives it a concrete milestone. It can test whether rights-cleared games retain paying players and generate positive contribution after model costs. The live product, paid plans, and reported seed and angel investment make an initial test plausible. The memo leaves the cost of producing original games, Yam’s runway, and the capital needed to scale open. It supplies no evidence of an eligible secondary, funded payout, or other independent liquidity route. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale cases below assume a full cash acquisition within eight years, an eligible holder selling the entire stake, and pro rata distribution after illustrative net debt and deal adjustments, with no additional senior claims.

| Scenario | Assumed proceeds bridge | Cash to holder |
|-----------|-------------------------|----------------|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in net debt and deal adjustments yields $35M in distributable equity | $1.05M, or 2.1× |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in net debt and deal adjustments yields $90M in distributable equity | $2.70M, or 5.4× |
| Prolonged private | Yam reinvests available cash and no eligible share purchase occurs within eight years | $0 realized |

The strong scenario clears the hurdle by $200,000 to the holder. At 2.5% ownership, the same sale would yield $2.25 million. The memo provides no basis for treating either sale price as realizable.

### Countercase

A buyer could build its own romance scenarios using available models and existing roleplay tools. Yam’s most-played title is a fan game, and its takedown process creates a specific risk that a buyer could lose content responsible for some of the catalog’s demonstrated engagement. If original games fail to carry that audience, the buyer has less reason to pay for the platform. That risk lowers transaction feasibility. The memo establishes neither a route-wide closing block nor a supported range of sale values that falls short of the proceeds hurdle, so neither Pass gate is triggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Yam’s rights-cleared catalog retain enough paying players at positive contribution after model costs to support a strategic sale yielding at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? Cohort data tied to credible buyer feedback on price and deal adjustments would answer it. A supported range clearing the hurdle would move the action to Reach out. A supported range materially below it under the strongest credible route would move it to Pass.
