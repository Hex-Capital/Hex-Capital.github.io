# Stratum Industries -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Stratum Industries is an unsexy-regulated swing trapped inside an intangible AI wrapper: agents touch consequential government adjudication, but no normal human can yet touch, use, or observe a working Stratum Industries deployment. That high-regulation, low-verifiability signature usually produces procurement theater before product truth. Millions of permits, benefits claims, and drug approvals make the problem real; an inaccessible website, zero disclosed pilots, and no processing volumes make the artifact imaginary.

Anduril is the high rung because regulated deployment, institutional resistance, and technical risk compound into the moat. Stratum Industries shares the ugly institutional surface—benefits, licenses, permits, sensitive data—but not Anduril’s founder-origin mechanism. Neither Raj nor Vihaan Jagiasi has disclosed government procurement or adjudication experience, and Stratum Industries has disclosed no security certification, deployment architecture, or agency contract. The category is wonderfully difficult; the founders still look like visitors to it.

DeepMind is the low rung for physical-world verifiability: its abstraction could not be installed, ridden, scanned, or photographed by a normal person, and only the founders’ extraordinary polymath compounding justified the exception. Stratum Industries currently exhibits the same verification problem without that exception. A reviewer is supposed to upload an application and rubric, yet Stratum Industries had no accessible website in July 2026, no public demo, and no disclosed agency usage. I cannot watch the decision happen, inspect an audited case, or ask an adjudicator whether the summary is trustworthy.

Raj Jagiasi’s 220% average quota attainment and Vihaan Jagiasi’s founding-engineer role at acquired Cadastral give Stratum Industries Biz and Tiz, but lived experience compounds differently over 18 months. Government adjudication is full of exceptions that never enter the written rubric; founders who have sat beside adjudicators accumulate those exceptions into product judgment, while outsiders accumulate sales objections. Stratum Industries has no disclosed physical moment when either brother felt the backlog, so the absence is not merely missing biography—it is the anti-pattern of capable founders discovering a market analytically.

ClearOS already markets AI adjudication for disability claims, permits, FOIA, and licensing, while GovWell serves more than 130 governments across 34 states. Stratum Industries would move from moderately non-consensus to magically weird if agencies were rejecting it because cited AI recommendations were politically or aesthetically unacceptable while adjudicators kept using the prototype anyway. The disclosed evidence instead places Stratum Industries inside a busy 2026 GovTech-AI wave alongside a $34.5 million-funded GovWell and Salesforce Agentforce. The market is hard, but hard and uncrowded are different spells.

HQ Trivia is my single-anchor warning on governance: a delightful product can temporarily outrun founder ambiguity, but the Yusupov-Kroll fracture eventually collects its debt. Stratum Industries names Raj and Vihaan Jagiasi only as “Founder,” identifies no CEO, and documents no tiebreaker authority; being brothers makes trust plausible but does not manufacture a deciding vote. Stratum Industries also lacks HQ Trivia’s known-exception override—there is no explosive, observable user pull capable of paying that governance debt.

Salesforce’s FedRAMP-authorized Government Cloud makes my 36-month forecast falsifiable: by July 2029, Stratum Industries will have pivoted from government adjudication to compliance-case preparation for government contractors and reached $1 million–$3 million ARR, rather than winning production authority inside federal agencies. Stratum Industries can sell assistance faster than delegated judgment because cited summaries preserve much of the value while avoiding the accuracy, privacy, and procurement burden that favors Salesforce. I’m never playing the game I seem to be playing; the eventual business may clear paperwork near government without clearing government’s own backlog.

Raj Jagiasi’s 220% quota history and Vihaan Jagiasi’s acquired-startup experience form the strongest bull case: Stratum Industries could pair exceptional founder-led distribution with technical velocity just as agencies’ generative-AI use cases are accelerating, then turn audited decisions into proprietary workflow data. That case stings because pre-seed companies are allowed to begin without metrics, and DeepMind reminds me that unusual technical range can override an imperfect origin story. Stratum Industries still loses the argument today because ClearOS already describes nearly the same wedge, GovWell already has 130-plus government customers, and neither brother has disclosed the lived-domain access that made my Crusoe and Anduril bets legible. One signed, paid agency pilot processing at least 10,000 cases with independently published accuracy and appeal-rate results would flip my verdict.
