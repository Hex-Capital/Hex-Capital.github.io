# Prodigy Research -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The proposed post-money valuation is unknown, the competing-term-sheet count is unknown, and nothing establishes that this is a genuinely scarce first check. That is not a cosmetic omission for me. Prodigy Research is selling a very fashionable combination—foundation models, agents, and quantitative trading—inside YC. Without round terms, I cannot tell whether I am financing an unfashionable belief or buying consensus after the uncertainty has already been bid away.

The strongest evidence is the team. Michael Wang reportedly trained Gemini 3.1 Pro at Google DeepMind and previously generated billions in P&L as a Jane Street quant and Series 57 trader. Yuhua Wang reportedly built AI infrastructure serving billions of Apple users and millions of daily Salesforce queries. This is a real translation layer between foundation-model engineering, production infrastructure, and market execution—not two résumés assembled around an AI-finance meme.

The product has also produced a result worth investigating: a company-reported 108% live return over two months using delta-neutral strategies, with no down week, while the S&P 500 returned 0.6% and Nasdaq fell 3.5%. If independently verified with meaningful capital, daily returns, volatility, drawdowns, fees, and market-impact assumptions, that would be unusually hard evidence. As disclosed, however, the benchmark remains an internal scoreboard: Prodigy publishes neither the trading record nor the methodology behind claims that it beats GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Fable, and a 90th-percentile Jane Street trader. Rare errors, leverage, selection, or execution assumptions can turn a spectacular backtest-shaped number into bullshit very quickly.

There may be a proprietary dark pool forming from trade outcomes, execution data, and the model’s interaction with markets. But it has not yet caused a customer purchase, retained deployment, or independently observed capital allocation. Prodigy appears to trade company capital; it publishes no pricing, fund terms, software contracts, revenue, or distribution channel. Standard Signal already runs an end-to-end AI hedge fund, EquiLibre trades billions of dollars daily, and Numerai reports $550 million in AUM. Prodigy’s closed loop could compound, but resemblance to a data moat is not proof that one exists.

The capital plan is equally unresolved. “Train the best quantitative-finance foundation model” contains several simultaneous objectives—model performance, strategy discovery, execution, risk management, and capital formation—not one dated experiment that this round flips from unknown to known. Nor can I delete the next financing from the model: the dossier provides no gross margin, burn, runway, trading-capital requirement, or route to break-even. The 108% return is a potentially powerful cash signal, but two unaudited months cannot yet underwrite a durable profitable path.

My strongest opposing case is straightforward: these founders may possess exactly the technical and market access needed to turn proprietary live outcomes into a compounding advantage before larger competitors can reorganize around foundation models. That could make today’s missing commercial evidence irrelevant. I am still passing because the supposed override—extraordinary live performance—is supplied only by the company and lacks the verification needed to overcome missing price, capital use, and economics. One condition flips me: an independent administrator or prime broker verifies at least twelve months of net live returns, including capital employed, volatility, maximum drawdown, leverage, and fees, while the company offers a non-auctioned round tied to scaling that exact strategy.
