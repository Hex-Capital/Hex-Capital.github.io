# Torus -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Marcus Lima has led more than $100 million of capital projects, yet Torus turns that physical experience into an artifact most people cannot touch: a document graph operating behind an enterprise pilot. That is Torus’s signature—high founder-problem contact, low physical-world verifiability. Marcus appears to be *of* capital-project delivery rather than visiting it, while Rahul Thayil brings prior AI-company experience; but Torus still asks me to experience engineering work through claims about 50 consistency-check categories rather than through a machine, camera, vehicle, or public product I can observe.

Anduril is the high rung because Palmer Luckey carried a decade of shipped hardware into defense; biography and product were nearly inseparable. Torus shares the important mechanism: Marcus did not discover engineering coordination in a market memo after Bain—Torus says he personally led more than $100 million of capital projects, and Rahul previously built an AI threat-intelligence company. Torus diverges because the dossier does not identify the physical moment Marcus felt a document mismatch become a costly rework order, and neither founder has yet demonstrated that Torus is the inevitable continuation of a prior product. The Biz and Tiz are visible; the Rizz remains outside the dossier.

DeepMind failed my tactile test: I could not ride it, scan it, or watch it cross a street, and the exception required Demis Hassabis’s extraordinary polymath compounding. Torus presents the same low-rung abstraction without equivalent evidence of the exception. Torus offers a 15-minute demonstration using five or six uploaded documents, but that demonstrates a workflow rather than an observable physical artifact; no public benchmark, customer output, or deployed project can be inspected. Marcus and Rahul are repeat founders, which is interesting, but the dossier does not establish a Hassabis-level polymath override.

Trunk Tools has raised $70 million and already interprets drawings against full project-document corpora, so Torus is not yet magically weird merely because construction software is unsexy. Over the next 18 months, Torus’s non-consensus score will compound only if its project graph becomes specific to the ugly engineering details competitors avoid—customer-network deployment, procurement approvals, and cross-discipline inconsistencies—not if Torus remains “Legora for physical engineering firms.” Multiple Fortune 500 and major-EPC deployments could make Torus underpriced, but unnamed customers, undisclosed revenue, and a 24.8% market-growth narrative could just as easily become a consensus AI-wrapper story.

Marcus Lima is explicitly Torus’s CEO, which clears the first governance gate, while Rahul Thayil is explicitly Torus’s CTO. Torus would move into my highest governance tier if one document established Marcus’s tiebreaker authority, founder vesting, and the protocol for an irreconcilable product disagreement. No public co-founder history or operating agreement appears in the dossier, so Torus cannot receive the benefit of a conversation I do not know occurred. HQ Trivia taught me that two talented founders can create something addictive while leaving the explosive device under the table.

SpaceX is the single-anchor parallel on capital intensity: the moat came from choosing a domain where conventional investors recoiled from the physical and financial burden. Torus serves energy, construction, and industrial projects, but Torus itself is managed-cloud or customer-network software rather than frontier hardware. Engineering liability, infrastructure security, and SOC 2 certification in progress give Torus some unsexy friction; those barriers become valuable only if accuracy data and deployment history compound into trust. Today, Torus inherits industrial complexity without yet owning an industrial moat.

Multiple Fortune 500 companies and major EPCs reportedly deploying Torus lead me to predict that by July 2029 Torus will have narrowed from general document consistency into electrical-procurement and RFI approvals, reaching $4 million to $8 million in ARR but remaining an independent specialist rather than the system of record. Torus can reach that outcome because Marcus knows how capital projects actually move and Rahul can turn recurring document relationships into software. Torus will narrow because Trunk Tools already occupies the broader corpus-and-agent position, while liability will reward one trustworthy workflow before it rewards a universal engineering brain.

Three active hires at salaries up to $250,000 and reported Fortune 500 deployments form the strongest bull case: Torus may already have the product love that HQ Trivia taught me can override a weak tactile or governance signal. Repeat founders, Marcus’s $100 million project history, and customer-controlled-network deployment could make Torus an enterprise compounder before public revenue appears. The bear evidence wins today: Torus names no customers, publishes no accuracy benchmark, shows no retention, claims no moat, and faces Trunk Tools’s overlapping Cortex product. I am a professional daydreamer, but I invest my own money; documented expansion from three named customers into repeat paid projects, producing at least $2 million ARR with published accuracy and human-override data, would flip my verdict.
