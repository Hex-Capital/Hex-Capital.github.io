# Metalcraft -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Metalcraft’s strongest path. A life-science model developer could buy the taskset catalog and the rights to maintain it if exclusive control materially reduced the cost or time of running recurring evaluations. The memo documents six public tasksets, samples, and a way to request access. Metalcraft publishes model-performance results for its tasksets. The buyer savings and value of exclusive ownership are inferences, and the memo provides no paid adoption, measured buyer benefit, or evidence of transferable exclusive rights. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

A paid production pilot that measures a buyer’s savings against its internal benchmark would be a concrete milestone. The published catalog gives Metalcraft a product to test, while pilot funding, cost, and timing remain open. The memo also supplies no independent liquidity case. It reports no revenue signal to underwrite a sponsor buyout, public listing, or company-funded payout, and no secondary demand for Metalcraft shares. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Illustrative proceeds.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-ons. With $5 million of assumed net debt, a $25 million enterprise-value sale at year eight returns $600,000 gross, or 1.2×. A $100 million sale returns $2.85 million, or 5.7×. The $2.5 million target requires about $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, or $88.3 million of enterprise value with that debt assumption. At 2% and 4% ownership, the enterprise-value hurdles are $130 million and $67.5 million. These are gross pro rata scenarios before investor taxes. The memo supplies no deal pricing or shareholder terms to substantiate them. If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and Metalcraft reinvests all available cash without a secondary or payout, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. Wind-down proceeds would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo does not quantify. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

OpenAI and Benchling already build overlapping evaluations, and Metalcraft’s request-based catalog suggests licensing could give a buyer access without an acquisition. That is direct evidence of an in-house alternative and a plausible case for licensing. The memo leaves open whether either option fully substitutes for Metalcraft’s catalog. The Pass gate requires stronger evidence of a structural block or materially inadequate proceeds under the best supported sale economics. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production pilot show an identified buyer enough incremental value from owning Metalcraft’s tasksets to support an acquisition around $88.3 million or more under the 3% scenario? Measured savings against the buyer’s internal benchmark, paired with buyer-backed economics supporting that price range, would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that the buyer can capture the same benefit through licensing or internal development would weaken the acquisition case.
