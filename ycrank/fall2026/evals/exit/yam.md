# Yam -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a romance-game publisher is Yam’s strongest path. A publisher could buy Yam’s persistent story engine and creator network to gain an audience and a pipeline of new games. The case for ownership depends on whether Yam can build a rights-cleared catalog whose creators and paying players would be costly for a publisher to attract on its own. The public catalog spans 112 fandoms, and its top listing displays 2.4 million plays. Yam separately reports 1 million beta play hours and more than three hours of average daily play per daily active user. The memo shows paid offers but supplies no realized revenue, paid retention, or contribution margins. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The operating milestone is repeat paid play across several original or properly licensed titles, with positive contribution after model and moderation costs. Yam’s live product and paid offers give it a way to run that test. The disclosed seed and angel investments leave runway and the cost of building a rights-cleared catalog unknown. The memo supplies no usable acquisition-price evidence or separately supported route to cash for this investor. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** The illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3%, with no follow-on checks. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years. The holder needs $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume all cash at closing, zero net debt, fees, or other price adjustments, and a simple pro rata waterfall with no senior claims. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. Under those assumptions, enterprise value equals distributable equity proceeds.

- **Modest sale, year 4.** At $30 million enterprise value, the holder receives $900,000 in cash before investor taxes, or 1.8×.
- **Strong sale, year 7.** At $100 million enterprise value, the holder receives $3 million, or 6×. At 2% ownership, the same sale yields $2 million, or 4×.
- **Prolonged private, through year 8.** Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and Yam reinvests available cash. The holder retains the shares and realizes $0, or 0×.

The sale prices are scenario assumptions. The memo leaves buyer willingness to pay at the required price unresolved.

### Countercase

A publisher could build a similar romance feature with commercially available models. Yam’s most-played listing is a fan game, and rights holders can request takedowns. A buyer could therefore face the cost of rebuilding the very catalog it would buy Yam to obtain. That risk weakens the ownership rationale and makes transferability a diligence issue. Yam’s announced original-IP plans leave a testable route open, so the evidence supports Watch. The memo establishes neither a binding barrier to selling the business nor a price ceiling that triggers the Pass override. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Yam reproduce repeat paid play across several titles whose rights a buyer could acquire, while earning positive contribution after model and moderation costs? Verified rights and cohort results at a scale that makes the acquisition hurdle credible would move the action to Reach out to test what a publisher would pay for ownership. If paid engagement remains concentrated in fan titles vulnerable to takedowns, keep Yam on Watch.
