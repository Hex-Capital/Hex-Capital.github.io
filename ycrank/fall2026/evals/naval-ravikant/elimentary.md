# Elimentary -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Elimentary’s founders have earned the right to attempt this. Gorjilla and Saini operated credit products; Sarda built debt-collection automation at Skit.ai. Their previous billion-dollar loan books belong in the assessment of their knowledge, never in Elimentary’s traction. The stronger evidence is what this team has now shipped: financial tools executing reconciliation and covenant workflows in production. These founders understand that financial operations involve exceptions, changing agreements, and accountability. That is specific knowledge acquired through action.

The unresolved question is who owns the accumulated learning. Elimentary’s semantic layer, customized tools, and clause-level provenance make the product useful. The dossier does not establish that they become harder for a competitor to reproduce with each deployment. Elimentary explicitly says institutional knowledge and learning loops remain with clients. That can help win procurement while leaving the supplier without a compounding asset. If a competitor copied the code, the dossier identifies no exclusive data rights, proprietary model, or demonstrated switching cost that would preserve Elimentary’s advantage. Useful software can create substantial customer value while capturing little durable value itself.

The strongest argument against withholding a check is TresVista. Its October 19 partnership already includes 500-plus BDC filing checks, reducing approximately 20 hours of work to an agent run under 30 minutes plus an hour of human sign-off. More than 25 agents reportedly operate in production. This is meaningful evidence that Elimentary can cross the gap between a demonstration and institutional use. TresVista could also spread deployments across existing client relationships, making customization an entry cost into repeat distribution. But a partner supplying customers does not establish that customers improve the product for one another. The permissionless participation and customer-created supply I favor are absent here; the distribution relationship is promising, while the user-to-user loop remains unproved.

There is room to maneuver beyond private-credit covenant work. Reconciliation, fund administration, and FP&A share a need to turn financial records into reviewed operational outputs, and TresVista gives that adjacency a concrete route. Finley’s combination with Concord independently demonstrates providers pursuing these operational budgets. It also identifies the competitive problem: incumbents already combine financial infrastructure with verification and human oversight. Elimentary must capture part of those budgets economically. The reported $150,000 annual customer saving measures value delivered; it reveals neither Elimentary’s price nor its gross profit. With no disclosed revenue or billing unit, I assign monetization its absent-evidence default.

The correctness problem could determine whether this becomes scalable software or a capable implementation business. Elimentary reports 36 filing-contradicting statements in open-ended benchmark answers even with its prepared data layer. Without a denominator, that is not an error rate, but it explains why analyst review remains necessary. The TresVista result shows review can coexist with substantial savings. What remains unknown is whether review and customization decline as deployments accumulate. No documented founder-integrity, artificial-deadline, or morale-dependence red flag appears in the dossier. Publishing residual errors is useful candor, although it cannot establish founder integrity by itself.

I would keep watching and withhold my personal check today. My exceptions for missing monetization depend on an emerging self-reinforcing network; my exceptions for missing distribution depend on unusually strong product and capital-efficiency evidence. Elimentary has production proof, but neither exception is established strongly enough to override the missing compounding mechanism. One verifiable result would change my decision: an independently evaluated deployment showing that a proprietary cross-client asset, which Elimentary has contractual rights to reuse, materially improves accuracy over an otherwise identical system without that asset. That would demonstrate that each installation builds something Elimentary owns.
