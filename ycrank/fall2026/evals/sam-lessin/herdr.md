# herdr -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Herdr has already raised $6 million led by Bessemer, alongside YC and prominent technology executives. What scarce check am I supplying? The September 8 financing announcement establishes institutional backing and a prestige-heavy syndicate; it does not disclose valuation or competing offers. I will not invent an auction price. But this is affirmative evidence that capital has found the company, while the evidence that my personal check buys an overlooked belief is absent.

The interesting asset is the developer’s execution workflow. Herdr owns terminal processes independently of attached clients and exposes agent states through a CLI and socket API. That fits my pattern of software reorganizing a neglected operating system: developers keep their existing terminals and machines while gaining coordination. The problem is ownership of the resulting economics. The runtime is Apache-2.0, Cloud remains forthcoming, and Herdr Extended already distributes upstream-based binaries with additional functionality. Developers can find herdr indispensable without the company capturing much of that value.

The strongest objection to passing is that Can Celik has already shipped something people actively build around. The dossier reports 46,194 stars, 1,644,092 cumulative installs, and 1,789 community plugins; DigitalOcean and Fly.io have demonstrated integrations. Combined with his AI-assisted ultrasound development and fintech AI experience, this is credible evidence of a builder connecting AI development with everyday engineering operations. A community that supplies its own extensions could make Cloud distribution unusually cheap. But cumulative installs do not establish retained users, and plugin availability does not establish that proprietary access caused a purchase. I give Celik substantial execution credit without converting the same evidence into imaginary customers.

NanoCo is the relevant exception to my price discipline: both companies attracted substantial developer attention around open agent software. The material difference is that NanoCo also had early enterprise customers and a rejected acquisition offer—external evidence that someone valued the asset beyond free adoption. Herdr’s dossier supplies neither. Its technical substance makes dismissing it as a generic AI wrapper lazy; its existing fork makes assuming defensibility equally lazy. The question is whether the community relationship produces purchases that a compatible distribution cannot capture.

I pass with my own money. The dossier names no capital-funded hypothesis with a metric and deadline, so that dimension receives its missing-evidence default. Likewise, absent Cloud pricing, margin, burn, and runway receive the economics default; I am not treating undisclosed revenue as zero or assuming another round is necessary. My flip condition is an executable sub-$10 million post-money investment opportunity whose six-month budget reaches operating break-even on already contracted Cloud receipts. That would establish what my capital buys and what happens if the next financing never arrives.
