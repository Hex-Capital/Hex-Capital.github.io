# Caladh -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Protecting a port or subsea cable could have consequences far larger than the price of its defenses. But Caladh has not quantified that leverage. The dossier supplies a $1.98 billion adjacent maritime counter-drone market in 2026, not the infrastructure value protected, the present cost of protection, or the improvement Eclipse and Cloak could deliver. I can imagine an enormous consequence. I cannot underwrite my imagination as company evidence. My decision is **Pass** on the current dossier.

The important technical distinction is detection versus defeat. Sonardyne advertises underwater detection at 1,200 metres; Caladh proposes denying navigation, communications, and sensing from buoys or tow-fish, plus seabed concealment. That could address a different constraint. But a different advertised function does not establish a technical discontinuity. There are no disclosed defeat rates, operating ranges, or comparative trials. The decisive question is whether Caladh can reliably interrupt a vehicle’s mission when that vehicle can continue without external communications or navigation signals. The dossier does not answer it.

Kristen Kita is the strongest reason to take that question seriously. Her MIT–WHOI doctorate and February 2022 research on passive underwater tracking amid passing boats establish relevant scientific depth. This is substantially more credible than a generic team discovering defense procurement. The strongest bull case is that someone who understands noisy underwater environments has identified a practical intervention outsiders would miss. That fits my willingness to fund difficult physical systems and scarce scientific expertise. Yet tracking research is evidence of tracking expertise; it does not validate denial. Nor does the biography document belief revision or complementary recruiting, so those specific founder criteria remain at their missing-evidence default.

Caladh’s first capital should resolve that distinction. Briefing, assessment, and pilot request forms are distribution openings, not completed experiments. YC participation supplies no additional product validation. I would prioritize a controlled harbor trial against a predeclared autonomous threat set before expanding deployments: define mission defeat, range, interference with friendly operations, budget, and the response to failure. Those are my proposed requirements, not milestones the company has disclosed. Pricing, deployment cost, power requirements, and maintenance are also absent, leaving no demonstrated path to competitive protection economics.

There is no documented category exclusion, premature scaling, or refusal to learn here. Missing public information is not misconduct. I also checked the exception case: a deeply qualified scientist can justify investment before a product exists when the quantified consequence is overwhelming. Caladh supplies the qualification, but not that consequence or a demonstrated mechanism strong enough to override it. My one condition for reopening the investment decision is an independently witnessed trial showing mission defeat on at least 90% of a predeclared threat set—including vehicles operating without external navigation or communications—within a disclosed operating envelope. That would turn the central claim into evidence. Until then, I would keep my money.
