# Quivly AI -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Conditional on strong product-market fit, Quivly’s strongest path is selling its existing post-sales software to usage-priced B2B software companies across markets, then growing subscription revenue as those customers manage more accounts. The product turns kickoff transcripts, email and Slack conversations into launch plans and uses account data to flag risks and prepare renewals. The same inputs and recurring workflow give the current product a credible path across markets where buyers can connect their systems and approve data access. That portability is an inference from the product design. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Mycroft describes using Quivly to track implementation milestones and billable hours, giving the product a recurring role in customer delivery. Published plans rise from $500 to $1,000 per month as capacity doubles from 50 to 100 accounts and from five to ten agents. That gives growing customers a concrete upgrade path, with actual upgrades and renewals still unverified. Quivly reports that downstream accounts managed rose from 10,000+ at its October YC launch to 18,000+ in November. The current product launched in May, and the memo lists a four-person team. Those figures show early usage growth. Realized revenue, paying-customer growth and billed volume remain unknown. (Quivly AI, Defensibility, The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

At full plan utilization and monthly list prices, either tier yields $10 in monthly revenue per included account. Within a tier, more agent activity can raise processing costs while the bill stays flat. The contribution bridge starts with the subscription fee and deducts agent processing, connector operations, support and attributable sales and setup time. The memo gives prices and plan limits, while costs and staff time per deployment remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The evidenced sales motion centers on CEO-led demos, workshops and a paid Enterprise pilot offer. Published prices give buyers clear terms, while independent purchase and production setup remain unverified. If ordinary buyers need hands-on integration, data approval and ongoing support, Quivly could add substantial staff effort for each deployment even with strong customer demand. The structural gate stays open because the memo leaves room for standard connectors and reusable workflows to reduce that effort and establishes no requirement for market-by-market rebuilding. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of ordinary usage-priced software buyers discover Quivly through an existing channel, buy a listed plan and run a live onboarding workflow through standard connectors with minimal staff help? Verified payments, activation records and staff time showing a repeatable path with positive contribution after acquisition, setup and serving costs would move the action to Reach out. Continued dependence on founder-led setup would keep it at Watch.
