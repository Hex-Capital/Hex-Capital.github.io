# Liquid Energy -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is **channel-led or repeatable sales** through inference platforms that could distribute NanoDC capacity to AI companies with recurring inference workloads. The factory-built module integrates GPUs, solar panels, batteries, cooling, and satellite connectivity. The same design could serve buyers across markets where sites can support sufficient solar output and reliable connectivity. The memo records a November 5 launch. Liquid Energy reports 20 units on hand, agreements with unnamed inference platforms, and activation within 30 minutes of arrival. The memo contains no confirmed customer go-live, buyer budget commitment, or repeat paid usage. The first unproven link is whether a platform can turn an agreement into a paid, live workload through a process it can repeat. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Even if platforms bring buyers, Liquid Energy owns the hardware and may need to fund and deploy another module for each increment of capacity. Battery autonomy and satellite connectivity could also limit billable throughput. In the company’s commodity-token pricing scenario, a NanoDC generates about $1,500 in annual compute revenue. At that revenue, annualized hardware and deployment costs plus ongoing operating costs would need to come in below $1,500 per unit for positive contribution. The memo provides neither realized revenue nor measured costs for an incremental deployment. The structural downside gate stays open because field evidence is still needed to establish whether costs rise in proportion to revenue and whether factory repetition can improve them. (The Idea; Key Risks; Key Facts; Defensibility)
