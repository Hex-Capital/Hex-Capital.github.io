# Sutrix -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Sutrix has sold something before asking me to believe in the software. That matters. But proposed post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and what this check specifically buys are all undisclosed. I cannot call the deal cheap or crowded. I also cannot identify a capital-funded experiment from a description of an already operating business. Those two missing pieces keep me at **Neutral**, despite unusually relevant founder experience and actual purchases.

The founders have built the bridge I look for between systems that do not naturally understand each other. Ritvik ran clinical research, including a 300-participant concussion study; Supratik shipped software, worked at Amazon, and previously founded and exited Kairo. Their reported four years building together includes work at HSS, with additional software experience inside NYU Langone research environments. This is stronger than a clinician advising an unrelated engineering team. Sutrix combines direct access to research operations with demonstrated technical execution. The named CEO also gives me no basis to invent an equal-founder decision problem.

The commercial mechanism is more interesting than “AI reads charts.” Sutrix reports paying research teams at **10+ institutions**, and its founders describe **15+ labs and 50+ studies** preceding wider software access. Researchers have purchased work that the software now attempts to reproduce. Source-linked fields, approved mappings, and recorded reconciliation decisions could make that workflow increasingly valuable inside a lab. That fits my preference for access earned through operating work. But customer relationships are not exclusive data rights, and completed studies do not establish repeat software purchases. Layer Health already offers overlapping abstraction capabilities; Sutrix needs to own the research preparation relationship, because model capability alone will not protect it.

The strongest case against my hesitation is the **$150K+ in founder-reported cumulative revenue over the past few months**. At this stage, paid delivery can be a better discovery engine than a polished subscription product. Sutrix may already have customers financing its transition into software, which would make institutional fundraising optional. That is a serious bull case. Yet the dossier does not separate services from software or disclose delivery labor, gross margin, burn, or repeat purchasing. Human validation is sensible for scientific correctness, but it remains an expense. Sandbox FHIR connections and individually scoped deployment also leave open how much work each new institution requires. I give the revenue credit; I cannot turn it into demonstrated self-financing economics.

My exceptions to missing financing gates require harder operating receipts: measured conversion and retention, demonstrable cash economics, or a structure that removes repeated fundraising. Sutrix has meaningful purchases and founder receipts, but not enough evidence to establish those overrides. Nor does the dossier show the specific AI red flag of margins depending on errors disappearing; the approval gates suggest the team recognizes the problem. My verifiable flip condition is **an executable financing proposal below $10 million post-money that explicitly funds a 90-day test of positive contribution margin on repeat study deliveries, including integration and reviewer labor**. That would connect the deal to one consequential truth. Until then, I see a credible research business whose investment proposition remains unfinished.
