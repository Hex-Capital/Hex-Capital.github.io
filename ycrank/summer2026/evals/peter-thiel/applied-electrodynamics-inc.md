# Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Bilgehan Avser’s electromagnetics PhD, 35+ patents, and iPhone antenna work reveal Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.’s signature: exceptional hardware lineage attached to an indefinite commercial plan. Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. can plausibly produce a camera that sees through walls at 1-mm scale, yet the dossier offers no two-to-five-year roadmap for turning that breakthrough into a monopoly. Technical courage is present. Definite optimism is not yet visible.

**Anduril** is the high rung for operator pedigree: Palmer Luckey had already shipped Oculus to a billion-dollar exit before attacking defense. Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. shares the mechanism through Avser’s Apple antenna work, Brian Huppi’s iPhone multitouch and Nest thermostat, Paul Leutheuser’s shipped iPhone hardware, George McLean’s X-ray imaging, and eight years of working together. Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. diverges where it matters: Humane produced technical artifacts but not a durable business, and none of the four founders has independently built and exited a company. This fits my unstated operator re-up pattern, but only one rung below Luckey.

**Quora’s** weak dimension was the indefinite consumer roadmap: even excellent founders could not specify the sequence by which product iteration would become monopoly. Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. has definite engineering claims—an under-one-minute scan, across-room penetration, and 1-mm-scale reconstruction—but no named milestones for certification, manufacturing, material coverage, or customer concentration. Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. instead names construction, industrial inspection, security, cargo, and defense, each with different buyers and compliance regimes. One product architecture is not yet a definite company plan.

**Walabot’s** $199.95 scanner establishes the secret’s 18-month test. Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. claims Walabot stops around four inches into drywall while WaveSight reconstructs an across-room, scale-native point cloud; that is a specific incumbent limitation, not merely “AI for construction.” If Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. validates 1-mm performance across concrete, masonry, and composite materials, proprietary reconstruction data could compound faster than Walabot’s shallow-scan architecture. The missing clause is why Vayyar, after raising more than $300M, cannot follow. A better instrument is a technological lead; a monopoly requires an incumbent failure mode.

**Qorvo’s** March 2025 integrated UWB radar chip makes Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.’s monopoly trajectory counterfactual unusually clear. Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. moves into the Invest tier if its reported 250+ patents are assigned to the corporation, paid deployments create proprietary material-response data, and workflow integration gives contractors costly reasons not to switch. Today no patent assignment, dataset size, deployment volume, price, or commercial terms are public. Component commoditization therefore advances faster than demonstrated pricing power. Monopoly is the condition of every successful business; accessible components are the condition of competition.

**SpaceX** converted atoms-heavy engineering into a structural moat through capital intensity, regulation, and a definite reusability roadmap. Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. has the favorable half of that parallel: custom radio hardware, imaging algorithms, manufacturing requirements, and FCC coordination under 47 CFR §§15.509–15.510 and §15.525. Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. also touches construction and defense, sectors where schlep-blindness can create pricing vacuums. The unfavorable half is that FCC classification may constrain Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. without excluding Vayyar or another well-capitalized entrant. Regulation is not a moat merely because it is difficult.

**$457.32M** is the 2025 global ground-penetrating-radar proxy, and Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. currently has undisclosed preorders rather than a proven wedge into that market. By July 2029, I expect Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. to have narrowed from general construction imaging into defense or industrial nondestructive inspection, producing $1M–$3M in annual recurring hardware-and-service revenue but not becoming the construction industry’s imaging standard. Exceptional hardware operators will find a technically valid niche; the absent definite distribution plan will keep the niche from becoming a monopoly.

**Brian Huppi’s** co-invention of the Nest Learning Thermostat supplies the strongest objection: Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. may be another Asana-style exception where extraordinary operator lineage overrides missing monopoly evidence. Four complementary founders, 250+ reported patents, completed radio hardware, hundreds of Build Expo conversations, and a potentially 10x performance advantage could allow Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. to define a category before Vayyar recognizes it. The objection fails today because the 1-mm claim lacks independent validation, preorder counts are undisclosed, the patents may not belong to the corporation, and Qorvo is commoditizing the component layer. Ten paid enterprise deployments showing repeat use across multiple materials and defensible gross margins would flip my verdict.
