# Hesper AI -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Hesper AI’s most interesting proposition is that insurers need completed investigations more than another fraud score. The founders first built scoring software in April 2026, failed to sell it, and rebuilt around investigation files. That sequence supplies an actual experiment about customer behavior. The proposition would weaken if completed files failed to produce sustained adoption after the introductory offer expired. An unnamed MGA’s reported production deployment supports the hypothesis, but the dossier identifies no named competitor rejecting it. This is a useful commercial discovery; its status as a secret remains provisional.

Who gets to own that discovery? Hesper targets claims and investigation teams at carriers, MGAs and TPAs, while its Launch offer provides a more specific entry through new MGAs and TPAs. But Shift already offers overlapping fraud, coverage and subrogation agents; FRISS also addresses investigations. Producing an evidence-backed file is a product distinction without a demonstrated monopoly mechanism. Customers supply their own external-data licences, and Hesper’s contract prohibits pooling their data for shared model training. Those arrangements do not prevent proprietary technology, but they leave the obvious data-compounding thesis unsupported. I assign the absent-evidence default here, rather than pretending that competitive overlap proves technical interchangeability.

The founding structure is more encouraging. Pankaj Dhariwal and Nitish Badu previously worked at Nanonets and attended BITS Pilani; they have a relationship preceding this company and have already built and replaced a product together. That deserves affirmative credit beyond two impressive biographies. Their respective engineering and commercial responsibilities also fit the work. The dossier does not establish both founders’ full-time status, ownership or economic exposure, so the highest founding-team band remains unavailable. There is no evidence of convenience matching, outsourced core engineering or investor-majority ownership.

The strongest objection to passing is substantial: Hesper reports more than 10,000 claims investigated, approximately 80% lower handling costs, and an MGA deployment reducing adjuster time by 82%. A two-person team producing those results could have discovered an unusually efficient way to sell investigative labor as software. The reported $15 million in potential fraud and $1.2 million identified over two weeks strengthen the case for customer value, although neither represents Hesper revenue or independently confirmed recoveries. Hesper also fits my less advertised interest in financial infrastructure and adversarial information systems. My known exceptions allow demonstrated customer behavior or an independent technical breakthrough to outweigh an initially weak monopoly case. Here, the founder-reported results establish a serious reason to investigate; they do not yet demonstrate a capability competitors cannot reproduce.

Distribution is concrete enough to evaluate. Badu owns the commercial function, and Hesper uses founder-led demonstrations, retrospective claim evaluations and insurance conferences. The first 200 claims free and 90 days without a platform fee reduce the initial buying obstacle. But no conversion rate, sales cycle or implementation cost establishes repeatability, particularly when customers must arrange database entitlements. Custom usage pricing supplies one coherent revenue engine, yet the dossier provides no numerical price or margin from which to build power-law arithmetic. The adjacent $8.5 billion fraud-detection market cannot supply those missing economics. Nor can the founders’ prior $200,000 and $4 million ARR achievements at Nanonets be counted as Hesper’s sales.

I pass on a personal check today. Hesper has credible builders and meaningful reported use, but the evidence supports customer usefulness more strongly than durable value capture. My one verifiable flip condition is an independently documented competitive renewal in a defined MGA segment, showing that a customer pays to retain Hesper because a specific investigative capability cannot be matched by the incumbent alternatives. That would begin converting the current workflow advantage into evidence of an ownable market.
