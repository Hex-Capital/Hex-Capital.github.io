# Wingman Law -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Wingman Law has founders I can underwrite more readily than its financing. Post-money valuation: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. What my check uniquely makes possible: undisclosed. YC membership establishes none of those answers, and it does not establish an auction either. I am passing on the investment described here, while taking the operating team seriously.

The strongest evidence is that these founders have worked across the systems they are joining. Nicholas Le’s 700-plus prior injury cases give him experience with the messy legal execution that an agent demonstration can conceal. Sun Woo Lee’s litigation experience and $350,000 of legaltech sales during law school establish commercial execution beyond credentials. Le’s StrongSuit product work adds another operating receipt; its reported $4 million ARR belongs to that prior business. This fits my pattern of backing translators between established operations and software. Wingman’s founders have a credible basis for deciding which work to automate and which judgment must remain with an attorney.

Direct representation also puts Wingman on the economically interesting side of legal AI: it owns the client engagement and can retain the benefit of better internal workflows. That is the strongest argument against my pass. A competent California practice could become a worthwhile, profitable business without conquering national legal services. But owning delivery does not yet establish proprietary distribution. The documented acquisition channels are introduction requests and branded-car advertising, with no reported client conversion. The public visualization uses licensed litigation data. Meanwhile, Supio sells overlapping automation to incumbent firms, so software capability alone does not establish a durable cost advantage.

The contingency model makes cash timing decisive. Wingman collects attorney fees following recovery, while acquisition, case work, and attorney supervision consume resources beforehand. The dossier supplies no fee percentage, expense allocation, collection cycle, burn, or margin. Those are unknowns, not evidence of bad economics. Nevertheless, deleting the next financing leaves me unable to determine whether this firm can fund its own growth. Attorney-led agents are a sensible response to accuracy risk; they also mean the economics must include real review costs. I would not underwrite margins that require consequential errors to disappear.

My known exceptions do not bridge that gap. Exceptional conversion, retained customer demand, or a financing structure that demonstrably eliminates repeated fundraising could outweigh missing conventional evidence. Wingman currently documents founder execution, without those operating overrides. Nor does the dossier establish the red flags of an auctioned round, equal-founder paralysis, or dependence on flawless automation. The problem is narrower: the strongest affirmative evidence sits in the team, while the capital plan and customer economics remain unproved. My single flip condition is a completed seed-funded operating period showing firm-wide cash break-even from collected contingency fees, including acquisition, case expenses, and attorney review, without another equity raise. That would establish a functioning base business and materially change my underwriting; price would still determine whether I buy.
