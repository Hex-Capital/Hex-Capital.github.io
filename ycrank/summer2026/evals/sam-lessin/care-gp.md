# Care GP -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I first need the deal, and the dossier does not provide it. No post-money valuation, no competing-term-sheet count, no proof that this is the first scarce check. That earns 6/28—not because Care GP is a normal pre-seed company, but because I cannot distinguish cheap non-consensus risk from an auctioned YC asset. The operating evidence is unusually good; it cannot tell me whether the security is good.

Care GP has already found a real dark pool inside Australian general practice. The important number is not 3 million documents processed; it is that inbound referrals and word of mouth produced threefold growth in 90 days, while 85% of pilots converted, reported churn was zero, and more than 150 clinics adopted the product within six months. This is owned workflow causing purchase and continued use, not followers or integration theater. The Bp Premier connection embeds distribution where clinics already work, and one customer reducing document allocation from six hours to under 30 minutes explains why the referrals propagate.

The economics strengthen the exception case. Care GP reached US$200,000 ARR after 3.5 months, approximately US$5,000 ACV, and reported profitability within six months. Delete the next financing and there is still a business. But profitability without gross margin, burn, or runway is not yet a one-and-done model—especially when a 5%–20% human edit rate may conceal labor inside the software margin. I give the economics the maximum defensible score where revenue exists but unit-level cash mechanics remain undisclosed.

The bear case stings because Facere already offers both document allocation and voice automation inside Bp Premier. Care GP’s broader roadmap across email, billing, referrals, and records transfer could become an operating system, or it could become several simultaneous goals wrapped around commodity models. The 3 million processed documents matter only if clinic corrections generate proprietary accuracy or switching costs; the dossier supplies neither an independent accuracy comparison nor a retention cohort. The likely 36-month outcome is still US$4 million–US$7 million ARR concentrated in Australian primary care, but direct product overlap could compress this into a profitable feature business rather than a defining software company.

Melvin Chen has shipped companies and clearly knows how to commercialize, but the dossier does not document clinical operations or healthcare-software deployments before Care GP. The earlier co-founders’ status is also unresolved. A sole accountable CEO is cleaner than an equal-founder committee, yet founder clarity is not the same as having operated both the clinic workflow and the technical system being joined. The product’s adoption earns more credit than an inferred résumé fit.

The remaining failure is use of capital. Hiring against a broad multi-agent roadmap is not a venture moment. What finite truth does this round reveal, by what date, and with what pass/fail metric? My one verifiable flip condition is a financing memo showing a sub-US$10 million post-money valuation with proceeds committed to proving, within 12 months, that a second agent raises retained clinic ACV without increasing human-review cost. If Care GP can document that experiment, I invest; without it, I am being asked to admire a good business without knowing whether this is a good check.
