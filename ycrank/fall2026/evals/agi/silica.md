# Silica -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** As robot learning expands into contact-heavy tasks, training teams using low-cost arms could need reliable force data that cheaper models alone cannot supply. Silica publicly demonstrated gearbox-integrated output torque sensing. The company claims it can detect paperclip-weight contacts and retrofit existing training arms. The demo supports the sensing mechanism. Whether it improves usable training data over motor-current estimation at a viable installed cost remains unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Silica’s San Francisco location and prototype-stage hardware fit the seed mandate. Milletti’s product design and automated manufacturing experience supports early execution, while the one-person team still has to establish production and qualification capacity. Silica reports requests for more than 60,000 units in one month through an email-confirmed preorder process. Paid conversion and buyer commitments to qualification remain unknown. (Silica, Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The expansion unit is a qualified joint on a deployed arm. **Illustrative scenario:** 1,000 shipped and paid units at the founder’s quoted price of under $500 would generate under $500,000 in hardware revenue. Contribution depends on assembly yield, calibration and testing costs, and warranty exposure. The memo provides no measured inputs for those costs or for production throughput. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Facts)

### Countercase

With abundant capital, competitors can build on existing torque-sensing products. HEBI already sells force-sensing modules, and Synapticon offers custom torque-sensing joints. If either meets a training team’s accuracy and installed-cost requirements, Silica’s integration could become a feature buyers can readily source elsewhere. Silica’s own accuracy under temperature changes and repeated loading also remains unproven. Buyer qualification and repeatable calibration could protect its position, but both are prospective. The alternatives leave the control gate open pending a like-for-like test. Path B has no separate evidence of a newly viable transaction. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent training-arm maker qualify a production-representative Q Series lot and commit to a paid rollout? A buyer-run trial should compare usable contact data, accuracy after repeated loading and temperature changes, and installed cost against its current setup and best available alternative. A paid commitment tied to a repeatable advantage and a credible production cost would support Reach out if the updated score clears the outreach threshold. A readily available equivalent on the buyer’s requirements would trigger Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
