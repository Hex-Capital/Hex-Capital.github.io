# ChampionEffect Inc. -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, an employee changes jobs and the new company’s working knowledge comes along as a coach: how this team actually closes its books, handles an exception, or gets a shipment unstuck. ChampionEffect Inc.’s employee walkthroughs suggest a possible beginning. But getting there requires capturing judgment accurately, maintaining it as workflows change, and transferring it without transferring someone else’s permissions. The dossier describes coaching through Slack, Teams, and calls; it does not establish those transitions. I can imagine the future. I cannot yet give the company credit for having found the path.

Why does the helpful colleague become the training department? Because teaching competes with doing the job. ChampionEffect identifies a real constraint: practical knowledge sits with employees whose time cannot stretch indefinitely. Reusing their walkthroughs could return time to those champions and independence to coworkers. That earns some credit for repairing an irritating system. There is no quantified teaching burden, identifiable institutional rent, or demonstrated transfer of control, however. An employer buying coaching does not by itself establish that employees gain agency.

The founders are the strongest reason to keep looking. Kevin Law co-founded Cambly and served as CTO; Emre Simdi previously led its GTM organization and reports helping grow **Cambly’s** revenue from zero to $100M+. That is relevant operating tuition, with complementary technical and commercial experience and an existing working relationship. It goes substantially beyond impressive former employers. What remains unproven is whether this particular problem grew from a persistent, independently pursued obsession. The dossier does not document earlier experiments or an operating wound that became ChampionEffect’s specification.

Califrais signing before launch is the fact that most challenges my pass. Someone committed before the finished product existed, and these founders have experience building and distributing a learning business. My exceptions for pre-revenue companies matter here: revenue is unnecessary when a working prototype and unusually strong founder evidence make the destination credible. But the launch announcement does not show employees repeatedly using Coach, contributing walkthroughs, or completing unfamiliar work independently. Section already offers personalized workplace-AI coaching. The decisive question is whether ChampionEffect’s captured knowledge changes employee behavior enough to distinguish it from another coaching service.

ChampionEffect also touches my preference for concealed infrastructure beneath an ordinary workflow. A reusable organizational memory could become such infrastructure; claimed knowledge collection does not yet demonstrate a controlled bottleneck or a learning advantage. Its access to employee calls and company workflows makes consent, custody, and permission boundaries consequential. Missing security documentation establishes an unresolved question, not a proven privacy violation; neither equal founder control nor closed-model dependence is established here. I would pass with my own money today. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: an independently observed 90-day Califrais deployment showing that a second employee cohort repeatedly completes company-specific tasks using captured walkthroughs without assistance from the original champions.
