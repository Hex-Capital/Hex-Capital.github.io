# Luca IQ -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Luca IQ’s 1,100 customer hours saved and 57% reduction in administrative preparation time identify a venture-shaped tax workflow business with no demonstrated value-seed entry. Luca IQ is highest on capital structure: fixed per-return pricing, completed customer seasons, and regulatory implementation work can create a default-alive revenue machine. Luca IQ is lowest on round mispricing: HBS Demo Day investor-vote heat, YC S26 branding, and an active AI-tax category arrive without one disclosed price, rejected term sheet, or tier-1 pass. That pairing says “sound business, uncertain investment”—the regatta may work, but I cannot yet see why my boat gets favorable odds.

Marina Mogilko / LinguaTrip is the high rung because $1.7 million bought exposure to an existing cash-flow asset through 5% of earnings and 5% of IP, rather than forcing the asset into a generic software cap table. Luca IQ shares the underlying mechanism: per-return pricing maps capital to taxable production, while IRS e-filing authorization and document-level traceability make each completed return economically legible. Luca IQ diverges because no revenue, dollar pricing, profitability path, or unusual financing instrument is disclosed; founding-member pricing is a commercial choice, not capital-structure innovation. Luca IQ looks bootstrappable but venture-acceleratable, which is good, yet the advertised REST API could still become ordinary land-and-expand SaaS founder-drainage.

Robinhood’s low-rung failure mechanism for a new investor is consensus arriving before entry—the business can win while the SAFE cannot. Luca IQ tied for the most investor votes at HBS Demo Day, entered YC as “AI, API, infrastructure,” and operates beside TaxGPT’s $4.6 million round, April’s $38 million Series B, and a $600 million SafeSend exit. Luca IQ provides no evidence on valuation or competitive round dynamics, so I apply the absent-evidence pricing default and then mark it down for visible category heat. Robinhood’s exception required Slow entering before the markups; Pinterest’s exception required network access plus an extraordinary missionary founder. Luca IQ documents neither exception.

Angelo Policicchio’s CPA background, first-hand tax-season experience, and more than 300 firm-owner interviews give Luca IQ real pre-pitch conviction rather than an MBA-generated market map. Over the next 18 months, that conviction compounds only if Luca IQ converts reviewer corrections into a deterministic corpus: every source document, corrected field, and completed return should make implementation faster for the next firm. Luca IQ loses points because Angelo has no public writing or X account, Akash Sadashivapeth’s GitHub footprint is thin, and the tax engine remains “coming soon.” Missionary evidence is present in the operating work, but not yet in the public intellectual record.

Angelo Policicchio’s approximately 3,000 LinkedIn followers sit above Luca IQ’s 101 company followers, but neither constitutes owned distribution. Luca IQ has hundreds of pilot applicants and CPA referrals, which is more valuable than vanity reach, yet Luca IQ still depends on founder-led demos, outbound sales, and an account-executive internship. The score flips upward if Angelo turns the 300 interviews into a named CPA practitioner community—newsletter, benchmark dataset, or tax-season operating forum with 10,000 organically acquired professionals. Without that, the founder is credible in the category but not an asset already sitting on the cap table.

Ro is the useful single-anchor parallel: regulatory rails and operational schleps can justify venture capital when the capital intensity itself becomes the moat. Luca IQ’s IRS-authorized e-filing, deterministic calculation, human-review controls, and migration across UltraTax-style workflows resemble that mechanism, but Luca IQ has not yet proven that building a replacement engine is necessary rather than expensive theater. “Software is no longer investible.” Fine—that is deliberately overstated; Luca IQ becomes investible if tax liability and workflow integration create a regulatory rail, not if Luca IQ is merely an LLM wrapper for document entry. Tax infrastructure is outside my established crypto, creator, DTC, and prescription-rail sleeves, so the category fit remains weak until production e-filing proves the Ro exception applies.

SafeSend’s $600 million acquisition frames my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Luca IQ will reach $3 million–$7 million in ARR but will derive most revenue from intake, workpaper assembly, and integrations atop incumbent tax engines rather than from a scaled standalone 1040 replacement. Luca IQ’s fixed per-return model and 30-minute intake-to-review time can compound into a sound seasonal business; Luca IQ’s human-review requirement, migration burden, and incumbent distribution make full-stack engine replacement a much harder tack. That is a credible company outcome, but without discounted entry terms it is not automatically a credible angel return.

Hundreds of pilot applicants support the strongest bear case: Luca IQ could still die before reaching that range because Thomson Reuters can bundle SafeSend with its tax engines, while one deterministic-calculation error creates liability that workflow vendors avoid. Luca IQ’s defense is unusually concrete for pre-seed—one completed customer season, 1,100 hours saved, and a measured 57% reduction in administrative preparation time—so I do not accept “incumbents will crush it” as sufficient. I remain Neutral because workflow validation is not engine validation. I would flip to Invest if, by May 2027, Luca IQ can name three paying firms that each e-filed at least 100 returns through Luca IQ’s deterministic engine and the round is priced below the YC batch median.
