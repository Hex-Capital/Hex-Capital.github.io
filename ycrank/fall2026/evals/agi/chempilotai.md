# ChemPilot.ai -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

ChemPilot.ai has no supported AGI path in the Fall 2026 memo. For **Building blocks of superintelligence**, its initial programs target defense and aerospace materials. The memo identifies no AI workload that depends on those materials or scarce input that ChemPilot could supply. For **Industry enabled by superintelligence**, ChemPilot says conventional materials R&D relies on manual trial and error and can take a decade or more. Its model-guided lab could reduce the work needed to screen candidates, but the memo identifies no specific buyer or project previously excluded by cost or turnaround time. It gives no comparison of fully loaded cost per accepted result. (The Idea, Key Risks)

ChemPilot solicits briefs with performance, cost, and qualification requirements, which suggests a project-based R&D business. The founders report an operating lab where robots work alongside scientists and say they train models on proprietary experimental data. Those assets and the team’s prior materials research support early execution and some defensibility. A competitor already combines models and physical labs, and ChemPilot has yet to show that its data improve results across programs. The reported pre-incorporation collaborations show access to potential buyers. The memo provides no defined commercial trial, paid deployment, or delivery-cost data. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An aerospace R&D team might be able to commission a screening project it previously shelved if ChemPilot can reduce the cost of reaching an accepted result. The reported lab and prior research collaborations make that scenario credible enough to test. Even with comparable models, a buyer would still need physical testing and, where applicable, qualification. The decisive gap is evidence that a specific buyer can now pursue a previously unaffordable project. With both AGI paths unsupported, the Pass gate applies. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can ChemPilot deliver a paid aerospace screening project that the buyer previously shelved on cost grounds? A buyer-confirmed pilot that meets agreed acceptance criteria within the buyer’s budget, with staff time, failed runs, and required testing included in the cost comparison, would change the action to Reach out and make repeatability the focus of the conversation.
