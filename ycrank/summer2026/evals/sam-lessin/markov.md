# Markov -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot underwrite Markov’s round before knowing the post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and what this check uniquely proves. The dossier provides none of those facts. More importantly, it names no finite capital-funded experiment—only a broad opportunity to supply computer-use data. “Collect more workflows and sell them to labs” is a business plan, not a venture moment. What application, by what date, must Markov make an agent competent enough that a named buyer converts? Until that card is visible, the round risks financing activity rather than revealing truth.

The strongest evidence is the product already shipped. Two founders have assembled 48,478 videos, roughly 12,300 hours of professional-software activity, an 856 GB public corpus, and an AutoCAD environment retaining files, screenshots, model turns, and input events. The resulting 150,000-plus cumulative Hugging Face downloads—including 17,814 in the preceding month—show real developer attention. This is substantially better than a slide-deck data company. It also remains usage rather than commerce: there is no named customer, paid pilot, renewal, or evidence that an owned workflow caused a purchase.

That distinction matters because the public corpus is not yet a dark pool. It is distributed under CC-BY-4.0, apparently assembled from internet-sourced recordings, and its card does not establish the chain of title for individual assets. Scale AI, Mercor, Turing, Invisible Technologies, and the laboratories themselves can collect trajectories; research has already shown that 312 human demonstrations can be amplified with synthetic actions. Markov’s defensible asset would be proprietary buyer workflows, environments, evaluators, and outcome data generated through repeated engagements. The dossier describes that feedback loop only as an inference.

The bull case is sharp: public distribution could be the top of a sales funnel, the AutoCAD environment suggests the team understands that evaluation infrastructure is more valuable than undifferentiated video, and the founders have shipped enough to earn attention despite being extremely early. But the same AutoCAD benchmark contains only 50 tasks, and its evaluator can pass visually similar yet semantically incorrect geometry. That failure is actually the interesting technical problem. If Markov becomes the trusted truth layer for whether computer-use agents completed professional work correctly, it may own something scarce; if it remains a large recording supplier, better-funded vendors and synthetic generation compress the asset toward commodity pricing.

The founders have relevant proximity but not yet the full outsider-insider bridge. Dev Mandal worked at Sarvam AI, DevRev, and Telekinesis and built doubt.ai; Harish Ashok previously built the Zenith robotics-development assistant. Those are shipped technical receipts on the tooling side. There is no documented operation inside an AI lab’s data-buying function, no scaled annotation business, and no commercial access to the enterprises whose private workflows would make the dataset proprietary. Likewise, with no revenue, gross margin, burn, or runway disclosed, deleting the next financing from the model leaves no demonstrated route to profitability.

I am passing today. My one verifiable flip condition is a paid design partnership with a named model developer that uses Markov’s proprietary environment and evaluator, reaches a predefined task-success threshold by a dated milestone, and renews from operating budget. That single result would turn downloads into purchase evidence, define the venture moment, and begin converting a public dataset into an owned workflow. I would still need the round price; scarce evidence at an auction price is no longer scarce.
