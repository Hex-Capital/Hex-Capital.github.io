# OpenCrawl -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Conditional on strong product-market fit with engineering teams running several coding agents, OpenCrawl has a credible global growth path. Its searchable session history and shared board address the recurring job of carrying context across agents, tools, and machines. MCP search and deployment across local and cloud environments make the same workflow portable across markets with modest adaptation. Live mentions depend on supported listeners, and team-visible transcripts with imperfect secret masking could limit adoption by security-sensitive teams. (The Idea, Key Risks)

YC, X, and the public repository give developers ways to discover the product. The company describes self-serve hosted access with a no-card free tier and provides self-hosting instructions. The CTO reported onboarding a user whose agents exchanged messages with another user's agents. That report shows the workflow in use, while independent activation and paid checkout remain unverified. The November 16 launch and repository activity document early attention for the two-person team. The memo leaves the current product's revenue status unresolved, so commercial traction receives the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Repeated coordination and an accumulating searchable history could keep teams using OpenCrawl. If the reported overage rate is live, adding agents and recording more sessions could increase a retained team's bill. The free allowance makes that expansion contingent on sustained usage above the threshold. The memo provides no evidence of repeat invoices or paid expansion within an account. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Facts)

In a hosted scenario that assumes the reported $0.50 per additional million tokens is billed, a standard team ingesting 200 million tokens in a month would generate $50 after its 100 million free tokens. That $50 must cover ingestion, indexing, storage, search, and attributable acquisition, setup, and support costs for the team, including costs associated with the free usage. Project and session exclusions could limit the cost of capturing low-value transcripts. Actual contribution remains unknown. The product and distribution mechanics are concrete, while paid conversion, recurring revenue, and serving costs remain material evidence gaps. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

OpenCrawl could attract developer interest while generating little recurring hosted revenue. Claude Code's native messaging and shared tasks could satisfy single-tool teams, and Agent Relay already offers cross-tool messaging and searchable sessions. Teams that need OpenCrawl's cross-tool workflow may stay within the free allowance or choose self-hosting because of transcript exposure. That combination could leave too few paid accounts to cover hosted delivery. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo documents a portable workflow and gives no evidence that bespoke rollout or uneconomic delivery is unavoidable. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
