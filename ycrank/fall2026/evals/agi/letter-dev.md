# Letter -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**New Economies** is Letter’s strongest path. Letterpress-1 turns search and visitor signals into proposed website changes with live previews. The potential new use is routine growth work, such as fixing sitemap issues or updating landing pages, at teams that previously deferred it because investigation, implementation, and review consumed scarce staff time. The memo describes how Letter could reduce that labor, but gives no baseline cost or measured cost per successful deployment. The relevant comparison includes human review, rework, and agent usage across all attempts, including changes that never ship. (The Idea; Key Risks)

Letter reports more than 100 customers and thousands of pull requests. Those claims support early usage, while paid adoption, repeat use, and the share of changes that shipped remain unverified. The documented beta launch and Pregasen’s experience running a technical writing agency support execution at this stage. The next execution test is reliable delivery of production changes. (Market & Traction; Key Facts; Founders & Team)

Letter reports site-specific brand evaluations and integrated infrastructure. Accumulated site configuration and automation history could make switching more labor-intensive, though the memo offers no evidence of buyer preference when alternatives perform the same work. For scale, the expansion unit is a Git-based site running recurring jobs. Published plans start at $250 per month, and agent runs are priced at model cost plus 20%. Contribution per site remains uncertain because the memo gives no realized revenue or cost data for inference, sandbox use, or support. (Defensibility; The Idea; Key Risks; Key Facts)

### Countercase

Letter may be automating work that growth teams already buy, with general-purpose coding agents handling parts of the workflow and AirOps overlapping on content optimization. The memo establishes overlap. It leaves end-to-end parity and buyer switching untested, so the evidence supports Watch. The memo identifies no independently supported scarce complement. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent teams pay to keep Letter for recurring website work they previously skipped when a general-purpose agent workflow can deliver comparable results? Move to Reach out if a paid cohort shows that Letter brings those previously deferred changes into production at a materially lower cost per successful deployment than the prior manual workflow, and customers renew after trying the comparable alternative.
