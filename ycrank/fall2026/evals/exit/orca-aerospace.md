# Orca Aerospace -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by Orbital is the strongest exit route. The signed LOI, worth up to $20 million and tied to Orbital’s planned fleet, shows commercial interest in Orca’s software. Orca proposes to integrate once with Reflex’s flight software and reuse that integration across the fleet. If flight tests show faster, safe fault recovery outside ground contact windows, owning the software could give Orbital control over a capability it would use fleetwide. The value of that control is an inference. The LOI’s contingent ceiling and undisclosed payments leave acquisition pricing unanchored. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The milestone is a flight test that measures how much faster Orca can safely recover from a fault compared with waiting for ground contact. Orca plans human telemetry review before expanding command authority, with a possible Pathfinder test in July 2027 and Orbital-1 use planned for 2028. The LOI and Lyra concept study support a plausible testing path. Test funding and capital needs are unverified. Transfer rights, acquisition funding, required approvals, and holder eligibility are also unverified. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale scenarios assume an eligible holder, a full cash sale by Fall 2034, $10 million in combined net debt and deal adjustments, and a pro rata waterfall with no preference reducing the holder’s share. The prices are illustrative assumptions.

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Gross cash to holder |
|-----------|-------------------------------------------|----------------------|
| Modest sale | $50M less $10M = $40M | $1.2M (2.4×) |
| Strong sale | $100M less $10M = $90M | $2.7M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale | $0 (0×) |

At the assumed $100 million sale price, combined deductions above roughly $16.7 million would put the holder below the objective. The strong case clears the hurdle under the stated assumptions. The memo leaves both the sale price and the waterfall unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Orbital may get enough value through the proposed per-satellite license. Shield AI and Sedaro have already demonstrated onboard AI with independent command checking in flight, and AIKO has flown onboard mission replanning. Those demonstrations make the case for buying Orca harder, especially while Orca’s flight validation and Orbital’s fleet rollout are pending. The memo leaves open whether those systems can substitute for Orca on Orbital’s stack or what an acquirer would pay. The evidence therefore supports Watch under the decision framework. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

How much incremental value would Orbital get from owning Orca beyond what the proposed license delivers? A buyer-side estimate grounded in measured flight performance and a funded fleet rollout would move the action to Reach out if it supported a cash purchase price that clears the holder’s hurdle after deal adjustments. If that analysis showed the strongest credible sale would leave the holder materially short, the action would move to Pass. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
