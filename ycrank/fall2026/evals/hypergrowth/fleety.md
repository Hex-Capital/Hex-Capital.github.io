# Fleety -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with plumbing and HVAC shops, Fleety’s best growth path is to sell the same dispatch add-on wherever shops use compatible field-service software. The job recurs daily, and ServiceTitan writeback is a reusable part of the current product. Portability depends on whether the existing connectors can access the necessary data and whether local dispatch rules can be handled through configuration. Fleety lists Housecall Pro and Jobber integrations, with the clearest evidence of production workflow integration on ServiceTitan. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Demo-led sales and Fleety’s reported on-site work for every deployment make onboarding high-touch. Each additional rollout currently adds staff time and travel, with limited evidence that those costs can fall as volume grows. Daily dispatch and shop-specific rules support continued use. Fleety’s terms provide for subscription agreements and advance billing, while billing cadence and expansion pricing remain unclear. Its zero-churn claim has no disclosed cohort or measurement period, leaving net revenue retention uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Fleety reports assigning about 92% of jobs and saving about $60,000 annually in overhead at Albert Nahman. That result supports potential customer value. Named deployments and the October launch establish early activity, while payment status and realized commercial growth remain unverified. Initial traction takes the default. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Fleety may remain a labor-intensive add-on to a platform that already offers revenue-based technician matching. Native competition could limit what shops will pay while shop-specific setup and on-site deployment keep acquisition and activation costs tied to each new account. The structural gate stays open because the memo establishes the current deployment practice and leaves its necessity and payback unresolved. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of plumbing and HVAC shops in a new country find, buy, and activate Fleety remotely through an existing connector and standard shop configuration? Paying shops reaching live dispatch with positive contribution after acquisition, setup, and support would justify rescoring for Reach out if the result reaches 65 with at least Medium confidence. Evidence that unavoidable on-site work makes the rollout uneconomic would change the action to Pass.
