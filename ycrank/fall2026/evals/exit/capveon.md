# Capveon -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to a procurement intelligence platform is Capveon’s strongest supported route. The memo records Capveon’s October launch and a sponsor’s majority acquisition of GovSpend, an adjacent business. Capveon describes linking early public-project signals to equipment specification checks and page-cited bid review. A platform that already helps suppliers find opportunities could gain a way to help them decide which bids to pursue. Capveon’s reported coverage across more than 185,000 agencies suggests breadth. The economic case for buying that capability depends on measurable customer gains. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A seed-stage milestone is repeat paid use of the current product, with measured reductions in bid-review time or gains in qualified pursuits. The launch and targeted demos and bid reviews provide a way to test that milestone. The company’s earlier paying-customer claim concerned its workflow-handoff offering. Current-product revenue, retention, and the capital needed to reach the milestone are unknown. Transfer rights and closing conditions are also unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million by year eight, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. Both sale scenarios below assume $5 million in net debt and deal deductions, an eligible holder selling its entire stake for cash by year eight, and a pro rata waterfall with no preference diversion.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Cash to holder |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M leaves $45M to distribute | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $5M leaves $95M to distribute | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operations with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or distribution | $0 |

The strong scenario falls to $2.28 million if the holder can sell only 80% of its stake at closing. The sale values are sensitivity assumptions. The adjacent acquisition has no disclosed price in the memo, and the memo supplies no Capveon buyer economics, holder terms, or independent liquidity route. The proceeds, feasibility, and alternatives dimensions therefore use their absent-evidence defaults. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An incumbent could extend features it already offers. GovSpend covers meeting intelligence and opportunity ranking, while GovSignals offers cited bid analysis and proposal drafting. Capveon’s equipment-specific workflow could still save suppliers enough time to justify an acquisition, but the memo supplies no measured benefit or repeat paid use of the current product. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the evidence leaves both a structural closing block and materially inadequate proceeds under the strongest credible route unresolved. (Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can verified gains across repeat paid deployments support buyer economics consistent with a cash acquisition above roughly $88.3 million in enterprise value under the modeled deductions? Measured customer gains paired with economics from a plausible strategic buyer that clear that hurdle would move the action to Reach out. Customer gains alone would keep it at Watch.
