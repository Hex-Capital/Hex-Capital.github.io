# Orikata Bio PBC -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the stronger path. Noto already offers browser-based analysis, hosted model runs, and an initial assistant. The company plans a shared notebook that links inputs, code, methods, and results across local and hosted compute. If that notebook reduces setup, handoff, and review time at equivalent quality, a lab with limited computational support could run recurring analyses it currently skips. The memo provides no comparative labor measurements or evidence that such a lab has adopted the workflow. Launch-post views measure attention, and published prices establish an offer. Buyer commitments, paid usage, and repeat demand remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Orikata plausibly fits the seed mandate. Casey Mogilevsky’s documented work in experimental biology and machine learning, together with the launch of hosted runs, supports early execution. The next milestone depends on shipping and validating the shared notebook, whose release timing remains uncertain. If teams come to rely on that record for reviewing and reproducing work, they could have a reason to keep paying Noto when the same models are available elsewhere. That switching-cost hypothesis remains untested. Path A lacks a separately supported case. Orikata reports large sequence and structure collections, with full customer access still in development and no evidence that buyers depend on access Orikata controls. (Orikata Bio PBC, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Defensibility)

The initial expansion unit is a paying team at $200 per month. In a full-use scenario, assuming 52 weekly allotments per year, the included $50 per week represents about $217 per month in credit face value. Contribution depends on the actual cost of redeemed compute plus incremental support and exception handling. The memo gives no basis to estimate those costs, typical utilization, or how many newly served teams would pay. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Noto could make existing analyses more convenient while opening little new paid demand. Benchling already offers hosted model runs alongside existing research records, and Phylo offers collaborative research agents. With the same models, a lab might get adequate results in its current workspace and avoid moving its records. Hosted processing also sends research inputs to external compute providers, which could limit adoption among labs with disclosure restrictions. Feature overlap weakens the current value-capture case. The memo contains no like-for-like trial that resolves whether Noto opens a new use or whether buyers prefer it under model parity, so neither Path B condition clears the outreach gate. A feasible test keeps the case at Watch. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can independent labs that currently skip a recurring analysis complete it at equivalent quality with less total human labor in Noto, then pay to repeat it when their usual workflow has access to the same models? Test the released notebook in a paid comparison that counts setup, review, retries, and exceptions. Repeat purchases at a price that covers incremental delivery costs, with teams choosing Noto because they rely on the shared record, would support Reach out if the updated case reaches 65 with at least medium confidence. If the comparison shows the workflow remains uneconomic or buyers readily switch at parity, move to Pass.
