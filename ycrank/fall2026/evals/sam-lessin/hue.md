# Hue -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Hue has shipped something concrete, but I cannot yet underwrite the check. Proposed post-money: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: unknown. Whether my money supplies genuinely scarce capital: unknown. The website’s references to investors affiliated with YC, Cursor, Clay, and Vercel establish neither an auction nor a bargain. I give no credit or penalty for those associations. What matters is the price and what the money proves; neither is documented.

The strongest reason to care is Aneesh Kethini’s experience building and launching Datadog’s AI SRE. He has shipped on the agent side of the reliability problem, and Hue’s released SDK supplies evidence that this team can build the testing side. The Python package went from its September 15, 2026 debut to version 0.14.0 on October 25. That is more useful than a collection of employer names. It partially fits my preference for founders translating between systems they understand. But Josh Le’s engineering background does not establish customer access, and neither biography proves that buyers will entrust Hue with production context.

The bull case is that production context becomes the asset. Hue reconstructs tasks, simulates APIs and MCPs, and preserves state inside resettable environments. If customers embed those environments in release decisions, the regression suite could become an accumulating, difficult-to-replace workflow. Better agents might increase demand for that assurance. This is the serious objection to passing: a founder who shipped an AI SRE may understand failures that ordinary trace scoring misses. Yet the proprietary workflow remains a hypothesis. The dossier documents no customer purchase, repeat usage, or exclusive access. Zero repository stars do not invalidate enterprise infrastructure; equally, publishing an SDK does not demonstrate distribution.

The capital experiment should therefore test whether Hue’s simulated worlds reliably catch consequential failures that existing evaluations miss. The SDK’s inability to observe uninstrumented calls makes coverage a real constraint, while the redaction boundaries add work before sensitive production data can enter the system. Against Braintrust, LangSmith, and Patronus, environment reconstruction must earn a separate purchasing decision. No disclosed use of proceeds specifies a metric or deadline for proving that. Hue does not trigger my red flag about assuming agent errors disappear; its product addresses those errors. It does face the adjacent-platform risk, and there is no documented customer result strong enough to override the missing financing gates under my known exceptions.

I would pass with my own money today. Revenue, pricing, margins, and burn are undisclosed, so the dossier establishes no route to operating without another round; that is missing evidence, not evidence of bad economics. My flip condition is a signed round proposal below $10 million post-money that funds one bounded, 90-day paid-customer experiment with a pre-agreed test of production-regression detection against the customer’s existing evaluation stack. That would connect price, scarce capital, and a truth worth learning. Today Hue offers credible product-building evidence, but no documented transaction—customer or investor—that tells me what that evidence is worth.
