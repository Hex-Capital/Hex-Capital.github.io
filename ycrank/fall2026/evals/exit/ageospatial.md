# Ageospatial -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by an insurance analytics platform. Ageospatial’s dated property records and carrier integrations could help a buyer add inspection triage and property-change monitoring to an existing underwriting product. Moody’s acquisition of CAPE Analytics shows that a buyer in this category has acquired an adjacent capability. The founder reports signing Ageospatial’s largest insurer customer, with contract value undisclosed. The seed-stage milestone is to show measurable underwriting or inspection savings across multiple carriers at a repeatable deployment cost. The reported signing gives Ageospatial a starting point, while deployment costs and available capital remain unclear. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and zero follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million for the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. In both sale scenarios, assume an all-cash close within eight years, $5 million of net debt and deal costs, zero senior preference payments, and full pro rata participation by the holder.

- **Modest sale.** $50 million in enterprise value yields $45 million in distributable equity and $1.35 million of holder cash, or 2.7×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value yields $95 million in distributable equity and $2.85 million of holder cash, or 5.7×. At 2.5% ownership, the same sale yields about $2.38 million, or 4.75×.
- **Prolonged private operation.** Continued reinvestment without a distribution or eligible share purchase yields zero realized cash within eight years. The memo supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route.

The sale prices are scenario assumptions. The memo provides neither a transaction price for CAPE nor Ageospatial revenue or measured customer ROI to anchor a realizable price range. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A buyer may already have enough of the functionality. Moody’s owns CAPE, and Nearmap already offers imagery-based underwriting workflows. Engineer-led installation at each carrier could make Ageospatial costly to scale, weakening the case for an acquisition premium. Carrier change-of-control terms and third-party imagery rights also remain unverified. These risks leave the structural-block and inadequate-proceeds Pass gates untriggered. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)
