# Fire Wallet -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a wallet or payments platform that wants private per-request settlement and revocable agent spending controls. Fire reports an e402 mainnet test that returned a paid response in under two seconds. That supports technical feasibility. The buyer rationale is an inference. A platform could acquire Fire to add both capabilities faster if live merchants demonstrate recurring demand and a measurable advantage over the platform’s existing tools. Fire also reports that MobileCoin was acquired, with buyer and terms undisclosed. That provides a limited adjacent transaction signal and leaves Fire’s potential sale price open. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Founders & Team*)

Fire reports a signed Trustboop LOI and an enterprise customer onboarding. The roughly $1.5 million per month is the customer’s existing volume. The memo leaves actual volume through Fire unverified. The recorded $500,000 in YC financing gives Fire some capacity to test a production rollout. A useful milestone is 90 days of recurring live payments with a measured cost or commercial benefit. Pilot cost and runway remain unknown. Merchant-controlled wallets make a software acquisition plausible, with continuity of network access and third-party arrangements still to verify. (*Market & Traction*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

Using the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The $2.5 million cash objective requires about $83.3 million of distributable equity. Illustrative cases assume a cash sale within eight years, full holder participation, and zero net debt, deal deductions, or senior claims. A modest $30 million enterprise value yields $900,000 to the holder, or 1.8×. A strong $100 million enterprise value yields $3 million, or 6×. At that price, $16.7 million in net debt and deal deductions would bring the holder to roughly the hurdle under a pro rata waterfall. Selling only half the stake at closing would yield $1.5 million even with zero deductions. These prices illustrate the hurdle. The memo gives no supported sale range for Fire. Prolonged private operation with cash reinvested yields zero realized proceeds, and the memo supplies no evidence of an independent payout or eligible secondary. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*)

### Countercase

A platform could assemble much of the functionality through existing products. Crossmint offers programmable agent wallets and per-request payments, and Hinkal offers confidential merchant settlement. Fire advertises zero merchant and e402 fees, leaving direct monetization unproven. Together, these facts weaken the case for paying an acquisition premium. The memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a supported price ceiling materially below the holder hurdle. The Pass gate therefore does not trigger. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a recurring production deployment show enough incremental value over a payments platform’s existing stack to support an acquisition that delivers $2.5 million in cash to the benchmark holder? Measured savings or incremental gross profit, paired with buyer-validated pricing and a plausible holder payout, would move the action to Reach out if the bridge clears the hurdle. A supported payout ceiling materially below it would move the action to Pass.
