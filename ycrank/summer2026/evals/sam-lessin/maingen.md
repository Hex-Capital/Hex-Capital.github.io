# Maingen -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot underwrite the deal before I know the deal. Maingen discloses neither post-money valuation nor competing-term-sheet count, and nothing establishes that this is a scarce first check rather than another auctioned YC AI round. More importantly, the dossier never says what one finite experiment the capital buys. “Embed with more operators” and “build factory environments” describe activity, not a dated card flip with a measurable result.

The real asset is not SolarBench’s simulation code; it is the operator access behind it. Hundreds of hours with solar personnel produced scenarios incorporating telemetry, work orders, SOPs, inventory, vendor behavior, and expert grading. That is a plausible proprietary dark pool. The public benchmark also proves technical execution: eight tasks, 11 models, and 880 graded simulated weeks. But “working with frontier labs” is unnamed and unpriced. There is no contract, purchase, renewal, or evidence that this access already causes customer action.

The founders are the strongest part of the case. Phillip Yan has built energy-trading software and exchange infrastructure associated with more than $150 million in transaction volume; David Yang brings Scale AI experience and published model-interpretability work. Together they can translate between energy systems and model training. What remains unproven is the industrial-operator side: neither founder has documented responsibility for running a plant, deploying into a live control environment, or carrying the consequences when a simulated decision reaches physical equipment.

The bull case stings because model labs reportedly contemplated more than $1 billion of environment spending, competitors concentrate on coding or broad enterprise workflows, and the best model passed only 53.8% of SolarBench runs against the authored oracle’s 100%. That gap creates a measurable training problem, and Maingen reached proprietary operators before better-capitalized vendors specialized. But this is not yet the AI exception represented by Ando: the shared mechanism is proprietary workflow knowledge improving model performance, while the material difference is that Maingen has disclosed neither paid conversion nor retention nor customer economics.

My 36-month base case is that Maingen becomes a bespoke industrial-data and evaluation vendor with $1 million–$3 million in ARR, rather than a compounding environment platform. The same operator embedding that creates differentiation also creates service cost, while frontier labs can internalize successful methods and Mechanize, Bespoke Labs, Prime Intellect, and AfterQuery can compete for the buyer. With no revenue, margin, burn, or break-even evidence, deleting the next financing from the model leaves no demonstrated business.

I pass today. The translation layer is real, but the capital plan and cash mechanism are missing. One verifiable condition flips me: a named frontier lab signs a renewable contract worth at least $500,000 annually for an operator-derived environment at greater than 60% gross margin. That would show the dark pool causes a purchase and can finance continued access without becoming another capital factory.
