# Touchmark -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Roman Yanushevskyi is an IOI gold medalist, but Touchmark has published no customer, revenue, documentation, or working demo. That pairing is Touchmark’s signature: unusually strong hacker genealogy attached to unusually weak evidence of animal resourcefulness. Touchmark may have built a clever machine, but the dossier does not yet show the founders dragging it into anyone’s workflow. Animals leave tracks. Here the tracks stop at a LinkedIn launch announcement and a “Coming soon” page.

**Stripe** (2010) is the high rung for hacker genealogy: the Collisons’ Lisp and commerce history became a developer tool they were uniquely equipped to build. Roman Yanushevskyi’s IOI gold medal, Citadel Securities quantitative-research internships, NUS mathematics and computer science, and Lovable AI-engineering internship give Touchmark the same important first ingredient: technical taste before startup fashion. This activates one of my unstated patterns more strongly than an ordinary CS credential would. But Touchmark diverges where Stripe became convincing. Stripe exposed working software to developers; Touchmark has no public repository, documentation, evaluation results, or verifiable deployment. Roman looks like a hacker. Touchmark has not yet shown the hack.

**Kiko** (2005)’s failure mechanism was not an inability to write software; it was allowing a built artifact and an acquihire-shaped outcome to substitute for relentless persistence. Touchmark has a reported SDK, but Touchmark’s public evidence consists of a launch post, YC participation, and a placeholder website. There is no named user manually onboarded, no billing dispute personally resolved, and no unscalable stunt comparable to Airbnb’s cereal boxes. The red flag is demo-adjacent presentation without demonstrated user contact. I would not call Touchmark soft, but I cannot call Ilia Bolgov or Roman an animal from the evidence supplied.

Ilia Bolgov worked in product at Revolut, which gives Touchmark adjacent experience with pricing systems but not evidence that Touchmark arose from pain he personally suffered. Organic ideas compound because founders know which ugly details matter before customers can articulate them. Over the next 18 months, Touchmark must learn how vendors define quality, attribute outcomes, reverse disputed adjustments, and explain bills to angry customers. Without firsthand pain, Touchmark risks optimizing an elegant quality score while customers merely want auditable outcome events. The dossier says “relate to pricing-system design”; it does not say Bolgov previously needed this product. I apply the absent-evidence default.

Touchmark’s dependence on evaluating output quality creates a genuine schlep: automated grading, outcome attribution, billing reconciliation, and customer disputes all meet in one unpleasant system. Touchmark would move into my top tier on this dimension if it published an evaluation methodology that matched customer-perceived value and showed vendors accepting adjusted invoices without manual negotiation. Today Touchmark has no accuracy results, dispute process, customer deployment, or dataset size. Metronome and Orb can already ingest new metered events, so merely producing a quality score is software; owning the arguments caused by that score would be the schlep.

Reddit (2005) worked partly because its founders had already lived and built together; the pair was a functioning organism rather than two impressive résumés stapled together. Touchmark has the preferred two-founder configuration, with Ilia covering product and Roman covering quantitative and AI engineering. But Touchmark publishes no co-founder history, prior shared project, or evidence that Bolgov and Yanushevskyi have survived disagreement together. Two is the right number. Whether Touchmark has a pair rather than merely two people remains unknown.

Metronome’s $128 million and Orb’s $44 million make my 36-month prediction falsifiable: by July 2029, Touchmark will have pivoted from quality-adjusted billing into AI evaluation and observability infrastructure, with less than $2 million in ARR, because Roman’s technical strength can produce useful evaluation tooling while Touchmark’s missing founder-pain and customer-hustle evidence make the billing wedge hard to force into production. The mechanism is straightforward. Customers will use Touchmark’s scores for monitoring before trusting Touchmark to alter invoices.

Roman’s IOI gold medal and Ilia’s Revolut product experience form the strongest opposing case: Touchmark could be an early Stripe-like artifact hidden behind a placeholder page, entering a nasty pricing schlep just as AI vendors abandon token billing. **Loopt** (2005) is the relevant known exception—weak public product timing can be overridden by exceptional founder force—and Airbnb shows that an initially implausible behavior can become the business. But Touchmark’s dossier contains neither Altman-like force nor an Airbnb-like stunt; it contains credentials and a reported SDK. Five named production customers, at least $25,000 in monthly recurring revenue, and evidence that Touchmark-adjusted bills survive customer disputes would flip my verdict to Invest.
