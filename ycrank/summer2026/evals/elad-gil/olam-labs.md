# Olam Labs -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: production agents now run longer, multi-party workflows; frontier labs need to measure coordination, negotiation, and deception; Olam Labs becomes large only if performance in its simulations predicts consequential behavior well enough that labs repeatedly pay for pre-deployment evaluation or training. That transfer validity is the controlling variable. The dossier identifies it indirectly but provides no measured correlation, buyer-defined threshold, or paid deployment. The explicit absence of evidence connecting social-strategy games to production outcomes leaves the primary market gate unresolved.

The strongest signal is product pull before the commercial story is polished. Olam reports more than 1,000 daily matches, thousands of early-access users, nearly one billion tokens processed per day, and 86,729 graded table-talk turns by July–August 2026. Those figures demonstrate that two founders can operate an instrumented arena at meaningful scale. They do not yet demonstrate demand from the actual buyer: the arena is free, enterprise pricing is undisclosed, and no frontier lab is identified as a customer. Human interest in beating models at social poker may generate data without producing an evaluation budget.

Technically, deterministic environments, identical human-agent action spaces, replayable game states, transcripts, and behavioral measurements constitute more than a leaderboard. Better models should make the arena more informative rather than erase it. However, Olam currently owns an experimental loop, not an end-to-end institutional workflow: there are no documented integrations into model-development pipelines, release gates, fine-tuning systems, or production monitoring. The corpus could become useful infrastructure, but “proprietary data” is premature when a funded competitor can reproduce games and the corpus’s external validity remains unproven.

The bull case is that evaluation is still crystallizing and Olam has found a cheap public distribution mechanism for producing differentiated human-agent data. Braintrust is the relevant analogy only at the mechanism level: changing models increase the need for stable evaluation infrastructure. The material difference is that Braintrust evaluates application traces tied to deployed work, while Olam must still prove that game behavior transfers to tasks for which customers have budgets and consequences. Patronus AI’s $50 million Series B for simulated digital environments makes this both market validation and a warning that Olam’s window may close quickly.

The economics are presently upside down. Nearly one billion daily tokens support a free product, while neither gross margin nor conversion into private evaluations, datasets, or training environments is documented. The broader evaluation market may reach $2.25 billion in 2026, but that does not establish a sufficiently large multi-agent social-evaluation segment. The technology shift is real; the margin and adoption step change for this product are not yet demonstrated.

Om Buddhdev brings direct agent-infrastructure and multiplayer-product experience from Latitude/AI Dungeon, while Shreshth Sharma brings reinforcement-learning and production data work. Both left degree programs, and their relationship dates to middle school, which supports commitment and execution cohesion. It does not establish second-time-founder learning, exceptional frontier research, or the rare-team override that would justify ignoring the missing market proof.

I would not write the check today. The verifiable flip condition is one named frontier-model lab paying for a repeated evaluation program and publishing—or allowing Olam to document—a statistically meaningful relationship between arena scores and failures in its production or pre-deployment agents. Without that, my 36-month base case is a pivot toward custom agent-training environments with less than $3 million ARR, or acquisition as a dataset and talent asset, rather than an independent evaluation platform.
