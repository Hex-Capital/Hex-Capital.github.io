# Faraday -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Faraday has published a price for running its benchmark, but no price for buying its equity. I would pass on the evidence available. The reported September 2026 YC financing is $500,000; that establishes neither the proposed post-money nor competing investor demand. I cannot call this an auction, and I cannot call it a scarce first check. For my money, that distinction matters more than the industrial-AI label.

What truth does the next dollar reveal? Faraday’s workflow mappings, approval gates, and replayable environments suggest a testable hypothesis: company-specific controls can make agents economically useful inside messy manufacturing operations. But the dossier supplies no capital-funded experiment with a deadline and a measurable result. The product architecture is concrete; the venture moment remains unspecified. I would want one bounded workflow to establish whether deployment produces repeatable economic value before underwriting the ambition to coordinate everything from orders to robot controllers.

The strongest argument against passing is the team’s ability to connect systems that software founders often misunderstand. Oscar Rangel built factory systems at Worthington Steel and shipped medical devices at Stryker. Datta Kaligotla brings enterprise engineering experience, including Boeing and founding engineering at Handshake AI Enterprise. Those are credible ingredients for the outsider-insider translation layer I like, with Rangel supplying the clearest shipped receipts. Their published harness also scored **96.2 versus 71.7**, at **2.6 times lower run cost**, on their synthetic industrial benchmark. This is a serious technical starting point, not merely a claim that the next model will fix manufacturing ([founders](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/faraday), [benchmark](https://faradaycompute.com/faraday-industrial-benchmark)).

The unresolved mechanism is whether that engineering becomes proprietary customer access. Faraday maps permissions, dependencies, and approvals—the sort of embedded workflow that could become a dark pool of operational knowledge. But the benchmark’s 74 synthetic episodes do not establish that a manufacturer depends on those mappings or buys because of them. Fabtech conversations establish prospect contact; walkthroughs using prospects’ records establish a sales approach. Neither establishes conversion. Didero already executes procurement workflows, and Tulip provides adaptable frontline software. Faraday needs evidence that its broader coordination layer earns a place customers will preserve when those vendors expand.

Delete the next financing from Faraday’s model and the economic destination is still unknown. Subscription and agreed-pilot terms establish a way to charge, but disclose no price, implementation cost, margin, or burn. Lower benchmark run cost cannot answer how much human work a live deployment requires. Approval gates are sensible controls; they also make the cost of supervision a question to measure. I would not assume agent errors disappear at scale, but neither does the dossier establish that Faraday makes that assumption.

I would override missing financing gates for demonstrated conversion and retention, compelling cash economics, or durable proprietary access. Faraday’s technical result and founder experience do not yet supply those exceptions; nor is there a documented prior exit and alternative financing structure that changes the underwriting. The small team and absent revenue history are ordinary pre-seed conditions. My pass reflects where the affirmative evidence sits: strong builders, with purchase causality and the capital experiment still unproven. **One verifiable condition would reopen diligence: a paid production renewal whose collected fees exceed the fully loaded implementation, inference, and ongoing support costs.** That would begin to establish a functioning base business beneath the AI option.
