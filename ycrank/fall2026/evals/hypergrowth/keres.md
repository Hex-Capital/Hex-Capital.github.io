# Keres -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Channel-led or repeatable sales** is Keres’s strongest path. Military buyers in the U.S. and UK have selected competing systems for the recurring job of drone defense. That supports demand across markets, while the changes Garmur would need to qualify and deploy in a second market remain unknown. Keres says Garmur can return for reuse after an intercept. If buyers validate that benefit, government hardware sales could expand through follow-on orders for additional sites. Both the sales model and the expansion mechanism are inferences. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)

The U.S. defense accelerator gave Keres access to operators and acquisition officials, and the organizer listed it as an awardee. Even assuming strong operator fit, the first unresolved link is converting that access into a paid deployment through a process Keres can reuse with the next buyer. The memo leaves buyer-funded testing, onboarding effort, repeat purchasing, and marginal delivery costs unverified. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks; Key Facts)

### Countercase

Military buyers already have alternatives they procure. Even if Garmur proves useful, buyers could place occasional hardware orders that each require substantial evaluation and rollout work. The memo also leaves recovery after an intercept and return-to-service costs unverified, so the claimed benefit of reuse remains uncertain. The structural downside gate requires affirmative evidence of a durable blocker. Here, whether delivery effort rises in proportion to sales remains testable. (Defensibility; Key Risks)
