# Theo -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is Theo’s strongest path. An established website builder could buy Theo’s integrated hosting and weekly SEO workflow to improve retention or sell more growth services to its existing small-business customers. Ownership could make sense if Theo’s workflow produces a durable lift in qualified leads that the buyer cannot match economically with its own tools or a white-label agreement. That advantage is an inference. In one testimonial published by Theo, Buy My Lunch’s Google clicks rose from 131 to 929 over 90 days. Theo also reports average traffic uplift above 50%, with the cohort size and baseline undisclosed. The memo records paid tiers and one reviewer reporting a roughly $69 monthly payment. Aggregate revenue and paying-customer count are undisclosed. *(The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)*

A paid-cohort test against a builder’s existing SEO workflow could establish whether the lift translates into qualified leads and persists through renewal. Theo’s reported audit volume offers a recruitment channel for that test. YC backing is reported, with the amount undisclosed, so funding and runway to complete the test remain uncertain. Customer-owned domains and Google authorization are relevant to migrating accounts. The memo leaves software ownership and the transferability of customer contracts and data unverified. It supplies no independently supported fallback that could pay this holder. *(Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Defensibility, Key Risks)*

**Proceeds bridge.** With no check terms supplied, assume a $500,000 investment at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, or 5×. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. For both sale scenarios, assume zero applicable net debt, zero transaction expenses, and a simple pro rata waterfall with no preference adjustment. Enterprise value therefore equals equity consideration, paid entirely in cash at close. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each modeled at zero. The prices below are illustrative scenarios. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction price to substantiate them.

| Scenario | Timing and assumed outcome | Holder cash and gross multiple |
|----------|----------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Modest sale | Year 5 at $40 million enterprise value | $1.2 million, 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 at $100 million enterprise value | $3 million, 6× at 3% ownership. At 2%, $2 million, 4×. |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and operating cash is reinvested | $0 realized, 0×. Shares remain held. |

### Countercase

Wix Harmony already offers AI-assisted site creation and SEO, Durable automates blog research and drafting, and Theo offers white-label fulfillment. A buyer could capture enough of the benefit through its own product or a commercial agreement, capping what it would pay for ownership. Theo’s testimonials establish reported search gains for individual customers and leave incremental leads, paid retention, and buyer value unresolved. The overlap makes the ownership case weak today. The memo does not establish that a substitute matches Theo’s results or that every credible sale would fall short, so the structural Pass override does not apply. *(The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)*

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid-cohort comparison show that owning Theo would create enough incremental value for a website builder to justify a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A comparison against the builder’s own SEO tools and a white-label option, with qualified leads and renewal measured through a renewal cycle, would give the buyer a basis to estimate incremental cash flow. Move to **Reach out** if those results support an ownership advantage and a price above the applicable hurdle after transfer costs and deal adjustments. If the cheaper options deliver equivalent results and cap the price below the hurdle, move to **Pass**.
