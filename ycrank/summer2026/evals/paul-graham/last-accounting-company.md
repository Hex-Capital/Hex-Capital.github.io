# Last Accounting Company -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is not the market size. It is what broke. Last Accounting Company has built a real system, but the dossier never names a concrete obstacle, what one founder did when it appeared, or how quickly that changed the result. I cannot infer resourcefulness merely from the finished artifact. This is missing evidence rather than adverse evidence, but it fails my people-first gate.

The artifact itself is unusually substantial for pre-seed. The proprietary ledger exposes reviewed data through MCP and REST interfaces while preserving evidence links, permissions, and accounting controls. More than $80 million in transactions from dozens of Finnish companies has passed through it ([company blog](https://lastaccountingcompany.com/news/interfaceable-general-ledger), Jul 2026). That is a working programmable substrate, not a diagram. It also matches the pattern I like of assembling difficult infrastructure before claiming to be a platform. But the volume and architecture are company-reported, and LAC discloses neither evaluation results nor independent accuracy measurements, so this is strong technical proof rather than conclusive proof.

The demand evidence is less informative than the transaction figure first appears. Finnish startups and SMBs have an unavoidable monthly job, and dozens reportedly use the system for accounting, analysis, and decisions. Yet transaction value is not revenue, retention, or even automation rate. The customers are unnamed, the fixed price is unpublished, and the dossier does not show whether users returned for several monthly closes. Founder-led direct-message outreach is the right beginning; 944 LinkedIn followers and press attention are nearly irrelevant. Who keeps trusting the crude product with the books is the useful question.

The founders are better matched to the work than a generic “AI for accounting” team. Riki Karjalainen and Sami Laakkonen built petabyte-scale financial-data infrastructure at Token Terminal; Veikko Pere brings finance and legal experience; and the four founders have known one another since childhood ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/last-accounting-company), Aug 2026). The schlep is real: banks, payroll, filings, OAuth credentials, audit trails, and liability. But the dossier does not show that the idea arose from a specific accounting problem they personally encountered. Nor does it show a rejected hypothesis followed by a changed product route. Domain adjacency deserves some credit. It is not the same as an organic origin or running-back evidence.

The strongest bear case is that LAC has built an efficient accounting firm rather than a software company. Pilot, Truewind, and Zeni already combine automation with human expertise, while improving general-purpose agents reduce the exclusivity of LAC’s technical approach. Human review may remain the expensive part precisely because accounting errors affect statutory records. In that case, $80 million processed is a flattering denominator masking modest revenue and service-like margins. The proprietary ledger could become a genuine advantage, but customer corrections and “daily evaluations” are only a prospective feedback loop until accuracy, automation, and retention are measured.

My base case is that by August 2029 Last Accounting Company reaches €3–7 million in ARR as a credible Nordic accounting service, but has not yet become the general agent-native ledger platform implied by its interfaces. I am Neutral today. The technical proof is strong enough to keep watching, but no known exception replaces the missing evidence of founder behavior under resistance. I would flip to Invest if a dated account showed the founders bypassing a specific bank or tax-system blocker within two weeks and that workaround then carried ten paying customers through three consecutive monthly closes.
