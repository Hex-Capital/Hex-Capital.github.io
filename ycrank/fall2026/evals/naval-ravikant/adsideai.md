# Adside.ai -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Adside.ai’s founders have earned attention through action. Robin Choy and Guillaume Miara built and sold Botdog, operated paid ads for their own business, and have now shipped advertising tools with externally documented output: Apify’s September 2026 case study describes research covering more than 133,000 ads across 6,055 YC companies. Those records are evidence of a working system, not customers. The relevant specific knowledge comes from buying advertising themselves and building the machinery to manage it. The prior exit belongs in my assessment of the founders; it does not become Adside’s moat.

The uncomfortable fact is that Adside’s explanation for shipping quickly also explains why competitors can follow quickly. Reusable scraping infrastructure reduced development from months to days. Public advertising libraries supply the research material. Omneky and Madgicx already sell overlapping capabilities. If someone copies the implementation, what remains exclusively Adside’s? The dossier establishes no proprietary model, exclusive data rights, or demonstrated workflow lock-in. Its privacy policy also rules out assuming that customer campaigns train a shared model. Private account learning could become valuable, but that asset has not yet been demonstrated.

The strongest argument against passing is that these founders may build the business faster than competitors reproduce the product. Adside reports 30 companies live, inbound interest from its own advertising, and a workshop attended by more than 40 founders and growth practitioners. Its pricing identifies a direct payer: brands buy subscriptions or pay 10% of advertising spend, with a $3,500 monthly managed-service minimum. This could support a substantial business without a network effect. But live accounts do not establish paid retention, and published prices do not establish realized margins. I would be buying the possibility that operating skill eventually produces a durable asset.

Adside fits my interest in tools that turn individual knowledge into scalable output. It has not demonstrated the complementary pattern of users creating value for other users. A workshop is company-produced distribution; another connected advertising account has no documented benefit for existing accounts. The market offers room to maneuver across research, creative production, and campaign operations, but the cited $11.97 billion AI-marketing category does not establish an accessible adjacent market for this particular product. The exception I would consider is backing demonstrated builders before their network develops. Their shipped work makes that exception worth examining; the evidence of reproducible infrastructure keeps it from carrying the decision. There is no documented integrity or financing-pressure red flag. My objection is structural.

I pass on investing my own money today. Adside.ai has stronger evidence of capable founders than of an asset that compounds beyond their effort. One result would change my decision: an independently verified six-month comparison showing that accumulated private account learning lowers customer-acquisition costs by at least 20% against a fresh implementation using the same models and public advertising data, without additional human management. That would turn a plausible future advantage into something I could underwrite.
