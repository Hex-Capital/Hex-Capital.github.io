# Spine -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition by a benefits brokerage** is Spine’s strongest path. Spine acts as broker of record, earns carrier commissions, and handles payroll, benefits, and compliance in customers’ existing systems. An acquirer could gain the commission stream and the team and workflows that keep customers through renewal. A partnership could provide access to those workflows while leaving the carrier commissions with Spine. That gives a brokerage a reason to own the business if customer appointments survive a sale and commissions exceed the cost of servicing the accounts. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Spine claims more than 30 startup customers and about 15% average healthcare cost savings. The memo also records a $3 million seed round and reports 4.4× commission revenue growth over six months. The round provides capital to test whether customers renew and produce positive contribution after specialist costs. The memo gives no absolute commission revenue, renewal results, service margins, or burn, so runway and buyer economics remain uncertain. The agreements governing transfer of customer appointments and continued access to customer systems are also unverified. The memo supports no independent fallback that would pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The illustrative seed check is $500,000 at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-on checks. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The equity hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume no net debt, excess cash, or fees, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. They also assume pro rata allocation with no preference adjustments. All consideration is cash at close, with $0 each in stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation. The prices are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no basis for a buyer price range. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A brokerage could win customers directly and build or partner for the back-office service. Bennie already combines brokerage with benefits administration technology, and Rippling already sells HR and compliance support without co-employment. Spine’s reported customer savings could help win accounts while leaving a buyer little reason to pay a premium. If customers switch brokers at renewal or specialist costs absorb the commissions, the acquired book could be worth materially less than the hurdle. That risk supports Watch. The memo establishes neither a binding price ceiling nor that the prolonged-private scenario is the probable outcome, so the Pass override does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Spine show that its first renewal cohort supports a brokerage acquisition at a price that clears the investor’s cash hurdle? Move to Reach out when verified retention of broker-of-record appointments and commission contribution after specialist costs support a credible path to the required price, grounded in what a brokerage would gain by buying the book instead of building or partnering. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
