# RonanRx Inc. -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I want—post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and use of proceeds—are absent. That is not routine paperwork for me; it is the underwriting. RonanRx may be a cheap first check into an unfashionable clinical-operations problem, or an auctioned YC company selling “AI-personalized peptides” into the hottest obesity market in America. The dossier does not distinguish those radically different investments, so I cannot give the round scarcity credit it has not earned.

The company itself is more substantial than the slogan. Lloyd Armbrust is a repeat YC founder with three self-reported exits and a $50 million medical-manufacturing business; Emily Colman spent more than a decade in healthcare sales, including Eli Lilly weight-management work across hundreds of physicians. Together they can translate between physical medical operations and pharmaceutical distribution. The product also reflects operating knowledge: physician-directed dosing, pharmacist release, code-enforced workflow gates, lot genealogy, compounding, shipping, and refills. This is the strongest part of the case.

The potentially valuable asset is not the AI. It is the clinic workflow and the longitudinal record connecting labs, wearables, prescriptions, formulations, side effects, and outcomes. If clinics repeatedly run patients through that system, RonanRx could own a compact data pool competitors cannot purchase off the shelf. But “could” is doing all the work. The dossier identifies no active clinics, patient count, repeat usage, paid subscription, or treatment outcome. Sixty-five YC Launch votes prove nothing about medical purchasing, while Hims, Ro, Calibrate, and Form Health already demonstrate that intake, clinician review, customized treatment, and fulfillment are reproducible.

The bull case stings because this team may be unusually capable of manufacturing the missing evidence quickly. Texas-only availability creates a bounded operating environment, and the physician-plus-pharmacist controls could turn regulatory complexity into a workflow advantage. This resembles the mechanism behind **Ando**—AI placed on top of a functioning enterprise process—but the material difference is that Ando had measured accuracy, complete pilot-to-paid conversion, and complete retention. RonanRx has disclosed none of the conversion or outcome evidence required to override my AI and pricing objections.

Deleting the next financing makes the problem clearer. RonanRx has a recurring subscription model, but it discloses neither price nor revenue, gross margin, burn, acquisition cost, pharmacy economics, or a seed-funded route to break-even. The regulatory surface is also real: patient-specific compounding must satisfy FDA restrictions, service is currently limited to Texas, and the underlying drugs are not FDA-reviewed for safety, effectiveness, or quality. There may be a profitable clinic-infrastructure business here, but the dossier does not yet show that the current program survives without continued capital or favorable compounding rules.

My 36-month base case is that RonanRx pivots from consumer-facing “personalized peptides” toward a Texas-first white-label GLP-1 clinic-operations platform and remains below $5 million ARR. I am passing today because experienced operators and an implemented workflow do not substitute for a priced round, a named venture experiment, or observed customer behavior. One verifiable condition flips me: a cohort of at least 25 paying clinics showing 12-month retention, positive contribution margin, and materially better refill or adverse-event performance than their prior workflow.
