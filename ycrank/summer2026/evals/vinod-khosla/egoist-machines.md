# Egoist Machines -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

David Khachaturov’s Cambridge machine-learning PhD and rejection of provider-owned memory make **Egoist Machines** a contrarian consumer-infrastructure bet whose strongest dimension is founder defiance; its weakest is capital fit, because **Egoist Machines** currently looks capable of reaching market with commodity software economics. The economic mechanism Egoist Machines wants to break is real: OpenAI and Google should not own every user’s accumulated identity. But changing who stores context is not yet the same as changing what society can do. Governance over AI memory is useful. It is not automatically “too important not to do.”

**OpenAI** (2019) is the high rung for defying expert consensus: an unconventional, no-revenue structure pursuing intelligence at civilizational scale before consensus understood either the product or moat. **Egoist Machines** shares the instinct to reject the architecture incumbents prefer—provider-specific memory—and Khachaturov brings OpenAI PaperBench and AI-security experience while Erin McGurk approaches the field from Land Economy, privacy, and legal infrastructure rather than incumbent product management. The divergence is load-bearing: OpenAI built the foundational capability, while **Egoist Machines** presently asks ChatGPT, Gemini, and third-party applications to accept another permission layer. Egoist Machines has no documented public thesis confrontation, named technical breakthrough, or controversial commitment showing that its founders have earned the high rung.

**DoorDash** (2013) exposes the low-rung capital mechanism: a capital-light deal can still become enormous, but it must compound through a distribution or network effect strong enough to override the absence of science risk. **Egoist Machines** advertises its consumer passport as free for life, publishes no developer pricing, and identifies no roadmap requiring more than $50M before a commercial milestone. DoorDash had marketplace density as its exception mechanism; **Egoist Machines** has no public Passport retention, switching-cost, or network-effect evidence. Egoist Machines therefore gives me no reason to deploy Khosla-scale capital rather than a normal YC seed check.

The claimed 800-plus connector sources could make consequence compound over 18 months if every new integration increases the value of the user’s portable memory and every new user improves developer distribution. **Egoist Machines** could then weaken the economic lock-in that lets OpenAI and Google turn personal context into proprietary switching costs. Today, however, **Egoist Machines** names no live integration partner, developer customer, user count, or retention cohort; permission prompts and receipts may add friction while incumbents continue bundling memory for free. Egoist Machines currently improves control over an existing AI workflow rather than collapsing an incumbent regime—the 20–30% optimization pattern I usually pass on.

**Mem0’s $24M financing** demonstrates that investors already recognize agent memory as fundable software infrastructure, which works against the claim that **Egoist Machines** carries technical risk repelling consensus VCs. Egoist Machines has sensitive-data security risk, category-scoped permissions, revocation, and read/write receipts, but risk is not a technical miracle. The score flips upward only if **Egoist Machines** proves a proprietary architecture that safely reconciles conflicting memories across applications, prevents inference leakage under adversarial access, and outperforms provider-native memory on a published benchmark. Without that, Egoist Machines is closer to an API and consent layer than to the stack-deep engineering of Physical Intelligence.

**Slash** (2024) earned an exception because its documented Kanye West pivot revealed exceptional learning rate, not because YC pedigree excused missing evidence. **Egoist Machines** provides no comparable pivot, explicit thesis update, or “we were wrong” moment: McGurk supplies a 1M-plus-person audience and Khachaturov previously founded game publisher mazette!, but neither fact demonstrates how Egoist Machines changes direction under contradictory evidence. At pre-seed I apply the absent-evidence default rather than invent rigidity. Egoist Machines can improve this score quickly, but biography is not learning rate.

The free-for-life consumer passport and absence of published developer pricing lead to a falsifiable 36-month forecast: by July 2029, **Egoist Machines** will have abandoned the standalone consumer Passport as its primary product and pivoted into B2B permissioned-memory infrastructure for AI applications, producing between $500,000 and $2 million ARR without becoming a cross-assistant consumer standard. **Egoist Machines** has the technical founder and developer interface for that adjacent market, but not the incumbent cooperation or demonstrated consumer network effect required for universal identity infrastructure. Products follow the customer who pays.

Erin McGurk’s 1M-plus audience is the strongest objection: **Egoist Machines** could use unusually cheap consumer distribution to create the installed base that forces developers to integrate, reproducing the capital-light **DoorDash** exception while Khachaturov’s 4.7K-star BetterJoy history signals that he can ship software people adopt. That bull case stings because most infrastructure founders have neither audience nor technical credibility. The dossier still records only 268 company LinkedIn followers, an unopened general-availability product, 89 LodeDB stars, and no Passport integration or retention counts; audience is not transferable distribution until behavior proves it. I would flip my verdict if **Egoist Machines** secures a production integration with ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude that reaches 100,000 monthly active Passport users with at least 30% six-month retention.
