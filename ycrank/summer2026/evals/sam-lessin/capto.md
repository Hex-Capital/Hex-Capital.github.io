# Capto -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Capto has evidence that contractors will pay for help finding government work. It has no disclosed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or capital-use experiment. Those are different underwriting questions. Paid pilots establish a reason to investigate; they do not establish that my money buys an attractively priced risk. I’m passing on the evidence available, without assuming that YC membership means an auction or that undisclosed financing terms are bad.

The founders are the strongest reason to keep looking. Ananth Sankaralingam built RFP-search automation for a contractor before this product, and separately reports a prior acquisition of Promptly AI with 40K-plus users. The acquisition details are undisclosed, but this is more substantive than employer logos. Approximately 50 contractor-office visits also suggest deliberate acquisition of domain knowledge. Capto fits my pattern of backing people who translate between systems: software builders entering procurement through actual contractor problems. The limitation is that partnerships with procurement veterans do not establish that the founders themselves have operated a contracting business.

The strongest objection to my pass is that Capto may already be earning the access competitors will later struggle to buy. Snovation says the product found an RFI its existing pipeline missed; the CEO reports paid pilots; customers reportedly tripled proposal output and won awards. For a two-person pre-seed company, those are meaningful signals. But the evidence stops short of connecting proprietary access to repeat purchases. The “Company Brain” uses customer documents; the dossier establishes neither exclusive data rights nor retained workflow ownership. Customers’ 100-plus prime awards and $250 million in obligated value describe the customers, not Capto’s economics. I give credit for observed product use and reported payment, with limited credit for defensibility.

That distinction matters because GovDash already overlaps discovery, capture, and proposals while extending into post-award management. Capto needs a reason customers keep paying when comparable extraction and matching become ordinary features. Source-linked requirements offer a useful review mechanism, but retrospective PWin claims do not establish prospective accuracy. The relevant AI red flag is substitution risk; there is no evidence here that Capto assumes errors disappear or runs structurally uneconomic automation. Likewise, neither an auctioned round nor an equal-founder decision deadlock is documented. The known exceptions do not rescue this investment case: there is no comparable evidence of complete paid conversion and retention, an external acquisition offer, or a financing structure that removes repeated fundraising.

Delete the next round from Capto’s plan and the dossier cannot tell me what survives. Paid pilots deserve attention, but prices, delivery costs, burn, and runway are undisclosed; I cannot turn them into a profitable base business. Nor is there a named experiment with a deadline and pass/fail metric attached to the proceeds. My flip condition is a documented sub-$10 million post-money deal whose proceeds fund a six-month path to cash break-even supported by signed recurring customer contracts. That would connect price, customer commitment, and capital purpose. Today, the founders have demonstrated more than the investment proposition has.
