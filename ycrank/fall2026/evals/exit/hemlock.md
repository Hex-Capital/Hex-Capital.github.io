# Hemlock -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Hemlock’s strongest path. A robotics foundation-model developer could buy the Bracelet, its data collection workflow, and a library of force-labeled demonstrations. Hemlock shows synchronized video with contact, hand-pose, and depth overlays. It reports that adding force data raised egg-placement success from 10% to 100% and lightbulb-screwing success from 30% to 80%. Those are company-reported results on two tasks. The acquisition rationale is an inference. Ownership could be worth paying for if a transferable library and collection workflow let a buyer train across tasks faster or at lower cost than licensing data or building its own system. The memo gives no evidence that a buyer has measured that advantage. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A paid production pilot across workers and tasks could measure task success and cost per usable demonstration. The working prototype and the founder’s wearable-sensing and robot-data experience make that test plausible at seed. Worker-specific calibration could raise collection costs, and funding, runway, and the cost of scaling the test are unverified. The memo describes the Bracelet as proprietary. Transferability of the IP and data rights remains unverified. There is no separately supported fallback that would pay this holder. (Defensibility, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Illustrative proceeds.** With no deal terms supplied, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in gross cash to the investor, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

For both sale scenarios, assume zero net debt, fees, and other purchase-price adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. Assume all consideration is cash at closing, with zero stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, or compensation. Assume simple pro rata distribution with no senior preference deductions, before investor taxes.

| Scenario | Timing and sale value | Gross investor cash |
|-----------|-----------------------|---------------------|
| Modest sale | Year 5 at $50 million | $1.5 million (3×) |
| Strong sale | Year 7 at $100 million | $3 million (6×) |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested | $0 (0×), with shares still held |

The strong scenario clears the hurdle at 3% ownership and yields only $2 million (4×) at 2%. Both sale prices are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no usable valuation evidence or buyer economics to support either price. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could license task-specific data, build on ForceBand’s published wrist-EMG design and code, or use direct tactile capture. If one of those options delivers comparable production results at materially lower cost, Hemlock’s data could be useful while the price a buyer would pay for ownership falls below the investor’s hurdle. The memo establishes that substitutes exist and leaves their production economics unresolved. That supports Watch. The evidence falls short of triggering a structural Pass, and the modeled $0 prolonged-private outcome alone does not trigger the override. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would owning Hemlock give a robotics model developer enough economic advantage over its best build or licensing option to justify a price that clears the investor’s proceeds hurdle? Move to Reach out if a paid, buyer-validated production pilot across workers and tasks measures a repeatable advantage and the buyer’s own cost and time estimates support a price above the ownership-adjusted hurdle for assets with transferable rights.
