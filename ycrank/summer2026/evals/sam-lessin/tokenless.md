# Tokenless -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Tokenless has demonstrated that evaluation systems can reward the wrong thing. It has not demonstrated what my check buys. Post-money valuation, competing offers, and the capital-funded experiment are all undisclosed. I cannot infer an auction from YC membership, and I cannot infer cheap entry from silence. The reported 110 defects across DeepSWE, Terminal-Bench, and BFCL establish technical output; they do not establish a finite investment experiment with a deadline and a result that changes business value.

The strongest reason to take this team seriously is the bridge between alignment research and working infrastructure. Agarwal researches agent alignment; Wang previously built GPU infrastructure at SkyPilot. EnvCheck adds shipped evidence: agents inspect graders, probe tasks in separate sandboxes, and submit findings for objective checks or human review. That fits my pattern of backing people who can translate between systems. The remaining gap is direct operating access to the organizations buying this work. Research competence and infrastructure experience help produce an audit; they do not automatically produce an audit business.

The bull case is uncomfortable for a pass: Tokenless could become the independent party laboratories trust to discover when expensive training runs are optimizing garbage. Its 66 published write-ups give prospective customers something concrete to inspect, and the October 20 report of early partners exploring domain-specific reward hacking suggests movement toward private environments. But those partners are unnamed, their commercial status is unknown, and no repeat engagement is documented. The proprietary dark pool would be permissioned customer workflows and accumulated audit histories. Today, that asset is hypothetical. Public findings and an MIT-licensed grader establish credibility while also giving capable buyers tools to reproduce the work.

The economic question is whether each engagement leaves Tokenless with reusable advantage and cash. Current audit prices, delivery costs, margin, and burn are absent. Touchy’s $14.99 in-app purchase listing establishes neither sales nor economics for this product. Nor does the dossier establish the operating evidence that has justified exceptions to my usual gates: measured paid conversion and retention, an external acquisition offer, or a prior exit paired with an explicit capital structure. The acknowledged possibility of exploiting Tokenless’s own judge also matters. Human verification could support a valuable service, but its cost must appear in the margin rather than disappear into an automation promise.

I pass at 31. Tokenless has more technical substance than its commercial evidence can presently underwrite. Adjacent vendors such as Gray Swan already sell automated adversarial testing, making ownership of customer access consequential. There is no evidence of financing pressure tactics, equal-founder paralysis, or economics premised on errors vanishing; those red flags remain unestablished. My verifiable condition for reopening underwriting is a renewal of a paid audit on a proprietary customer environment with positive contribution margin after compute and human review. That would connect this team’s demonstrated ability to find defects with a business customers repeatedly fund. Price and the round’s specific purpose would still determine whether I write the check.
