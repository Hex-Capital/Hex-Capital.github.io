# Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Bilgehan Avser’s 35+ patents and the founders’ eight-year operating history make **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** a missionary hardware syndicate with unusually high pre-pitch conviction and unusually little owned distribution. Hardware is either illegible technical leverage or an expensive science project—usually both until proven otherwise. **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** has the credentials to survive Type 2 fun, but 148 company LinkedIn followers and no founder-built community mean the founders must manufacture every customer relationship alongside every device.

**Venmo** is the high rung on founder conviction because Andrew Kortina kept turning the crank through multiple forms before the product became legible. **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** carries the same load-bearing mechanism: Avser’s electromagnetics doctorate, Brian Huppi’s Nest sensing work, George McLean’s X-ray-imaging experience, and eight years of shared operating history are not résumé decoration. **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** falls below that rung because no founder has published the opinionated category argument I want to read, while the claimed one-millimeter accuracy and across-room performance remain self-published rather than independently demonstrated.

**Sublime Security’s** low-rung founder-brand mechanism is enterprise distribution without a person-owned audience; the product must earn every account through performance and sales. **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** shows the same pattern more starkly: Avser’s 1,120 LinkedIn followers, 148 company followers, and hundreds of Nashville Build Expo conversations are professional reach, not an owned community that compounds. **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** therefore receives almost no creator or founder-brand credit, although the modest footprint is not fatal for construction hardware—it simply means distribution belongs in the burn model rather than on the cap table.

No disclosed SAFE price, round size, named tier-one pass, or commercial preorder count means **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** gets my price-discipline default before receiving credit for occupying a genuinely illegible hard-tech category. The counterfactual is clean: a sub-batch-median cap plus evidence that mainstream software funds passed because radio imaging looked too niche would move **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** into the invest tier; a competitive YC round priced on the phrase “camera that sees through walls” would move it toward an automatic pass. Non-consensus technology does not make a non-consensus deal.

FCC wall-imaging coordination requirements create at least three 18-month tacks for **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.**: prove material-independent performance, secure the correct construction-use classification, and establish a repeatable manufacturing and service model across fragmented contractors. An upfront device plus recurring mapping software could become venture-shaped, but **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** publishes neither pricing nor commercial terms, and construction, cargo, security, industrial inspection, and defense are five procurement systems masquerading as one market. The structure compounds only if one beachhead finances the next; otherwise each vertical becomes another founder-drainage tack.

**Allbirds** is the single-anchor parallel: a physical product dismissed by software investors can be attractive precisely because capital-efficient execution makes the asset legible before consensus arrives. **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** has the same non-SaaS, unsexy-operator appeal, but **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** has not shown Allbirds-like margin evidence, brand pull, or repeat demand, and radio-imaging hardware is not one of my revealed crypto, DTC, prescription-rail, creator, or Wizard-of-Oz sleeves. The category fit is real enough to investigate, not strong enough to override structure and price.

The company-cited $20B rework problem leads to a falsifiable forecast: within 36 months, **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** will have shifted its primary go-to-market from general contractors to defense and industrial inspection, with $2M-$5M in annualized revenue from fewer than 20 customers, because **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** has deep technical conviction but lacks the owned distribution needed for a broad construction rollout.

The four founders’ Apple, Nest, Humane, antenna, sensing, and X-ray experience form the strongest opposing case: **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** could resemble the **Ro** exception, where regulation and capital intensity become the moat because only venture financing can clear the technical and licensing schleps. That bull case stings—250+ reported patents and a working radio-plus-algorithm stack could make the $199.95 Walabot comparison irrelevant—but **Applied Electrodynamics, Inc.** has not established patent assignment, independent one-millimeter validation, paid repeat use, or an FCC path available to ordinary construction operators. Three named general contractors each paying at least $100,000 for repeat deployments under a confirmed construction-use classification would flip my verdict to Invest.
