# HERA -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

**More than 200 customer-discovery conversations** make HERA a learning-oriented industrial SaaS company whose weakest fit is the capital profile: HERA can become useful without needing my money. Meera Patel and Noelle So understand a costly workflow, but catching a thousand-dollar drawing error before a $40,000 part is scrapped is still an optimization play. HERA improves the existing engineering-review regime; HERA does not yet name the economic mechanism that disappears. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself” does not mean every good company is a Khosla company.

**Slash** is the high rung on founder learning rate because its documented Kanye West pivot proved that new evidence could overturn the founders’ operating thesis. HERA’s 200-plus interviews and one-week deployment promise suggest rapid learning, but HERA discloses no pivot, abandoned hypothesis, or “we were wrong” moment. Customer discovery is activity; a thesis update is evidence. HERA sits below Slash because learning rate is demonstrated by changed behavior, not interview count.

**DoorDash** failed my required-capital test: capital-light logistics did not need a $115 million Commonwealth Fusion-style Series A, even though ambition and network effects ultimately justified the exception. HERA exhibits the same dimensional mismatch without DoorDash’s exceptional mechanism. HERA appears to be founder-led enterprise SaaS offering a free 20-drawing review, with no plant, fleet, foundation-model training program, or disclosed path requiring more than $50 million. I am over-resourced for HERA, and consensus generalists are perfectly equipped to finance it.

**A missed issue scrapping a $40,000 part** gives HERA a real mechanism: more reviewed drawings can produce more customer-specific rules, fewer escapes, greater trust, and wider deployment across aerospace or medical-device factories over 18 months. But HERA compounds savings inside the current manufacturing system; it does not collapse that system. Even at scale, HERA resembles better quality assurance rather than Impossible Foods replacing animal-protein production. HERA must evolve from “catch errors” to autonomous design-and-release before consequence rises above incremental productivity.

**Axial’s reported recall above only 60%** exposes the counterfactual that could make HERA technically interesting. HERA would move up a tier if source-traced findings delivered independently measured, near-human recall across ASME Y14.5, BPVC, B31, AWS, ISO, and plant-specific rules while running securely inside customer walls. That would be a falsifiable engineering miracle, not an API wrapper. HERA publishes no precision or recall benchmark today, while F4, NexCAD, Axial, and RapidDraft already attack overlapping checks; until the benchmark exists, HERA carries ordinary software execution risk rather than consensus-repelling technical risk.

**Meera Patel’s UW industrial-engineering training** places HERA closer to expert continuity than to the Physical Intelligence pattern. Chelsea Finn and Sergey Levine brought publicly legible research reputations and pursued a technical thesis many roboticists considered premature; Patel and Noelle So bring relevant factory exposure, data-science training, and Noelle’s 40 L/s water-treatment project, but no documented rejection of manufacturing orthodoxy. HERA may be right, but HERA is not yet contrarian. Experts extrapolate the past, and adding vision models to an established drawing-review process is currently extrapolation with better tools.

**NexCAD’s 1,000-plus engineers and 2,500-plus reviewed drawings** define HERA’s likely 36-month reality: by July 2029, HERA reaches roughly $1 million to $3 million ARR serving regulated engineer-to-order manufacturers, remains independent, and is still selling human-supervised drawing review rather than autonomous engineering release. HERA’s 200 interviews can support a durable niche, while confidentiality demands, standards maintenance, and four overlapping competitors constrain expansion. HERA can succeed modestly. I invest for probability-distribution shape, not for a respectable median outcome.

**HERA’s free, under-NDA review of 20 drawings** supports the strongest bull case: those pilots could become a proprietary plant-specific system of record whose accumulated corrections create switching costs, then expand from review into autonomous design release. DoorDash proves I sometimes underestimate capital-light systems whose workflow position becomes strategic. The objection stings because HERA’s founders have direct industrial exposure and can deploy within one week. But HERA has disclosed neither a corpus advantage nor an accuracy advantage, while NexCAD already claims substantially more usage and F4 offers deterministic on-device analysis. I would flip if HERA produces an independently verified benchmark above 95% precision and recall across customer drawings and converts it into at least $2 million ARR with multi-site expansion.
