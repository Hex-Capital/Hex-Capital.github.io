# Kairos Health -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**New Economies** is the strongest path. Kairos combines AI outreach with direct scheduling under each practice’s booking rules. The candidate new use is routine phone outreach to overdue patients whom busy practices would otherwise leave uncalled. The former delivery cost is staff time spent on callback attempts and booking. The memo gives no baseline for that cost or comparison of cost per completed recall visit after accounting for staff corrections and no-shows. Pecan Dental reports more than 1,200 calls handled and more than $21,000 in average monthly dental production. Those figures show live use and leave incremental completed visits unresolved. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Kairos’s documented schedule integrations and practice-specific rules give buyers a plausible reason to stay when voice models are widely available. The founder-reported 13 paying practices show early willingness to pay, with renewals and demand specifically for previously uneconomic outreach still unverified. The team has shipped into live practices and brings dental front-desk and voice-routing experience. Booking and verification accuracy remain open execution tests. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

Expansion is priced per location. At the reported $1,200 monthly list price, the company’s reported 85% gross margin would imply $180 in monthly cost of revenue per location. Assuming that cost also holds at the $600 lifetime launch price, monthly gross profit would be $420 per location. This is an illustrative scenario. The memo leaves actual location-level pricing, call costs, onboarding effort, and human exception costs unresolved. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Kairos may make an existing recall workflow cheaper while practices continue to buy on price or bundle convenience. Practices already use manual callbacks and automated texts. Arini sells overlapping recall and verification workflows, and Weave and Peerlogic bundle reception with other tools practices use. The memo shows no buyer preference under functional parity and no independently supported scarce complement. Competitive overlap leaves both New Economies conditions open. The memo shows neither observed switching at parity nor a failed market-opening test, so the path stays on Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will a paid cohort renew Kairos at a viable price after testing it on overdue patients the practices previously skipped and comparing it with a functionally comparable alternative? Move to Reach out if the cohort shows materially lower cost per incremental completed visit than staff-led recall, including corrections and no-shows, and buyers renew with Kairos after the comparison. Move to Pass if the test shows the outreach remains uneconomic or buyers readily switch at parity.
