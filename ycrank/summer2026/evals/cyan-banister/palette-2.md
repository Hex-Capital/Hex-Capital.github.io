# Palette -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Palette’s self-serve video generator is tactile enough to use today, yet Palette sits in the least magically weird neighborhood imaginable: a consensus AI-video market surrounded by Runway, Higgsfield, Luma AI, and Krea. That pairing is the signature. Palette has produced an observable artifact, but the founders have entered a crowded parade rather than emerged from an ignored corner of their own lives.

**Niantic** is the high rung for physical-world verifiability because I played Ingress for years before pursuing John Hanke; the product escaped the pitch and occupied my life. Palette shares the load-bearing mechanism: a normal person can submit a prompt, image, or document and receive a video, storyboard, or character sheet through self-serve access. Palette diverges where it matters most: no usage, repeat behavior, or user love is disclosed, and the listed domain redirected to a domain-for-sale page. I can touch Palette’s output, but I cannot yet feel Palette pulling people back.

**The consensus YC AI-agent darling** fails when an oversubscribed category substitutes social proof for a contrarian founder origin. Palette shows the same failure mechanism in video: Higgsfield reports a $200 million annual revenue run rate, Runway raised a $315 million Series E, Luma AI raised $900 million, and Krea has $83 million behind an overlapping suite. Palette may be underfunded at $500,000, but underfunded is not the same as underpriced. Nothing in Palette’s dossier shows sophisticated investors rejecting the idea because it is ugly, regulated, or magically weird; the market is already wearing sequins.

**Serena Pei’s diffusion, computer-vision, and multimodal research** gives Palette real Tiz, while Josephine Lee’s Broad Institute software work contributes useful Biz. The founder-origin mechanism fails to compound, though: neither biography shows Palette growing from years spent making films, running an agency, or suffering inside a production workflow. Over 18 months, Palette’s 130 regression tests can improve routing, but without lived creative judgment Palette risks optimizing whichever upstream model benchmark looks best that week. The June HR-agent page followed by July multimedia positioning makes that risk visible already: Palette appears to be visiting the problem, not of the place.

**Palette’s $0.01-per-credit pricing** buys access to third-party models rather than capital-intensive infrastructure, hardware, or a regulated moat. Palette could flip this dimension if Josephine Lee and Serena Pei were building proprietary video infrastructure whose cost curve compounded, or if Palette became the rights-and-provenance system enterprises needed to produce legally defensible media. The cited Copyright Office limitation is merely ambient regulation today; Palette has no disclosed compliance workflow, protected dataset, or ownership technology. At present, Palette is a software orchestration layer whose suppliers can change the rent.

**Josephine Lee’s CEO title** places Palette above the unresolved-authority pattern that damaged **HQ Trivia**, but the parallel still makes me nervous. HQ Trivia taught me that a delightful artifact can temporarily cover a crack between founders, not repair it. Palette names Lee as CEO and Pei as CTO, which is better than shared titles, yet Palette discloses no equity structure, founder-vesting arrangement, or tiebreaker protocol. A title is a suggestion until Palette can answer who decides when Lee wants enterprise workflows and Pei wants model infrastructure.

**Higgsfield’s $200 million run rate** points to the mechanism behind my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Palette will have abandoned the general-purpose AI-video studio, pivoted into enterprise training-content production, and remain below $2 million ARR. Palette’s highest signal—a usable, observable media artifact—can support a narrower workflow. Palette’s lowest signal—a consensus aggregation layer with no structural barrier—means the horizontal product will be squeezed as upstream models and funded suites absorb routing, editing, and character consistency.

**Palette’s internal benchmarks of 35% faster generation and 50% lower cost** form the strongest bull case, and the 130 regression tests suggest Serena Pei may be building a genuine routing engine rather than another glossy wrapper. Palette could become my **HQ Trivia** exception: tactile product love overwhelms weak founder biography and a crowded category. The evidence that wins today is absence of disclosed customers, revenue, usage, retention, or proprietary models alongside a website-identity discontinuity and four heavily funded competitors. I would flip from Pass to Invest if Palette verifies 10,000 weekly active creators with at least 40% eight-week retention while maintaining the reported 50% cost advantage across independently reproducible tests.
