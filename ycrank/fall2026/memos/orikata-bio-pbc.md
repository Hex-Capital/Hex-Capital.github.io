## The Idea

- **Problem:** Computational biology teams assemble datasets, manage local Python dependencies, and provision GPUs to run analyses across separate tools ([YC directory](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/orikata-bio-pbc)).
- **Approach:** Noto offers browser-based analysis, hosted model runs and an initial assistant, with a planned shared notebook that links inputs, code, methods and results across local and hosted compute ([Noto product page](https://megure.ai/noto/), [YC directory](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/orikata-bio-pbc)).
- **Differentiation:** Noto’s design centers on a shared record for human researchers and agents across compute environments, compared with Benchling’s experiment and sample records, LatchBio’s cloud analysis workflows, Seqera’s Nextflow orchestration, and Phylo’s biomedical research agent ([Noto](https://megure.ai/noto/), [Benchling](https://www.benchling.com/bioresearch), [LatchBio](https://latch.bio/solutions), [Seqera](https://seqera.io/platform/), [Phylo](https://phylo.bio/)).
- **Business Model:** Orikata offers free browser tools, a $20/month Individual plan with $5/week in compute credits, a $200/month Team plan with $50/week in shared credits, prepaid credits, and optional metered billing ([Orikata terms, Nov 6, 2026](https://orikata.ai/terms/)).
- **TAM/SAM:** No public TAM/SAM data found for this specific segment.
- **GTM / Distribution:** Orikata distributes through Launch YC, open-source releases, and self-serve browser tools that researchers can try without an account before signing in for hosted runs ([Launch YC, Nov 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/XSp-orikata-bio-megure-labs-learning-from-evolution-and-mastering-the-art-of-folding-proteins), [Noto](https://megure.ai/noto/)).

## Defensibility

- **Moat today:** Orikata reports curating more than 100 billion unique protein sequences in Umi and nearly 2 billion protein structure records in Sora, with customer access across the full collections still in development ([YC directory, Nov 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/orikata-bio-pbc)).
- **Future moat:** [Inferred]: Accumulated project histories could create switching costs if teams depend on the links among their inputs, methods and results, a hypothesis that remains untested publicly while the full multiplayer notebook is in development ([YC directory](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/orikata-bio-pbc)).
- **Market structure:** No structural barrier identified at this stage.
- **Commoditization risk:** Benchling already offers hosted model runs inside its research workspace, and Phylo already offers collaborative research agents, establishing overlap with features Noto markets today ([Benchling](https://www.benchling.com/bioresearch), [Phylo](https://phylo.bio/), [Noto](https://megure.ai/noto/)).

## Key Risks

- **Multiplayer release timing:** Launch YC stated mid-November for the full workspace, and Megure Labs’ LinkedIn page states late November, leaving the shared notebook’s release date uncertain as hosted runs are already available ([Launch YC, Nov 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/XSp-orikata-bio-megure-labs-learning-from-evolution-and-mastering-the-art-of-folding-proteins), [Megure Labs LinkedIn, Nov 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/company/megure-labs/)).
- **Incumbent substitution:** [Inferred]: Labs already using Benchling can run models alongside their existing experimental records, reducing the need to adopt another workspace for those jobs ([Benchling Bioresearch](https://www.benchling.com/bioresearch)).
- **Third-party data handling:** Hosted jobs send research inputs and intermediate files to external compute providers, which may affect adoption by labs with disclosure restrictions, and Orikata requires workspace authorization to retrieve signed-in job results ([Orikata privacy policy, Nov 6, 2026](https://orikata.ai/privacy/)).
