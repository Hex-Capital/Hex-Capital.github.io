# Hopper -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Hopper’s strongest path to cash for a seed investor. A voice infrastructure platform could buy Hopper’s custom inference kernels, cache-aware routing, and customer-specific tuning workflows to deploy them across its own customer base. Deepgram’s announced acquisition of Daily shows that a speech API vendor is willing to buy adjacent voice infrastructure. For Hopper, the ownership case depends on whether controlling the serving and tuning stack would deliver durable latency, cost, or quality gains beyond what the buyer could secure through a hosting contract. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Hopper reports one signed customer in production and more than 10 billion tokens per day across its inference platform. The volume is platform-wide, and the memo gives no breakdown of paid voice usage. The CTO also reports that p95 latency on one deployment rose from about 11 to 42 seconds under higher load. A repeatable advantage at peak traffic across multiple paying voice deployments would strengthen the acquisition case. The existing deployment gives Hopper a starting point, while the cost of spare GPU capacity and the runway to fund a broader test remain unknown. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

The proceeds test uses the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with ownership diluted from 5% to 3% and no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The memo’s usage prices provide no usable basis for estimating acquisition value. (Key Facts)

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Gross cash to holder at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale at $40 million enterprise value | Year 5 | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | Year 7 | $3.0 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged-private outcome | Through year 8 | $0, with shares still held | 0× |

The sale prices and timing are scenario assumptions. Both sales assume all-cash consideration, with enterprise value flowing to distributable equity at face value after zero net debt and other closing adjustments, then allocated pro rata with no preference deductions. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation each contribute $0 in these illustrations. At 2% ownership, even the strong sale returns only $2 million, or 4×. In the prolonged-private case, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. The memo provides no separately supported route to cash for this holder if a sale fails. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer may be able to get the same result by partnering, self-hosting open-weight models, or using a managed stack like Baseten’s. Hopper’s tuning also depends on written customer consent and agreed retention terms. The memo leaves open whether a buyer could retain access to the calls and reuse customer-specific artifacts after a sale. Together, those risks make the ownership premium and transferability too uncertain for outreach today. The available substitutes are credible, but the memo does not establish that they match Hopper’s customer-specific results or impose a binding price ceiling. The structural Pass override does not apply. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Hopper demonstrate an advantage in production that a voice platform would pay to own? Move to **Reach out** when multiple paying customers show repeatable latency or serving-cost gains at comparable voice quality under peak load against a credible managed or self-hosted alternative, and the economics of deploying that transferable capability across a buyer’s customer base support a price near the proceeds hurdle. Until then, **Watch**.
