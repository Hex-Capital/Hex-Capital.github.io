# Last Accounting Company -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Riki Karjalainen and Sami Laakkonen built petabyte-scale financial-data infrastructure at Token Terminal, yet Last Accounting Company discloses no revenue or repeat-usage curve. That high-shipping, low-velocity pairing defines Last Accounting Company: technically credible builders moving unusually fast into agent-native accounting, without evidence yet that customers are pulling the product from them. The proprietary ledger and Björn agent make Last Accounting Company more substantive than an “OpenAI for bookkeeping” wrapper, but “built quickly” and “just working” are different signals.

**Perplexity** is the high rung on founder iteration because Aravind Srinivas repeatedly turned conversations into shipped artifacts within a week. Last Accounting Company shares the important mechanism: four founders moved from a rough idea to onboarding initial customers in roughly ten weeks, while Riki Karjalainen and Sami Laakkonen had already shipped petabyte-scale financial systems. Last Accounting Company diverges because the dossier names no successive customer-driven iterations, independent products, public repositories, or repeat-usage loops. The shipping evidence is real and well above credential-only founding teams, but it has not reached the Perplexity pattern of observable iteration between conversations.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism on velocity: demand can exist while the curve still fails to prove that the economics are inverting in your favor. Last Accounting Company is onboarding first customers, but Last Accounting Company reports no price, revenue, retention, monthly growth, or named customer. The 430 LinkedIn followers and Helsingin Sanomat mention are false signals for this criterion; neither indicates buying behavior. Pre-seed status explains the short history, but accounting is on my *AI Market Clarity* open-market list, and in an open AI market the winners usually disclose a screaming curve quickly. No curve is visible, so I apply the absent-evidence default.

Last Accounting Company attributes its window to recent computer-use agents and MCP interfaces that can act across finance systems. Over the next 18 months, that catalyst could compound because every improvement in tool use expands the share of reconciliation, payroll, VAT, and filing work that Last Accounting Company can automate. The weakness is that the catalyst is inferred rather than demonstrated through an accuracy step-function, regulatory change, or incumbent collapse tied directly to adoption. Bench’s December 2024 closure provides some incumbent-failure evidence, but Pilot, Truewind, and Balance already recognized the same opening. Last Accounting Company is skating for the puck, although not alone and not yet on a uniquely opened patch of ice.

Last Accounting Company’s proprietary general ledger could become an index on agent-mediated finance if every customer, external agent, bank, payroll system, and accountant must touch it. Today Last Accounting Company instead looks like a managed accounting service that must individually win and migrate each Nordic SMB away from an existing ledger, accountant, and workflow. The score flips into the top tier if the MCP server becomes a neutral transaction and audit rail used by outside accounting agents—even when Last Accounting Company does not provide the bookkeeping. Without that counterfactual, the proprietary ledger increases switching costs after acquisition but does not create Stripe-like distribution before acquisition.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow and data infrastructure captured value on top of commodity sequencing without requiring new chemistry. Last Accounting Company similarly builds workflow on top of increasingly capable models, with certified accountants approving Björn’s output. Last Accounting Company nevertheless owns more dependency risk than Color’s template: reliable agent bookkeeping, migration into a proprietary ledger, jurisdiction-specific VAT and filing compliance, and economical human review must all work together. No new science is required, so this is not a classic science project; however, four co-founders with no named final decision-maker and no enumerated jurisdiction coverage add operational failure surfaces. The Color exception partially applies, but it does not erase the multi-system execution burden.

Ten weeks from idea to first-customer onboarding predicts that Last Accounting Company will ship through the product risk faster than it proves distribution. By July 2029, I expect Last Accounting Company to reach $3 million–$8 million of ARR, concentrated among Nordic technology companies, while remaining a service-heavy accounting firm rather than becoming the default ledger for third-party agents. The mechanism is straightforward: strong financial-data builders can automate enough work to support a real managed service, but absent revenue velocity and an index-like channel, each customer still arrives through founder-led sales and a costly migration.

The $688.2 billion accounting-services market supplies the strongest opposing case: Bench’s collapse reopened customer demand, computer-use agents changed automation economics, and Last Accounting Company already combined a proprietary ledger, daily error detection, accountant review, and an MCP interface in ten weeks. That could be a **Decagon**-style workflow-infrastructure opening, with Last Accounting Company exceeding $20 million ARR before incumbents unwind legacy systems. The case stings because accounting is explicitly one of the open AI markets I would fund. The dossier still gives the deciding evidence to the bear case: no revenue, no retention, no accuracy rate, no integration list, no supported-jurisdiction list, and direct overlap with Balance, Pilot, and Truewind. Six consecutive months above 20% net ARR growth, paired with named customers retaining after two closes and independently verified low correction rates, would flip my verdict to Invest.
