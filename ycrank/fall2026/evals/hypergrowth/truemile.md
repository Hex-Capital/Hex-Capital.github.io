# TrueMile -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** TrueMile’s initial buyer is a shipper with recurring truckload freight. Independent owner-operators supply capacity by leasing onto TrueMile. The memo documents a direct inquiry and booked-call process for shippers in Texas and the Sunbelt, operator recruitment through the website and launch, and a fleet solver in use in TrueMile’s own carrier operation. If shippers award additional lanes, the solver could pool those commitments across trucks and pair outbound loads with backhauls. That could reduce empty miles and let each dispatcher handle more trucks. The direct sales process and lane expansion are still unproven. (The Idea, Defensibility)

TrueMile reports $57K in freight booked over three weeks, an early signal of commercial demand. The memo gives no completed-load revenue, repeat shipper tenders, or operator retention. The two dedicated-lane partnerships were solicited. TrueMile also reports 9× dispatcher capacity, but the memo gives no measured labor cost per completed load. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Second-market scenario.** Assume the solver and dispatch workflow carry over to a market outside the U.S. TrueMile would still need to win local freight, recruit eligible operators, find viable return loads, and establish local carrier compliance. The contribution test starts with freight revenue from completed loads, then deducts operator payouts, any empty-mile costs TrueMile bears, dispatch and approval labor, agent costs, and incremental sales, onboarding, insurance, and compliance costs. Any gap between paying operators and collecting from shippers would also increase working-capital needs. The memo leaves both the portability of the carrier operation and its incremental contribution unresolved. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

TrueMile could grow freight bookings while taking on proportionally more carrier work and exposure. Every load requires human approval and a signed rate confirmation. Cancellations can break linked trips and add manual work, thin backhaul supply can leave trucks empty, and TrueMile assumes safety and compliance responsibilities for leased equipment. The reported dispatcher capacity suggests a possible labor advantage. The Pass gate remains open because the memo contains no rollout data showing how total costs move with volume. (Key Risks, Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Can TrueMile use its existing sales and dispatch process to win an independent shipper outside the U.S. and serve repeat paid loads at positive incremental contribution? One buyer-funded pilot with repeat tender and payment records, logged sales and dispatch effort, and a lane-level cost breakdown would answer it. A successful pilot would move the action to Reach out. A pilot that shows structurally proportional or worse costs with no supported improvement would move it to Pass.
