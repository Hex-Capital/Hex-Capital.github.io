# Edgerun -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence is real but bounded. Infantry carrying 90–140 pounds against a 48-pound doctrinal limit is a physical failure, and load carriage reportedly causes twice as many evacuations as enemy contact. But Edgerun has not shown an orders-of-magnitude consequence: the disclosed military-exoskeleton market is $1.16 billion, and the claimed 30–45% assistance improves one part of loaded walking rather than eliminating the underlying burden. This could materially improve soldier endurance. The dossier does not yet establish a 100x option.

The technical case is more interesting. One-pound actuators exceeding 30 Nm, 21700 cells approaching 450 Wh/kg, a 10-pound system, 12-plus-hour runtime, and sub-five-second removal form a plausible engineering discontinuity from older exoskeletons. More than 100 Ukrainian field tests since May 2026 are substantially better evidence than laboratory demonstrations. But 30–45% assistance is internal data, third-party verification is unfinished, and no result shows a 5–10x improvement in endurance, metabolic expenditure, injury incidence, or mission effectiveness against conventional load-bearing equipment.

The strongest bull case is that Edgerun is doing what physical-technology startups should do: putting imperfect hardware on real operators, integrating with Balistyka, and building around components that only recently became practical. David Venegas has relevant experience across Stoke Space, Hadrian, and Durin, while H.R. McMaster provides credible defense access. This fits the unstated pattern of accepting manufacturing and procurement complexity when a small team can repeatedly learn in a scarce technical workflow.

But the risk-removal graph is not yet visible. One hundred tests sound impressive, yet the dossier provides no prespecified performance threshold, failure rate, experiment cost, or decision branch. The largest kill risk is not whether soldiers will try the device; it is whether assistance survives mud, impacts, battery degradation, electronic warfare constraints, maintenance, and long-duration use while producing a measurable physiological or casualty benefit. Capital spent before answering that question risks becoming procurement theater.

Economics are the terminal problem. There is no price, bill of materials, manufacturing yield, service burden, replacement cycle, or comparison with the cost of conventional equipment and load-related evacuations. German Bionic’s administration after raising $60.42 million and Hypershell’s reported $1.3 million of 2024 revenue despite substantial financing show that building powered wearables is not the same as finding durable economics. Edgerun’s Taiwan manufacturing, Kyiv operations, and Washington procurement path add cost and execution dependencies rather than resolving them.

I pass today. None of the known exceptions applies: there is no documented all-in scientific mission against a trillion-dollar resource pool, no exponential technical result, and no platform-scale value pool that overrides the missing benchmark and economics. My one flip condition is a preregistered independent loaded-march trial demonstrating at least a 2x improvement in mission endurance, with no increase in injury, failure, or maintenance burden. That would convert field activity into a technical result worth underwriting.
