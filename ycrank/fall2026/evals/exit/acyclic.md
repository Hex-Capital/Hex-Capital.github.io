# Acyclic Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. An agent platform or post-training provider could pay for Acyclic’s coordination training environments if owning them let it improve delegation across production workloads faster or more cheaply than building or licensing the capability. The best observed operating evidence is a 19-agent research demo that ran in 12.5 minutes for $2.52. It establishes a measured run, with no comparison against a single agent or another orchestration stack. Acyclic reports document-processing use and capacity for 10,000 agents in one session. A sustained training advantage from accumulated coordination traces remains an inference. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

The milestone is a repeatable production result showing lower cost per successful task at matched quality against the buyer’s available substitutes, with the gain attributable to training assets Acyclic can transfer. The recorded $500,000 accelerator financing and early onboarding support attempting that test. The memo gives no burn, runway, or test budget. The voluntary strike-off notice for the UK entity adds a transaction risk despite the startup’s active listing. The memo leaves open which entity owns the relevant assets and which entity would issue the investor’s shares. No independent fallback has evidence of a path to cash for this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The financing record does not supply terms for the evaluated check, so the scenarios use the thesis’s $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The 5× objective is $2.5 million in cash within eight years. The sale scenarios assume no net debt, transaction fees, or senior claims, and a simple pro rata waterfall before investor taxes. Enterprise value therefore equals distributable equity consideration, paid entirely in cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero.

| Scenario | Timing and assumed outcome | Cash to holder | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5, $40M enterprise value and distributable equity | $1.2M | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7, $100M enterprise value and distributable equity | $3.0M | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. The investor retains the shares. | $0 | 0.0× |

The hurdle is $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, and $62.5 million at 4%. The strong scenario clears the hurdle at 3% and falls short at 2%. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction pricing to support either modeled sale value. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A platform can self-host Acyclic’s Apache-2.0 harness, and Claude Managed Agents already offers parallel delegation. Acyclic also reports that coordination failures can make swarms underperform a single agent. A buyer could therefore get enough value from available substitutes while avoiding the cost of an acquisition. That weakens the ownership rationale today. The memo leaves open whether transferable training assets could produce a material advantage over those substitutes, so the evidence supports Watch. The price ceiling, a binding transfer barrier, and the likelihood of the prolonged-private scenario remain unresolved. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a prospective platform buyer verify that owning Acyclic’s transferable coordination training assets is worth more than building, licensing, or self-hosting by enough to support the modeled $83.3 million equity hurdle? A repeatable production comparison at matched quality, documented ownership of the assets driving the gain, and buyer-side economics that support the hurdle would move the action to Reach out.
