# Reconero -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what happened when an officer first tried Reconero and something broke. What did Riwan and Edward do next? How long did it take? The dossier reports police-unit trials and iterative feedback, but gives no specific obstacle, shipped workaround, or elapsed time. That leaves my most important founder test unanswered. Riwan’s reported **$150K ARR from a prior sentiment-analysis business** is encouraging evidence that he has built something people bought. It does not establish how these founders handle resistance in police software ([Le Figaro, September 19, 2026](https://etudiant.lefigaro.fr/article/etudes/comment-riwan-jeune-francais-de-18-ans-a-ete-accepte-a-y-combinator-le-meilleur-incubateur-de-start-up-au-monde-20260919/)).

There is something here beyond an application and credentials. Edward co-developed ReconeroRMS for the Gemini Live Agent Challenge, and the reported trials suggest software has reached prospective users. Alyx has concrete jobs: search departmental evidence, extract information from body-camera footage, and prefill reports for review ([Reconero Intelligence](https://reconero.com/intelligence/)). This is Reconero’s strongest evidence. But a working prototype and a measured technical result are different things. There is no published measurement of report accuracy, correction burden, or time saved. My pattern of backing technical teams before their markets look obvious depends on inspectable proof; neither an independently measured result nor developers building on a programmable interface is established here.

Police officers doing paperwork are a plausible narrow well. The claim that documentation consumes over **40% of their day** makes the job worth investigating, but says nothing about whether they want this particular solution ([YC profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/reconero)). Trials and officer feedback deserve some demand credit. They do not establish repeated voluntary use, referrals, or payment. Reconero’s own revenue and contract terms remain unknown; the prior business’s revenue belongs to another product. I would want to know whether officers ask for Alyx again after discovering how much editing its reports require.

The strongest argument against passing is that these founders have already crossed several boundaries that could have stopped them. They met through their high school’s Hack Club, built police software, and reportedly got police units to try it. Their relationship predates the accelerator, so the hastily assembled cofounder red flag does not apply. Riwan’s age and Edward’s departure from Stanford earn neither a penalty nor a bonus. My known exceptions for young or unfamiliar teams depend on a compelling working product, unmistakable pull, or validated technical proof. Reconero has enough evidence to make that possibility credible, but not enough to establish an override of the missing resourcefulness evidence.

The ugly work could become the opportunity. Reconero offers migration from legacy records, dispatch exports, spreadsheets, and scanned documents. But offering to do that work is not evidence of having mastered it, and the dossier does not connect the idea to a problem either founder personally encountered. Nor does “iterative feedback” identify a rejected hypothesis and a changed product route. Meanwhile, Mark43 already combines records and dispatch, and Axon offers report generation. Reconero needs a small job it performs so well that an officer pulls it into the department; attempting to replace the whole stack could bury that discovery under implementation work.

I would pass today. The decisive gap is evidence of founder action under resistance, rather than the absence of a long operating history. One verifiable flip condition would be a named department’s deployment record showing a blocker, a founder-built fix shipped within a week, and officers repeatedly using the corrected workflow afterward. That single sequence would supply distinct evidence about both resourcefulness and demand. It would make Reconero a substantially different bet.
