# ChampionEffect Inc. -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by an enterprise digital-adoption vendor such as SAP WalkMe. The memo documents SAP’s ownership of WalkMe and WalkMe’s existing in-app guidance. ChampionEffect says its Slack and Teams coach learns from live employee walkthroughs and reuses that knowledge within the same customer. A buyer could pay to own the software if that capability reliably turns one employee’s walkthrough into accurate, permissioned guidance for coworkers and gives the buyer a measurable advantage over building or licensing it. The memo provides no measured reuse, retention, accuracy, or buyer interest. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The seed milestone is a paid, administrator-approved rollout that measures repeated cross-team reuse and employee time saved against existing guidance. The founders’ engineering and go-to-market experience at Cambly supports running that test. The memo records launch activity and investor backing, but reports conflicting YC financing figures of $125,000 and $500,000. Available runway and the cost of getting through the pilot remain open. A sale would also depend on software ownership and customer agreements permitting continued use of the workflow knowledge after closing. The memo supplies no evidence on those rights. It also supplies no usable evidence of an independent route to shareholder cash. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The illustrative seed case assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. Both sale scenarios assume zero net debt, no other closing adjustments, and simple pro rata allocation with no preference deductions, so enterprise value equals distributable equity and all consideration is cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. The prices and timing are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no purchase-price evidence or applicable waterfall terms. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value and distributable equity | Holder cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $30 million | $900,000 | 1.8× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100 million | $3 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No sale | $0 | 0.0× |

The strong scenario clears the hurdle at 3% and falls short at 2%, where it returns $2 million, or 4.0×. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and the company reinvests available cash. The investor still holds the shares and realizes $0 within the horizon.

### Countercase

WalkMe already embeds step-by-step guidance, and Section already sells role-specific AI coaching. A buyer may be able to add employer-specific workflows to an existing product or license ChampionEffect’s capability, limiting the premium for ownership. Slack and Teams approval requirements can delay the rollout needed to demonstrate an advantage, while restricted or stale workflows could erode the value of reuse. The memo establishes partial substitutes and deployment risks. It does not establish that a substitute delivers the same result or caps a credible sale below the proceeds hurdle. The structural Pass override therefore does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid enterprise rollout show an advantage from owning ChampionEffect’s workflow-coaching capability large enough to support a sale that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? Repeated, accurate reuse across teams and measured time savings would answer the operating part. A prospective buyer’s comparison with building or licensing would need to put a plausible purchase price within range of the hurdle. That combination would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that an ordinary license delivers equivalent results and caps the purchase price below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
