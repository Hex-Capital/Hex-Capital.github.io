# Shielded -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. A manufacturer’s financial system continuously understands every supplier, ingredient, tariff, and commodity exposure, then changes purchasing or hedging before margins move. Shielded has a small artifact from that world: it converted restaurant ERP data into a live product covering 200 SKUs within 48 hours and completed defense, CPG, and manufacturing deployments in under 72 hours. But the dossier does not map the intervening transitions—better supplier provenance, trusted recommendation validation, workflow authority, and continuous outcome feedback. July 2026 tariffs explain urgency today; they do not explain what Shielded uniquely owns six years from now.

James Simon has real proximity to the problem. Computer science and economics at MIT, methane-infrastructure research, and commodities work at Balyasny provide technical and market fluency for tracing how external events reach company margins. What is missing is the first-believer artifact: a dated pre-company build, an operating wound he personally endured, complementary recruiting, or evidence that this obsession existed before supply-chain risk became a timely sales category. The biography supports founder-market fit, but not yet the deeper founder prior I use when the product has no demonstrated moat.

The current product proof is better than a concept or waitlist. Defense-company parts, multinational CPG ingredients, a global manufacturer’s tariffs, and a restaurant’s 200 SKUs are meaningfully different data environments. Completing each deployment in under 72 hours suggests Shielded can cross the ugly boundary between ERP records and outside events. The strongest bull case is that this rapid translation becomes the company’s concealed asset: every deployment teaches a reusable mapping between suppliers, products, prices, and realized margin effects. Yet all usage is company-reported, with no named customer, repeat cycle, payment, retained workflow, or measured recommendation outcome. Fast deployment proves a working artifact; it does not yet prove costly user commitment.

I keep asking why the analysis takes weeks. The dossier’s answer is fragmentation across ERP exports, spreadsheets, market data, news, bills of materials, and organizational silos. Shielded returns decision time to finance and procurement teams by calculating company-specific exposure instead of giving them another generic risk alert. That is a genuine leak in the system, but the rent remains blurry: there is no quantified analyst cost, missed-margin loss, institutional restriction, or gatekeeper whose power is being transferred. This may be excellent workflow compression without being a broken rule.

The harder issue sits beneath the product. Interos already has an 11-billion-relationship knowledge graph, while Altana, Prewave, and Everstream overlap across mapping, tariffs, monitoring, and prediction. Shielded currently controls no scarce dataset, distribution channel, technical bottleneck, or validated learning loop. Its inferred future moat depends on customer-specific mappings, but customer data dependency can just as easily make every deployment bespoke and prevent compounding. Recommendation quality is also untested, which matters when the product moves from explaining exposure to prescribing hedges.

I pass today, narrowly. The verifiable flip condition is one named customer using Shielded through three consecutive procurement or hedging cycles and documenting a realized margin benefit from its recommendation. That single artifact would establish repeated behavior, validate the economic consequence, and show whether the ERP-to-outcome mapping compounds into infrastructure rather than remaining a rapid integration service.
