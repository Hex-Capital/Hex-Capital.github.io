# Viraj Aero -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong initial product-market fit, GENA could serve the same recurring power and cooling needs at data centers in multiple markets. Its modular 10 MW design and integrated exhaust-water recovery give Viraj a plausible way to reuse the core equipment. Gas supply and air approvals constrain eligible sites, and the extent of adaptation across jurisdictions remains uncertain. The power, efficiency, and emissions figures are company specifications. Continuous-duty performance and cooling-water yield remain unverified at a data center site. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The October 29 launch and production-slot reservations give buyers a common entry point. Founder-led selling and likely site-specific engineering and commissioning leave little evidence of a repeatable path from reservation to first power. A commissioned unit could become embedded in daily operations and create switching costs. That supports continued use, while recurring billing and same-customer expansion remain uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Viraj reports more than 1.3 GW of interest under letters of intent and says the first GENA unit was under construction in November. The reported paying engine manufacturer has undisclosed scope and payment, so its relevance to GENA is unclear. The memo therefore leaves realized revenue and its growth rate unresolved for the target product. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under an equipment-sale scenario, incremental contribution would start with the unit sale price and subtract manufacturing, delivery, attributable sales and commissioning labor, and any required capacity expansion. Reusing the module could reduce engineering effort on subsequent projects, but the memo supplies no price or deployment cost with which to test that inference. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

GENA could become a series of bespoke power projects. If individual sites require substantial redesign and field work, and each increase in volume requires comparable additions to production capacity, strong buyer demand could still yield slow, costly growth. Site gas and air approvals are concrete constraints, and the first unit was still under construction. The structural downside gate remains open because the memo leaves the extent of redesign and the economics of repeat deployment unresolved. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Viraj commission a second paid GENA installation in a different permitting jurisdiction using the same core module and a repeatable installation plan at positive incremental contribution? A customer-accepted continuous-duty run and a deployment cost breakdown covering site engineering, commissioning, and added capacity would answer it. Success would warrant rescoring, with Reach out contingent on meeting the score and confidence thresholds. Evidence that unavoidable bespoke work makes repeat deployments uneconomic would change the action to Pass.
