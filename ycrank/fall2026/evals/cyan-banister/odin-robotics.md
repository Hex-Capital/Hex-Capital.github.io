# Odin Robotics -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I want a small factory owner to change the parts going into a machine without commissioning another automation project. Odin Robotics is working on a useful piece of that world: programmed routines for exact operations, learned handling for variation, and installation into existing factories. But the distance between an adaptable arm and an adaptable deployment business matters enormously. The dossier does not establish that solving one installation makes the next materially easier. I can imagine the future; I cannot give Odin credit for having demonstrated that path.

Why is someone still loading the machine by hand? The founders identify changing products and inconsistent part placement as obstacles to fixed-program automation ([YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/WJg-odin-robotics-robot-arms-that-do-repetitive-manual-work-in-existing-factories)). That is a concrete systems problem. Removing it could give smaller operators control over production that currently requires specialized integration work. Yet no installation cost, changeover delay, or customer saving is quantified, and this is a technical constraint rather than a documented artificial gatekeeper. Satisfaction-contingent pilot payment and monthly rental sensibly shift some adoption risk onto Odin. They are commercial terms, not evidence that customers have accepted the bargain.

The two founders have relevant operating material. Samriddhi Singh brings electrical engineering and humanoid and industrial robot experience; Alper Sametoglu brings propulsion, autonomy, and hardware engineering. Founding GirlsCanCode also shows Singh has initiated something beyond a classroom assignment. Those details deserve more weight than the ETH label. What I cannot establish is a dated sequence of independently pursued experiments, recruited believers, and lessons from factory customers. The dossier identifies a CEO and CTO; two founders alone do not establish the equal-control problem I worry about. Their biographies support technical credibility, with less evidence of the sustained obsession and commercial command that would make me follow them into a difficult manufacturing journey.

The strongest objection to passing is that Odin already has CNC-tending and conveyor-packing demonstrations. Physical work is happening. A quiet company doing unpleasant integration work can become the enabling layer beneath a much louder robotics market. My pre-revenue **CosmicBrain AI** exception shares that manufacturing thesis and functioning-prototype mechanism; the material difference was its signed industrial proofs of concept and unusually deep, documented founder history. Odin’s dossier supplies neither equivalent customer commitment nor that depth of biography. Missing revenue would not stop me. The missing substitute for revenue matters.

The public demonstrations establish an artifact, but no disclosed customer has repeatedly committed production time, money, or labor to it. NVIDIA Inception membership does not fill that gap. Reusable skills and machine integrations are the potentially valuable asset, yet their transferability remains company-reported, without measured deployment improvements. Meanwhile, RobCo already combines industrial hardware with learned task execution. Odin must show why its accumulated work becomes an advantage rather than a growing support obligation. There is no established dependence on one closed model supplier or documented severe privacy problem here; the concern is whether Odin controls a scarce capability at all.

I would pass with my own money today. This is close to a future I want, but the evidence currently supports a technically credible installation offering more strongly than a compounding infrastructure company. My verifiable flip condition is a three-factory deployment record showing the same learned skill reused across different customer setups, with engineering hours falling by at least half from the first installation to the third while meeting customer-agreed production targets. That would connect the working arm to the larger business I am trying to see.
