# Agent Relay -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

An agent has an address: `name@machine`. That little detail makes me stop. Move the clock to 2032 and I can imagine software teams whose agents ask one another questions while their humans sleep. Agent Relay has built a piece of that world. But delivering a message is the beginning. Agents must also know whose instructions to trust, which context they may share, and when to wake somebody up. The dossier establishes present-day communication; it does not yet establish the transitions from communication to dependable delegation. I give the working artifact credit without pretending the whole future has arrived.

The most persuasive evidence is somebody rearranging work around it. Preslav Mihaylov reports months of communication between his agents and teammates’ agents. That is much more interesting to me than approximately 1,000 GitHub stars. Allowing software to exchange working context is a meaningful commitment, even without a payment. The testimonial comes through a mirrored post, so I would keep its evidentiary limits attached. Likewise, the reported 229.6K package downloads have no specified measurement period; they cannot tell me how many teams depend on Agent Relay.

Washburn and Gant have paid some operating tuition. Washburn reports fourteen years building Tailwind to eight-figure ARR and more than 50,000 paying customers; Gant brings founding-engineer experience at Nango and engineering leadership at Overview. Their relationship began in high school and includes building software together. This is stronger than two impressive résumés meeting an attractive category. Their reported use of more than fifty parallel agents also supplies a credible source of irritation. What remains missing is a dated record of pursuing this particular coordination problem before the company. I see commercial and technical competence, with less evidence of the independently derived obsession that makes me follow a founder into an uncertain future.

Why must a person carry a question from one terminal to another? Because the tools have made the human their connecting tissue. Removing that dependency returns time and lets work continue across schedules. That is a concrete workflow repair, and it fits my tendency to find consequential businesses hiding inside mundane operating wounds. But the dossier identifies no quantified delay, artificial restriction, or intermediary collecting a rent. Agent Relay saves coordination labor; evidence that it transfers durable economic control to the user is thinner.

The strongest case for writing a check is that this becomes common plumbing across competing agent systems. Connecting existing tools, offering an Apache-2.0 core, and supporting local operation fit my preference for the enabling layer beneath a visible wave. There is no demonstrated dependence on one closed model supplier. A working prototype and credible builders can absolutely outweigh missing revenue at this stage. The harder objection is that neither an open repository nor searchable session history establishes control of a scarce dependency. Agent providers and orchestration vendors can add overlapping communication. Agent Relay must earn a position teams would struggle to replace.

My money also follows the transcripts. Cloud run data is retained indefinitely by default, deletion requires manual handling, and complete Cloud self-host packaging is unavailable. Scoped credentials, revocation, and locally operated components are meaningful mitigations, but they do not resolve the custody question for the hosted experience. I would remain **Neutral**: useful behavior is visible; durable infrastructure and acceptable data boundaries are less established. My one verifiable flip condition is a referenceable production deployment where an independent team repeatedly coordinates agents from competing providers through Agent Relay under enforced access, retention, and deletion controls. That would show both why this layer survives and why users can safely entrust it with their work.
