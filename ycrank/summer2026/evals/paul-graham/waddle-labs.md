# Waddle Labs -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The useful fact is that Waddle Labs has spent six months making robots work, not merely describing how agents might control them. End-to-end models require fresh data and fine-tuning when the robot, workspace, or task changes. Song and Ye built a system that instead writes editable control code, watches execution, and replans after failure. Approximately 1,000 overnight LEGO pick-and-place repetitions show that the loop runs on hardware. This is a shipped workaround to a real technical blocker, though the dossier does not reveal what first failed, how quickly the founders responded, or which measurable result changed after the workaround. That missing chronology keeps the resourcefulness evidence below the operational gate.

The strongest evidence is the artifact itself. The preview API, shared skill library, execution traces, and real-hardware repetitions are unusually concrete for a two-person pre-seed company. The founders also have relevant technical depth: Song researched program induction and multimodal models, while Ye co-authored robot-policy work. This resembles the kind of programmable substrate technical users can turn into things the founders did not anticipate. But Waddle’s own article says robotics results are hard to compare, and there is no standardized benchmark or independent deployment. One thousand repetitions of one internal task proves that something works; it does not yet prove that the agent generalizes.

Who wants the crude version now? The dossier cannot name anyone outside Waddle Labs. The 971 X followers, early-access registration, and YC launch are attention, not pull. There is no repeated external use, payment, deployment count, or user who forced an unplanned workflow. This matters more than the $1.671 billion market estimate. A robot-control agent can produce an impressive demonstration while failing as a product because every new factory contains different hardware, safety constraints, and integration work. The schlep may be the opportunity, but Waddle has not shown that anyone will pay it to perform the schlep.

There is also an unexplained route change. Song’s CV describes a Summer 2025 teleoperated data-collection company with Oscar Melendez as a co-founder; the current company has two founders and sells agent-generated robot control. That could be excellent running-back behavior: the team may have discovered through operating robots that data collection was the wrong abstraction. Or it could be discontinuity without learning. The dossier supplies neither the falsifying observation nor the reason for the co-founder change, so I cannot award the pivot itself much credit. Song and Ye meeting at RSI and later rooming together is reassuring, but it does not answer what happened to the earlier product and team.

The strongest bull case is the known exception for very young technical founders who arrive with numerical proof and direct exposure instead of enterprise credentials. Waddle shares the youth and the numerical artifact with Bindwell. The material difference is that Bindwell had laboratory validation and lived exposure to the underlying problem; Waddle’s evidence is internally reported, its benchmark is non-standardized, and its users are still the founders. Foundation-model dependency makes this sharper: if better third-party models supply most of the performance, public code-as-policy research and competitors with billions of dollars can compress Waddle’s advantage before its trace library becomes proprietary.

I would not write the check yet. My 36-month base case is that Waddle pivots back toward robot data collection, evaluation, or simulation tooling and remains below $1 million ARR, because the technical loop is ahead of the evidence for external control deployments. The red flag is not founder age; it is asking the technical demonstration to substitute for founder-led recruitment and user behavior. One verifiable condition would flip me to Invest: two named external robotics teams each using Waddle weekly across at least three tasks for eight consecutive weeks, with at least one paying and execution logs showing successful recovery from real failures.
