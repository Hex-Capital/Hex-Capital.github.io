# Preload -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B, repeatable sales with account expansion,** is Preload’s strongest path. The initial buyer is a robotics team training dexterous manipulation policies for tasks such as gripping and knot tying. Preload’s founder solicits these teams directly and offers data collection and training integration. The memo documents a synchronized video, tactile, and EMG capture rig and a ProHand control demonstration. Preload separately reports preliminary separation of light and heavy handling effort using EMG. Buyer commitment and improvement in robot task performance from the added EMG channel remain unverified. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Facts)

**Scenario assumption.** If EMG improves task success enough for a robotics team to pay, Preload could win similar teams through direct sales, use a common data format and integration workflow to get the first task live, and sell additional task datasets as teams train more policies. Serving a buyer abroad with the same package would require reproducing sensor fit, calibration, and recording quality wherever new data is collected. The memo identifies a plausible buyer and sales route. It leaves cross-market demand, paid activation, repeat orders, and marginal delivery costs unverified. (The Idea; Key Risks; Key Facts)

### Countercase

EMG could distinguish handling effort while adding little to physical-robot task success over video and tactile data. Even if buyers value the signal, each new task could require fresh operator time, glove fitting, calibration, and quality checks. Each new account could also need hands-on training integration, and surgical collection could add site approvals and scheduling. Those demands could make delivery effort rise with sales. The memo’s technical and staffing evidence identifies this risk, but it does not establish proportional delivery costs or a structural ceiling on demand. The Pass gate therefore remains untriggered. (Defensibility; The Idea; Key Risks)
