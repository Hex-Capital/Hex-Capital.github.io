# Shielded -- Paul Graham Evaluation

James Simon spent time on Balyasny’s commodities desk, then built Shielded around tariff, commodity-price, and supplier effects hidden in ERP exports. Shielded is a schlep-rich company without a visible hacker trail. That pairing matters. The unpleasant work—integrating sensitive ERP data, interpreting Federal Register changes, and explaining margin movements—is Shielded’s strongest signal. Simon’s MIT computer-science degree is respectable, but no public repository, pre-company artifact, or technical stunt shows what sort of hacker he is. Credentials tell me someone passed tests. Artifacts tell me whether he is an animal.

**Stripe** (2010) is the high rung for schlep blindness: the Collisons turned payments integration and regulatory misery into the reason Stripe won. Shielded shares that mechanism because a purported 48-hour deployment must reconcile supplier origins, volumes, commodities, tariffs, and product margins. Shielded diverges where the analogy becomes load-bearing. Stripe’s API was demonstrably simpler than the alternatives, while Interos already sells tariff-cost and product-revenue impact analysis and Shielded has published no integration-depth or retention evidence. The schlep is real. Whether Shielded has actually compressed it is still unproven.

**Loopt** (2005) is the low-rung warning: force of personality can justify a bet before the technology is ready, but it cannot manufacture a hacker artifact. Shielded presents Simon’s MIT master’s, methane-emissions research, and commodities experience, yet Shielded shows no GitHub trail, open-source work, demo video, or unusual technical construction predating fundraising. Loopt was a known exception because Sam Altman passed the animal test so overwhelmingly that I accepted the missing infrastructure. Shielded has not supplied equivalent evidence that Simon’s resourcefulness should override the absent hacker genealogy.

Hundreds of interviews with finance and procurement teams, customized prospect-data demos, a $1,000 referral payment, and three first-party use cases are better than a polished deck. Shielded’s resourcefulness could compound over the next 18 months if Simon personally performs every ugly integration, discovers recurring data errors, and turns those repairs into software. Shielded could also become a consultancy wearing an agent interface: every customer brings a different ERP schema, supplier taxonomy, and regulatory interpretation. The difference will be whether the restaurant chain’s 200-SKU deployment becomes repeatable without Simon rebuilding the machinery by hand.

Balyasny’s commodities desk gives Shielded credible proximity to the problem, but proximity is not yet an organic idea. Shielded would move up a full tier for me if Simon could say, precisely, “I had to calculate this exposure at Balyasny, the available tools failed, so I built the first version for myself.” The dossier instead says Shielded followed exposure to commodity analysis with hundreds of customer interviews. That may be genuine founder pain, but it may also be intelligent market selection. **Dropbox** (2007) did not begin with interviews about lost files; Drew Houston had already lost the USB stick.

**Stripe** (2010) also exposes Shielded’s cofounder gap. Two Collison brothers who had already built Auctomatic could divide the hacking while preserving a shared technical language. Shielded publicly identifies only James Simon, lists no cofounder history, and even reports a team size of zero. I will apply the absent-evidence default rather than declare Shielded solo, but enterprise integrations, regulatory interpretation, security, and founder-led sales are too many simultaneous schleps for one person. Shielded needs a strong technical or operational counterpart, not a post-funding manager hired to “scale the organization.”

Shielded will, by July 2029, narrow into tariff and commodity-margin monitoring for food and industrial manufacturers, reach roughly $1–3 million in ARR, and remain an integration-heavy specialist rather than displacing Interos. Shielded’s schlep creates enough customer value to support a real business, while the missing hacker artifact makes it more likely that implementation labor grows alongside revenue instead of collapsing into a Stripe-like default layer. The prediction is falsifiable: broad cross-industry adoption above $5 million ARR would prove Shielded converted the schlep into software faster than I expect.

Three first-party use cases and a claimed 48-hour ERP deployment make the strongest opposing case. Shielded may already have built the artifact the public dossier cannot see; Susa Ventures’ backing and Simon’s MIT-plus-Balyasny background strengthen that possibility. An **Airbnb**-style exception is conceivable: customized demos and hundreds of interviews could be Shielded’s cereal boxes, evidence of an animal hiding behind sparse public information. But unnamed customers, no revenue signal, no repository, no cofounder evidence, and direct Interos overlap leave too much of that case inferred. I would flip from Neutral to Invest if Shielded produced one named paying customer that deployed within 48 hours, used the product repeatedly for three months, and required no bespoke analyst work after onboarding.
