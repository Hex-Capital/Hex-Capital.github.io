# HABU -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A **strategic acquisition by a multifamily developer** is the strongest path. HABU reports a working parcel-screening and underwriting module, and the memo describes records of sites it pursued or rejected. A buyer could have a reason to own the workflow and its development records if linking screening decisions to actual approvals, costs, and lease-up outcomes lets it consistently select better sites or develop them at lower cost. That ownership advantage is an inference. HABU’s targets of five times as many projects per manager and up to 20% faster delivery lack a completed-project comparison. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

The first Phoenix site was reported under contract, and HABU recorded $1.126 million in corporate securities sales. Those are early execution signals. The first project still requires construction debt and project equity, and the CEO’s reported interest in $100,000–$1 million project checks is uncommitted. A financed project carried through lease-up could produce the first measured comparison. Corporate runway and the rights needed to transfer the software, records, and project interests remain unverified. Project fees and promotes could contribute to corporate cash, but the memo gives no evidence of distributable cash or a payout plan for this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility, Key Facts)

The memo gives no usable acquisition price or holder terms. Under the illustrative seed case, one $500,000 check buys 5% at a $10 million post-money valuation and dilutes to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or **$83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds** at 3% ownership. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale cases below are assumptions, with years measured from investment. They assume zero net debt, seller costs, and other price adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. They also assume simple pro rata allocation without senior claims and full cash payment at close. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation each contribute $0.

| Scenario | Timing and assumed outcome | Gross holder cash |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | Year 6, $50 million enterprise value and distributable equity | $1.5 million (3×) |
| Strong sale | Year 8, $100 million enterprise value and distributable equity | $3 million (6×) |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The holder retains the shares. | $0 (0×) |

The strong scenario clears the hurdle at 3% and yields only 4× at 2%. The memo supplies no buyer economics to support the assumed sale price. (Defensibility, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An incumbent can already partner with HABU through a co-GP or joint venture. It may also be able to reproduce parts of first-pass screening with public data and its own underwriting records. HABU’s manual collection of Phoenix approval knowledge raises a further question about how much of the advantage a buyer could replicate across markets. If partnership or internal development delivers comparable gains, a buyer has little reason to pay the required price for ownership. The memo establishes those substitutes as credible possibilities, but leaves their effectiveness and any resulting price ceiling unresolved. The structural Pass override therefore does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can HABU demonstrate a repeatable economic gain through lease-up that a multifamily developer would need to own the platform to capture? Move from Watch to Reach out if completed-project comparisons show realized gains that survive a build-versus-partner comparison and support a buyer valuation high enough to clear the holder’s cash hurdle under plausible dilution and deal terms.
