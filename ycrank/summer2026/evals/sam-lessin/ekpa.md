# Ekpa -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I want are missing: proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. For me, that is not administrative cleanup. Ekpa sits in an extremely legible 2026 consensus—cheap inference plus autonomous agents plus retail investing—and Robinhood Cortex and SoFi’s newly acquired Composer already validate that consensus. Without terms, I cannot know whether I am being paid to take an unpopular risk or buying an option after the market has bid away the doubt.

The second missing item is the card this capital flips. Ekpa has assembled a useful research surface—connected holdings, filings, news, alerts, portfolio analysis, and backtesting—but the dossier names no finite experiment, deadline, or pass/fail metric. “Onboard customers and build more features” is not a venture moment. The clean test might be whether portfolio-aware research changes a measurable investing decision and creates durable willingness to pay, but that is my proposed experiment, not the founders’ documented use of proceeds.

The strongest bull case is real for a pre-seed company. Users have reportedly connected more than $3.4 million in assets and run 2,400 analyses. Those actions are more informative than a waitlist because people are exposing actual portfolios and invoking the product against them. Anant Asthana has built agentic drug-discovery pipelines, while Yuga Patel combines CS, economics, and investing experience. The team plausibly spans the research-agent and investing sides of the system.

But connected assets are not managed assets in the economic sense, analyses are not purchases, and a one-week return above 1.5% without methodology is closer to metric theater than underwriting evidence. Basic remains free; Plus pricing, paid conversion, repeat usage, gross margin, burn, and runway are undisclosed. Ekpa therefore has workflow engagement without proof of a proprietary dark pool or profitable distribution. SnapTrade owns an important ingestion dependency, and brokerage incumbents already own the accounts, execution, customer acquisition, and increasingly the assistant itself.

The painful counterargument is that the connected-account history could become proprietary before incumbents serve cross-brokerage users well. If those 2,400 analyses come from a small cohort repeatedly interrogating real holdings, Ekpa may already possess the beginnings of an owned workflow that the public figures obscure. That could override weak seed-price evidence—but the known exception requires hard conversion and retention, not merely plausible future data. Nothing here yet shows that Ekpa’s workflow causes payment, retained behavior, or better decisions.

I would pass today. My single flip condition is a verifiable cohort of at least 100 paying connected-account customers with 70% or better 90-day paid retention by January 31, 2027. That result would convert the $3.4 million from a vanity denominator into evidence that Ekpa owns a recurring research workflow. Without it, I expect the company within 36 months either to pivot toward institutional research tooling or remain a sub-$1 million ARR consumer feature vulnerable to brokerage-native substitution.
