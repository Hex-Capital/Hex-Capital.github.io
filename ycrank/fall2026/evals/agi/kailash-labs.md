# Kailash Labs -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest path. Kailash could make a short-run packing or kitting station economical to automate if learning parts on the line reduces commissioning and changeover labor. With equally capable models widely available, a manufacturer could still pay Kailash to install, integrate, and keep the station running at the required throughput and quality. Both conditions remain unproven. The physical work gives Kailash a service to sell, and its ability to deliver that service better than other integrators remains open. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Observed evidence.** The memo records a public station demo and an open pilot booking page. Buyer commitments, paid conversions, and production uptime remain unverified. Sharma’s robot-learning background and the team’s release of Marlin-2B support early technical execution. The Marlin benchmarks cover video tasks, leaving factory throughput and reliability untested. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

**Company claims and scenario assumptions.** Kailash advertises installation in days and a seven-day keep-or-return pilot with no upfront customer capex. Assume a manufacturer previously found a short-run station uneconomic to automate. At equivalent throughput and defect rates, the trial would need to show that human hours per accepted unit fall after counting setup, changeovers, supervision, and exception handling. For Kailash, the recurring fee would also need to cover equipment, integration, support, downtime, and the cost of installing and removing stations from pilots that fail to convert. The memo provides neither a service rate nor measured inputs for that bridge. The Fall 2026 YC batch is consistent with seed-stage evaluation. Kailash’s Bengaluru location leaves fit with Decimal’s primarily U.S. strategy open. (Company memo overview, The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Formic and India-based Corelyn already offer managed automation. If competitors with the same models and ample capital can deliver equivalent uptime and changeovers, buyers may have little reason to choose Kailash. The advertised installation lead time has yet to be tested on a comparable line, and hands-on support could absorb the labor savings. The memo also identifies no controlled input or qualified capacity that would support a separate Path A case. The competing offerings make substitution credible, while actual buyer switching and like-for-like performance remain untested. The decisive Path B conditions are unresolved, so the action is Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent manufacturer keep paying for a Kailash station on a line it previously could not justify automating once it has compared the results with its manual baseline and a comparable managed cell? A buyer-verified trial should record throughput, defects, total human hours, and Kailash’s delivery costs through a representative changeover. Paid continuation at a rate that covers those costs, with a documented preference for Kailash tied to integration or uptime under comparable model capability, would support rescoring and a move to Reach out if the score clears 65 and mandate fit is confirmed. A representative trial showing that exception labor erases the savings or that buyers readily switch to an equivalent service would support Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
