# Palette -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Serena Pei’s diffusion and multimodal research makes **Palette** a strong-pair, weak-schlep startup. Josephine Lee and Pei spent 2022–26 studying computer science at MIT, so **Palette** has more technical coherence than the usual AI wrapper. But the current product routes other companies’ models, and no unpleasant work appears to get harder—or more valuable—as Palette grows. Good founders may have built a reasonable product. Reasonable is not the same as inevitable.

**Stripe** is the high rung for co-founder configuration: two brothers with years of shared commerce hacking, complementary abilities, and a relationship that could survive the payments schlep. **Palette** has the right number of founders and relevant technical coverage, but the dossier establishes only that Lee and Pei attended MIT during the same four years. It does not say they previously built together, lived together, or endured anything together. **Palette** sits above an assembled-for-the-application pair, but well below the Collisons.

**Kiko** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism: a technically competent software product can fail to compound when it contains too little unavoidable schlep, leaving an eBay sale instead of a durable company. **Palette** currently sits uncomfortably near that pattern. Routing Seedance, Veo, Kling, MiniMax, and LTX is engineering work, but Higgsfield and Krea can access overlapping models and reproduce connected workflows. Copyright uncertainty belongs mostly to Palette’s customers; it does not force **Palette** through a Coinbase-like compliance grind that competitors will avoid.

**Palette’s** 130-plus regression tests are a shipped artifact, but they are not yet evidence of an animal. There is no named customer won manually, no rejected application overturned by a new prototype, and no unscalable revenue stunt: no evidence—applying the absent-evidence default. Over 18 months, **Palette** can make routing faster and cheaper, but upstream models will change beneath those tests and absorb much of the technical learning. Animal resourcefulness would appear as Lee and Pei personally producing customers’ campaigns at 2 a.m., discovering a workflow nobody could describe in a prompt, then turning that schlep into software.

The June 29 page describing **Palette** as an HR-agent platform is evidence against an organic idea, because it suggests the founders may be searching for a market rather than escaping a problem they personally had. No evidence—applying the absent-evidence default. **Palette’s** score would flip a tier if Lee or Pei had repeatedly needed to turn documents into consistent videos at MIT, Broad, Amazon, or A*STAR and built Palette first for that workflow. Without such a story, “prompt, image, document or reference” reads like a list of available inputs rather than the scar tissue of a user.

**Dropbox** had the hacker signal in its demo: Drew Houston showed a working solution to his own problem before asking anyone to believe the deck. **Palette** has a weaker but real parallel in Pei’s CSAIL, Media Lab, LIDS, Amazon, and A*STAR work, plus public image, video, music, asset, and model endpoints. But **Palette** reports no public repository, hacker-community trail, or independently reproducible benchmark; the 35% speed and 50% cost improvements remain internal claims. These founders can probably build. The missing question is whether they compulsively build things people pull from them.

Higgsfield’s reported $200M annual revenue run rate shows that demand for AI-video workflows is real, but it also shows where **Palette’s** horizontal layer is most exposed. My 36-month prediction is that by July 2029 **Palette** will have abandoned general-purpose model routing, pivoted into enterprise training and internal-content production, and remain below $2M ARR. The strong founder pair should be capable of finding an adjacent workflow; the weak schlep and replicable routing layer make it unlikely that **Palette** owns the broad category.

Serena Pei’s multimodal research, **Palette’s** public API, 130-plus tests, and claimed 50% cost reduction form the strongest bull case: perhaps Lee and Pei are simply pre-publicity animals, and the HR-to-video discontinuity records fast learning rather than drift. **Loopt** is the known exception showing that weak timing can be overridden by extraordinary founder force. But **Palette** supplies no corresponding force-of-personality evidence, no current-product revenue, no customer usage, and no founder-origin story, while Runway, Higgsfield, Luma, and Krea already crowd the surface area. I pass. I would reverse that verdict if **Palette** reaches $500K ARR from at least ten enterprise customers maintaining monthly usage for six consecutive months by July 2027.
