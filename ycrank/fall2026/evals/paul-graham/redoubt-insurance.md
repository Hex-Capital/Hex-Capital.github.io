# Redoubt Insurance -- Paul Graham Evaluation

A contractor who cannot start work until an insurance document arrives has an urgent problem. Redoubt Insurance has chosen a place where software could do something immediately valuable. But the contractor’s obstacle is not yet evidence of the founder’s resourcefulness. The dossier does not show Andre Beukers encountering a particular blockage, building a workaround, and getting someone back to work. That sequence would tell me more than the size of the insurance market.

There is some evidence that Beukers builds things. Redoubt publishes an interactive Utah contractor setup checker, and his public research code concerns deep reinforcement learning. The checker earns modest technical credit as a shipped utility. The research supports technical capability, but it does not establish that Redoubt’s proposed experience-learning system works. My recurring preference for concrete proof before a round is only weakly satisfied here. Nor is there evidence of technical users adopting a programmable interface or inventing unexpected uses for it.

The strongest argument for Redoubt is that Beukers has entered the machinery of the business. The company publishes Utah agency license **1116212** and names access relationships including Progressive Commercial and Burns & Wilcox. Its guidance distinguishes a certificate correction from a change that requires an underlying policy endorsement. That distinction matters. Someone who automates the document while misunderstanding the coverage could make the customer’s problem worse. A founder who personally handles these cases could discover exactly what to automate. This is a credible route from manual service to software. However, the dossier does not establish that Beukers encountered this problem through prior work, or show a customer observation causing him to revise the product.

The missing link is customer behavior. Contractors and trucking operators are identifiable niches, but Redoubt’s **115 LinkedIn followers** and reported **$500,000 YC financing** do not show that either niche returns for the service. I would keep demand at the absent-evidence default rather than infer rejection. Likewise, there is no evidence of obstinacy, premature delegation, or dependence on one promised distribution partner. Carrier dependence is an operating constraint, not proof of those red flags. The economic question is whether each placement teaches Redoubt something that makes subsequent placements substantially easier. The **$158 billion premium market** does not answer that question, and commissions remain an inferred business model. Harper already advertises automated submission workflows; merely adding automation would not establish the answer either.

I would pass on Redoubt Insurance today. The documented exceptions in my framework depend on an impressive working product, unmistakable user pull, or validated technical results; none supplies an override here. The founder-action gate remains unmet. This could still become an excellent investment if the agency is a learning instrument whose lessons rapidly become software. My one condition for reopening the decision would be an independently verifiable case study tracing a specific customer blockage through a founder-built workaround shipped within a week to repeated paid use by ten contractors. That would connect the promising setting to the founder behavior I actually invest in.
