# Stoa -- Elad Gil Evaluation

A public RFQ marketplace spanning A100 through GB200 NVL hardware makes **Stoa** a single-miracle infrastructure bet with no visible revenue curve. **Stoa** does not need better semiconductor physics; Stoa needs fragmented institutional trading to consolidate around its workflow. That is attractive architecture, but 146 LinkedIn followers and zero disclosed RFQ, participant, or transaction counts do not show something “just working.” The signature is operationally straightforward exchange infrastructure without proof of liquidity.

**Color Genomics** is the high rung on single-miracle architecture: workflow and data infrastructure built on commodity sequencing rather than a bet on new chemistry. **Stoa** shares that mechanism by layering RFQs, KYB, settlement tracking, and pricing data over existing NVIDIA hardware. **Stoa** diverges because marketplace liquidity and counterparty trust must emerge together, while Stoa expressly declines to inspect hardware, take title, or assume dispute responsibility. That adds a second adoption dependency, but not a science miracle.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism: activity can be a false signal when the market structure favors another network. **Stoa** is earlier and less legible—Stoa discloses no completed transactions, repeat buyers, GMV, or revenue, while Compute Exchange already aggregates procurement across more than 100 compute providers. At pre-seed I do not require meaningful revenue, but an exchange should be unusually eager to disclose even primitive liquidity. Silence earns the 8/25 absent-evidence default.

**Compute Exchange’s July 2026 launch** and Ornn’s $33 million June financing establish that GPU trading is being standardized, but they do not identify the catalyst that uniquely opens **Stoa’s** window. Over 18 months, accelerating GPU refresh cycles could create secondary inventory, fragmented condition grades, and enough clearing-price dispersion for **Stoa** to compound transaction data into better quotes. The contrary mechanism is equally plausible: those launches mean the category is already consensus and Stoa is merely one more venue. Stoa has skating-for-the-puck timing, but no Ukraine-, ChatGPT-, or API-level discontinuity.

**Berat Celik’s public marketplace** is evidence that **Stoa** can ship, while Kaan Yigit’s Uniper commodities systems experience and Eren Berke Saglam’s Series 7 and 63 registrations are relevant inputs rather than generic pedigree. **Stoa** would move into my Invest tier if the founders demonstrated Perplexity-like iteration—three named dealer requests converted into shipped workflow changes, followed by repeat RFQs. The dossier instead names prior titles, ForgeMind and XFunds without adoption evidence, and no user-feedback loop. I see a product artifact, not yet a founder shipping curve.

**Stripe** became an index on e-commerce because every new internet merchant could increase payment volume without Stripe choosing which merchant would win. **Stoa** could become an index on AI hardware turnover: every GPU generation, neocloud buildout, liquidation, and financing event creates another potential transaction and pricing observation. Stoa’s 0% fee for eligible data partners is a thoughtful bootstrap mechanism, but Stoa lacks a structural barrier and faces Compute Exchange in the identical physical-GPU workflow. Today Stoa must win share against another venue before it can ride the category.

**Stoa’s undisclosed transaction volume** leads me to predict that within 36 months **Stoa** will pivot from a transaction exchange into GPU pricing-data and procurement software, reaching less than $3 million ARR rather than becoming the dominant physical-hardware venue. **Stoa** can build the software because the single-miracle architecture is tractable; Stoa will struggle to manufacture exchange liquidity while users retain inspection, conformity, export, and counterparty risk. The data exhaust may become the product even if the marketplace does not.

**Kaan Yigit’s commodities-desk experience** is the strongest bull case: **Stoa** has domain-relevant market-structure knowledge, a working institutional RFQ product, transaction-based fees, and a data-partner subsidy precisely when Ornn and Compute Exchange validate the category. The Known Exception is Mistral—capital intensity and missing revenue did not matter once the catalyst and uniquely qualified builders were obvious. **Stoa** does not yet clear that exception because Stoa’s founders have no disclosed scaled marketplace artifact, its competitor already spans 100-plus providers, and its own liquidity remains invisible. I would flip to Invest if Stoa documents at least $10 million of completed quarterly GMV, repeat activity from five named institutional counterparties, and greater than 20% monthly transaction growth.
