# Anywhere -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A consumer maps or social platform could pay cash to own a social map that draws users back to plan outings with friends. The asset would be the combination of accumulated place history, reciprocal sharing and agent-assisted planning. Ownership could give a buyer control over how that experience develops inside its product, provided the user relationships and necessary rights transfer. Snap’s $213.3 million cash acquisition of Zenly shows that a buyer has paid for an adjacent social-map product. The memo gives too little operating detail to use that price for Anywhere. (The Idea, Defensibility, Founders & Team)

The memo records a live, free iPhone app with a 2.8/5 rating from nine U.S. ratings. YC reported thousands of first-week signups, and Delaroche said connected agents had full read/write access at launch. A useful seed milestone would be a 90-day cohort showing that friend groups repeatedly retrieve saved places, share them and use them to make plans. The launch gives the team a cohort to measure, and Delaroche’s map engineering experience supports execution. Retention, the test budget and runway remain unknown. A sale would also depend on what IP, data rights and third-party licenses can transfer. The memo identifies revocable agent access and reliance on Apple and Google map and place data, with transfer terms unverified. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** With no investment terms supplied, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash. Under a simple pro rata waterfall, that requires $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume an all-cash price paid at closing, zero net debt, no other closing adjustments or senior claims, and $0 each from stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover and compensation. Enterprise value therefore equals distributable equity, and holder proceeds are before taxes. The prices and timing are illustrative assumptions. The memo provides no buyer economics to support either price. (Founders & Team, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Timing | Distributable equity | Holder cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|-----------|--------|----------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| Modest cash sale | Year 4 | $30 million | $900,000 | 1.8× |
| Strong cash sale | Year 7 | $100 million | $3 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private ownership | Through year 8 | No distribution | $0 | 0.0× |

The strong sale clears the hurdle at 3%, but yields $2 million, or 4.0×, at 2%. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and any available cash is reinvested. The investor retains the shares. The memo supports no independent route to holder cash. Anywhere is free, and the paid plans cited belong to a separate Fenn product. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could deliver much of the experience through its own map or a standard agent integration. Google already offers conversational discovery with custom map results, standard connectors support external read/write access, and Bump offers live friend locations and shared visits. If users can get the same planning value through those options or a partnership, a buyer has little reason to pay an ownership premium. The overlap makes the proceeds case too uncertain for outreach today. The memo leaves open whether Anywhere’s friend groups develop durable habits that those substitutes cannot reproduce, so the evidence falls short of a structural Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would owning Anywhere’s social map create enough incremental value for a maps or social platform to pay a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A 90-day buyer-side comparison showing durable repeat planning among friend groups, with a measurable advantage over a connector-only integration and buyer economics supporting at least $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, would move the action to Reach out.
