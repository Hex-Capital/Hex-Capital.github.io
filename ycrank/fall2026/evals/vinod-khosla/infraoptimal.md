# InfraOptimal -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

InfraOptimal is attacking a consequential bottleneck: how much useful inference existing hardware produces. If autonomous optimization could multiply that output, cheaper machine intelligence could reach far more applications. But the dossier establishes neither the wasted-capacity baseline nor the improvement required to unlock those applications. The $132.7 billion inference-market estimate includes hardware outside this offering. It establishes a large surrounding market, not the consequence InfraOptimal can capture. I would pass with my own money today.

The strongest evidence is actual engineering. September’s EvoCompiler research reports approximately 3–5.6% lower decoder time on an Apple M2 Pro, supported by reproducible experiments and a scoped Z3 equivalence proof. That deserves more attention than an endpoint landing page. It also defines the present limit: an agent-assisted experiment, outside the current GPU-serving product, with a proof of an integer identity rather than whole-program correctness. Maintainers warned the change could regress on microcontrollers and DSPs. InfraOptimal has demonstrated a useful local optimization; it has not demonstrated a mechanism that makes inference 5–10x better than Baseten, Together, or another named alternative.

The strongest argument against passing is that local improvements could become the raw material for a much larger search system. Cassidy’s processor-validation and compiler background complements Kocheta’s robotics and research-systems experience. These founders have relevant tools for attacking the problem. Their combination fits my preference for several necessary disciplines assembled around an expensive workflow. Retaining successful transformations could eventually create a proprietary learning loop. But cross-customer compounding remains an inference, and [AWS already offers agentic kernel-development tools](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/stop-hand-tuning-kernels-how-neuron-agentic-development-accelerates-aws-trainium-optimizations/). The exception case for backing a modest initial product requires evidence that its mechanism or its founders can expand into a much larger infrastructure opportunity. Their technical backgrounds make that possible; the dossier does not yet establish it. Nor does it establish the adverse claim that this is merely an interface wrapper.

The next dollar should resolve whether the search system generalizes safely to production inference. The dossier provides no ranked experiment with a numerical acceptance threshold, budget, deadline, and stop-or-change response. That is missing planning evidence, not evidence of reckless spending. Similarly, moving from September’s compiler materials to dedicated endpoints does not establish fast belief revision without knowing what customer evidence caused the change. Leaving school demonstrates commitment. It does not independently demonstrate recruiting ability or learning rate. I give those dimensions their missing-evidence defaults rather than inventing either strength or weakness.

Economics could erase a technical win. InfraOptimal’s [terms charge for dedicated capacity by the minute, including idle time](https://infraoptimal.com/terms). A faster kernel can coexist with an expensive endpoint if utilization is poor or optimization consumes substantial compute and engineering attention. There is no disclosed comparison of delivered cost per accepted output at matched quality and latency, including those costs. This is not evidence of subsidy dependence. It leaves the economic gate unanswered. The absence of revenue, hiring, or a long customer history carries no separate penalty at this stage.

One result would change my decision: an independently reproducible production benchmark showing InfraOptimal delivering equivalent-quality inference at least 5x cheaper than a named managed endpoint across several materially different customer workloads, including idle capacity and optimization overhead, with correctness and latency acceptance tests satisfied. That would connect the compiler work to a consequential cost collapse. Until then, I see credible technical founders and a measurable experiment, but insufficient evidence that this becomes the infrastructure breakthrough I want to own.
