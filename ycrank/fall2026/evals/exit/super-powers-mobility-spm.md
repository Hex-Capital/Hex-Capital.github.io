# Super Powers Mobility (SPM) -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is SPM’s strongest path to shareholder cash. A defense prime or military power-systems supplier could buy SPM to bring modular battery packs, power electronics, and vehicle integration in-house. Ownership could pay if it lets the buyer reuse a validated design across programs and measurably reduce integration time and cost. The memo records EnerSys’s acquisition of military battery supplier Bren-Tronics, establishing that a strategic buyer has paid for an adjacent capability. The value of owning SPM’s specific capability remains an inference. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

SPM reports $421,815 in realized revenue in one month. Its launch separately describes more than $400,000 in contracted revenue in one month, and the memo leaves their overlap unresolved. SPM also reports participation in four federal contracts and bookings into February 2027. These claims support testing whether a prime will place and accept repeat production orders at predictable cost and delivery times. The historical $1.3 million pre-seed round preceded the pivot, and current runway and scale-up funding remain unverified. (*Key Facts*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

**Investor proceeds.** With investment terms unspecified, the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. Bren-Tronics sold for $208 million against approximately $100 million in prior-year sales, a reported purchase price of about 2.1× sales. At that ratio, clearing the 3% hurdle in an all-cash sale would imply roughly $40 million in annual sales. This is a scale check. The memo leaves SPM’s repeatable revenue and buyer-specific economics unverified. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, transaction costs, and other price adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. An assumed pro rata waterfall allocates 3% to the investor with zero preference diversion. All consideration is cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. The scenarios illustrate the hurdle at different prices and timings.

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value → equity consideration | Gross cash to investor | Gross multiple |
|-----------|--------|------------------------------------------|------------------------|----------------|
| Modest acquisition | Year 5 | $50M → $50M | $1.5M | 3.0× |
| Strong acquisition | Year 7 | $100M → $100M | $3.0M | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested | $0, shares retained | 0.0× realized |

At 2% ownership, even the strong scenario pays $2 million, or 4.0×. SPM’s reported patent lacks a disclosed identifier, and the memo leaves IP ownership and contract transferability unverified. A sponsor acquisition of Inventus Power shows category interest, while SPM’s own sustainable cash flow and buyout financing remain unsubstantiated. The memo supplies no usable evidence of an eligible secondary, a public resale, or a funded shareholder payout. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A prime can already buy SPM’s units and contract separately for custom engineering. Inventus Power also offers complete military battery and power systems. If those procurement options deliver comparable schedule and integration benefits, buying SPM could add too little value to clear the proceeds hurdle. The memo leaves the adequacy of those substitutes for SPM’s integrated vehicle designs unresolved. The strongest sale scenario can clear the hurdle at 3% ownership, and the evidence does not establish that prolonged private ownership is the probable outcome. No Pass override applies. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a prime that has accepted repeat production quantify enough incremental value from owning SPM’s integrated design to support a price that clears the investor’s proceeds hurdle? A buyer-validated comparison of integration cost and time under ownership versus continued procurement would answer it. Evidence supporting a price that clears the hurdle after applicable adjustments would move SPM from Watch to Reach out.
