# Sinter -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A rapid-molding provider or manufacturing marketplace could buy Sinter to deploy its quoting workflow and photopolymer tooling process across more customer jobs. The ownership case depends on whether Sinter can turn production results into proprietary tool-design know-how that improves cost and turnaround across repeat jobs. A buyer would need to capture more value by owning that capability than by contracting for parts or building a similar process. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

**Observed evidence.** The memo documents public prototype ordering, automated manufacturability checks and mesh repair, and recruitment for molding pilots. The founders’ hands-on manufacturing experience supports running a production test. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

**Company claims.** Sinter advertises molded parts in 48 hours at one-tenth the time and cost of traditional methods and reports producing more than 500 kg of parts with customers in its first month. The memo gives no molding-specific breakdown of that volume, repeat-job economics, or tool-life results. It also leaves the capital and runway for a production test unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** Under the illustrative seed terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted by 40% to 3%. With no follow-on checks, the eight-year 5× hurdle is $2.5 million in gross cash. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The memo supplies no buyer economics or applicable transaction price to support a range. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Illustrative scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Holder cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale in year 5 | $50M → $50M | $1.5M | 3× |
| Strong sale in year 7 | $100M → $100M | $3.0M | 6× |
| Prolonged private through year 8 | No transaction or distributions | $0, shares retained | 0× |

The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, transaction expenses, and senior claims, with all cash paid pro rata at closing before investor taxes. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. At 2% ownership, even the illustrative strong sale returns only $2 million, or 4×. The memo leaves IP title and transfer terms unverified. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. The memo supplies no independently supported fallback that pays this holder. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An incumbent can already offer quick-turn aluminum molding, trial printed resin inserts using commercially available equipment, or pay Sinter per part. Heat and creep could erode Sinter’s claimed savings if tools need frequent replacement. Those are credible substitutes, but the memo does not establish their relative cost on the jobs Sinter targets or show that they cap every credible sale below the proceeds hurdle. The evidence supports waiting for a production benchmark and does not trigger a structural Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a repeat-production pilot with a prospective strategic buyer show that owning Sinter’s process would create enough incremental value to support a price near the $83.3 million base-case hurdle? The pilot would need to measure lead time, usable shots per tool, and cost per accepted part on matched jobs against the buyer’s best build or outsource option. A durable advantage that plausibly supports the hurdle when applied to the buyer’s eligible job volume would move the action to **Reach out**. Evidence that the buyer can capture the same economics through a substitute at a cost that caps the acquisition price materially below the hurdle would move it to **Pass**. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
