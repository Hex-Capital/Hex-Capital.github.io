# Open Actuation -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Open Actuation has built something more tangible than a robotics financing story: custom motors, manufacturing tooling, and samples getting customer feedback. But what does my check buy? The dossier supplies no proposed post-money, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is the first scarce check. Those are unknowns, not proof of an auction. It also supplies no funded experiment with a deadline and a measurable result. Four-week delivery is a product promise; it does not establish what truth this round would reveal. Both primary gates receive their missing-evidence defaults.

The strongest case is the bridge Jon Facer is building between manufacturing software and physical component production. Five years as 1Factory’s first employee, experience at Gecko Robotics, and actual motor-building activity give that bridge substance. The LA facility includes winding, magnet insertion, balancing, and testing equipment. This fits my unstated preference for founders reorganizing difficult physical operations. I give Facer credit for operating access and delivered samples, while stopping short of assuming he has previously scaled either a software platform or a motor factory.

The question is whether customization creates an owned customer relationship that compounds. Open Actuation reports builds for unnamed drone OEMs, and July 2026 reporting describes initial samples undergoing customer feedback. That is useful product evidence, but it does not establish that proprietary access or embedded workflow has caused purchases or repeat orders. Authorized CubeMars distribution provides a way to meet buyers; it does not establish ownership of those buyers. Meanwhile, Atlas Motion also offers custom motors within weeks, and Westmag has disclosed $11 million in funding. Domestic sourcing and customization are already recognized opportunities. The potential dark pool is accumulated customer designs and production knowledge, but the dossier has not yet demonstrated its economic effect.

The bull case stings because a small supplier can become indispensable well before its public numbers look impressive. Facer describes demand from drone reshoring, the team has equipment in place, and customer samples could become recurring production orders. Open Actuation could build a meaningful business without winning the entire actuator market. But delete the next financing: the $187 CubeMars listing tells me neither realized revenue nor contribution margin, and there is no disclosed burn, working-capital requirement, or route to break-even. Imported magnets and silicon steel make that cash question consequential. The conflicting thousands-per-week and thousands-per-month capacity claims also cannot substitute for paid shipments. My concern is an unproved cash cycle, not the two-person team.

I pass on the evidence supplied. The known exceptions to my financing gates relied on hard conversion, cash economics, exceptional external validation, or a deliberately structured capital plan; Open Actuation’s samples do not yet supply that override. Nor would I manufacture an auction red flag from YC membership or infer a financing treadmill from a careers page. The verifiable flip condition is a documented seed proposal below $10 million post-money that funds one dated production test, backed by signed customer orders and a fully costed cash budget showing that passing the test makes another round optional. That would connect the manufacturing capability to an investable deal.
