# Verdun -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** Multi-site dealership groups are the clearest initial buyer. They need recurring alarm verification and response across properties. Verdun says it connects to existing cameras and offers white-label monitoring that security dealers can sell under their own contracts. If dealers can reuse the installation and trial process, they could bring successive buyers onto the service, and a group could add sites and cameras under the per-camera pricing model. The memo documents the dealer offer and a free side-by-side trial. It gives no example of a dealer bringing a buyer live and converting it to paid monitoring. *(The Idea, Key Facts)*

The memo records an anonymized screenshot showing alarm activity across sites. Verdun separately reports that AI closed about 85% of alerts in a 14-day sample. The screenshot gives a concrete signal of activity, while the reported closure rate suggests a way to absorb more camera volume. The memo supplies no buyer-confirmed outcome, paid conversion, repeat-use or expansion record, or measured rollout cost. The global market estimate establishes category breadth. Portability is still an inference. In a second geography, the same camera integration and triage could carry over if dealers can configure local response procedures and meet video-handling requirements without rebuilding the service. *(Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)*

### Countercase

Verdun could scale like a staffed monitoring service. Each new site may require camera access, site-specific rules, and response setup. More cameras add inference costs, and escalated alerts still consume operator time. If alerts cluster at peak times, staffing could rise even with a high average AI closure rate. The 14-day sample does not establish operator minutes saved or threat recall, and a missed threat may never reach an operator. These gaps keep the case at Watch because the memo does not establish that delivery costs must rise proportionally or that the buyer problem has a structural geographic ceiling. *(The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)*

Can an independent security dealer in a second geography convert a dealership group from a parallel trial to paid monitoring through a reusable setup? A rollout record showing setup hours, operator minutes and inference cost per camera, and threat recall against the incumbent would answer it. Move to Reach out if the buyer pays and the rollout shows that local response and video handling can be configured with bounded effort while maintaining recall and leaving room for contribution after delivery costs. *(The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)*
