# Repovive -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Repovive could help make machine-checkable reasoning abundant enough to verify chips and critical software. That consequence interests me. But the disclosed business sells training environments to AI labs, and the dossier does not quantify how those environments reduce verification cost or expand what can be verified. Selling inputs to a potentially transformative technology does not automatically capture its consequence. I give the societal-impact dimension its missing-evidence default.

The strongest argument for writing a check is unusually concrete for pre-seed: **$100K in contracts across two formalization RL environments**, alongside founders with Lean-dataset and model-post-training experience. Repovive has found buyers for a scarce technical input. Its reported 12,000-person medalist community could make expert task creation repeatable—the scientific-workflow-to-platform pattern I like. The separate **$200K coding-data LOI** strengthens the commercial possibility, although it is neither collected revenue nor proof that the formalization product works.

The technical evidence is narrower. A founder reports an **8.3-percentage-point pass-rate improvement using GRPO on Leanstral 1.5**. That earns credit for an actual experiment. Without the starting pass rate, evaluation method, matched compute budget, or published benchmark, it cannot establish a fivefold improvement against an incumbent. More fundamentally, verifier acceptance can improve while intended specification coverage remains inadequate. Repovive must establish that its training environments produce more trustworthy reasoning, rather than better optimization of an incomplete reward.

That is the uncertainty I would attack first. The dossier identifies specification hacking and production transfer as risks, but supplies no company-owned experiment with a numerical threshold, budget, or stop condition. I will not turn the researcher's worry list into evidence of the founders' risk management. Shayan's formal-proof work and Amir's Gemini post-training experience are relevant preparation; they do not establish fast belief revision or difficult recruiting against a named weakness. Likewise, the earlier programming tools and later lab contracts suggest evolution, but do not document what evidence caused it.

The bull case could still make this pass expensive: expert supply, relevant founders, and two contracted environments might become a proprietary learning loop before a public benchmark appears. My exceptions allow an early check without a finished business model when the consequence or founder instigation is overwhelming. Repovive has not yet documented either override at that strength. Nor do I see affirmative evidence of an ordinary API wrapper or wasteful expansion. The unresolved issue is whether bespoke expert work becomes a compounding technical asset. Harmonic's verification product and Repovive's training-environment contracts address different layers; competitor fundraising does not answer that question. Contract value also reveals nothing about contributor compensation, compute, expert review, or cost per usable task.

I pass with my own money today. **One verifiable result would reopen the decision:** a paying lab independently reproduces at least a fivefold reduction in fully loaded cost per correctly specified, successfully verified task on held-out production-relevant problems, against its existing workflow, with specification quality independently audited. That would connect Repovive's commercial wedge to the technical and economic discontinuity I am buying.
