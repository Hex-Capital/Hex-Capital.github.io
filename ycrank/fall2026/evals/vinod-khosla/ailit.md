# Ailit -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Ailit’s consequential possibility is electrifying freight without replacing the locomotive fleet or wiring the continent. A battery tender feeds an existing diesel-electric drivetrain; swapping separates charging time from train availability. That could remove an important adoption constraint. But the dossier does not quantify diesel consumption displaced, addressable routes, or emissions avoided. The advertised 20% customer saving is an economic target, not evidence of an orders-of-magnitude societal consequence. I see a potentially important infrastructure option whose magnitude remains unmeasured.

The central uncertainty is whether swapping makes the entire system cheaper while preserving railroad operations. Ailit owns the batteries and exchange sites. Every spare pack improves availability but absorbs capital; every additional site improves coverage but needs utilization. Diesel backup makes initial deployment more forgiving, while potentially concealing how little diesel the battery system actually displaces. The advertised discount must survive electricity charges, charging losses, battery degradation, financing, handling, and backup fuel. The global $108/kWh pack average does not establish those economics, particularly when North American packs cost more. There is no disclosed cost model supporting unsubsidized parity.

The strongest argument for writing a check is Emil Tagesson. His reported work on Koenigsegg’s battery team and taking Candela’s ferry through certification and production is relevant experience turning electrical hardware into an operable vehicle. Ailit also fits my revealed preference for climate systems compatible with installed infrastructure and for founders willing to face certification and manufacturing. Those are meaningful positives. They support more confidence than an academic concept alone, but do not establish rapid belief revision or successful recruiting against specific weaknesses. The reported SEK10M of earlier financing and separate $500K YC investment provide resources; they do not validate railroad demand or operating performance.

Technical novelty remains unresolved. Dayton-Phoenix already reports integration testing of a modular battery tender at MxV Rail. Ailit’s distinguishing proposition is the exchange system, yet no measured exchange time, route availability, or performance advantage appears in the dossier. Its full-scale prototype under construction and summer 2027 deployment target make the plan concrete, but a date is not a decisive experiment. The public record gives no numerical acceptance threshold, test budget, or response to failure. I cannot award risk-removal credit for work that has not yet produced that evidence.

I pass today. This is not an objection to patient hardware development. The relevant exceptions would require an unusually compelling instigator or a quantified consequence large enough to overwhelm the missing proof; Ailit’s dossier establishes neither sufficiently. Nor does it establish adverse behavior such as subsidy dependence, premature organizational scaling, or founders ignoring contrary evidence. My objection is narrower: the exchange network is simultaneously the proposed advantage and the unproven economic burden. **My flip condition is one independently verified, railroad-operated route pilot demonstrating the advertised 20% saving against diesel on a fully burdened, unsubsidized basis while meeting the railroad’s agreed departure and availability requirements.** That would show capital buying down the uncertainty that matters.
