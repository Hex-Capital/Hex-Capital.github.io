# Degla Inc -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Degla Inc has flown something. In October 2026, the company reported a VTOL demonstration in San Diego in which voice commands directed vehicle search and tracking. That is a better starting point than an autonomy diagram. But the founder question remains unanswered: what failed, what did Aziz Hanafi change, and how quickly did it work afterward? The dossier gives me an artifact without the sequence that would reveal resourcefulness. I cannot infer that sequence from his MIT and InstaDeep credentials.

The strongest case for investing is that Hanafi has already crossed from relevant research into physical execution. His work on autonomous-system coordination and MIT MSEAS research beginning in spring 2024 make the flight demonstration more credible than an unrelated founder discovering drones through a market report. This fits my pattern of backing technical founders who produce concrete proof in difficult industries. The limit is the proof itself. A company-reported demonstration establishes some integration ability; it does not establish measured mission reliability, autonomous recovery, or coordinated operation across multiple aircraft. I give the functioning demonstration credit without treating the entire proposed system as demonstrated.

The next question is who wants this badly enough to keep using it. Search-and-rescue teams and infrastructure operators are plausible users, but they are still broad descriptions. FBI, CBP, and DIU representatives observing a flight does not tell me whether one operator requested another mission. Neither do 545 LinkedIn followers. Revenue is undisclosed, which is ordinary at this stage. What matters here is the absence of a specific recurring job that pulls the product back into use. With Skydio already offering public-safety multi-drone operations, that job must justify adopting Degla rather than adding another feature to an existing supplier’s system.

The ugly work could contain the opportunity: operating authorizations, aircraft integration, and making missions dependable under degraded communications. But an ugly barrier earns credit when the founders have encountered and worked through it. Hanafi’s prior work establishes technical proximity, not a documented personal collision with this customer problem. Likewise, UChallengeMe reportedly reaching more than 10,000 users before closure and a codebase sale is useful evidence of previous building, but it supplies no dated observation that caused a product revision. I see no affirmative evidence of the red flags about waiting for a partner, delegating product work, or confusing a launch with distribution. The missing history remains missing rather than adverse.

I would pass on Degla Inc today. The strongest objection is that a technically relevant founder with flying hardware may be precisely the person worth backing before customer evidence becomes public. I take that seriously. My exceptions allow unusually strong working-product evidence, unmistakable pull, or independently validated results to overcome an uncertain founder prior. This reported demonstration does not yet establish that strength. My single flip condition is a documented deployment episode in which a named operator’s mission fails, Hanafi ships a workaround within weeks, and that operator subsequently uses it weekly for a month. That would connect the aircraft to both founder behavior and a real need.
