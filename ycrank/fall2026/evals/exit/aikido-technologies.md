# Aikido Technologies -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition is Aikido’s strongest supported exit route. Its AO60DC design combines offshore wind generation and a floating data center. A shipbuilder or marine infrastructure operator could buy Aikido to shorten the time needed to offer compute capacity in markets with long grid connection waits. That rationale is an inference from the design and the customer problem. Aikido reports prospective GPU renters, but reservations are nonbinding and the memo gives no pricing, payments, or measured savings that a buyer could use to value the business. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

The next milestone is an offshore pilot that delivers paid compute at measured uptime and cost. Aikido reports that the retrofit began in June and that it received final platform permits in September. The memo records $1.72 million in equity sold, which supports the plausibility of near-term pilot work. The pilot’s remaining budget, full-scale financing, and certification are unresolved. The patent assigned to Aikido gives a corporate buyer an identifiable asset to acquire. Permit and contract requirements on a change of control remain unverified. The memo provides no evidence of an independent route that would pay this holder. (Market & Traction; Key Risks; Defensibility; Key Facts)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale cases below are sensitivities. Both assume the holder is eligible to sell the entire stake for cash by year eight and receives 3% of equity proceeds after the stated deductions, with no further preference or holdback reducing the payout. The memo provides no supported acquisition price or investor waterfall. (Key Facts)

### Countercase

Samsung Heavy Industries, Capital Clean Energy Carriers, and Lloyd’s Register are already developing a competing floating data center design. A potential acquirer could develop its own platform or partner with that effort, especially if Aikido’s pilot fails to demonstrate reliable uptime and economical offshore servicing. Even a successful pilot could leave Aikido raising capital for full-scale deployment, which could dilute the holder below the benchmark 3%. The competing design weakens the case for paying an acquisition premium. The evidence establishes neither a structural block nor a credible price ceiling below the required payout, so the Pass gate is open pending evidence. (Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a named strategic buyer use paid offshore pilot results and a costed full-scale deployment plan to substantiate a purchase price that would deliver at least $2.5 million in cash to the benchmark holder within eight years? A buyer-backed price range and an estimated waterfall that clear the hurdle would move the action to Reach out. A supported range that leaves the holder materially short even on the strongest credible route would move it to Pass.
