# EveryApp -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Product-led adoption is EveryApp’s strongest path. The initial buyer is a small team that runs a recurring SEO content workflow and wants control over the software. EveryApp offers a documented path from GitHub or OpenSEO to an agent-assisted install, with shared sign-in and team access through the Gateway. The same workflow could travel across markets that can use the Cloudflare setup. A paid Cloudflare account, a configured domain, and service credentials narrow the pool of teams that can activate. The memo provides no buyer geography or paid second-market rollout. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The founder reports hundreds of paying customers for standalone OpenSEO. Importivity reports building a content workflow on an OpenSEO fork and seeing about seven times as many organic clicks over six months. That workflow commitment gives early evidence of fit for the SEO entry point. EveryApp’s 906 GitHub stars show discovery, and the documented install shows how activation is supposed to work. The memo provides no independent accounts traced from discovery through Gateway activation and payment, and no evidence of repeat Gateway use or paid expansion. (Market & Traction, The Idea)

Even assuming strong fit for the SEO workflow, the first link likely to break is conversion from OpenSEO interest to paid Gateway use. If the announced managed tier launches at the proposed $10 per month starting price, shared access could give teams a reason to pay and a reason to add apps later. Free self-hosters pay Cloudflare and connected providers directly. The memo gives no measured onboarding effort, support cost, or incremental hosting cost for the managed tier, so contribution at that price is unknown. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

OpenSEO may satisfy the recurring job on its own. Teams can stay on its hosted service or deploy it independently, and Importivity’s reported outcome came from a fork. The Gateway adds setup requirements, while free self-hosting gives teams that complete setup a route to shared access without paying EveryApp. This could leave the company with a viable standalone SEO product and weak paid demand for the platform. The structural downside gate does not apply because the memo establishes setup friction and alternatives, leaving paid conversion through a standardized managed rollout testable. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a first cohort of independent teams arriving through OpenSEO use the same onboarding flow, pay for the managed Gateway, and keep using it for shared SEO work over their normal content cycle? Account-level records showing that sequence without bespoke founder setup would move the action to Reach out. Standalone OpenSEO payments or Gateway installs alone would leave it at Watch.
