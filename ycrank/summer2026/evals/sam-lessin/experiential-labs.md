# Experiential Labs -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot underwrite the deal before I know the deal. Experiential Labs discloses neither post-money valuation nor competing-term-sheet count, and “YC plus angels affiliated with Meta, DeepMind, Nvidia, and Waabi” does not establish that this is a scarce first check. It may be cheap non-consensus capital, or Demo Day consensus sold through prestigious affiliations. Those are economically different securities.

The product does contain a legitimate experiment: can production traces train a customer-owned 9B model that preserves useful agent behavior while eliminating frontier-model rent? The reported macOS deployment produced 7× speed, 97% lower inference cost, and 50% more simulated-task completions than the untrained baseline. But Experiential Labs has not connected the round proceeds to one dated pass/fail milestone. “Train, route, and continuously improve models” is a product loop; it is not yet a capital plan that tells me what new truth this check buys.

The dark pool is potentially real because each customer owns the production traces, task simulation, and resulting weights. That is more interesting than a generic AI wrapper. Yet the public proof remains one unnamed deployment evaluated on 1,000 generated tasks with an LLM judge and simulated environment. The independent result is considerably less flattering: the trained Qwen model solved 27.5% of trials against its teacher’s 49.0%, and maximum savings reduced measured quality. GitHub’s 337 stars show technical attention, not a purchase, renewal, or embedded workflow that Experiential Labs owns.

The strongest bull case is the team. Kion Fallah led mixed-reality simulation for autonomous-vehicle testing at Waabi; Silen Naihin contributed to AutoGPT, agent benchmarks, continual learning, and evaluations. They have shipped on both sides of the proposed bridge—simulation infrastructure and agent systems—and researched together for six months before founding the company. If anyone at pre-seed can make trace-built training work, these two have unusually direct receipts.

But technical fit does not repeal the AI economics. Together AI already offers continued training, downloadable checkpoints, and production inference; internal ML teams can assemble open models, evaluation, and routing; and Experiential Labs publishes important workflow components itself. Falling model costs can also shrink the customer savings pool faster than specialization improves it. There is no disclosed revenue, contract value, gross margin, burn, retention, or governance posture for ingesting production traces. A 97% customer cost reduction may be excellent customer value while leaving a thin, services-heavy business that needs another round.

I am passing at the evidence and terms available. The known exception for an expensive AI seed would require overwhelming external validation; 337 stars and one simulated, unnamed case study do not qualify. The one condition that flips me is a named customer renewing a paid production deployment after six months with independently measured task quality at least 90% of its frontier fallback and positive gross margin. That would show the traces cause a purchase, the economics survive reality, and the model is more than an out-of-the-money option on clever research.
