# COACH -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: improving speech models now let field-sales organizations observe every in-person meeting, and COACH becomes large if its coaching produces enough measurable conversion lift that managers deploy it across entire teams rather than treat it as conversation analytics. The controlling variable is sustained sales improvement per rep. COACH reports a 32% average conversion-rate lift, 100% pilot conversion, and zero churn, but the lift also appears publicly as 27% and lacks cohort methodology. The mechanism is credible; the threshold and measurement system are not yet rigorous enough for top-quartile conviction.

Demand is the strongest evidence. COACH went from a founder-reported €500,000 ARR with 120% monthly growth to a company-reported €1 million ARR within eight months. Thousands of reps across five large French field-sales companies and 74,473 analyzed meetings demonstrate repeated operational use, not registrations or an aspirational pipeline. The claim of 200 companies does not reconcile cleanly with the five large customers, while 50-plus Google Play downloads suggests enterprise distribution rather than broad bottom-up adoption. Even with those ambiguities, this is unusually strong product pull for a pre-seed company.

The technology shift expands observational coverage: software can replace 100-kilometer manager ride-alongs and analyze meetings that previously disappeared. That lowers the marginal cost of coaching and could improve rep productivity, but COACH has not quantified the customer’s coaching cost, payback period, or net revenue gain. “Better models” is also not a proprietary discontinuity. Rilla and Siro already provide mobile recording, analysis, and coaching, so the market is crystallizing around an established product shape rather than opening uniquely for this team.

The bear case is therefore about value capture, not whether the problem exists. COACH relies on Anthropic for AI and transcription, and its public differentiation—one skill at a time plus a shared sales brain—is product design, not yet an architectural barrier. The 74,473-meeting corpus matters only if outcomes are linked reliably enough to improve recommendations; the dossier provides no validation method, proprietary evaluation loop, or estimate of what it would cost Rilla, Siro, or Gong to reproduce the data. Recording-consent requirements add deployment friction precisely when the company is moving from France into California. A stronger model helps analyze meetings, but it may also compress differentiation among vendors.

The founders partly resolve this concern through execution evidence. Mathieu and Thomas previously operated a 44-rep field-sales company that generated €3.5 million in 14 months, so they lived the workflow and have second-time learning rather than résumé-level familiarity. Mathieu also closed an earlier startup before beginning COACH, while Nicolas contributes a decade of software work and mobile engineering. This is the favorable version of a repeated category bet: a team with direct distribution knowledge entering an already validated market and moving faster than its stage would predict.

I would invest, but as a speed-and-distribution bet rather than a data-moat bet. The likely 36-month outcome is $15–30 million ARR if COACH converts its French proof into repeatable U.S. enterprise expansion; the failure mode is being compressed into a regional feature-equivalent of better-capitalized competitors. My flip condition is verifiable: if a defined U.S. customer cohort does not show at least 90% gross retention and documented conversion improvement after 12 months, I would pass because the controlling variable would have failed rather than merely remained unmeasured.
