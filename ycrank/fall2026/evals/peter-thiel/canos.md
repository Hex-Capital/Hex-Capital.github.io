# Canos -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Canos has a measurable technical claim, but what important truth does it possess that its competitors reject? Its integrated canine cfDNA model reports 90.6% sensitivity at 95.1% specificity in a 303-dog diagnostic analysis. Those numbers make the research worth examining. They do not establish that the marketed assay detects cancer prospectively or that longitudinal measurements change treatment decisions. The dossier supplies neither a named prevailing belief being overturned nor a demonstrated result against it. I leave the secret criterion at its absent-evidence default; I do not treat an undisclosed secret as a disproved one.

The strongest argument for investing is that Canos could turn an initial blood draw into the foundation of a longitudinal diagnostic business. Its research benchmark covers 341 dogs and 896 sequencing runs across four studies; Origin banks a baseline, and OncoTrack addresses dogs already undergoing treatment. Repeated samples linked to outcomes could make each subsequent interpretation more valuable. This is the bull case that matters. But research assembled from existing datasets does not establish exclusive commercial data ownership, and a banked baseline does not by itself produce network effects. CanCan already sells sequencing-based canine blood testing. Canos has a plausible starting segment; it has not demonstrated the mechanism that would let it own that segment.

The founding relationship is more encouraging than the market position. Tsai and Li met in Oxford in 2024 and previously built veterinary AI pathology together at ZYTCA. That is affirmative evidence of shared work, rather than a team assembled around an application deadline. Their molecular-diagnostics and computational-pathology backgrounds also make the technical undertaking credible. Canos fits the operator-origin pattern I find attractive, although the dossier does not establish proprietary company incubation or a founder-discovery network. Full-time status, ownership, compensation and control remain undisclosed. None of the specific founding red flags is documented; neither is complete economic alignment.

Canos has also made concrete distribution choices: San Francisco group draws, Bay Area mobile veterinarians and nationwide collection kits through owners’ veterinarians. These are actual collection routes, deserving more credit than an “enterprise partnerships” slide. Tsai’s earlier product reportedly reached 10,000 clinics, but those are not Canos customers or committed channels. At $699 including the veterinary visit, Origin has an identifiable transaction. Collection, sequencing and interpretation must all fit inside that price; the dossier provides no margin evidence or repeatable acquisition economics. For scale, $100 million in annual sales would require approximately 143,000 tests at that price. That arithmetic describes the task, not evidence that Canos can accomplish it.

The known exceptions do not rescue the investment today. Canos has research validation, but no independently demonstrated technical discontinuity in its commercial assay, persistent paid customer behavior or realized economics that directly resolves the monopoly question. Nor does it exhibit the documented technical reversals or synchronized factory-and-licensing dependencies that would justify an aggressively negative judgment. The distinction matters: this is a credible scientific founding team whose route to durable value capture remains unproved. My pass concerns that investment structure, rather than the ordinary absence of pre-seed revenue.

I would pass with my own money today. One verifiable milestone would reopen the decision: a prospective, paid veterinary-oncology cohort demonstrating actionable relapse lead time over standard monitoring while generating contractually exclusive longitudinal outcome data for Canos. That would connect clinical usefulness to an asset a competing laboratory cannot simply reproduce. Until then, the company may create useful diagnostic information without establishing why it will retain exceptional economic value.
