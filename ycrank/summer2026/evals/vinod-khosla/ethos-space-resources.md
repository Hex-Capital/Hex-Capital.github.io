# Ethos Space Resources -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence could be enormous, but Ethos Space Resources has not quantified it. If lunar regolith becomes landing pads, oxygen, aluminum, and solar-grade silicon, lunar infrastructure stops importing nearly every kilogram from Earth. The dossier gives one useful physical constraint—payload is roughly 2% of launch mass—but no dollars-per-kilogram comparison, required material volumes, power-generation cost curve, or population-scale benefit. A $5.97 billion adjacent market forecast for 2034 does not establish a 100x consequence. It establishes an interesting space supplier.

The technical work is more substantive than the market arithmetic. Ethos reports making material from geologically accurate simulants in a lunar-vacuum chamber and testing it under rocket-plume conditions. That is evidence of a physical process, not a presentation. But there is no measured benchmark against Earth-launched silicon, ICON’s construction approach, or another named incumbent: no energy per kilogram, purity, yield, throughput, equipment mass, or delivered cost. Producing silicon on the Moon is not yet a discontinuity merely because producing it there is difficult.

The risk-removal sequence is incomplete. A completed NASA contract, the Astrolab operating partnership, and a planned 2028 lunar mission are meaningful pre-seed signals. Yet the dossier does not identify which single uncertainty—energy intensity, solar-grade purity, autonomous operation, plume survival, or processing-equipment mass—kills the company first. Nor does it state the cheapest experiment, numerical pass threshold, cost, or stop condition. A lunar mission is a date, not an if/then plan.

The strongest bull case is the team. Ross Centers brings specialized space-resources training; Brogan BamBrogan was SpaceX employee 23 and worked on Falcon and Dragon. They have terrestrial technical evidence, one government contract, and a reported $1 billion of customer LOIs. This resembles the unstated pattern I do like: scarce technical workflows becoming repeatable production platforms. But the material difference is economic proof. The LOIs have no disclosed customers, deposits, pricing, or binding terms, while the organization reportedly contracted from three founders and 14 employees in 2024 to two founders and a team of two. That unexplained discontinuity directly weakens the case for adaptive recruiting and execution.

The terminal problem is Chindia-price parity. Ethos provides no bottom-up model comparing lunar production with Earth launch: equipment delivery, power, utilization, yield, maintenance, purification, and mission failure are all absent. Program dependence on Artemis may be unavoidable, but government-funded development cannot substitute indefinitely for competitive unit economics. The legal uncertainty around resource rights compounds this because installed capacity may arrive before enforceable commercial ownership does.

I pass today. This is a high-technical-risk project attached to an emerging demand pool, not yet an asymmetric option on an undeniable existing one. The one verifiable flip condition is a signed, deposit-backed commercial contract accompanied by audited pilot data showing solar-grade silicon purity and a delivered-cost pathway below Earth-launched silicon at stated lunar throughput. Prove that, and the economic mechanism—not the romance of lunar manufacturing—changes the decision.
