# Petrichor -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Petrichor could remove a real bottleneck: models can propose biology experiments faster than new laboratories can be built. If experimental feedback became radically cheaper and faster, more scientific intelligence could become useful in the physical world. But the dossier supplies laboratory construction timelines measured in years without quantifying the experimental cost, capacity, or population Petrichor could affect. I can describe a consequential future. I cannot yet attribute an orders-of-magnitude consequence to this mechanism.

The important product choice is recording what happens between instruction and result: photographs, raw instrument files, timestamped actions, and failures. Failed experiments could be valuable training examples. The strongest bull case is that scientists can execute unfamiliar protocols while automation remains constrained, allowing Petrichor to discover which experimental records actually improve customer models. That fits my preference for scientific workflows that can become repeatable production platforms. But the dossier establishes the proposed workflow, without a demonstrated learning advantage. Customer ownership and the prohibition on shared-data training also mean I cannot underwrite a pooled scientific-data moat; any accumulating advantage must reside in execution methods and workflow integration.

Emerald Cloud Lab and Strateos already offer remote experimentation. Petrichor therefore needs to establish what their architectures cannot deliver economically. Scientist execution might shorten the path to serving heterogeneous requests, but it also introduces labor and scheduling costs. Neither implication settles the comparison. There is no measured throughput, reproducibility, training improvement, or cost benchmark against these alternatives. Lila’s reported hundreds of thousands of AI-driven experiments support the existence of this workflow, but its $550 million financing is neither Petrichor customer demand nor proof that outsourcing wins. Public pricing and unit economics are absent, so unsubsidized cost competitiveness remains unproven rather than disproven.

The founders’ earlier instrument-data business gives the current direction some continuity. The move from Qokedas’s data offering to wet-lab execution is an observable change in product scope; it does not establish that Wilhelm Hedenskog, Jesper Johansson, and Elias Reinfeldt revised a belief after customer evidence. Their high-school relationship says little about complementary scientific capabilities. I assign neither a recruiting premium nor a familiarity penalty. Likewise, YC Fall 2026 participation is real, but it does not substitute for customer validation. Missing revenue at this stage is ordinary; missing evidence of risk removal leaves the milestone criterion at its default.

I would spend the next dollar testing whether richer experimental records improve a customer’s model enough to justify their full production cost. That is the central uncertainty, ahead of expanding laboratory capacity. The dossier contains no completed test or disclosed threshold, budget, and response to failure. My exception cases require an extraordinary instigator addressing a quantified giant value pool, or demonstrated progress with overwhelming consequence. Neither override is established here. There is also no evidence of exit fixation, permanent subsidy dependence, or founders rejecting contrary evidence; this is a Pass based on unestablished thesis fit, without an active misconduct or category objection.

I pass with my own money today. The bull case deserves a decisive experiment: Petrichor might make human-executed laboratories the fastest route to useful scientific training data. My verifiable flip condition is a paid customer trial demonstrating at least a 5x reduction in fully loaded cost per reproducible model improvement against that customer’s existing experimental workflow, including scientist labor and failed runs. That would establish a technical-economic mechanism worth underwriting. The current dossier establishes a plausible service.
