# Simantic -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Usage-based expansion** is Simantic’s strongest path. Embedded firmware teams can use Pyrite, Cloud Sim, and the GitHub bot to test compiled firmware on each pull request while physical boards are unavailable. The first workload could be a single repository, with expansion into more repositories or test scenarios. That scenario assumes the new workloads generate revenue beyond included credits. The Developer plan charges $100 monthly or $1,000 annually and bundles credits and concurrency. The memo leaves open how runs translate into incremental charges. (*The Idea*)

The workflow could travel across markets wherever teams use supported chips and can meet their data-handling requirements through the available hosting and firmware-storage controls. A different chip or peripheral may require additional modeling and validation. Self-serve signup and the GitHub app provide a plausible route to a first run, but the memo does not trace an independent commercial team from discovery through activation and payment. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

A Waterloo student reported that the simulator matched the physical board in coursework, and Simantic reported that the course used it for automated grading. That supports usefulness in a concrete workflow. Simantic also reported its first satellite customer and more than 1.5 million runs over two months, with 70% average weekly run growth. The satellite customer’s budget and workflow commitment are undisclosed, and the aggregate run count leaves commercial retention and paid expansion unknown. The first unresolved link is whether a firmware team’s recurring test activity turns into additional paid usage. (*Market & Traction*)

### Countercase

Simantic reported that its top 15 users accounted for most runs. A few heavy users could drive the headline growth while staying within included credits. If each expansion requires new peripheral models or silicon validation, engineering effort could rise alongside compute consumption. The reported 3,000-plus compute hours show infrastructure use, while incremental revenue, compute cost, and modeling and support effort per added workload remain unmeasured. That leaves a testable gap and does not trigger the structural downside gate. (*Key Risks*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *The Idea*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent commercial firmware team add a second recurring CI workload that increases its actual bill and generates positive incremental contribution? An invoice and run history over the team’s normal release cadence, alongside measured compute, modeling, and support costs for the added workload, would move the action to **Reach out** if they show repeat paid use and positive contribution. Usage within existing credits or unclear incremental costs would keep the action at **Watch**.
