# Super Powers Mobility (SPM) -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B. Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** The first buyer is a defense prime integrating power into vehicles without sufficient in-house electrical expertise. SPM offers modular packs, power electronics, and integration in one engagement. If the same core system can be qualified across programs with limited engineering, SPM could win an initial vehicle program, grow into fleet orders, and sell additional capacity or power-export equipment. The memo documents the offering and quote-based sales process. Repeatable onboarding and fleet expansion remain untested in the memo. (The Idea; Defensibility)

SPM reports participation in four federal contracts, production bookings into February 2027, and $792,000 in qualified pipeline. It also reports $421,815 in realized revenue in one month, while a separate disclosure describes more than $400,000 in contracted revenue in one month. The memo leaves the relationship between those figures unresolved and provides no shipment or payment records. The reported commitments are a concrete early signal after the pivot. Buyer-level conversion, repeat orders, and current product-market fit remain open. (Market & Traction; Key Facts)

### Countercase

SPM could scale as a custom engineering and manufacturing supplier, with each new program requiring a separate sale, design work, and qualification. SPM sells custom engineering separately and relies on externally sourced cells and a planned production scale-up. Each shipped unit adds cell, assembly, and testing costs, and larger orders could tie up more cash in inventory. In a second geography, the same design would still have to meet local procurement, cell-sourcing, and qualification requirements. Pack reuse and automation could reduce the work per order, but the memo gives no rollout cost breakdown or second-market test. Those gaps support Watch. The Pass gate would require observed evidence of a structural ceiling or proportional costs without a supported path to improvement. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent defense prime outside the U.S. pay for and accept a deployment of SPM’s existing modular system with limited redesign and positive contribution? A paid invoice, buyer acceptance, and a rollout cost breakdown covering sales, integration, qualification, fulfillment, and working capital would move the action to Reach out if they show the same core system can serve the same buyer segment economically in a second market.
