# Amorphic Labs -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what happened when something broke. Operating an arbitrary application, seeding believable data, and recording a clean narrated workflow must produce failures. But the dossier names no failed run, customer constraint, founder-built workaround, or elapsed time. Kelly and Li may be resourceful; their résumés cannot establish it. A decade-long friendship and shared competitions reduce cofounder risk, but they do not satisfy the operative gate.

There is more evidence of technical ambition. Amorphic Labs claims an end-to-end system that researches a prospect, controls the vendor’s application, inserts data, records a workflow, edits it, and generates narration. Its July 2026 terms describe an agent that can type, click, navigate, and alter connected applications, suggesting more than a slide-deck idea. Li’s OpenAI project leadership and ML-infrastructure work make the claim credible enough for intermediate credit. But there is no recorded demonstration, reliability rate, execution threshold, or independently observed output. This is a self-described artifact, not yet a measured proof spike.

Who wants the crude version now? The answer is still a category—software founders, sales leaders, and RevOps leaders—not a named user niche exhibiting pull. The YC launch page and *Company Launch Tracker* coverage disclose no customers, repeated use, revenue, or signed commitments. Subscriptions and metered usage are contemplated, but no public price or transaction shows that buyers value an agent-generated video enough to tolerate review work, credential access, and occasional incorrect actions. Those burdens could become useful schlep. Today they remain risks rather than evidence of a wedge.

The strongest bull case is the team. Two technical founders who have known each other for nearly a decade are attacking a workflow that combines browser control, data generation, video production, and personalization just as computer-use APIs make the combination possible. Meanwhile, Consensus, Reprise, and Walnut have raised approximately $143 million, $82 million, and $56 million, respectively, proving that companies spend heavily around product demos. Amorphic’s generated recording is also materially different from routing prerecorded segments or customizing captured sandboxes. A fast team could exploit that seam before incumbents rebuild around agents.

But the same facts produce the bear case. The funded competitors can add research, narration, and application operation; Amorphic has disclosed no proprietary workflow corpus, correction history, or reliability advantage. Nor does the dossier show the concrete pre-round proof spike that would convert technical pedigree into evidence. I looked for an override in a working product with unmistakable pull or measured performance. None is documented. There is also no evidence that either founder encountered this problem personally, or that customer evidence forced a route change. This currently looks like capable builders arriving at a plausible “AI for sales demos” noun, not founders being dragged toward an urgent problem.

I would pass now. The resourcefulness gate fails, and neither measured technical proof nor user pull replaces it. My 36-month base case is that the team pivots from personalized sales videos into browser-agent testing or demo-environment infrastructure and remains below $1 million ARR, because its strongest evidence is technical capacity while its weakest is an experienced user need. One result would flip me: three named B2B software vendors each paying for and repeatedly sending at least 100 Amorphic-generated videos per month, with a documented failure that the founders corrected in weeks and a resulting improvement in completion or conversion.
