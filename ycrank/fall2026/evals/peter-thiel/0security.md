# 0.security -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

What does 0.security know that Aikido does not? Scheduled testing, persistent repository context, sandboxes and remediation pull requests describe a product architecture. They do not yet establish a secret. The proposition worth testing is whether persistent context lets autonomous agents uncover exploitable attack paths that existing security products systematically miss. The dossier supplies neither a named prevailing belief this contradicts nor a comparative experiment. I assign the absent-evidence default here; a factual dossier’s failure to articulate a secret does not establish that the founders lack one.

The more consequential problem is ownership of the resulting market. Aikido already combines code security and autonomous penetration testing. Meanwhile, 0.security addresses developers and enterprises across several industries without identifying a bounded segment it can dominate. Open source can accelerate adoption, but publishing the harness does not establish proprietary technology, network effects or scale economics. Accumulated investigation history might eventually create an advantage; today that is explicitly an inference. This activates my competitive-overlap red flag: the company can make security better while leaving customers with several adequate substitutes.

The strongest objection to passing is that these founders have already done something more substantial than assemble a fashionable interface. Doruk has cyber-defense experience and independently credited vulnerability research. The reported EY work and the Spotify engineer’s account of scanning almost 30,000 files in under a minute suggest access to real workflows. Making adversarial information legible also fits an unstated pattern in my investing. But my exceptions to weak monopoly evidence require a demonstrated technical discontinuity or persistent customer behavior that directly removes the concern. An advisory credited to a founder does not establish the platform’s comparative performance; scanning speed does not establish exploit discovery; reported organizational work does not establish recurring purchases. The bull case is credible, but its decisive inference remains unverified.

I would not manufacture a founding-team objection. Doruk and Fabian attended FHNW and met through their former girlfriends; this is not evidence of convenience matching for a YC application. Nor does it establish prior joint execution, full-time status or economic alignment. Those missing facts receive the prescribed default. Their reported exits have undisclosed terms and cannot substitute for current ownership evidence. Distribution is further along: the public harness has 526 stars and 58 forks, free onboarding exists, and founders offer enterprise consultations. That is an operating acquisition route, although conversion, sales cycles and delivery costs remain unknown.

The $30 monthly subscription, additional usage charges and custom enterprise pricing describe one coherent revenue engine. They do not yet provide margins or account-expansion arithmetic supporting a defining outcome. The claimed reach across three billion devices is downstream software exposure, not a customer base. I pass on the present evidence because market ownership is weakly supported, not because pre-seed revenue is undisclosed. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: an independent customer-run benchmark in a defined application class showing that 0.security discovers at least ten times as many distinct, reproducible exploitable vulnerabilities as Aikido and directly prompted coding agents at the same testing cost. That would turn an architectural difference into evidence of proprietary technical advantage.
