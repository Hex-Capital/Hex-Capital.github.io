# Coasty -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Coasty’s 82.81% OSWorld Verified score reveals a schlep-heavy, chemistry-unproven startup: Coasty has built unusually good machinery for surviving broken legacy software, but shared attendance at Purdue is nearly all the dossier says about how Prateek Jannu and Nitish Kovuru work together. The unpleasantness is the attraction. Pop-ups, payer portals, focus changes, audit trails, and recovery are what software-only founders quietly route around. Coasty looks strongest where the work becomes grubby and weakest where I would normally rely on years of cofounder history to survive that grubbiness.

**Coinbase** (2012) is the high rung for schlep blindness because compliance was not an incidental burden; enduring it became the advantage. Coasty shares that mechanism when Coasty offers to build proofs of concept inside accounting, healthcare, tax, and compliance software, then verifies outcomes and records replayable trails. The analogy stops at production. Coasty’s healthcare demonstration used mock portals, and Coasty’s terms allow customer content and session data to pass through its AI and infrastructure providers. Coinbase had already run into the real regulatory wall. Coasty has built an impressive vehicle for approaching one.

**Coinbase** (2012) is also the dangerous low-rung exception on founder configuration: a solo founder ordinarily creates a single point of failure, but Brian Armstrong’s hand-built Android wallet supplied extraordinary evidence that overrode it. Coasty has the preferred pair, yet Coasty supplies only Purdue attendance as evidence of the relationship. Two names on a YC page are not the same as the Collisons hacking commerce together. Prateek’s Stanford departure and Nitish’s prior agent work are individually encouraging, but Coasty has not shown the shared-history signal that makes a pair more than arithmetic.

Thousands of active users and paying customers across four continents could compound Coasty’s resourcefulness over the next 18 months because every failed workflow produces another chance to improve recovery. Coasty also shipped a public benchmark repository, reached 82.81% verified performance, and changed its positioning between March and July instead of remaining attached to the original “AI employee” story. Those are animal tracks, though not yet cereal boxes: the dossier contains no named customer won through an absurd manual stunt and no revenue figure generated before funding. Coasty looks persistent and fast, but not yet relentlessly resourceful in the special sense.

Nitish Kovuru’s prior agentic-sales-automation work and Prateek Jannu’s fault-tolerant ML pipeline experience put Coasty near the problem, but neither proves that Coasty began as a tool they personally needed. That distinction matters. An organic idea keeps generating correct product decisions when customers disagree; a merely fashionable one drifts toward whichever demonstration looks best. Coasty would flip this score into the top tier if Prateek or Nitish could identify a legacy workflow they personally operated, the repeated failure that annoyed them, and the primitive internal tool they built before anyone requested a proof of concept.

**Dropbox** (2007) converted a technical demonstration into conviction before market evidence existed. Coasty’s closest parallel is the independently confirmed 82.81% OSWorld result and the replayable benchmark repository, not Prateek’s credentials or Nitish’s Columbia vision coursework. That is real hacker evidence. Coasty falls short of the Dropbox rung because Prateek’s cited open-source project has 111 stars and Nitish has no public repositories; Coasty has produced a formidable artifact without yet revealing a deep pre-company hacker trail.

Thousands of paying users, combined with Coasty’s weak evidence of organic founder pain, lead to a specific 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Coasty will have narrowed from a horizontal computer-use API into healthcare payer-portal automation and reached $3 million to $8 million in annual recurring revenue. Coasty’s recovery and audit machinery should matter more inside one ugly vertical than as a generic layer priced at $0.03 to $0.10 per action. Customers will teach Coasty the organic problem the founders did not clearly begin with.

UiPath’s $1.43 billion of FY2025 revenue supplies the stinging bear case: Coasty may be a benchmark lead wrapped around commercially available models, while Coasty’s “thousands” claim may belong partly to the earlier AI-employee product rather than the current developer API. Foundation models improve, UiPath copies recovery, and regulated buyers reject Coasty’s data-processing terms. What keeps me on the other side is concrete shipping: 82.81% verified versus the cited 67.1% for UiPath, paying users on four continents, and a willingness to enter legacy workflows incumbents have still not made reliable. If Coasty cannot show by July 2027 three current-product customers that each retained it for six months while running at least 10,000 regulated workflow actions per month, I would flip from Invest to Pass.
