# Theo -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Theo’s strongest path. An established site builder such as Wix could buy Theo’s hosted SEO publishing workflow and customer base if ownership generated more profit from paid upgrades and retention than an internal build or a partnership. The memo documents the hosted workflow and paid Starter and Pro plans. Theo reports that Buy My Lunch’s Google clicks rose from 131 to 929 over 90 days. That case shows the size of one reported gain, while its effect on customer retention and buyer economics remains unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A six-month paid Starter and Pro cohort measuring organic-search gains, renewals, and publishing costs would test whether the workflow creates durable value at attractive margins. The live product makes that test plausible at seed. Paid cohort size, burn, and runway are undisclosed. A buyer could keep the service on Theo’s hosting, with customer contract assignment and continued Google access still to verify. Customers own their domains. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash target within eight years. At 3%, the holder needs $83.3 million of distributable equity, or $88.3 million of enterprise value with illustrative net debt of $5 million. The equity hurdle ranges from $62.5 million at 4% ownership to $125 million at 2%. With the same illustrative debt and simple pro rata allocation before taxes, a $25 million sale in year eight returns $600,000 (1.2×), and a $100 million sale returns $2.85 million (5.7×). The memo gives no basis for a realizable sale range or adjustments for Theo’s actual debt, fees, or preferences. (Key Facts)

If paid growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. The memo supplies no evidence of eligible secondary demand, IPO readiness, cash generation to fund a sponsor buyout, or excess cash for dividends or buybacks. Any wind-down recovery would depend on assets left after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Wix already offers AI site creation and in-editor SEO, and Durable already automates blog research and drafting. Those products make internal development a credible alternative, and Theo’s white-label offering gives buyers a reason to explore partnership. The reported build count can include free drafts, and the launch uplift claim omits its cohort and baseline. The Pass gate remains open because these facts weaken the reason to buy while leaving buyer willingness and realizable price unresolved. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Theo’s paid SEO cohorts support an acquisition price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? Cohort data showing sustained search gains, renewals, and delivery costs, together with a buyer-specific estimate of the profit from owning the workflow, would move the action to Reach out if they support a sale range that clears the applicable proceeds hurdle.
