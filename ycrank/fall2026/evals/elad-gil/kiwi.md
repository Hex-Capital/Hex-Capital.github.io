# Kiwi -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Kiwi’s market depends on whether increasingly capable GTM agents make reliable business context a separate purchasing priority for GTM operators. The controlling variable is the reduction in human correction time attributable to better data. The dossier connects unreliable inputs to operational work, but does not establish how much of that work Kiwi removes. That distinction determines whether this becomes an infrastructure budget or a feature inside an existing automation platform.

The strongest argument against passing is Kiwi’s architecture. Versioned records, SQL traces, historical replay, and recorded human decisions address problems that better models do not automatically eliminate. A stronger model still needs to know which customer record was valid when an action occurred. If Kiwi retains that state and the evaluation loop, model improvements could increase its usefulness. These documented capabilities earn substantial workflow credit, although customer deployment depth and switching costs remain unmeasured. This is a more credible product boundary than simply formatting model output.

The harder question is who captures the economics. Clay reported $100 million ARR in December 2025 and now describes company context and agents learning from outcomes. That establishes an incumbent with distribution into an overlapping workflow; it does not establish demand for Kiwi. My tendency to back infrastructure across an AI transition supports examining this layer, but an increasingly crystallized GTM market requires a specific reason customers buy it separately. The dossier provides no measured labor savings, implementation costs, or pricing with which to establish that reason. The inferred why-now therefore receives the absent-evidence default for economic change.

Melanie Chen’s experience makes the problem unusually credible. Her report of spending 20 hours correcting agent work in one week is evidence of personally encountering the pain. Founding engineering and post-sales work at Chalk also provide startup operating experience; Bill Qin’s Chalk SQL work is relevant technical context. I give credit for that learning and personal need, without inferring motivation from MIT, CMU, or Jane Street. The team could understand the failure modes better than a general agent builder. It has not yet shown that customers will pay materially to resolve them through Kiwi.

More than 100 discovery conversations are research, and order-form provisions describe how purchases could occur. Neither establishes repeat usage or actual revenue. Those omissions receive the specified pre-seed default, without treating them as failed adoption. The research-stage exception does not change my decision: relevant data engineering expertise is present, but the dossier does not establish frontier research and a capital path that substitute for commercial pull. Nor is there evidence of frozen-model dependence, premature secondary liquidity, or an unsupported exclusive-data claim. The concern is value capture, rather than an active founder red flag.

I would pass on a personal check today. Kiwi has a credible workflow asset, but the market thesis remains less demonstrated than the architecture. One verifiable flip condition would be a 90-day cohort report showing five independent paying GTM teams sustaining at least a 50% reduction in correction hours after including Kiwi setup and maintenance time. That would directly test whether the reliability layer creates a budget worth owning.
