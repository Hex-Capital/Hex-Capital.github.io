# Talos -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact about Talos is what happened when something broke. Amitav Kohli has worked on power engineering and predictive maintenance at Constellation. That makes me interested in what he encountered there. But the dossier supplies no specific obstacle, founder-built workaround, or elapsed time. I cannot turn relevant experience into evidence of resourcefulness. This is an unanswered question about the founders, not evidence that they are passive.

The strongest evidence is the FieldSense demonstration: 304 magnetic readings mapped around a condensate pump. Talos has something concrete to show, which matters more than its founders’ university names. But collecting a spatial map and predicting a transformer failure are different achievements. There is no independently verified detection accuracy, false-alarm rate, or warning lead time. My pattern of backing technical teams after a numerical proof applies only partially here. The number measures readings collected, not diagnostic success. I give the demonstration intermediate technical credit without treating it as validated predictive maintenance.

Kohli’s prior predictive-maintenance work gives Talos a credible connection to the problem. Kevin Hwang’s research on physiological waveforms using over 100 TB of ICU data also suggests relevant technical tools, though that dataset is not Talos’s proprietary asset. The opportunity could lie in the unpleasant work between a sensor and a maintenance decision: getting equipment access, checking alerts against actual faults, and earning an operator’s trust. But the dossier does not establish that a particular encounter in Kohli’s work caused this product, or that the team has mastered those burdens. Nor does it document a rejected hypothesis and a subsequent product change. The pump demonstration alone proves neither an organic origin nor adaptability.

The strongest argument against passing is that these founders may already have the right combination: direct power-industry exposure, waveform expertise, and an inspectable sensing demonstration, aimed at assets whose replacements can take years. A crude product could be valuable well before it becomes comprehensive. Yet who is pulling it back into use? Talos names no returning operator or customer deployment. Its 95 LinkedIn followers and YC participation cannot answer that question. Pricing is unpublished, and recurring monitoring revenue is an inferred business model. Meanwhile, Oktogrid already offers magnetic-adhesion sensing, so easy attachment by itself will not make an operator choose Talos. The useful distinction must emerge in maintenance outcomes.

I would pass on this evidence. The dossier establishes no affirmative red flag about partner dependence, premature delegation, or resistance to feedback; unknown cofounder history is simply unknown. But the exceptions that could overcome the missing founder evidence require a compelling working product, unmistakable user pull, or independently validated technical results. This demonstration does not yet supply that override. My one flip condition is a documented transformer field trial in which the founders overcome a named deployment obstacle, correctly flag a subsequently verified fault, and earn continued paid monitoring from that operator. That single episode would connect the people, the instrument, and the need. Today those remain separate pieces.
