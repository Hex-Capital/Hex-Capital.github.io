# Clean -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Clean has evidence that sellers will pay for better buying signals. It has not established what becomes radically cheaper if its technology works. A $4.75 billion intent-data market measures spending on a category; it does not quantify the resources Clean could release or the population it could serve. Customs shipments, permits, loans and contracts could reveal demand that browsing behavior misses. The consequential question is whether those records make commercial matching dramatically more efficient. The dossier does not yet answer it.

The strongest argument for investing is already commercial. Clean reports $240K ARR before YC, and Vivameda reports $60K in contracts within two weeks, with reply rates rising from approximately 4% to 19%. That is a 4.75x improvement in a customer outcome. At pre-seed, it deserves attention. But replies do not establish prediction accuracy, and a testimonial does not isolate the contribution of buying-window detection from contact quality, targeting or message changes. I give the working product and customer result modest technical credit. I cannot turn them into a demonstrated discontinuity against Clay or Apollo.

The founders provide the clearest evidence of adaptability. The September 2026 EO interview documents a summer pivot from developer infrastructure to sales intelligence. They changed the route rather than merely describing themselves as flexible. Pavan’s Cisco sales experience supplies relevant workflow context, alongside the team’s software and research backgrounds. What remains unproven is which evidence forced that revision and whether they can recruit someone specifically capable of solving the prediction problem. A documented pivot earns credit; exceptional learning rate and magnet recruiting remain unestablished.

The largest kill risk is specific: does an operational event predict demand for **this seller’s product**, or merely identify a company doing something? The dossier names no experiment, spending limit or pass/fail threshold addressing that question, so milestone discipline receives the absent-evidence default. Clean also discloses neither pricing nor the data, inference and human-research costs required to serve an account. Revenue establishes willingness to pay. It does not establish an unsubsidized cost advantage.

My actual investing pattern leaves room for execution-driven software; scientific novelty is not an absolute requirement. Clean’s traction therefore challenges a reflexive pass. But the exceptions require an unusually compelling consequence or founder-specific reason to purchase the option despite missing technical proof. Neither is established here. The relevant red flag is overlapping research automation: Clay announced context-aware agents and signal monitoring in September 2026. Clean claims its own datasets, but exclusive rights and a purchase-outcome learning loop remain unverified. This creates a credible competitive question without proving that Clean is merely a wrapper.

I pass at 31/100. The founders may build a valuable sales business; the evidence does not yet justify my personal bet on a discontinuity. One verifiable result would flip my decision: an independently audited, prospective comparison against Clay or Apollo showing at least 5x more realized customer gross profit per dollar of fully loaded prospecting cost across multiple unrelated customers, using signals recorded before the purchases. That would connect the operational-record mechanism to both technical advantage and economic consequence. Today, those links remain open.
