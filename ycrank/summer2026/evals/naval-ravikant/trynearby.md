# TryNearby -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The founders learned creator distribution by operating inside it. Yousef Abdelfattah joined FaZe Clan at fourteen and helped build it through a public listing. Obaida Albaroudi and Ahmad Ibrahim previously built Clipping Exe, reportedly growing a consumer app from $360K to $2M ARR while tracking 10B-plus views across tens of thousands of videos. Those are shipped systems and commercial outcomes in the exact domain. The résumé is merely the residue; the action is the evidence.

TryNearby has already converted that knowledge into a functioning local marketplace: 120-plus restaurants paying $399 monthly after ten months, 37% month-over-month growth, more than 90% monthly retention, and 2,500 creator videos producing roughly 35M views. The implied run rate is about $575K annually before accounting for churn or discounts. This does not require a Known Exception for absent monetization or adoption. Restaurants are paying directly, and approximately half of new restaurant customers reportedly arrive through owner referrals.

The compelling mechanism is the movement of fragmented creator supply into a coordinated local network. Thousands of creators provide availability, travel, preference, and rejection signals through more than 10,000 agent-managed texts each month. More supply should improve scheduling and matching; more participating restaurants give creators more opportunities; owner referrals lower distribution costs. This fits the pattern of making disconnected offline supply searchable and transactable. But it is not yet a proven flywheel: the dossier shows referrals and participation, not city-level liquidity cohorts demonstrating that each added participant measurably raises retention or match completion for the next.

If a competitor copied every line of code tomorrow, TryNearby would retain its creator relationships, interaction history, restaurant base, and some local density. It would not retain an impenetrable advantage. Hyper and Tablevert already offer automated creator campaigns to restaurants, while TikTok and Instagram control the underlying discovery channels. The proprietary asset is emerging data and coordination density, but no evidence yet shows that the data produces better outcomes than a competitor could obtain by recruiting the same creators. Software schedules the marketplace; it does not constitute the moat.

The strongest bear case is severe. Greater than 90% monthly retention may still permit churn that compounds badly, $399 leaves limited room for creator incentives and human operations, every new city must bootstrap both sides, and platform or FTC policy changes can impair the product without TryNearby’s consent. The published revenue testimonials lack independent attribution. No founder veto appears in the dossier—there is no evidence of stakeholder mistreatment, artificial deadlines, reassurance-seeking, or integrity problems—but execution quality cannot repeal marketplace economics.

My 36-month base case is $5M–$10M ARR across multiple restaurant markets while the company remains primarily a restaurant-marketing network rather than proving the broader “every local business” market. The cited $34.2B influencer-platform TAM does not establish a causal path from restaurant visits into other local categories, and no local-restaurant SAM is supplied. I would still invest my own money because exact-domain founders, direct revenue, referrals, and early network density create a bounded-downside option. My one flip condition to Strong Invest is verifiable replication: two markets outside Southern California each reaching 100 paying locations within six months of launch while maintaining at least 90% monthly retention and majority referral-driven acquisition.
