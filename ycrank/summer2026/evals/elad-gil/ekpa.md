# Ekpa -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Cheaper inference now makes continuous portfolio research feasible for busy retail investors; the controlling variable is whether enough users will pay and remain retained for a cross-broker research layer when their broker offers a bundled assistant. Ekpa has not yet measured that variable. The dossier reports no subscription price, paid conversion, retention cohort, or threshold at which consumer economics work.

The market structure is already unfavorable. Robinhood Cortex can analyze and enact trades inside the brokerage, SoFi acquired Composer, and PortfolioPilot provides regulated portfolio advice. These products possess distribution, transaction access, or regulatory infrastructure that Ekpa lacks. The projected $6.8 billion AI-in-asset-management market does not establish a consumer research SAM, and aggregation across brokerages is a feature rather than a structural barrier.

The bull case is more credible than a generic AI-finance pitch. A greater than 280-fold decline in inference cost makes continuous processing of filings, news, and economic data economical. Ekpa connects live holdings, maintains portfolio context, runs backtests, and has produced 2,400 analyses across more than $3.4 million of company-reported connected assets. At pre-seed, that demonstrates real curiosity and some product use. But connected assets are not assets under management, the dossier does not identify how many users generated those analyses, and a company-reported 1.5% one-week return supplies neither retention nor validated performance.

The workflow is partially owned but stops at the point where value compounds. Ekpa imports positions through SnapTrade and produces alerts and research, yet it neither executes trades nor documents a feedback loop from recommendations to decisions and outcomes. A stronger model improves the analysis while making the same capability easier for brokerages to reproduce. The proposed longitudinal-data moat remains hypothetical, with no evidence that its data would be expensive or slow for a scaled competitor to replicate.

Anant Asthana’s agentic-pipeline experience and Yuga Patel’s CS, economics, and investing background fit the product, but the dossier documents neither second-time learning nor costly commitment that distinguishes this from a capable student project. The research-stage exception does not apply: Ekpa is not demonstrating scarce frontier technology, a rare model-building team, or a capital-intensive technical breakthrough. Regulatory ambiguity also creates an awkward tradeoff—remaining “research and education” limits workflow ownership, while offering compensated personalized advice may trigger adviser obligations.

My 36-month base case is that Ekpa pivots from a consumer subscription toward B2B research infrastructure for RIAs and smaller financial platforms rather than reaching $1 million in consumer ARR. I pass today. One result would flip the decision: a cohort of at least 1,000 paying users retaining above 60% after twelve months despite access to broker-native AI assistants.
