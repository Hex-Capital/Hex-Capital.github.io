# horizontal. -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years from now, a late shipment should trigger a factory to check substitute materials, rearrange production, and tell the customer what changed before someone starts an email chain. horizontal.’s proposed agents touch those exact actions. But getting there requires reliable execution across factory systems, then permission to act without constant supervision. The dossier establishes the intended workflows; it does not establish that either transition has happened. I can picture the future. I cannot yet point to the little artifact proving horizontal. is bringing it into existence.

The founders give me a reason to keep looking. Niklas Marxer reports bringing agentic AI into more than 150 industrial companies, and Jakob Hermeler coauthored research on autonomous quadrotor collision recovery. They met doing robotics research at Stanford. That is relevant technical and industrial context, stronger than two people discovering manufacturing in an accelerator application. Still, the 150-company claim lacks deployment detail, and the October 2026 paper establishes research ability rather than a dated history of obsession with factory coordination. I see technical credibility; independent commercial command and the ability to recruit first believers remain less established.

Why does a production planner have to become the human connection between email, spreadsheets, materials, and quality systems? That is horizontal.’s useful systems question. Its incremental approach—start with one process inside existing systems—could return time and decision-making capacity to plant staff without requiring a factory-wide replacement. But the dossier identifies coordination labor, not a quantified toll or an artificial restriction. It does not show who captures an avoidable rent or how much control changes hands. There is a concrete operating wound here, with less evidence of the institutional release that particularly attracts my money.

The strongest objection to passing is that these founders may already possess the hard part: familiarity with industrial deployments and an understanding that adoption starts with one manageable process. That could produce the concealed infrastructure I repeatedly look for beneath a visible application. Yet plant-specific procedures, integration mappings, and validated execution histories are still hypothetical assets in this dossier. Siemens and Pelico already address connected industrial orchestration. horizontal. needs evidence that it controls something those platforms cannot readily supply. Its statement that it works with international manufacturers does not establish repeated production use; the seven-day-old launch, 485 LinkedIn followers, and UnternehmerTUM participation establish visibility and access.

My pre-revenue investment in **CosmicBrain AI** is the relevant exception: industrial ambition can earn a check before revenue when a functioning prototype and signed industrial proofs of concept make the path tangible. horizontal. shares the technical setting, but the dossier does not supply equivalent product evidence. Nor does it establish a privacy violation, confused leadership, or dependence on one closed model supplier; those red flags are unresolved rather than demonstrated. I pass with my own money today. One verifiable flip condition would be a named manufacturer’s production audit showing horizontal. repeatedly resolving disruptions across existing systems, with fewer planner interventions and documented authorization and rollback controls. That would turn the proposed future into something I could inspect.
