# Control Seat -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first underwriting facts are missing: proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. Summer 2026 YC funding does not prove an auction, but it does not prove cheap, non-consensus capital either. More importantly, Control Seat has not named the card this round flips. “Build faster because AI writes software” is a technology tailwind, not a finite experiment with a deadline and a truth-revealing result.

The founders are the affirmative signal. Jack Grodnick has worked on controls and SCADA at Tesla and SpaceX, then on Amazon GenAI serving more than 1.5 million employees. Warren Shepard brings SpaceX experience across networks, manufacturing, and flight software plus Microsoft security. That is a credible outsider-insider bridge between operational technology and modern software—not two students discovering factories from a market report. The ability to sit above existing SCADA through OPC UA and MQTT also attacks switching friction without requiring a reckless rip-and-replace.

The bull case is that integration work becomes Control Seat’s proprietary dark pool. The Battex Chile deployment demonstrates that the team can remotely monitor and control a real battery system, and every subsequent plant could add configuration knowledge, operational history, and trusted relationships. But one named deployment is not yet evidence that this workflow caused a paid purchase, survived renewal, or produced reusable data. FactoryTalk and Siemens WinCC support remain “coming soon,” while Seeq, Litmus, and Augury have collectively raised at least $567.6 million to occupy overlapping parts of the stack.

The economics are equally unresolved. Customized cloud, on-premise, integration, support, and SLA pricing could create a durable software-plus-services business. It could also create an excellent two-person systems integrator whose complexity rises with every installation. There is no disclosed revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or deployment payback period. Delete the Series A and I cannot yet tell whether Control Seat compounds from customer cash or finances bespoke engineering indefinitely.

The AI framing cuts both ways. Natural-language investigation and faster software development may improve the product, but competitors receive the same development-cost reduction. Control Seat’s defensible base must therefore be reliable plant operation, reusable integrations, and retained customer workflow—not anomaly-detection features or 341 LinkedIn followers. The dossier also identifies two co-founders and CEO/CTO titles but does not establish who has final authority when product velocity conflicts with industrial safety.

My 36-month base case is a real but integration-heavy business with $1 million–$3 million in annualized revenue across fewer than 20 deployments, with services still carrying much of the economics rather than a repeatable control-software platform. I am passing today. I would flip if Control Seat documented five named plants that remained paid and live for six months at greater than 70% gross margin under a round priced below a $10 million post-money valuation; that single result would prove purchase causality, repeatability, and capital efficiency rather than merely technical competence.
