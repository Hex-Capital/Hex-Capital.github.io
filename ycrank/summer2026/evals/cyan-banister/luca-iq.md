# Luca IQ -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032: tax preparation is mostly machine ingestion, deterministic calculation, and exception review, while CPAs choose their own interfaces instead of living inside four desktop-era vendors. The steps from here are legible—broaden return coverage, finish the security layer, integrate firm workflows, and make the API dependable enough to become regulated plumbing. Luca IQ has one genuine leak from that future: its engine completed a customer tax season before receiving federal 1040 e-file authorization in July 2026. The dossier does not yet prove that Luca IQ, rather than another API vendor, captures the resulting platform.

The most compelling fact is not “AI for tax.” It is that approximately 95% of firms reportedly file through four legacy vendors, forcing manual re-entry and closed workflows. One customer says Luca IQ eliminated 1,100 hours, reduced administrative preparation by 57%, and moved intake to review-ready in 30 minutes. That names the broken arrangement, measures its labor rent, and shows the agency returned: firms and developers can build around a source-level audit trail and deterministic engine instead of adapting themselves to an incumbent’s interface.

Angelo Policicchio understands the wound. He is a CPA who worked in public accounting at EY and forensic accounting at the FBI; Akash Sadashivapeth supplies engineering and data-science depth. The roles are complementary, and Angelo is clearly identified as CEO, so I see no leadership shell game in the public record. But credentials are not obsession. I cannot find a dated pre-company experiment, an operating incident that became the specification, a shipped artifact predating Luca IQ, or evidence that either founder recruited believers before the market validated the category. This is excellent founder-market fit with thin first-believer proof.

The artifact is real enough to clear the concept stage: customers used it through a tax season, hundreds of firms applied for the pilot, and IRS Assurance Testing validates more than a narrated demo. Still, every usage figure comes from Luca IQ’s own materials, with no paid-customer count, cohort, renewal rate, or return volume. A CPA firm risking a filing season is costly behavior; an application is not. I therefore credit the working system while refusing to turn one testimonial into demonstrated repeat demand.

The bull case stings because this is beneath the visible AI wave, not merely another chat interface. AI and human review establish inputs, but Luca IQ controls deterministic calculation, diagnostics, audit trails, and e-file transmission for CPA firms, consolidators, and fintech developers. The bear case is that April and Column Tax already expose filing infrastructure, Luca IQ covers only federal 1040s publicly, and SOC 2 Type II remains unfinished. Tax data makes consent, custody, and surveillance architecture a gate, not paperwork. No severe failure is documented, but the missing completed security posture prevents me from treating accumulated filing data as a moat.

I am neutral today. My 36-month base case is that Luca IQ reaches roughly $5–10 million ARR and is acquired by Thomson Reuters as an API engine rather than becoming an independent tax platform: the workflow repair is strong, but the controlled bottleneck is not yet unique. One result flips me to investing: at least 25 unaffiliated CPA firms renew for a second paid tax season while collectively filing 10,000-plus returns through Luca IQ with no material compliance or data-custody incident. That would turn professional interest into the strange, expensive behavior I can underwrite.
