# Palisade -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: computer-use models now let online marketplaces deploy persistent sales agents, operators buy when those agents create incremental transactions, and Palisade becomes large only if attributable gross profit exceeds the combined cost of inference, integration, and human oversight. The dossier establishes the mechanism—remember abandoned searches, re-engage buyers, navigate listings, and update CRM records—but not the threshold economics. More than $2.7 million in GMV is meaningful; without an attribution baseline, take rate, or agent cost, it does not yet prove the controlling belief.

The strongest evidence is product pull before the story is polished. Shorebreak and Vaulted Atelier are live in production, and the reported GMV arrived during the Summer 2026 batch. That is substantially better than an LOI or demo. I discount it because Palisade does not disclose revenue, repeat usage, retention, or how much of that GMV was genuinely incremental rather than merely touched by the software. Still, two production deployments and transaction volume from a one-person company register as real pre-seed signal.

This is also more than a chat wrapper. Palisade connects catalog, pricing, booking, PMS, and CRM systems; retains user histories; communicates over email and SMS; and spans buyer, seller, and staff workflows. Better models should improve navigation and task completion while those integrations, permissions, and histories remain valuable. The counterpoint is sharp: Rep AI, Constructor, and Nosto already own adjacent customer relationships, while Anthropic exposes the underlying computer-use capability. Persistent histories are not a moat unless Palisade can quantify the time, access, or contractual restrictions preventing an incumbent from reproducing them.

The technology shift is credible but economically incomplete. API-accessible computer use arrived in October 2024 and makes interface-level marketplace automation feasible. Yet human approval remains necessary for repricing, listing, interface, and code changes, while execution errors and customer-data exposure create operating costs. There is no quantified reduction in sales labor, conversion improvement, gross margin, or deployment time. This may expand adoption, but the dossier does not show that it changes the market’s margin structure.

The bull case is that $2.7 million in GMV already answers the hard question: marketplaces will entrust an unfinished agent with revenue-generating workflows, and the first integration becomes distribution for seller operations, merchandising, and staff automation. That could make the product unusually broad before the category crystallizes. The exception case does not rescue the current underwriting, however: Salama has directly relevant Amazon and Sage AI experience, but the dossier shows neither rare frontier-model capability nor a structural barrier warranting investment without better economics. It also provides no evidence of prior startup learning, costly commitment, or the desperation required to score founder biography above the default.

I would monitor rather than write a personal check today. The market gate remains unresolved even though the product and usage signals are promising. My flip condition is one controlled cohort across at least five named marketplaces showing more than 10% incremental gross profit after inference and human-review costs, with the customers still paying after 90 days. That would convert reported GMV into a falsifiable market variable and show that Palisade owns an economic workflow rather than a feature incumbents can absorb.
