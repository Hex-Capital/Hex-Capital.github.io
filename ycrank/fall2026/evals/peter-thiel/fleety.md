# Fleety -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

What does Fleety know about dispatch that ServiceTitan has misunderstood? A testable proposition would be that an independent system combining call transcripts, equipment records, and technician skills produces materially better assignments than the native CRM. Fleety has not yet established that proposition. ServiceTitan already optimizes assignments using predicted job value and technician performance. Revenue-aware dispatch is therefore an existing competitive premise; the potential secret lies in an advantage Fleety has yet to demonstrate. That distinction makes monopoly formation the decisive weakness.

The initial market is concrete: plumbing and HVAC operators managing complex schedules, with some handling 100-plus jobs daily. Compatibility with ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, and Jobber creates a useful opening across platforms. But compatibility does not establish proprietary technology, exclusive data, or a compounding network effect. Fleety depends on access to systems whose owners can supply overlapping functionality. The dossier’s inferred learning advantage remains a hypothesis. This is affirmative incumbent substitution risk, beyond the ordinary absence of a pre-seed moat.

The strongest objection to passing is that these founders have actually done the work. Ben Zhou built a roofing business and Timrep; Ariyaman Molanorouzi operated property-management automation covering 3,000-plus units. Both worked as dispatchers. Their weeks inside customer shops fit my unstated preference for companies formed through operating experience: difficult implementation can reveal opportunities invisible to a platform’s product organization. Albert Nahman, Ace Plumbing, and Apex Plumbing provide named customer references. That combination makes Fleety more credible than a generic AI application. It still does not establish that customer knowledge becomes an asset competitors cannot reproduce.

The founding structure deserves an unresolved judgment. Meeting as students in Montreal supplies some relationship history, but the dossier does not document prior joint execution, full-time status, ownership, or economic exposure. I see no evidence of the part-time-founder or investor-dominated-cap-table red flags, and I will not invent them. Separately, demo selling and onsite configuration identify a real distribution route. Whether weeks of implementation fit the contract economics remains unproved: subscription terms exist, but prices, margins, and sales cycles are undisclosed. The founders’ earlier six-figure businesses establish experience; they supply no arithmetic for Fleety’s eventual scale.

I would pass on investing today. My known exceptions require evidence that directly resolves the failed gate: an independently demonstrated technical breakthrough, persistent customer behavior proving an unusual advantage, or realized economics powerful enough to overcome the concern. Fleety’s references justify attention but do not yet resolve incumbent substitution. One verifiable flip condition would be a controlled, multi-shop comparison showing that an exclusive technician-outcome dataset gives Fleety a sustained, material gross-profit advantage over native dispatch optimization. That would connect its operating knowledge to something it can own.
