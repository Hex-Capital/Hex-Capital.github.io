# Rindler -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what happened when a real customer’s portal broke. Rindler says it centrally repairs routes after layout changes and falls back from Kernel to Browserbase, but the dossier does not name the blocker, the founders’ action, the elapsed time, or the customer outcome. Those are product capabilities, not yet evidence that Arthur De Los Santos and Michael Serrano are relentlessly resourceful. Their MIT CSAIL work and Serrano’s Roblox experience make them plausible builders. They do not answer the first question I ask about founders.

The strongest evidence is the thing they have already built. Rindler turns Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, and state registries into typed, verifier-gated actions, with encrypted session reuse and centrally maintained mappings. The 38-run benchmark reports 4× faster completion, 3× lower cost, and 2× fewer failures than Browser Use. I discount an internal benchmark over two grocery tasks, but not to zero: this is functioning infrastructure with a measured result, an MCP endpoint, and paid checkout. The founders have shipped before obtaining SOC 2, enterprise customers, or permission from incumbent browser vendors.

This also matches one of my less explicit preferences: programmable interfaces that let technical users compose systems. But the other half of that pattern is missing. There is no pre-round proof spike—no named customer, paid deployment, repeated workflow usage, or developers forcing Rindler into an unexpected use case. Pricing at $100 and $1,000 per month proves that the company knows how it wants to charge, not that anyone pays. LinkedIn followers and two-star repositories are almost irrelevant. Who wants this now, and what do they repeatedly make it do?

The opportunity does contain real schlep. Authentication, bot defenses, session custody, changing layouts, website terms, and eventual security procurement are exactly the work that makes “just use a browser agent” less true than it sounds. Yet the dossier never connects this burden to a problem either founder personally encountered. Without that origin, Rindler could be an organic discovery or merely a well-constructed “agents need web access” thesis. Nor is there evidence of a rejected route and a fast revision, so I cannot tell whether the founders are running backs or simply capable engineers executing version one.

The bull case stings because a working prototype can sometimes override missing conventional evidence. The relevant comparison is Dropbox: both entered crowded technical categories with a product that could be judged directly. The material difference is that Dropbox’s prototype came with evidence of unusual founder speed and correction; Rindler currently supplies the artifact but not the behavior around it. Its standard red flags are otherwise mostly absent: the cofounders have worked together since meeting at MIT in 2022, they are doing founder-led sales, and the plan does not depend on press, premature sales hiring, or a magical distribution partnership. But Kernel, Browserbase, Browser Use, and Deck can all move upward into mappings, while Rindler has disclosed neither a large proprietary mapping corpus nor user pull that would make its layer difficult to absorb.

I would pass today. The operational gate fails because the dossier has neither documented resourcefulness nor a narrow well of users pulling the product back. The known working-prototype exception does not replace both. One event would flip me: a named paying customer’s portal changes, Rindler restores its production workflow within 24 hours, and that customer continues repeated use. That would simultaneously show the founder reaction, the value of centralized repair, and demand strong enough to survive breakage.
