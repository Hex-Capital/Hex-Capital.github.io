# Lantern AI -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Lantern AI reports crossing **$1M ARR**, yet I cannot price the investment: post-money valuation, competing offers, and round proceeds are undisclosed. Those are different questions from whether employers want the product. Financing silence does not establish an auction, and YC membership does not establish consensus pricing. But with my own money, I need to know what I am buying and what the check proves. Both financing dimensions receive their missing-evidence defaults.

The strongest argument for Lantern is that customers pulled it upstream. Crypto.com, Hyatt, and Gammon reportedly used the original interviewer; customers then requested candidate discovery because hiring criteria were getting lost between sourcing and screening. That is a credible mechanism for expanding an embedded workflow. Gammon reports **40% faster time-to-shortlist**, and Hyatt reports **three times more candidates screened weekly**. These outcomes matter more than the company’s 100K cumulative interviews. Shared hiring decisions and feedback could become proprietary access to what a particular employer actually buys. The missing link is evidence that existing interview customers purchase and retain the newer sourcing product.

The founders give that mechanism some credibility. Thomas Chan reports two previous recruiting ventures generating “2M+” in sales, although the currency is unspecified; both founders worked at Career Hackers. This is stronger than recruiting-sector job titles alone. Lantern fits my pattern of backing people who translate between systems: here, employer judgment and software execution. But the dossier documents the recruiting side more clearly than a prior scaled technical operation. I give the team meaningful credit without inventing exceptional technical depth or assuming that CEO and CTO titles resolve decision authority.

The bull case against passing is substantial: a four-person company with founder-reported $1M ARR, named enterprise users, and customer-requested expansion may already have the functioning base business that makes AI upside worth owning. My known exceptions allow operating evidence to outweigh missing gates. Here, however, revenue is not allocated between products, and there is no margin, burn, or retention evidence. Subscription and research-credit billing establish how Lantern charges; they do not establish whether it can finance itself. Human review required by the terms also belongs in the cost model. I cannot delete the next financing and demonstrate that this business survives from the information supplied.

Gem already offers agents sharing ATS and CRM history, while Juicebox offers feedback-adaptive sourcing. That makes “shared context” an exposed product claim until Lantern demonstrates that its accumulated customer judgment causes durable purchases. The AI commoditization red flag is therefore relevant, though the dossier does not show that the founders assume errors disappear or that model access itself is their moat. Likewise, interview counts and followers would be metric theater if substituted for economics; the reported ARR and customer outcomes keep this from being merely that story. **I pass on the investment as documented.** One verifiable condition would change that to active underwriting: six consecutive months of positive operating cash flow from renewing customers paying for both interviewing and sourcing. That would show the customer pull funds a durable business rather than another fundraising cycle.
