# machine0 -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is not a market statistic. It is what broke. Barnaby Malet saw coding runs stretch to 6–8 hours and moved from the Director MCP gateway to persistent cloud computers, but the dossier does not identify a specific obstacle, the workaround he improvised, or how quickly it changed a user’s outcome. Founder-led onboarding is a promising method, not yet evidence of resourcefulness under resistance. I have to assign the founder test its absent-evidence default.

The product itself is much stronger evidence. A solo founder has shipped a working service that provisions SSH-ready machines through `machine0 new mybox`, with root access, static IPs, snapshots, credential profiles, two operating systems, and configurations reaching eight H200 GPUs. It launched publicly by June 15, 2026 and has real pricing from $0.013 per CPU hour. This is a fierce-nerd artifact built before permission, not a diagram of something to be built after funding.

But who wants it now? PACIFIC appears under “Trusted by,” Geoffrey Négiar is identified as a customer, and the Show HN launch received 95 points and 36 comments. Those facts establish a niche and some contact with users. They do not establish repeated use, payment, retention, or agents pulling the product into unplanned workflows. Attention is especially weak evidence here because developers will try almost any convenient infrastructure tool once.

The Director-to-machine0 change is the most encouraging fact about Malet’s behavior. He kept working on infrastructure for agents while changing the route after models improved and tasks became long-lived. That looks more like a running back changing direction than a founder chasing an unrelated fashion. The evidentiary weakness is timing: the dossier does not show a rejected hypothesis, the user observation that falsified it, and a rapid before-and-after sequence. So the pivot earns substantial but not top-band credit.

There is also genuine schlep. Persistent machines require credential handling, SSH key management, snapshots, networking, operating-system provisioning, billing, and incident response. Malet’s cloud resource-allocation research and responsibility for Upflow’s infrastructure and security make this more organic than a generic “AI infrastructure” brainstorm. Yet machine0 currently resells DigitalOcean capacity, has no disclosed moat, and competes with E2B, Daytona, and Modal, which can converge on most of these features. The likely durable product is therefore the agent fleet’s control plane—identity, credentials, state, and lifecycle—not merely a nicer way to rent a VM.

The bull case is that machine0 becomes a daily primitive before the category settles. The CLI is simple, the computers are persistent, and the founder already understands the ugly infrastructure beneath them. The bear case stings more today: Daytona reportedly reached $5 million in annualized revenue, Modal exceeds $300 million, and neither named machine0 customer is shown returning or paying. My verifiable flip condition is ten paying agent-fleet customers, with at least five still running workloads weekly after eight weeks and one documented instance where Malet personally shipped around a blocker within fourteen days. Without that, I would not write the check yet.
