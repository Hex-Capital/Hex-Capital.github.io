# Zaplar -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The most important missing sentence is: “This broke, so we built this workaround in two weeks, and this changed.” Zaplar interviewed more than 100 hotels and now visits prospects in person, but activity is not yet evidence of resourcefulness under resistance ([Bronn](https://bronnhq.com/blog/zaplar); [Zaplar](https://zaplar.com/blog/we-dont-cold-email-hotels)). The dossier names no failed integration, rejected sale, operational emergency, or founder-built response with an observable result. I cannot infer the quality I care about most from four capable résumés, SEK 3 million raised, or YC admission. This receives the missing-evidence default rather than a penalty, but it fails my people-first gate.

The technical evidence is real but narrow. Zaplar’s property graph and constrained optimizer reportedly reduced estimated housekeeping walking distance by 20% across tens of schedules ([Zaplar R&D](https://zaplar.com/blog/the-surprising-mathematics-behind-housekeeping-scheduling)). That is more persuasive than an architecture diagram. It partially matches my pattern of young technical teams producing numerical proof before institutional permission. But the result is company-reported, uses estimated distance, and tests one workflow. It does not yet prove that agents can coordinate bookings, payments, messaging, housekeeping, and vendor work safely across as many as 15 systems. The claimed hotel operating system is much larger than the measured artifact.

There may be a narrow well, but I cannot see its depth. Pilot customers operate in several countries, and Zaplar sensibly targets independent, boutique, luxury, and smaller hotel groups rather than “all hospitality.” Yet there is no customer count, named hotel, revenue, repeat usage, renewal, or workflow that users pulled the crude product into. Founder-led hotel visits are the right behavior. They are not user pull. Meanwhile, adoption is tied to infrequent core-replacement windows, and each sale may require migration, retraining, integrations, and trust around operational agents. Mews already serves 15,000 customers after raising $300 million in January 2026, while Apaleo connects more than 250 applications. Zaplar must therefore win both a product argument and a replacement argument.

The strongest evidence is where the idea came from. Douglas encountered hotel operations through his family, Axel worked in hotels and restaurants, and the first product handled event booking for Douglas’s family ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/zaplar)). This is not a generic “AI for hotels” idea assembled from a market report. It also contains genuine ugly work: property modeling, legacy integrations, migration, payments, staff approval, and operational reliability. Expanding from event booking toward a full operating system after 100 interviews suggests some willingness to change route, but the dossier does not name the rejected hypothesis or the observation that caused the change. It could be learning. It could also be scope expansion.

The bull case stings. A technical founder with cybersecurity and autonomous-systems experience, two founders with direct operational exposure, international pilots, a measured 20% optimization result, and founders personally visiting hotels could produce a proprietary substrate of layouts, rules, and workflow histories. If hotels begin using that substrate daily, incumbents may indeed find it difficult to replace their architectures without disrupting existing integrations. That would satisfy two of my less obvious preferences at once: a concrete pre-round proof spike and a difficult programmable substrate assembled before claiming platform status.

But no known-exception override applies yet. There is neither unmistakable user pull nor an end-to-end technical proof strong enough to replace the missing resourcefulness evidence. The eight-person team already has CEO, CTO, COO, and CFO titles and is recruiting a Founding Engineer; without evidence that the founders themselves are handling the hardest product and migration work, this risks looking like manager mode before the machine works. My 36-month base case is that Zaplar narrows to housekeeping and property-resource optimization and remains below $2 million ARR rather than replacing the hotel operating system. I would pass today. One condition would flip me: three named hotels complete paid core migrations and use Zaplar’s cross-system agents weekly for 12 consecutive weeks.
