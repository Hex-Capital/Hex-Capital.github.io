# Induction Labs -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I need—post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and the amount being raised—are absent. That forces the round into the low prior, not because every YC financing is an auction, but because I cannot distinguish a scarce first check from consensus-priced AI exposure. The capital plan is equally undefined: no named milestone, deadline, or pass/fail experiment connects the round to a truth that would dramatically reprice Induction Labs.

The technical work is real. Two founders trained a 106B-A5B model on 575 million frames, built a differential encoder with more than 100x compression, and claim 30x less pretraining compute and 3x lower serving cost than Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite. Jonathan Li’s ACL publication and Cohere work on reasoning and reinforcement-learning infrastructure make this more than résumé theater. But Photon-1’s superiority remains internally measured, and the dossier does not show that either founder has operated the enterprise workflows the model is supposed to transform. They have shipped on the model side of the bridge, not both sides.

The strongest bull case is that the artifact itself is unusually substantial for pre-seed. NanoCo established that expensive, crowded financing can be overridden when technical adoption becomes undeniable. Induction Labs could follow that mechanism because its unlabeled-video approach attacks a genuine bottleneck and its announcement drew roughly 80,000 views. The material difference is that NanoCo had thousands of users, early enterprise customers, and a rejected acquisition offer; Induction Labs has 515 LinkedIn followers, 215 X followers, no disclosed users, and no independent benchmark. Attention has not yet become behavior.

The claimed dark pool is also weaker than it first appears. An index of two billion public videos, a filtered corpus of approximately two million screen recordings, proprietary filtering, and custom kernels create reproduction work. They do not yet create proprietary demand or feedback. The source material is public, the distribution path is inferred, and H Company already offers APIs and open-weight models. No purchase, pilot, referral, or retained workflow demonstrates that Induction Labs owns access rather than merely possessing an interesting training recipe.

Delete the next financing and the business model disappears. There is no pricing, revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or customer-controlled cash metric. Lower serving cost is promising engineering evidence, but it is not evidence of profitable operation—particularly when training-data rights remain unresolved and an open-weight competitor can compress pricing. This is not a generic AI wrapper, which helps, but it still carries the red flag of asking internal model performance to stand in for a functioning base business.

My 36-month base case is that Induction Labs remains below $1 million ARR and either licenses Photon’s training assets to H Company or is absorbed as a technical team rather than becoming an independent platform. I pass today. The one verifiable flip condition is three named external customers converting repeat Photon usage into paid annual contracts after an independently reproduced benchmark win; that would show the corpus causes economic action rather than merely producing an impressive model release.
