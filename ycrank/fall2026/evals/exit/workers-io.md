# Workers IO -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest supported route is a strategic sale to a reliability platform such as Gremlin. The memo documents Gremlin’s targeted fault injection and Workers IO’s claimed ability to simulate unmodified systems, explore failure paths guided by customer-defined state, and replay failures. The founder reports finding PostgreSQL issues while simulating one customer’s stack. A buyer could use that capability to sell pre-deployment verification to its existing reliability customers. The reported finding comes from one unnamed customer, and the memo gives no measured buyer benefit or acquisition interest. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A seed-stage milestone would be two paying customers showing that Workers IO repeatedly catches consequential failures their existing tools miss, then expanding paid usage. Reported paying customers, the CEO’s expansion claim, and reported early funding make the test plausible. The expansion claim has no denominator, and runway and the cost of supporting additional deployments are unknown. Transfer and consent terms are unverified, and the memo supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Without holder-specific terms, the benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash target within eight years. That requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale prices below are illustrative. Both assume an eligible holder, a full cash sale closing and paying within eight years, $5 million in net debt and deal adjustments, and a pro rata waterfall with no senior claims diverting proceeds.

| Scenario | Cash realized by the holder |
|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40 million enterprise value less $5 million leaves $35 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $1.05 million, or 2.1×. |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value less $5 million leaves $95 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $2.85 million, or 5.7×. If only half the stake can be sold at that price, cash falls to about $1.43 million, or 2.85×. |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with reinvested cash and no eligible share purchase or payout yields $0 realized within eight years. |

The memo supplies no acquisition pricing, buyer economics, or payout terms to support the strong case. Proceeds, transaction feasibility, and independent alternatives therefore stay at their absent-evidence defaults.

### Countercase

Antithesis already offers full-system deterministic simulation and a self-serve Teams plan with a coding-agent plugin. Workers IO’s coverage also depends on the workloads customers define. An adjacent platform could meet customer demand through an existing offering or integration, leaving little reason to pay for Workers IO’s state-guided branching. That is a credible substitution risk. The memo establishes neither a structural barrier to closing nor a supported price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle, so the Pass gate does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What measured advantage over existing simulation tools would make an adjacent reliability platform willing to acquire Workers IO at a price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? Repeatable results from paying customers and a credible buyer-side estimate supporting that price would move the action to Reach out. A supported price ceiling materially below the hurdle on the strongest route would move it to Pass.
