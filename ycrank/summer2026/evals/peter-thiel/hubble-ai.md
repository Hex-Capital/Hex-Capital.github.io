# Hubble -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Aaron Leon built EHR and AI infrastructure serving 1M+ patients and led 50+ engineers, while Hubble discloses no two-to-five-year product milestones. Hubble is therefore an operator-rich but plan-poor regulated-infrastructure bet: unusually credible people attacking a real institutional failure, without yet showing the definite plan that turns competence into monopoly. Hubble’s founders understand the maze; the unanswered question is whether they intend to own the maze or merely navigate it.

**Affirm** is the high rung on operator pedigree: Max Levchin had already shipped PayPal to scale before rebuilding credit infrastructure. Hubble shares the adjacency—Leon at Amazon One Medical and Prabha Dublish as Grow Therapy’s first product hire—but Hubble diverges where the analogy becomes decisive: neither founder has previously built and exited a category leader. Leon’s 1M-patient infrastructure and Dublish’s telehealth, marketplace, integration, and AI-product record are meaningful pre-seed signals, so Hubble sits above ordinary first-time founders. The **Asana** exception does not fully apply; Dustin Moskovitz had already built Facebook, while Hubble’s experience is senior-operator pedigree rather than founder-scale proof.

**Quora’s** low-rung failure mechanism was indefinite planning: an intelligent product and exceptional pedigree without a definite roadmap. Hubble shows the same omission more starkly. Hubble names 40+ live tools across Epic, Cerner, athenahealth, UnitedHealthcare, and Aetna, but names no coverage milestone, certification sequence, architectural commitment, or five-year destination. “APIs, browser automation, and voice” describes a repertoire, not a plan. Without a declared path from retrieval utility to system-of-record control, Hubble risks becoming a permanent integration workshop—the healthcare form of indefinite optimism.

Particle Health, Health Gorilla, 1upHealth, and Zus Health make Hubble’s secret only partially contrarian. Hubble’s real hypothesis appears to be that national interoperability networks fail at the last mile because patient- and provider-mediated retrieval across browsers, voice, and APIs is more complete than FHIR alone. That is more specific than “AI for healthcare,” but Hubble has not named why Health Gorilla’s QHIN position or Zus Health’s browser-and-API stack cannot reproduce it. Over 18 months, Hubble’s 40+ tools either compound into proprietary routing reliability and unreachable-record coverage, or decay into connector maintenance. Competition is for losers; Hubble has identified competitors, but not yet the structural reason they must lose.

Hubble’s 40+ integrations provide breadth without demonstrated pricing power. For Hubble to move from workflow layer to monopoly trajectory, one fact would need to become true: customers that begin with record retrieval must become unable to operate without Hubble’s deterministic identity, provenance, permissioning, and audit layer. Evidence of multi-year contracts, net retention above 120%, or uniquely reachable records would establish that transition. Without it, Hubble competes against Health Gorilla’s $80M-funded national network and 1upHealth’s 75+ enterprises and 35M+ patients. Hubble presently looks useful; usefulness is not monopoly.

**Palantir** is the relevant single-anchor parallel on regulated-sector structure. Palantir turned government complexity, trust, and integration depth into durable pricing power; Hubble could do something analogous with PHI consent, source provenance, payer access, and provider write-back. CMS-0057-F’s January 1, 2027 deadlines create a real institutional forcing function, and Hubble’s per-call audit logs address a regulated surface that generic software cannot casually enter. Hubble nevertheless inherits regulation as exposure before proving regulation as moat: consent scope, browser credentials, voice retrieval, and incomplete coverage can create liability just as readily as switching costs.

Hubble will reach roughly $3M-$8M ARR by July 2029 and then be acquired by Zus Health rather than become an independent category monopoly. Hubble’s founder pedigree should produce a credible retrieval and workflow product, while the absent definite plan and crowded aggregation market should keep Hubble below system-of-record status. Zus would buy the 40+ integration surface and patient/provider-mediated access channels; the operator-rich, plan-poor signature points toward valuable infrastructure absorbed by a broader platform.

CMS-0057-F supplies the strongest objection to that forecast: Hubble could become the **Stripe** of patient data by turning an approaching regulatory deadline into a developer-default layer that legacy networks cannot ship quickly enough. Dublish’s Grow Therapy experience, Leon’s 1M-patient infrastructure record, and Hubble’s live multi-channel tools make that bull case sting. The evidence that still wins is structural: no named incumbent incapacity, no disclosed customer or revenue signal, no proprietary benchmark, and no milestone plan. The **Asana** and **Quora** pedigree exceptions do not rescue Hubble because neither founder has Moskovitz- or D’Angelo-level founder-scale proof. A verifiable $5M ARR run rate with net retention above 120% by July 2027 would flip my verdict to Invest by demonstrating that Hubble’s audit and integration depth creates pricing power rather than merely acquisition value.
