# Aikido Technologies -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Aikido’s strongest AGI path is a scarce complement: reliable power for AI compute where onshore grid connections take years. The memo reports at least 180 GW of data center projects in advanced queues globally. That supports the power bottleneck. Aikido’s proposed way around it is AO60DC, a floating platform designed to serve 10+ MW of IT load with a 15+ MW wind turbine, batteries, diesel backup, and seawater cooling. If that system delivers buyer-grade uptime sooner than an onshore connection, Aikido could sell earlier access to powered compute capacity. The design ratings establish the intended scale. Delivered uptime remains unmeasured in the memo. (*The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks*)

Aikido’s granted patent gives it control over a specific folding platform design. Its founders bring floating-wind and hydrodynamics experience, and the memo records completed structural assembly of the earlier Aikido One platform. Aikido reports that the A1DC retrofit received final platform permits. Those milestones support the team’s ability to build marine hardware and advance a pilot. Aikido also reports prospective GPU renters, but its reservation form is nonbinding and the memo establishes no paid commitments. (*Defensibility, Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Risks*)

The expansion unit is one AO60DC platform. In a colocation scenario, revenue depends on contracted IT capacity and billable availability. It would need to cover annualized platform and power-system capital costs, backup fuel, and vessel-based servicing. Wind variability could increase backup costs, and outages could reduce billable capacity. The modular design gives Aikido a plausible replication unit. Installed cost, paid utilization, and offshore service costs remain unverified. (*The Idea, Key Risks*)

### Countercase

The grid bottleneck can be real while Aikido’s capacity remains substitutable. Competing marine designs use other power and platform architectures, and Aikido’s patent covers a specific floating-wind design. The memo establishes no exclusive site or capacity rights. Wind variability and vessel-dependent maintenance could also erode the delivery and uptime advantage buyers would pay for. These risks keep the scarce-complement path below the Reach out gate. The memo shows competing approaches, with no direct evidence that buyers can readily substitute equivalent capacity. Watch remains appropriate while the buyer qualification test is open. (*Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks*)
