# Wingman Law -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B is the closest fit.** Wingman represents California accident victims directly. Its public intake form, local claims guides, and referral requests give it ways to reach the next thousand claimants. Wingman says its internal agents gather records and draft communications for attorney approval. If those channels repeatedly produce signed cases and automation reduces attorney time per case, Wingman could grow caseload faster than staffing. Growth would come mainly from winning new matters. Repeat matters and paid expansion from existing clients remain unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The memo reports multiple six-figure cases in progress. That is an early signal that claimants will engage the firm. The figure describes pending matters, and realized fees, acquisition source, and conversion to signed representation remain unknown. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Scenario assumption.** For a comparable injury claim in a second jurisdiction, assume Wingman can reuse its intake and document workflows. It would still need to verify who can represent clients there and adapt solicitation, attorney review, and confidential-data handling to local requirements. For each cohort, start with fees actually collected on recoveries, then subtract acquisition and screening costs, attorney review and rework, AI and support costs, and any unreimbursed case expenses. Track the cash committed before recovery separately. The memo gives no case-level measurements for that calculation. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The strongest case for Pass is that Wingman scales like a local law practice. Each new market may require local attorney capacity and compliance work, while each new matter adds review and can tie up cash until recovery. California’s delegation and confidentiality rules make attorney oversight a concrete constraint. The Pass gate turns on whether those costs rise in proportion to volume or whether local requirements prevent reuse of the core workflow. The memo establishes the constraints but leaves both outcomes unresolved. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Wingman take a comparable cohort in a second jurisdiction from the same intake channel through signed representation and fee collection, while reusing its core workflow and reducing attorney hours per matter as volume rises? Reach out if a side-by-side comparison with California shows repeatable conversion, positive contribution after local rollout and case costs, and declining attorney time per comparable matter. Pass if the rollout establishes that each market needs a rebuilt workflow or that added volume requires proportional attorney time and cash, with no supported way to reduce either.
