# DeepMark -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032: software agents call banks, negotiate bills, and arrange appointments for us. A convincing voice tells the recipient almost nothing. The useful question becomes, “Who authorized this agent, and what may it do?” DeepMark’s embedding, detection, and identity-resolution workflow points toward that world. There are two transitions between here and there: generators must carry identifiers, and receivers must use them to make decisions. The dossier describes the machinery for both, but does not establish that banks actually grant narrowly defined authority through it. That remaining distance matters more to me than the broader watermarking market estimate.

The founders give me a reason to stay curious. Slavko Kovačević and Kosta Pavlović report six years working together, starting as researchers at the University of Montenegro; both researched audio watermarking before commercialization. Pavlović’s research implementation and the company’s public AWARE code give that history something I can inspect. This is independently developed technical obsession with an accountable CEO and a complementary scientific lead. The €33,392.30 received in 2023 and €200,000 approved for development in 2025 also show an ability to recruit institutional belief, although grants do not establish commercial judgment. Recruiting beyond their research circle remains less visible.

DeepMark has persuaded people to do more than applaud a demonstration. Confident Security describes production use, Pencil supplies a named testimonial, and the founders report a platform license signed before YC. Integrating security software takes engineering labor and puts someone’s reputation behind the decision. Those are meaningful pre-seed actions. I would give them substantially more weight than the company’s 1,088 LinkedIn followers. But the dossier provides no deployment duration, verification volumes, or renewal behavior, so I cannot yet call this an indispensable habit.

The strongest argument against my caution is that DeepMark fits my recurring preference for the hidden machinery beneath a visible wave. It owns the proprietary Vigil model, publishes research tooling, and has an April 2026 Ortana Cubix integration extending beyond voice-agent licensing. My pre-revenue investment in **CosmicBrain AI** used a similar mechanism: longstanding technical obsession plus a working artifact could outweigh missing revenue. The material difference is that DeepMark’s advertised outcome requires cooperation across a communications boundary. An excellent generator integration can still leave the person answering the telephone unable to verify anything. Missing revenue is tolerable; missing that connection changes the business.

Then I pull on the loose thread: does detecting a mark establish authorization, or merely identify marked audio? The dossier does not settle replay, revocation, or the scope of delegated permission. DeepMark also says its telephone comparisons use simulated paths and actual customer routes need evaluation. Meta’s MIT-licensed AudioSeal and Resemble’s alternatives make “we embed a watermark” an increasingly difficult place to collect durable margins. Proprietary technology gives DeepMark an enabling asset, but demonstrated control of a scarce bottleneck remains incomplete. Its identity records also make consent and data custody consequential; the dossier establishes no severe privacy violation, but supplies too little architecture to clear that gate confidently.

I am **Neutral** on investing my own money today. DeepMark could restore confidence in answering a call, but the dossier identifies no specific institutional rent, artificial restriction, or quantified delay that it transfers back to an individual. Security infrastructure can still be valuable; it simply activates less of that part of my framework. My single verifiable flip condition is an independently observed, consenting bank deployment in which a real telephone call produces a correctly scoped authorization decision, with replayed or revoked credentials rejected. That would connect the founders’ technical work to the future I can imagine—and demonstrate that DeepMark controls something customers cannot obtain merely by downloading a watermarking model.
