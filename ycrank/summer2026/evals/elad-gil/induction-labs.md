# Induction Labs -- Elad Gil Evaluation

**Photon-1’s 106B-A5B launch makes Induction Labs a high-output research builder with no demonstrated position in the value chain.** Founder shipping is Induction Labs’s strongest dimension: Jonathan Li moved from Cohere reasoning and RL infrastructure through Axiom 1 to a model trained on 575 million frames. Index-like distribution is Induction Labs’s weakest dimension: no API, pricing, customers, or GTM path shows that agent developers must pass through Photon-1 as computer use compounds. Induction Labs can build; the open question is whether it becomes an index on agents or a talented model lab that an index owner acquires.

**Perplexity** is the high rung for shipping: Aravind repeatedly turned conversations into working product and user feedback. Induction Labs shares the artifact velocity—Axiom 1 in August 2025, then Photon-1 in July 2026 with a 100×-compression encoder, 2-billion-video index, and 575-million-frame corpus. Induction Labs diverges because Perplexity’s iterations occurred against users, while Photon-1’s claimed Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite advantage remains an internal benchmark. Shipping research artifacts is real; shipping between customer conversations is more valuable.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism for index position: participation in a compounding category does not matter when another platform owns the network. Induction Labs risks the infrastructure version of that problem. World Labs has raised $1.23 billion, General Intuition $454 million, and Runway $860 million, while hyperscalers can combine proprietary interaction data with existing multimodal distribution. Induction Labs has a differentiated training method, but without a consumption API or embedded workflow, Induction Labs must win model share against capitalized platforms instead of automatically benefiting from agent growth.

**Photon-1’s roughly 30,000 H200 GPU-hours provide Induction Labs with a credible recent catalyst: sparse models and 100× state compression make raw-video pretraining materially more feasible now than two years ago.** Over the next 18 months, that mechanism compounds only if cheaper pretraining produces faster model cycles, more deployments, and proprietary rollouts; otherwise falling compute requirements help World Labs, Google, and every other model lab too. Induction Labs therefore sits below Anduril’s post-Ukraine reopening: the technical cost curve changed, but Induction Labs has not shown a distributional or regulatory event that uniquely opened the window.

**Photon-1 has no disclosed revenue, customer, repeat usage, or signed LOI, so Induction Labs receives the absent-evidence default on revenue velocity.** The tier would flip if Induction Labs showed that agent developers were buying inference repeatedly—roughly $1 million ARR with greater than 20% monthly growth would distinguish “just working” from benchmark tourism. Four thousand X followers and two media articles measure attention, not buying behavior. Induction Labs is an AI company in the hottest capital cycle; silence on usage remains a negative signal even at pre-seed.

**Color Genomics worked because the hard scientific capability was commoditized and Color owned workflow and data above it.** Induction Labs currently owns the hard layer itself: frontier-model performance, lawful access to public screen recordings, sufficient compute, independent benchmark validity, and downstream developer adoption must all work. That is more than a single miracle. Induction Labs’s 30× lower claimed pretraining compute is impressive, but Induction Labs still resembles the science-project pattern I generally avoid more than Color’s workflow-on-top-of-the-hard-thing architecture.

**Google DeepMind is the most likely 36-month destination for Induction Labs: an acquisition below $300 million before Induction Labs reaches $5 million ARR.** Photon-1’s observational-learning architecture, compressed state representation, and curated screen-video corpus are strategically useful to a platform already distributing Gemini computer-use models. Induction Labs’s shipping strength creates acquisition value; Induction Labs’s weak index position and lack of commercial evidence make an independent compounding business less likely.

**Mistral is the strongest objection to that forecast.** Induction Labs could be the same known exception: a capital-intensive foundation-model startup entering after the core AI miracle, staffed by founders with relevant model-training experience and armed with a dramatic efficiency advantage. Jonathan Li’s Cohere work and Photon-1’s claimed 30× compute advantage make that bull case sting. Mistral’s founders had shipped Llama, however, and hyperscaler partnerships supplied distribution; Induction Labs has neither equivalent validation nor a commercial channel. A third-party benchmark plus at least $1 million ARR from recurring agent-developer inference within 12 months would flip my verdict to Invest.
