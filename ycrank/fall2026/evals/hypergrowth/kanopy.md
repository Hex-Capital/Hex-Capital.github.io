# Kanopy -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with electric utilities, Kanopy’s strongest path is to distribute recurring vegetation surveys through contractors such as ACRT. Arborcision already combines ACRT’s vegetation management services with Kanopy’s video analysis. Ordinary video is a reusable input for measuring clearances, and the credentialed SDK lets utilities upload footage and retrieve results from their own applications. Those capabilities could transfer to utilities abroad with the same inspection job. How much local validation and workflow adaptation they would need remains uncertain. The contractor channel and SDK make discovery and setup more repeatable, while the documented route to paid use still appears to depend on enterprise agreements and contractor involvement. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Repeat surveys and inspection records embedded in utility workflows give customers a reason to keep using the product. If contracts bill for surveyed miles, expansion to more circuits could grow spend within an account. That billing model is a scenario assumption, and the memo does not establish recurring fees or paid expansion. Under that scenario, incremental contribution would be revenue from additional miles less attributable selling and setup, video processing and storage, support, and any capture or field validation Kanopy pays for. Reusing the same SDK and contractor workflow could spread setup effort across more miles. The memo gives no billing rates or operating costs to establish contribution. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Kanopy reported 3,093 cumulative miles, including more than 300 across four states in two weeks. That shows reported field activity for a two-person team. The 270,000 miles associated with six utilities or contractors represent potential scope across letters of intent and master service agreements. Paid mileage, realized revenue, and time to paid production are unknown, so initial traction takes the absent-evidence default. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Utilities could require extensive ground checks before relying on video-derived clearances. The California utility plan described in the memo calls for field validation and retains ground inspections where remote methods fall short. If other markets require bespoke validation and each survey still incurs substantial field labor, the contractor channel could expand distribution while delivery costs rise with volume. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo does not establish that such work is unavoidable across markets or that it makes faster growth uneconomic. Those unresolved dependencies, together with unknown paid conversion, keep confidence Low. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a contractor bring a utility in a second market into paid production on the existing workflow and grow its paid survey volume on a repeat cycle? A rollout record and repeat invoices showing bounded local validation, limited Kanopy onboarding labor, and positive incremental contribution would justify rescoring for Reach out if the total reaches 65 and confidence reaches Medium. Evidence that each market requires substantial rebuilding without a credible transfer path, or that unavoidable field labor makes growth uneconomic, would change the action to Pass.
