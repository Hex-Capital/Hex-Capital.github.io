# Stacktrace -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Fleet’s public launch and the founders’ prior work in software supply-chain security, AI-platform security, and cloud infrastructure support seed-stage execution credit. For the building-blocks path, the relevant workload is unattended coding-agent runs, and the proposed input is policy enforcement across agents. Stacktrace reports that companies restrict who can use agents and what those agents can access. The memo documents overlapping native permissions and hooks, with no buyer evidence that suitable controls are scarce or slow to supply. That leaves the building-blocks path at zero. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

The enabled-industry path also scores zero. Scheduled coding jobs that require a person to supervise them could become economical if Fleet handles routine decisions and escalates exceptions. That is a scenario assumption. The memo identifies neither a specific job excluded by supervision cost nor a measured reduction in cost per successful run. Founder-reported design partners provide an early demand signal, with paid use and conversion unverified. Centralized policies and approval histories could create switching costs, but native controls already overlap and the memo gives no retention or migration evidence. Published pricing identifies how Stacktrace would charge, while realized revenue and delivery costs remain unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Fleet’s strongest case is a team using multiple coding agents that keeps recurring jobs attended because supervising them under separate native controls costs too much. Fleet’s advertised cross-agent policy could make unattended runs viable by enforcing the same rules across agents and escalating exceptions. Stacktrace’s report of restricted agent access and its recruitment of design partners make that workflow plausible. The memo gives no buyer-level evidence that Fleet opened a previously excluded use, so both path scores remain zero and the Pass gate applies. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can one design partner use Fleet to run a recurring coding job unattended that it previously ruled out because supervision cost too much? A paid pilot that holds the agent models and safety requirements constant and shows that Fleet brings cost per successful, policy-compliant run below the buyer’s adoption threshold would move the action to Reach out. The comparison should include native controls, human approvals, false blocks, and failed runs. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
