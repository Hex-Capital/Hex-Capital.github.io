# Versine -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Versine’s strongest path. A consumer payments incumbent such as PayPal or Block could buy Versine to bring assistant-initiated person-to-person transfers into its own payment network. Versine describes an MCP integration with passkey approvals, standing payment rules, and bank claim links for recipients without accounts. Ownership could give an incumbent control over the approval and recipient experience and bring it repeat payment volume. That benefit is an inference. The memo records launch attention and 5+ Android downloads, and leaves repeat usage and buyer interest unverified. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

The operating milestone is sustained repeat transfers after launch credits expire, with enough data on approval errors and payout costs for a buyer to estimate the value of owning the workflow. The launch gives Versine a way to run that test. The reported $125,000 seed entry dates to the earlier sign-in product, so current runway and the capital needed to reach the milestone remain unknown. Bridge handles identity verification and bank payouts, so a buyer would need to establish whether those services could continue after a sale. The memo leaves transfer rights and any required consents open. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

Under the illustrative seed terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, which falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution and no follow-ons. To realize $2.5 million gross within eight years, the holder needs $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. At 3% with $5 million of assumed net debt, the hurdle is $88.3 million of enterprise value. The memo supplies no Versine sale-price range or usable holder terms, so these figures are hurdles and sensitivities. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

- A **$25 million enterprise-value sale** in year eight yields **$600,000 gross (1.2×)** after $5 million of assumed net debt.
- A **$100 million enterprise-value sale** on the same terms yields **$2.85 million gross (5.7×)**.
- A **private plateau** with no buyer interest, public-market scale out of reach, continued reinvestment, and no eligible secondary or funded payout yields **$0 realized** through year eight, with the holder retaining the shares. In a wind-down, common holders receive only the residual after obligations and senior preferences. The memo leaves that residual unquantifiable.

The sale illustrations assume a full cash sale, pro rata distribution, and no additional fees or senior claims, before investor taxes. Backup routes remain unevidenced. The $1 instant-payout fee establishes pricing, while cash generation for a sponsor buyout or company-funded payout remains unknown. The historical seed entry gives no evidence of eligible secondary demand, and the launch metrics leave IPO scale and readiness unestablished. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

### Countercase

An incumbent could build the assistant interface on its existing payment rails or partner for it. Venmo already handles person-to-person transfers, Cash App has shareable request links, and Stripe Link shows that an incumbent can add assistant-authorized payments in merchant checkout. Versine’s launch credit also makes early interest a weak signal of repeat demand. If the workflow is easy to replicate, the buyer’s reason to pay an acquisition premium fades. This supports Watch. The evidence leaves both a structural barrier and a materially inadequate sale price unproven, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Versine’s repeat transfer activity after launch credits expire support a buyer’s case for acquiring the company at a price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity within eight years? A buyer-side comparison of owning, building, and partnering, grounded in retained payment volume and measured error and payout costs, would answer it. A supported acquisition range that clears the hurdle would move the action to Reach out. A supported range that leaves the holder materially short even under favorable ownership assumptions would move it to Pass.
