# Super Powers Mobility (SPM) -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

What does SPM know about mobile power that Inventus or Bren-Tronics believes is impossible? The dossier establishes an operational problem: generators and idling engines create detectable signatures and require fuel deliveries. It also describes a concrete product—15 kWh modules with 12 kW continuous AC and 14 kW DC output. But it supplies no disputed proposition explaining why established suppliers cannot satisfy this demand. The claimed power-density advantage remains independently unverified. A useful product exists in the evidence; the secret remains undisclosed.

The strongest argument for investing is the speed of commercial validation. Super Powers Mobility reports $421,815 in realized revenue in one month after its September 2026 battery-and-power-export pivot. That is unusually informative at pre-seed. Its $792,000 qualified pipeline is additional prospective demand, while the earlier $769,000 of vehicle preorders belongs to a different product and carries conversion uncertainty. Direct quotations, evaluation units, and defense-prime subcontracting provide identifiable customer routes. The missing link is attribution: which route generated the realized revenue, from which buyers, and with what acquisition and implementation burden? The sales deserve credit under distribution; they do not establish a repeatable channel.

Can those sales produce a position SPM can keep? Vehicle-integrated demonstrations to special operations forces establish access to a relevant operating environment. In-house battery, electronics, drivetrain, and controls development establishes engineering substance. Neither establishes exclusive technology or customer dependence. Inventus already supplies custom military battery systems, and Bren-Tronics already sells military batteries and chargers. Qualification could eventually make replacement expensive, but the dossier identifies no retained production program demonstrating that mechanism. The monopoly question remains open rather than disproved.

Jonathan Powers has unusually relevant experience across Tesla vehicle integration, Honda powersports engineering, and Workhorse braking systems. That makes the integrated product credible. It does not answer the separate founding question: who owns the essential work, how long have the core team worked together, and how economically exposed are they? The dossier supports in-house development but leaves founder commitment, ownership, and shared history largely unspecified. I apply the missing-evidence default. There is no documented part-time founder, outsourced core engineering, or investor-dominated capitalization to justify an adverse judgment.

SPM fits the unstated preference for supplying a physical constraint beneath a larger technological system. Silent electrical power could become increasingly important to deployed sensors and electronic warfare equipment. Yet the economics of capturing that importance remain unspecified. The $1,799 consumer battery price cannot establish the economics of quotation-based defense systems. Industrial pricing, gross margins, and expansion within customer programs are absent. The $1.6 billion military-battery market provides context, but no bottom-up path to a defining outcome. My 36-month expectation is that SPM remains primarily a custom battery-and-power integration supplier, with bespoke systems and engineering generating more than half its revenue.

I would pass on a check today. The strongest objection is substantial: this team may already have found customers willing to pay for an integrated solution incumbents have neglected. Its realized sales also weaken the red flag that every technical, manufacturing, and commercial dependency must succeed before any business exists. I checked the known exceptions: the dossier establishes neither an independently verified technical breakthrough nor customer behavior that directly resolves the missing monopoly mechanism. One verifiable condition would change my decision: a named customer's repeat production award documenting that SPM's independently measured performance advantage prevents substitution by qualified incumbent products. That would connect the engineering to durable value capture.
