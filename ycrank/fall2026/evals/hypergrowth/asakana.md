# Asakana -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit, Asakana’s strongest path is to start with seafood distributors whose ERPs have APIs and reuse the same order-capture workflow across comparable distributors abroad. Asakana describes multilingual intake from messages, calls, and images, catalog matching informed by order history, and staff review of ambiguous orders before ERP writeback. Those components plausibly transfer across markets. The amount of work needed to connect different ERPs and adapt outbound messaging is uncertain. Referrals can generate leads, while the documented demo and email-quote process still requires human involvement. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The documented transaction-volume commission gives existing accounts a way to spend more if they route more orders through Asakana at a stable rate. Order history and catalog mappings could reinforce repeat use. Expansion sufficient to offset churn remains an inference. The four-person team’s reported live U.S. deployments and 40%+ of order-desk time returned to selling indicate adoption and customer value. Payment status and commercial growth are undisclosed, so initial traction uses the default. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

In the API-enabled scenario, incremental contribution is the agreed commission on processed volume less attributable sales and setup labor, processing costs, and ongoing support. Reusing an ERP connection could reduce setup labor per buyer, and higher processed volume could spread that cost. Commission rates, staff time, and processing costs are undisclosed, leaving contribution uncertain. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

If most reachable distributors need custom connections for legacy ERPs, each sale could bring substantial implementation and support work. Supplier consent and registered sending channels could also add rollout work where outbound SMS matters. The structural gate stays open because the documented custom-integration requirement applies to ERPs without APIs, leaving a plausible path through API-enabled distributors. The memo does not establish how much of the target segment that path can reach. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of referred distributors, including one outside the U.S., start paying and process live orders through the same ERP connector with minimal help from Asakana? A rollout showing independent setup and payment, recurring billed volume, and positive contribution after attributable labor and processing costs would move the action to Reach out.
