# Shielded -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Shielded compresses supplier-price and tariff analysis from weeks to under 72 hours, but faster analysis is not yet a consequential rewrite of the cost curve. The dossier quantifies a $3.1 billion supply-chain risk-management market growing to $5.2 billion by 2030, not the underlying margin losses, working capital, or population whose economics could improve by 10x or 100x. This is useful enterprise software. The evidence does not yet make it an asymmetric societal option.

The strongest fact is deployment speed. Shielded connected fragmented ERP, bill-of-materials, price, and event data for defense, CPG, restaurant, and manufacturing users; one restaurant deployment covered 200 SKUs and produced a live product within 48 hours. That could represent a 5–10x improvement over work taking weeks. But it is a delivery benchmark, not a demonstrated technical discontinuity: there is no disclosed accuracy, back-test, realized margin improvement, proprietary architecture, or comparison showing that Interos, Altana, Everstream, or Prewave cannot reproduce the result. Integration labor dressed as intelligence is not a Black Swan.

The bull case stings. James Simon combines computer science, economics, commodities experience at Balyasny, and direct familiarity with how external events propagate through markets. A one-person company producing several enterprise deployments in under 72 hours is meaningful pre-seed execution. The known-exception case would be an adaptable instigator using a narrow product to enter an enormous financial value pool. Shielded has the instigator signal, but the dossier supplies neither an enormous quantified value pool nor evidence that these deployments are becoming a broader financial platform. The inferred data moat remains a possibility, not an asset.

The largest kill risk is recommendation validity. Shielded proposes operational and hedging responses, yet no public evidence shows whether those recommendations improve realized outcomes. Capital should first buy a prospective test: for one named customer, freeze the recommendations, measure forecast error and realized gross-margin savings against the existing process, and disclose a pass/fail threshold. Instead, the available milestones demonstrate setup speed. There is also no pricing, ROI, retention, or cost-to-serve model showing that a 48-hour custom deployment becomes profitable software rather than recurring implementation work.

My 36-month base case is that Shielded narrows into tariff and commodity-procurement workflow software and remains below $2 million ARR, because integration speed is real while differentiated intelligence and scalable economics are unproven. I pass today. One verifiable result would flip me: a named enterprise customer showing at least a 10x reduction in decision time, independently measured margin savings exceeding ten times Shielded’s annual fee, and repeatable deployment without founder-intensive customization.
