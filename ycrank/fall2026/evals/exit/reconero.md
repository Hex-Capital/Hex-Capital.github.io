# Reconero -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A records or dispatch platform could buy Reconero to add a workflow that turns footage from multiple camera systems into timestamp-linked narratives and agency-specific forms. Ownership could matter if it lets the buyer roll out proven integrations and form mappings across its installed base faster than building or licensing the same capability. That advantage is an inference. The memo documents the October launch and agency demo intake. Reconero describes the workflow, reports consulting more than 100 officers, and says several police units tested an earlier beta. The memo provides no paid production deployments, measured customer benefit, or buyer interest. It also establishes no independent fallback that could pay this holder. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A paid production pilot across agencies would test whether the workflow can reduce total reporting and review time while preserving accuracy, draft retention, and audit trails. The earlier beta and agency-data demos make that test plausible at seed. Pilot costs, burn, runway, and available financing are unverified. The memo also leaves IP ownership and the transferability of agency contracts and data-access rights open. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The illustrative seed check is $500,000 at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership declining to 3% after 40% relative dilution and no follow-on checks. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder before taxes, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The following sale prices and dates are scenario assumptions. Both sales assume enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration, with zero net debt, fees, or preference adjustments, and simple pro rata allocation. All consideration is cash at close. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each modeled at $0. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction terms to substantiate either price. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Timing and gross holder proceeds |
|---|---|
| Modest sale | $50 million enterprise value in year 5 pays $1.5 million at 3%, or 3×. |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value in year 7 pays $3 million at 3%, or 6×. At 2%, it pays $2 million, or 4×. |
| Prolonged private | Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. The holder retains the shares and realizes $0 through year 8, or 0×. |

### Countercase

Axon already bundles drafting into its evidence platform, and Code Four already offers source-linked drafting across camera vendors and integrations with records and dispatch systems. A prospective buyer could build or license enough of the workflow to avoid paying an acquisition premium. A trial of a competing tool also found no drafting time savings, leaving Reconero’s claimed workflow benefit to be demonstrated. That evidence makes the ownership rationale weak today. It does not establish that substitutes match the full workflow in production or cap a strong outcome below the proceeds hurdle. The Pass override is therefore unproven. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid, buyer-backed production pilot show that owning Reconero is worth more to a records or dispatch platform than building or licensing an equivalent workflow? Move to Reach out if the pilot demonstrates repeatable agency-level savings with acceptable accuracy and auditability, and the platform’s own rollout economics support a purchase price above the applicable proceeds hurdle. Stay on Watch if the evidence shows agency demand alone.
