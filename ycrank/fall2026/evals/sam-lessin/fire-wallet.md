# Fire Wallet -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Fire Wallet gives me a prospective customer’s payment volume before it gives me the price of the equity. The proposed post-money, competing offers, and whether my check enables something otherwise unfundable are all undisclosed. That leaves the deal ununderwritten; it does not establish an auction. The more interesting fact is that an enterprise prospect introduced ten additional companies. Someone apparently wants this product enough to put business relationships behind it. That is the beginning of a distribution argument worth examining.

The strongest bull case connects that referral behavior with Joshua Goldbard’s experience founding MobileCoin. Fire has a shipped iPhone app, a named Trustboop LOI, and prospective access to businesses with substantial payment needs. This fits my preference for founders translating between systems that do not work well together: here, crypto custody and everyday merchant payments. But the receipts are uneven. Goldbard has built on the crypto side; the dossier does not establish prior merchant-finance operating results. The reported MobileCoin acquisition lacks a buyer or terms. I give credit for having built a payments product, without treating fundraising or an unspecified exit as proof of an economic outcome.

Those ten referrals are Fire Wallet’s best evidence of a possible proprietary relationship network. The approximately $100 million in annual prospective GMV is not processed volume, and the prospect’s $1.5 million monthly flow is not Fire revenue. The LOI establishes customer intent, while referrals establish an observed action beyond signing up for a waitlist. Neither yet shows that Fire owns a workflow customers repeatedly pay to use. My documented exceptions to missing financing gates depend on unusually strong conversion, operating economics, or a security that captures proven founder value. Fire’s evidence is promising enough to investigate, but does not supply that override.

The economic tension is specific. Fire promises least-cost routing while excluding spreads and payment for order flow, leaving disclosed interface fees as its stated monetization mechanism. What can it charge while preserving the savings that attract merchants? There is no numerical fee schedule, margin, burn, or seed-funded break-even plan to answer that. Dependence on 0x and MoonPay also makes the boundary of Fire’s own advantage consequential. Meanwhile, Ethereum-only transfers and recovery phrases in the App Store description sit awkwardly beside multichain, email/PIN, and distributed-HSM descriptions elsewhere. Those discrepancies leave the deployed custody and recovery model unresolved. For a wallet selling privacy and control, that uncertainty bears directly on whether merchants will entrust it with recurring payments.

I pass with my own money today. The dossier names no capital-funded experiment with a metric and deadline, so I cannot distinguish a finite venture moment from financing continued product development. My strongest objection to passing is that this team may already have the scarce asset: trusted access to merchants, demonstrated by introductions before meaningful public traction. That could support a compact, profitable payments business. One verifiable condition would reopen my decision: a priced seed proposal below $10 million post-money whose finite budget reaches break-even using an existing merchant cohort’s reconciled repeat transactions and contribution margin. That would connect the relationships, the fee model, and my check to one underwritable outcome.
