# Hundred -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Hundred has a concrete inspection proposal and an undocumented investment proposition. Post-money valuation, competing offers, and whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital are all unknown. I will not infer an auction from YC membership. But I cannot underwrite a cheap, non-consensus entry from financing silence either. The second question is equally unanswered: what specific truth does this round buy? Deployment to three founding partners is underway, but the dossier supplies no capital allocation, deadline, or measurable pass/fail milestone.

The strongest reason to take Hundred seriously is Erica Diaz’s experience connecting consumer-hardware engineering with suppliers in China and Mexico. Five-and-a-half years designing hardware at Meta, Google, and Magic Leap gives her direct exposure to the boundary where this product must work. Gosserand brings production-software experience from Twitch; Liao brings quantitative and ML research. That fits my preference for people who can translate between technical systems and physical operations. It earns meaningful founder credit, although the dossier names no shipped product or completed factory installation attributable to this team.

The bull case is stronger than “AI finds defects.” Controlled illumination, ambient-light exclusion, and whole-tray imaging could make inspection repeatable enough that manufacturers change their receiving workflow. Calibrating from one acceptable part could reduce setup friction. If the photographs then become the evidence used to resolve supplier disputes, Hundred could own a useful workflow and proprietary history. That is the neglected physical operating system I am willing to finance. But the ownership and customer behavior remain hypothetical: three unnamed deployments underway establish neither completed adoption nor purchases caused by proprietary access. Elementary’s existing-camera retrofit also means a customer can pursue automation without adopting Hundred’s dedicated station.

Now delete the next financing. Preferential founding-partner pricing tells me nothing about whether equipment, installation, monitoring, and customer-specific changes produce cash. There is no disclosed billing unit, margin, burn, or route to break-even. Those omissions do not establish bad economics; they leave economics unproven. Likewise, the claimed sub-100-micrometer inspection capability lacks independent false-accept, false-reject, and throughput results. The relevant automation risk is whether exceptions and onsite work consume the savings. The dossier does not show that happening, but it provides no operating evidence that resolves it.

I pass at this point. Hundred fits my physical-world translation pattern, and the dossier establishes neither financing pressure nor confused founder control. Still, the exceptions I make to missing financing gates require hard conversion, retention, cash economics, or unusually strong operating receipts. Deployments underway do not yet meet that standard. My flip condition is a documented, sub-$10-million post-money round funding a dated factory trial with pre-agreed accuracy and throughput thresholds, whose customer acceptance triggers a paid rollout at a disclosed positive contribution margin. That would connect scarce capital, a finite experiment, customer action, and a business that can finance its own progress.
