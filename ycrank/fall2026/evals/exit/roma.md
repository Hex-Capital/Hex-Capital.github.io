# Roma -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a work-management platform is Roma’s strongest path. Roma 2.0 shipped two-way delegation across agents, with task histories that record decisions, edits, and attribution. Roma says some users connected at least 10 agents, and its CEO reported that one user completed 39 follow-ups in a week with Claude on the original app. Those reports suggest a workflow worth testing. A buyer could have a reason to own Roma if paying teams repeatedly rely on its cross-provider handoffs and accumulated histories, and owning those workflows would win or retain customers that a native feature or partnership could not. The launch establishes that Roma has shipped the capability. Paid retention, measured customer benefit, and the capital and runway to prove both remain open. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

The memo supplies no deal terms or buyer pricing evidence. Using the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons, the eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%, versus $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, transaction fees, and senior waterfall deductions, so enterprise value equals distributable equity and the holder receives 3% pro rata before tax. All consideration is assumed to be cash at closing, with $0 each in stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Gross holder cash | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---|---|
| Modest sale at $40M enterprise value | Year 4 | $1.2M | 2.4× |
| Strong sale at $100M enterprise value | Year 7 | $3.0M | 6.0× |
| Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale stays out of reach, and cash is reinvested | Through year 8 | $0, with shares retained | 0× realized |

The strong scenario clears the hurdle at 3% and yields only 4.0× at 2%. Neither sale price is supported by buyer economics in the memo. Third-party service dependencies warrant checking whether integrations would carry over to a buyer. The memo leaves IP ownership and transfer terms unverified and supplies no independently supported route to cash for this holder. (*Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

ClickUp already offers assignable agents, and Notion connects its agents to external tools through MCP. A platform could build enough delegation in-house to satisfy customers. Roma would then need to show that its cross-provider workflows and task histories create enough incremental customer value to justify an acquisition. The overlap makes that a decisive gap. The memo leaves open whether native tools can match the cross-provider workflow, so the structural Pass gate does not apply. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a retained cohort of paying teams show a sustained improvement in completed cross-provider handoffs over a prospective buyer’s native tools, large enough that the buyer’s own build-versus-buy economics support roughly $83 million in distributable equity value? Production results paired with a buyer-side estimate of incremental customer wins or retention at that scale would move Roma to Reach out.
