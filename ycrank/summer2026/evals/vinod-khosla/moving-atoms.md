# Moving Atoms -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is not yet written. An $81.64 billion “physical AI” market and a $6.88 billion simulation market are consultant categories, not quantities Moving Atoms will transform. The dossier says robot checkpoints can take days, but not what those delays cost, how much physical testing Atom 1 eliminates, or whether faster evaluation changes robot deployment by 10% or 100x. I cannot price the upside merely because robotics is important.

There is one real technical signal. Atom Harness scored 56.6 across 198 Physics-IQ Verified cases, ranking second overall, and Moving Atoms distinguishes itself from Isaac Sim and MuJoCo by generating conditions from footage and prompts. But this does not establish a 5–10x discontinuity. Physics-IQ measures generated-video prediction, not whether simulated failures predict failures on physical robots. The harness also delegates generation to Seedance 2.5 and classification to Claude Opus 5. Today this looks more like intelligent systems integration than a proprietary world model.

The largest kill risk is therefore simulation-to-reality validity. The public milestone attacks benchmark performance instead. There is no stated correlation threshold, robot trial, experiment cost, or stop condition. One decisive result would flip my view: three independent design partners showing that Atom 1 predicts deployed policy failures with at least 0.8 rank correlation while reducing physical evaluation time or cost by 10x. That would convert a leaderboard result into risk removal.

The bull case stings. Shehryar brings large-scale video-model experience from TikTok; Ahmad ranked first in his Oxford college and left during his second year; two mathematically strong brothers have already produced a credible benchmark and attracted 41,000 launch views. Moving from hosted MolmoAct2 access to evaluation infrastructure may reflect tactical flexibility, and a tiny team using evaluation as a platform-shaped wedge fits a pattern I take seriously. Yet the dossier documents neither the evidence that caused this change nor a complementary robotics recruit, repeat customer usage, or proprietary data loop. The exceptional-founder override is not earned by credentials and a large market estimate alone.

The economic test is also missing. No price, compute cost, integration burden, retention, or gross-margin model is public. Dependence on two outside model vendors could improve quickly, but it could equally leave Moving Atoms reselling expensive inference plus founder labor while NVIDIA, Google, and billion-dollar robotics-model companies absorb the feature. The red flag is not competition; it is financing distribution and integrations before proving that the generated world predicts the physical one.

My 36-month base case is that Moving Atoms pivots into vendor-neutral robotics evaluation and integration software but remains below $3 million ARR because customers will not trust world-model grades without physical-policy validation. I would not buy the option today. The downside is one check, but the dossier has not yet demonstrated an uncapped upside mechanism—only a promising technical team and an experiment aimed one layer away from the decisive risk.
