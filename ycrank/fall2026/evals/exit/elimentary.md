# Elimentary -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest route. A credit servicer or fund administrator could buy Elimentary’s client-configured reconciliation, covenant testing, and reporting workflows and deploy them across its existing clients. TresVista announced a multi-year partnership with initial agents live. A customer reported that a filing reconciliation covering more than 500 checks went from roughly 20 hours to under 30 minutes of agent runtime plus about an hour of human sign-off. Concord’s acquisition of Finley shows that a servicer has bought adjacent credit software. Elimentary’s potential ownership premium depends on whether a buyer could reuse validated workflows across enough clients to earn more from owning them than from licensing them. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

A proposed seed-stage milestone is to deploy the same core workflows at two additional paying fund managers and measure implementation cost, review time, and savings across several monthly closes. The live TresVista rollout and Elimentary’s separately reported 25-plus production agents make that test plausible. Client-by-client validation could increase the capital required, and the memo leaves contract economics, burn, and runway open. A sale would also depend on rights to transfer the core software and client configurations, retain access to sensitive data, and secure any required consents. The memo leaves those terms open. (*Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

**Illustrative proceeds.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million gross before investor taxes. Under a simple pro rata split, that requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% hurdle is $88.3 million of enterprise value, before any additional fees or senior claims. The memo gives no transaction price for Finley or Elimentary revenue figure, leaving the realizable sale price unsupported. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

| Eight-year scenario | Gross cash to the holder at 3% |
|---|---:|
| Modest sale at $25 million enterprise value, less $5 million of assumed net debt | $600,000 (1.2×) |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value, less $5 million of assumed net debt | $2.85 million (5.7×) |
| Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested | $0 realized, with shares still held |

The memo leaves each backup route unevidenced. It gives no predictable cash flow or financing basis for a sponsor buyout, eligible share demand for a tender, operating scale or reporting readiness for an IPO, or excess cash and payout terms for dividends or buybacks. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

TresVista already has access through a multi-year partnership. If administrators can capture the same savings that way, ownership may add little. Allvue already integrates overlapping loan workflows into its ledger, and Elimentary’s deal-by-deal validation could limit the savings from rolling out the product across a buyer’s client base. That combination could sustain a business while leaving strategic bids below the holder’s hurdle. The memo establishes neither a structural barrier to a sale nor a ceiling on supported sale proceeds, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Could a credit servicer justify paying about $88 million in enterprise value at the modeled 3% ownership based on the incremental economics of owning Elimentary? Buyer-side estimates grounded in paid rollouts, including implementation costs and the economics of licensing the same workflows, would move the action to Reach out if they supported that price within eight years.
