# Letter -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A web deployment platform could buy Letterpress-1 to turn search and visitor signals into previewable Git pull requests inside its release workflow. The potential advantage of ownership is control over the brand evaluations and sandbox that govern which changes are safe to ship. Letter reports those capabilities. The memo documents a beta launch and subscription pricing, and Letter claims more than 100 customers and thousands of pull requests. Those claims suggest early use, with paid adoption, retention, and production impact still unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A paid platform pilot that measures review time saved and repeat usage on live sites could test the ownership case. The reported pull-request volume gives Letter a starting point for that test. The memo lists YC financing entries, while burn, runway, and the cost of reaching the milestone remain unknown. IP ownership, customer contract assignability, and holder rights are also unverified. The memo supplies no independently supported fallback that could pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Facts)

The illustrative seed check is $500,000 at a $10 million post-money valuation, with no follow-ons. Relative dilution of 40% takes ownership from 5% to 3%. The eight-year 5× cash objective is $2.5 million, requiring $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

Both sale scenarios below assume cash at closing, zero net debt or other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, and simple pro rata allocation before investor taxes. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each modeled at $0. Actual note treatment and the shareholder waterfall are unknown. The prices are illustrative sensitivity inputs. The memo supplies no usable acquisition valuation evidence. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A platform could combine a general-purpose coding agent with SEO tooling and use Letter’s MCP interface or a commercial integration to cover much of the workflow. The memo identifies that overlap and provides no measured production advantage that would justify paying for ownership. A substitute could cap the price below the hurdle, but its adequacy and cost are unproven. The evidence supports Watch, and the modeled $0 downside alone does not trigger Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a web deployment platform validate that owning Letterpress-1 creates enough incremental value over building or partnering to clear the investor’s proceeds hurdle? A paid production pilot with repeatable review-time savings and a platform-validated comparison of build and partner costs would move the action to Reach out if the economics support a price that leaves at least $2.5 million for this holder after dilution and senior claims.
