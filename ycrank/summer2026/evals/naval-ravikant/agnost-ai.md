# Agnost AI -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The founders have learned through action. Shubham worked in analytics at Cisco and became Formbricks’ first hire; Parth built terabyte-scale pipelines at Microsoft. More importantly, they already shipped an MCP analytics product processing roughly 2.5 million calls monthly, then moved toward conversation-level diagnosis. The present system handles more than 1 million events daily and is reportedly used by teams at Google, Exa Labs, and Corgi Insurance. This is credible specific knowledge, not résumé theater.

The decisive question is what remains when LangSmith, Braintrust, Arize, or Langfuse copies the workflow. Agnost clusters intents and violations, connects them to traces, and proposes reviewed pull requests tested against historical conversations. That is useful integration, but the dossier establishes neither pooled proprietary data nor a model that improves across customers. Retention can be as short as seven days. Customer-specific histories may create modest switching costs, but a private archive is not yet a compounding asset.

The market is growing: agent observability and governance is projected to expand from $1.23 billion in 2025 to $8.62 billion in 2031. Yet Agnost has not demonstrated the causal path from its conversation-analytics wedge into a broader transaction layer. The incumbents already own adjacent tracing, evaluation, deployment, and prompt-management workflows. A growing category creates demand; it does not decide who captures it.

The strongest bull case is hiding in the usage. One million daily events at pre-seed, named technical users, 77 Hacker News points, and an installable developer product could be the beginning of a proprietary feedback system. The posted $49 and $499 monthly plans also permit direct monetization without advertising. If reviewed fixes generate labeled outcomes that improve diagnosis across deployments, today’s feature could become tomorrow’s model. But the dossier does not show paid customers, retained cohorts, completed transactions, or cross-customer learning. Pricing proves intent to capture value, not captured value.

The unstated pattern does not yet resolve in Agnost’s favor. Customer conversations become useful product knowledge, but customers are not shown creating value or distribution for one another; every tenant appears isolated. The capital-efficient-prototype exception also fails for now because there is no demonstrated behavioral network or unusually durable product language that neutralizes suite encroachment. No integrity, morale, or artificial-deadline red flag is disclosed, so there is no founder veto. Absence of a veto is not a moat.

My base case is that within 36 months Agnost remains below $3 million ARR and repositions as an agent product-feedback module inside a broader observability workflow. I would not invest yet. One condition flips the decision: independently verified evidence that adding a customer measurably improves failure detection for existing customers through a proprietary cross-customer model. Then usage becomes leverage rather than workload.
