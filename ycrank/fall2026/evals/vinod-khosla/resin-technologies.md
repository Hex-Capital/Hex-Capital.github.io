# Resin Technologies -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Resin Technologies could make scarce electronics expertise substantially more available. Requirements become editable schematics; datasheets become footprints; boards get placed and routed. If that works reliably, engineers can spend less time translating intent into files and repeating fabrication cycles. The dossier identifies a $6.1 billion PCB design software market in 2026, but does not quantify engineering labor displaced, fabrication failures avoided, or additional hardware projects made possible. There is a consequential bottleneck here. The magnitude of its removal remains unproven.

The decisive question is whether Resin reduces the work required to produce a **functioning board**. Generating a schematic quickly accomplishes little if verification consumes the time saved. Resin’s terms acknowledge incorrect wiring, unsuitable components, and misread datasheets. Its proprietary routing engine might address a genuine engineering constraint, but there is no benchmark against a named incumbent measuring total engineering hours through successful fabrication. Flux already offers an integrated editor and AI assistance. Resin has described a mechanism; it has not established a technical discontinuity. General-purpose model access does not prove this is merely a wrapper, either.

The strongest argument against passing is the founders’ combination of skills. Anmol Jawandha brings enterprise AI deployment experience from You.com; Koosha Rezaiezadeh brings robotics and electronics experience, including Bosch and Kodak. That combination fits my unstated preference for teams joining several necessary disciplines around an expensive physical workflow. An integrated schematic-to-routing system could also remove errors introduced at tool boundaries. This is a credible bull case. However, those credentials do not document rapid belief revision, difficult recruiting, or a proprietary learning loop. I would not turn relevant biographies into evidence of those separate qualities.

Resin’s largest apparent kill risk is correctness after accounting for human review. The dossier supplies no company-defined experiment, numerical acceptance threshold, budget, or stop condition for resolving it. That receives the missing-evidence default. It does not justify accusing the founders of spending irresponsibly. Likewise, the October 2026 launch and 176 LinkedIn followers establish visibility, not repeated engineering use. Missing revenue at this stage is ordinary.

The $199 monthly Pro price and $149 per-user Team price make trying Resin accessible. They do not establish unsubsidized economics. Credits obscure the cost per completed design, and the dossier provides neither inference costs nor the review labor required to reach a working board. My relevant price test is total cost per successful design, including rework. There is no evidence of permanent subsidy dependence; there is also no demonstrated cost advantage.

I pass with my own money. The known exceptions do not supply an override: the dossier establishes neither an extraordinary founder commitment tied to a quantified societal consequence nor demonstrated exponential capability progress. It also establishes no prohibited business category or founder misconduct. This is an evidence-limited technical opportunity. **My flip condition is an independently reproducible, matched-board trial showing Resin cuts total engineering hours through a functioning fabricated board by at least 5x versus an established ECAD workflow, without increasing failure rates, at positive contribution margin.** That would connect its product claims to the economic consequence worth purchasing.
