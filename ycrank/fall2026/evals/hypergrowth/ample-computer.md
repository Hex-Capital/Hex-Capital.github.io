# Ample Computer -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Reach out  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with builders using AI agents, Ample has a credible path to serving the same deployment job across markets. The memo documents a verified Claude connector, a CLI, direct signup, and 2,144 published deployment recipes. Ample says it detects dependencies from source code, provisions services, and returns a live URL. A customer testimonial about a registration site in Quibdó supports the portability inference. The product supports a plausible self-serve path to first deployment, while the memo leaves paid checkout unverified. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

Live apps create an ongoing hosting job. Published monthly tiers let accounts expand from 2 free apps to 10 at $20 and 50 at $99. That gives existing customers a concrete way to spend more as they deploy more apps, with renewal and upgrade behavior still unknown. Ample reported more than 4,000 users in 13 days. That shows launch adoption. The memo leaves realized revenue, paying-customer growth, and pre-revenue status unknown, so initial traction takes the rubric default. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

In a Pro-to-Scale upgrade scenario, the extra $79 per month would need to cover added compute, database, storage, delivery, email, support, and any capacity expansion attributable to the account. Automated provisioning offers a way to limit incremental setup labor. Workload costs and contribution remain unmeasured in the memo. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Launch users may deploy short-lived apps and stay on the free tier. If retained customers run increasingly resource-intensive apps within a fixed-price tier, hosting costs could rise without a corresponding increase in revenue. The structural downside gate stays clear because the memo supports reusable deployment across workloads and leaves the severity of any capacity or contribution constraint unresolved. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Do self-serve launch users become recurring paid production customers at positive incremental contribution? Follow a launch cohort through its first paid renewal and reconcile invoices with attributable acquisition, hosting, and support costs, including the cost of serving users who stay free. Recurring paid use with positive cohort contribution would support Reach out. Sparse renewals or weak contribution would shift the action to Watch.
