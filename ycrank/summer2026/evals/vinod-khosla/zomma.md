# Zomma -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Jihyun Kim helped launch a $1B multi-strategy hedge fund, giving Zomma unusually direct knowledge of broken financial workflows. Zomma’s analytical signature is technical difficulty without capital asymmetry: reliable computer use across changing portals, PDFs, and desktop applications is the strongest dimension, while Zomma’s commodity SaaS capital profile is the weakest. That can produce a useful business. It does not yet produce the probability-distribution shape I seek with my own money.

**Commonwealth Fusion** (2019) sat at the high rung because difficult, falsifiable engineering milestones repelled investors and demanded $115M before the commercial outcome was obvious. Zomma shares a weaker version of that mechanism: layout changes, pop-ups, indirect prompt injection, credential security, and irreversible financial actions create genuine reliability problems. Zomma diverges because Anthropic and OpenAI already supply the underlying computer-use capability, human approval contains rather than solves autonomy risk, and no proprietary model, verification architecture, or exclusive dataset is documented. Zomma has engineering difficulty. Commonwealth had science and capital risk that became the moat.

**DoorDash** (2013) would have failed my required-capital screen because consumer logistics did not need a $115M scientific milestone; ambition and network effects supplied the exception. Zomma shows the same low-capital shape without DoorDash’s demonstrated network effect: forward deployment, workflow workshops, and direct enterprise sales can begin with ordinary venture financing. Zomma discloses no training program, fleet, plant, hardware stack, or other use for more than $50M before commercial validation. I am over-resourced for Zomma unless its reliability layer becomes foundational infrastructure rather than customer-specific integration work.

The $7.78B financial-services agent market gives Zomma a real but bounded consequence mechanism. Over 18 months, every additional reconciliation or KYC deployment could compound workflow maps, corrections, and audit histories, but human approval before final actions preserves much of the labor and liability structure Zomma claims to replace. Zomma currently automates movement through the incumbent regime rather than breaking the regime. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself,” but a faster back office inside asset management is still an optimization play, not the **OpenAI** (2019) displacement of a civilizational variable.

Varun Arumugam built ML systems at Bronco AI and verification infrastructure at Apple, while Cashel Fitzgerald built vision systems before working on software handling $20B in annual transaction volume. Those credentials establish execution capacity for Zomma, not defiance of expert consensus. Zomma would move up a tier if Arumugam or Fitzgerald demonstrated that prevailing computer-use architectures are structurally incapable of regulated execution and produced a falsifiable verification breakthrough that OpenAI, Anthropic, Zalos, and Eloquent AI had missed. No such controversial thesis appears in the dossier. Experts often extrapolate the past; Zomma’s founders currently appear to be applying their past directly.

**Slash** earned its exception through a documented pivot that exposed founder learning rate under pressure. Zomma has no equivalent “we were wrong” moment: Kim’s path from Goldman Sachs and Endowus to Arrowpoint and then finance automation is coherent, while Zomma’s product history appears linear. I apply the absent-evidence default rather than penalizing a Summer 2026 company for being young. Zomma could still learn quickly, but Zomma has not yet demonstrated learning through a pivot, failed hypothesis, or public thesis revision.

The unnamed hedge funds and asset managers using Zomma make my 36-month forecast concrete: by July 2029, Zomma will have narrowed from general computer-use agents to investment-reconciliation software and will generate $2M-$5M ARR, but it will not control a general financial-operations agent platform. The mechanism is straightforward. Brittle interfaces and approval requirements reward specialization, while Zalos, Eloquent AI, Stacks, OpenAI, and Anthropic compress the horizontal layer. Zomma becomes a respectable vertical software vendor. That is a good outcome for founders and an uninteresting outcome for consequence-first capital.

**DoorDash** is the strongest objection because Zomma could use tedious forward deployment to accumulate workflow data and become the default execution layer for financial institutions, just as DoorDash turned operational grind into a $72B network. Kim’s $1B hedge-fund launch experience, active use by unnamed financial firms, and a reported long workshop waitlist give that bull mechanism substance. The objection loses because Zomma shows no exclusive data, retention evidence, structural barrier, or network effect, while three funded substitutes and two frontier-model vendors already attack the layer. I would flip to Invest if Zomma documents at least $1M ARR with greater than 120% net retention and demonstrates a proprietary verification system sustaining over 99.9% correct execution without human approval across three named custodians.
