# Ailit -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A, scarce complements, is the strongest fit.** Ailit proposes supplying freight railroads with charged battery tenders at scheduled stops. Off-board charging could let existing locomotives keep running while depleted packs recharge, with onboard diesel available as backup. If utility capacity at suitable stops is scarce, a qualified swap network with dependable access to those sites could retain value even when competitors have cheap frontier models. Cheaper intelligence could improve pack scheduling and site load management. Ailit is developing AilitOS for those tasks, and the memo provides no measured improvement over conventional scheduling. Path B remains unproven because the memo identifies no newly viable transaction opened by lower delivery labor. (The Idea; Key Risks)

The Richmond prototype demonstrates that the team built and staged full-scale hardware. Loaded-haul performance and repeatable swap times remain unverified. The CTO’s prior battery integration and vehicle certification work supports a credible next test. The announced 2027 deployment is a plan, and the memo documents no railroad commitment of budget, equipment time, or integration work. That leaves a plausible U.S. seed-stage fit with limited direct evidence on the investment gate. (Market & Traction; Founders & Team; The Idea; Key Risks)

**Illustrative route economics.** Assuming diesel costs 100 for equivalent freight work, Ailit’s proposed discount implies a battery-service charge of 80 for full displacement. Any diesel still burned reduces the railroad’s savings. At that tariff, Ailit needs enough paid throughput to cover electricity and charging losses, site operations, and the annualized cost of grid connections and pack inventory, including spares and replacements. The reported decline in average pack prices helps one input. The memo leaves route utilization and delivered costs unmeasured, so the margin on adding the next site remains unknown. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Countercase

With abundant capital, competitors could secure comparable sites or meet the same uptime requirement through Voltify’s in-motion charging or Wabtec’s dedicated battery locomotive. Ailit’s patent application has undisclosed scope, and the memo establishes neither durable site rights nor any operating advantage that improves with deployment. A cheaper diesel baseline could also compress the tariff while site and pack costs persist. These risks keep Ailit below the Reach out gate. The memo leaves equivalent substitution and loss of control unproven, so the Path A override does not apply. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can the planned 2027 deployment qualify one representative loaded freight route as a commercially viable swap service that Ailit can control? A railroad-verified trial would need to show repeat swaps within scheduled dwell, diesel-comparable uptime, and positive route economics at the proposed discount, backed by a commercial commitment and durable access to the required swap sites and grid capacity. That result would support Reach out if rescoring reaches 65 or higher with at least Medium confidence. Failure on route economics or access without a credible remedy would move the action to Pass.
