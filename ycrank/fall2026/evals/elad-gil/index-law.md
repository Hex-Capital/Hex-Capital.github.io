# Index Law -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Better drafting models let Index Law sell legal work directly to businesses that previously coordinated outside counsel themselves. The economic variable is how many legally usable matters Mike can complete per dollar of human oversight. Persistent context could reduce repeated explanation; selective escalation could reserve expensive lawyers for difficult decisions. That is a plausible market mechanism. The dossier does not establish the completion rate or oversight threshold at which it works, so I cannot yet underwrite the central proposition.

The strongest evidence is the company-reported turnaround below 60 minutes and quotes up to 80% below traditional firms. Those are meaningful potential changes in delivery economics. But a lower quote does not establish a lower delivery cost, especially when independent lawyer fees are charged separately. Forthcoming pricing of $149 monthly for Pro and $499 for Teams creates a specific constraint: frequent specialist intervention could consume the subscription economics. Index Law needs to show that automation removes enough work to expand margins while preserving an acceptable legal outcome.

Mike has more architectural substance than a document-answering interface. Persistent company memory, contract preparation, negotiation workflows, and counsel referrals could give better models an increasingly useful operational context. Those disclosed capabilities earn credit; agent harnesses under development do not establish a defensible advantage. Sending still requires configuration and confirmation, and independent lawyers provide regulated advice. These are sensible boundaries, but they leave responsibility for the completed matter divided. Legora already describes multistep agents, while Crosby integrates delivery and liability. As this market crystallizes, Index Law needs a reason the business buyer stays with its coordination layer.

The strongest argument for investing now is that the founder has personally experienced the purchasing problem, previously operated an AI company, and is building for a different buyer from legal-professional workspaces. Pativada’s reported $20 million-plus booked revenue belongs to his prior venture, but it does support experience operating a business. Mike also launched only on November 12; limited public demand evidence three days later says little about eventual demand. Still, 191,088 launch-post views and 169 templates measure reach and distribution inventory. Reported contract outcomes supply a small amount of usage evidence, without establishing repeated reliance or willingness to pay.

My pattern of investing across a technology transition makes this category interesting, but category exposure alone does not select Index Law as a winner. The relevant red flag is claiming software economics while retaining substantial professional-service delivery costs; the dossier leaves that unresolved rather than demonstrating it. The research-stage exception does not apply: there is no documented frontier-research team and capital path that would substitute for commercial evidence. I would pass on a personal check today. **My flip condition is a verifiable cohort of paying businesses repeatedly completing matters through Mike at positive contribution margin after model costs and all required lawyer work, including separately billed fees in the customer’s total cost.** That would test the controlling belief directly.
