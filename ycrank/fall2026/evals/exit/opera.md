# OpEra -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a supply-chain planning or ERP vendor is OpEra’s strongest path to liquidity. OpEra describes an optimizer that plugs into existing systems and reruns plans when site constraints change. A buyer could sell that capability into its installed base if OpEra can reuse the software across sites without rebuilding each implementation. The memo records a public launch. The company reports that the founders’ MIT retailer project cut delivery costs by 38% and increased on-time deliveries by 10%. It separately reports a 10.4% higher premium over market for a sugar producer and 54% fewer unfulfilled orders for a services marketplace. Those results suggest customer value, while paid production use and sustained performance remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

A paid pilot that expands to a second site with comparable savings and limited new engineering would make the acquisition case more credible. The one-site offer, backtesting and live-data pilot process, and founders’ optimization experience support testing that milestone. Pilot funding, deployment cost, and time to rollout are unknown. (The Idea, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

With deal and investor terms undisclosed, the benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, starting ownership of 5% diluted to 3% without follow-ons, and a target of $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years. The sale scenarios are sensitivities. Both assume an eligible holder sells the entire stake for cash at closing within eight years, with proceeds allocated pro rata after $5 million of assumed debt and deal adjustments and with no preference claims, escrow, or earnouts.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M leaves $45M distributable equity | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $5M leaves $95M distributable equity | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested and no eligible share sale or distribution occurs within eight years | $0 |

The hurdle is about $83.3 million in distributable equity, or $88.3 million in enterprise value under these adjustments. Selling only 80% of the stake in the strong case would yield $2.28 million. The memo gives no basis for a clearing sale price, leaves transfer and holder rights unverified, and provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A planning vendor could use Timefold’s overlapping scheduling and routing APIs and build its own integrations. If OpEra’s site rules and data mappings require substantial custom work at every deployment, acquiring the company may buy implementation capacity with limited value across the buyer’s installed base. The memo establishes neither that a substitute covers OpEra’s full workflow nor that the strongest plausible sale would leave the holder materially short. The Pass gate remains untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could a planning-software buyer justify once OpEra demonstrates a repeatable paid rollout? A buyer-backed indicative range grounded in customer-verified savings and second-site deployment costs would move the action to Reach out if it supports at least $2.5 million in holder cash within eight years under plausible deal terms. Without that evidence, the action stays Watch.
