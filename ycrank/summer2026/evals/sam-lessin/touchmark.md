# Touchmark -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Roman Yanushevskyi’s IOI gold medal and two Citadel Securities internships make Touchmark a technically formidable founder bet trapped inside my least attractive category: consensus AI infrastructure. Touchmark’s analytical signature is strong latent founder conviction paired with terrible sleeve fit. “Solving AI pricing” is a real problem, but quality-adjusted billing currently looks like another software layer between model output and an invoice—exactly where Metronome, Orb, Stripe, and Lago can collapse the supposed category into a feature.

**Drop.io** is the high rung on asymmetric conviction because I spent years pursuing a file-sharing primitive against free incumbents before Facebook bought it; the obsession existed before the financing narrative. Touchmark has ingredients for that temperament: Ilia Bolgov worked in product at Revolut, while Roman Yanushevskyi combined quantitative research with a Lovable AI internship. Touchmark diverges where it matters: one July 2026 announcement is not years of category writing, no public repositories demonstrate a technical obsession, and no evidence shows either founder manually operating a quality-priced AI product before deciding to sell the infrastructure.

**OpenAI** is the low-rung mechanism here: foundation-model economics became so legible and consensus-priced that seed investors started funding every adjacent abstraction as though “AI infrastructure” were itself differentiation. Touchmark exhibits that pattern almost perfectly—Summer 2026 YC, an AI-infrastructure label, a coming-soon website, and an SDK whose evaluation methodology is unpublished. Touchmark is not building a foundation model, but it inherits the same red flag: Orb or Metronome can treat a quality score as one more metered event, leaving Touchmark as SaaS on top of the AI cycle rather than a sleeve I already believe is structurally mispriced.

Ilia Bolgov’s 1,646 LinkedIn followers and Roman Yanushevskyi’s 838 do not constitute owned distribution. Over 18 months, founder-brand compounds when an audience supplies design partners, lowers acquisition costs, and makes the founder the durable asset; Touchmark instead appears dependent on founder-led enterprise outreach and YC introductions. Touchmark could build a practitioner community around AI-pricing disputes, benchmarks, and contract design, but no newsletter, podcast, Discord, or 10,000-person channel exists today, so the creator-cap-table mechanism never starts compounding.

Touchmark’s coming-soon website discloses no round price, financing terms, revenue, or named investors, so the value-seed test defaults against Touchmark. The score flips an entire tier if the SAFE is demonstrably below the Summer 2026 median and a tier-1 fund passed because quality-indexed billing looked too niche or too hard to underwrite. Without that evidence, Touchmark looks like the hot YC deck by construction: AI infrastructure, accomplished technical founders, a current pricing trend, and no visible entry-price edge. A secret at the wrong price is still a bad deal.

**Fin** began with a manual Wizard-of-Oz operation because the manual work exposed what software should eventually automate. Touchmark reports an SDK that scores outputs and adjusts billing, but Touchmark provides no named customer, accepted invoice adjustment, dispute history, or manually administered outcome-pricing contract. The financing therefore looks like a standard SaaS cap table funding several simultaneous tacks—evaluation accuracy, billing integration, buyer trust, and dispute resolution—rather than a bootstrappable service acquiring a venture-shaped software moment.

Metronome has raised $128 million and Orb has raised $44 million, so my 36-month forecast is that Touchmark pivots from standalone quality-adjusted billing into AI evaluation or observability middleware and remains below $1 million ARR rather than displacing either billing incumbent. Touchmark’s strongest asset is founder technical range, while Touchmark’s weakest is category structure; technically strong founders usually discover the adjacent problem, but a weak asset-class position prevents the original product from becoming the system of record.

Zendesk’s 2026 move to independently verified resolution pricing is the bull case that stings: Touchmark may be early to a real pricing-rail transition, and Bolgov’s Revolut product experience plus Yanushevskyi’s quantitative depth could turn quality verification into the regulatory-like moat that justified the **Ro** known exception. Touchmark would then resemble an unstated healthcare-rail pattern—capital intensity creating infrastructure defensibility—rather than generic AI SaaS. The dossier still loses that argument because Touchmark has no evaluation methodology, customer deployment, accuracy result, or dispute process, while Metronome and Orb already own the invoice path. One named customer paying at least $50,000 ARR with audited quality-based adjustments and a dispute rate below 2% would flip my verdict.
