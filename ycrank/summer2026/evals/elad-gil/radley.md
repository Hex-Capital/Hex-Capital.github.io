# Radley -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Radiology-specific AI is becoming deployable as provider capacity tightens, and the customer now buys completed interpretations rather than another software seat; the controlling variable is whether Radley can increase verified scans per radiologist hour enough to expand contribution margin after clinical-review costs. The dossier establishes the capacity problem—one in six scans lacks sufficient radiologist coverage—but does not quantify Radley’s throughput improvement, accuracy threshold, or target margin. That leaves the central market belief plausible but not yet validated.

Owning the practice is strategically important. It gives Radley contracts, clinical workflows, credentialing infrastructure, and a place to deploy agents without waiting for a health system to adopt another vendor. The pending acquisition would also bring $4.3M in annualized revenue. But acquired revenue is not product-market pull for the new operating model, and the deal has no disclosed closing date, financing terms, or integration milestones. The prior software product’s $2.2M of signed contracts and live deployment proves that these founders can sell into radiology; the pivot means it does not yet prove customers prefer Radley’s AI-operated practice.

The strongest bull case is the combination of that commercial history with unusually relevant operators. Maryam Jahed helped scale Carbon Health from 31 to more than 100 clinics, previously built Airo Health to six-figure revenue, and worked on an FDA-designated clinical product. Seak Meng Lay built Carbon Health’s engineering and EHR infrastructure through the same expansion. This is real second-time learning, not credential theater. The team may understand that clinical operations, rather than model output, are the durable product.

The opposing evidence is market structure. Radiology Partners already combines practices with proprietary technology across more than 25 million annual examinations. Rad AI reaches more than 40% of U.S. health systems, while Sirona has 12 million studies under contract. Radley therefore is not entering an empty category. It must show that agents change the cost curve rather than make an ordinary radiology practice marginally more software-aware. The PowerScribe 360 replacement window and 26.2% forecast teleradiology growth improve timing, but neither demonstrates lower interpretation cost, faster credentialing, or better margins.

The full-stack design could become durable because stronger models would improve task completion while Radley retains workflow state, permissions, clinical governance, and responsibility for the finished service. Today that is architectural intent. No proprietary dataset, deployed agent loop, validation program, or measurable workflow improvement is public. The known physical-business exception does not apply yet: unlike Anduril’s documented replacement of cost-plus economics with product-style margins, Radley has not shown an economic discontinuity.

My base case is that Radley closes the acquisition and reaches roughly $8M–$12M in annualized practice revenue within 36 months, but remains valued as a technology-enabled radiology operator because its AI margin advantage is unproven. I would monitor rather than invest today. The verifiable flip condition is two consecutive quarters showing at least 30% more finalized scans per radiologist hour, noninferior clinical quality, and a contribution-margin improvement of at least 15 percentage points against the acquired practice’s pre-deployment baseline.
