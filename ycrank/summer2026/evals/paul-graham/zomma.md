# Zomma -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Jihyun Kim helped launch a $1B multi-strategy hedge fund, while Zomma now automates the portals, PDFs, and desktop applications surrounding investment operations. Zomma is a schlep-native startup with an animal-evidence deficit. Kim plainly lived near the problem, but proximity is not the same as the cereal boxes. Zomma has unnamed financial firms using the product, yet the dossier contains no story showing what the founders did when a customer, interface, or investor said no.

**Coinbase** (2012) is the high rung for schlep: compliance was not incidental work but the work everyone else avoided. Zomma shares that mechanism. KYC, reconciliation, credential vaulting, exception review, and human approval across systems without APIs are an excellent nest of unpleasant problems. Zomma diverges because Coinbase’s schlep was embodied in Armstrong personally building an Android wallet after rejection; Zomma’s SOC 2 remains in progress, its customers are unnamed, and its ugliest operational scars are not yet visible. Still, 14/15: approval-gated financial operations are closer to Coinbase than to generic browser automation.

**Kiko** (2005) is the low rung for animal evidence because the eBay sale was an endpoint, not proof by itself that the founders would keep attacking the problem; Kan and Shear became interesting again only when they returned and built what became Twitch. Zomma has conducted a finance-operations workshop with a claimed long waitlist and has deployed working agents to unnamed hedge funds and asset managers. Those are tracks, but faint ones. Zomma has no named customer, revenue improvisation, rejection reversal, or unscalable stunt. The known exceptions do not rescue this: Airbnb had cereal boxes, and Loopt had Altman’s force of personality. Zomma therefore sits above the polished-demo low rung but below the Dropbox-style shipped-artifact rung.

Kim’s Goldman Sachs, Endowus, and Arrowpoint history makes Zomma plausibly organic rather than a market selected from an AI spreadsheet. The counterfactual is simple. If Kim built Zomma’s first workflow because Kim personally spent nights re-keying Arrowpoint data across custodian portals, Zomma rises toward Dropbox’s forgotten-USB-stick origin. If Zomma instead emerged from customer interviews after computer-use models became fashionable, it falls toward the generic AI-wrapper pattern. The dossier establishes direct exposure but not the decisive first-person wound, so the organic-idea score is strong rather than complete.

**Stripe** (2010) had hacker genealogy before it had a payments empire: Lisp work, Auctomatic, and two brothers already accustomed to shipping together. Zomma has relevant technical credentials—Varun Arumugam built verification infrastructure at Apple, and Cashel Fitzgerald built aerial-object-detection systems—but Zomma shows no public repositories, competitive-programming record, open-source artifact, or Hacker News trail. Credentials say the founders can build. Hacker artifacts would show that they build without permission. Zomma gets partial credit for difficult prior engineering, not the Collison presumption.

Three Zomma founders cover finance operations, ML infrastructure, and computer vision, which can compound over the next 18 months: Kim finds the pathological workflows, Arumugam makes execution verifiable, and Fitzgerald improves screen perception. Zomma’s trio is structurally better than a solo founder attempting both enterprise deployment and agent reliability. But Zomma provides no evidence that Kim, Arumugam, and Fitzgerald worked together before the startup; shared Cornell affiliation for Kim and Fitzgerald is not a working history. The configuration is promising, while the chemistry remains unproven.

Zalos has raised $3.6M for overlapping computer agents, yet my 36-month forecast is that Zomma reaches $4M–$8M ARR by July 2029 as a narrow investment-operations vendor rather than a general computer-use platform. Zomma’s engine will not win merely by clicking better; Anthropic and OpenAI will keep commoditizing that layer. Zomma can win by accumulating workflow maps, exception histories, approval logic, and trust inside asset managers—the schlep Zalos and general-purpose models may not pursue deeply enough.

Eloquent AI already reports $0.5M ARR, Stacks has raised $35M, and Zomma has disclosed neither revenue nor a moat. The strongest bear case is that Zomma is a forward-deployed sales organization wrapped around commodity computer-use models: no public hacker trail, no named customer, no founder stunt, unknown cofounder history, and direct substitutes already funded. That case stings because it activates several of my actual red flags at once. Kim’s lived investment-operations experience and Zomma’s reported production users barely win today, so I would invest, but narrowly. One condition would flip the verdict: if within 12 months Zomma cannot name at least one paying financial institution with recurring production use and measured exception rates, I would conclude that the schlep story was a demo story.
