# Chromie -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The founders have shipped, but they have not yet earned specific knowledge in government contracting. Ananth built Promptly AI to more than 40,000 users and an acquisition; both founders have AI and browser-agent experience. More relevantly, they visited 50 GovCon offices and put a working product in front of multiple state and federal contractors. That is action, not résumé theater. But Chromie moved from extension building to browser infrastructure to insurance and then GovCon during 2026. The learning is real; its depth in this exact problem remains unproven.

The product’s weakness is structural. A Company Brain, source-traced extraction, opportunity scoring, and pursuit tracking are useful features, but the dossier identifies no asset that survives another team copying the code. GovDash, Procurement Sciences, GovSignals, and Govly already overlap heavily, with funded competitors reporting nearly 200 and more than 300 customers. Customer documents may eventually create switching costs, but private context is not automatically proprietary leverage. Today each deployment improves that customer’s workflow, not the system for every other customer.

The wedge addresses real spend. Government-contracting software was estimated at $8.1 billion in 2025, and the same company context could plausibly extend from discovery into proposals, pricing, compliance, and post-award delivery. Competitors have already demonstrated demand across those adjacent workflows. But this cuts both ways: the adjacency is large precisely because established competitors already occupy it. Chromie has named the battlefield, not an asymmetric route through it.

Three customer logos and Snovation’s report that Chromie found a missed RFI are meaningful pre-seed product evidence. They show utility, but not yet value capture: there is no disclosed price, revenue, renewal, or completed-contract outcome. Nor is there a permissionless loop. Each contractor appears to require founder-led selling, private onboarding, and continued source ingestion. The 50-office trip demonstrates energy; it does not demonstrate distribution that compounds while the founders sleep.

The strongest bull case is that young technical founders have repeatedly built, moved quickly, and found an overlooked buyer pain through direct fieldwork. I have made exceptions when an early product lacked monetization or a user network but already revealed an unusual, capital-efficient product language. Chromie does not yet clear that exception. The product category is legible, competitors are well financed, and the claimed future moat is still an inference. The rapid pivots are a red flag because persistence can become reaction-chasing, although the dossier supplies no evidence of integrity problems, artificial deal pressure, or founder misconduct.

My 36-month prediction is that Chromie either pivots into proposal and post-award automation or remains below $2 million ARR as standalone procurement discovery is absorbed into broader suites. I would pass today. One condition flips the decision: a cohort of at least 10 contractors renewing at $25,000-plus annual contracts because Chromie’s proprietary recompete predictions repeatedly surface opportunities before competing platforms.
