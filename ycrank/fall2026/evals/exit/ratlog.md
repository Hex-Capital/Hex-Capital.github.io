# RATLOG -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by an established launch provider is the strongest path. The memo records a constellation operator with hundreds of flight-ready satellites awaiting launch. RATLOG proposes a reusable launcher with a simplified engine and reports $400 million in signed launch reservations. Customer identities, deposits, and cancellation terms remain undisclosed, so the reservations provide a demand signal with uncertain conversion. The ownership case is an inference. A launch provider could buy the engine technology to bring it into its own fleet faster than it could develop an equivalent, if flight-ready tests establish a material cost or schedule advantage and licensing cannot deliver comparable economics. The memo supplies no buyer-specific evidence that such an advantage exists. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

RATLOG reports a prototype hot-fire in four months for under $30,000. The memo records a €30,000 grant and YC backing. The company targets a flight-ready engine in 2027, a first-stage test in 2028, and orbit in 2029 or 2030. The prototype is a concrete step toward the first milestone. The capital required for scale-up and the funding available to complete it remain unquantified, and launch-site selection is still open. The memo also leaves the entity holding the engine IP and the applicable transfer rights unverified. It supplies no separately supported route to cash for this investor. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The illustrative seed case assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. A 5× gross cash return by Fall 2034 requires $2.5 million to the holder, or $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership. The hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, transaction fees, and preference adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity and cash is allocated pro rata before investor taxes. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and separate compensation are each assumed to be $0. These prices are scenario inputs. The memo supplies no usable acquisition valuation or quantified buyer savings. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

An incumbent can already buy rideshare capacity, and the memo records an orbital booster recovery by Blue Origin. If RATLOG’s engine shows little economic advantage at flight scale, an incumbent could meet its needs through existing capacity, internal development, or a partnership. That would weaken both its reason to buy and the price it could justify. The memo establishes that these alternatives are credible, but it does not establish that they match RATLOG’s eventual economics or cap an acquisition below the proceeds hurdle. The structural Pass override is therefore unproven. Watch is appropriate while the ownership advantage remains unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)
