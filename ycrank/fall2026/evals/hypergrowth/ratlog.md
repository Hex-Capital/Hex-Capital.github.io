# RATLOG -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales with account expansion.** Constellation operators need recurring launch capacity as batches of satellites become flight-ready. Amazon Leo had hundreds of flight-ready satellites awaiting launch, a concrete signal of the job RATLOG is targeting. Existing rideshare and constellation missions show that operators buy this service, and the memo puts worldwide commercial launch revenue at $12.4B in 2025. RATLOG’s addressable segment and the geographic mix of its prospective buyers remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

RATLOG reports $400M in signed reservations and more than 10 flights described as booked. Customer identities, deposits, and cancellation terms are undisclosed, leaving buyer commitment uncertain. The YC launch post directs inquiries to the CEO, with no demonstrated route from inquiry to paid payload integration. The growth scenario assumes a standard payload interface and per-kilogram pricing let the same operator book later satellite batches with less sales and integration work. The memo gives no customer-level record of repeat bookings or usage. Serving overseas buyers from one site would also depend on payload transport, clearances, and compatible mission profiles. RATLOG is still evaluating launch sites. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

RATLOG could fill flights and still find that growth consumes capital as quickly as it adds revenue. The reported prototype hot-fire cost under $30K, and RATLOG’s engine design targets 90% fewer parts. Those are early engineering signals. Flight contribution would depend on paid kilograms and realized price, less incremental payload integration, launch operations, recovery, and refurbishment. Higher cadence could require additional vehicles or site capacity. With first orbit targeted for 2029 or 2030, the memo provides no operating cost data that establish either improving contribution or the proportional cost burden that would trigger a structural Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent constellation operator outside Europe put a material deposit at risk for a defined payload under RATLOG’s proposed per-kilogram terms? A buyer-verifiable agreement that specifies the intended launch site and payload integration requirements would move the action to **Reach out**. It would test paid demand across borders early enough to make a conversation about repeat sales and delivery economics worthwhile. Until then, **Watch**. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
