# Risklytics -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

$125.36 billion of brokerage spend gives Risklytics room to maneuver, while pending producer licensure makes every transaction permissioned. That pairing is Risklytics’s signature: a large-market opportunity trapped inside a low-leverage architecture. Risklytics has found a real timing discontinuity—explicit AI exclusions—but the current machine runs on licenses, carrier appetite, underwriter relationships, and founder labor. Code scales. Brokerage headcount scales brokerage.

**Uber** is the high rung on pivot-room: black cars became UberX because the underlying market was transportation, not limousines. Risklytics similarly sits above robotics, autonomy, data centers, energy infrastructure, construction, and manufacturing, and Risklytics has already moved from Ember’s natural-disaster scoring into commercial insurance. The analogy breaks where Uber’s marketplace could expand category by category through software, while Risklytics remains narrowly positioned around physical-AI exclusions with no published SAM and cannot place a single policy until licensed.

**Anchorage Digital** is the low rung on permissionlessness: an OCC charter made gatekeeper approval intrinsic to the product. Anchorage earned an exception because regulation became a moat inside a crypto category I had accumulated into since MetaStable. Risklytics has the dependency without the exception. Risklytics is still obtaining producer licensure, depends on admitted and E&S carriers accepting submissions, and shows no proprietary carrier access, policy data, or insurance-specific knowledge that converts permission into leverage.

Newfront’s $3.1 billion in annual premiums shows how the network mechanism fails over the next 18 months. Each Risklytics placement could improve an internal map of exclusions and carrier appetite, but one insured does not automatically make Risklytics more valuable to another insured, and one carrier does not permissionlessly attract the next. That is accumulated brokerage knowledge, not user-added network value. Newfront, Vouch, and Founder Shield can copy the intake workflow while bringing existing carrier relationships that Risklytics still has to build.

Samuel Gold’s investment-group background and Alexander Risio’s Harvard computer-science degree do not establish specific knowledge in underwriting, producer licensing, robotics liability, or commercial risk. Risklytics would flip a full tier if Gold had previously built a brokerage book or Risio had shipped a policy-exclusion parser validated across thousands of real forms. Risklytics instead shows no public repositories, no prior insurance operating history, and an Ember beta that addressed natural-disaster scoring before the brokerage pivot. Intelligence is legible. Obsession is not yet legible.

**Twitter** paired Jack Dorsey’s shipping energy with Ev Williams’s clean Blogger exit, making the 10-year-partner test observable before the business model existed. Risklytics offers no comparable history: the dossier does not explain how Gold and Risio began working together, divided authority, or handled a prior venture. Risklytics also presents no integrity warning, and moving quickly from Ember to a sharper January 2026 problem is evidence of energy rather than deception. Silence keeps the founders near neutral; it cannot manufacture a long-term partnership record.

Newfront’s nationwide licenses and carrier channel make the 36-month outcome concrete: by July 2029, Risklytics will have pivoted from standalone brokerage into AI-exclusion review software and will be acquired by Newfront for under $20 million while generating less than $1 million ARR. Risklytics has identified a valuable workflow, but Risklytics has not built a leverage architecture capable of defending the customer relationship. In markets governed by permission, the incumbent usually buys the tool and keeps the distribution.

January 2026 carrier exclusions create the strongest bull case: Risklytics could become the Anchorage Digital of physical-AI insurance, where licensure and specialized policy knowledge compound into a regulatory moat rather than a burden. Ember’s beta suggests Risklytics can build technical risk models, and the $125.36 billion brokerage market supplies ample pivot-room. The case still loses because Risklytics has no license, placements, carrier-access advantage, insurance founder history, or demonstrated proprietary model today. I would flip to Invest if Risklytics binds $10 million of annualized premium through a self-serve workflow with more than half of qualified demand arriving organically. Until then, it is labor and capital wearing an AI label. Pass.
