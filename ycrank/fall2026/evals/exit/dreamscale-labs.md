# Dreamscale Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition is the strongest path.** A GPU cloud provider could buy Dreamscale’s robot-specific transport, model optimization, and integrations to launch a managed robotics service faster. Ownership could matter if controlling the stack lets the buyer reproduce a production cost or reliability advantage across its customers. That ownership premium is an inference. The memo documents a HUD research integration and an A$100,000 grant. Dreamscale reports a joint excavator evaluation and a DreamZero benchmark of 320 ms versus 350 ms at half the GPU count. These signals support a production test, with financing and runway for that test still unknown. The memo supplies no buyer-backed price, documented transfer rights for the core stack, or independent route to shareholder cash. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** Using the illustrative seed terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted by 40% to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash before investor taxes, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds under a simple pro rata waterfall. The equity hurdle is $125 million at 2% ownership and $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale prices below are scenario assumptions. Each sale assumes zero net debt and transaction fees, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. The waterfall assumes no senior preference claims and all consideration paid in cash at closing, with zero stock, escrow, earnout, rollover, or compensation.

| Scenario | Timing | Holder cash | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest acquisition at $40 million | Year 5 | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong acquisition at $100 million | Year 7 | $3.0 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private ownership | Through year 8 | $0, with shares retained | 0× realized |

The strong sale clears the objective at 3% and falls to 4× at 2%. The memo provides no valuation evidence for either sale price. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and the company reinvests its cash.

### Countercase

A cloud provider could use Dreamscale’s usage-priced service, build on general-purpose GPU infrastructure, or use a competing hosted service for overlapping models. Customers that require offline operation can choose a local runtime. Dreamscale’s packet-recovery result came from one home Wi-Fi link with synthetic frames and simulated compute, leaving fleet reliability unproven. These facts make an acquisition premium uncertain. The memo leaves open whether substitutes can match production performance or cap the price below the hurdle, so the Pass override does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid robot-fleet deployment demonstrate an advantage that a GPU cloud provider would pay to own? Sustained fleet data on tail latency, reliability, and cost per robot-hour, paired with a prospective buyer’s comparison of acquisition, licensing, and internal development, would answer it. Move to **Reach out** if that comparison supports an ownership premium large enough to clear the holder’s proceeds hurdle.
