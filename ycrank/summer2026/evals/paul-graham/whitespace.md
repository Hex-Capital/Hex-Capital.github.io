# Whitespace -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what happened when something broke. Whitespace has connected a working product to ERP systems, inboxes, and business data, but the dossier never names a failed integration, resistant buyer, inaccurate agent, or other obstacle followed by a founder-built workaround. A two-to-four-week implementation claim suggests competence; it does not show what Alex Tung or Leon Yao did under resistance ([company platform](https://whitespacehq.ai/)). I cannot infer resourcefulness from credentials, so this criterion remains at its missing-evidence default.

The product itself is more convincing. Agents are already handling order intake, customer service, and inventory planning at Kingfisher Direct and Warwick, with company-reported productivity improvements above 80% ([customer stories](https://whitespacehq.ai/); [founder launch post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/linghong-yao_we-are-launching-whitespace-backed-by-y-activity-7475932900534898688-oYkP)). This is functioning software inside consequential workflows, not a diagram awaiting funding. But the measurements come from Whitespace, and there is no baseline methodology, error rate, revenue, retention, or repeated-usage history. The two customers establish a narrow well of potential need; they do not yet show that users pull the crude product back when the founders stop pushing.

The strongest evidence is why these founders entered the market. Both grew up in wholesale-distribution families. Tung later advised manufacturers and distributors at BCG, while Yao built optimization products for logistics-distribution companies at InstaDeep ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/whitespace)). They have encountered both the users and the ugly work: ERP mappings, inconsistent product data, email orders, accounting systems, exception routing, and operational mistakes that can propagate into real purchases. This is not an “AI for supply chains” idea manufactured from a trend. It is a founder-originated problem with a genuine schlep-heavy wedge. Their 12-year friendship and prior experience living and working together also remove the usual application-assembled-cofounder concern.

The bull case is quite good. A technical founder with machine-learning research experience and a commercial founder who helped an earlier company reach seven-figure ARR have shipped into two named distributors before Demo Day. Human corrections produce customer-specific knowledge, and a claimed three-month payback could make expansion unusually easy if it survives scrutiny ([company FAQ](https://whitespacehq.ai/)). This could qualify as the kind of concrete proof spike I often see before an exceptional company becomes obvious. But today it is still a resemblance to that pattern, not the pattern itself: there is no independently measured benchmark, usage curve, paid expansion, or customer referral.

The bear case stings because Whitespace calls itself an operating system while simultaneously covering inventory, procurement, sales, service, finance, and planning. Plato, Proton.ai, Canals, and Conexiom already attack overlapping workflows with substantially more funding. Breadth may therefore be an accumulation of demos rather than a wedge. The founders are asking for introductions to wholesalers, but there is no evidence that distribution depends on a large partner, so that red flag has not fired. The more important risk is subtler: broad industry engagement and impressive outcome claims may be investor-legible substitutes for evidence that one workflow is indispensable. No known exception repairs the failed resourcefulness gate; the working product is real, but the pull is not yet unmistakable.

My 36-month base case is that Whitespace reaches $2–4 million ARR from several dozen distributors but remains an integration-heavy vertical software company rather than the operating system for the industry. I would not write the check today. The single flip condition is verifiable: show that one founder personally converted at least five distributors onto the same initial workflow, with three expanding paid usage after six months, and document one major deployment blocker that the team worked around in weeks. That would simultaneously prove resourcefulness and pull, the two pieces the current dossier cannot supply.
