# Care GP -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Care GP’s strongest founder evidence is not Melvin Chen’s Oxford degree or investing résumé. It is a product sold into more than 150 clinics within six months, reaching US$200,000 ARR after 3.5 months, 85% pilot conversion, and profitability at roughly US$5,000 ACV. Three million processed documents and a reduction from six hours of allocation work to under 30 minutes show costly contact with the exact problem. The unresolved departure or status of former co-founders Ethan Atkins and Andy Bui limits attribution, but there is no evidence of unfair conduct or another integrity veto.

The product captures value directly. Clinics pay to remove administrative labor, usage reportedly grew 140% monthly, and zero churn was reported six months after launch. This is stronger than pipeline: the payer, price, deployed workflow, conversion, and economic outcome are visible. Care GP also tripled over 90 days through referrals and word of mouth without cold calls. Customers are creating distribution, although they are not yet creating the product’s supply or utility for one another. Referrals lower acquisition cost; they do not constitute a true network effect.

The central asymmetry is that adoption is compounding faster than defensibility. ISO 27001, Australian data residency, Bp Premier integration, and three million processed documents make Care GP operationally credible. They do not yet prove that the underlying advantage survives copied code. Facere already offers overlapping document and voice automation inside Bp Premier, while the reported 5%–20% human edit rate leaves room for a competitor or incumbent to match the experience. Clinic corrections could become proprietary learning, but “could” is not an asset. The dossier provides no accuracy comparison, retention cohort, or evidence that mature deployments outperform new ones.

There is nevertheless room to maneuver. The initial 7,000 Australian GP clinics lead naturally into email, billing, referrals, records transfer, and secure exchange, all using the same capability: interpreting patient communications and taking actions inside healthcare systems. The cited US$37.5 billion global administration-technology market confirms adjacent spend, but geography is not merely a dropdown. Australian integrations, residency controls, and workflow knowledge may not transfer cleanly overseas.

The bear case stings because this may be a fast-growing feature suite trapped between practice-management incumbents and heavily financed specialists. It lacks the permissionless contribution loop present in my strongest network investments, and none of the known exceptions excuses a copyable substrate merely because usage is strong. Still, the red-flag review finds no artificial deadline, disclosed founder misconduct, reassurance dependence, or fashionable demo standing in for adoption. Care GP has paying users behaving as though the product matters. That earns an angel check, not blind follow-on conviction.

I would invest as a bounded option and make the moat the next gate. My verifiable flip condition is simple: if, within twelve months, clinics with long usage histories show no lower document-edit rate than newly onboarded clinics, I pass on further investment. Data only compounds when later deployments become measurably better than cold starts.
