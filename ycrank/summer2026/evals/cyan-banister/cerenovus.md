# Cerenovus -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. An executive should be able to ask how the company actually works—not how its dashboards claim it works—and receive a living map of processes, dependencies, owners, and contradictory evidence. Cerenovus has a small leak from that future: live pilots and a product that resolves entities, runs 26 investigative sweeps, preserves citations, and ranks findings by impact. But the dossier does not map the intervening transitions from scoped deployment to trusted institutional memory. Permissions, entity resolution, outcome measurement, and continuous monitoring all still require proof. I can daydream the future for them; they have not yet made it legible themselves.

The present-day wound is real but blurry. Executives pay consultants and internal analysts to reconcile ERP, CRM, email, chat, contracts, and accounting records. Yet Cerenovus does not quantify that rent, identify a rule creating it, or show whose agency materially changes after a finding. “Fragmented data” is a condition, not an economic mechanism. More troublingly, the product requests broad access to some of a company’s most sensitive records. Read-only access, inherited source permissions, and separate data agreements are useful controls, but they are not an explicit custody architecture covering retention, deletion, reviewer access, and secondary use. This is a privacy gate, not a procurement footnote.

The three founders have demonstrated that they build. Jonathan Waldorf produced neutral-atom visualization tools, Oliver Moreland has serious mathematical and computer-science training, and the team shipped Compendium before developing the present product. That is better than a deck. It is not yet pre-company obsession with enterprise operations. Compendium launched in July 2026 as a $40-per-seat collaboration tool; by August, Cerenovus was selling scoped operational investigations to enterprises, private-equity firms, and advisers. That rapid repositioning may be customer learning, but the dossier provides no customer trail connecting the two. It also identifies three co-founders and a CTO without naming the accountable CEO. I do not ignore that ambiguity when the product itself assigns owners to organizational failures.

The strongest bull case is that Cerenovus is building the company model beneath the visible agent wave. Customer-specific entities, permissions, reviewed exceptions, source histories, and outcomes could compound into a difficult-to-replace operating graph. Live pilots suggest somebody has already accepted the integration burden. But today this is still a claimed asset, not a controlled bottleneck: no deployment duration, retention, accuracy, renewal, or realized-impact data is public. Glean already connects more than 275 tools and exceeds $100 million ARR; Hebbia has reached $13 million ARR; Coworker and Sana overlap on organizational memory. Cerenovus must prove that process reconstruction produces a uniquely valuable feedback loop, not merely another enterprise-AI interface over context assembled elsewhere.

The pre-revenue exception I made for CosmicBrain AI depended on three things together: a functioning prototype, signed industrial proofs of concept, and a founder whose robotics obsession predated the fashionable market. Cerenovus has the first piece in partial form—live pilots—but neither named commitments nor comparable founder history. Product Hunt’s 122 points and 130 followers belong to Compendium, while the current pilots have no reported repeated use, purchase, or operational outcome. The exception does not fire.

I pass. My 36-month prediction is that Cerenovus narrows into a service-heavy private-equity diligence or portfolio-operations product reaching roughly $1–3 million ARR, rather than becoming a general enterprise knowledge layer. I would flip if three named customers each run a second investigation within twelve months and verify at least $500,000 of realized savings under customer-auditable retention and deletion controls. That would turn the 26 sweeps from an impressive mechanism into costly user behavior—and prove the sensitive company model earns the right to persist.
