# UXDuck -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

UXDuck’s post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and proposed use of proceeds are undisclosed. I cannot tell whether my money would be scarce or what specific truth it would purchase. YC membership establishes neither an auction nor a bargain. I’m passing on the evidence available: there is a credible builder here, but the dossier does not establish the deal or the capital-funded experiment.

Butterworth is the strongest reason to resist that conclusion. His documented work spans mission-planning software, MAVLink integration, controller interfaces, and command-and-control applications. The current Rust runtime connects vehicles over serial, UDP, and TCP and supports ArduPilot/PX4 simulation. That is concrete engineering across developer tooling and drone operations—the sort of translation between systems I like. His prior employer’s 1,000-plus drones are not UXDuck deployments, but his operating experience deserves substantially more credit than his degrees or job titles.

The commercial question is who owns the relationship once that bridge works. UXDuck’s Apache-2.0 runtime makes its implementation accessible; Mission Planner and QGroundControl already supply overlapping workflows and extensibility. MCP and a plugin SDK could make UXDuck easier to build around, but public interfaces alone do not establish proprietary access. Five repository stars and zero forks tell me almost nothing about purchase behavior. The November 14 hackathon, with $500 in prizes, has yet to happen. I give neither a penalty for being early nor affirmative credit for an ecosystem that has not formed.

The strongest bull case is that Butterworth has already encountered the expensive integration problems that drone teams repeatedly solve internally. If UXDuck becomes their embedded development workflow, customer-specific integrations could make replacement painful even while the core remains open source. That fits my unstated preference for software reorganizing difficult physical operations. But the exception cases that would override missing financing evidence require hard receipts: demonstrated conversion, retained commercial use, acquisition interest, or an explicit alternative capital structure. None is documented here. Nor does the AI label itself trigger a rejection: command approvals and the 60-second default denial show attention to physical consequences. Persistent permissions leave reliability questions unresolved, and no measured field performance supports an automation-based economic advantage.

Delete the next financing from UXDuck’s plan and the financial result is simply unknown. Commercial support and managed features are inferred possibilities; pricing, revenue, margins, burn, and runway are undisclosed. I therefore use the missing-evidence defaults for economics and the venture moment rather than inventing either a capital factory or a profitable business. My verifiable flip condition would be a documented seed transaction below $10 million post-money funding a signed manufacturer deployment, with a dated acceptance test and contracted cash receipts sufficient to reach break-even within that financing. That would connect this founder’s demonstrated capability to a purchase, a finite experiment, and an investable price.
