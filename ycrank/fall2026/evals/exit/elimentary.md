# Elimentary -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A fund administrator or loan servicer could buy Elimentary to deploy its client-configured reconciliation, covenant testing, and reporting workflows across its customers. The memo records a multi-year TresVista partnership with agents live in fund administration and financial planning. A customer testimonial reports that 500+ filing checks went from roughly 20 hours of work to under 30 minutes of agent runtime plus about an hour of human sign-off. Concord’s acquisition of Finley shows that a servicer has bought adjacent software to bundle with its operations. Elimentary separately reports more than 25 agents in production. The acquisition hypothesis is that owning the workflow software and reusable calculation rules would make deployment across a buyer’s client base faster and cheaper than licensing or building them. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

The operating milestone is repeatable deployment across several funds, with measured implementation cost, recurring labor savings, and accuracy through successive reporting cycles. Live deployments support a path to that test. Client-specific validation could make each rollout labor-intensive, and the memo gives no rollout budget or runway. IP ownership, contract assignment, and client-data rights are unverified. The memo supplies no acquisition price, revenue figure, or buyer economics that can support a price range, and no independently evidenced fallback that could pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** The memo supplies no terms for the proposed check, so the seed case assumes one $500,000 check, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, or 5×. Under simple pro rata allocation, that requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

For both modeled sales, assume zero net debt and transaction fees, no senior claims, and a pro rata cash waterfall. Enterprise value therefore equals distributable equity consideration. Cash at close accounts for the full price. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. The sale prices below are scenario inputs.

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value → distributable equity | Holder cash before taxes | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $40M → $40M | $1.2M | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100M → $100M | $3.0M | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No sale or payout | $0, shares retained | 0× realized |

The strong sale clears the objective at 3% ownership and yields only $2 million, or 4×, at 2%. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and the company reinvests its cash.

### Countercase

TresVista’s existing partnership shows that an administrator can deploy the workflows without buying Elimentary. Allvue already offers overlapping automation inside its accounting platform, and deal-by-deal validation could erode the savings a buyer expects from a broader rollout. A partnership or an incumbent bundle could therefore cap acquisition value below the proceeds hurdle. The memo establishes credible substitutes, with their coverage and price impact still unresolved. That supports Watch. The structural Pass override would require evidence that the substitutes cap even a strong outcome below the hurdle or that prolonged private ownership is probable with no credible route to shareholder cash. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a fund administrator or loan servicer substantiate, using measured results across several client rollouts, enough incremental value from ownership over licensing or building to support a price that yields roughly $83 million in distributable equity within eight years? A buyer-validated analysis that clears that hurdle under plausible rollout costs and deal terms would move the action to Reach out. If the economics remain unverified, keep watching.
