# Asakana -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to an ERP vendor or distributor-software platform is Asakana’s strongest liquidity route. Its published workflow turns multilingual orders into ERP records and also handles returns, credits, and purchase orders. A buyer could use those capabilities to expand what it sells to existing distributor customers. That is an inferred acquisition rationale. The memo gives no buyer-side evidence of willingness to pay. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Asakana reports live deployments at U.S. seafood distributors and says they return 40%+ of order-desk time to selling. The memo records $20,000 in non-dilutive awards. A plausible next milestone is repeat paid deployments across multiple ERP systems, with measured time savings, acceptable error rates, and implementation costs low enough to support rollout across an acquirer’s customer base. The deployments give Asakana a starting point. Paid adoption, rollout costs, and the capital needed to reach that milestone remain unclear. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity. The scenarios below assume an eligible holder sells the full stake for cash within that window and participates pro rata. The sale cases assume zero net debt, deal deductions, and senior claims.

| Scenario | Illustrative holder proceeds |
|---|---:|
| Modest sale at $40 million enterprise value | $1.2 million, or 2.4× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | $3.0 million, or 6.0× |
| Prolonged private operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share purchase | $0 realized |

To bridge enterprise value to holder cash, subtract net debt and deal deductions, then apply the distribution waterfall and the fraction of the stake sold. In the strong case, about $16.7 million in aggregate deductions and senior payouts ahead of the holder would exhaust the cushion. The memo describes commission pricing and reports early funding entries, leaving realized revenue, support for either sale price, the actual waterfall, and transfer eligibility unverified. There is no evidenced independent liquidity route. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Choco already posts orders to existing ERPs and flags exceptions. Pepper already extracts orders from voicemail and email. A strategic buyer could extend capabilities it already has, especially if Asakana’s custom connections for legacy ERPs require substantial work at each new distributor. That overlap weakens the case for an acquisition premium. The memo leaves both the strongest credible sale price and any structural closing barrier unresolved, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

At what demonstrated scale would an ERP or distributor-software buyer value Asakana highly enough to leave at least $83.3 million distributable to equity? Buyer-validated economics grounded in repeat paid rollouts, measured workflow savings, and integration costs would answer it. A credible price range that clears the hurdle after debt, deal deductions, and senior claims would move the action to Reach out. A supported ceiling materially below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
