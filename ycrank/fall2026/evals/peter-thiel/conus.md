# CONUS -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

CONUS’s possible secret is that medical-device manufacturers will finance a neutral knowledge platform because accurate use of their products is worth more than preferential placement. Its institutional terms identify manufacturer services as a financing source while prohibiting paid preferential sorting. That is a concrete business hypothesis. But the dossier identifies no prevailing belief that named industry participants reject, nor an experiment establishing the proposition. I would test whether manufacturers renew payments when neutrality is enforced. Today, the secret remains a candidate.

The distinction between organizing knowledge and owning a market is decisive here. CONUS combines manufacturer portfolios with hospital implant inventories—a more specific starting point than general medical search. But 500,000 papers and 2,000 manufacturer documents establish coverage, not exclusivity. Hospital protocols could eventually make replacement expensive; the dossier labels that possibility as inferred. C8 Health and Incision already organize adjacent clinical workflows. Their presence does not prove that CONUS is interchangeable, but neither has CONUS demonstrated a monopoly mechanism that would prevent them from entering its segment.

The strongest argument against passing is that CONUS has already crossed an institutional boundary that many software companies never cross. More than 20 European hospitals are reportedly live, and the company names relationships with Johnson & Johnson, Stryker, Zimmer Biomet, and B. Braun. Free hospital access coupled with paid manufacturer services gives the team a concrete distribution architecture: reduce adoption friction on one side and charge the participant with a commercial interest on the other. This deserves affirmative credit. The unresolved issue is whether those relationships produce repeatable, economical sales. Hospital deployment does not establish recurring clinician use, and manufacturer relationships do not establish paying contracts.

The founding structure offers modest reassurance. Paffrath began building in 2022; Becker and Schulz joined in 2024, giving this team a longer operating history than a group assembled to apply to YC. Their AI, automation, and biomedical engineering experience supports the ability to build the product. Full-time status, ownership, and economic exposure remain undisclosed. I see no documented part-time founder, outsourced core engineering, or investor-dominated capitalization. CONUS partially resembles the operator-network formation pattern I favor through its five chief-surgeon medical founders/advisors, but their precise operating roles and proprietary contribution are not established.

The economics have not yet identified the engine that could produce a power-law outcome. Indexed individual subscriptions run from approximately €10 to €50 monthly; institutional licenses and manufacturer services introduce other potential payers. These are related monetization routes, not evidence of strategic incoherence. Nevertheless, the dossier supplies neither manufacturer contract values nor margins and expansion economics from which to construct a large outcome. Moving toward implant selection might increase the value captured, but current terms exclude patient-specific product-selection decisions. I cannot capitalize a future purchasing position as though CONUS already owns it.

My known exceptions would matter if CONUS demonstrated an independent technical discontinuity or persistent transaction demand strong enough to overcome uncertain market ownership. Source-linked answers, hospital installations, and undisclosed manufacturer-service revenue do not yet establish either. There is also no documented regulatory wind-down order or collapsed technical claim that warrants an active-mismatch judgment. I pass on a check today because the evidence supports access to a useful workflow more strongly than ownership of an enduring business. **One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: a renewed, paid manufacturer contract granting CONUS exclusive access to device data essential to serving a defined hospital network.** That would begin to turn distribution into a position competitors cannot simply reproduce.
