# Trepo -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Trepo’s strongest exit route is a strategic acquisition by a grocery retailer. Its fridge-mounted device and app use household inventory to draft grocery orders. A retailer could gain a recurring source of orders and control where those orders go. The founder reported a San Francisco beta with orderable carts, and a seed investor reported grocery-partner pilots with undisclosed counterparties and terms. The app listing showed 4.9/5 from 59 U.S. ratings. The beta and ratings support testing the workflow. Repeat ordering and incremental retailer profit remain unmeasured in the memo. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The next milestone is to ship the device and show that households keep ordering through it and generate profitable incremental purchases for a retailer. Preorders and the beta make that test plausible at seed. March 2027 is the company’s ship target, and the capital needed for manufacturing, fulfillment, and warranty support remains unknown. An acquisition would also depend on the buyer’s ability to use household data, assign any relevant retailer agreements, and cash out this holder. Those terms remain unverified. The memo leaves independent liquidity routes unevidenced. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3% without follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. At 3%, the holder needs about $83.3 million in distributable equity. The sale cases below are illustrative. Both assume the holder is eligible to sell the entire stake, receives a pro rata share with no preference diversion, and is paid fully in cash within eight years.

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Holder cash |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40M less $5M in net debt and deal adjustments = $35M | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M less $10M in net debt and deal adjustments = $90M | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested, with no eligible share purchase or payout | $0 |

The strong case clears the hurdle, but another $6.7 million in deductions before the pro rata payout would erase the cushion. Trepo’s advertised device and subscription prices establish what it plans to charge consumers. The memo supplies neither realized unit economics nor evidence that a buyer would pay the price needed to clear the hurdle. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

A retailer can already get cart-building software from Cooklist, and Samsung’s announced Instacart integration shows another way to turn kitchen inventory into orders. Trepo’s retrofit device could reach households using existing fridges, but missed check-ins could leave inventories stale and weaken the reorder experience. The substitutes give buyers a reason to demand proof that Trepo adds profitable orders before paying to own it. They expose a testable gap in buyer economics. The memo does not establish a structural closing block or show that the strongest credible route would necessarily leave the holder short, so the Pass gate does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a post-launch retailer pilot show enough sustained incremental contribution profit from Trepo-generated orders to support an acquisition that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A cohort comparison against the retailer’s existing ordering flow, paired with a costed rollout estimate, would answer it. If those results supported a buyer price range yielding at least $83.3 million in distributable equity within eight years under the benchmark terms, the action would move to Reach out.
