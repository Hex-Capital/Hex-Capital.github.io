# Kiwi -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Kiwi’s strongest exit route is a strategic acquisition by a CRM or GTM automation platform. Kiwi describes versioned SQL, source snapshots, and reviewer corrections that teams can reuse to test workflow changes. A platform could buy that capability to reduce review and rework across connected tools. Chen reports spending 20 hours correcting agent output in one week at her prior employer. The memo records Kiwi’s October 22 launch and subsequent coverage, while customer savings and buyer interest remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A paid production pilot that measures review time and catches workflow regressions would make the buyer case more concrete. The founders’ post-sales and SQL infrastructure experience and Kiwi’s documented pilot pricing make that test plausible. Pilot funding, deployment timing, and customer results remain open. Kiwi documents hosted and customer-cloud deployments, making a software acquisition plausible, while customer data permissions, contract assignment, and holder participation remain unverified. The memo identifies no independent liquidity route. (The Idea, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale scenarios below are sensitivities. The memo supplies no basis for an acquisition price or Kiwi-specific investor terms. Both sale cases assume an eligible holder sells the full stake, receives cash at close within eight years, and participates pro rata with no preference or other senior claim beyond the stated deductions.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Gross holder cash |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $45M | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M | $2.70M (5.4×). If only 90% of the stake can be sold, cash falls to $2.43M (4.86×). |
| Prolonged private | Kiwi continues operating and reinvests available cash, with no distribution or eligible share purchase within eight years | $0 realized |

### Countercase

HubSpot already uses MCP to connect tools such as Gong and Notion for account-planning summaries. If native agents handle enough of the workflow, a CRM platform could have little reason to buy Kiwi’s replay and testing layer. The overlap weakens the buyer case. The memo leaves open whether native tools can match replay and regression testing, and whether any credible sale would leave the holder materially short. Neither Pass gate is established. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Kiwi show that its replay and regression testing saves enough review and rework in a paid production deployment to justify a platform buying the capability? A measured comparison with native CRM tooling and a buyer-side rollout model supporting a price above the $83.3 million distributable equity hurdle would move the action to Reach out.
