# Fire Wallet -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Fire Wallet’s candidate secret is that businesses will move payments into private self-custody when confidentiality and least-cost routing become simple enough to use. What important truth does that contradict? The dossier identifies neither a named peer rejecting this proposition nor a deployment that tests it. Privacy and cheaper payments are desirable properties; the secret would be explaining why everyone else has misunderstood how to deliver them. The conflicting descriptions—Ethereum versus multichain, recovery phrases versus email/PIN, distributed HSMs—leave the actual experiment unclear. That is unresolved evidence, not proof the technology fails.

The decisive weakness is value capture. Fire targets cross-border, ecommerce, and DTC businesses moving $1M+/month, but those categories do not yet constitute a market it can dominate. Request Finance, Rain, and Fireblocks offer different combinations of the relevant workflows and infrastructure. Fire’s threshold-HSM description supplies no demonstrated exclusive capability, while 0x and MoonPay provide important components. There is no observed monopoly mechanism. Making payments more convenient could create substantial customer value while leaving Fire competing away its transaction margin.

The founding structure provides some affirmative evidence. Joshua Goldbard and Manuel previously worked together building MobileCoin; that shared operating history matters more than fundraising credentials. It does not establish alignment across all three founders. Michael Divine’s prior relationship with them, everyone’s full-time status, ownership, and responsibility for core engineering remain undisclosed. I give credit for the documented working relationship without inventing either founder commitment or a dysfunctional cap table. Goldbard’s previous company also does not establish that Fire is rebuilding a particular operating system whose failure he uniquely understands.

The strongest objection to passing is that distribution may already be emerging through a small business network. Trustboop has signed an LOI. An enterprise prospect handling approximately $1.5M/month reportedly introduced ten additional companies. Those introductions are more informative than the app’s three five-star ratings: one merchant relationship could open a concentrated customer cluster. Fire also fits the consumer-finance side of my investing patterns, and two founders bring a shared operator background. Dismissing it because crypto wallets are unfashionable would miss the strongest evidence in the dossier. Nevertheless, introductions establish access; they do not establish conversion, paid usage, or economical implementation.

The economics remain unformed. Fire permits transaction interface fees but publishes no rate or realized revenue. Even the approximately $100M/year prospective GMV would produce only $100,000 annually at an illustrative ten-basis-point fee, before costs; that volume is not yet being processed. A much larger business is possible, but the dossier supplies no pricing or expansion arithmetic supporting it. Nor does a known exception justify overriding the monopoly concern: there is no independently demonstrated technical breakthrough or persistent paid transaction behavior. The architecture discrepancies warrant verification, while the privacy exceptions do not themselves establish deception or a regulatory violation.

I pass on Fire Wallet today. The missing secret receives a cautious prior; the competitive position supplies the affirmative reason to decline. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: three unrelated merchants moving more than $1M/month each renew paid Fire contracts after six months specifically because an independently verified privacy capability is unavailable from their previous providers. That would begin connecting differentiation, customer choice, and retained value. The present dossier establishes promising access to buyers, but not ownership of an overlooked market.
