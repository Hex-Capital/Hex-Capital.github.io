# Definite -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Definite’s contested truth is that finance agents become reliable only when interpretation sits above one live, deterministic model of the books. The falsifying event is straightforward: if a general workflow tool or accounting agent can reproduce Definite’s reconciliation accuracy without building that model, the supposed secret collapses into a feature. The company reports that its verification engine with DeepSeek V3 beat GPT-5 on FinBalance at roughly 50% lower cost and exceeded published FinAuditing results. That is a testable technical claim, but it is company-reported, independently unreplicated, and does not identify a belief that Numeric, Stacks, or Basis rejects. It is an intriguing hypothesis, not yet a demonstrated secret.

The founders are the strongest part of the case. They have known one another since their first year at Waterloo rather than assembling for YC, and their experiences converge on the problem: Gurshabd encountered manual attestations and approval controls at BitGo; Mazin saw regulatory-number rechecking at Optiver and built finance agents at Boosted.ai; Farhan developed machine-learning systems at Meta. This is a long-aligned technical team formed around observed institutional failure. Yet the dossier does not establish founder ownership, compensation, full-time status for every founder, or whether all core engineering remains in-house. The foundation looks coherent, but economic exposure is unproved.

What can this team monopolize? Today the answer is unclear. Numeric has disclosed $38 million, Stacks $35 million, and Basis $137.6 million while pursuing overlapping accounting, close, and audit agents. Definite’s reusable mappings, policies, evidence relationships, and approval logic could eventually create switching costs, but “could” is not a moat. There is no retention, dominant share, exclusive data, customer-qualified accuracy result, or structural barrier. The movement from Ledgix to Bylaw, then bank reporting, then broader reconciliations and controls also suggests the beachhead is being searched for rather than dominated. Competition is evidence here: several well-financed companies have independently reached substantially the same destination.

The strongest opposing case is that Definite has chosen the right abstraction while its competitors automate isolated tasks. A persistent financial model joined to deterministic calculations and evidence-linked workpapers could become the control plane for regulated finance, and the founders’ direct exposure to BitGo and Optiver gives that claim more substance than an ordinary AI wrapper. If one deployed model accumulates customer-specific policies and becomes the basis for every subsequent control, switching costs may compound. But no Known Exception rescues the current evidence: there is neither an independently verified zero-to-one result nor persistent customer behavior strong enough to override the crowded market. Eleven Launch YC votes and 170 LinkedIn followers are not negative at pre-seed; they simply do not validate the mechanism.

Distribution remains named but unsolved. The team is pursuing founder-led sales to banks, credit unions, fintechs, and insurers and asking for introductions to partner-bank finance or engineering executives. That identifies buyers and a channel, but not the sales cycle, implementation burden, conversion rate, contract value, or the security certifications likely to govern regulated procurement. Private order forms and advance payment terms establish a commercial structure, not revenue. With no disclosed customer, price, gross margin, or expansion behavior—and a cited financial-close market of only $928.9 million in 2025—the dossier cannot yet support fund-return arithmetic.

I pass today. Definite is a credible team pursuing a real problem, but the analytical signature is founder strength without market ownership: capable founders may build a useful company while better-capitalized rivals capture the category. The verifiable flip condition is one named regulated institution renewing after two reporting cycles and expanding the same live financial model across at least three control workflows. That would begin to prove both the narrow wedge and the compounding mechanism. Until then: technical proposition, not secret; crowded category, not monopoly.
