# Alkera AI -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I first write down the deal: valuation unknown, competing-term-sheet count unknown, and no evidence that this is a genuinely scarce first check. YC and Pareto Holdings establish backing, not price discipline. With no disclosed financing terms, I cannot tell whether I am buying an unfashionable belief cheaply or paying Demo Day pricing for a category investors already recognize. That is a gating distinction when I am investing my own money.

The technical case is much better than the deal case. Alkera’s founders have worked across the two systems they are joining: Tony Li built a multi-agent Python framework at ByteDance and quantitative software at Carthage; Andrew Tran worked on data infrastructure at HRT and Ramp as well as reasoning models; Rick Gao built financial models and optimizations at Verition. Their product reflects those receipts through column-level lineage, graph planning, verified organizational knowledge, and granular SQL and shell permissions. The 83.28% Pass@1 result on DataAgentBench is legitimate product evidence, even if its 1.43-point lead over SCRIBE is too narrow to constitute a durable moat.

What I cannot find is the venture moment. There is no stated use of funds, dated experiment, or pass/fail milestone. “Build integrations, obtain compliance, and sell the product” would be several ordinary company-building tasks, not one truth-revealing use of scarce capital. A real experiment might ask whether a production data team will let Alkera map its full lineage, govern destructive actions, and replace an existing tool within six months. The dossier does not say that this round is financing such a test.

The supposed dark pool is also prospective. Customer-specific lineage graphs and verified team knowledge could become proprietary, but presently they are an inference rather than accumulated assets causing purchases or retained usage. The 542 LinkedIn followers, published $50 and $250 monthly plans, and free download do not establish that anyone has paid. PromptQL already offers shared context and deterministic permissions, while Prophecy and Altimate overlap the workflow. If Alkera does not own deployed organizational knowledge, it is selling a feature set into a market where well-funded competitors can copy the public thesis.

The strongest bull case is that unusually relevant builders can turn incomplete infrastructure into a modest enterprise business before public traction appears. My 36-month base case is therefore not death: Alkera reaches roughly $1 million–$3 million ARR by selling controlled deployments to data teams, but remains a useful tool rather than the category’s defining platform. The bear case stings because its strongest current metric is a 54-query benchmark, its BI integrations and compliance certifications remain unfinished, and model or platform incumbents can compress the differentiation before customer knowledge compounds. There is no hard-demand exception—no paid conversion, retention, proprietary dataset, or acquisition signal—strong enough to override that distribution risk.

I am passing on the present evidence. The founders can ship, but the company has not shown who owns final decisions among three co-founders, what exact truth the capital buys, whether the round is cheap, or how the business survives without another financing. My one verifiable flip condition is a sub-$10 million post-money round explicitly funding conversion of one named design partner into a 12-month paid production deployment by a fixed date. That would turn the price, experiment, proprietary workflow, and cash path from stories into underwriting.
