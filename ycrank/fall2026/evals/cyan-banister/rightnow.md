# RightNow -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a developer leaving several coding agents running overnight without worrying about which model company owns the meter. RightNow’s session-aware routing could help make that ordinary: keep context near the computation, avoid processing it repeatedly, and make open models economical. But that is my imagined destination. The dossier establishes a present optimization problem without establishing the intermediate unlocks or showing why this particular company becomes necessary along that path. I want to fund what today’s infrastructure enables next; cheaper inference alone does not answer that question.

Why should a developer keep paying to process context already submitted? That is the useful systems question here. RightNow identifies a specific waste mechanism and gives developers compatible APIs through which to escape some of it. Its $5 prepaid entry point and $10/$29/$99 subscriptions make experimentation accessible. Yet the economic benefit depends on sessions finding cached context, and the published cache rates describe the highest qualifying account. I cannot translate that into ordinary customer savings. There is a real opportunity to return control over inference spending, but no quantified rent or demonstrated transfer of economic power yet.

The founders have done more than collect AI credentials. The earlier editor launched in April 2025, and both founders coauthored model research, including RightNow-Arabic. That sequence earns more attention than their university or employer affiliations. It shows sustained building across related problems. It does not establish the pre-company obsession, complementary recruiting, or commercial command that would make me unusually confident in this team. Separately, the July and October RunInfra launches establish public availability, but Product Hunt points and repository stars tell me little about people repeatedly paying or rearranging their work around the service. OpenFang’s approximately 18,200 stars belong to another product.

The strongest argument against my pass is Autokernel. A public kernel optimization implementation with a correctness harness is a tangible technical artifact beneath the visible application wave. That fits my pattern of backing concealed infrastructure, and open models give this team room to build without making one closed model supplier its entire foundation. I can make exceptions for missing revenue when technical proof meets exceptional founder history and concrete customer commitments. Here, those supporting facts are absent. The MIT license also lets others reuse the implementation, while Together already develops kernels and inference systems. RightNow has demonstrated engineering work; control of a scarce enabling constraint remains unproven.

I would pass with my own money today. The benchmark omissions and best-effort caching weaken the economic claim; their disclosure does not establish fabricated results. Session caching also makes custody and isolation of customer code a diligence gate, although the dossier establishes no severe privacy violation. My decision would reopen on one verifiable result: an independently reproducible eight-week deployment showing repeat paying coding-agent teams achieving lower fully loaded inference costs than Together at typical cache-hit rates. That would turn an appealing optimization mechanism into evidence that someone’s working life has actually changed.
