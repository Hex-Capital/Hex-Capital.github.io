# Prescience, Inc. -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. An employer does not choose a health plan once a year and then abandon employees inside it; software continuously compares care paths, resolves bills, schedules treatment, and learns which interventions actually reduce cost. Prescience has pieces of that future—Time Machine, Crystal, automated filings, and California Administrator License #6019366—but not yet the causal map. I need validated trajectory predictions, state-by-state operating authority, trusted custody of health data, and evidence that better routing changes outcomes. The license and reported employer service are small leaks from that world. Without model-performance or savings data, they do not tell me whether the future arrived early or whether this is another benefits administrator with ambitious language.

The brothers are the strongest evidence. Rishab Jain’s decade-spanning combination of computer science, neuroscience, more than ten ML and health papers, open-source work, and two company-reported acquisitions looks like pre-company obsession rather than a recent AI costume. Aditya Jain adds an MD, biomedical engineering, and medical-AI research. They have built together for ten years, and the CEO/COO division at least identifies Rishab as accountable; I would still verify ownership and contractual control because sibling trust is not a governance mechanism. This team has real Tizz and plausible Biz. The California license suggests they have also encountered some of the hidden operating complexity that thin healthcare wrappers usually ignore.

The broken system is painfully concrete: employers paid an average $26,993 for family coverage in 2025, yet workers still absorb deductibles, denied claims, and administrative labor. Brokers, PEOs, carriers, and fragmented administrators mediate the relationship. Prescience’s savings-linked compensation is the cleverest part of the design because it converts that rent into an employer outcome rather than another administrative fee. But the agency returned to employees is less proven. Crystal can navigate appointments, questions, and bills; the dossier does not show that a patient can choose more freely, receive care sooner, or reverse a denial.

The bull case stings: exceptional technical and clinical founders have assembled licensing and operational integrations that most pre-seed teams would not touch, and even an undisclosed employer could be meaningful when deploying regulated infrastructure. My known pre-revenue exception allows missing revenue when a functioning prototype, signed proofs of concept, and extraordinary founder preparation jointly establish the bridge. Prescience has the founder preparation, but the other two legs remain indistinct: unnamed employers, an open waitlist, no customer-level savings study, and no public validation for Time Machine. That is not stage prejudice. It is the difference between a difficult artifact and a difficult claim.

The red flag is data custody. Prescience may process health records, claims, prescriptions, financial information, and connected Gmail documents while having no completed SOC 2 Type I or Type II examination. A system recommending care trajectories can become unusually valuable, but it can also concentrate surveillance and clinical error in the same layer. The inferred learning moat from de-identified data earns no credit yet, especially when Angle Health and Collective Health already offer overlapping AI-enabled administration. Prescience controls a license and integrations, not yet a scarce technical asset or demonstrated data flywheel.

I would not write the personal check today. My base case is that this becomes a capable AI navigation or TPA product for self-funded employers before it becomes the operating system implied by “abundant intelligence.” The single condition that flips me is verifiable evidence from at least three named employers showing repeat deployment, clinically reviewed Time Machine recommendations, and measured annualized savings—alongside a completed independent security examination. That would turn the present artifact from paperwork into a signal.
