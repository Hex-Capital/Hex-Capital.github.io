# Boat -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Reach out  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Conditional on strong product-market fit, Boat can serve agent developers across markets that accept its hosting and snapshot policies. The same CLI, API, and SDK provision persistent Ubuntu VMs for a recurring job. EU-only VM hosting, possible snapshot storage outside the EU, and SOC 2 still in progress narrow the eligible buyer pool. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Boat documents self-serve signup, a seven-day compute trial, a quickstart, and published monthly plans with per-second billing. The founder reported that X and Product Hunt brought in comparable numbers of trial users at launch. That supports a low-touch route from discovery to a first VM and paid use, with acquisition evidence concentrated around launches. (The Idea)

Persistent environments give developers a reason to return, and running more agents or running them longer can raise the same account’s bill. Expansion could offset churn if retained customers move more workloads into production. That is an inference from the workflow and pricing. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The founder reported 350+ paying users in September and $1 million ARR on November 7, up from $200,000 one month earlier. That is a steep reported trajectory for a three-person team roughly six months after the May launch. The ARR definition and the share of reported VM starts that translated into paid runtime remain uncertain. (Key Facts, Market & Traction, Boat)

### Countercase

Bursty customers could require spare host capacity while generating few billable hours. If Boat realizes the listed rate, a default VM hour brings in $0.036 before attributable host capacity, snapshot storage, support, and trial costs. Auto-stop and snapshot-based resume can free capacity for reuse, but the benefit depends on whether other paid workloads fill it. Burst fees and limits give Boat some control over peak demand. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo identifies ways to manage that load and leaves their effectiveness uncertain. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Boat add paid production VM hours at positive incremental contribution under representative burst loads? Match customer invoices to host utilization, spare capacity, snapshot storage, and attributable onboarding and support costs across several billing periods. Positive contribution through capacity step-ups would support continuing diligence. Persistent losses caused by unavoidable spare capacity would change the action to Pass.
