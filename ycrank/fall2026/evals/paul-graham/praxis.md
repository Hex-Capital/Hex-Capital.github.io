# Praxis -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Praxis has shipped something law firms are using. What I cannot find is the episode that would make me bet on these founders: something broke, they found a way around it, and a customer’s situation changed. Diana’s operating experience and Maxence’s engineering background make that episode plausible. They do not establish it. I would pass on the evidence here, without mistaking an incomplete founder history for evidence of passive founders.

The strongest argument against passing is Diana. She reports taking Gorgias’s AI Agent to more than 1,000 customers and $8 million ARR in under a year, and she worked with law firms at McKinsey ([founder website](https://diana-khramina.com/), [Praxis About](https://runpraxis.ai/about)). Those are relevant experiences for selling automation to cautious buyers. Maxence has built multimodal systems at SightCall and software at NextDNS. Together, they could have the unusual combination required to install the product personally, understand why a lawyer distrusts it, and fix the underlying software. But the Gorgias numbers describe a prior employer. They supply neither Praxis demand nor a specific account of Diana overcoming resistance.

The current deployment earns more credit than a proposed architecture. YC says Praxis is live with law firms across the United States ([YC profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/praxis)). That is preliminary technical proof, though the dossier provides no measured completion rate, error rate, or independently demonstrated workflow. Demand is less established. The corporate-law partner spending 1.5 hours daily on setup and engagement letters identifies a painful job, but the testimonial gives no post-adoption result ([Praxis](https://runpraxis.ai)). Who keeps using this after the founders stop helping? Deployment supplies some evidence of acceptance; it does not yet show the narrow well of users who would fight to keep the product.

Praxis could discover that well inside the unpleasant work between existing systems: document chasing, approvals, billing, and exception handling. But identifying a schlep is different from demonstrating that these founders encountered it themselves and learned something others missed. Their origin story and evidence of product revision are both missing. Meanwhile, Lawmatics already qualifies inquiries and triggers follow-ups, and Clio offers workflow execution ([Lawmatics](https://www.lawmatics.com/blog/lawmatics-launches-qualifyai), [Clio](https://www.clio.com/about/press/clio-500-million-arr/)). This makes the distinction consequential: a firm must need Praxis enough to introduce another system into an already fragmented operation.

The company has not demonstrated my recurring pattern of an unusually concrete proof spike or users appropriating a new technical primitive. None of the known exceptions supplies an override: the dossier establishes neither unmistakable user pull nor independently validated technical results. Nor does it establish the active red flags of waiting for a partner, delegating early sales, or resisting contrary evidence; the October 30 event alone proves none of those. My founder gate remains unmet. One verifiable condition would change my decision: a dated customer account showing a blocked workflow, a founder-built workaround shipped within weeks, and subsequent repeated paid use of that workflow. That would connect the capable team to the behavior I actually invest in.
