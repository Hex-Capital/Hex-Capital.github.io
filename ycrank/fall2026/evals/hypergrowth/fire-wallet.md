# Fire Wallet -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Fire’s strongest path is private per-request payments for API providers whose customers use software agents. Assuming strong fit with those buyers, the same e402 payment challenge and verification flow could serve the same recurring job across markets where agents can fund eUSD accounts and merchants can use MobileCoin settlement. Fire reports a mainnet test that returned a paid response in under two seconds. The memo identifies MoonPay eligibility and 0x routing as potential constraints for users who need fiat conversion or swaps. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Fire’s developer launch and integration documentation provide a discovery channel and a reusable route to first value. Merchants must implement and verify payments, and the memo shows no independent merchant completing that flow in production. The observed App Store listing covers an Ethereum-only wallet, so its reviews give no evidence of e402 activation. Fire reports a signed LOI and a first enterprise customer onboarding. The customer’s existing volume and the projected volume from introductions are pipeline signals. The memo provides no realized Fire revenue or live customer volume for this product. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Agent spending policies and merchant integrations could encourage repeat use once embedded in production. Fire advertises no subscription or e402 fee, so that mechanism supports behavioral retention while revenue retention and expansion remain unproven. **Scenario assumption:** If Fire introduced a per-request fee and customers kept routing payments at that price, growing request volume could expand existing-account revenue. At today’s advertised price, Fire earns zero per e402 payment. The disclosed $0.00256 is a network fee, and the memo gives no basis for estimating Fire’s incremental integration, infrastructure, or support costs. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

API providers must implement acceptance while agents need funded accounts. A successful mainnet test shows that settlement can work, while independent adoption and willingness to pay Fire remain open. Charging could make small payments less attractive, and merchant support could rise with each deployment. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo establishes neither substantial market-by-market rebuilding nor unavoidable servicing costs that make growth infeasible. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
