# Chromie -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot underwrite Chromie before seeing the deal. There is no proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is a genuinely scarce first check. For most investors that is missing administration; for me it is missing thesis evidence. A YC Summer 2026 label can produce an auction even when the underlying company remains unproven, so financing silence receives the low prior, not the benefit of the doubt.

The product has one real signal: three named customer logos, and Snovation says Chromie surfaced an RFI its existing pipeline missed. That demonstrates useful workflow action, but not yet a purchase caused, bid submitted, contract won, or software invoice paid. The “Company Brain” could become a proprietary pool of capabilities, past performance, pursuit decisions, and outcomes. Today it is an inferred future moat sitting on public procurement feeds while GovDash, Procurement Sciences, Govly, and GovSignals already overlap the workflow with $5.5 million to $40 million-plus of disclosed funding.

The founders have shipped software. Ananth Sankaralingam built Promptly AI to a reported 40,000-plus users and an acquisition; both founders bring credible AI and engineering experience. Their Washington trip and visits to 50 GovCon offices show willingness to acquire access manually. But Chromie is a translation-layer business, and the dossier documents shipping only on the software side—not operating receipts inside government procurement. The rapid sequence from browser-extension builder to browser-agent infrastructure, insurance workflows, and then GovCon makes the current domain look discovered rather than earned.

The strongest bull case is that this is exactly how the earned side begins: founders enter an ugly physical workflow, sit with contractors, find opportunities incumbent tooling misses, and accumulate customer-specific data competitors cannot scrape. At pre-seed, three customers and a concrete missed-RFI result are meaningful. But the known AI exception requires measured conversion and retention atop a functioning business. Chromie discloses neither revenue nor pricing, and nothing establishes that finding an RFI causes a bid, a win, or retained paid usage.

Nor is there a named venture moment. The dossier gives no use of funds, deadline, pass/fail metric, gross margin, burn, runway, or route to break-even without another round. “Build the product and sell it” is not a capital experiment. In a crowded AI application category, the source tracing and replayable audit logs are sensible mitigations, but they do not remove dependence on public feeds, model extraction, security readiness, or subsequent financing.

I pass. My 36-month base case is that Chromie either pivots into broader commercial RFP automation or remains below $1 million ARR because funded GovCon suites compress standalone discovery and qualification. One result flips me: a signed customer record showing that a Chromie-discovered opportunity progressed through qualification to a submitted bid and awarded contract, with paid recurring software revenue attributable to that workflow. That would turn an interesting retrieval tool into evidence that Chromie owns a cash-producing decision loop.
