# Voyager -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Voyager’s strongest path. Voyager says its desktop agent can execute edits in installed creative applications including Blender and DaVinci Resolve. A video platform such as Runway could buy that integration layer to bring its generation tools into more of a studio’s production workflow and earn more model usage. Ownership could make sense if controlling and maintaining the integrations delivers more value than licensing them or building similar connectors. The memo establishes a product fit. Buyer interest and the economic benefit of ownership remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The memo records a desktop release and a customer’s account of completing a short with Voyager alongside Meshy and ElevenLabs. The customer reported $128.99 in generation spend across the workflow. Voyager publishes paid plans, while realized revenue and paying-customer counts remain unknown. A proposed seed-stage milestone is a cohort of paying studios that renews after using Voyager across multiple productions, with measured editing time saved against their existing workflows. The shipped product and reported production make that test plausible. Voyager’s full financing and the capital needed to maintain its integrations through the test remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The memo also leaves ownership and assignment of the core code and integration rights unverified. App versions and permissions already affect which controls work, so a buyer would need to establish which workflows it could keep operating after a sale. No separate liquidity route has usable evidence of buyout cash flow, demand for eligible shares, public-market readiness, or distributable cash. (Defensibility, Key Risks, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Actual shareholder terms are unavailable. The eight-year scenario assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× gross cash target is $2.5 million. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. At 3% and with illustrative net debt of $5 million, the enterprise-value hurdle is $88.3 million before any additional fees or senior claims. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction pricing that supports clearing that hurdle. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Eight-year scenario | Gross cash to the holder |
|---|---|
| $25 million enterprise-value sale | $600,000, or 1.2×, at 3% ownership after $5 million of assumed net debt |
| $100 million enterprise-value sale | $2.85 million, or 5.7×, on the same terms. Dilution to 2% reduces proceeds to $1.9 million, or 3.8× |
| Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and Voyager reinvests available cash without a secondary or payout | $0 realized, with shares retained |

The sale scenarios assume pro rata distribution before investor taxes. A wind-down recovery is unquantifiable because the memo gives no basis to estimate residual assets after obligations and preferences. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A strategic buyer can build or partner. Adobe already offers a native Firefly agent with third-party models, while Runway and Krea have agent connectors and Higgsfield integrates external apps into its workspace. Those shipped alternatives are more direct evidence of buyer options than Voyager’s launch attention or tool count is of a reason to buy. The risk is that Voyager helps creators finish projects while giving a platform little reason to pay for ownership. The memo leaves both a structural block and a materially inadequate supported sale price unestablished, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can measured workflow gains from repeat paying studios support a credible strategic purchase price that delivers $2.5 million to the modeled holder within eight years? A repeat-use cohort with measured time savings, alongside a buyer-side estimate of what owning the integrations would be worth versus building or licensing them, would move the action to Reach out if the supported range clears the holder’s hurdle. If the strongest supported price leaves the holder materially short, change the action to Pass.
