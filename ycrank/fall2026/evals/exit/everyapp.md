# EveryApp -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. Cloudflare is a plausible buyer because EveryApp bundles ready-to-install business apps with shared sign-in and agent access, all running on Workers. Owning the managed service could give Cloudflare a way to acquire and retain teams that deploy multiple apps and generate recurring Workers usage. That benefit is an inference. The memo documents the installable-app relaunch and 906 platform repository stars. The founder reports hundreds of paying customers for standalone OpenSEO, and EveryApp announced a managed tier starting at $10 per month. A measurable milestone is a cohort of paying EveryApp teams that deploy a second app and renew, with attributable Workers usage. The launch shows the team can ship the integration, while platform conversion, retention, and the capital needed to reach the milestone remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under the shared scenario, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target requires $2.5 million in gross cash, or $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership. That hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. With $5 million of illustrative net debt, the 3% case needs about $88.3 million in enterprise value. At year eight, a $25 million sale returns $600,000 gross (1.2×), and a $100 million sale returns $2.85 million gross (5.7×). Both use simple pro rata allocation before investor taxes. The memo establishes neither actual shareholder terms nor a realizable sale price, so these figures are scenarios.

A sponsor buyout lacks evidence of cash generation after reinvestment. A secondary lacks eligible buyer demand and transfer terms. An IPO lacks evidence of operating scale and a credible resale path, and dividends or buybacks lack evidence of distributable cash. If growth plateaus below listing scale, buyers stay away, and EveryApp reinvests all available cash, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Cloudflare already offers agent deployment infrastructure and can reuse the MIT-licensed Gateway. OpenSEO users can stay on its hosted service or deploy it independently, so its founder-reported paying customers may never become EveryApp platform customers. The strongest reason to buy is therefore a retained customer base that Cloudflare would struggle to win through its own infrastructure or a partnership. Customer-account deployments give no assurance that an acquirer would obtain those relationships, and the memo leaves managed customer contract transferability unverified. The evidence supports Watch. It establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported sale price that leaves the holder materially short. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can EveryApp show that a retained cohort of paying teams running multiple apps generates enough incremental Workers usage to support a strategic purchase above the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Cohort renewal and usage data, paired with a buyer-side estimate of what it would cost to win those customers through building or partnering, would justify Reach out if they support a sale range around or above $88.3 million in enterprise value under the illustrative terms.
