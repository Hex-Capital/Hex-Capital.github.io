# Axonnic -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to a dexterous-robotics vendor or factory automation integrator is Axonnic’s strongest route. The live demonstration with a Proception hand shows that Axonnic’s EMG system can control a third-party hand. Axonnic separately reports lower hand-pose error than Meta’s baseline for all 20 held-out participants. A buyer could have a reason to own the capture and training stack if it reliably cuts the cost of deploying robots on force-sensitive tasks such as sewing. The production savings and the value of owning the stack remain unmeasured. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, The Idea)

Axonnic reports paid pilots and a signed sewing LOI. Those give it a route to a production test, with the capital and time needed for repeatable deployment still unknown. The sleeve and SDK are offered as a standalone package, making a sale of the core technology plausible. A buyer’s ability to take over factory recordings would depend on the rights negotiated for each engagement. The memo provides no evidence of a separate route to holder liquidity. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

**Illustrative proceeds.** A $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership. With dilution to 3% and no follow-ons, the 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. For both sale cases, assume a full cash sale by year eight, $5 million in combined debt and deal adjustments, and an eligible holder selling its entire stake with a pro rata payout and no senior preference claims. The sale values are scenario assumptions. The undisclosed pilot revenue and posted sleeve prices provide no basis for an acquisition price range. (Key Facts)

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M yields $35M in distributable equity | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $5M yields $95M in distributable equity | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private | Operating cash is reinvested through year eight, with no eligible share purchase or payout | $0 (0×) |

The strong case clears the hurdle by $350,000. If cash at closing covers only 80% of the holder’s payout and the balance arrives after year eight, cash within the horizon drops to $2.28 million.

### Countercase

A strategic buyer could buy or build a substitute. Proception already offers a capture glove, and OYMotion sells an EMG kit with early access to finger-angle and force estimation. Axonnic’s recording interference has already forced repeat collections, and engagement-specific data rights could shrink the asset a buyer would acquire. These risks could weaken both the reason to buy and the price. The memo leaves them unresolved, so neither the structural-block nor the inadequate-proceeds gate is triggered. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Does the sewing pilot show a repeatable production advantage worth acquiring? A paid deployment that measures accepted units per hour and cost per accepted unit against the integrator’s current approach, paired with a potential buyer’s build-versus-buy estimate supporting a price above the roughly $88.3 million enterprise-value hurdle under the modeled adjustments, would move the action to Reach out.
