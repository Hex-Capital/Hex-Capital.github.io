# Insurf -- Elad Gil Evaluation

CMS prior-authorization requirements taking effect January 1, 2026 give **Insurf** a real market-reopening catalyst, while the absence of disclosed revenue, customers, or production outcomes leaves **Insurf** without the curve that distinguishes “just working” from a false signal. That pairing defines Insurf: a well-timed healthcare workflow bet whose market thesis is materially stronger than its evidence of capture. The 80.7% overturn rate on appealed Medicare Advantage denials makes the economic waste obvious, but obvious waste does not establish that Insurf owns the recovery workflow.

**Stripe** is the high rung because APIs transformed payments from bank integration into developer infrastructure, creating immediate distribution pull. Insurf shares the load-bearing mechanism: CMS-0057 standardizes prior-authorization data and reporting just as hospitals face 53 million annual determinations, potentially turning appeals from manual exception handling into software infrastructure. Insurf diverges because the catalyst is partly inferred, production PHI remains disabled, and no hospital has been named as changing behavior because of the rule. Insurf is skating for the puck, but Stripe already showed merchants moving toward it.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism: activity without a curve sufficient to change structural market position is not a winning signal. Insurf discloses a live demo and private “customer evidence,” but Insurf publishes no ARR, recovered-payment volume, repeat usage, signed counterparty, or production-outcome series. At pre-seed I do not require revenue, but an AI workflow aimed directly at recoverable dollars should produce unusually legible evidence quickly. Until it does, the revenue-velocity score stays near my absent-evidence default.

**Bryan Chung’s** decision-graph work and **Chang Lu’s** BS/MD background are relevant inputs, but the closest founder parallel is Perplexity: Aravind repeatedly arrived with the discussed artifact already built. Insurf has shipped a public live demo, which is better than credential-only founders, yet Insurf shows no prior independently shipped product, public repository, customer-feedback iteration, or sequence of weekly releases. The demo moves the founders above the default; it does not yet demonstrate the Perplexity shipping rhythm.

**Amperos**, with $20.2 million raised, already automates portals, calls, collections, and appeals, while Ember, Cofactor, and Red Sky occupy adjacent denial workflows. Over 18 months, Insurf’s index position compounds only if every resolved claim improves a proprietary payer-behavior graph that makes the next appeal more accurate and easier to sell. If Insurf remains an appeal-drafting point solution, funded competitors commoditize the LLM layer and force Insurf to win hospitals one integration at a time. No graph size or outcome count is published, so Insurf currently fights for share rather than functioning as an index on healthcare reimbursement.

**HealthAdminBench’s 36.3%** maximum end-to-end success rate makes the single-miracle test mixed. Insurf sensibly keeps physicians in the loop, preserves source snapshots, and leaves submission under staff control; Insurf therefore does not require autonomous agents to become clinically perfect. The score would flip upward if human-reviewed appeals could deploy through existing channels without a second miracle in security and integration. Today production PHI is disabled, no SOC 2 report exists, and payer-policy fragmentation means Insurf must solve model reliability, hospital compliance, and continuously changing payer rules together.

**The 80.7% appeal-overturn rate** should support real customer ROI, so my 36-month forecast is that by July 2029 Insurf will have expanded from appeals into prior-authorization and denial prevention and reached $2 million to $5 million in ARR, but will not yet be the independent “decision infrastructure” layer it describes. Insurf can monetize the reopened workflow before proving an index position; the competitive pressure from Amperos and Ember will push Insurf upstream because preventing a denial is operationally more valuable than appealing one.

**Insurf’s physician-attested, source-snapshot workflow** is the strongest objection to that restrained forecast. The bull case is Color Genomics: workflow infrastructure layered over a difficult underlying domain, with CMS standardization creating the opening and every resolved claim strengthening a proprietary dataset. That Known Exception could make Insurf the Abridge-like system of record for payer decisions. The dossier does not yet earn that analogy because Insurf has no published graph scale, production outcomes, named hospital, or revenue curve, while four funded competitors already overlap the workflow. Three named hospital systems producing more than 20% monthly growth in recovered revenue for six consecutive months would flip my verdict to Invest.
