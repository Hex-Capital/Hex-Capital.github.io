# EveryApp -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is EveryApp’s strongest path. A developer infrastructure provider could buy EveryApp to acquire a base of paying teams already running several apps through one account. The ownership case would be faster access to those customer relationships and their recurring workloads than the buyer could achieve by hosting the Gateway itself. That advantage remains an inference. The memo records 906 stars for the EveryApp repository and 28,098 for standalone OpenSEO. The founder reports hundreds of paying OpenSEO customers. Those signals support OpenSEO as a distribution channel, while conversion to paid EveryApp use remains unverified. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

EveryApp shipped a Gateway-only release and then relaunched with installable apps. It announced managed pricing starting at $10 a month. The operating milestone is a retained cohort of paying teams that starts with OpenSEO and adopts a second app. The releases make that test plausible at seed. The memo does not establish EveryApp’s runway or the cost of reaching the milestone, and the reported $125,000 in early funding is listed under OpenSEO. The ownership and transfer terms for the managed customer base and the investor’s shares are unverified. No independent route to holder cash is supported. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

**Proceeds bridge.** Using the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 40% relative dilution takes ownership from 5% to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, or 5×. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, and $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale scenarios assume enterprise value converts dollar for dollar to distributable equity consideration, with zero net debt, fees, or other closing adjustments. They assume a simple pro rata waterfall, no preference diversion, and all cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. The memo supplies no buyer economics or applicable transaction pricing to support either sale price.

- **Modest sale in year 5.** A $30 million enterprise value yields $900,000 to the holder at 3%, or 1.8×.
- **Strong sale in year 7.** A $100 million enterprise value yields $3 million at 3%, or 6×. At 2%, it yields $2 million, or 4×.
- **Prolonged private through year 8.** Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The investor still holds the shares and realizes $0, or 0×.

### Countercase

A buyer could reuse the MIT-licensed Gateway on deployment infrastructure Cloudflare already offers. OpenSEO users can stay on the standalone hosted product or deploy it independently, and the reported customer outcome came from an OpenSEO fork. Together, these facts weaken the case that owning EveryApp is necessary to capture the benefit. They leave outreach premature, while the memo gives no basis for concluding that the substitute would also cap the price of an established, transferable managed customer base below the hurdle. The structural Pass override does not apply. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*, *Market & Traction*)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a developer infrastructure buyer pay enough for EveryApp’s retained, paying multi-app customers to clear the holder’s cash hurdle when it could host the MIT Gateway itself? A buyer-backed comparison of acquisition and self-build costs, grounded in observed paid conversion and retention, would move the action to Reach out if it supported a price that cleared the hurdle under plausible ownership and cash-at-close terms.
