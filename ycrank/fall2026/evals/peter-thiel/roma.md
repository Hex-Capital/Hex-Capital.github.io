# Roma -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Roma’s possible secret is that a persistent task record will replace the chat window as the control surface for personal AI. That is a proposition worth testing: do people repeatedly delegate consequential work through tasks, or return to their existing assistants once the novelty disappears? The dossier establishes that Roma has shipped the interface, with background execution and results stored beside each task. It does not establish a prevailing belief the founders have disproved. I would give the proposed secret its absent-evidence prior, without treating the researcher’s description as proof that the founders lack one.

The more consequential problem is ownership of the opportunity. Roma addresses consumer productivity broadly; no bounded initial segment is identified. Meanwhile, Todoist already exposes tasks to external AI agents, Lindy executes work across business tools, and Motion combines scheduling with workflows. These products differ, but their overlap makes task-based execution a contested capability. Roma’s personal memory might eventually preserve customer value, yet the dossier provides no observed switching costs, exclusive data, or technical advantage. Its use of OpenAI and Composio supplies useful infrastructure without establishing something competitors cannot purchase. A better interface can create value while leaving its producer little power to retain it.

The strongest argument against passing is the founding relationship. Eichenwald and Senderek reportedly met approximately 20 years ago and built a running app together. That is affirmative evidence of alignment, rather than a team assembled for an application deadline. Senderek previously built Loom, acquired by Dropbox in 2014; Eichenwald built Senic, acquired by Gira; Osborne brings Dropbox creative experience. This is a credible team for making consumer software feel inevitable. But the documented working history covers two founders, and the dossier does not establish all three founders’ commitment, ownership, or control of essential engineering. Those remain unknowns, not red flags. Prior acquisitions also do not establish that selling Roma is their objective.

Roma fits my less-public pattern of backing consumer and financial products; its category alone is no objection. Nor does a three-person team or limited public traction count against a pre-seed company. The actual customer evidence is narrower: nine US App Store ratings averaging 5.0 suggest some early enthusiasm, but cannot establish persistent delegation. The Mac download, iPhone app, YC launch, and public Claude plugin are concrete acquisition routes. None yet demonstrates repeatable conversion or acquisition economics. Browser verification blocks and user-completed payments also matter: every interruption returns responsibility to the person the product promises to relieve.

The free iPhone listing leaves pricing and execution margins unknown, so I cannot construct one revenue engine capable of supporting an exceptional outcome. I would not demand mature financial metrics here. I would demand that stronger product evidence carry the investment case while those economics remain open. Roma has not supplied the independent technical discontinuity, persistent transaction demand, or customer-validated deployment path that supports my known exceptions. The bull case is that experienced consumer founders turn reliable delegation into a daily habit before incumbents recognize the interface shift. The unresolved issue is whether that habit belongs to Roma or simply follows whichever assistant executes best.

I pass on this evidence. The decisive adverse signal is competitive convergence without a demonstrated monopoly mechanism; missing revenue is secondary. My one condition for reopening the decision is an independently measured 90-day paid cohort in a named customer segment showing that accumulated Roma task history produces materially higher retained task completion than the same underlying models operating without that history. That would begin to turn a pleasant interface into a position the company can own.
