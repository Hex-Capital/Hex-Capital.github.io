# Micora -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements** is Micora’s strongest fit. The bottleneck is getting dependable power commissioned at grid-constrained industrial sites. Micora proposes using drone surveys and autonomous forklifts to place movable solar blocks alongside containerized batteries. Cheaper autonomy could reduce specialized crew time and shorten the wait for usable power. Micora claims most setups reach available power within three weeks of confirmed order, subject to site review. The memo contains no independently verified result at a specified load and uptime. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

The LAKI Robotics co-design gives Micora a concrete integration effort involving specialized forks and its own field-autonomy software. The memo leaves Micora’s rights to the joint design and any performance gains from repeat deployments open. Micora reports projects underway at Pacific island and manufacturing sites. The observed purchase and lease offers establish a route to transact, while buyer commitments and realized revenue remain unverified. The founders’ prior robotics, manufacturing, and solar-storage deployment work supports a representative field trial at a plausible U.S. seed entry. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Founders & Team*)

**Scale scenario.** For a purchase, compare site revenue with hardware, freight, site preparation, installation, commissioning, and any backup equipment Micora supplies. For a lease, test whether paid utilization and hardware reuse cover fleet capital, financing, idle time, and moves. The displayed terms exclude ongoing operations, so the buyer comparison must include those costs and any backup needed to meet the same uptime. Site pricing, crew hours, and redeployment economics remain unverified. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

With cheap intelligence and ample capital, integrators can source standard batteries and pursue their own installation automation. Prefabricated alternatives already exist, and diesel remains a fallback. If one of those options can match Micora’s time to dependable power and delivered cost at the same site, buyers have little reason to pay Micora a premium. The memo establishes credible alternatives and leaves site-level parity unresolved, so the Path A override does not trigger. Path B has weaker support because the memo does not establish a newly viable buyer or transaction. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Micora complete a paid, independently verified commissioning at a representative grid-constrained industrial site materially faster than the best feasible alternative at the same load and uptime? Buyer acceptance, logged crew hours, actual site costs including backup, and documented rights to reuse the installation system would answer the question. A material speed advantage at a viable margin with reusable deployment rights would support Reach out once the score and confidence gates clear. A comparable alternative that matches schedule, reliability, and cost would support Pass if no separate thesis path emerges.
