# Index Law -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Index Law gives me forthcoming subscription prices—$149 and $499 a month—but no price for the equity. Proposed post-money, competing term sheets, whether my money is genuinely scarce: all unknown. I cannot infer an auction from YC membership. I also cannot underwrite a cheap, non-consensus entry from silence. The second gate is equally open: nothing in the dossier identifies the finite experiment this round finances, its deadline, or the result that materially changes the business’s value. Shipping Mike is progress; it does not establish what my check buys.

The interesting mechanism is the bridge between a business that needs an answer and independent lawyers who supply professional judgment. Persistent company context could eliminate repeated intake and make each subsequent contract easier to handle. That fits my pattern of backing translation layers between systems. But who owns the resulting advantage? Index Law describes memory, agent harnesses, and evaluations without showing that accumulated context causes customers to return or pay. Its [191,088 launch-post views](https://doomers.ai/launches/index-law) and 169 free templates establish exposure. They do not establish a proprietary pool of demand. Meanwhile, Legora already describes multistep legal agents; access to capable automation will keep spreading.

The strongest objection to passing is Pativada himself. His previous AI company reportedly secured government contracts and booked more than $20 million in revenue, and he has experienced the legal-service purchasing problem directly. Those are operating receipts, although that revenue belongs to his previous business. Mike has also shipped, and Index Law [reports contract turnaround below 60 minutes and quotes up to 80% lower](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/XP1-meet-mike-1-your-company-s-ai-lawyer). Together, these facts make a credible case that he can turn frustrating procurement into a useful service. Dominic Raab adds legal-system access. Still, an advisor’s experience does not establish that the founder has operated regulated legal delivery, and the customer claims lack named buyers, sample sizes, or repeat-use evidence.

Delete the next financing from the spreadsheet. Does Mike work as a business? The free preview and forthcoming subscriptions cannot answer that yet. Separately approved lawyer fees could keep professional labor outside the subscription, but the dossier supplies no inference costs, supervision costs, gross margin, burn, or break-even plan. Index Law explicitly recognizes wrong governing law and missed escalation as failure modes; its confirmation requirements and specialist oversight are sensible responses. The underwriting question is whether customers still buy at a price that covers those safeguards. An 80% cheaper quote does not reveal the seller’s profit.

I am passing with my own money. The exceptions to my usual gates require unusually hard evidence: paid conversion and retention, cash economics, compelling external validation, or a financing structure that removes repeated fundraising. Index Law’s prior-founder receipts strengthen the team case; they do not supply those overrides for Mike. My one verifiable condition for reopening is a completed 90-day cohort of at least ten paying businesses that renew at the published subscription prices while generating positive contribution margin after inference, support, and required legal supervision. That would demonstrate that remembered context compounds into repeat purchases at a sustainable delivery cost. Today, the product possibility is stronger than the investment evidence.
