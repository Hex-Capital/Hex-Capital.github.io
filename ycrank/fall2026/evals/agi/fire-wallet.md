# Fire Wallet -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Fire Wallet’s strongest commercial case is private, per-request checkout for agents operating within preset spending limits. For **Building blocks of superintelligence**, the memo establishes a payment workflow and documents competing approaches, including Crossmint’s per-request payments and AWS’s managed payments preview. It leaves open whether payment access limits a specific AI workload or whether equivalent capacity would be slow to add. For **Industry enabled by superintelligence**, Fire attributes the affordability of small payments to its settlement rail. The memo gives no evidence that AI reduces the fully loaded cost per successful purchase enough to open a previously excluded use. Both paths score zero, triggering Pass. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Fire reports a signed Trustboop LOI and an enterprise customer onboarding. The reported $1.5M per month is the customer’s existing volume. Repeat use through Fire remains unverified. The founders’ MobileCoin experience and Fire’s reported sub-two-second mainnet test support seed-stage execution. Existing alternatives and Fire’s documented support for merchants accepting e402 alongside x402 limit the evidence for durable value capture. Fire advertises zero fees of its own, and the $0.00256 network fee provides no basis for estimating Fire’s contribution per transaction. The lack of verified paid deployments, revenue, and scaled operating data limits evidence confidence. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Defensibility, Key Risks, The Idea)

### Countercase

Agents could make frequent API purchases that are uneconomic when each request requires human approval. That scenario assumes preset budgets let agents handle routine purchases and exceptions cheaply enough to cross the buyer’s affordability threshold. Fire’s spending controls and reported mainnet test make the workflow plausible, and private settlement would still require merchant integration under model parity. The memo establishes neither the prior approval burden nor savings that unlock recurring use, so the countercase leaves the Pass gate in effect. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent API customer use Fire for repeat paid requests that were previously uneconomic because of manual approval? A live deployment would move the action to Reach out if it showed that agent automation crosses the customer’s affordability threshold after settlement, integration, and exception-handling costs, and that the customer still needs a separate payment service under model parity.
