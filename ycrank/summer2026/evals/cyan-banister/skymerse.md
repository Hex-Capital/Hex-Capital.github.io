# Skymerse -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock six years forward: airline operations software continuously reconciles weather, aircraft condition, procedures, and airport changes before a dispatcher opens a briefing. Skymerse has a real artifact leaking from that future. LOT Polish Airlines already uses Notamify across its network, turning incoming notices into approximately 15–20 filtered alerts daily with processing in about 30 seconds. What is missing is the path from alerting to trusted operational autonomy: certification, authoritative-data access, operator integrations, and liability allocation remain unnamed transitions rather than solved steps.

The strongest evidence is behavioral. Skymerse has processed 3 million NOTAMs across 487 days, accumulated 3,800 registered users, and reports thousands of daily users. More importantly, an airline placed the product inside a live monitoring workflow. That is costly behavior: LOT is allowing an outside system to filter safety-relevant information across its network. The $24.90 Pro subscription proves only that there is a purchasing surface, not meaningful revenue, but the production deployment is far more informative than a pre-seed waitlist.

Damian Szumski also looks like someone who found this problem from inside the machinery. He is a licensed dispatcher with experience spanning LOT, Lufthansa Systems, airline-software implementation, and Flightradar24’s API product. That is genuine operating tuition, and Notamify is first-believer proof rather than a newly assembled pitch. I cannot award the highest founder score because the dossier supplies no dated pre-company experiments or recruiting record, and the disappearance of previously identified co-founder Michał Pasierbski needs a clean explanation. YC now lists Szumski alone, so I will not invent a 50/50-control problem, but I would inspect ownership, departures, and final decision authority before signing.

The broken system is concrete but not fully economic. Skymerse estimates that fragmented NOTAM, weather, runway, aircraft, and procedure systems consume three planning hours per flight. Automating reconciliation returns attention and decision capacity to dispatchers, yet the dossier does not identify who captures the present rent or quantify the cost of errors and delay. This is less an artificial gatekeeper being removed than a pile of safety rules that accumulated for defensible reasons. The product must absorb that complexity without pretending it can erase it.

The magically weird part sits underneath the “AI copilot” language: 2,005,126 affected-element exposures convert hostile aviation text into structured runways, taxiways, navigation aids, timing, and applicability. That schema can support alerts, APIs, airport monitoring, and eventually flight-level decisions. It is a controlled enabling asset, not merely a chat interface. But it is not yet a protected layer. Jeppesen ForeFlight’s Airflow combines aviation data, customer data, and certification expertise inside an incumbent distribution system, while Overwatch AI already spans NOTAMs, weather, fuel, MELs, manuals, and flight plans.

That incumbent threat is the case that stings. Skymerse is not an authoritative aeronautical source, its end-to-end verdicts become fragile when any input is stale, and its public product moved from ATC transcription to Notamify and then “autopilot” within roughly a year. This could become a useful NOTAM feature that Jeppesen bundles away. The reason I still invest is that the structured corpus and repeated production behavior arrived before the grander positioning; the artifact led, rather than the fashionable story. My single flip condition is verifiable: if Skymerse has not put a second named airline into paid production use integrating operator manuals and MEL constraints by August 2027, I move to Pass.
