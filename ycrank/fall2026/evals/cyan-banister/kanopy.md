# Kanopy -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a utility truck collecting the grid’s next inspection simply by driving its usual route. Video becomes geometry; geometry becomes a clearance measurement; that measurement becomes somebody’s work order. Kanopy already describes those transformations. What I cannot yet see is how separate inspections become a persistent understanding of the network that utilities trust between visits. That is my imagined destination, not an established company capability. The dossier supports a useful inspection product more clearly than the infrastructure operating layer in its headline.

Why does measuring a tree near a wire require specialized hardware and weeks of processing? That is the ordinary question here with potentially interesting economics. Kanopy’s customer-owned cameras could let inspection contractors produce useful measurements without buying another expensive sensing system. Exportable work records could also shorten the distance between noticing a hazard and assigning the work ([Kanopy website](https://kanopy-ai.com/)). That returns some operational control to the people doing inspections. But the dossier identifies equipment expense and delay, not a quantified toll or an artificial restriction whose removal fundamentally changes who can participate. I like the repair; I cannot yet underwrite a larger redistribution of power.

The brothers have a credible division of labor for attempting it. Yann’s utility advisory work supplies exposure to the institution they must sell into, while Nicolas’s computer-vision leadership at ShapeScale and robotics research supply relevant technical experience. Yann’s documented participation in Aerate in 2019 adds a pre-Kanopy building artifact ([Penn Almanac](https://almanac.upenn.edu/articles/aerate-2019-startup-challenge-grand-prize)). These are more useful than impressive school names alone. They do not establish a long-running obsession with this particular problem, or show how the founders recruited others into that obsession. Yann is identified as CEO; being brothers supplies no evidence of equal control or a governance failure.

The strongest argument for writing a check is that Kanopy has escaped the slide deck. Six utilities and contractors signed LOIs or MSAs covering 270,000 scoped miles, and the company reports mapping 3,093 miles, including more than 300 across four states in two weeks ([Antler, April 2026](https://www.antler.co/blog/announcing-the-spring-2026-u-s-portfolio-showcase); [company update](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/yann-pfitzer-63b404142_big-things-coming-soon-for-kanopy-activity-7519062909490970624-DRPy)). That activates my preference for discovering companies through operating artifacts. But mapped mileage measures company activity; it does not establish that customers repeatedly relied on the output. **CosmicBrain AI** supplies a relevant exception to requiring revenue: a functioning prototype and industrial commitments can justify an early check. The material difference is that its founder’s lifelong robotics record supplied independent conviction about the journey. Kanopy’s relevant experience is promising, but less conclusive.

The bear case bites precisely where this could become infrastructure. A reconstruction is valuable only if its measurements and asset associations survive operational use. Advertised 2–4-inch accuracy lacks independently retrieved validation, and the September–October release notes describe pole-association and wire-fitting corrections. Those corrections show engineering work, not proven reliability. Meanwhile, public reconstruction models make geometry generation more available, and Schneider’s announced AiDASH acquisition could strengthen an incumbent’s distribution. Kanopy fits my pattern of unglamorous workflow repair, but has not demonstrated control of a scarce enabling asset. Nor does the dossier establish a severe privacy problem or dependence on one closed model supplier; those red flags should not be invented.

I am **Neutral**. I would spend time with the product before committing my own money. Missing revenue is not the obstacle; the unresolved step is whether camera-derived measurements become trusted customer decisions. My flip condition is one utility independently documenting repeat paid deployment through a full vegetation cycle, with blinded clearance measurements meeting its operational acceptance thresholds. That would turn reported mapping activity into evidence that Kanopy has changed how somebody actually maintains the grid.
