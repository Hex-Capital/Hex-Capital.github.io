# Cesara -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest supported route is a strategic acquisition by a revenue-cycle software vendor. Cesara’s public demo shows a workflow that prices claims against payer contracts at submission and prepares clause-backed appeals. Owning that workflow could help a buyer recover more for hospitals using its existing claims and remittance integrations. The CEO reports pilots identified over 90% of valid underpayments and recovered over 70% of those identified. Customer names, sample sizes, recovery dollars, and realized fees are undisclosed, leaving the incremental value to a buyer unquantified. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

The seed-stage milestone is to convert service-line pilots into paid production deployments and demonstrate repeatable net recoveries after integration and appeal costs. The demo and reported pilot outcomes give Cesara a starting point. Conflicting financing figures leave runway and additional capital needs unclear. The memo describes security reviews and business associate agreements for claims access. Contract assignment, continuity of data access after a sale, and holder participation remain unverified. The memo supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with ownership diluted from 5% to 3% and no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. Both sale scenarios assume an eligible holder sells the entire stake for cash within eight years, with $5 million deducted from enterprise value for net debt and deal adjustments and the remaining equity proceeds distributed pro rata with no preference deductions. These are illustrative prices. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction pricing to anchor them. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

- **Modest sale.** At $40 million in enterprise value, $35 million is available for distribution and the holder receives $1.05 million, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** At $100 million in enterprise value, $95 million is available for distribution and the holder receives $2.85 million, or 5.7×. Total deductions above about $16.7 million would put this case below the hurdle.
- **Prolonged private operation.** If Cesara reinvests cash and no eligible share purchase or distribution occurs within eight years, the holder realizes $0.

### Countercase

Adonis and Trek Health already monitor reimbursement variances, giving them a starting point to build clause-linked appeals internally. If Cesara’s incremental recoveries are small or costly to reproduce across hospitals, a vendor could build the feature or offer a price that falls short of the holder’s hurdle. The memo establishes workflow overlap. The cost to replicate Cesara’s workflow and the strongest credible sale price remain unresolved, so the Pass gate stays open and Watch fits the evidence gap. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could a revenue-cycle vendor justify from Cesara’s incremental net recoveries over its existing workflow? Verified results across paid hospital deployments and a buyer’s assessment of the cost to build equivalent functionality would answer it. Move to Reach out if that evidence supports a price range that can clear the holder’s eight-year cash hurdle after deal deductions. Move to Pass if it shows the strongest credible route would leave the holder materially short.
