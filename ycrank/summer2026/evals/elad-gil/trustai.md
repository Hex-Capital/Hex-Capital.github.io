# TrustAI -- Elad Gil Evaluation

TrustAI’s 51-test assessment catalog makes TrustAI a buildable compliance workflow with a low science burden, but the absence of a defensible index position is the analytical signature: TrustAI can probably ship the product, yet must win every account against Noma, WitnessAI, Zenity, Patronus AI, and increasingly the ERP vendors themselves. The recent pivot from browser automation reinforces the distinction. Product construction is not the bottleneck; durable distribution is.

**Color Genomics** is the high rung on the single-miracle ladder: workflow and data infrastructure built on commodity sequencing rather than a bet on new chemistry. TrustAI shares that architecture because TrustAI assumes agents, MCP hubs, and ERP connectors already exist; the remaining miracle is becoming the trusted assessment layer around them. TrustAI diverges where its sample report says reconstruction-local findings transfer to production only as hypotheses and leaves some controls unassessed. Color controlled the workflow sufficiently to make the evidence useful; TrustAI has not yet shown that its 51 tests predict live-agent behavior well enough for an enterprise to rely on the go/no-go report.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism for index positioning: occupying a secondary position in a market where the leader captures the compounding economics. TrustAI currently resembles that pattern more than Stripe’s “index on e-commerce.” TrustAI integrates with SAP, Oracle, NetSuite, Jira, and ServiceNow, but Noma has runtime access control, WitnessAI has intent-based governance, Zenity spans build time and runtime, and the ERP vendors control identity and authorization at the source. TrustAI’s analyst-reviewed evidence report is a feature wedge, not yet a workflow every agent deployment must touch.

The EU AI Act duties beginning August 2, 2026, SAP’s 2025 Joule-agent rollout, and Oracle’s October 2025 NetSuite MCP connectivity constitute a real market re-opening. TrustAI benefits as agent access to sensitive systems expands faster than legacy GRC controls can adapt, and TrustAI’s ERP blast-radius mapping could compound through recurring assessments, control updates, and remediation data over the next 18 months. The limitation is ownership of the catalyst: SAP and Oracle can embed controls directly, while TrustAI must persuade enterprises that independent evidence is necessary. The window is real, but TrustAI has not yet demonstrated that the window belongs to TrustAI.

TrustAI’s order forms contemplate annual platform fees, quarterly consumption, professional services, and implementation charges, but TrustAI discloses no revenue, customer, repeat usage, or signed LOI. LinkedIn’s 679 launch reactions and 105 comments are attention, not the “just working” signal I saw in Harvey’s revenue curve. The score flips into invest territory if TrustAI produces three named enterprises running repeat quarterly assessments, at least $250,000 of contracted ARR, and greater than 20% month-over-month growth; without that, the revenue dimension remains at its absent-evidence default.

**Perplexity** is the relevant founder parallel because Aravind Srinivas repeatedly returned with the discussed product built. Hannah Chung and Medha Venkatapathy have built together since freshman year, pivoted TrustAI from browser automation, and shipped TrustAI’s MCP connection flow, five named enterprise integrations, and a 51-test catalog. That is credible iteration, but the dossier offers no sequence of customer conversations followed by shipped changes and no prior independently scaled product. MIT, Virtu, World Bank, and LLM post-training credentials establish capacity; they do not establish Perplexity-speed execution.

TrustAI reaches roughly $1 million to $3 million ARR and is acquired by ServiceNow for less than $75 million by July 2029, rather than becoming an independent agent-security platform. TrustAI’s single-miracle architecture should produce a functional product and several regulated-enterprise customers, while TrustAI’s weak index position pushes value toward a platform owner that already controls enterprise workflows, identity context, and distribution. The analyst-reviewed assessment layer is more plausible as a ServiceNow governance module than as a standalone category winner.

SAP’s Joule rollout, Oracle’s MCP connectivity, and the August 2026 EU AI Act deadline create the strongest bull case: TrustAI could become independent assurance infrastructure precisely because enterprises will not accept vendors grading their own agents. TrustAI’s 51 tests, six risk categories, four framework mappings, and human review could become the agent equivalent of a recurring SOC 2 workflow. The bear case still wins because TrustAI has no disclosed buyer, reconstruction results remain partly projected, and four funded competitors have raised more than $342 million while offering broader runtime control. I would flip to Invest if TrustAI verifies production—not reconstructed—agent behavior for three named Fortune 500 customers and those customers renew assessments while ARR compounds above 20% month over month.
