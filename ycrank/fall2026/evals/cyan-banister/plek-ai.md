# Plek AI -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years forward, I can imagine a company testing changes to its organization before making employees live through them. Getting there requires knowing what people actually do, measuring which interventions work, and feeding those outcomes back into the model. Plek AI maps an employee census onto 50,000 jobs and 250,000 tasks. That is a starting instrument. The dossier does not establish the feedback loop that would turn workforce estimates into a dependable company brain. I would be supplying that future myself, so I give it no affirmative credit.

Pierre Bouquet is the strongest reason to keep looking. His MIT workforce research directly overlaps the product’s task-estimation mechanism; this is more substantial than a founder discovering workforce optimization after discovering language models. His reported Biot exit in 2022 adds evidence that he has built a business before. The published research and personal software projects show someone who makes things. They do not establish a long pre-company obsession with this particular problem or an ability to recruit complementary believers, but the domain connection is real.

Why does an assessment take six to eight weeks? Plek identifies the consulting study and spreadsheet reconciliation as the machinery consuming that time. Reconciling organizational, compensation, location, and AI scenarios employee by employee could remove genuinely tedious work, especially when several proposed savings overlap. That fits my interest in unglamorous workflow repair. But the agency returned here belongs primarily to the PE owner and operating partner. The dossier neither quantifies consulting fees displaced nor shows employees gaining control over their work. Faster restructuring is a legible business benefit; it earns only partial credit against my broken-system criterion.

The strongest objection to passing is that Plek may already have compressed an expensive expert service into software. Its reported identification of $30 million in annual EBITDA opportunities at a 3,600-person company, matching an adviser’s six-week assessment, deserves attention. Combined with Bouquet’s research, that could support a useful product before meaningful revenue exists. My pre-revenue exception allows that possibility when a functioning artifact and committed users make the technical destination credible. Here, however, the benchmark is unnamed and company-reported; payment, repeat use, and realized outcomes are undisclosed. Agreement with a consulting estimate does not establish that either estimate survives implementation. The November 2026 operating-partners summit appearance supplies access to buyers, without resolving that question.

Plek also has to earn the right to examine these people. Its privacy policy says free-text survey responses reach AI providers without automatic scanning or redaction. Excluding direct identifiers from structured inputs and excluding API content from model training are meaningful limits, but they do not prevent an employee from identifying themselves or a colleague in prose. I cannot establish a severe privacy violation from this dossier, and I would not invent one. I also cannot treat that architecture as a cleared privacy gate. Meanwhile, publicly available research does not establish control of a scarce enabling asset, and Orgvue already offers AI-assisted role classification. Plek’s reconciliation might be valuable without becoming infrastructure other products must depend on.

I pass with my own money today. The founder has more substance than the demonstrated future does. My one verifiable flip condition is an independently auditable repeat deployment: a paying customer renews after measured outcomes validate Plek’s employee-level recommendations, with documented controls preventing identifiable survey text from reaching external model providers. That would connect technical insight, costly customer behavior, and responsible data handling in one observable event.
