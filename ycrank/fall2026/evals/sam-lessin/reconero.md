# Reconero -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Reconero’s proposed post-money, competing-term-sheet count, and use of proceeds are undisclosed. I cannot tell whether my check buys an overlooked experiment or joins an adequately financed software company. YC membership establishes neither. Those are missing underwriting inputs, not evidence of an auction. The product gives me something concrete to examine: can access to police workflows become an economic asset the founders control?

Alyx searches departmental records, extracts information from body-camera footage, and prefills reports for officer review. The September 2026 reporting of police-unit trials and iterative feedback matters: somebody inside the institution is trying the product. That earns more credit than a demo invitation or 53 LinkedIn followers. But the department owns the underlying records, and Reconero promises open-format exports. Its potential dark pool is the working relationship and accumulated workflow knowledge. The dossier does not yet show that either causes a purchase or keeps an agency using the software.

The strongest argument against passing is Riwan Azeroual’s previous sentiment-analysis business reporting $150K ARR. He has already demonstrated that he can build something people pay for; that revenue belongs to the prior business. Edward Liu co-developed ReconeroRMS, and the team has reached police users despite having no documented policing operating background. These are builders with commercial receipts and an emerging bridge into a difficult institution. That fits my preference for founders translating between systems. It earns founder credit without establishing that they can own procurement, implementation, and ongoing departmental operations.

The economic question is what survives once implementation and human review are charged honestly. Reconero offers migration across legacy RMS, CAD exports, spreadsheets, and scanned documents while competing with Mark43’s integrated platform and Axon’s report-generation product. Each successful migration could deepen the customer relationship; each bespoke migration could also consume the subscription margin. Officer review is a sensible product boundary, but there are no Reconero accuracy measurements or delivery-cost figures showing how much labor remains. I would not assume errors disappear with scale, and I would not attribute errors from a comparable tool to this team. Pricing, burn, and contribution economics remain unknown.

A narrow departmental experiment could make this investable: one workflow, a fixed implementation budget, a dated result, and a measurable change in cash economics. The dossier supplies no such capital plan, so I cannot award credit for the experiment I would like them to run. Nor does the evidence justify an exception to my gates: reported trials do not establish paid conversion and retention, and prior-business revenue does not establish Reconero’s self-financing path. My verifiable condition for reopening diligence is a current trial converting into a named, paid deployment with positive contribution after migration, support, and required review costs. Until then, I pass. There is enough founder execution to take the company seriously, but insufficient evidence that my money purchases durable access or a finite, value-changing result.
