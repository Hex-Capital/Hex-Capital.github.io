# Hundred -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Hundred’s possible secret is that inspecting every incoming component can become cheaper than sampling once controlled optics and one-reference calibration remove the setup burden. That is my reconstruction of its opportunity; the dossier does not establish that named competitors reject it. The proposition fails if normal material variation produces enough false rejects, or inspection takes enough time, that factories retain sampling. Sub-100 μm cosmetic detection is a specific engineering claim. It does not yet establish the economically important truth.

The dedicated tray station gives Hundred a recognizable starting market: incoming consumer-electronics components. But what would let it own that market? Elementary can upgrade installed cameras; Instrumental connects inspection to production analysis; Overview supplies configurable industrial cameras. These are different approaches, so their existence does not prove Hundred is interchangeable. Nevertheless, controlled illumination and complementary detection methods establish a product architecture, without yet demonstrating proprietary superiority, exclusive data, or another monopoly mechanism. Photographic records could become valuable infrastructure for supplier disputes. Until retention rights and customer dependence are demonstrated, that remains a hypothesis.

The founding structure supplies the strongest affirmative evidence. Diaz and Gosserand met in 2017, well before this Fall 2026 company; that is meaningful evidence against founders assembling merely to apply to YC. It does not establish prior joint execution, and Liao’s working history with the others remains unspecified. Full-time status and economic ownership are also undisclosed, rather than adverse. Separately, Diaz’s five-and-a-half years in consumer hardware, including supplier troubleshooting in China and Mexico, make the problem selection credible. The team combines relevant hardware, production-software, and quantitative skills. Credentials cannot settle the optical question, but these founders have a plausible basis for discovering something competitors missed.

The strongest objection to passing is that Hundred may be solving inspection at the wrong location in everyone else’s workflow—and therefore the right location for a new company. Catching defects before assembly could make a separate station worthwhile even where cameras already exist. Company-reported deployments underway to three founding partners provide an opening to test that proposition. The partner program also names actual buyer classes and offers onsite implementation, earning more distribution credit than an abstract enterprise-sales plan. Yet completed installations, payment, conversion, and delivery cost remain unknown. With no published price or hardware/software fee split, I cannot connect that route to a scalable revenue engine. The broader $25.3 billion machine-vision market supplies no substitute for that arithmetic.

Hundred fits my less-public preference for companies attacking physical production constraints. It also avoids the documented coordination failure of requiring a factory, licensing regime, and manufacturing ecosystem to arrive simultaneously. There is no disclosed founder-liquidity or convenience-matching red flag. Those distinctions keep this from an active thesis rejection. But the exceptions that could overcome weak monopoly evidence—a verified technical discontinuity, persistent customer transactions, or a concrete validated deployment path—are not established by deployments described as underway. My central expectation is that implementation and customization will determine the business before a compounding monopoly emerges.

I pass on the present evidence. Missing pre-seed metrics do not prove failure; the current evidence simply supports a credible inspection company more strongly than an exceptional investment. One verifiable event would reopen the decision: a founding partner places a paid repeat order following an independently documented production trial showing a tenfold reduction in escaped defects at unchanged throughput, with positive contribution margin after installation costs. That would connect the proposed secret to customer behavior and begin answering who captures the value.
