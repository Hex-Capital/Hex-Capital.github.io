# Elithia -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition is Elithia’s strongest supported path to liquidity. The memo describes an editable, source-linked medical chronology built for physician sign-off. An adjacent platform such as EvenUp could acquire that workflow to reach physician experts and medico-legal agencies. Elithia reports sales to both groups, with pricing and billing terms undisclosed. The founder reports that one expert, his mother, cut report preparation from nearly a week to about two days. That result suggests a reason to buy the workflow if the savings repeat across paying customers. Buyer interest and the economics of acquiring the workflow remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Elithia reports 70+ experts using the product on live cases and two agencies under contract. A plausible 12–18 month milestone would be sustained paid case volume at those agencies, with measured review-time savings and a check for material omissions. The existing deployments provide a place to run that test. Its cost and Elithia’s runway are unknown, so the timeline is an assumption. A software acquisition appears feasible in principle, with IP ownership, contract assignment, and health-data permissions still open. The memo records a UK incorporation for Minerva Labs Limited and differing company profiles, leaving the identity of the entity that holds the IP and contracts to be verified. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Using the shared seed benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale cases below assume $10 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, no further senior claims against the remaining equity, and an eligible holder cashing out the full stake within eight years. The sale prices are illustrative. The memo gives no transaction price or buyer economics to support either one. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

- **Modest sale.** $40 million of enterprise value leaves $30 million in distributable equity and $900,000 in holder cash, or 1.8×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million of enterprise value leaves $90 million in distributable equity and $2.7 million in holder cash, or 5.4×. If the holder can cash out only 80% of the stake by year eight, realized proceeds fall to $2.16 million, or 4.32×.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested yields zero realized proceeds in eight years. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route.

### Countercase

EvenUp already bundles medical chronologies with demand drafting, and Medrecords AI already serves physician reviewers with source-linked chronologies. A buyer could extend its own product or work with an incumbent. Elithia’s reported usage and agency contracts leave paid volume and retention unknown, so the buyer may see little reason to pay for the distribution. This supports Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural block to a sale nor a credible price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle, so the Pass gate is unmet. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Would an adjacent platform value Elithia’s paid physician-review workflow and agency relationships enough to support a sale above $83.3 million in distributable equity within eight years? A buyer-side build-versus-buy assessment grounded in retained paid case volume and independently measured review savings would answer it. A credible value range that clears the holder’s cash hurdle after deal deductions would move the action to Reach out. A supported ceiling below the hurdle on the strongest route would move it to Pass.
