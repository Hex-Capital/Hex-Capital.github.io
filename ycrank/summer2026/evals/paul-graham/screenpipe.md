# screenpipe -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Louis Beaumont built screenpipe on a weekend, then turned screenpipe into a 358-release repository with 18,900 GitHub stars, 250,000 claimed users, and tens of thousands of dollars in revenue. That is an animal founder attached to an organizational question mark. The strongest signal is resourcefulness before institutional money; the weakest is that screenpipe still presents Beaumont as a solo founder despite a team size of six. The analytical signature is a prolific hacker moving faster than his founder configuration may safely support.

**Airbnb** is the high rung for animal resourcefulness because the cereal boxes proved the founders could manufacture money when ordinary financing failed. Screenpipe shares the load-bearing mechanism: Beaumont shipped screenpipe in a weekend, accumulated hundreds of thousands of installs, and converted an open repository into paid plans reaching $150 per enterprise seat. Screenpipe diverges because 18,900 stars and founder-led LinkedIn distribution are successful channels, not an improbable improvisation like selling $4 cereal for $40. Beaumont has left tracks everywhere, but screenpipe has not yet shown the stunt that makes resistance look futile.

The polished AI-wrapper pair from my pass example is the low rung because its founders planned to hire technical ability after funding instead of embodying it. Screenpipe does not have that failure mechanism: Beaumont created the Rust/TypeScript capture stack, and screenpipe already exposes a REST API, MCP access, OCR fallback, and programmable pipes. But the adjacent red flag remains. Screenpipe names one founder, provides no co-founder history, and has six employees without a documented partner who can contradict Beaumont when speed becomes confusion. **Loopt** shows that a sufficiently forceful solo personality can override weak timing; it does not show that founder configuration stops mattering.

Screenpipe’s schlep compounds each time screenpipe captures another awkward application, operating-system edge case, or regulated conversation. Continuous recording means exclusions, redaction, accessibility APIs, OCR, transcription, storage, and enterprise policy all have to work simultaneously; typical CPU usage of 5%–10% and storage growth of 5–10 GB monthly make the grind visible. Over 18 months, Microsoft Recall can copy the obvious feature, but Microsoft is less likely to delight in supporting macOS, Linux, local models, per-pipe permissions, and every strange enterprise deployment. That is schlep blindness working in screenpipe’s favor.

Beaumont’s prior work with LLM retrieval, FAISS, defense AI, and screenpipe since 2024 gives screenpipe credible founder-market fit, but it does not prove the idea began with Beaumont’s own recurring pain. The tier would flip upward if screenpipe showed that Beaumont originally built the weekend prototype to recover a specific lost meeting, investigation, or workflow he personally needed, then kept using it before anyone asked. Without that fact, screenpipe is closer to a technically natural opportunity than Dropbox’s forgotten USB stick. I apply a meaningful penalty because genuine organic ideas usually come with a story too specific to omit.

**Stripe** is the useful hacker-genealogy parallel: the artifact existed before the institution knew how large the opportunity was. Screenpipe’s 18,900 stars, 1,800 forks, 358 releases, local SQLite architecture, and cross-platform capture engine are stronger evidence than a CS credential would be. Screenpipe also satisfies my unstated hacker pattern unusually well; the repository is not decoration around a sales organization. The gap from Stripe is that Beaumont’s public history shows prolific startup shipping, not yet the Collisons’ longer trail of teenage technical achievement and prior company-building.

The 36-month prediction is that screenpipe reaches $8 million–$20 million in annual recurring revenue by July 2029 as a local-first context layer for enterprise agents, while Beaumont either recruits a true co-founder or screenpipe’s execution becomes visibly bottlenecked around him. The mechanism is not generic AI demand. Screenpipe already has 250,000 claimed users and early paid conversion, while the continuous privacy and cross-platform schleps turn 358 releases into accumulated knowledge that a meeting-note wrapper cannot reproduce quickly.

Microsoft Recall, Granola, and Otter.ai form the strongest bear case: screenpipe could be squeezed between a bundled operating-system feature and focused products with mature enterprise distribution, while default PostHog telemetry and a source-available license undermine the trust and community adoption screenpipe depends on. That objection stings because sensitive-data capture is the product, not a peripheral risk. But screenpipe’s hundreds of thousands of installs, tens of thousands of dollars in launch revenue, Linux/macOS/Windows support, and 1,800 forks show demand for something broader and more programmable than meeting notes or Windows Recall. I would flip to Pass if, by July 2027, screenpipe has not converted its current reach into at least $1 million ARR or documented repeat enterprise deployments; that would show the stars were applause rather than need.
