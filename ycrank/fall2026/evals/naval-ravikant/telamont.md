# Telamont -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Telamont’s strongest evidence is that the founders have put a product into construction teams’ hands. Canmec, TCI+, and Béland Lapointe appear among its customer logos; the company reports 220+ product updates and 300+ client touchpoints. That is learning through action. Goulet’s construction exposure and Gagnon’s industrial AI work at Poka make the learning credible. I give more weight to the shipped product than to their previous employers. The remaining uncertainty is how deeply either founder has personally operated the contractual workflows the product now evaluates.

The decisive question is what Telamont accumulates with each deployment. Its construction-specific playbooks and expert feedback could become valuable intellectual property. The dossier establishes their existence, but supplies no evidence of exclusive rights, a proprietary model, or improvements competitors cannot reproduce. External AI processing alone does not establish a thin wrapper. Equally, continuous monitoring and ten integrations do not establish a durable advantage. If a competitor copied the implementation tomorrow, I cannot identify a demonstrated asset that would keep Telamont ahead.

The strongest objection to passing is substantial: the team may already be turning expensive construction mistakes into a repeatable learning system. Company-reported outcomes include $1.4 million in avoided rework and $2–3 million recovered on subcontract packages. Even allowing for unnamed customers and unverified attribution, those are consequential problems. Frequent releases and professional feedback could produce the compounding judgment I want. My exceptions allow an unusually capable founding team or distinctive prototype to precede distribution and monetization. Telamont has enough evidence to deserve that consideration. It lacks enough evidence that its learning survives beyond individual projects to justify the override.

The market offers useful room to maneuver. Comparing contracts, drawings, and revisions can support payment notices, delay management, and compliance review for the same construction buyers. However, the cited $1.83 billion market includes risk applications outside this wedge; it does not establish accessible adjacent demand. Meanwhile, Trimble owns Document Crunch and can distribute overlapping intelligence through existing project-management and ERP relationships. Telamont must earn its place inside a workflow where a competitor already has distribution.

Subscriptions establish an intended route to direct value capture. Private commercial terms disclose neither a price nor collected revenue, and project coverage measured in billions is not subscription income. Customer savings belong to the customer until Telamont demonstrates capture. Separately, its Autodesk listing provides a channel, but the dossier shows no customer making acquisition or utility better for another customer. Against my unstated patterns, this currently resembles enterprise automation more than a permissionless contribution system or a network organizing fragmented supply.

I would pass with my own money today. No documented integrity issue, artificial financing deadline, or founder behavior triggers a personal veto; the concern is structural. Telamont has demonstrated useful execution while its compounding advantage remains unproven. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: a rights-cleared expert-correction library that measurably improves risk detection on previously unseen customers’ documents at a fixed false-positive rate. That would show each deployment creating an asset for the next.
