# Central Blue -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to a spacecraft integrator is the strongest plausible route. An integrator could acquire HELION and CORE to offer customers more usable power during eclipse, when onboard batteries constrain imaging or electric propulsion. That rationale is an inference from the proposed system and use cases. The memo identifies a propulsion test partner but offers no acquisition interest or measured mission economics. (*The Idea; Market & Traction*)

Central Blue reports a nearly complete prototype and a planned December 2026 ground demonstration with Wave Motion. YC backing and prototype progress give the test plan some support. The milestone that would strengthen the exit case is reliable on-orbit power delivery to a customer’s power bus through the dedicated receiver, with a measured mission benefit. The announced ground test covers propulsion, and the budget, funding and schedule for orbital power validation remain unverified. CORE flight validation is also unclear. The memo leaves acquisition feasibility and independent liquidity routes unevidenced. (*Market & Traction; Key Risks; Key Facts*)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The scenarios below assume the holder is eligible to sell its entire stake at closing within eight years, receives 3% of distributable equity pro rata, and faces no senior preferences, escrow or earn-outs. The sale values are illustrative assumptions. The memo provides no price evidence. (*Market & Traction; Key Facts*)

- **Modest sale.** $50 million in enterprise value with no net debt or other deal deductions yields $50 million in distributable equity and $1.5 million to the holder, or 3×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value less $10 million in net debt and deal deductions yields $90 million in distributable equity and $2.7 million to the holder, or 5.4×. At $20 million in deductions, the same sale yields $2.4 million, or 4.8×.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share purchase or distribution yields zero realized proceeds within eight years.

### Countercase

Star Catcher’s standard-panel approach could give integrators a simpler option than adding a dedicated receiver. Its reported orbital tracking demonstration and ten announced power purchase agreements strengthen that countercase, while Central Blue’s announced power demonstration is ground-based. The memo leaves open whether the competing architecture can meet the same mission requirements and whether Central Blue can produce enough incremental value to justify an acquisition. The evidence establishes neither a structural block nor a ceiling on proceeds, so neither Pass gate is triggered. (*Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a funded orbital pilot with a spacecraft integrator show that HELION’s measured mission benefit justifies the cost of a dedicated receiver and supports an acquisition above the proceeds hurdle? Move from Watch to Reach out if pilot results and the integrator’s quantified economics support a plausible sale yielding at least $83.3 million in distributable equity under the benchmark terms.
