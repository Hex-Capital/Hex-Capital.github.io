# Shire Intelligence -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path B, New economies, is Shire’s strongest path. Shire uses camera-derived table events to alert staff and guide seating by live server workload. The seed-stage hypothesis is that automation makes continuous, table-level monitoring affordable for full-service restaurants that currently rely on intermittent floor scans. The January prototype used reusable vision models, and its report identified inconsistent classifications. Shire claims 12% faster table turns, but the memo gives no comparison baseline or measurement window. The labor test is manager and staff minutes per correctly handled table event at comparable service quality, including alert review, missed events, and manual fallback. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Shire reports 13 live restaurants at launch. That is an early adoption signal, with payment, repeat use, and prior inability to afford continuous monitoring unverified. The Mathews testimonial concerns payment-processing savings, leaving willingness to pay for floor intelligence open. The founders’ front-of-house, restaurant management, and machine-learning experience supports their ability to run a field trial across different layouts and shifts. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

The expansion unit is one restaurant location. Existing cameras could reduce equipment costs where coverage is adequate. A per-site contribution bridge starts with realized recurring vision revenue and deducts inference, onboarding, integration, support, and footage-governance costs. The memo provides quote-based plans and leaves realized prices, per-site costs, and the number of reachable locations unquantified. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Shire may improve an existing host workflow while leaving manual checks in place. If comparable vision becomes cheap, camera-based competitors already cover parts of the detection workflow, and Toast can satisfy part of the seating workflow through the existing POS. Shire’s terms give it limited rights to process restaurant data for platform operation and leave cross-customer training rights unestablished. The memo offers no buyer preference under functional parity or measured network effect. These facts weigh against durable capture, though functional substitution remains untested. Path B therefore stays open but falls short of the outreach gate. Path A has no separately evidenced bottleneck that Shire controls. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid field trial show that restaurants previously unable to staff continuous floor monitoring save net labor with Shire at comparable service quality and renew after trying a functionally equivalent alternative? Compare matched shifts against manual monitoring, log all review and fallback work, and observe renewal at a price that covers per-site delivery costs. A positive result would support Reach out if the updated score clears the outreach threshold with at least medium confidence. If the trial shows that exception handling erases the savings or that buyers readily switch under parity, the action changes to Pass.
