# Locke -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Locke has found demand, not yet a Black Swan. More than $500K in annual revenue run-rate after six weeks is exceptional pre-seed commercial evidence. It proves that defense, healthcare, and GovTech companies will pay for help navigating government. It does not prove that government affairs becomes radically cheaper, more accurate, or more accessible. The dossier quantifies a $1.10B adjacent advocacy-software market, but not the larger lobbying, compliance, or procurement cost pool Locke could displace—or the societal consequence of doing so.

The technical claim is weaker than the revenue claim. AI agents analyze legislation, monitor signals, and draft briefs and proposals, but Locke reports no accuracy, throughput, cost, or win-rate benchmark. Quorum already serves more than 2,000 organizations with policy data, AI intelligence, stakeholder management, and professional services. Against that named incumbent, “we execute the whole process” describes service scope, not a 5–10x discontinuity. Without evidence that usage creates a proprietary learning loop, the agents remain replicable labor augmentation.

The strongest bull case is that I am underrating execution. Parth Badhwar has worked inside the White House, Deloitte policy, a campaign, and startup GTM. He has converted that network and domain fluency into revenue faster than most pre-seed founders. An AI-enabled service can be an excellent wedge: deliver the outcome manually, capture the workflow, automate its repeatable parts, and eventually own the system of record. Locke’s expansion across Congress, agencies, procurement, and proposals could produce a larger platform than the $1.10B software estimate suggests.

But the current operating choices cut against that platform thesis. Locke is hiring policy staff at $72K–$200K while service throughput is already an identified constraint. That risks scaling expert labor before testing the largest kill question: can the agents perform regulated, citation-sensitive work accurately enough to make each additional client require materially fewer human hours? No published experiment specifies a task set, error threshold, human baseline, cost, or stop condition. Money appears to be buying capacity while the decisive automation risk remains unmeasured.

The economics therefore look like a better government-affairs firm, not yet a cost-collapse company. No pricing, gross margin, utilization, delivery-hours, or cost-to-serve data shows a path to globally accessible, unsubsidized parity with conventional lobbying and proposal work. My 36-month base case is $5M–$10M ARR with substantial policy headcount: a successful specialist firm, but not a defining technical platform. The one verifiable condition that flips my decision is a mature customer cohort demonstrating at least $1M of ARR per delivery employee at over 80% gross margin, with human hours per client down at least 80% from onboarding.

I would not invest my own money today. The downside is one check, but the upside currently scales with lobbyists rather than software. Strong early revenue can finance the experiment; it cannot substitute for it. Show that Locke removes expert labor from the marginal unit of delivery while maintaining regulatory accuracy, and the option changes character.
