# Evident Machines -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest supported route is a strategic acquisition by a frontier AI lab. Evident Machines reports that models passed all 10 benchmark tasks in simulation but averaged a 29.3% pass rate on physical hardware. That gap gives a lab a reason to test whether instrument readings, expert workflows, and physical failure data improve model performance. A reason to acquire the company could emerge if owning that workflow delivers a durable advantage over licensing the data or building an equivalent capability. Training gains and data exclusivity remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The benchmark launch shows the two-person team can run a physical evaluation. The next milestone is a lab-funded pilot that demonstrates measurable improvement on unseen hardware and tracks the cost of producing each usable task. Manual wiring and probe placement make throughput a material capital question. The memo records YC backing and a $125,000 funding entry, with total funding and pilot costs still unverified. Contributor agreements and rights to transfer the resulting data and environments also remain open. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, dilution from 5% to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale cases below assume $5 million in net debt and deal adjustments, no senior preference payouts, and an eligible holder selling the entire stake for cash within eight years. The prices and terms are illustrative.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M leaves $35M in distributable equity | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $5M leaves $95M in distributable equity | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private | The company reinvests cash and the holder sells no shares | $0 |

At 2.5% ownership, even the strong sale yields $2.375 million, below the objective. The memo supplies no acquisition pricing evidence or supported independent liquidity route, so proceeds and alternatives retain their absent-evidence defaults. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Anthropic’s collaboration with Diode shows that a frontier lab can build overlapping electronics evaluations with a hardware partner. A lab could use the same approach to obtain enough physical-hardware data for its needs, especially if Evident Machines’ manual bench work proves expensive to scale. The collaboration establishes a credible alternative for some evaluation work. Whether it can replace Evident Machines’ physical experiment workflow, and what that would mean for acquisition pricing, remain open. The evidence supports Watch under the decision gates. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid frontier-lab pilot show repeatable performance gains on unseen hardware at a cost that gives the lab an economic reason to own the workflow? Move to Reach out if the pilot results and the lab’s cost of building an equivalent capability support a plausible acquisition range that clears the $83.3 million distributable equity hurdle. Stay at Watch if the pilot establishes technical value while leaving the case for ownership unresolved.
