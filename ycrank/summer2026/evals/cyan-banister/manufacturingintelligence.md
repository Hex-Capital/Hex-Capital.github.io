# HERA -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

More than 200 customer-discovery conversations make HERA a factory-adjacent founder story trapped inside an increasingly consensus AI category. Noelle So built a water-treatment facility adding 40 L/s to her family’s industrial park; Meera Patel worked on manufacturing projects at Pfizer and TE Connectivity. HERA’s highest signal is that the founders encountered industrial systems before inventing the pitch. HERA’s lowest signal is magical weirdness: four competitors already automate overlapping drawing checks. This is not three uninvestable things stapled together. It is a useful, unsexy problem with unusually authentic young founders—and a market already discovering the same answer.

**Crusoe** is the high rung because Chase Lochmiller possessed the social license to walk onto oil fields before proposing an energy company. HERA shares that mechanism: Noelle So’s family industrial park and Meera Patel’s factory work give HERA vocabulary that cannot be learned from a TAM slide, while meeting on a bus to a factory tour is exactly the environmental signal I notice. HERA diverges because neither founder is documented owning drawing release, scrapping a $40,000 part, or shipping an adjacent product at scale. They have touched factories; the dossier does not prove they have carried the scar tissue of a failed tolerance stack. That places HERA below Crusoe, but well above founders who discovered manufacturing from a market map.

**The consensus YC AI-agent darling** fails my magical-weird test when the model is the moat, competitors are everywhere, and the founders are replaceable. HERA shows part of that pattern: F4, Axial, NexCAD, and RapidDraft already cover GD&T, standards, tolerance, or company-rule review, while 2026 vision models reportedly extract drawing details at 85%–95% accuracy. HERA has no disclosed pricing or evidence that its round is underpriced. The saving distinction is aesthetic: source-traced ASME compliance for pressure vessels is uglier and less fashionable than another inbox agent. HERA is non-glamorous, but HERA is not yet non-consensus. “Unsexy” earns attention; crowded prevents magic.

Meera Patel is identified as Founder while Noelle So is CTO, giving HERA more role clarity than a shared-CEO arrangement. Over the next 18 months, however, HERA must turn titles into authority while accuracy disputes, enterprise sales, and product priorities collide. “Best friends” is warmth, not a tiebreaker. HERA compounds if Meera owns customer and capital decisions while Noelle owns technical execution under a written conflict protocol; HERA fractures if friendship substitutes for governance. The unstated pattern cuts both ways: the founders’ factory-tour origin feels organic, but the red flag from **HQ Trivia** and **GameCrush** remains because the dossier discloses no equity split, vesting arrangement, or final decision authority.

ASME Y14.5-2018, BPVC, B31, AWS, ISO, aerospace, medical devices, and pressure vessels put HERA near regulated physical industry without making HERA a frontier-hardware company. For this score to flip into the top tier, HERA would need to become part of the factory’s trusted release infrastructure: on-premises deployment, validated performance by drawing class, auditable standard updates, and customer-specific rules that survive personnel turnover. Prints supposedly never leaving customer walls points in that direction, but no security certification or accuracy benchmark is public. Today HERA is software applied to hard artifacts. Tomorrow it could become an accumulated engineering-judgment system that competitors cannot casually reproduce; that tomorrow is still inferred.

**Flock Safety** was observable in the physical environment before its market narrative hardened. HERA offers a narrower version of that test: an engineer uploads a real drawing, HERA returns source-traced contradictions in minutes, and the resulting decision determines whether a potentially $40,000 part reaches the shop floor. HERA is not a consumer artifact I can ride like Uber or play like Niantic, but a customer can test HERA through the free 20-drawing review within one week. The evidence chain matters more than the AI label. A flagged datum can be inspected; a prevented scrap can be counted. HERA passes physical-world verifiability because the software’s truth eventually becomes metal.

The $40,000 scrap risk makes my 36-month forecast specific: HERA reaches $2 million–$5 million ARR by narrowing around aerospace and pressure-vessel drawing compliance, but HERA does not become the horizontal default for CAD review. Founder proximity produces the narrow adoption; weak magical weirdness prevents easy category ownership. HERA’s system of record compounds only after accepted findings and company rules accumulate inside a regulated workflow, so the path is founder visits, 20-drawing trials, one painful plant at a time. I’m never playing the game I seem to be playing: the bet is not generalized drawing vision. The bet is whether HERA becomes trusted institutional memory for a few factories where one missed line costs more than the annual contract.

NexCAD already claims more than 1,000 engineers and 2,500 reviewed drawings, while HERA reports no revenue, corpus size, precision, or recall. The bear mechanism stings: HERA could be two earnest industrial engineers wrapping increasingly available vision models while better-integrated competitors capture the correction data. HERA’s best rebuttal is 200 conversations, reported use across three regulated categories, source-traced findings, and deployment within one week. The Known Exceptions do not rescue weak execution: HERA has none of **HQ Trivia’s** explosive virality, no **HandUp**-level biographical mission override, and no demonstrated **DeepMind** polymath compounding. I would write a small check, but I flip to Pass if HERA cannot show five paying factories with repeat usage across at least 100 drawings each within twelve months.
