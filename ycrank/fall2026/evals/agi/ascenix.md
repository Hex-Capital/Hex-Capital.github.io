# Ascenix -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Cheaper text extraction could make routine fall review viable for senior-care homes that currently review falls less often because staff must reconstruct incidents from EHR notes and spreadsheets. That buyer segment and review cadence are scenario assumptions. Responsive Health Management reported 80% less time on manual fall analysis and 15% fewer non-compliance incidents over three months. The testimonial supports a labor-saving mechanism in an existing workflow. The memo leaves total labor per completed review and adoption by previously excluded homes unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Responsive reported deploying Ascenix across all its homes, a meaningful rollout at one operator. Ascenix separately reports 55 live facilities across Canada and the U.S. Payment, renewals, and demand from newly viable buyers remain unverified. The founders lived in a retirement residence and built their first workflow around a director of care’s falls spreadsheet, showing they could turn firsthand operating feedback into software. The case is plausible at seed. Ascenix is based in Toronto and reports deployments in the U.S. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team)

The unit of expansion is one facility. A home would need enough staff time savings to cover the subscription and local setup while maintaining review quality. Ascenix would need monthly fees to cover integration, configuration, and ongoing support. Reusable components could lower the cost of adding the next site. Local policy validation, scanned charts the software cannot read, and notes flagged for follow-up could reduce those savings. The memo gives no per-site cost or margin figures. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The strongest countercase is that Ascenix improves an existing workflow whose economics ultimately accrue to the EHR vendor. PointClickCare already offers native incident management and controls Ascenix’s access to its live notes and incident records. Guardoc already offers automated chart checks. Ascenix reports integrations across several EHRs, and its facility-specific rules could give multi-site operators a reason to stay under model parity. The memo contains no buyer comparison showing they would pay for that advantage when comparable automation is available through their EHR. It also identifies no scarce capability Ascenix controls that would independently support Path A. These facts leave both Path B conditions open and keep the outreach gate closed. A comparative trial remains feasible, which supports Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid trial show that senior-care homes previously priced out of routine fall review adopt it and then renew Ascenix when offered an EHR-native alternative with comparable model capability? Measure staff time per completed review at equivalent quality, including setup, verification, scanned records, and exceptions. Repeat payment at a price that covers delivery would support moving to Reach out if the updated score clears the outreach threshold. A credible trial showing that labor savings fail to open the segment or that buyers switch at parity would move the case to Pass.
