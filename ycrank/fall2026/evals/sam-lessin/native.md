# Native -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Native reports $2M ARR within six months. That gets my attention. It does not tell me what my check buys. The dossier supplies neither proposed post-money valuation nor competing offers, and it names no experiment the round will fund. I will not infer an auction from YC admission. But I also cannot underwrite scarce capital at an unknown price for an unspecified purpose. Those two gates remain at their absent-evidence defaults, despite a substantially more developed business than the usual pre-seed company.

The founders are the strongest part of this underwriting. Bekken has produced content for OBOS, NTE, and BERRE; Hauga built a marketing company; Gråsberg combines applied ML experience with having shipped Wepost alongside this team. Wepost reportedly reached NOK80,000 MRR in September 2025 before they wound it down. That is operating evidence on both sides of the bridge: producing marketing and building software to deliver it. Native fits my preference for people who can translate between ecosystems. The prior revenue belongs to that assessment of execution, however; it is not Native’s revenue or evidence of its profitability.

There is also a purchased workflow here. Native reports 2,200-plus paying customers and more than 40,000 posts published in one month. Customers are doing more than joining a waitlist: they are paying to hand over part of their marketing operation. That earns meaningful credit. The unresolved question is who owns the advantage. Norn learns from approvals, edits, rejections, and performance, but the dossier does not establish that those records caused purchases or that customers would lose material value by switching. Training opt-outs further limit the presumed data advantage. Native owns customer relationships; publication still depends on Ayrshare and the social platforms. A useful workflow can exist without a proprietary dark pool.

The strongest objection to passing is straightforward: perhaps Native has already built the functioning base business that makes AI upside cheap optionality. Its reported ARR and customer count are roughly consistent with the $79 monthly Pro price. The team has actual marketing experience, and choosing ideas before generating assets addresses a customer decision that cheaper generation alone does not solve. That is a serious bull case. But the AI exceptions I would make require evidence of conversion, retention, or customer economics strong enough to override the missing gates. The Briggs on Homes engagement comparison—5.5% versus 2.9%—does not establish incremental sales, and Native’s observational research acknowledges selection and survivorship biases. Blaze already offers a comparable strategy-to-publishing loop. Neither superior prediction nor durable economic differentiation is established yet.

Delete the next financing from the model. Reported subscription revenue gives Native a plausible starting point, but without gross margin, churn, acquisition cost, or burn, I cannot tell whether $79 buys profitable automation or expensive recurring work. I would not accuse the founders of building a financing factory; the capital plan is simply missing. I pass on the investment presented here, while taking the operating business seriously. My verifiable flip condition is a documented sub-$10M post-money round funding one budgeted, dated experiment whose pass threshold demonstrates that retained customer contribution can cover operating burn without another raise. That would connect price, purpose, and cash in a way the current dossier does not.
