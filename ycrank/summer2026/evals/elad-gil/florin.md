# Florin -- Elad Gil Evaluation

**Shaurya Aggarwal deployed encryption across 100,000-plus hosts**, while Amol Pant built autonomy systems and Aydin Sorensen operated infrastructure at AWS, Twitch, and a 50-plus-vehicle fleet. Florin is therefore an infrastructure-shipping profile without a visible revenue curve: founder execution is Florin’s highest dimension, and disclosed usage velocity is its lowest. That pairing usually produces technically ambitious systems searching for a market, not something “just working.”

**Perplexity (2022)** is the high rung for iterative shipping: Aravind Srinivas repeatedly turned an investor conversation into a working product within a week. Florin shares the capacity to build difficult systems—TPUs, encryption, Autopilot compute, rover prototypes—but Florin diverges because every named artifact predates the startup and came from a large institutional environment. Florin has disclosed no customer-feedback loop, independently shipped fintech product, or iteration between conversations. The engineering credibility is real; the Perplexity mechanism is not yet demonstrated.

**Lyft at Series C** is the low rung for curve quality: adoption existed, but the curve did not establish the dominant economics required in a winner-take-most market. Florin sits closest to that low rung because Florin discloses no revenue, transaction volume, active accounts, repeat usage, or named customer—not even a weak curve whose durability can be debated. At pre-seed, silence is not fatal by itself, but Florin is entering a category where Mercury already reports 300,000 customers and $650 million in annualized revenue. “No public data found” means no evidence—applying the absent-evidence default.

**The OCC’s March 2025 clarification** permits national banks to use distributed ledgers and stablecoins for certain payment activities, but Florin has not said it uses either. Florin therefore has no company-specific catalyst—applying the absent-evidence default—only an inferred regulatory development near its market. The score would flip a tier if Florin showed that this clarification enables a previously impossible settlement path, with a regulated bank connection and a measured reduction from legacy settlement times. Without that causal chain, Florin is skating in a mature corporate-banking market, not skating for a newly opened puck.

**Mercury’s 300,000 customers and $650 million annualized revenue** make distribution the next 18-month problem. Florin’s single ledger could compound into richer treasury data, faster underwriting, and switching costs as accounts, cards, and payments accumulate in one system. Florin must first persuade startups to move regulated financial workflows away from Mercury, Brex, Rho, and Arc; until then, the ledger compounds engineering cost rather than distribution. Florin is trying to win share against category leaders, not yet becoming an index on startup formation.

**Color Genomics (2015)** worked because commodity sequencing was the hard layer and the company owned workflow and data above it. Florin could fit that unstated pattern if regulated banking rails are the commodity layer and Florin’s ledger is the workflow infrastructure. Florin’s dossier instead leaves custody, payment-network access, licenses, settlement partners, fraud controls, and customer acquisition unresolved. That is not one miracle; it is simultaneous regulatory execution, operational reliability, incumbent displacement, and trust formation. The Color exception does not apply yet because Florin has neither demonstrated domain expertise nor isolated the hard layer beneath the product.

**Zero disclosed revenue and an invite-only membership** lead to a falsifiable 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Florin will have pivoted from a full-stack financial operating system into ledger or treasury infrastructure sold to other fintechs, with less than $5 million ARR, rather than becoming a scaled Mercury competitor. Florin’s systems talent makes an infrastructure product plausible, while Florin’s missing regulatory pathway, distribution wedge, and buying evidence make the integrated neobank strategy unlikely to survive contact with the market.

**Google encryption across 100,000 hosts and AWS-scale operating experience** form the strongest opposing case: Florin’s builders may be early Stripe-like founders whose technical architecture is legible before the revenue curve, and the owned ledger could turn Florin into an index on every financial operation performed by venture-backed startups. That bull mechanism stings because infrastructure quality can create the product discontinuity incumbents cannot retrofit. Florin’s current facts still lose the argument: no named customer, no fintech shipping history, no regulated partner, and no verified performance advantage against Mercury’s integrated stack. I would flip to Invest upon one verifiable result—a cohort of named customers producing more than 20% month-over-month transaction-revenue growth for three consecutive months. Until then, I pass with my own money.
