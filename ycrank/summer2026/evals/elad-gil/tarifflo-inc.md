# Tarifflo Inc. -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: tariff volatility increases compliance errors, U.S. importers now buy automated recovery rather than additional consultant hours, and Tarifflo becomes large if free audits consistently convert recoverable duties into high-margin contingency revenue. The governing belief is not that customs compliance is a $23.78 billion market. It is that enough entries contain economically recoverable errors—and that Tarifflo can identify, file, and collect on them cheaply enough—to make the free product an efficient acquisition channel.

There is meaningful evidence for that belief. Tarifflo reports auditing more than one million products, and Orly recovered $1.3 million after more than 10,000 items were reviewed. Public companies had begun signing onto the predecessor classification product by February 2025. These signals are more informative than a waitlist, but they do not establish Tarifflo’s contingency percentage, realized revenue, repeat audit cadence, or collection time. The decisive metric is therefore cohort economics: dollars collected by Tarifflo per dollar spent auditing and pursuing a customer’s entries.

The technology and institutional shift is credible but incompletely quantified. Consultants charge $250–500 per hour, while changing tariff stacks and the February–April 2026 policy changes increase the value of continuously updated rules. Software that checks entry lines against HTS, Chapter 99, PGA, and CBP requirements can reduce the marginal cost of finding an error. Yet the dossier does not show gross margins, audit costs, or whether licensed-broker review remains proportional to transaction volume. A contingency model with 300-day correction windows can hide a working-capital-heavy services business inside apparently free software.

The product is deeper than a classification wrapper. It ingests entry packets and ACE reports, maintains more than 10 million linked shipment records, drafts corrections or protests, and routes filings through licensed brokers. The ABI filer code and direct CBP connection add permissions and implementation work that a stronger model does not immediately reproduce. Better models should improve classification and rule matching while Tarifflo retains the filing workflow, customer history, and outcome feedback. The weakness is that ABI remained in private beta in August 2026 and partners still filed the Orly petitions, so end-to-end ownership is demonstrated unevenly.

The strongest bear case is that $1.3 million recovered for a customer measures customer value, not Tarifflo’s revenue or software economics. Zonos has CBP selection, KlearNow has five customs connections, and Altana already serves government and enterprise customers. Tarifflo’s purported data advantage has no demonstrated exclusivity or learning effect; a funded competitor may be able to reproduce it with money, broker access, and public shipment data. This activates the red flags around unpriced “proprietary data,” service-heavy margins, and financing scale before unit economics are known. The unstated pattern of entering a category while it crystallizes helps only if Tarifflo’s recovery wedge creates reusable distribution into filing and supplier intelligence. No known-exception override applies: this is neither frontier research nor a physical-market reconstruction that excuses missing economics.

Bryce Judy has unusually direct founder-market fit through KPMG trade work, a CBP broker license, and published HTS benchmarking research, but the dossier documents neither repeat-founder learning nor a costly personal commitment, so I do not award motivation by inference. I would invest at pre-seed, narrowly, because the named recovery result and owned compliance workflow clear the market gates despite unresolved value capture. The verifiable flip condition is cohort data showing that, within nine months, fewer than 20% of completed audits produce paid recoveries or that broker labor and collection costs consume more than half of Tarifflo’s contingency revenue; either result would make this a pass.
