# AnyAlpha -- Paul Graham Evaluation

AnyAlpha’s founders have built together for three years and report reaching $500K ARR with previous apps. That makes me want to meet them. It doesn’t tell me what they did when AnyAlpha got stuck. The dossier contains no specific obstacle, founder-built workaround, and resulting change. I wouldn’t mistake that silence for passivity. But I also wouldn’t turn successful prior apps into an invented story about resourcefulness. That is the missing fact on which my decision turns.

There is something to inspect here. AnyAlpha has shipped mobile software, and Shirley reports that an agent turned $100 into $153 during an October demo. I give the product meaningful technical credit, discounted because the current agent’s execution reliability and testing have not been independently established. One profitable demonstration says little about whether a trading system works repeatedly after fees and failed orders. The more interesting possibility is a programmable interface through which traders can express strategies they previously couldn’t execute. That matches my preference for tools users can bend to their own purposes. AnyAlpha hasn’t yet shown users doing the bending.

The strongest argument for investing is the combination of relevant builders and an existing habit of shipping together. Shirley built trading systems at Coinbase and LSEG. Jerry reports six years developing quantitative data, models, backtests, and execution systems with his own capital. These founders could understand a trader’s awkward workflow well enough to build something outsiders would miss. Their shared history also removes the concern that the team was assembled for an application. But experience trading is not yet a documented account of encountering this particular problem, and venue eligibility is currently an unresolved integration burden rather than evidence that they have mastered a neglected schlep.

Who wants this badly enough to keep using it? The 42 iOS ratings and 100-plus Android downloads span an earlier product, so they cannot answer that question for the agent. The listed $4.99–$79.99 purchases establish prices, not paying customers. Nor does the move from sports analytics to agents establish learning: the dossier gives neither the observation that rejected the old route nor the customer response to the new one. Composer and Capitalise.ai already demonstrate that strategy creation, backtesting, and execution can be packaged together. AnyAlpha needs a narrow group whose particular demands force it beyond that familiar bundle. Five traders repeatedly using an ugly version would tell me more than a polished demonstration.

I would pass at **36/100**. The founders make the bull case substantial, but the exceptions that could overcome my missing founder evidence require unmistakable user pull or unusually strong validated proof. Neither is established here. The old sports-information terms alongside agent execution deserve clarification; they do not establish deliberate regulatory evasion. Likewise, soliciting power traders is sensible, and there is no evidence of dependence on a large partner or a ceremonial launch. My objection is narrower: I cannot yet connect these capable builders to a demonstrated cycle of resistance, adaptation, and returning users. My flip condition is a dated, inspectable case in which a named trader’s execution blocker led the founders to ship a workaround within two weeks, followed by that trader using it weekly for a month.
