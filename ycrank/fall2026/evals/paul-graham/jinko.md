# Jinko -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Jinko’s founders have built travel companies together and shipped another travel product. What I cannot find is the incident that tells me how they behave when something breaks. Browser automation gets blocked; conventional APIs constrain searches. Those are specific obstacles. But the dossier does not show which obstacle these founders encountered, what they built around it, and how quickly it worked. I would not turn that missing episode into a judgment against them. I would not substitute their previous acquisition for it either.

The strongest reason to like Jinko is that these founders have already worked inside the machinery they now propose to hide. Gauthier, Texier, and Zhang built Mowgli together before working at Hopper; their experience spans airline operations, distribution, and booking engineering. Their shared execution also answers my concern about cofounders assembled for an application. More unusually, Jinko assumes the contracting travel-operator role and carries professional liability coverage ([sales terms](https://gojinko.com/terms/)). Booking infrastructure includes what happens after the booking goes wrong. This team has a credible reason to understand that work and accept it.

There is a product to inspect. Jinko documents search, live pricing, payment, booking, and servicing through API, MCP, and CLI interfaces, and Exa independently lists it as a provider ([documentation](https://docs.gojinko.com/introduction), [Exa Connect](https://exa.ai/connect)). That earns technical credit beyond a diagram. It does not establish successful end-to-end bookings or reliability under real servicing failures. Likewise, the consumer-to-infrastructure pivot was documented by August 25, 2026, but the dossier does not identify the customer observation that caused it. Changing direction is visible. Learning from a falsified hypothesis remains unproven.

The strongest objection to passing is that Jinko may have found precisely the ugly work that makes a useful developer primitive. Its advertised coverage spans 800 airlines, 2.6 million hotels, and 28,000 car-rental locations. If developers can hand it the entire transaction and its aftermath, they may care very little which supplier exposes an MCP endpoint. That fits my pattern of backing interfaces on which other people build. But the important part of that pattern is people actually building and returning. An Exa listing establishes availability. It establishes neither repeated use nor dependence. None of my exceptions supplies an override here: the dossier has neither unmistakable user pull nor independently validated technical proof strong enough to replace the missing founder evidence.

The economics make that distinction consequential. Jinko charges 1% of flight-booking value and $0.005 per excess search above a 1,500:1 search-to-book ratio ([pricing](https://gojinko.com/pricing/)). Exploratory agents can generate activity without generating bookings. Meanwhile, Etraveli launched its own flight MCP on October 28, including payment, ticketing, and servicing. Jinko therefore needs evidence that developers choose its complete workflow and keep using it. Targeting banks and platforms is reasonable, but dependence on a large partner would trigger a distribution red flag; the dossier does not establish such dependence or a working alternative acquisition loop.

I would pass on writing a personal check today. The operating experience is substantial, and the product deserves a closer look, but my founder-action gate remains unmet. My flip condition is one documented customer episode: a named agent developer encounters a booking or servicing failure, the founders ship a workaround within two weeks, and that developer then completes paid bookings in four consecutive weeks. That would connect resourcefulness to a user who needs the result.
