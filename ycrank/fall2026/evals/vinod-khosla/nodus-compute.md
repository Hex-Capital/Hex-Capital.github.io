# Nodus Compute -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Nodus Compute could make stranded GPUs useful again. That matters: cheaper completed AI workloads can expand access without manufacturing another chip. But the dossier does not quantify how much capacity is recoverable, whose spending changes, or how utilization improvements become an order-of-magnitude resource multiplier. The adjacent $3.96B orchestration market establishes a commercial opportunity. It does not establish the consequence that makes me willing to lose my entire check.

The strongest evidence is the founder-reported claim of 10 startup pilots saving more than 30%. Matching workloads to capacity and restoring checkpoint files is a concrete mechanism, and documented generation fencing and integrity checks suggest substantive engineering. However, the savings claim comes through a mirrored social-media snippet, without a measurement method or named baseline. Thirty percent could be valuable. It does not demonstrate a 5–10x technical discontinuity against Modal or SF Compute. The decisive question is whether Nodus lowers the cost of a successfully completed job after interruptions, checkpoint overhead, and its own fees—not merely whether it finds a cheaper GPU-hour.

The bull case deserves attention. My portfolio pattern includes execution-heavy infrastructure and marketplaces; scientific invention is not the only route to a consequential company. Block is the relevant exception: a small team can turn access to existing infrastructure into a much broader transaction platform. Nodus could similarly turn fragmented compute into dependable execution. The material difference is that Modal already pools capacity across clouds and SF Compute already supports capacity resale. Nodus must establish a distinct technical advantage at their intersection. Its proposed workload-to-research feedback loop could supply one, but the dossier explicitly describes an agenda rather than demonstrated cross-customer learning. I cannot credit that future capability today. Nor does the dossier establish the extraordinary consequence that justified my pre-product science exceptions.

Kotra and Varshney have relevant infrastructure exposure, including Varshney’s Google cost-optimization work. That makes the problem selection credible. It does not document rapid belief revision or recruiting complementary owners for specific weaknesses, so I retain the absent-evidence score for that dimension. The pilots likewise do not disclose a ranked kill risk, decisive threshold, experiment budget, or next action. Six advertised openings reveal neither the actual spending plan nor whether capital is buying risk reduction. I see no evidence of founder rigidity, exit fixation, or a generic model wrapper; my concern is the unproven discontinuity and learning mechanism.

The published $2.600 H100 SXM supply hour excludes Nodus fees. Without realized selling prices, recovery costs, and contribution margins, it cannot establish durable customer savings with viable supplier economics. I pass at 27/100. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: an independently reproducible comparison showing at least 5x lower all-in cost per successfully completed job than Modal on a predefined, commercially representative workload suite, with equivalent output quality and completion deadlines, while Nodus earns positive contribution margin without promotional subsidies. That would connect technical advantage, consequential affordability, and a business that survives its own pricing.
