# Sutrix -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest fit for this seed-stage case. Sutrix links clinical records across sources, applies study-specific rules, and routes ambiguous records to reviewers with source evidence. A customer reported turning questionnaire records into findings in days instead of weeks, and another reported less manual reconciliation with the steps visible for expert review. Sutrix claims an 80% reduction in preparation time. The investment hypothesis is that a biostatistics core could afford to run smaller studies or refresh cohorts more often. The memo leaves open whether any buyer has paid for a study or refresh it previously would have skipped. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Under cheap model parity, approved protocol rules, field-level provenance, and reviewer decisions could give repeat buyers a reason to stay if switching means rebuilding and revalidating that work. That switching cost is an inference. Sutrix reports paying teams at 10+ institutions and more than $150,000 in revenue before the software release. Those claims support willingness to pay for prepared datasets. Recurring software revenue and buyer preference under parity remain unverified. The founders bring clinical study and production AI experience and previously built a hospital injury-surveillance system together. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks, Founders & Team)

The expansion unit is a completed study or cohort refresh per institution. Scale depends on how many cores can be onboarded, how often each buys, and what remains of the engagement price after data access, inference, reconciliation, retries, and expert verification. Sutrix prices each engagement separately. The reported time savings leave both contribution per accepted dataset and the burden of adding another institution unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Sutrix could be a faster way to prepare datasets for studies that already have budgets. Production EHR access may depend on institution-approved endpoints or exports, and investigators still need to verify record linkage and protocol interpretation. Those steps could consume enough labor to keep smaller studies uneconomic. Competing tools already automate parts of chart abstraction, and a buyer with equivalent models and approved data access may be able to reproduce the rest of the workflow. Under Path A, the memo identifies no Sutrix-controlled bottleneck that well-funded entrants would have to obtain from it. The evidence leaves both Path B gates open: it establishes neither a newly viable paid transaction nor end-to-end substitution. A paid comparison at a biostatistics core can test both, supporting Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent biostatistics cores repeatedly pay Sutrix, at a price that covers delivery costs, for studies or cohort refreshes they previously skipped when an equivalent-model workflow is available? A paid trial would compare accepted datasets at the same quality, count all integration, review, and rework, and require a repeat purchase because the core values retaining its approved rules and audit history. A positive result would support Reach out if the updated score clears the outreach threshold. If the trial shows that full delivery labor keeps those studies uneconomic or that buyers switch readily to the equivalent workflow, move to Pass.
