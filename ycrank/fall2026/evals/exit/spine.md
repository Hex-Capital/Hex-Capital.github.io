# Spine -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Spine’s strongest exit route is a strategic sale to a benefits broker seeking a recurring commission book and a channel into early-stage employers. Spine says it becomes the broker of record and uses carrier commissions to fund specialist support across benefits, payroll, and compliance. The founders report 30+ startup customers and about 15% average healthcare cost savings. Those savings could help retain customers at renewal. A buyer’s economic case depends on the commissions it can retain after paying for specialist delivery. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

The memo records a $3 million seed round and an investor-reported 4.4× increase in commission revenue over six months through July 2026. That gives Spine a plausible opportunity to measure retention and contribution margin over a full renewal cycle. The absolute commission base, service costs, runway, and any additional capital needed to reach that milestone remain unknown. Closing diligence would also need to establish whether broker appointments, carrier arrangements, and access to customer systems could carry through a sale. The memo supports no independent liquidity route for this holder. (*Market & Traction*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

**Proceeds bridge.** With holder terms unavailable, the benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The acquisition scenarios assume the holder can sell its entire stake for cash within eight years and receives 3% of residual equity after the stated deductions, with no further preference or escrow reductions.

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Cash received by holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40 million less $5 million in assumed debt, senior claims, and deal adjustments leaves $35 million | $1.05 million (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100 million less $10 million in assumed deductions leaves $90 million | $2.70 million (5.4×). If only 80% of the stake is sold for cash, proceeds fall to $2.16 million (4.32×). |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested, with no distribution or eligible share purchase within eight years | $0 realized |

The prices and deductions are illustrative assumptions. Without an absolute commission base, renewal economics, or comparable sale prices, the memo cannot establish that a buyer would pay enough to clear the hurdle. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

An incumbent could serve these employers with capabilities it already has. Rippling offers HR and compliance support without co-employment, and Bennie already combines brokerage with benefits administration technology. Spine’s customer savings could drive adoption while specialist labor absorbs much of the commission income. A buyer could then value only the commission book it expects to retain, leaving the holder short of the target. This is a credible downside. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported price ceiling that would trigger Pass. (*Defensibility*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

What would a credible benefits broker pay for Spine’s commission book after seeing a full renewal cohort’s retained commissions and specialist delivery costs? A buyer-backed valuation range that could clear $83.3 million in distributable equity under plausible deal terms would move the action to Reach out. If that evidence puts the strongest credible sale materially below the hurdle, the action moves to Pass.
