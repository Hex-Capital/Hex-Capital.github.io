# Hopper -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Hopper has built something, but I cannot yet price what I would own. The dossier gives me no post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that my check supplies otherwise unavailable capital. YC and the named technology backers establish backing; they establish neither an auction nor a bargain. The second missing piece matters just as much: what exact truth does this round buy? Serving faster speech models is a product objective. There is no disclosed capital plan connecting one experiment, a deadline, and a measurable result to a dramatic change in business value.

The founders are the strongest reason to keep looking. Pavan Katta brings Salient post-training experience; Jashwanth Pedapudi brings Meta inference-kernel and Uber streaming-infrastructure experience. Their published connection-reuse client gives me an actual shipped artifact alongside the job titles. This is a plausible bridge between model optimization and the operational mess of live voice calls—the kind of translation layer I like. But the dossier does not document a customer deployment demonstrating that these two sides work together economically. I give the team credit for relevant engineering and shipping, without turning their résumés into customer access.

The best bull case is that Hopper becomes the place where a customer's production calls turn into a better, embedded operating system. Customer-built evaluations and post-training could make the relationship more valuable with every deployment. That would survive a new open-model release better than today's advertised 80ms time to first token. The [Dograh integration merged October 1](https://github.com/dograh-hq/dograh/pull/831) also puts the product inside an existing developer workflow. But selectable infrastructure is not purchased infrastructure. No paid usage, retention, or conversion is disclosed, and [training requires separate written consent](https://withhopper.com/privacy). Hopper has a possible route to proprietary access; it has not demonstrated ownership of that access or the purchase it causes. The follower counts do not close that gap.

The uncomfortable counterfactual is that better open models increase demand while leaving Hopper with little economic leverage. Fireworks already combines specialization and inference, and an OpenAI-compatible interface makes adoption easier while also easing substitution. Hopper's connection optimization addresses a real problem, but the disclosure establishes no exclusive rights. At the advertised $0.30 per million input tokens, I still need to know what remains after compute, idle capacity, tuning, and support. Prepaid credits help collection timing; they do not establish revenue or positive margins. There is no evidence of explicitly broken economics, so I will not invent it. There is equally no demonstrated route to funding operations without another round.

I pass at **30/100**. An exception to my usual AI skepticism would require hard operating evidence: measured conversion, retained purchases, cash economics, or unusually strong external validation. Hopper's technical artifacts and relevant founders make that exception conceivable, but do not establish it. This is a capable team whose strongest evidence concerns building the product, while the weakest concerns owning demand. I would reopen diligence on one verifiable event: a paid production renewal granting tuning rights and demonstrating positive contribution margin while meeting the customer's latency requirement under peak load. That would begin to show an owned operating relationship worth underwriting.
