# Ryhla -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Ryhla’s strongest path is to sell early feeder screening to developers with portfolios of 1–50 MW projects. Assuming strong product-market fit, developers could use the same workflow to screen each new set of sites before paying utility application fees. Ryhla lists live feeder models across 11 utility territories in eight states. It reports a live Midwest solar deployment and about $160,000 in avoided fees after screening out eight of one customer’s ten planned battery projects. That outcome illustrates the value of screening several sites for one buyer. Payment and repeat purchasing remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The website’s project submission form and YC referral request give developers a way to find Ryhla and submit a project. The memo documents no independent path from submission through payment to a screening result. For a hypothetical per-project pricing model, the contribution bridge would start with the screening fee and deduct the cost of processing, reviewing, and supporting another screen, plus any feeder data and validation work required to extend coverage. The memo gives neither pricing nor enough information to estimate that work or establish a commercial growth rate. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Ryhla may have to source feeder data and recalibrate its models for each new utility territory. Filing management adds exposure to local utility procedures and approvals. Even within existing coverage, the company-reported 91% approval rate applies only to sites Ryhla flagged as viable, with the sample size and outcomes for rejected sites undisclosed. These constraints put global demand in the adverse band. The structural gate stays open because the listed domestic footprint makes reuse of the screening workflow plausible, and the memo leaves unresolved how much bespoke engineering each new territory requires. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can developers in a new foreign utility territory independently buy and repeat portfolio screening using Ryhla’s existing workflow, with bounded data and validation work and positive incremental contribution? A paid rollout documenting setup hours, buyer steps, and delivery costs would answer it. Move to Reach out if that evidence brings the score to at least 65 with Medium or High confidence. Move to Pass if the rollout shows that broad coverage requires substantial bespoke rebuilding with no credible near-term way to reduce it.
