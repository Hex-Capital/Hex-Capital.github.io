# Simulithic -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. Simulithic’s product documentation describes a visitor-behavior SDK, workspace journey maps, and per-device comparisons of pull-request previews against baselines. An inferred buyer is a testing platform such as BrowserStack, which could use those assets to turn observed customer journeys into regression tests on its device cloud. Ownership could be worth paying for if integrating the workflow delivers measurably better coverage or lower verification costs than building or licensing it. The memo offers no measured buyer benefit or acquisition price evidence. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is a funded production pilot that benchmarks visitor-derived tests against the buyer’s existing tools. The founders report several live deployments and more than 2× weekly growth on an unspecified metric. The launched product and reported $500,000 in funding make a pilot plausible. Its cost, runway, and paid adoption remain unverified. The documented privacy controls show how visitor capture is managed. Rights to transfer journey histories and any required customer consents remain open. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

**Illustrative proceeds.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× target is $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years. A pro rata payout requires $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. Using $5 million of illustrative net debt, the 3% case requires about $88.3 million in enterprise value. A $25 million sale in year eight yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million gross (5.7×), before investor taxes. Fees and senior claims could reduce those amounts. The memo provides no basis for choosing either sale price. (Key Facts, Market & Traction)

Sponsor buyout and company-funded payouts lack cash-flow evidence. A secondary lacks evidence of demand for eligible shares, and an IPO lacks evidence of operating scale. If deployments plateau, strategic buyers stick with their own tools, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. A wind-down would pay only the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

BrowserStack already combines a device cloud with specialized testing agents, and TestSprite already discovers and tests application flows. Those documented capabilities give buyers credible build and partner options. If visitor-derived journeys add little coverage at a comparable run cost, an incumbent has little reason to acquire Simulithic. The memo establishes competitive overlap while leaving the value of Simulithic’s distinct workflow unresolved. That supports Watch and leaves the Pass gate untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a funded, head-to-head production pilot show that Simulithic catches enough additional consequential regressions per testing dollar to support a strategic acquisition near the modeled proceeds hurdle? Move to Reach out if measured pilot results and a buyer-side estimate of the value of owning the capability support a price range that could clear roughly $88.3 million in enterprise value under the illustrative 3% ownership and debt assumptions.
