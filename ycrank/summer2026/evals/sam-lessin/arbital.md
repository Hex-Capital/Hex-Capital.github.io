# Arbital -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I start with three blanks: proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely Arbital’s first scarce check. Financing silence does not prove an auction, but it earns exactly 6/28 because price is part of the thesis, not paperwork to inspect after deciding I like the product. The same problem appears in the capital plan. The dossier names no finite experiment, deadline, or pass/fail metric for the round; “perps, memes, and stocks” describes expanding surface area, not the one truth this check buys. That is 6/24.

The strongest evidence is operational. In seven months, 2,600 traders generated $1.3 billion of cumulative volume, with $377 million in current monthly volume. Those are real customer actions inside an embedded workflow, not followers masquerading as demand. But volume is not yet purchase evidence: Arbital discloses no pricing, fee revenue, repeat cohorts, or retention, and the dossier cannot separate activity in the existing market-making engine from the forthcoming social, mobile-first terminal. I give the workflow 14/20—well above default, but short of high because incentive-driven transactions can disappear when exchange points and rebates do.

The founders are unusually credible on the machinery. Karon Pangestu helped grow Aevo from roughly $1 billion to $100 billion in cumulative volume; Steven Mulyawan onboarded more than 10,000 traders through KOL, agency, and high-net-worth relationships; Vincent Owen Toniwan built backend and market-making infrastructure operating below two milliseconds at 100,000 events per second. That is a genuine translation layer between traders, distribution partners, and exchange execution, earning 9/10. The uncomfortable bull case is that this team has already done the hard thing and the undisclosed economics are excellent. If Arbital monetizes even a thin slice of $377 million monthly volume, today’s missing revenue line could be misleadingly conservative.

I cannot underwrite that possibility as fact. No public revenue, take rate, gross margin, burn, runway, or contribution economics establishes a profitable path without another round, so economics receives the specified 7/18 default. Delete exchange incentives from the model and the causal chain becomes unclear: do traders still route through Arbital, does volume persist, and does maker-only execution produce positive net revenue after losses and adverse inventory? Venue fee schedules, liquidity, and third-party integrations remain externally controlled inputs. Axiom has already converted overlapping terminal functionality into more than $100 million of cumulative revenue, while Hyperliquid owns the underlying venue and reportedly generated $844 million in 2025. Arbital currently owns neither demonstrated economics nor structural control.

The unstated-pattern test is mixed. The founders possess access on both sides of the system, but the purported dark pool—cross-venue data and a trader social graph—is still inferred. Multi-venue integrations are reproducible, and the public distribution case rests partly on exchange programs and approximately 5,000 X followers. The red flags also compound: three founders without disclosed final decision authority, a transition between materially different products, and an expansion into perps, memecoins, and tokenized equities without disclosed regulatory scope. The known-exception bar is not met; large trading volume is impressive, but it is not the hard cash conversion, retained enterprise demand, or proprietary access that would override missing price and capital-purpose evidence.

I am passing at this stage. My base case is that within 36 months Arbital retreats from the broad consumer “everything terminal” and becomes a narrower B2B execution or market-making product, because the team’s strongest receipts are infrastructure while its weakest evidence concerns owned distribution and cash economics. One verifiable flip condition: show three consecutive months above $100,000 in net fee revenue from retained cohorts after exchange rebates and points are excluded. That would demonstrate that the $377 million monthly volume belongs economically to Arbital rather than temporarily passing through it.
