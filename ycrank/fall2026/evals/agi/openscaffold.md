# OpenScaffold -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest path. OpenScaffold says it turns scattered customer communications into source-cited requirements, briefs coding agents, and checks their changes against customer rules. The scenario worth testing is whether this makes small customer integrations viable for implementation teams that previously declined them because manual scoping and review cost too much. The memo identifies the workflow and buyer, while leaving both the labor savings and the newly viable segment unverified. (The Idea)

Faraday’s CEO publicly identified himself as a customer, and OpenScaffold’s founder reported testing the product on an unnamed partner’s calls. These are early demand signals. The public plan is free, and the memo establishes neither payment nor repeat use on previously uneconomic deployments. Both founders have relevant deployment experience, and the public launch soon after the Sigma pivot supports a seed-stage execution case. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team)

The scale unit is the next completed deployment. At equivalent quality, paid revenue per deployment would need to cover model usage, incremental setup, and human work for review, retries, and exceptions. The public pricing provides no paid price for that bridge, and the memo provides no measured costs. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

OpenScaffold may speed up deployments teams already serve while leaving enough manual work to keep smaller jobs uneconomic. The founder reported that the product failed to distinguish customer requests from deployment-team requests 82% of the time in one pilot test. Source citations and review give people a way to catch those errors, and the time that takes could erase the savings. With equally capable models, an incumbent could add similar briefings through open MCP integrations. Approved playbooks might give teams a reason to stay, but reuse and retention are unverified. The memo identifies no scarce input OpenScaffold controls that would independently support Path A. Both Path B conditions remain open, so the evidence supports Watch. (Key Risks, Defensibility, The Idea)

### Decisive diligence question

Can independent implementation teams repeatedly choose and pay for OpenScaffold to complete small deployments they previously declined, when an alternative has comparable functionality, models, and source access? A matched trial should verify equivalent delivery quality, measure all human work against the manual workflow, and observe repeat purchases at a price that covers incremental delivery costs. A positive result on both labor savings and paid preference would support Reach out if the updated score clears 65. A credible trial showing that review erases the savings or that buyers readily switch under parity would move the action to Pass.
