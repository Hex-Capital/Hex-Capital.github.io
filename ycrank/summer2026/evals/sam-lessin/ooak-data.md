# Ooak Data -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers are missing: proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. That matters more here because Ooak Data already advertises seven-figure contracts with the two largest AI labs. This could be a cheap first check into a non-consensus asset, or YC could be auctioning demonstrated demand at a price that transfers the convexity to the founders. Until I know which, I am underwriting the company—not the investment.

The strongest evidence is the operating loop. Ooak licenses real company communications, documents, and tool activity, anonymizes them into structurally preserved digital twins, and sells RL-ready environments to frontier labs. The two sides normally mistrust each other: enterprises cannot casually release operational data, while labs need realistic multi-step workflows. Signed seven-figure contracts show that the resulting access already causes purchases. That is a real proprietary dark pool, not 566 LinkedIn followers or 300 dataset downloads pretending to be distribution.

The proposed venture moment is less clean. Acquiring and anonymizing more than 300 company ecosystems within six months is finite and measurable, and six open roles imply that capital is being organized around the attempt. But 300 environments is an output target, not yet a stated truth-revealing experiment. I need to know whether success proves scalable data acquisition, repeatable anonymization, superior model performance, or lab renewal. Hiring across GTM, marketing, operations, ML, and engineering risks turning one useful test into six simultaneous goals.

The bear case is brutal despite the contracts. “Seven-figure contracts” is not recognized revenue, collected cash, retention, or gross profit. Source companies may receive up to $300,000, privacy review is inherently expensive, and the two largest customers can internalize production or consolidate with Mercor, Scale AI, or AfterQuery. My 36-month base case is still $15 million–$30 million of ARR with more than half concentrated in the top two labs, but the same concentration that makes that forecast plausible could leave Ooak as a customized data operation with weak bargaining power rather than a compounding library.

The founders plausibly span the bridge, although the receipts are uneven. Thomas Aubry has shipped across robotics, Samsung ML, PayLead’s data operation, and Ooak’s APEX Explorer; Grégoire Lamy spent more than five years moving from product into operations at Epsor; Pierre-Louis Vouteau contributes distribution and company sourcing. Collectively, they can speak to both operational-data owners and technical buyers. The unresolved organizational risk is three co-founders with CEO, COO/CPO, and CTO titles but no evidence identifying who has final authority when privacy, product quality, and contract delivery conflict.

The closest exception is Ando: an AI-dependent product became investable because measured enterprise conversion and retention made the speculative technology sit on top of a functioning business. Ooak has the shared mechanism—real enterprise buyers—but not the disclosed conversion funnel, retention, margins, or cash economics that justified the exception. I am Neutral, not passing, because the contracts and licensed workflow access are unusually strong pre-seed evidence. I flip to Invest if a signed first-check term sheet shows a sub-$10 million post-money valuation without an auction process; at that price, the existing commercial proof compensates for the still-unresolved capital plan and economics.
