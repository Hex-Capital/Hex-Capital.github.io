# Qlo -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Qlo has evidence of a useful company, not yet a consequential option. Commercial underwriting is an expensive expert workflow, but the dossier quantifies a $5.7 billion software market and a 3× improvement in primary-quote throughput—not a 10× cost collapse or a societal bottleneck removed by orders of magnitude. If everything works, Qlo presently makes underwriters more productive. It does not yet show that insurance becomes radically cheaper, more available, or more accurately priced.

The product is more substantive than another chat interface. Qlo converts macro-enabled Excel raters into live workflows, preserves source references, executes approved pricing logic, and produces artifacts from quote through renewal without replacing the policy administration system. A customer uses it daily, so this is not a mockup. But 3× throughput is below the 5–10× discontinuity test, and Federato, Sixfold, and Kalepa already overlap ingestion, rating, and quote-and-bind. Customer-specific configuration may create switching friction; it does not establish a technical moat or proprietary learning loop.

The strongest evidence is Qlo’s de-risking procedure. A two-week setup followed by a paid 30-day pilot, parity check, and refund attacks the correct first kill risk: can the system reproduce spreadsheet logic and policy documents accurately enough to enter production? Daily quoting at the first enterprise customer and the Distinguished Programs result suggest that experiment has produced some evidence. But parity is only the first threshold. The next kill risk is whether each deployment remains a bespoke integration project, and Qlo supplies no cost, utilization, error-rate, or stop condition for that test.

Ryan Hu brings approximately 2.5 years inside MGA, carrier, and broker workflows, plus a prior product studio reportedly reaching about $500,000 ARR and receiving acquisition term sheets. Liv Huang adds engineering experience, while two underwriting advisers contribute 14-plus and 22-plus years of domain knowledge. That is credible founder-market fit for pre-seed. It is not yet evidence of fast belief revision or risk-tuned magnet recruiting: the dossier records no important thesis changed after data and no exceptional recruit installed to own accuracy, integrations, or enterprise distribution.

The bull case is the known execution-heavy fintech exception: a narrow workflow wedge can expand into infrastructure even without a scientific breakthrough. Qlo shares that mechanism with **Block**—start inside an ugly, expensive operational process and widen from there. The material difference is that Qlo has not demonstrated an American Express-scale value pool, platform expansion, or economics that improve as deployments accumulate. Its flat-fee design partnership has undisclosed pricing, while the two-week co-build may hide services labor. A plausible 36-month outcome is $3–7 million ARR as an implementation-heavy MGA workflow vendor, not displacement of the underwriting stack.

I pass today. The company may build a sound vertical SaaS business, but current evidence prices the upside too modestly for my money: limited technical discontinuity, crowded alternatives, and no demonstrated scale economics. One result would flip the decision—a paid deployment across three named MGAs showing at least 10× quotes per underwriter, parity accuracy after no more than two weeks of setup, and at least 80% gross margin. That would convert workflow convenience into a cost curve.
