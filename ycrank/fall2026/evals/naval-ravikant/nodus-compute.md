# Nodus Compute -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Nodus Compute has shipped something harder than a pitch: a system that places workloads and restores saved files when machines change. The public SDK and documented recovery controls give Kotra and Varshney evidence of action beyond their internship histories. Varshney’s Google cost-optimization work is particularly relevant. I give the founders meaningful credit for specific knowledge, while treating the claimed ten startup pilots and greater-than-30% savings as founder reports awaiting customer corroboration.

The investment question is what those deployments leave behind. Checkpoint integrity and generation fencing are necessary engineering, but the [security documentation](https://www.nodus-compute.ai/security/) does not establish an exclusive asset. If a competitor reproduces the scheduler and recovery system, what does Nodus still own? The dossier identifies no exclusive capacity, proprietary model, or demonstrated customer-data advantage. The proposed workload-to-research feedback loop could supply an answer; an unpublished research agenda cannot supply today’s moat.

The strongest argument against my hesitation is that fragmented GPU supply could become a network. Nodus’s outreach to holders of idle reserved GPUs fits my preference for bringing offline or inaccessible supply into a usable market. More suppliers could improve placement; more workloads could make participation worthwhile. But permissionless participation and customer-created value remain possibilities here. Ten pilots do not demonstrate that one participant improves another’s outcome. My exceptions for an early, unusual prototype or an emerging network require corresponding evidence of exceptional product development or self-reinforcing behavior. Nodus has a working product, but neither exception is yet strong enough to override the missing durable asset.

There is economic room to maneuver. Container placement and recovery could serve training and inference workloads beyond the initial unused-capacity problem. Modal’s reported annualized revenue above $1 billion and Runpod’s reported ARR above $325 million establish substantial adjacent spending, although neither figure belongs to Nodus. Those companies also constrain the opportunity: multi-cloud execution and capacity resale already have capable suppliers. The [published prices](https://www.nodus-compute.ai/pricing/)—$2.600 per H100 SXM hour and $0.290 per RTX 4090 hour—describe supply before Nodus fees. Usage billing is a sensible capture mechanism; those prices do not establish Nodus’s revenue or margin after interruptions and recovery.

I am neutral, and I would keep my money uncommitted today. There is no documented integrity violation or artificial fundraising deadline triggering a veto. The concern is structural: capable founders may build useful infrastructure while customers and compute suppliers capture most of its value. My flip condition is a production cohort demonstrating that additional independent suppliers create a sustained cost-to-completion advantage through capacity relationships or deployment data competitors cannot readily obtain. That would turn the proposed network from a diagram into an asset.
