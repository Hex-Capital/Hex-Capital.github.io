# Boat -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a cloud or developer infrastructure provider is Boat’s strongest route. Boat documents full Ubuntu VMs with Docker and a desktop, plus API controls to resume and fork persistent environments. The CTO says Boat built its snapshot system in-house. A buyer could use that stack to add persistent agent workloads and bring paying users onto its infrastructure. That rationale is an inference. The memo provides no buyer-side evidence of integration savings, incremental revenue, or willingness to pay. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The founder reported 350+ paying users in September and $1 million ARR in November, up from $200,000 one month earlier. That gives Boat an existing customer base for a seed-stage milestone of two quarters of retained paid VM usage at positive contribution margins after host and storage costs. The memo gives no cohort retention, host utilization, or current funding data to estimate the capital needed to scale. Ownership of the snapshot IP, contract assignability, and holder participation rights are unverified. The memo supports no independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, or 5×, which requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale scenarios assume a full cash closing within eight years, $5 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, no senior preference diversion, and an eligible holder selling their entire stake pro rata. The revenue levels and 5× ARR multiple are illustrative assumptions. Boat’s reported ARR provides a starting point for the growth comparison. (Key Facts)

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Gross holder cash |
|----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Modest sale | $10M ARR × 5 = $50M enterprise value, less $5M in deductions = $45M equity | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $20M ARR × 5 = $100M enterprise value, less $5M in deductions = $95M equity | $2.85M (5.7×). At 2.5% ownership, $2.375M (4.75×). |
| Prolonged private | Boat reinvests cash through year eight with no funded payout or eligible share sale | $0 realized |

### Countercase

A buyer could extend its own sandbox offering or build on an open-source runtime. AWS already offers managed browser and code execution, and E2B offers a self-hostable runtime. Those substitutes may cover enough workloads to erase the premium for Boat’s persistent VM stack. The reported revenue growth leaves open how much demand depends on Boat’s distinctive features and whether that demand can support the strong sale case. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported sale-price ceiling below the proceeds hurdle, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Could owning Boat’s persistent VM stack create enough incremental profit or save enough build cost for a strategic buyer to justify roughly $90 million in enterprise value? A quantified buyer-side analysis grounded in retained paid workloads and their compute margins would move the action to Reach out if it supported that price under the stated holder assumptions.
