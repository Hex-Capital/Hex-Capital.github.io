# Florin -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Amol Pant built Tesla Autopilot compute and NASA rover prototypes, while none of Florin’s founders has documented banking experience. That pairing is Florin’s signature: unusually strong infrastructure hackers pursuing an idea that does not appear organic to their own lives. Florin may be able to build the machine, but the dossier does not explain why these three were compelled to build this particular machine.

**Stripe** is the high rung for hacker genealogy: the Collisons were already commerce hackers, and their technical artifact emerged from that history. Florin approaches that rung through Shaurya Aggarwal’s TPU and encryption work, Pant’s perception systems, and Aydin Sorensen’s seven years at AWS and Twitch. But Florin diverges where the analogy becomes decisive. No public repository, working demo, transaction volume, or independently verified ledger performance shows Florin’s hackers building financial infrastructure before they began pitching it.

**Loopt** is the low rung on organicity: weak product timing was excused because Sam Altman’s force of personality supplied the missing conviction. Florin’s biographies describe compute, encryption, autonomous vehicles, and teleoperation, but no founder personally suffered through banking, payments, treasury, or compliance. Florin therefore exhibits the red-flag version of a startup idea discovered from the outside. Unlike Loopt, Florin has not yet supplied the animal-founder exception that would override this absence.

Shaurya Aggarwal deployed encryption across more than 100,000 hosts, which proves Florin’s founders can execute inside hard technical systems. It does not prove animal resourcefulness before funding. Over the next 18 months, Florin will have to obtain bank access, manually onboard nervous startups, investigate fraud, and move money correctly at 2 a.m.; those jobs compound resourcefulness because every ugly exception teaches Florin what to automate. The dossier contains no cereal boxes, rejected-application rescue, unscalable customer acquisition, or even a live-customer anecdote. Animals leave tracks, so I apply nearly the absent-evidence default.

Florin’s claimed single real-time ledger places Florin high on the schlep dimension because BSA/AML controls, sanctions screening, custody, audits, and fraud operations are precisely the work software founders unconsciously avoid. The score would collapse if “end to end” means a neat interface over an undisclosed sponsor bank, because then Florin has selected the appearance of the schlep rather than the schlep itself. The score would move toward Coinbase if Florin disclosed regulated partners, live payment volume, and founders personally handling compliance exceptions.

**Airbnb** had three founders with complementary abilities and a relationship tested by desperate work before the check. Florin also has three founders, and Florin’s combination of Google infrastructure, Tesla robotics, and AWS operations is technically formidable. But no public evidence establishes how long Aggarwal, Pant, and Sorensen have worked together or whether one founder owns the non-technical banking grind. Three impressive résumés are not yet a founder configuration; the test is whether they behave like one organism when the ledger is wrong and the regulator is waiting.

Mercury’s 300,000 customers and $650 million in annualized revenue make a frontal startup-banking attack exceptionally unforgiving. Within 36 months, I predict Florin will pivot from an invite-only banking membership to selling ledger and reconciliation infrastructure to fintechs, reaching roughly $1 million to $3 million in ARR rather than displacing Mercury as a primary bank. Florin’s hacker strength makes a useful infrastructure product plausible; Florin’s weak organic connection to startup banking makes persistence with the original customer proposition less plausible.

Florin’s three founders present a painful bull case against that forecast: Florin may have assembled exactly the sort of hackers capable of owning a ledger while Mercury and Brex remain collections of partner integrations, and the regulatory grind could become Coinbase-like schlep protection. Airbnb and Loopt show why rejecting an odd or mistimed idea can be a mistake when exceptional founders are underneath it. But those exceptions contained visible animal evidence, while Florin discloses no customers, revenue, licenses, regulated partners, or pre-funding stunt. I would change my verdict if ten named startups moved the majority of their operating cash onto Florin and used Florin’s accounts, cards, payments, and treasury repeatedly for six months.
