# Kanopy -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Kanopy’s most revealing evidence is in its release notes. Pole associations and wire fitting needed corrections, and the team shipped them in September–October 2026. That is a small, concrete instance of encountering resistance and changing the software. It earns more credit than the founders’ credentials. But the dossier does not show how quickly they diagnosed these problems or whether the fixes rescued an inspection. I see some resourcefulness, without enough evidence to make it the reason I write a check ([release notes](https://status.kanopy-ai.com/changelog.html)).

The product has gone far enough to deserve attention. Kanopy reports 3,093 mapped miles, including over 300 miles across four states in two weeks. Turning ordinary camera footage into useful infrastructure measurements would remove an expensive obstacle to inspection. This fits my preference for technical founders who build something inspectable before claiming a platform. But mapped mileage measures processing volume. The advertised 2–4-inch accuracy remains unvalidated in the public evidence. A reconstruction can look convincing while putting a wire on the wrong pole. Whether a utility can safely order work from the result is the important technical threshold ([mapping report](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/yann-pfitzer-63b404142_big-things-coming-soon-for-kanopy-activity-7519062909490970624-DRPy), [product claims](https://kanopy-ai.com/)).

There is a plausible narrow well here: inspection contractors and utility vegetation teams that already collect footage and need actionable clearance records. Six utilities and contractors signed LOIs or MSAs covering 270,000 miles by April 2026. That is meaningful early access to potential users. It does not establish that those users bought inspections, returned for another survey, or changed their workflow around Kanopy. Nor can I connect the subsequent mapped miles to those six organizations. I would give the agreements partial demand credit and keep the field-processing evidence in the technical score ([Antler](https://www.antler.co/blog/announcing-the-spring-2026-u-s-portfolio-showcase)).

The strongest case for investing is that these brothers have unusually complementary preparation for making that connection. Yann worked with utilities at McKinsey; Nicolas led computer-vision development at ShapeScale and researched robotics. They have a product, field activity, and access to buyers. Their relationship also avoids the obvious concern about strangers assembling a founding team for an application, although being brothers does not establish shared execution under pressure. The missing founder evidence is more specific: which inspection problem did Yann personally encounter, and what observation caused the team to change its approach? Prior employment makes an organic origin plausible. It does not document one. Software corrections likewise do not establish an evidence-driven change of product route.

Kanopy could become valuable by doing the awkward work between reconstruction and a contractor’s work order: maintaining asset identity, handling imperfect footage, and producing records someone trusts. Public reconstruction models do not automatically solve those problems. But that opportunity is still a hypothesis. Schneider’s proposed acquisition of AiDASH could put an overlapping supplier inside existing utility relationships, so Kanopy needs users who pull it through procurement. The dossier shows no affirmative dependence on a major partner or ceremonial launch; demonstrating at Trees & Utilities is ordinary customer recruitment. The concern is whether founder-led field work is producing indispensable software or merely more demonstrations ([Schneider announcement](https://www.aidash.com/news/schneider-electric-announces-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-aidash/)).

I would hold off on a personal check. The working-product exception deserves consideration, but the present evidence does not show extraordinary validated performance or unmistakable repeat demand strong enough to replace the incomplete founder test. My flip condition is one documented customer cycle: an inspection contractor identifies a clearance or asset-association failure, the founders ship a correction within two weeks, and that contractor pays for a repeat survey using the corrected system. That would connect resistance, founder action, and user pull. Kanopy has promising pieces of that proof. It has not yet connected them.
