# ernd -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is ernd’s strongest path. A corporate-card or spend-management platform could buy ernd to bring an active pool of AI spending onto its platform and gain the supplier terms that spending can command. Ownership could be worth paying for if the buyer can retain the spend and preserve supplier economics it would struggle to reproduce through its own negotiations. That advantage is an inference. The memo records live Wafer cash back, higher Firecrawl usage limits, and provider-level spend integrations. Priority GPU and inference capacity remained a company plan. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

The operating milestone is sustained card spend from the same customer cohorts, with positive contribution after rewards and program costs and measurable purchasing advantages. ernd reported 30+ first-week card requests and eight signed partners, and the memo records YC backing. Those signals support testing the milestone. Active spend, retention, contribution margin, runway, and the capital needed to reach it remain unknown. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

The memo supplies no terms for the proposed check, so the proceeds test uses the illustrative $500,000 investment at a $10 million post-money valuation. It assumes 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-on checks, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. The holder needs $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale scenarios are assumptions for testing the hurdle. The memo supplies no usable sale-price evidence. Both sales assume zero applicable net debt and no other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity value. The modeled waterfall allocates cash pro rata at closing with no preference adjustments. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero.

| Scenario | Timing | Distributable equity | Holder cash | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 4 | $40 million | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100 million | $3.0 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No distribution | $0 | 0× |

The strong sale falls to $2 million, or 4×, at 2% ownership. In the prolonged-private case, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The investor retains the shares. Paid listings and standalone key protection are monetization signals, with no evidence of distributable cash or an independent route to liquidity for this holder. (*Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Incumbents may be able to build the useful features and negotiate directly with providers. Dash.fi already offers AI-specific rewards, and Ramp already tracks AI costs by provider, model, and project. Providers can end perks at any time, and Coastal Community Bank controls card approval and cardholder terms. Those dependencies put the continuity of the acquired program at risk. The overlap weakens the reason to buy, but the memo leaves open what durable purchasing terms an incumbent could replicate and at what cost. The evidence supports Watch because the ownership premium remains unproven. It establishes neither a binding transfer barrier nor a price ceiling that triggers Pass. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Would an incumbent’s buy-versus-build analysis support a cash acquisition that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds within eight years? Move to Reach out when a credible buyer-side estimate ties that price to retained, contribution-positive card spend and supplier economics that can survive a change of control. An estimate showing that the incumbent can secure equivalent terms at a cost that caps the acquisition below the holder’s hurdle would move the case to Pass.
