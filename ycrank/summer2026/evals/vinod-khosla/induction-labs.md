# Induction Labs -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The attractive claim is not “intellectually curious AI.” It is that 2 million unlabeled screen recordings can replace application-specific action labels as the raw material for computer-use intelligence. Photon-1 compresses frames by more than 100x, predicts future states, and converts those imagined states into actions through instruction tuning and online reinforcement learning. If this architecture generalizes, the scarce input to automating computer work changes from curated demonstrations to abundant video. That could be consequential. But Induction Labs has not quantified the expert-labor cost pool displaced, the population served, or a mechanism supporting a 10x–100x societal consequence. A $7.63 billion agents-market estimate is market research, not an option-value calculation.

The technical evidence is substantially better. Two founders trained a 106B-A5B model on 575 million frames—18 years of screen video—and report lower pretraining requirements than conventional inverse dynamics, 30x less pretraining compute, 3x lower serving cost than Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite, and superior internal computer-use results. The 2-billion-video index, proprietary keyframe filter, differential latent encoder, and fused kernels constitute real systems work, not a prompt wrapper. This matches the recent foundational-AI pattern I find interesting: architectural leverage applied to an expensive real-world workflow. The limitation is severe, however. Every decisive benchmark is internal, the Gemini compute comparison partly uses company estimates, and H Company already offers a model scoring 78.85% on OSWorld-Verified through APIs and open weights.

The bear case must be taken seriously. Induction Labs may have built an ingenious pretraining system whose savings do not survive deployment. Its evidence outside desktop interaction is limited to checkers and synthetic billiards; it has no disclosed customers, repeat usage, revenue, pricing, or proprietary production-feedback loop. Open-weight substitutes could capture developers while Photon-1 remains a research demonstration. Training on roughly 2 million public screen recordings also creates unresolved licensing and privacy exposure. If independent testing erases the claimed performance advantage, the company becomes another capital-intensive model laboratory without either distribution or a protected data source.

Jonathan Li’s ACL 2023 research and work on reasoning, synthetic data, and reinforcement-learning infrastructure at Cohere are unusually relevant. David Li adds software and ML experience, and the brothers have already completed a model-training effort far beyond the normal pre-seed demo. Yet credentials and execution do not prove the founder behavior I care about most. The dossier shows no material belief revised after disconfirming data, no difficult complementary recruit, and no hiring plan mapped to evaluation, data-rights, or commercialization risk. The team has demonstrated technical ambition, but not yet risk-tuned organization building.

The largest problem is capital sequencing. Induction Labs has spent enough compute to train Photon-1, but it has not named the cheapest decisive experiment against its largest kill risk. Is the risk independent task performance, cross-application generalization, lawful data access, or serving economics at customer workloads? There is no pass/fail threshold, budget, timing, or pivot branch. Likewise, 3x lower serving cost is promising but not a scale model: no price, utilization, inference-volume assumptions, or comparison with the total cost of human-delivered work is disclosed.

I would not write the check today. This is not an active thesis mismatch—the architecture is novel, the technical team is credible, and none of the known exceptions is needed to excuse a wrapper or incremental product. It is a technically interesting option whose consequence and falsification plan remain underwritten. One verifiable condition flips my decision: Photon-1 must beat Holo3.1 on OSWorld-Verified in an independently reproducible evaluation while sustaining at least a 3x measured inference-cost advantage on the same task set. That single result would validate both the discontinuity and the first serious step toward economic leverage.
