# Melty -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

David Miller has acted inside gaming: YC reports that he coached millions of gamers, and Melty has shipped a Windows launcher that automates mashup installation and multiplayer invitations. That earns more attention than credentials. Coaching gives him proximity to players; shipping gives him contact with their technical problems. Neither yet establishes exceptional knowledge of creator economics or cross-game compatibility. I see a credible founder signal, with limited evidence of how much he has learned from actual usage.

The decisive question is what Melty keeps when another launcher reproduces its features. Its October 18, 2026 terms require exclusive marketplace distribution while content is listed and for twelve months afterward. But a contractual restriction becomes an asset only when creators contribute something players want. The dossier establishes the restriction, without establishing a valuable exclusive catalogue. Compatibility reports could eventually compound into better installation reliability; there is no demonstrated advantage today. I would not capitalize that possibility as an existing moat.

The strongest argument against passing is that Melty could turn cheaper creation into scarce distribution. External coding agents help creators produce mashups; the launcher makes those creations playable; invitations bring friends into the experience. Each creator could supply entertainment for many players without equivalent company labor. The reported 10,611 Discord members make this worth investigating, even though the figure comes from a third-party snapshot. But community membership does not establish gameplay, creator contribution, or repeat invitations. My exceptions for strange prototypes and unmonetized networks require evidence of exceptional execution or self-reinforcing behavior. Melty has the product architecture for that exception; the dossier does not show the behavior.

There is room beyond the first mashup. The same installation and publishing capabilities could serve mod creators seeking distribution and players seeking simpler access to community-made content. Overwolf’s reported $10 million of historical 2020 revenue supports the existence of adjacent commercial demand, without establishing Melty’s reachable market. Nexus Mods and CurseForge already occupy distribution positions. Melty must give creators and players a reason to build their relationships here. Free downloads help discovery, but all current mashups are free and future payments remain provisions in the terms. Direct value capture is unproved.

Melty fits my pattern of letting individuals turn knowledge into scalable output. Its exclusivity requirement also creates friction with that permissionless appeal: creators retain ownership while accepting a substantial restriction on distribution. Whether they willingly accept that trade is an operating question. Likewise, the reported allegations about inherited recording components are unverified; they do not establish dishonesty. The current privacy policy describes limited launcher-window recordings. Trust matters especially for software installed alongside games, but I see no substantiated integrity veto or evidence of deadline pressure in this dossier.

I pass with my own money today. Melty has shipped a promising tool, while the customer-created asset remains hypothetical. My verifiable condition for reopening is a 90-day cohort report showing that unpaid creator invitations bring in players with at least 30% day-30 gameplay retention. That would demonstrate creators supplying both distribution and enduring utility—the mechanism that could make this company compound.
