# TrustAI -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Medha Venkatapathy brings MIT LLM post-training research, while TrustAI sells analyst-reviewed assessments rather than a user-compounding network. That pairing is TrustAI’s signature: unusually intelligent builders applying specific knowledge to a structurally ordinary enterprise-control product. The knowledge may compound inside the founders; the customer value does not yet compound across customers. TrustAI is closer to expert software-enabled services than permissionless infrastructure.

Stack Overflow is the high rung for specific knowledge: developers built for developers after living the problem publicly. TrustAI shares the technical half of that mechanism through Venkatapathy’s LLM-evaluation work and Hannah Chung’s exposure to quantitative finance and regulated decision systems. TrustAI diverges where the analogy matters most: neither founder has operated enterprise identity, ERP authorization, or GRC at scale, and neither has a public repository or domain-writing trail demonstrating years of obsession. The founders are above the credential-only founder pattern, but below the lived-problem intensity that made Stack Overflow inevitable.

Perplexity’s low-rung network profile shows the failure mechanism: each new user does not automatically make the prior user’s product more valuable. TrustAI is lower still on that axis because every assessment is a bilateral enterprise engagement involving proprietary systems, order forms, and analyst review. TrustAI could eventually aggregate permission-path and remediation data, but that moat is explicitly inferred and no customer-scale dataset exists. Perplexity earned the exception through top-quartile permissionless distribution, founder-specific knowledge, and pre-consensus timing; TrustAI does not clear those other gates emphatically enough to waive the missing network.

TrustAI’s demo-led sales motion, professional-service fees, and analyst signature place labor inside the delivery loop. Over 18 months, TrustAI’s code can replicate the 51 tests and four framework mappings cheaply, but SAP integrations, procurement, implementation workshops, and manual review will cause distribution costs to rise with customer count. TrustAI therefore has code leverage in production but labor and permission leverage in distribution. Anchorage Digital is the tempting exception—permission can become a moat—but TrustAI holds no charter or exclusive regulatory position. A compliance report is not a regulatory monopoly.

The $3.59B projected AI-governance market gives TrustAI room to move from predeployment assessments into continuous monitoring, agent identity, ERP authorization, or audit evidence. TrustAI reaches the next tier on market breadth only if its MCP hub becomes a control plane across SAP, Oracle, NetSuite, Jira, and ServiceNow rather than a narrow assessment workflow. If those platforms absorb governance natively—as SAP’s Joule identity services and Oracle’s secure MCP connectors suggest—the apparent pivot room belongs to the incumbents, not TrustAI.

Notion is the useful single-anchor parallel for the founder trio: young founders can earn the decade-long relationship through repeated building before conventional proof arrives. Chung and Venkatapathy have built together since freshman year, and TrustAI’s pivot from browser automation shows energy and willingness to discard an idea. Intelligence reads high; integrity remains unproven, as it should at pre-seed. TrustAI’s Conduct AI-branded legal links and conflicting London addresses are operational sloppiness, not evidence of dishonesty, but governance software sells trust before it sells features. The paperwork must be cleaner than the customer’s paperwork.

Noma Security’s $132M funding makes the likely path narrower: my 36-month forecast is that by July 2029 TrustAI will have pivoted from analyst-reviewed compliance reports into continuous SAP-and-Oracle agent-access monitoring, reached $1M-$3M ARR, and remained a single-sided security vendor rather than a network. TrustAI’s 51-test catalog supplies the wedge; repeated assessment demand pulls TrustAI toward runtime controls because static reconstruction results transfer to production only as hypotheses. Software follows the recurring event stream. Reports do not compound; control planes can.

SAP’s embedded authorization services, Oracle’s secure MCP connectors, and more than $342M raised by overlapping competitors form the strongest bear case: TrustAI may be dead before the pivot because platform owners control distribution while Noma, WitnessAI, and Zenity already control the security budget. TrustAI’s 506 LinkedIn followers and 679 launch reactions are attention, not adoption, and analyst review makes the red flag sting. The bear case does not fully win because TrustAI already shipped 51 preregistered tests, mapped four frameworks, and executed one product pivot with technically strong founders. I remain Neutral because architecture outranks activity. If TrustAI demonstrates 10 paying enterprises whose repeat assessments are at least 80% automated and acquired without outbound enterprise sales, I flip to Invest.
