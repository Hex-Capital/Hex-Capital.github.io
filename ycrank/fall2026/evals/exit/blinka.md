# Blinka -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Blinka’s strongest path. A business banking or payments platform could buy Blinka to gain relationships with U.S.-incorporated companies operating abroad and add a workflow that combines remote USD account opening with local Latin American payouts. Ownership could save the buyer the time and cost of building that distribution if customers stay active and generate attractive margins. That buyer benefit remains an inference. The memo documents the product and its provider arrangements, reports more than 400 businesses onboarded in two months, and records payment-volume milestones above $10 million and $15 million before the CEO reported more than $18 million. Those figures support a seed-stage test of repeat usage. Current-product retention, margins, burn, and runway remain unknown, and the disclosed financing dates to earlier product iterations. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under the shared scenario, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3%, compared with $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. Assuming $5 million of net debt and simple pro rata distribution before investor taxes, a year-eight sale at $25 million enterprise value yields $600,000 (1.2×), while a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). The 3% case needs about $88.3 million in enterprise value under that debt assumption. These are scenario prices. The memo supplies no supported sale-price range or company-specific debt, fee, or preference amounts for the bridge. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

If growth plateaus, buyer interest fails to emerge, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. The memo supplies no independent evidence of sponsor-ready cash flow, an eligible secondary, IPO readiness, or funded dividends or buybacks. A wind-down recovery would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could build or partner for similar capabilities. Slash and Jeeves already offer overlapping products, and Blinka relies on separate providers for deposits, cards, and stablecoin settlement. Preserving those services through a sale would depend on continued provider participation or viable replacements. The memo leaves change-of-control terms open and gives no retention or multi-product adoption evidence to show why buying Blinka would beat those alternatives. This weakens the ownership case and creates closing risk. Neither a structural block nor a supported price ceiling that would leave the holder materially short is established, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Blinka show six months of repeat cross-border usage with positive contribution margins at a scale that would justify an acquisition over building or partnering? Cohort data and a buyer-specific valuation range that credibly clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle within eight years, with a plausible path to keeping the core services operating through a sale, would move the action to Reach out.
