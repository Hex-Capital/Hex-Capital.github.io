# Invertix -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Invertix uses agents to triage solar alarms, coordinate technicians, and verify production after repairs. Altenia’s CEO reported that some work went from days to minutes. That testimonial supports a narrower workflow improvement. The investment hypothesis is that smaller operators could afford frequent fault follow-up they previously deferred. That scenario remains untested. The memo gives no like-for-like measure of labor per correctly resolved fault after approvals, retries, and exceptions. Plant-control changes require customer approval. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

TotalEnergies selected Invertix for its accelerator and tested it with solar teams, an observed buyer commitment of time. Invertix reports roughly 2 GW of agent coverage. Paid conversion and repeat orders are unverified. The founders bring relevant early execution evidence through operator discovery and experience in autonomy and privacy research. The pre-seed stage fits the strategy, while the Munich headquarters and U.S. outreach leave geographic fit open. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea)

The expansion unit is a customer portfolio. The memo estimates roughly 150 independent asset managers in the initial Italian segment and describes custom quotes tied to portfolio size and integration scope. In a **small-portfolio scenario**, the buyer’s savings from more frequent, correctly resolved faults must cover the fee and its remaining review and field labor. Invertix’s first-year contribution would be that fee less engineer-led onboarding, inference, support, and any exceptions it handles. The memo supplies neither actual fees nor the delivery costs needed to establish that bridge. (Key Facts, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Invertix may speed up work existing solar operators already buy, while competitors with the same models can offer comparable results. Metris and Areg.AI already overlap in agent-led solar workflows. Invertix’s embedded asset histories and approval rules could make switching costly, but the memo offers no buyer comparison under functional parity or retention evidence. Path A has no separate supported case because the memo establishes neither measured relief of a usable power constraint nor Invertix’s control of scarce operating capacity. Both Path B conditions remain open, which keeps the case at Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will a small solar operator that previously deferred routine fault follow-up pay to continue after a side-by-side trial against a workflow using comparably capable models? The trial should show comparable restoration quality, lower total labor per correctly resolved fault after reviews and exceptions, and paid continuation at a fee that covers Invertix’s delivery costs. That result could move the action to Reach out if the revised score reaches 65 and mandate fit is plausible. If the trial shows that the workflow still costs too much for that buyer or that the alternative delivers the same outcome and the buyer readily switches, move to Pass.
