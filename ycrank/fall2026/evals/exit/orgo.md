# Orgo -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by an agent-infrastructure platform that wants to add persistent, multi-OS desktops to its sandbox offering. Orgo documents persistent disks, live desktop takeover, and a single API for Linux, Windows, and macOS. A customer reported saving about 12 hours on one workflow. Orgo separately claims more than 1,000 teams and thousands of production desktops. The documented capabilities and reported usage support a production test. Buyer savings and willingness to pay remain unmeasured. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

Ownership could give a buyer control over fleet economics, reliability, and the product roadmap for workloads that retain state between sessions. The operating milestone is a paid, platform-scale deployment that demonstrates sustained usage, acceptable uptime, and positive contribution margin after fleet costs. Orgo’s status page reported 99.760% compute-fleet uptime over 30 days, which gives the test a baseline. The capital and runway available to reach that milestone are unknown, as are IP ownership and the transfer terms for essential infrastructure and customer contracts. The memo provides no usable evidence of an independent fallback that would pay this holder. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *Defensibility*, *Key Facts*)

The illustrative seed case assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash. Under a simple pro rata waterfall, the holder needs $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, zero other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, and no senior claims. Under those assumptions, enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration, paid entirely in cash at close. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and transaction-related compensation are each assumed to be $0.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Distributable equity proceeds | Holder cash and gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest acquisition | Year 5 | $50 million | $1.5 million, 3.0× |
| Strong acquisition | Year 7 | $100 million | $3.0 million, 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | $0 distributed | $0 realized, 0.0×, shares retained |

The strong scenario clears the hurdle at 3% and falls short at 2%, where it yields $2 million. Both sale prices are scenario assumptions. The memo gives no usable valuation or buyer economics to support either price. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

AWS already offers Linux and Windows agent desktops with identity integration, and Daytona already exposes graphical desktops through its sandbox API. A prospective buyer could extend its own fleet or license Orgo’s capabilities. Either option could cap the acquisition price if it delivers comparable results on the persistent workloads that matter. The memo establishes meaningful overlap and leaves performance and cost parity unresolved. That gap warrants Watch. The evidence does not establish a structural price ceiling or trigger either Pass override. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a prospective platform buyer justify an ownership price that would deliver at least $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership? A paid production deployment and a buyer-side comparison of owning, building, and licensing would answer it if they quantify the advantage of ownership at credible scale. Evidence that the advantage supports the hurdle would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that building or licensing delivers comparable results and caps the price materially below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
