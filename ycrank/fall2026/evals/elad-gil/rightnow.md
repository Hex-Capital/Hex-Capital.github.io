# RightNow -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Coding agents repeatedly resending context create a specific opening for RightNow: sell developers cheaper inference by routing sessions to replicas that already hold their context. The controlling variable is the realized cost per successfully completed coding task, after cache misses, GPU costs, and latency are included. If session locality creates a persistent advantage on that measure, a focused provider could build a business inside a market increasingly organized around large platforms. RightNow identifies a credible mechanism, but its published cache rates describe the highest qualifying account rather than typical performance. That leaves the central economic proposition unproven.

The distinction between a technical implementation and an economic advantage matters here. Autokernel includes actual GPU-kernel optimization code and a correctness harness; this is stronger evidence than a performance claim attached to a landing page. But RunInfra’s historical benchmarks omit runtime details, and its engine-comparison harness and raw data are unavailable. Open-weight models provide a plausible adoption catalyst, yet the dossier establishes no quantified improvement in customer costs or provider margins. The $10/$29/$99 subscriptions establish an offer, not profitable delivery. Together AI already develops kernels, compilers, and inference systems, so RightNow must demonstrate an advantage against competitors improving the same underlying machinery.

The strongest bull case is that two technically productive founders can specialize faster than those broad platforms. Both founders coauthored inference and model research, and Autokernel’s approximately 1,600 GitHub stars give the team a relevant developer audience. This fits my pattern of backing several layers of a technology transition, including infrastructure. But the research-stage exception requires unusually strong technical evidence and a credible capital path; the dossier does not establish that combination. Osama’s previous founding of Antilab supplies some startup context without documenting lessons or outcomes. Employer and university affiliations do not establish costly commitment or repeat-founder learning.

Distribution and workflow ownership remain separate questions. The July 1 and October 20 launches ranked #14 and #8 on Product Hunt, respectively, but neither demonstrates recurring paid inference demand. OpenFang’s approximately 18,200 stars belong to a separate agent project and cannot be counted as RunInfra users. Session-aware serving maintains useful runtime state, but the dossier does not establish durable customer integrations, operational feedback loops, or responsibility for completing the coding workflow. Better models could increase inference demand while leaving customers free to switch providers. Autokernel’s MIT license also makes its public implementation reusable; there is no established proprietary-data advantage to underwrite.

I would pass with my own money. This is a plausible infrastructure specialization with genuine technical output, but the market is already crystallizing around well-funded providers, and the evidence does not yet connect RightNow’s specialization to superior economics or customer pull. That is an underwriting gap, not evidence of failed adoption. My single flip condition is a reproducible 90-day production cohort showing customers repeatedly paying for coding workloads that RightNow serves at least 30% more cheaply per successfully completed task than Together AI, at comparable latency and positive contribution margin. The 30% is my investment threshold, not a reported result. That would turn session caching from an architectural proposition into a reason customers choose this company.
