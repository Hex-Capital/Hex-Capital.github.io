# Cerenovus -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Cerenovus’s live pilots and #16 Product Hunt launch reveal a usable artifact, while Jonathan Waldorf, Oliver Moreland, and Lucas Baur reveal no named CEO or visible tiebreaker. That is Cerenovus’s signature: a product people can touch attached to a three-founder structure nobody appears authorized to steer. I can open Compendium, pay $39.99 per seat, and connect real documents; I cannot tell whom I call when the executive-systems-map vision collides with the shared-agent-context vision.

**Niantic** is the high rung for physical verifiability because I lived inside Ingress before investing; the product’s pull was observable without John Hanke narrating it. Cerenovus shares the important mechanism: Compendium is publicly available, has live pilots, and reached 123 Product Hunt points rather than existing as a slide. Cerenovus diverges because enterprise knowledge retrieval becomes meaningful only after sensitive Drive, Slack, CRM, and accounting data accumulate; Product Hunt curiosity is not the obsessive, embodied pull I felt with Niantic.

**HQ Trivia** is the low-rung warning for governance: an addictive artifact temporarily concealed unresolved authority between its founders, then the fracture consumed the product. Cerenovus presents three Harvard co-founders—Waldorf as “Founder,” Moreland as CTO, and Baur as “Founder”—without naming a CEO, majority operator, or conflict protocol. Cerenovus therefore repeats the mechanism before earning the HQ exception: Product Hunt rank #16 and unspecified pilots are not explosive product love strong enough to override missing tiebreaker authority.

Jonathan Waldorf’s Harvard physics and electrical-engineering work, QuEra internship, and quantum-metrology research establish Tiz, but Cerenovus offers no evidence that Waldorf ever ran the broken enterprise handoffs Cerenovus wants to diagnose. Over 18 months, that missing lived relationship compounds: every ambiguous customer request can pull Cerenovus toward another impressive demo because none of the founders has scar tissue telling them which workflow is real. Lucas Baur’s Point72 program and Oliver Moreland’s Math 55 coursework reinforce a credentialed-builder pattern, not an autobiographical origin; Biz and Rizz remain unproven.

Glean’s $623 million of funding and more than $100 million ARR make Cerenovus’s category consensus even if Cerenovus’s particular workflow-inference layer is clever. The magically-weird score would flip if Cerenovus showed that sophisticated buyers or investors rejected the product because mapping internal power, staffing, and vendor failures felt invasive—and that pilot users nevertheless kept returning. Cerenovus currently looks less like Crusoe burning stranded gas and more like the consensus YC AI-agent pitch wearing a markdown knowledge graph.

**SpaceX** made capital intensity part of the moat: rockets force learning that cannot be copied with an API call. Cerenovus instead sends prompts and relevant vault content to Anthropic while Glean and Dust already possess enterprise connectors, graphs, and distribution. Cerenovus does face ugly security reviews around retention, permissions, and deletion, but compliance friction imposed on ordinary software is not the same as owning frontier hardware or operating inside a regulated market; the difficulty burdens adoption without yet protecting the architecture.

Cerenovus’s $39.99-per-seat product and split positioning produce my 36-month forecast: Cerenovus pivots from executive decision infrastructure into shared context tooling for AI agents and is acqui-hired by Notion for under $25 million before reaching $2 million ARR. Cerenovus has built something installable, but the two buyer profiles and absent authority map invite product drift; Notion is the natural home for a useful context layer that fails to become an independent organizational operating system.

Glean’s more than $100 million ARR supplies the strongest bull case: demand is proven, Cerenovus has pilots before Demo Day, and a longitudinal graph could compound into proprietary organizational memory. The **HQ Trivia** exception says tactile product love can outrun governance, and Cerenovus could become the executive instrument Glean never designed. The evidence does not yet clear that exception: no revenue, retention, accuracy benchmark, named pilot, or repeat-usage figure accompanies the 123 Product Hunt points. I would flip to Invest if Cerenovus names one CEO and converts its pilots into at least $1 million ARR with documented six-month retention.
