# Nines -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path A, scarce complements, is the stronger case. Nines is a San Francisco seed company targeting the technician time needed to connect and verify fiber before GPU racks can go into service. Nines describes this work as a commissioning bottleneck and proposes autonomous patching. The memo records a high-density GPU rack rollout and identifies Nines’ inspection sequence as a simulation. It gives no field benchmark showing that fiber work delays commissioning or that Nines can shorten it. The seed stage fits the mandate, and a representative field trial could resolve the technical question. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

If cheaper intelligence improves cable handling and recovery, Nines could free up qualified technician time and help operators commission racks faster. Even with equivalent models, operators would still need hardware that works on their racks and a team that can qualify and support it on site. Nines could capture value if it can carry that qualification work across deployments. The memo establishes no such advantage and records no operator pilot, purchase, or revenue. It also identifies no newly viable buyer or transaction to support Path B. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Li’s robotics and production inference experience and Eom’s work scaling a software product give the team a credible starting point. Delivery on an operator’s racks remains unproven. Under an assumed per-site service model, the scale test is whether each additional site produces enough verified rack throughput to cover hardware, setup, supervision, and rework. The memo provides no deployment measurements for that calculation. (Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Even if fiber patching constrains commissioning, operators could capture the savings themselves or buy equivalent capacity from another vendor. Microsoft’s transceiver manipulation prototype shows that an operator can build overlapping capabilities. Public simulation assets and manipulation benchmarks lower the cost of prototyping, while rack-specific setup and manual recovery could absorb the savings from automation. This leaves the Path A outreach gate uncleared. The evidence does not establish interchangeable production systems or show that Nines cannot retain value, so the override does not close the path. (Defensibility, Key Risks)
