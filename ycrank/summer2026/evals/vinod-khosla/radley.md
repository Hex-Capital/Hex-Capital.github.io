# Radley -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence could be large: one in six scans reportedly lacks sufficient radiologist capacity, and U.S. diagnostic imaging is a $149.54 billion service pool. But Radley has not shown that its agents can multiply radiologist capacity by 10x, materially improve diagnostic accuracy, or collapse the cost per interpretation. Acquiring a practice changes ownership. It does not yet change the cost curve.

The strongest evidence is the founders’ willingness to change the plan. Their former software product went live and signed $2.2 million in contracts, yet they pivoted to owning the clinical operation. That is meaningful belief revision, not a PowerPoint exercise. Maryam Jahed helped scale Carbon Health from 31 to more than 100 clinics; Seak Meng Lay built its engineering and EHR infrastructure. This is an unusually complementary two-person team for combining clinical operations with software.

The bull case stings. Closing the pending acquisition would place $4.3 million of annualized revenue, facility relationships, credentialing infrastructure, and production workflow under Radley’s control. Ordinary usage could then generate the proprietary operational data that standalone vendors cannot obtain. This resembles the unstated pattern behind execution-heavy healthcare platforms: control distribution first, improve the system from inside, and compound the integration playbook.

But the dossier contains no technical result supporting that loop. Rad AI already reaches more than 40% of U.S. health systems, Sirona has 12 million annual studies under contract, and Radiology Partners applies technology across more than 25 million examinations. Against those named incumbents, Radley reports no benchmark for reads per radiologist, turnaround time, error rate, labor cost, or agent autonomy. Today the differentiation is a transaction structure—buying the practice—not a demonstrated 5–10x discontinuity.

The capital sequence is also backward until proven otherwise. The acquisition is prospective, while financing terms, integration milestones, AI-governance validation, and stop conditions are undisclosed. Multistate licensure and facility credentialing are known expansion constraints. Buying revenue before testing whether the agents safely remove the binding labor constraint risks creating a conventional services roll-up with additional software risk.

My 36-month base case is a functioning radiology operator producing $5–12 million in annual revenue, but not a defensible AI platform: useful, perhaps profitable, and still bounded by radiologist labor. I pass today. One result flips the decision: after closing the acquisition, Radley must publish an auditable production cohort showing at least 5x more studies per radiologist at non-inferior clinical quality and a fully loaded cost per read below the acquired practice’s pre-AI baseline.
