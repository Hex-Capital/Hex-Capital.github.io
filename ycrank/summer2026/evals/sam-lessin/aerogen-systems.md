# Aerogen Systems -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first problem is not contamination; it is that the deal is illegible. ID Ventures participated in an undisclosed May 1, 2026 seed round, but Aerogen Systems provides no valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is genuinely the first scarce check. I cannot distinguish a cheap, ignored hardware bet from a YC semiconductor story already priced for consensus. That uncertainty matters because hard-tech qualification consumes time and capital before pricing mistakes can be corrected.

The product does contain a potentially clean venture moment: prove that Talos can destroy airborne molecular contaminants inside a semiconductor tool, without consumables, and improve wafer yield enough to displace qualified filters and nitrogen purges. But the financing plan does not convert that thesis into an underwritable experiment. There is no named fab, dated qualification milestone, yield endpoint, or stated use of proceeds. “On-site evaluation, integration, and multi-system deployment” is a sales sequence, not a finite card flip.

The strongest evidence is the team’s ability to build the underlying machine. Noah Vogel and Colin Lavery spent two years on NASA-backed VOC removal, co-authored the 2024 technical report, produced multiple Talos generations, and report reaching 7N purity in 16 minutes and below 1 ppb VOC concentration. Vogel also worked in SpaceX manufacturing, while Lavery built gamma-ray imaging systems at H3D. This is more than résumé fit: they have shipped purification, sensing, and integrated hardware. What they have not yet shipped is a qualified semiconductor production system, which is the other side of the bridge.

The bull case is real and uncomfortable. Advanced packaging is making surface contamination more consequential, Talos is reportedly inside semiconductor tooling, and eliminating consumables could give fabs both yield improvement and lower operating cost. A validated reactor could become strategically interesting to Entegris or another incumbent with distribution and qualification infrastructure. But today the technical proof is a company-reported 30-minute PID run, the pilot customer is unnamed, and no evidence connects contaminant destruction to wafer yield, a purchase, or retained production usage. Patent-pending chemistry and possible future qualification data are not yet a proprietary workflow that causes customer action.

Deleting the next round breaks the model because there is no pricing, gross margin, burn, runway, service burden, or controllable cash metric. Custom site assessments and tool-specific integrations may produce a valuable industrial business, but they may equally create a slow engineering-services company facing Entegris, Camfil, and Purafil. The prior pivot from spacecraft air purification to industrial ducts and then semiconductor tooling shows adaptable technology; it also says the durable customer and economic container remain unfound.

I pass at the current evidence level. The verifiable flip condition is one named fab converting a Talos evaluation into a paid production deployment, with independently measured yield or consumables savings, disclosed hardware gross margin, and a seed-funded path to break-even—alongside round terms showing I am buying that proof before it becomes consensus.
