# Vouch -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition is Vouch’s strongest path. An established Indian wealth platform could buy Vouch to acquire an ongoing relationship with households that approve recurring mutual fund investments through its product. The memo records Vouch’s published AMFI distributor registration and reports a fund-house commission model. Vouch claims empanelment with 27+ fund houses. The reason to own the business would be to secure the future commission stream if Vouch can acquire and retain funded households more economically than the buyer could through its own product or a partnership. The memo leaves funded household counts, retention, and commission collections unquantified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Facts)

A concrete pre-seed milestone is a 12-month cohort of funded households with measured retention of commission-bearing assets and positive commission contribution after servicing costs. The announced early-access launch and reported $500,000 pre-seed make an initial cohort test plausible. Runway and the capital needed to reach a buyer-relevant scale remain open. The existing registration establishes a basis for current distribution activity. Continuity of the registration, fund-house agreements, and customer data access after a change of control is unverified. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The reported pre-seed provides evidence of company funding. The memo leaves demand for existing shares and seller eligibility unverified. A sponsor buyout lacks evidenced cash generation and debt capacity. An IPO lacks evidence of operating scale and reporting readiness. Dividends or buybacks lack evidence of distributable cash or a payout commitment. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under the illustrative terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted by 40% to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year target is $2.5 million gross before investor taxes. At 3%, it takes $83.3 million of distributable equity, or $88.3 million in enterprise value with illustrative net debt of $5 million. The equity hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% ownership and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. At 3%, a $25 million sale yields $600,000 (1.2×) and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×), using the same assumed debt and a simple pro rata allocation. The memo provides no supported sale price or actual holder terms to validate either outcome. If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale stays out of reach, and Vouch reinvests available cash for eight years without a secondary or payout, the holder realizes $0 and retains the shares. Any wind-down recovery depends on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

PowerUp Money already offers household fund aggregation, personalized rebalancing, and continuous AI monitoring. That makes the workflow itself a weak reason to buy Vouch. A buyer could build or partner unless Vouch proves it has a retained customer base worth owning. The memo also describes client-level segregation rules that constrain an advisory buyer’s ability to keep distribution commissions on the same client relationship. These are credible risks to buyer economics. The memo establishes neither a structural block to a distribution-led sale nor a supported price range that leaves the holder materially short, so the Pass gate remains open. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What level of retained commission contribution would make Vouch’s household book worth enough to a strategic buyer to clear the holder’s cash target? A measured 12-month cohort and a buyer valuation grounded in the economics of retaining those customers, with a credible path to preserve the commission stream after closing, would move the action to Reach out if the resulting proceeds could reach $2.5 million within eight years.
