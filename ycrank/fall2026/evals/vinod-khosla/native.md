# Native -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Native reports $2M ARR within six months. That is substantial commercial evidence for this stage. It establishes that businesses will pay to delegate marketing. It does not establish that success changes a consequential resource bottleneck by orders of magnitude. The $36.4B social-media-management market measures spending, while the dossier supplies no baseline for expertise displaced, labor saved, or additional businesses served. I can see the subscription business. I cannot yet calculate the consequence that would justify buying a highly uncertain technical option.

The potentially interesting mechanism is Norn: predict which ideas will work before paying to generate and publish them. Native documents training on acceptance and performance signals, and it has a deployed product rather than merely an architecture proposal. That earns limited technical credit. But the reported 5.5% engagement versus 2.9% for Briggs on Homes’ own posts is an observational comparison, below a 5–10x discontinuity even before addressing attribution. Native’s research acknowledges selection and survivorship biases. There is no controlled benchmark against Blaze or Jasper demonstrating that the prediction layer creates an advantage customers cannot obtain elsewhere.

The strongest argument against passing is that Native already has the machinery to discover such an advantage. Its reported 2,200 paying customers across more than 20 countries generate repeated decisions, edits, and outcomes. If those records teach Norn something generalizable about audience response, generation could become the inexpensive execution layer beneath a valuable prediction system. My broader investment pattern includes execution businesses; scientific novelty is not an absolute requirement. The exception requires evidence of an unusually expansive mechanism. Here, training opt-outs, unproven predictive lift, and Blaze’s comparable workflow leave that mechanism unresolved. Customer count establishes opportunity to learn; it does not establish that learning has occurred.

The founders have relevant operating knowledge. Bekken brings content production, Gråsberg brings AI/ML experience at DNV and Norconsult, and Hauga brings agency operations. They also wound down Wepost, which reportedly reached NOK80,000 MRR, and started Native in January 2026. That is a concrete change of direction. The dossier does not establish which evidence changed their beliefs or document difficult recruits brought in to own specific weaknesses. I retain the founder default rather than awarding learning-rate points for a pivot whose rationale is unknown.

The largest technical kill risk is whether Norn predicts useful outcomes beyond brand context and ordinary generation. No budgeted, threshold-based experiment addresses it in the dossier. Likewise, $79 monthly pricing demonstrates an offer, without establishing cost parity with human delivery after inference, publishing infrastructure, support, and customer review. These are missing evidence, not proof of bad execution or subsidy dependence. Native receives the corresponding defaults. A cheap subscription alone does not demonstrate cheap delivery.

I would pass with my own money. Native’s marketing purpose directly encounters my advertising-tool avoidance, and neither quantified consequence nor demonstrated technical advantage supplies an override. The existing commercial success makes this a serious opportunity for another investor; it does not resolve my decision gates. My one verifiable flip condition is an independently audited, randomized deployment showing Norn delivers at least 10x more customer-approved, revenue-producing output per fully loaded dollar than an agency baseline, with positive contribution margins. That would demonstrate an expertise cost collapse large enough to reopen the category judgment.
