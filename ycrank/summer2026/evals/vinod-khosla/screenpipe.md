# screenpipe -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

250,000 users make screenpipe a potentially consequential agent-memory substrate trapped inside a commodity-capital profile. At maximum ambition, screenpipe breaks the economic mechanism of humans repeatedly reconstructing context for software: every meeting, screen state, and workflow becomes persistent machine memory. That could matter across a meaningful fraction of the $15 trillion labor economy. But screenpipe currently monetizes that ambition as $25-$150-per-seat software built around SQLite, OCR, transcription, and APIs. The analytical signature is high potential consequence paired with almost no capital-hostile miracle.

**OpenAI** is the high rung on consequence because persistent machine intelligence could reorganize most economically valuable work. screenpipe shares a narrower version of that mechanism: its 250,000 users and sixfold active-user growth suggest that missing context is a real constraint on useful agents. screenpipe diverges where the analogy becomes decisive. OpenAI owned the intelligence layer; screenpipe supplies context to models owned by others, while Microsoft Recall, Granola, and Otter.ai can attack adjacent territory. “AI powered by everything you have seen” is a large ambition, but screenpipe has not yet shown that an incumbent economic category disappears rather than becomes more convenient.

**DoorDash** was low on my required-capital axis because consumer logistics required no scientific miracle; founder ambition and distribution overrode the mismatch. screenpipe repeats that capital-light pattern more sharply: $25 Standard subscriptions, $50 Pro seats, a local SQLite database, and no disclosed roadmap requiring $50 million before a commercial milestone. screenpipe could become another DoorDash-style exception, but 18,900 GitHub stars are not yet a capital deployment thesis. I am over-resourced for a business whose present bottleneck is conversion, retention, and enterprise sales.

18,900 GitHub stars can compound into a technical advantage only if screenpipe turns capture history into deployment-specific workflow infrastructure over the next 18 months. Each programmable pipe, policy, and integration could make screenpipe harder to remove because switching would mean abandoning accumulated operational memory. The dossier labels that moat as inferred, however, with no retention or migration evidence. screenpipe also publishes an auditable Rust/TypeScript stack that competitors can inspect, while Microsoft can bundle Recall at the operating-system layer. Five-to-ten percent CPU usage and 5-10 GB of monthly storage are engineering frictions, not the falsifiable technical miracles that repelled investors from **Commonwealth Fusion**.

Louis Beaumont’s French-intelligence and defense-AI background gives screenpipe credible founder-market fit, but credentials are not contrarianism; experts usually extrapolate the past. screenpipe earns some credit for choosing cross-platform, local-default capture while Granola, Otter.ai, and Limitless emphasized narrower or cloud-oriented products. The score flips into an invest tier only if Beaumont articulates and proves a disputed technical thesis—for example, that local multimodal histories produce materially better agent completion rates than cloud meeting memory while preserving enterprise security. The dossier contains no documented public break with expert consensus, so screenpipe remains a thoughtful architectural dissent, not yet a field-defying bet.

**Slash** earned an exception because its documented Kanye West pivot exposed unusually high learning rate. screenpipe offers weaker but real behavioral evidence: Beaumont created screenpipe in 2024, shipped 358 releases, changed the license from MIT to paid source-available terms, and produced sixfold active-user growth over two months. Those actions show iteration and commercialization speed. They do not document a prior pivot, a rejected thesis, or a specific “we were wrong” update. screenpipe therefore resembles rapid execution more than Slash’s demonstrated willingness to abandon an original model.

“Tens of thousands of dollars” in launch revenue leads me to predict that by July 2029 screenpipe will reach $8-$15 million ARR, with most revenue coming from Pro and Enterprise desktop-context deployments, but will remain a useful agent-infrastructure vendor rather than the intelligence layer itself. screenpipe can reach that range through open-source distribution, 18,900 GitHub stars, and $50-$150 seat pricing without solving capital-hostile science. The same low-capital signature that makes screenpipe efficient also caps its strategic depth unless workflow history becomes a proprietary control point.

250,000 users, hundreds of thousands of installs, and sixfold active-user growth form the strongest objection to my restraint. The bull mechanism is **DoorDash**: overwhelming distribution can create consequence after the original product appears technically ordinary, and screenpipe already has more pre-seed adoption than most companies ever achieve. That case stings because “tens of thousands” in revenue within weeks shows some conversion, not merely GitHub curiosity. The evidence that still wins is screenpipe’s absence of disclosed retention, migration friction, or structural protection against Microsoft Recall and meeting incumbents, compounded by sensitive-data capture and default telemetry. I would flip to Invest if screenpipe demonstrates at least $10 million ARR with greater than 120% net revenue retention and a majority of enterprise users running proprietary pipes weekly.
