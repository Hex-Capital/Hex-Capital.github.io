# Mosaic -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I need are missing: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. YC participation does not answer them. Mosaic may be cheap and ignored, or it may be another fashionable agent company sold through Demo Day urgency. Those are economically different securities. Without terms, I assign the financing gate its explicit low prior rather than pretending silence is evidence of scarcity.

The product at least identifies a real coordination failure. Engineering teams running Claude Code, Codex, and OpenCode across isolated sessions lose decisions and handoff context; Mosaic has built shared terminals, persistent state, and simultaneous human-agent control. But the financing plan names no finite experiment, metric, or deadline. “Defining multiplayer AI” is a category claim, not a venture moment. The obvious experiment would be whether shared control converts several independent engineering teams into retained $200-per-seat customers without creating an unacceptable credential-exfiltration surface. The dossier never says the round is organized around proving that.

The strongest bull case is the team. Dorsa Rohani has worked on distributed inference at Tenstorrent and conducted ML research at NVIDIA, Vector Institute, and Deep Genomics; Shubham Patil reports building collaboration interfaces for developer teams. Mosaic’s public demonstration was concrete enough to earn coverage from The Neuron and a detailed Oflight analysis in July 2026. This is not two tourists stapling a generic interface onto an API. The founders plausibly connect agent infrastructure with collaborative developer tooling, although the dossier does not document a shipped commercial result on both sides or identify which co-founder has final authority.

The harder problem is ownership. Approximately 5,000 X followers, a 2,500-star GitHub repository, two articles, and a free Hobby tier establish reach, not a dark pool that causes purchases. There is no reported paid customer, repeat team usage, retention, proprietary dataset, or embedded workflow. Meanwhile, Superset and Pentagon can extend toward shared collaboration, Conductor already has $22 million behind adjacent orchestration, and upstream CLI or permission changes can alter Mosaic’s product boundary. The translation layer fits a pattern I like; the absence of proprietary access means Mosaic does not yet own it.

The $200-per-seat Team price is potentially attractive, but a price page is not an economic model. There is no revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, support burden, or evidence that a two-person company can secure shared terminals while reaching break-even without another round. The relevant AI exception is Ando: both companies place AI inside an enterprise workflow, but Ando earned the exception through measured accuracy, complete pilot-to-paid conversion, and retention. Mosaic has product proof, not commercial proof. Its security exposure and platform dependency therefore remain red flags rather than risks neutralized by operating receipts.

I am passing today. This is a credible product built by relevant people, but I would be buying an undocumented round to finance an unnamed experiment in a converging market. My one flip condition is a signed sub-$10-million post-money round whose stated milestone is five unaffiliated teams each paying at least $1,000 per month and remaining active for 90 days. That would simultaneously establish scarce pricing, a finite truth-revealing test, and evidence that the shared workflow causes a purchase.
