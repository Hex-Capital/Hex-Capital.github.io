# Spacesheep -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A **strategic acquisition by an enterprise collaboration or developer-tool vendor** is the strongest path. Spacesheep could give a buyer a shared destination for reviewing work from Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor. The memo records a public launch and 28 Chrome extension users. The company showcases a lab dashboard and a live thermal-camera stream, while paying customers, repeat team use, and measured workflow gains remain undisclosed. The ownership case is conditional. If paying teams repeatedly review and revise outputs from multiple agents in Spacesheep, a buyer could acquire an established workflow and customer base that would take time to build. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A paid production cohort could test that milestone by tracking repeat cross-agent reviews and measuring time saved against native publishing. The launched product and reported $125,000 seed entry make a limited test plausible. The memo supplies no burn, runway, or committed pilot budget. A company sale is a plausible structure, with IP ownership, customer data transfer rights, and required consents unverified. The memo supports no independent route to cash for this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** Using the thesis’s illustrative $500,000 check with no follow-ons, 5% initial ownership dilutes to 3%. A 5× return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds under a simple pro rata waterfall. The hurdle moves to $125 million at 2% ownership and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume enterprise value equals equity consideration, with zero net debt, fees, or other closing adjustments. They assume cash at close, with stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation each at zero, and no preference adjustment. The memo supplies no buyer economics or acquisition pricing to substantiate either sale value. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Realized holder cash | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale at $40 million enterprise value | Year 5 | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | Year 7 | $3.0 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private ownership | Through year 8 | $0 | 0× |

At 2% ownership, even the modeled strong sale yields $2 million, or 4×. In the prolonged-private case, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale stays out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The investor retains the shares and receives no cash within the horizon.

### Countercase

Claude Artifacts and ChatGPT Sites already handle native publishing, Type.com connects Claude and Codex to a shared workspace, and Spacesheep’s Drive and GitHub exports reduce switching costs. A buyer could build the remaining review features or partner for them, limiting what it would pay to own Spacesheep. The memo leaves open whether sustained use across competing agents creates an asset worth buying and where that buyer’s price ceiling would fall. The evidence supports Watch and leaves the structural Pass override untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a plausible acquirer justify a purchase price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at the modeled 3% ownership? A buyer-side build-versus-buy analysis grounded in measured, repeat use by paying teams across agents would answer it. A supported range that clears the hurdle within eight years, after applicable terms and closing adjustments, would move the action to Reach out.
