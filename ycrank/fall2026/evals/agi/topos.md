### Company case

Path B, New economies, is the strongest path. The plausible new transaction is routine hypothesis triage for biology labs that currently leave lower-priority questions unreviewed because expert time is scarce. That buyer segment is a scenario assumption. The memo documents a research beta accepting access requests. Topos says Egregore can coordinate thousands of agents and emphasizes formalizing and checking their reasoning. Both founders co-authored a machine-checked mathematical result, which supports their ability to build verification tools. The decisive seed-stage evidence would be a representative trial showing that Egregore saves expert time at equivalent quality and that buyers still choose to pay Topos when a comparable alternative is available. The memo records no buyer commitment. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

The scale unit would be one researcher-accepted triage job. Contribution would be the price less inference and delivery labor, including retries, exception handling, and any validation Topos provides. The memo supplies no inputs for that calculation or evidence of repeat paid volume. Path A lacks evidence of a scarce capability Topos controls. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Coordination can degrade performance on sequential tasks, and formal checks still leave biological conclusions requiring empirical validation. If researchers spend the saved review time resolving exceptions or checking outputs, the new transaction may remain uneconomic. Acyclic already offers swarm infrastructure, and Edison’s Kosmos serves an overlapping research audience. That weakens the case for capturing value through orchestration alone. The memo leaves workflow-level substitution and net labor savings untested, so neither concern triggers the thesis override yet. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent labs repeatedly pay for Egregore to triage questions they previously skipped when a competing workflow using comparably capable models delivers equivalent quality? A paid trial should compare both workflows with the labs’ existing process, measure total expert time through review, retries, and validation, and give labs a real choice of provider on the next job. A material labor saving and repeat paid preference at a price that covers delivery costs would support rescoring for Reach out. Reliable evidence that review absorbs the savings or that buyers readily switch at parity would move the action to Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
