# Versine -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A consumer payments platform could buy Versine to add assistant-initiated person-to-person transfers. Versine describes a workflow that combines passkey approvals, standing payment rules, and bank claim links for recipients without accounts. Ownership could be worth a premium if recurring users rely on saved contacts and payment rules that an incumbent would struggle to reproduce or obtain through a partnership. The memo records 182 launch upvotes and 5+ Android downloads. Repeat usage, payment volume, and buyer interest remain unverified. *(The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)*

The operating milestone is sustained, unsubsidized repeat transfer volume with measured payment errors and payout reliability. The launch and the team’s payments and systems experience support an initial test. The $125,000 seed entry dates to the earlier sign-in product, so funding and runway for the payments milestone remain uncertain. Bridge handles identity verification and bank payouts, and the memo leaves open whether those arrangements would continue through an acquisition. The disclosed $1 instant payout fee supplies no evidence of distributable cash, and the memo identifies no independent route that could pay this holder. *(Founders & Team; Market & Traction; Key Risks; Key Facts)*

**Proceeds bridge.** The illustrative seed check is $500,000 at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash. At 3%, that takes $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume all-cash consideration at closing, zero net debt and transaction costs, and a simple pro rata waterfall with no senior preference deductions. Under those assumptions, enterprise value equals distributable equity proceeds. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation each contribute $0.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Cash to investor | Gross multiple |
|----------------------|--------|------------------|----------------|
| Modest sale at $30 million enterprise value | Year 5 | $900,000 | 1.8× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | Year 7 | $3 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private ownership with growth plateauing, buyers staying away, public scale out of reach, and cash reinvested | Through year 8 | $0, with shares still held | 0× realized |

The strong scenario clears the objective at 3% but yields $2 million, or 4.0×, at 2%. The memo provides no usable valuation evidence to establish either sale price.

### Countercase

Incumbents may be able to build assistant payments on top of their existing wallets and approvals. Stripe Link already supports assistant-authorized merchant purchases, and Venmo and Cash App have established person-to-person payment workflows. Versine’s evidence of launch attention could reflect novelty or its $100 sending incentive. If an incumbent can reproduce the consumer workflow or obtain it through a partnership, the ownership premium could disappear. The memo establishes a plausible substitute, while leaving the cost of replicating Versine’s specific workflow and any resulting price ceiling unresolved. The structural Pass gate is therefore unproven. *(The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)*
