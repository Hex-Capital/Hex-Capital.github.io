# Trepo -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a grocery marketplace or large grocer. The reason to buy would be access to consumption signals from households using their existing fridges. If those signals produce more repeat orders or higher contribution per household, a buyer could capture that value through checkout and fulfillment. That benefit remains an inference. Trepo describes a San Francisco beta that generates grocery carts from household consumption, and the memo reports grocery-partner pilots with undisclosed counterparties, terms, and results. The app’s 4.9 rating across 59 U.S. ratings shows favorable feedback from reviewers. The memo gives no cohort retention or incremental order economics. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The next milestone is a controlled grocer pilot with a retained cohort after Halo ships. The beta and March 2027 shipping target give Trepo a plausible sequence for reaching it. Production funding, device fulfillment costs, and funding for a larger pilot remain unverified. A purchase of the device and app business is plausible, with assignment of any retailer agreements and permission to transfer and use household data still open. The memo identifies no independent route to holder liquidity. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Using the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. Both sale scenarios below assume $5 million in combined net debt and deal adjustments, eligibility to sell the entire stake, a pro rata waterfall with no senior preference claims, and full cash payment at closing within eight years. The prices are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction pricing to support them.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M yields $35M distributable equity | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $5M yields $95M distributable equity | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private | Available cash is reinvested, with no eligible share sale or payout within eight years | $0 (0×) |

At the strong sale price, selling only half the stake would yield $1.425 million within the horizon. The scenario shows how a sale could clear the hurdle. Buyer willingness to pay and the holder’s ability to cash out remain open.

### Countercase

Instacart already offers purchase-history replenishment and has announced a recognition-to-order integration with Samsung. Cooklist embeds cart building directly in grocer websites. Those workflows could satisfy much of a buyer’s need without taking on Trepo’s hardware fulfillment or relying on households to keep inventory current. If Trepo’s consumption signals add little incremental profit, a grocer could use an existing workflow or partner for the feature. The memo establishes credible substitutes, but it leaves their performance against Trepo’s approach unresolved and provides no supported ceiling on sale proceeds. The Pass gate is therefore untriggered. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a grocer-backed pilot show that Halo’s consumption data generates enough sustained incremental contribution to justify buying Trepo? A post-shipment pilot comparing retained Halo households with a purchase-history-only group should measure incremental grocery contribution net of fulfillment and device costs. Move to Reach out if the lift and a credible rollout support a buyer price above the proceeds hurdle. Stay at Watch if the economics remain unresolved.
