# TrueMile -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a carrier or virtual carrier is TrueMile’s strongest route to holder liquidity. The memo describes a fleet solver that plans outbound and return loads together and dispatch agents that execute those plans. TrueMile reports that one dispatcher can manage 9× as many trucks. A buyer could justify owning the system if it can reproduce that operating leverage across its fleet and reduce empty miles after accounting for staffing and carrier costs. The memo provides early operating claims, with buyer interest and realized savings at buyer scale still unverified. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

The next milestone is repeat paid runs on a dedicated lane that show contribution margin after empty miles, manual approvals, and carrier costs. TrueMile reports $57,000 in freight booked over three weeks and solicited two dedicated-lane partnerships. That makes the test plausible at seed. Signed lane commitments, the working capital needed for the test, and available funding remain unknown. The founder-built solver is a plausible acquisition asset. A buyer would still need to verify IP ownership and establish how driver leases, customer contracts, and carrier obligations would be handled. (*Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, dilution from 5% to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership. The sale scenarios assume an eligible holder, an all-cash close by year eight, sale of the full stake, and a pro rata waterfall with no senior preference reducing the holder’s share. The memo supplies no TrueMile valuation or deal terms to anchor either price. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

| Scenario | Assumed proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|----------|-------------------------|----------------------|
| Modest sale | $55M enterprise value less $5M in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $50M for equity | $1.5M (3×) |
| Strong sale | $110M enterprise value less $10M in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $100M for equity | $3.0M (6×) |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested through year eight, with no funded payout or eligible share purchase | $0 |

Even the strong case falls short if the holder can sell only 80% of its stake at that price, yielding $2.4 million in cash. The adjacent Cargofy secondary establishes that such a transaction occurred in freight software. TrueMile-specific share demand, holder eligibility, and payout capacity remain unverified. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A carrier could assemble much of the workflow from existing providers. FleetWorks offers freight matching, CloudTrucks offers route planning and dispatch, and TrueMile still requires a person to approve each load and sign its rate confirmation. If manual work and carrier costs absorb the reported productivity gains, a buyer has less reason to acquire the integrated operation or pay enough to meet the holder’s hurdle. The memo leaves the scale of those costs and the realizable sale price unresolved, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Do repeat paid runs on dedicated lanes show enough net savings at a strategic buyer’s fleet scale to support a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? Move to Reach out if lane-level results and a buyer-side valuation grounded in those results support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity under the benchmark. Move to Pass if the strongest credible sale price leaves the holder materially short after deal adjustments and the waterfall.
