# Dreamscale Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest exit path is a strategic sale to a GPU cloud platform. The HUD integration gives Dreamscale a robotics workflow a platform could acquire to attract inference workloads. Dreamscale reports that it ran DreamZero 14B in 320 ms versus 350 ms for NVIDIA’s implementation, using half the GPUs. If that advantage holds across paid production fleets, acquiring the serving stack could be cheaper than building it. The memo provides limited evidence of that economic advantage and no evidence of buyer interest. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The HUD evaluation integration, company-reported excavator evaluation with Actor Labs, and A$100,000 grant support a path to a paid fleet trial. The milestone is sustained paid usage with acceptable tail latency and lower all-in serving costs than self-hosting. The memo leaves the trial budget, runway, and production economics unquantified. The robot-specific transport gives a buyer a plausible asset to acquire. IP assignment and customer consents remain unverified, and third-party checkpoint licenses could limit what transfers. Support for customer-supplied models reduces dependence on those licenses. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Using the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity at full participation. For illustration, assume both sales close within eight years, $5 million in net debt and deal deductions, and an eligible holder who sells the entire stake for cash and receives 3% of the remaining equity proceeds with no preference or escrow deductions. A **modest** $40 million enterprise-value sale leaves $35 million for equity and pays the holder $1.05 million (2.1×). A **strong** $100 million sale leaves $95 million and pays $2.85 million (5.7×). Selling half the stake in the strong case at the same price per share would realize about $1.43 million. These prices and terms are scenario assumptions. The memo’s posted usage rates establish pricing, with paid volume, margins, and a supportable sale price still unknown. In the **prolonged-private** case, reinvestment and no share sale yield zero realized cash. The memo gives no evidence of an eligible secondary, listing, or funded payout. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A GPU platform could build or partner for robotics serving, while fleet operators could choose overlapping hosted products or run compressed policies locally where offline operation matters. Dreamscale’s reported tail-latency improvement came from a single home Wi-Fi test with synthetic frames and simulated compute. A buyer could therefore value the stack as a feature acquisition, with proceeds closer to the modest case. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a ceiling below the holder’s objective. The unresolved production economics support Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production fleet demonstrate a sustained serving-cost advantage over self-hosting, at acceptable tail latency and reliability, large enough to support an acquisition that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Fleet usage and cost records, measured against a self-hosted baseline and used in a credible buyer build-versus-buy analysis, would answer it. A supported path to at least $83.3 million in distributable equity within eight years under the benchmark terms would move the action to Reach out.
