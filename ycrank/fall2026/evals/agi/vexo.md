# Vexo -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Vexo could make routine follow-up economical for professionals who let informal commitments lapse because reconstructing the conversation and entering it into an assistant takes too much time. Vexo describes a wristband that captures conversations through a paired phone, turns transcripts into searchable memory, and drafts email or calendar actions for approval. The potential labor saving is less reconstruction and manual entry. The relevant comparison is labor time and full cost per correctly completed follow-up, including consent, review, corrections, retries, and manual recovery when capture fails. The memo provides no measured comparison or evidence that users now complete follow-ups they previously skipped. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The memo documents a $199 preorder charged at checkout and refundable before shipment. The founder reports distributing prototypes to an undisclosed number of preorder customers. That gives some evidence of willingness to try the device, while repeat use and retained payment remain untested. The listed iOS app and the founders’ embedded systems and wearable experience support seed-stage execution. The rollout unit is one band per wearer. If the upfront price is the only revenue, it needs to cover hardware, fulfillment, support, and ongoing cloud transcription and processing over the device’s useful life. The memo gives no measured costs for that calculation. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Under the scenario assumption that rivals have equally capable models, Vexo’s best reason for users to stay is its permissioned conversation history, retrievable with source references inside assistants they already use. The connector is documented. Preference for it and switching costs from accumulated history are inferences. Bee already offers wrist-worn conversation capture and follow-up workflows, and Omi makes wearable software and an assistant connector available to competitors. Bystander consent, phone dependence, and time spent correcting transcripts or reviewing drafts could also erase the labor savings on informal follow-ups. The memo establishes credible substitution and delivery risks, while comparable outcomes and actual switching remain untested. Both Path B gates remain open, supporting Watch. The memo identifies no scarce input that Vexo controls for Path A. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Vexo win a paid field trial with professionals who routinely skip informal follow-ups? Compare their manual baseline with Vexo and a wearable alternative using equally capable models. Move to **Reach out** if Vexo materially lowers full cost per correctly completed follow-up, users repeatedly complete commitments they previously skipped, and they pay a price that covers delivery costs while choosing Vexo over the alternative. Move to **Pass** if representative results show that consent and correction work erase the savings or that users readily switch while preserving the same outcome.
