# Talos -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Talos’s strongest exit path is acquisition by a power-equipment manufacturer that could sell its retrofit monitoring across an installed fleet. Talos describes external sensors and machine-specific baselines that could extend an OEM’s monitoring beyond the oil-based diagnostics offered by GE Vernova and Camlin. Siemens Energy’s announced agreement to acquire Camlin shows that an equipment manufacturer will buy monitoring capabilities. The price was undisclosed. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Talos reports deployments across 40+ assets and partnerships with utilities, plant owners, and national laboratories. Those deployments give Talos a starting point for a paid fleet rollout that measures warning lead time, false alarms, and maintenance decisions against incumbent monitoring. Field performance, commercial terms, rollout costs, and funding remain unknown. The memo also leaves rights to reuse field data unverified and supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Under the benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale scenarios assume an eligible holder sells their entire stake for cash by year eight and receives 3% of the remaining equity proceeds after net debt and deal adjustments, with no preference claims ahead of them. The sale values and deductions are illustrative assumptions. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Holder cash |
|----------|-----------------|-------------|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $45M for equity | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M for equity | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Operations continue with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or distribution by year eight | $0 realized |

The strong case clears the hurdle by $200,000. Selling only 75% of the stake on the same terms would yield about $2.03 million. The memo provides no Talos revenue or priced acquisition comparable to establish a realizable sale range. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An OEM could add external sensors to its own monitoring platform, or decide that Talos’s alerts add too little to its existing diagnostics. Siemens Energy’s Camlin agreement gives it an established monitoring platform, and Talos’s reported deployments leave incremental fault detection and paid fleet adoption unproven. This supports Watch. The memo establishes neither a ready substitute for Talos’s full offering nor a sale-price ceiling that would trigger the Pass gate. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would the results of a paid fleet rollout support an OEM acquisition price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity value? Move to Reach out if the rollout documents actionable warnings missed by incumbent monitoring, quantifies the maintenance benefit, and supports a buyer-side valuation that clears the hurdle after deal deductions.
