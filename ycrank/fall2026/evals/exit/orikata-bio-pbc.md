# Orikata Bio PBC -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A research software platform such as Benchling could buy Noto to make the shared record of human and agent work part of its core product across local and hosted compute. Ownership could make economic sense if paying teams depend on those project histories and controlling the workspace delivers more value than an integration or an in-house build. That advantage remains a hypothesis. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Hosted runs are available, and the published $200 monthly Team plan establishes an asking price. Orikata reports more than 45 models and tools at launch and gives conflicting release windows for the full shared workspace. The operating milestone is repeat paid production use of the shared notebook, with a measured workflow benefit over incumbent tools. Hosted runs provide a starting point, while the memo gives no paid-use results, funded test, burn, or runway. It also supplies no evidence on whether the essential IP and data rights can transfer. No independent route to cash for this holder is evidenced. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The memo supplies no investment or acquisition terms, so the bridge uses the thesis’s illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3%, with no follow-on checks. In both sale scenarios, enterprise value equals distributable equity proceeds under the assumption of zero net debt and other adjustments. Consideration is all cash at close, allocated pro rata with no preference adjustment. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Cash to investor | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale at $50 million enterprise value | Year 5 | $1.5 million | 3× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | Year 7 | $3.0 million | 6× |
| Prolonged private ownership with stalled growth, buyers staying away, public scale out of reach, and cash reinvested | Through year 8 | $0, shares retained | 0× realized |

The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in holder cash, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. Even the illustrative $100 million sale misses at 2%. The memo gives no buyer economics or transaction prices to establish a plausible sale range.

### Countercase

Benchling already offers hosted model runs alongside existing lab records, and Phylo already offers collaborative research agents. A platform could build or integrate enough of Noto’s functionality to capture the customer benefit at a lower cost. The overlap weakens the reason to buy, but the memo leaves open whether those options can reproduce the value of a shared project history across compute environments. The evidence supports waiting for a measured result and leaves the structural Pass override untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a research software platform quantify enough incremental value from owning Noto’s shared workspace to justify a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A buyer-validated production comparison against the best build or integration option, with economics supporting about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds under the base assumptions, would move the action to Reach out to test price and transfer rights.
