# Graphide -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B, repeatable sales with account expansion,** is the strongest fit. The initial buyer is an engineering team whose coding agents repeatedly trigger slow GitHub Actions jobs. Graphide documents a GitHub App installation and repository migration workflow, followed by a sales quote for fixed monthly CI pricing. If that workflow proves repeatable, launch-driven inbound could bring similar teams into a trial, and a successful first migration could lead them to move more repositories onto Graphide. The memo gives no conversion or expansion records. (The Idea, Key Facts)

Graphide reports that unnamed teams switched runners and completed a product in a few days. The switch is an early signal that teams will change their workflow to address the problem. The memo leaves payment, ongoing use, and the size of the production benefit open. The same GitHub Actions workflow could serve buyers abroad, assuming eligible jobs run on the same setup and the buyer’s security and code-handling requirements can be met. The memo identifies no buyer in a second market. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

At the quoted monthly price, moving more jobs can add prewarmed runner capacity, tuning compute, and support costs before it adds revenue. Each new account also brings quoting and migration effort. The growth case assumes Graphide can reuse its optimization library to limit hands-on work and price larger accounts to cover their added load. The memo gives no rollout costs or account contribution data to test that assumption. (The Idea, Defensibility)

### Countercase

Graphide may be selling a hands-on migration and tuning service around a runner fleet. The CEO offers migration help, and Graphide’s benchmark hand-tuned 10 jobs on 64- or 128-vCPU machines while customer runners topped out at 32 vCPUs. If production gains require comparable tuning effort or excess capacity, each new repository could add substantial cost while the account stays on a fixed quote. That risk warrants Watch. The memo leaves open whether the standard rollout can deliver the benefit with limited manual work, so the structural cost gate for Pass is unmet. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent engineering team outside the U.S. go from the standard GitHub App trial to paid production use with a meaningful speedup and limited hands-on tuning? One rollout with a paid invoice, before-and-after timings on normal customer hardware, and recorded migration labor and runner costs would answer it. A rollout that delivers the speedup at a price that credibly covers the added costs would move the action to Reach out.
