# Aikido Technologies -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

The strongest path is bring-your-own-hardware colocation for neoclouds and AI labs waiting for firm onshore power. Assuming strong product-market fit, Aikido could grow by repeating its AO60DC platform for buyers with the same need across markets. Aikido's design pairs 10+ MW of IT capacity with onboard wind, storage, backup generation, and seawater cooling. The modular design gives Aikido a reusable core. The reported A1DC pilot permit shows progress at one site, while the work required to permit and deploy the design at another site remains uncertain. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

YC introductions and a direct inquiry form give Aikido a repeatable way to find buyers. Each buyer still needs a negotiated capacity, price, and delivery agreement, and first use depends on a physical deployment. Installed customer hardware could make colocation sticky because switching would require moving that hardware. The memo gives no billing cadence, renewal terms, or mechanism for monetizing expansion. Aikido reports prospective GPU renters and pilot progress. Payment status and realized revenue are unknown, so commercial growth is unscorable beyond the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

For a repeat platform, assume colocation is billed by contracted MW. The contribution bridge starts with contracted price times billable MW and subtracts platform capital recovery, installation, buyer onboarding, offshore maintenance, and backup generation over the same period. Customer-owned hardware limits Aikido's GPU capital needs in this scenario. The seawater cooling design could lower operating costs, but the memo supplies a design target and no measured cost savings. Billable utilization and firm capacity during low-wind periods could materially change contribution. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Every capacity increment could require another capital-intensive marine deployment with site-specific approvals and vessel-dependent servicing. Full-scale certification is still planned, and wind variability could reduce deliverable capacity or raise backup costs. This is the strongest structural downside case. The gate stays open because the memo does not establish that each market requires substantial redesign or that a repeat platform would be uneconomic under realistic contracted utilization. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a second site use the same AO60DC design to deliver paid colocation at positive incremental contribution? A binding capacity contract and operating results from that site would answer it if they show the redesign required, billable utilization, and actual deployment and servicing costs. Evidence that each new site requires substantial redesign, or that repeat deployments remain uneconomic at realistic utilization with no credible cost lever, would move Watch to Pass. A profitable repeat deployment with bounded site work would support rescoring for Reach out.
