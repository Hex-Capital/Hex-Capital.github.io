# Spectre Intelligence -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: longer-context, more persistent models now let a two-person team automate portions of public-equity research and execution, but Spectre Intelligence becomes a large firm only if its agents produce repeatable risk-adjusted returns after costs and alpha decay. That is the controlling variable. The dossier offers no return series, benchmark, drawdown, AUM, or threshold for establishing repeatability. Two live semiconductor and biotech pods and self-reported profitability are useful evidence, but they do not yet separate durable alpha from a small capital base, favorable variance, or discretionary human intervention.

The technical mechanism is more substantive than a generic trading wrapper. Spectre records decisions, reasoning traces, executions, and ground-truth P&L, then uses competition among agents to improve subsequent trading. Better models could strengthen this loop because the company owns the evaluation environment and outcome labels, not merely a formatted model response. But the team itself says LLM traders are not yet effective and identifies memory, learning, and long-running robustness as unresolved. Expanding context windows from 8,000 to 8 million tokens may enable more analysis; it does not by itself improve net returns, trading capacity, or margins. The GPT Ladder has moved, but the economically relevant rung remains unproven.

The strongest bull case is that live capital resolves uncertainty unusually quickly. Even undisclosed profitability is more informative than a waitlist, and every realized trade can create a labeled record unavailable from simulated benchmarks. Li brings biology research and personal trading context to the biotech pod; Zyzak brings reinforcement learning, AI engineering, and Optiver program exposure. Li taking leave from Harvard is also real commitment. If this team discovers a repeatable learning loop before the market crystallizes, the proprietary dataset and sector expertise could compound together.

The problem is that the same evidence cuts both ways. Spectre concedes that successful strategies will be copied and early alpha will decay, while Abundance has raised $100 million, Nof1 $15 million, Numerai $49 million, and established quantitative firms can access comparable models, market data, and execution systems. “Proprietary data” is therefore not yet a moat claim I accept: the dossier does not quantify the dataset, reproduction cost, performance advantage, or whether returns improve as more trades are added. Its compensated expert network may supply differentiated inputs, but it may also create a service-heavy research operation whose economics scale with people rather than software.

No known exception overrides that missing market proof. This is not a frontier-model company supported by uniquely scarce foundational research, and trading capital is abundant rather than structurally unavailable. I would not invest from my own money today. The verifiable flip condition is twelve consecutive months of monthly returns showing positive net alpha against stated semiconductor and biotech benchmarks, with disclosed drawdown and at least $10 million deployed, while returns remain positive as capital scales. That would demonstrate that the feedback loop—not narrative, leverage, or a small denominator—is producing the result.
