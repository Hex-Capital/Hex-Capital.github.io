# Central Blue -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with initial low-Earth-orbit customers, spacecraft operators in multiple markets could use the same HELION delivery system and CORE software for recurring power needs during eclipse. That portability depends on whether different spacecraft can integrate the dedicated receiver with bounded engineering work. The mission intake form gives operators a common way to submit power, location, and timing requirements. Those inputs point to human-led delivery planning, and the receiver adds work before first use. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

Repeated eclipse exposure and an installed receiver could support repeat purchases. One scenario is that operators contract for more delivery windows as their power needs grow. Contract pricing and repeat purchases are unverified, so expansion sufficient to offset churn remains speculative. The two-person team reported a nearly complete prototype and a planned ground propulsion demonstration. The memo leaves revenue and paid production use unknown, so the prototype establishes development progress while the commercial traction score uses the default. (*The Idea*, *Founders & Team*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

Shared orbital capacity offers the strongest scaling path. If CORE can coordinate handoffs and existing HELION nodes can serve multiple operators, the next contract could add revenue without an immediate launch. Incremental contribution would be the mission fee less receiver integration, mission planning, operations and support, and any additional orbital capacity the contract requires. HELION’s stated target of 10 kW for four to five hours per full discharge is a design claim. The memo gives too little pricing, cost, or operating evidence to establish contribution, so cost economics uses the default. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Dedicated receivers and mission-specific delivery windows could require custom integration for each spacecraft and additional orbital capacity for each meaningful increase in demand. The announced ground propulsion demonstration leaves orbital delivery and shared capacity unverified. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo leaves room for a common receiver design and shared HELION capacity to serve multiple operators. A repeat deployment can test that path. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)
