# hiloop -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Karan Brar led ML infrastructure at DynamoAI, while Thomas Boser built production ML systems at Discord, SoFi, Crosswise, and Reducto. hiloop is a specific-knowledge-rich builder tool with a network-poor business architecture: the founders have lived inside the problem, but each customer’s experiments remain economically independent. hiloop looks more like expert infrastructure sold to AI teams than a platform whose users compound one another’s value.

**Stack Overflow** is the high rung for specific knowledge: developers built for developers, and the founders’ lived expertise became the product. Brar’s infrastructure work and Boser’s production ML history put hiloop near that rung; 4,188 experiments across 50 B200 GPUs show that hiloop can ship a difficult system, not merely describe one. The divergence is public proof of obsession and pull: Stack Overflow’s community existed in the open, while hiloop’s seven public repositories have no more than one star each and its core monorepo remains private. Specific knowledge is present. Permissionless evidence around that knowledge is not yet present.

**Perplexity** exposes the low network rung: a single-sided product can succeed, but one user does not automatically improve the next user’s experience. hiloop currently has the same weakness in a harsher form—one customer’s hosted or customer-cloud campaign does not clearly attract, subsidize, or improve another customer’s campaign. hiloop could accumulate task-to-experiment outcome data, but the dossier labels that moat inferred and provides no retained dataset or customer volume. Perplexity earned an exception through top-quartile distribution, founder specificity, and pre-consensus timing; hiloop has the founder specificity, but not yet the distribution evidence required to invoke that exception.

hiloop exposes snapshots, forks, restores, SDKs, CLI installers, SQL-queryable costs, and one-trace observability. Over 18 months, those code primitives can compound by making every new optimization campaign cheaper to reproduce and easier to automate. Yet hiloop currently acquires users through founder-led campaigns, offers on-premises deployment, and demonstrated the product with 50 B200 GPUs for two days. Code is doing the work, but labor and capital are still doing the distribution. hiloop becomes true permissionless leverage only when developers can adopt the SDK, run useful campaigns, and invite collaborators without a founder or enterprise deployment process in the loop.

$223.5 billion of projected AI-infrastructure spending by 2030 gives hiloop room to pivot across post-training, evaluations, inference optimization, prompts, tools, and experiment orchestration. hiloop scores well on pivot-room because its primitives—compute, lineage, secrets, and reproducibility—are horizontal rather than tied to one model or industry. The score flips down a tier if customer demand proves limited to bespoke autoresearch campaigns: beating a cited benchmark from 0.9109 to 0.9016 validation bits-per-byte is technically credible, but it does not establish that a broad market will pay for the general platform.

**Twitter** passed the long-term-partner test because Jack Dorsey shipped and Ev Williams had already completed Blogger’s journey cleanly. Brar and Boser show intelligence and energy through hiloop’s working infrastructure, the 4,188-experiment campaign, and Boser’s founding-engineer and production-ML history. hiloop provides little evidence about how either founder handles conflict, prior colleagues, or stewardship over a decade, so integrity remains at the absent-evidence default rather than becoming a negative. Intelligence can be demonstrated in a benchmark. Integrity compounds only through time.

4,188 experiments and a 12.54% NanoGPT runtime improvement support a falsifiable forecast: within 36 months, hiloop will pivot from generalized “recursive self-improvement” infrastructure into an enterprise agent-evaluation and optimization control plane producing $3 million to $8 million in ARR. hiloop’s founder-specific knowledge can build a valuable technical product, while hiloop’s weak network mechanics push the outcome toward recurring infrastructure revenue rather than marketplace-scale compounding.

Prime Intellect’s 6,000 reported customers, Together AI’s $1.21 billion of announced funding, and hiloop’s one-star repositories form the strongest bear case: incumbents bundle the features, founder-led enterprise campaigns remain labor leverage, and 50-B200 demonstrations become expensive technical theater without organic adoption. hiloop survives that objection because the founders have operated these systems directly and have already produced measurable results across thousands of experiments; the product is technically real before the distribution is proven. I would write a small portfolio check, not double down. If hiloop cannot show three unrelated customers repeating campaigns without founder-operated setup by July 2027, I flip the verdict to Pass.
