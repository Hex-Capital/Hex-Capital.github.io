# Databuddy -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Databuddy’s strongest exit path is a strategic acquisition by an analytics platform. Databuddy describes a workflow that combines traffic, conversion, errors, and payments into scheduled Slack investigations with inspectable queries. A buyer could use that workflow to help customers diagnose conversion drops faster and give them a reason to expand their use of the platform. That benefit is an inference. The memo reports 38 active subscriptions and $3,345 in company-wide monthly recurring revenue. Those figures show early paid adoption and leave retention and the value of scheduled investigations unresolved. Reported funding of more than $560,000 provides a basis for testing repeated use and renewal in a paid cohort. Runway and the cost of scaling remain unknown for the solo founder. *(The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team)*

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The 5× gross cash objective requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale scenarios assume $5 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, an eligible holder selling the entire stake for cash at closing within eight years, and pro rata distribution without preference leakage.

| Scenario | Enterprise value | Distributable equity | Holder cash |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|-------------|
| Modest sale | $25 million | $20 million | $600,000 (1.2×) |
| Strong sale | $100 million | $95 million | $2.85 million (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private operation with cash reinvested | No sale | No distribution | $0 realized |

The scenarios illustrate the hurdle. The memo provides no transaction pricing or buyer economics to support the strong case. The disclosed privacy controls leave acquisition consents and data-transfer rights unverified. An eligible secondary or funded payout could change the prolonged-private outcome, but the memo establishes neither. *(Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)*

### Countercase

Mixpanel already offers proactive metric monitoring, and PostHog exposes analytics to AI agents on data it already holds. Either could cover enough of the workflow using its existing customer data. The memo identifies no current moat, and the proposed switching costs remain untested by retention data. That weakens the case for paying an acquisition premium. The evidence leaves room for a buyer to value the combined workflow and establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible sale range that falls short of the hurdle, so the Pass gate does not trigger. *(Defensibility, The Idea, Key Risks)*

### Decisive diligence question

Can Databuddy’s scheduled cross-stack investigations create enough value for an analytics platform to justify an acquisition above the roughly $88 million enterprise-value hurdle in the illustrative bridge? Repeated use and renewal in paid deployments, paired with a plausible buyer’s estimate of the resulting retention or expansion value, would move the action to Reach out if the economics support that price.
