# FlowManual -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I start with three blanks: no disclosed post-money valuation, no competing-term-sheet count, and no evidence that the $500K YC check was scarce rather than standardized capital. FlowManual may be cheap, but the dossier does not prove it. More importantly, it never names the card this money flips. “AI for construction’s back office” is a category; drawings, contracts, quotes, invoices, takeoffs, bid leveling, and change orders are eight experiments pretending to be one.

The attractive mechanism is real. Contractors can lose $5K–$10K from a missed clause, and FlowManual puts scope, price, deadline, and exclusion changes back on the source line. Retaining each contractor’s vendor-price and bid history could create a proprietary workflow that improves with use without pooling customer data. That is the right shape of dark pool. Today, however, it is architecture rather than evidence: hundreds of interviews, 202 LinkedIn followers, and a free plan do not show that anyone repeatedly used the product, replaced a manual process, or purchased Pro.

The strongest bull case is speed. David Bae and Michael Lin began building on April 18, 2026, interviewed hundreds of construction professionals within two months, and shipped a broad product with source citations, audit logs, tenant isolation, and optional local parsing. Michael also has a software operating receipt from work associated with Dijie’s growth from $3K to $336K MRR. A narrow bid-to-contract wedge could become embedded before larger competitors reorganize around purchasing. But the known AI exception requires measured conversion, retention, and customer economics—not merely a plausible workflow. FlowManual has disclosed none of those.

The founder bridge is incomplete. The team understands engineering and document AI, but neither founder has worked inside a contractor’s estimating, procurement, or project-accounting operation. Interviews provide vocabulary, not operating access. This matters because Document Crunch already claims 500-plus companies and is being acquired by Trimble, while Trunk Tools has raised $70M. FlowManual is using Anthropic and Azure inputs available to those incumbents, with SOC 2 Type II and penetration testing still unfinished. The outsider perspective is present; the insider distribution and workflow authority are not.

Delete the next round and the underwriting disappears. Custom Pro pricing establishes a monetization surface, not revenue, gross margin, burn, or a route to break-even. The all-feature free tier may accelerate learning, but it may equally produce document-processing costs and support obligations without purchase behavior. My 36-month base case is a pivot into a narrower bid-to-contract comparison tool rather than an independent construction back-office platform, because breadth without proprietary usage compounds validation burden faster than advantage.

I am passing with one clean flip condition: three named contractors must pay for FlowManual and repeatedly run the same bid-to-contract or buyout-to-invoice workflow for 90 days, with documented labor savings and positive gross contribution. That would simultaneously identify the venture moment, demonstrate owned workflow conversion, and show that this is more than an Anthropic interface chasing a fashionable construction-AI market.
