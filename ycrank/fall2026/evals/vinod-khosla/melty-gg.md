# Melty -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Melty makes game mashups easier to create and play. What becomes radically cheaper if it succeeds? The dossier establishes installation friction, but supplies no baseline for creation costs, developer hours displaced, or people newly able to build games. Automating dependencies and multiplayer invitations is useful. The consequential possibility is turning players into creators across existing game worlds. That possibility remains unquantified; I cannot price a world-changing option from a download button alone.

The technical distinction matters. Melty launches owned games and connects creators to external coding agents; Nexus Vortex already handles mod installation and collections, while mod.io supplies studio-integrated infrastructure. Cross-game composition could require substantial engineering. There is no measured comparison establishing that advantage, however, and the proposed compatibility-data learning loop is explicitly inferred. Contractual catalogue exclusivity—including twelve months after removal—restricts distribution without demonstrating superior technology. I leave room for undisclosed technical work, but assign no breakthrough credit to it.

The strongest objection to passing is that Melty already has a product and an audience around a potentially powerful creation platform. The reported Discord membership is 10,611, albeit from a third-party snapshot, and David Miller’s documented gaming-coaching background gives him relevant audience knowledge. A small technical community can expose a platform opportunity before revenue appears; my actual investing pattern allows that. But membership does not establish committed creators, repeated publishing, or user retention. Miller’s background also does not document belief revision or recruiting against a specific weakness. The exception for an extraordinary instigator attacking a giant existing value pool remains possible, but this dossier does not establish it.

The largest immediate uncertainty is whether mashups can work reliably across other people’s machines with durable publisher permission. Creator-PC execution checks do not resolve that risk. Melty acknowledges compatibility limitations and publisher restrictions, but the dossier supplies no costed experiment, numerical release threshold, or stop condition. I would spend the next dollars on that question. The privacy allegations remain unverified and earn no adverse judgment here; the disclosed launcher-window recording policy still makes user trust a concrete operating concern. Gaming itself is not the gambling category I avoid.

The app and current mashups are free, with future payments contemplated but no prices or delivery-cost model. That is normal at pre-seed; it establishes neither permanent subsidy dependence nor competitive economics. My pass rests on an unproven consequence and mechanism, rather than missing revenue. I would reopen the decision after one independently auditable, publisher-authorized deployment demonstrates a tenfold reduction in fully loaded cost per reliably playable mashup against a documented manual/Vortex workflow. Count creator labor, compatibility repairs, and support. Until then, Melty offers a credible product experiment without enough evidence for my personal check.
