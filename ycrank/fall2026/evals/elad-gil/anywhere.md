# Anywhere -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Remote agent integrations let Anywhere offer consumers a personal map that assistants can update; the economic variable is whether that persistent context produces recurring use that Anywhere can monetize. My controlling belief would be that people will maintain a separate location record because agents make it useful between trips. The dossier establishes the product mechanism, but supplies neither a usage threshold nor evidence that this behavior is forming. I would not confuse a plausible investment hypothesis with a validated market insight.

The May 2025 introduction of remote MCP integrations creates a genuine opening. Anywhere can expose places, notes, visits, and trips to assistants without building every conversational interface itself. But standardized access also lowers the integration barrier for competing products. Mapstr already addresses saved places, and Wanderlog addresses collaborative itineraries. Anywhere needs agent access to change how frequently people use personal maps, rather than simply add another interface. There is no demonstrated improvement in acquisition cost, willingness to pay, or delivery economics yet. The free iPhone app establishes availability, not an economic step change.

The strongest bull case is that Anywhere owns useful state underneath increasingly capable models. Its documented API allows connected agents to read and modify the map, while permissions and connection revocation give the product responsibility beyond generating recommendations. Better models could make that accumulated context more actionable. This fits my pattern of backing infrastructure around a technology discontinuity and provides affirmative evidence against the simplest wrapper objection. However, the dossier does not establish an operational feedback loop, repeated task completion, or how expensive that personal context would be to recreate. I would give the architecture credit without awarding a presumed data moat.

The immediate weakness is product pull. Anywhere launched publicly on November 1, 2026; four App Store ratings averaging 2.0/5 are far too few to estimate retention. Still, a reported verification failure that prevents entry is concrete adverse evidence, and incomplete map graphics show execution remains uneven. The November 5 connector listing establishes another entry point, with no evidence of usage. I would not demand revenue history from a four-day-old launch, but these observations cannot support a claim that customers already tolerate rough edges because the underlying utility is indispensable.

Delaroche makes the opposing case substantially stronger: mapping work at Zenly, rebuilding Life360’s map experience, and co-founding amo put this product directly inside his experience. That could support a move toward persistent location infrastructure for agents if the consumer interface proves secondary. But relevant employment does not establish costly commitment, and the dossier gives no specific lesson from the prior startup. The known exceptions do not resolve the market gate: Anywhere has no documented frontier research breakthrough, scarce model-building asset, or reconstructed industry economics that would justify overriding weak demand evidence. I would pass with my own money today. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: an independently inspectable cohort of at least 100 activated users in which 40% still complete a real planning task through an agent reading or updating their Anywhere map in week 12. That is my proposed test of the controlling belief, not a result the company has reported.
