# Limnion -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is Limnion’s strongest path. Siglake gives incident-investigation agents read-only access to logs and traces stored in a customer’s own cloud. ServiceNow is a plausible acquirer because it previously bought Era Software, Persen’s log-management company. An incident-management vendor could pay for the team, managed deployment capability, and customer relationships if owning them accelerated paid adoption and generated more revenue than integrating the open-source project. That is an inference. The memo establishes the product and an adjacent acquisition, while Limnion describes managed deployment as still in development. It supplies no measured buyer benefit, buyer engagement, or usable transaction price. (The Idea, Founders & Team, Market & Traction)

A route-changing milestone would be a paid, multi-tenant production deployment that passes endurance and failover tests and measures telemetry costs and incident-investigation performance against the customer’s existing stack. The shipped release and company-run AWS tests at 1 TB and 2.02 billion rows provide a starting point. The release notes are more directly relevant to production readiness. They record a failed 72-hour endurance test and pending failover and tenant-isolation qualification. The reported $500,000 convertible note is a financing signal. Its terms, cash remaining, and adequacy to fund the milestone are unknown. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** In the absence of actual entry terms, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3%. The equity hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. At 3% with illustrative net debt of $5 million, the enterprise-value hurdle is $88.3 million.

| Eight-year scenario | Cash to holder at 3% |
|---|---:|
| $25 million enterprise-value sale less $5 million of illustrative net debt | $600,000 (1.2×) |
| $100 million enterprise-value sale less $5 million of illustrative net debt | $2.85 million (5.7×) |
| Adoption plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested | $0 realized, with shares still held |

The sale cases assume cash at closing, simple pro rata allocation before investor taxes, and no additional fees or preference deductions. If the reported note converted, its effect would run through ownership. If it remained outstanding and $500,000 of principal were repaid at closing, that principal alone would reduce gross holder cash by $15,000 at 3%, to the extent it was outside modeled net debt. The memo gives neither the note’s treatment nor a basis for estimating wind-down residual after obligations and preferences. The sale prices are sensitivities. (Market & Traction)

An equity sale is a conceivable structure, with IP ownership, transfer consents, and closing terms unverified. The memo supplies no predictable cash generation to support a sponsor buyout or funded payout, no eligible secondary demand or terms, and no operating scale or reporting readiness to support an IPO. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Snowflake’s Observe already offers OpenTelemetry ingestion and Iceberg storage in a customer-owned bucket. Siglake’s Apache-2.0 license lets other vendors reuse its code, and customers control the underlying telemetry. A buyer could integrate the software or use an existing stack without paying for Limnion. That weakens the case for ownership, especially while production reliability and paid adoption remain unproven. The memo leaves open the possibility that a proven deployment business would be worth buying. It establishes neither a structural block nor a supported sale price that leaves the holder materially short, so the Pass gate does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production deployment show enough value for a specific acquirer to justify buying Limnion at a price that clears the holder’s return hurdle? A deployment that passes endurance and failover tests, measures customer gains against the incumbent stack, and supports a buyer’s estimate of incremental revenue or avoided costs would answer it. Move to Reach out if that evidence supports ownership over integration alone and a plausible range clearing the $83.3 million distributable-equity hurdle at 3%. Stay on Watch if the deployment establishes reliability but leaves buyer economics unpriced.
