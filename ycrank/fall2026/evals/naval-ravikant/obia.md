# Obia -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Obia’s most useful evidence is the work it reports doing for a logistics operator: zoning screening, site combinations, and throughput per dollar of rent. Those are operational questions a generic property search misses. They suggest learning through action. But the October 23 engagement is unnamed, and the dossier does not establish which founder performed the work. Samuel Cho’s engineering background and Jason Henkel’s conversational-AI work provide relevant capabilities; neither establishes deep knowledge acquired inside commercial brokerage. I give the team some execution credit, without treating adjacent experience as specific knowledge already proven.

The decisive question is what Obia owns after completing the research. Its website reports accumulated tenant and buyer requirements, but establishes neither exclusive access nor a demonstrated improvement in matching. If a competitor copied the software tomorrow, the dossier identifies no durable asset that would keep customers with Obia. Crexi’s May 2026 release already puts overlapping research automation into existing brokers’ hands. Faster research can improve a brokerage’s margins. It does not automatically make the brokerage harder to compete with.

The strongest bull case is that Obia can earn its advantage through transactions. Industrial requirements are complicated; representing tenants could reveal demand that public listings miss. The same research capability could serve office tenants and property owners, giving the initial wedge room to expand. This fits my preference for making fragmented offline supply easier to discover. But the $292.6 billion market proxy includes property management, so it cannot establish the size of Obia’s reachable opportunity. And a database becomes a network only when one participant makes the service more useful for another. Tours and negotiations still require brokers; no matching, referral, or contribution evidence establishes that customer-created value yet.

Obia does have an intelligible payer: landlords fund representation that is free to tenants. That is a credible route to direct value capture, although no commission rate or completed fee-paying transaction is disclosed. My exceptions for uncertain monetization depend on an emerging network, unusually revealing founder behavior, or a demonstrated product with exceptional capital efficiency. The dossier does not establish those overrides. CoreNet sponsorship creates an opportunity to meet customers, but supplies no evidence of proprietary distribution. The discrepancy between Obia Inc. branding and sole-proprietor terms needs clarification; it is not evidence of dishonesty. No documented integrity, pressure-selling, or founder-behavior red flag supplies a veto here.

I pass because Obia currently offers more evidence of useful service delivery than of an asset that compounds. A successful brokerage remains a plausible outcome, and that is the strongest objection to passing. For my own money, I need evidence that success makes the next transaction structurally easier. My flip condition is a measured cohort showing that accumulated customer requirements generate completed, commission-paying matches with declining broker hours per transaction as volume grows. That would demonstrate that Obia’s work is creating something beyond the next assignment.
