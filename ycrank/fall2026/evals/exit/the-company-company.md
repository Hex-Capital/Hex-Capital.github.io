# The Company Company -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by an agent or workflow platform. The Company Company’s shared agent sessions, persistent roles, and ticket-to-pull-request workflow could give a buyer a faster way to add collaborative engineering workflows. The public beta is live, and one named customer reports using the product for months to coordinate several agents. The founder reports more than 20,000 pull requests shipped internally. The memo gives no measured customer savings or evidence that a buyer would pay to acquire the workflow. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The next milestone is sustained use across multiple paying teams, with measured reductions in engineering cycle time. The live beta and customer account give the company a starting point. YC backing is confirmed, but the round size, runway, and cost of reaching the milestone are unknown. The enterprise customer-hosted deployment option supports a plausible transfer of the software. IP ownership, contract assignment, and rights to transfer or use customer workspace data remain unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity. The sale cases below are illustrative. Both assume an eligible holder sells the full stake for cash within eight years, with a pro rata waterfall and no senior preferences, escrow, or deferred consideration.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $35M for equity | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less the same $5M leaves $95M for equity | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or payout | $0 |

At the strong sale price, more than about $16.7 million in combined deductions and senior claims would put the holder below the objective. The memo provides no transaction pricing or investor terms to support either sale case, and no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

OpenAI and Gumloop already offer overlapping agent execution or shared context. Either could extend its own product, weakening the case for paying an acquisition premium. The customer testimonial shows use, while the memo leaves paid retention and workflow savings unverified. Workspace data restrictions could also limit what a buyer can use after closing. These risks support Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible price ceiling below the holder’s objective, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can the ticket-to-pull-request workflow deliver repeatable savings across retained paying teams at a scale that gives a strategic buyer an economic reason to acquire it? Measured cycle-time or labor savings across multiple teams, combined with buyer economics that plausibly support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity within eight years, would move the action to Reach out. If credible buyer economics put even the strongest sale case materially below that hurdle, move to Pass.
