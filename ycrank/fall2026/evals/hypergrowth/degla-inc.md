# Degla Inc -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with search-and-rescue teams, Degla’s strongest scaling path would be to embed its autonomy software in a drone manufacturer’s aircraft and reuse that integration across operators and markets. The memo records an October field demonstration in Chula Vista. Degla says its Jetson-based system turns spoken objectives and camera input into flight plans and replans onboard. The recurring mission-planning job plausibly transfers across markets, and the onboard software is a reusable component. The work required to adapt it across aircraft, languages, and flight authorization regimes remains unclear. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Degla recruits manufacturers and agencies through direct outreach and scheduled flight demos. The demo is a reusable sales step, while the documented path to deployment involves hands-on work. An installation can support repeated missions on the same aircraft, giving operators a reason to keep using it. Recurring billing and paid fleet expansion remain unverified. The October demo and November launch establish a product timeline for the two-person team. Paid production use, realized revenue growth, and pre-revenue status are all unknown, so initial traction takes the default. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under an assumed per-aircraft OEM license, incremental contribution would be the license revenue less attributable sales, integration, flight validation, support, and any hardware Degla supplies. Onboard inference could limit Degla’s cloud cost per mission. Price and deployment effort are unknown, leaving contribution unquantified and cost economics at the default. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The strongest case for passing is that real deployments could require repeated aircraft integration, flight validation, and operator-specific authorization even after the product proves valuable. That could make growth depend on Degla’s deployment labor and constrain access across markets. The memo identifies integration and validation work and specific U.S. authorization constraints. The structural downside gate stays open because the extent to which a manufacturer could reuse one integration and handle subsequent deployments remains unresolved. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can one drone manufacturer reuse the same Degla integration for paid production deployments across two markets with limited additional work from Degla? A second-market rollout with documented software changes, authorization work, deployment hours, and contribution would answer it. Reuse with positive contribution would support rescoring for Reach out if the score reaches 65 and confidence rises to Medium or High. Evidence that each market requires substantial rebuilding without a credible near-term transfer path would change the action to Pass.
