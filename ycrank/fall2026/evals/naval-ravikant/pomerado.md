# Pomerado -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Akshay Manglik has worked where this problem occurs: deploying enterprise agents at Scale AI, including Mayo Clinic deployments. Pomerado’s public implementation supplies another artifact of action. The founders have built a system that explores websites, generates operations, tests them, and executes the saved integrations without further model calls. That is meaningful specific knowledge. Alan Ma’s enterprise AI strategy experience adds context, although the dossier establishes less direct implementation experience for him.

The investment turns on what accumulates after that implementation works. Pomerado publishes its core under an MIT license. Browser Use offers reusable scripts, Browserbase offers script generation, and Skyvern describes compilation and repair. A competitor can acquire much of the mechanism cheaply. Hosted authentication and maintenance could become valuable operating assets, but the dossier establishes no exclusive integration corpus, proprietary model, or demonstrated switching cost. I would be buying exposure to the team’s next advantage before seeing the first durable one.

The strongest argument for writing a check is that the founders understand an expensive deployment bottleneck and have attracted developers quickly. By October 27, 2026, the repository displayed **1.1k stars and 100 forks**; the October 16 Product Hunt launch ranked **#5**. Those signals make dismissing the product premature. My exceptions for an unusually strong prototype deserve consideration here. But the record does not establish exceptional capital efficiency, unusual recruiting ability, or a forming customer network strong enough to override the competitive structure.

Pomerado also fits part of my preference for tools that let individuals turn knowledge into scalable output. A developer can package a website operation for agents to reuse. The missing link is between participants: there is no evidence that one customer’s integration or repair improves another customer’s experience. Stars measure attention; forks establish the ability to build independently. Neither establishes a contribution loop. The same capability could serve insurance, healthcare, logistics, and internal operations, giving the wedge room to expand, although adjacent purchasing demand remains unquantified.

The **$50 monthly Premium plan** gives Pomerado a direct monetization mechanism. No paying customer, completed transaction, or realized margin is documented, so I give the posted price limited credit. Maintenance will determine whether subscriptions produce scalable profit: third-party website and authentication changes can interrupt integrations, and repair obligations could grow with deployments. The disclosed HIPAA and authorization restrictions are operating constraints. The dossier supplies no evidence of an integrity violation, artificial financing pressure, or founder instability.

I pass with my own money today. The founders’ experience makes a useful product credible; the available architecture leaves ownership of the resulting value unsettled. My verifiable flip condition is a production cohort showing that customer-contributed integrations or repairs measurably reduce failures for unrelated paying customers without proportional maintenance labor. That would demonstrate the compounding mechanism Pomerado currently lacks.
