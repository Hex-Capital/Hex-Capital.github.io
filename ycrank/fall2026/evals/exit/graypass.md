# GrayPass -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

GrayPass’s strongest liquidity route is a strategic sale to an identity or fraud platform. The memo documents signed proof tied to a specific application action, which could help a buyer protect payouts, exports, and permission changes within an authenticated session. LexisNexis’s acquisition of BehavioSec shows that a platform has bought an adjacent capability. GrayPass’s incremental value to a buyer remains an inference because the memo provides neither measured production benefit over existing tools nor a price for the adjacent deal. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

GrayPass reports more than 900,000 sessions and disclosed a $100,000 investment. Those signals support a seed-stage plan to move selected workflows from shadow testing into paid production enforcement and measure the results. The behavioral signals are experimental, customers must integrate enforcement into each workflow, and the memo leaves the test budget and runway open. A software acquisition is plausible, with customer contract assignment and rights to transfer or continue using customer-scoped biometric data still unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility)

The memo supplies no deal or investor terms, so the proceeds analysis assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. GrayPass’s reported $50,000 of October revenue is an early commercial signal. Its recurrence and relevance to an acquisition price remain unknown. (Key Facts)

| Scenario | Illustrative bridge | Gross cash to holder within eight years |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in debt and deal adjustments leaves $35M in distributable equity | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M in distributable equity | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested and no eligible share sale or funded payout occurs | $0 (0×) |

Both sale cases assume a full cash sale within eight years, holder eligibility, and a pro rata waterfall with no senior preference reducing the holder’s share. If only 90% of the strong-case entitlement is paid within the horizon, realized cash falls to $2.43 million. The strong case clears the hurdle under those assumptions, but the memo leaves that sale price unsupported and identifies no independent liquidity route.

### Countercase

An incumbent could replicate action-bound proof or serve the workflow with tools it already sells. Castle already combines behavioral and device evidence with action-time policy decisions, LexisNexis already bundles behavioral biometrics with identity and device intelligence, and GrayPass’s founder acknowledges that incumbents could build the capability. This weakens the reason to buy and puts pressure on price. The evidence establishes neither a structural block nor materially inadequate proceeds across the strongest credible route, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can GrayPass show a repeatable economic benefit from action-bound enforcement over an incumbent policy engine in paid production? Comparative results across customer workflows should quantify fraud losses avoided, legitimate actions interrupted, and integration cost. Move from Watch to Reach out if those results show a repeatable advantage large enough to make a strategic price above the holder’s proceeds hurdle credible.
