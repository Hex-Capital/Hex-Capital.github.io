# Viraj Aero -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Grid delays are creating buyers for on-site power, and Viraj Aero’s opportunity depends on whether recovered-water steam injection lowers the cost of reliable electricity after accounting for the condenser, cooling system, and their power consumption. That is the controlling variable. The proposed loop has a coherent mechanism: recover combustion water, use exhaust heat to generate steam, and increase turbine mass flow. But additional gross power does not establish additional economic value. Viraj identifies a plausible engineering lever without yet establishing the operating threshold at which customers should buy it.

The market change is more substantiated than the economic improvement. The IEA estimates grid bottlenecks could delay approximately 20% of planned global data-center capacity through 2030. Avoiding that delay can create substantial customer value. Viraj’s 45% electrical-efficiency and 2 ppm NOx specifications, however, remain development targets. The advertised power uplift also varies between 30% and 40%, with unexplained baselines. I give credit for a dated demand catalyst and a specific technical approach, discounted for forecast status. Neither the broader $9.7 billion generator market nor competitors’ financing establishes Viraj’s achievable margins or delivery advantage.

The strongest case for investing is that this is already more than an engineering concept assembled around the AI power shortage. Viraj has €13 million in aviation LOIs, company-reported full-scale aircraft-prototype integration, and €4.5 million in seed financing announced in August 2026. Risson’s propulsion experience and Bouilloux’s development of six power projects totaling over 120 MW connect the team to both sides of the opportunity. The LOIs deserve modest demand credit, although they establish aviation purchase interest rather than data-center pull. The founders’ experience makes execution more credible; it does not document prior founder learning or costly personal commitment, so that dimension stays at its absent-evidence default.

Prototype integration also provides some evidence that Viraj can own a functioning subsystem rather than merely license an isolated idea. Its retrofit offering could become a distribution channel through existing genset manufacturers, avoiding the need to build an entire equipment business immediately. That channel remains proposed. The condenser patent has no verified public claim scope, and the reported Safran work has no disclosed scope. I cannot yet underwrite recurring integration ownership, customer dependence, or a reproduction barrier. Better engineering could strengthen the installed system, but the dossier does not establish how much of that value Viraj retains.

My pattern of investing in physical industries applies when technology changes their economics. Viraj could qualify, particularly through retrofits, but it has not demonstrated that change. I also considered the exception for pre-commercial frontier teams: relevant expertise and financing are present, while independently demonstrated technical superiority and a credible path through industrial scale-up remain unestablished. This is an evidence gap rather than an active founder red flag. I would pass with my own money today. One result would reopen the decision: a paid data-center pilot independently demonstrating at least 10% lower fully burdened cost per reliable MWh than the unmodified host turbine over 1,000 operating hours at contracted hot-site conditions. That is my investment threshold, not a company claim.
