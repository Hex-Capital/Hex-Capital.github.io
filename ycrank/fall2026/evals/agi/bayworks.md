# Bayworks -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Dealership technicians spend billable time writing warranty narratives, and missing details can require follow-up. Bayworks lets technicians dictate against an assigned repair order, attach evidence, and review a manufacturer-formatted draft before saving it. The market-opening hypothesis is that dealerships could profitably file warranty claims they previously skipped because manual documentation took too much technician time. The memo describes a workflow that could lower that cost. It provides no measured savings or examples of previously skipped claims becoming viable. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Bayworks reports nearly seven figures in cARR within four months and activity across six dealership groups. A named Harvey Auto Group executive endorses the product. These are early demand signals for warranty documentation. The share of cARR attributable to live, paying deployments and whether any buyer is paying to pursue previously skipped claims remain unknown. The Android app and PBS extension show current delivery progress, alongside founder experience shipping voice AI and mobile software. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Founders & Team)

A dealership location is the plausible unit of expansion. The economic comparison should use **full cost per accepted claim**. Compare manual writing and follow-up with Bayworks’ price, dictation and review time, retries, exception handling, compliance, and downstream filing work. Bayworks’ own contribution depends on realized revenue per location after processing, onboarding, integration, and support costs. Reported coverage of 4,000 repairs per day indicates potential throughput, while claim acceptance, realized pricing, and delivery costs remain unmeasured in the memo. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Bayworks may save time on claims dealerships would already file. Under cheap, widely available model parity, its repair-order integration and evidence capture could still matter, but Wrench Voice already offers technician dictation, structured warranty documentation, and dealership-system writeback. Prior narratives may improve drafts, while cross-dealer reuse rights and cross-dealer performance gains remain unproven. The overlap makes substitution credible. The memo contains no direct buyer comparison or observed switching that would close the path, so both required conditions remain open and testable. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent dealership group repeatedly file previously skipped, warranty-eligible claims at viable economics using Bayworks and still choose to pay for it when offered a functionally equivalent alternative? A paid field trial comparing manual handling, Bayworks, and an equivalent rival with comparable dealer-system access would answer this. Track full cost per accepted claim and the buyer’s paid repeat choice. A repeat order at viable pricing, backed by newly economical claims and a demonstrated Bayworks-specific reason to stay, would move the action to **Reach out**. A representative trial showing that the claims remain uneconomic or that buyers preserve the outcome by switching would close the path and move the action to **Pass**.
