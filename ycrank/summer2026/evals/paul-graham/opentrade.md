# OpenTrade -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what happened when the founders were blocked. OpenTrade has shipped several products, but the dossier never names an obstacle, the workaround the team built, or the result it produced. Moving from an AI trade deck and execution language through fantasy leagues to market-training games could be resourceful search. It could also be random motion. Without the customer or technical observation behind each change, I cannot distinguish a running back from someone changing fields.

The strongest evidence is the artifact itself. By August 2026, the team had a working iOS app, browser drills such as Tickerdle and Higher/Lower, and a five-pick competition against ChatGPT. That is more meaningful than the founders’ credentials. But it is not yet an unusually difficult technical proof. OpenTrade’s published Market IQ scorecard has a 50% hit rate and a nine-point calibration gap, and there is no disclosed proprietary dataset, benchmark, or execution system. This is working software, not yet evidence of a fierce technical advantage.

The apparent user pull is too denominator-free. Weekly active users rose 3x in three days and total users 5x in five days, but OpenTrade disclosed neither starting counts nor retention. The 3,411 LinkedIn followers, 100,000-view animation, referrals, and creator contest demonstrate an ability to attract attention. They do not answer the important question: which young investors repeatedly use the crude product because it solves an urgent problem? The iOS app still had too few ratings for Apple to summarize, and no payment signal exists.

There is a real bull case in the team. Tario You has AI research experience at Ai2 and the UW Allen School, Dylan Pak reports consumer content reaching more than 600 million views, and all three founders worked together through 67 Labs. A team combining engineering and distribution could turn financial education into a habit product. But the current behavior activates two red flags: growth is being presented through launch spikes rather than cohorts, and a three-person pre-seed company is already recruiting engineering, design, and growth interns before it has shown that the founders personally understand why users return.

The origin is also weak. The dossier infers personal investing interest, but documents no founder collision with the problem. More importantly, OpenTrade currently avoids the difficult part of the market. It does not custody assets, execute trades, or provide personalized advice. Those boundaries reduce regulatory risk, but they also remove the schlep that might create a durable wedge. Alinea, Blossom, Invstr, and StockGro can reproduce games, briefs, AI research, and social mechanics; OpenTrade has disclosed neither exclusive data nor a business model to resist that replication.

The unstated pattern I look for is a concrete proof spike before the financing story: a difficult substrate, overwhelming usage, or numerical performance that changes what one believes. OpenTrade has a polished product and fast relative growth, but not that spike. Nor does a known exception rescue it: there is a working prototype, but no unmistakable pull or independently validated technical result strong enough to replace the failed resourcefulness gate. I would pass. The verifiable flip condition is a named niche of at least 500 users showing 30% or better 30-day retention while using the product at least three times per week.
