# Palette -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Josephine Lee’s June-to-July shift from an HR-agent platform to multimedia generation makes **Palette** an adaptive, capital-light orchestration startup: unusually strong on founder learning rate and conspicuously weak on required-capital fit. **Slash** is the high rung because its documented Kanye-driven pivot converted learning rate into a $100M Series C outcome. **Palette** shares the willingness to abandon a thesis, but Palette provides no explanation of what Lee and Serena Pei learned, what evidence invalidated HR, or why video became inevitable. A changed webpage is evidence of motion, not yet evidence of learning. I give Palette credit for the pivot and withhold credit for the epistemology behind it.

**DoorDash** (2013) is the low rung on required-capital fit: no scientific miracle, no $50M first check, and no laboratory milestone that ordinary venture capital could not finance. DoorDash escaped that weakness by becoming infrastructure for consumer-commerce flow. **Palette** has the weakness without the demonstrated escape mechanism. Palette raised $500K, routes Seedance, Veo, Kling, MiniMax, and LTX, and claims 50% lower generation cost; none of that identifies a use for $50M that changes the probability distribution. Capital cannot manufacture an upstream moat when the suppliers also serve Higgsfield and Krea. I am over-resourced for Palette.

The $42.3B forecast AI-video market gives **Palette** a large market but not a large consequence. Over 18 months, Palette’s 35% faster generation and natural-language editing could compound into workflow convenience, yet convenience is not an economic regime breaking. Palette has not shown that studios disappear, that training production becomes autonomous, or that a named labor pool is displaced; it instead promises marketing, entertainment, support, and product-media teams a smoother interface. That is a 20–30% optimization play until proven otherwise. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself” does not mean take a market forecast and call it consequence.

Serena Pei’s diffusion and multimodal research at MIT places **Palette** near technical orthodoxy, not against it. The founder-defiance score would flip if Palette published a falsifiable claim that model ownership is unnecessary because routing data produces superior economics at scale—and then beat Runway or Higgsfield on retained production workloads. Palette currently offers no public controversial thesis, no rejected expert doctrine, and no evidence that Josephine Lee or Pei contradicted their field. MIT credentials establish capacity. They do not establish instigation. Experts predicted fewer than one million U.S. cellphones by 2000; reality delivered 109 million. I want the founder willing to explain why today’s experts are equally wrong.

**Cognition** is the relevant technical parallel because software can merit a Khosla-scale bet when it attacks a vast labor pool with a stack-deep system. **Palette** instead exposes public endpoints over third-party models and reports 130 regression tests, while its own dossier says Higgsfield and Krea can reproduce the routing and connected workflows. Palette’s cost and capability gates are useful engineering, but they are neither a named technical miracle nor a barrier that repels consensus capital. The internal “100% task-capable” benchmark lacks methodology, customer usage, and independent validation. Palette is closer to an intelligent model switchboard than a new model of intelligence.

Palette’s 130+ regression tests and abrupt product change imply that by July 2029, **Palette** will have pivoted from horizontal model routing into an enterprise training-video workflow, reached $1M–$3M ARR, and still rely on third-party generation models rather than train a proprietary model. The mechanism is straightforward: Palette’s learning rate will find a narrow buyer, while Palette’s low capital requirement and reproducible routing layer will prevent category ownership. A modestly successful vertical SaaS outcome is more probable than either death or a Runway-scale platform.

**Slash** is the strongest objection to my pass because YC pedigree and a documented pivot can override weak consequence magnitude. Palette has two MIT founders, Pei’s CSAIL and Media Lab research, 130 regression tests, and claimed 50% cost reduction—enough raw material for Palette to discover a much larger product than the current website describes. The objection stings because learning rate is the one founder variable I rank above almost everything else. It still loses today: Palette has disclosed no customers, current-product revenue, proprietary model, structural barrier, or rationale for the HR-to-video pivot, while Higgsfield already reports a $200M annual run rate in overlapping workflows. I would flip to Invest if Palette signs ten enterprises above $100K ACV, retains them for twelve months at greater than 70% gross margin, and demonstrates that proprietary routing data—not temporary provider pricing—drives a measured quality or cost advantage.
