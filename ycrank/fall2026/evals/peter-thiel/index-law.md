# Index Law -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Index Law’s possible secret is that businesses can buy legal outcomes directly from software, using independent lawyers only at the boundary where professional judgment becomes necessary. What would prove this wrong? Customers continuing to route consequential matters through their existing counsel because Mike cannot preserve context, allocate responsibility, or recognize when to escalate. The dossier establishes a product organized around this proposition. It does not establish a disputed belief that Index has experimentally overturned. Faster legal drafting is already an industry objective.

The distinction between serving businesses and serving lawyers matters. Harvey and Legora sell principally to legal professionals; Mike addresses the client. Crosby integrates a law firm, while Index coordinates independent counsel. Those are different business architectures, and they could produce different economics. But a different position in the workflow does not itself confer ownership of that position. Persistent company memory could eventually make Mike difficult to replace. The disclosed harnesses and evaluations establish development activity, without demonstrating exclusive data, superior technical performance, or a retention advantage. Index has identified an entry point; its monopoly mechanism remains unproved.

The strongest objection to passing is that Index may already be discovering a much better delivery model. Its reported turnaround below 60 minutes and quotes up to 80% below traditional firms would be consequential if reproduced across comparable matters with appropriate accountability. Separating software subscriptions from approved lawyer fees might let the company serve businesses that cannot justify conventional counsel. Those claims deserve investigation, but unnamed customers and undisclosed sample sizes cannot distinguish a repeatable system from selected successes. The exception I make for a verified technical discontinuity does not apply yet, nor does the exception for persistent paid demand overcoming an institutional obstacle.

Pativada has operated an AI business and reports more than $20 million in booked revenue at his previous venture. That is evidence of commercial experience, not Mike revenue or proof of this company’s founding alignment. His operating background and Dominic Raab’s legal and governmental experience give Index some resemblance to the operator networks through which durable companies are formed. A senior advisor, however, does not establish who owns and builds the essential technology. The dossier leaves founder employment status, ownership, compensation, and prior team working history unresolved. I apply the cautious founding default; I do not infer either exceptional alignment or a defective foundation. Likewise, jurisdictional oversight is an execution requirement here, not evidence of an enforcement-triggered regulatory red flag.

The forthcoming $149 monthly Pro and $499 Teams plans give Index one intelligible subscription engine. At the Teams price, approximately 16,700 accounts would produce $100 million in annual subscription revenue before discounts. That arithmetic establishes a possible scale, not the margins or expansion behavior needed for power-law conviction. Distribution has more substance than an enterprise-sales slogan: YC outreach, setup calls, and 169 free templates offer identifiable routes to business buyers. But the 191,088 launch-post views measure attention. They do not establish that a channel can acquire and support paying customers economically.

I would pass on Index Law today. The decisive gap is between making legal work cheaper and owning the customer relationship durably enough to retain the value created. One verifiable result would change my decision: an independently audited six-month paid cohort showing that accumulated matter history causes customers to retain Mike over competing products at its advertised prices. That would turn company memory from a plausible feature into evidence of a compounding position.
