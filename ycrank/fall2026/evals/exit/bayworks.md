# Bayworks -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a dealership software or fixed-operations platform is Bayworks’ strongest path. Owning the workflow could let a buyer embed warranty documentation across its dealer base and capture the economics of bundling it. The memo documents repair-order writeback, a PBS extension, and a named customer endorsement. Bayworks reports six dealership groups, more than 50,000 repairs, and nearly seven figures in cARR within four months. The memo leaves live paying revenue and measured customer outcomes undisclosed. The operating milestone is repeatable technician time savings and improved warranty claim acceptance across paying dealer groups. Reported adoption gives Bayworks a base for that test, while the available runway and cost to complete it remain unclear. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The $200 million Dealer-FX acquisition, net of acquired cash, shows that a strategic buyer paid for a broader dealership service platform. It provides limited support for what a narrower workflow could command. The acquisition case depends on transferring the software and retaining dealer relationships and integrations through a sale. The memo leaves those terms unverified, and the case assigns zero value to cross-dealer data reuse given dealership ownership and deletion rights. The memo supplies no independently supported fallback that could pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** Investment terms are undisclosed, so the scenarios use the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, zero follow-on checks, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, fees, and senior claims, so enterprise value equals distributable equity and the holder receives 3% pro rata before tax. All consideration is cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. The sale prices are scenario inputs rather than estimates of what a buyer would pay.

| Scenario | Timing from check | Distributable equity | Holder cash | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $50 million | $1.50 million | 3.0× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100 million | $3.00 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No distribution | $0 | 0.0× |

The hurdle is $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, and $62.5 million at 4%. At 2%, even the modeled strong sale returns only 4.0×. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. The investor retains the shares and realizes $0 within the horizon.

### Countercase

Wrench Voice already offers technician dictation and dealership-system writeback. A strategic buyer could get comparable results through a partnership or an internal build, leaving Bayworks with a price below the holder’s hurdle. The memo establishes functional overlap while leaving comparative performance and the buyer’s cost of obtaining the same results open. That gap supports Watch. The evidence establishes neither a binding price ceiling nor a probable prolonged-private outcome that would trigger the Pass override. (Market & Traction, Defensibility)

### Decisive diligence question

Would measured results from paying dealer groups give a strategic buyer an economic reason to own Bayworks’ workflow at a price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? A buyer-validated comparison of ownership with building or licensing, grounded in measured technician time savings and warranty claim outcomes, would move the action to Reach out if the economics clear that hurdle under credible transfer and dilution assumptions.
