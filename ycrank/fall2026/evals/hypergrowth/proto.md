# Proto -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A, product-led adoption.** Proto says product designers are moving into developer IDEs to build interactive prototypes. Its proposed workflow turns tickets or requirements into working prototypes that designers can compare, link into user flows, and share. The memo records a November early-access launch, an alpha signup route, and about 161,000 views on the founder’s launch post. Those observations establish launch reach. Buyer activation, payment, and repeat use remain unverified. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Facts)

If shared prototypes give reviewers a reason to start their own projects, each design team could bring in the next without a separate sales effort. Repeat projects could support paid seats if teams make Proto part of their regular workflow. In a second-market scenario, that loop assumes designers can use the same ticket-to-prototype workflow with manageable changes to language, billing, and code-access requirements. Contribution would depend on what teams pay relative to any agent compute and hosting Proto funds per project, plus any setup or support it supplies per team. The memo gives no measurements for that chain. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Figma already offers code-backed prototype comparison in the canvas where design teams work. That overlap could leave Proto as an occasional tool for exploration, with too little repeat use to support paid seats. If teams also need substantial engineering review to move generated code into production, the workflow could lose much of its value after the prototype. The memo identifies both risks but provides no buyer or rollout evidence establishing a structural ceiling or proportional delivery costs. The Pass gate is unmet. (Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can independent product-design teams in a second geography discover Proto through a shared prototype, create and share their own without founder help, then pay and use it again on their next real project? A small cohort with recorded referral, activation, payment, and repeat use through that route would move Proto to Reach out.
