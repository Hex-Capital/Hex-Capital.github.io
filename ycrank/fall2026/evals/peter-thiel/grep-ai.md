# Grep AI -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Grep AI’s candidate secret is that repeated agent reasoning can become executable procedures while genuinely new exceptions still receive intelligent judgment. That is a testable proposition: on unseen compliance cases, does the conversion lower cost without degrading accuracy or requiring more human intervention? The dossier establishes a product approach, but neither a prevailing belief that named peers reject nor a controlled result validating this particular mechanism. Automating work is valuable. Discovering something competitors misunderstand would make it investable.

The strongest evidence concerns the founders. AJ Asver and Miguel Ríos met at Brex in 2020, worked on its platform team, and subsequently operated Parcha’s compliance agents. Their relationship predates the current agent fashion; the problem comes from operating experience, including Brex’s reported backlog of 2,000 businesses awaiting verification. Grep fits my preference for operators rebuilding a system whose limitations they encountered themselves. The convenience-matched-founder red flag is absent. Ownership, compensation, and explicit full-time status remain undocumented, so I give substantial credit for demonstrated working history without inventing economic alignment.

The strongest objection to passing is IG. Its testimonial reports screening automation increasing from 10% to 30%, flagged-customer activation falling from two or three days to under ten minutes, and accuracy above 99%. Months of shadow testing preceded governance approval. This is considerably more informative than preview signups: an institution allowed the product into a consequential workflow. It supports an enterprise delivery route, although implementation remains demanding and contract economics are undisclosed. But the evidence concerns a legacy compliance deployment continued under Grep; it does not isolate the contribution of the newer self-improving architecture.

Who owns the resulting advantage? IG’s encoded criteria are useful deployment assets, but the dossier establishes neither exclusive rights nor measured switching costs. Meanwhile, n8n, Relevance AI, and Gumloop already offer overlapping orchestration, deployment assistance, and governance. Apache-2.0 AgentRun makes part of the infrastructure reusable by competitors. Broadening from financial compliance into “growth bottlenecks” enlarges the competitive surface before Grep has demonstrated control of its original beachhead. That is the decisive adverse signal: customer value is visible, while a mechanism for keeping that value remains unproved.

The economics offer a coherent credit-based revenue engine, with $200 and $500 monthly subscriptions, but little basis for power-law conviction. At the published Ultra price, approximately 16,667 continuously subscribed accounts would produce $100 million in annual subscription revenue before additional usage purchases. That is illustrative arithmetic, not a demonstrated expansion path. Enterprise contract values and current margins are unknown. Asver’s reported experience with nearly $80,000 in monthly AI spending and loss-making runs makes the harness redesign consequential, but no disclosed current result establishes that the company captures the savings it creates.

I would pass at 41/100. The known exceptions do not overturn the monopoly concern: IG demonstrates meaningful demand, but demand alone does not remove substitutability; the dossier supplies neither an independently established technical discontinuity nor a constrained physical deployment advantage. My expectation is that Grep’s durable business will remain concentrated in financial compliance rather than become a general agent platform. One verifiable condition would change my decision: an independently audited comparison across multiple paying financial-services customers showing Grep’s procedure-learning system delivers at least tenfold lower total processing cost than the best competing workflow at matched accuracy and human-review rates. That would turn the proposed secret into evidence of proprietary advantage.
