# The Agentic Data Co. -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The Agentic Data Co. has produced something useful: conversational audio containing the interruptions, hesitation, and overlapping speech that voice systems must understand. The investment question is how much expensive expertise becomes accessible if this data works. The dossier supplies neither that spending baseline nor a quantified consequence for users. I can imagine radically cheaper voice-delivered services; I cannot credit this company with that outcome merely because it supplies one input. Consequence receives my missing-evidence default.

The strongest evidence is technical execution. Open Yap contains 1,000.245 released hours across 1,602 conversations and 239 speakers. Separate speaker tracks and conversations between acquaintances address specific limitations of Fisher and Switchboard. The company also reports first place among 24 entries for end-of-turn prediction and second among 23 for interruption prediction in its October 17, 2026 snapshot. That is substantially more informative than announcing an audio-data platform. But leaderboard position gives no magnitude of improvement, and the dossier does not isolate the contribution of the training data or establish performance in noisy deployment environments. This earns partial technical credit, well short of a demonstrated 5–10x discontinuity.

The bull case deserves attention: a two-person team has built a collection system, released a substantial corpus, and demonstrated a downstream model application. Its Hugging Face sample attracted 3,022 monthly downloads. This could fit my pattern of turning a scarce technical workflow into a repeatable production platform. If model failures continually determine which conversations to collect next, the company could build a learning advantage that compounds. The dossier establishes collection and evaluation; it does not yet establish that closed loop. David AI and Appen already offer overlapping audio services, so simply accumulating more hours leaves an expensive race to reproduce inventory.

Christian’s engineering and prior company-building experience complement Matias’s growth and operations background. Those skills plausibly cover platform development and contributor acquisition. They do not document rapid belief revision or recruitment against a specific technical weakness, so the founder criterion stays at its default. Likewise, the completed corpus proves execution, but no disclosed milestone identifies the largest kill risk, a decisive threshold, its cost, and the response to failure. I would make incremental customer-model improvement per dollar the first risk to resolve. Transcript accuracy, consent, and generalization belong inside that experiment.

Private dataset licenses provide a plausible commercial route, and the founder’s acknowledgment of customers is encouraging. Neither establishes economic leverage. There is no disclosed price, collection cost, reviewer burden, rejection rate, or gross margin. Human linguistic review could support valuable proprietary data or consume the apparent software economics; the dossier cannot distinguish them. Economics therefore receives the default, without assuming permanent subsidy dependence. The standard-model replication red flag is not established by using Deepgram for transcripts. The unresolved concern is whether growth buys a proprietary capability or merely more collection labor.

I pass with my own money today. I have checked the exceptions: an extraordinary instigator addressing a quantified giant value pool, or demonstrated exponential progress with overwhelming consequence, could justify missing commercial or milestone evidence. This dossier establishes neither override. My single flip condition is a paid customer’s independently reproducible, equal-budget comparison showing at least a 5x reduction in a specified conversational failure rate using The Agentic Data Co.’s data versus its incumbent source, with collection, review, and licensing costs included. That would directly test whether this is a technical resource multiplier worth financing.
