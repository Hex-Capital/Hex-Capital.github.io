# Cerenovus -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Cerenovus attacks a real pool of expensive expert labor: consultants and internal analysts manually reconcile ERP, CRM, email, contracts, and accounting records to diagnose operational leakage. But the dossier never sizes that pool, quantifies the leakage recoverable, or explains how reconstructing processes changes the economics by 10x rather than making investigations modestly faster. A $2.9 billion knowledge-graph market growing to $13.4 billion is a category forecast, not a consequence-of-success case.

The product is more ambitious than enterprise search. Entity resolution, process reconstruction, 26 investigative sweeps, citation verification, owner assignment, and outcome monitoring could become a proprietary learning system. Yet “different from Glean and Hebbia” is positioning, not a technical discontinuity. There is no measured improvement in investigation cost, cycle time, recall, finding accuracy, or dollars recovered. Glean already spans more than 275 tools and exceeds $100 million ARR; Coworker, Hebbia, and Workday’s Sana can converge on much of this functionality. Without a benchmark, Cerenovus may be a well-designed workflow assembled on increasingly standard enterprise-context infrastructure.

The live pilots are the strongest bull signal. Enterprise customers do not casually grant access to accounting, email, contracts, and internal communications, so even pilot access says more than 400 LinkedIn followers or Compendium’s 122 Product Hunt points. The five-person team has also moved rapidly from a $40-per-seat collaboration product to executive operational diagnostics. That resembles the route flexibility that can turn an initial tool into a platform. The material difference from the **Block** exception is that Cerenovus has not yet demonstrated either an American Express-scale value pool or founders revising a belief in response to observed customer evidence; the change could equally be an unvalidated repositioning.

The largest kill risk is whether the system’s findings are accurate, novel, and valuable enough for executives to act on. Cerenovus preserves contradictions, verifies citations, and escalates unresolved findings to reviewers, but it provides no pass/fail experiment: no blind comparison against consultants, no false-positive threshold, no percentage of findings accepted, and no realized customer savings. Capital spent broadening integrations or selling scoped deployments before answering that question risks scaling implementation labor rather than removing technical risk.

Economically, this could replace expensive analysts with software, but the dossier discloses neither current enterprise pricing nor the human-review and integration burden behind each deployment. Persistent entity mappings and outcome histories could create switching costs; they could also become customer-specific services work that never compounds across accounts. My 36-month base case is a pivot toward private-equity diligence and portfolio-operations projects, with less than $3 million ARR, because that narrower buyer can tolerate bespoke deployment while the horizontal platform confronts entrenched vendors.

I pass today. The verifiable flip condition is one completed pilot showing at least a 5x reduction in investigation cost or time versus a named consulting or internal-analysis baseline, with at least 80% of impact-ranked findings accepted by the customer and conversion into a repeat annual contract. That would establish a discontinuity, validate the learning loop, and show that the product—not implementation labor—is producing the result.
