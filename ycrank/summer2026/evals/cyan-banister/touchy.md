# Touchy -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. A useful personal agent sees what I see, remembers selectively, crosses application boundaries, and acts without turning my private life into a liability. Getting there requires cheaper inference, reliable tool execution, explicit permission architecture, and access to operating-system surfaces. Touchy has one real leak from that future: a live iPhone product launched from the lock screen or action button, combining voice, camera, memory, and more than 1,000 tools. But the founders acknowledge that current models cannot yet deliver the intended filtering and personalization, and that “airtight safety guarantees” must precede broader access. This is evidence that the future is arriving, not yet that Touchy owns the path into it.

The present-day wound is understandable but shallowly diagnosed. People interpret search results and manually hop among apps because assistants fail to complete cross-app tasks. What rule creates that failure? Who captures the rent? Touchy does not answer. Apple controls the permissions, distribution, and system-level context, and its June 2026 Siri AI announcement overlaps directly with Touchy’s visual understanding and cross-app action thesis. The company gives users a more convenient interface, but it operates inside the gatekeeper’s territory rather than transferring control away from that gatekeeper.

The team is the strongest part of this dossier. Rohit brings AI-alignment research and a Berkeley EECS record; Andrew built educational software used by roughly 500,000 students; Kevin was an Eventual founding engineer, contributed to Daft and SkyPilot, and produced a public repository with approximately 2,500 stars. Those are shipped artifacts and complementary technical muscles, not merely prestigious résumés. The problem is chronology: only about two months before Touchy launched, the team described Tokenless as model-selection infrastructure for agentic workflows. That makes the consumer-assistant thesis look discovered during the current wave rather than like a pre-company obsession. All three are also presented simply as founders, leaving ultimate operating authority unresolved.

The sharp bull case is that I am judging the artifact too early. Touchy is already downloadable, its multimodal interface is more immediate than a narrated demo, inference costs reportedly fell five- to tenfold annually from April 2024 through November 2025, and one strange repeated behavior could reveal a wedge that Apple will not prioritize. But there is no such behavior in the evidence yet: Apple cannot display a ratings overview, no retention or completed-action cohort is reported, and a launch post with about 11,000 views and 63 likes measures attention rather than changed habits. The $14.99 Touchy Plus listing does not establish even one purchase.

The deeper contradiction is architectural. Touchy may handle messages, contacts, photos, location, and audio while sending relevant information to OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google Cloud AI. Permission controls and a three-month post-termination retention limit are useful, but they are not a demonstrated consent-and-custody system for an agent that wants access to every surface. Meanwhile, the only proposed moat—accumulated memories and preferences—is inferred, and the product depends on Apple’s platform plus closed model suppliers. This sits in the fashionable application layer, with no controlled data asset, protocol, safety primitive, or lower-stack bottleneck yet visible.

I pass. My 36-month base case is that Touchy remains below $1 million ARR as a consumer assistant and the team pivots toward enterprise agent orchestration, model routing, or safety infrastructure—the technical territory their biographies and Tokenless history support more strongly. The one condition that flips me is a verifiable cohort of at least 1,000 users retained for twelve weeks, each completing ten or more cross-app actions weekly under explicit, auditable data permissions. That would show both weirdly costly behavior and a defensible trust relationship rather than borrowed distribution on someone else’s present wave.
