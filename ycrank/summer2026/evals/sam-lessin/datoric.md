# Datoric -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Nearly seven figures of revenue in 30 days is exceptional for a two-person pre-seed company. It is not, however, the first number I need. The proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and status of this check are absent. For a Summer 2026 YC company with striking launch metrics, that silence matters: I cannot tell whether I am underwriting a neglected belief or buying into an auction after the uncertainty has already been repriced.

The functioning business is more compelling than the AI story. Datoric has assembled more than 300,000 active contributors, delivered a commercial dataset containing 250,000 computer-use traces and roughly 15,000 hours of screen activity, and generated actual revenue rather than integrations or impressions. Private collection systems, project isolation, and sample-level consent and licensing records form a useful translation layer between contributors who need their rights preserved and AI labs that need defensible provenance. The nearly seven-figure month demonstrates that this workflow can cause a purchase, even though undisclosed customers and contract counts prevent me from knowing whether it caused one enormous purchase or a repeatable market response.

The sharpest bear case is that Datoric is a labor-intensive data project shop wearing a security moat. Scale AI, Turing, Mercor, and Prolific already possess much larger contributor or expert networks, while Datoric discloses no exclusivity, retention, repeat purchases, or structural barrier. Its gated Hugging Face repository has zero likes and one organization follower. If the 300,000 contributors are rented supply and the recent revenue came from one bespoke contract, neither the audience nor the buyer relationship is a durable proprietary asset.

The founders nevertheless have unusually relevant operating receipts. Nikhil Reddy previously automated more than 15,000 hours of annotation work by bypassing platform QA, giving him firsthand knowledge of the adversarial contributor behavior Datoric must prevent. Jeffrey Lin combines AI/ML and robotics work with authorship of the company’s procedural-video benchmark. Together they have now shipped a large computer-use dataset through the system they built. That is stronger than résumé fit, although it does not yet prove durable access on the AI-lab procurement side. The unexplained transition from Arzule’s announced Winter 2026 YC participation to Datoric’s Summer 2026 listing also leaves open whether this is disciplined discovery or rapid repackaging.

The economics remain incomplete. Revenue without gross margin, contributor acquisition cost, customer concentration, burn, or repeat-purchase data cannot establish a path to profitability without another round. Errors in consent or provenance can create nonlinear liabilities, while human collection and review costs may scale with every delivery. Datoric could become a real cash-generating data supplier, but the dossier does not yet show whether each additional dollar of revenue compounds proprietary workflow data or simply requires another dollar of managed labor.

I would not write the check from this evidence alone because the two capital questions are unanswered: what price am I paying, and what finite truth does the money reveal? The hard operating evidence is strong enough that verified terms could override ordinary skepticism, but it cannot override unknown terms and an unknown capital plan. My one flip condition is a signed financing proposal showing a sub-$10 million post-money valuation and committing proceeds to a dated test of repeat purchases at positive contribution margin across at least three unrelated customers. That would convert an impressive month into a scarce, measurable venture moment.
