# Wingman Law -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Wingman’s strongest path is to scale its California personal injury practice by using AI to handle more cases per attorney. Its public intake form and California claims guides give prospective clients a way to find the firm and request a free case review. Wingman says its internal agents gather evidence and prepare documents and communications for attorney approval. Intake can start online, while first legal value still depends on attorney-led case review and representation. Each client matter generates a one-off contingency fee upon recovery, with no evidenced recurring purchase or expansion within the same client account. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The memo reports multiple six-figure cases in progress shortly after the public launch by a team of two. These are reported case values. Collected fees and their growth rate are unknown, as is whether Wingman is pre-revenue. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Contribution scenario.** Per signed case, expected contribution is the probability of recovery multiplied by the average fee collected when a case succeeds, less attributable acquisition, intake, casework, AI processing, and attorney review costs across all signed cases. Wingman’s automation could reduce evidence-gathering and drafting labor. Review and rework could absorb those savings, and the firm incurs casework costs ahead of any fee collection. The memo gives no measured review times or collected-fee data to establish contribution. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The strongest case for scaling is that the platform’s calls, texts, emails, and case-file workflows could carry over to another market. If local attorneys could configure the same platform for their procedures and handle more cases per attorney, Wingman could replicate the practice with bounded changes. The current service, however, is direct representation built around California claims and delivered through attorneys operating under local professional rules. Even assuming strong California product-market fit, the documented workflow implies substantial adaptation to local procedure, professional requirements, and attorney delivery in other jurisdictions. The memo establishes no credible near-term transfer path. That triggers the structural downside gate on global portability. (The Idea, Defensibility, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can local attorneys in a foreign jurisdiction use the same platform to take a representative set of injury cases from intake through resolution with bounded configuration and no bespoke rebuild? A completed rollout documenting the changes to intake, case procedures, and attorney approval workflows would move the action to **Watch** if it demonstrates bounded adaptation. A rollout that requires rebuilding those workflows would keep the action at **Pass**.
