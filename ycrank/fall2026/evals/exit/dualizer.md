# Dualizer -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition by an industrial simulation vendor. Dualizer says its in-house solver calibrates process models against plant data and connects them to equipment costing and layout. A buyer could use that workflow to help its existing customers validate plant bottlenecks faster. Emerson’s purchase of its remaining AspenTech shares shows strategic demand for an established industrial software platform. AspenTech’s scale limits the deal’s relevance to Dualizer’s price, and the memo leaves Dualizer’s production savings and buyer interest unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The seed-stage milestone is to convert existing pilots into paid production deployments and reproduce the improvement across plants. The active pilots provide a place to test it, and the reported $100,000 prize and listed $125,000 seed investment indicate some funding. Runway, pilot payment terms, and the engineering cost of repeating the deployment remain unknown. Transferability of the solver and permission to carry customer models or calibration data into a deal are also unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and zero follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. Both sale scenarios below assume a full cash sale within eight years, an eligible holder selling its entire stake, and pro rata distribution after the stated deductions, with zero additional senior preference, escrow, or earnout.

- **Modest sale.** An illustrative $40 million enterprise value less $5 million in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million for equity and $1.05 million in gross holder cash, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** An illustrative $100 million enterprise value with the same deductions leaves $95 million for equity and $2.85 million in gross holder cash, or 5.7×. The memo leaves the realizable price and actual waterfall unknown.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested yields zero realized holder proceeds in the comparison period. The memo identifies no funded payout or eligible share purchase.

### Countercase

An incumbent can add conversational automation to existing HYSYS models through a workflow such as ProcAgent, while MaximaLabs already offers overlapping solver and calibration features. That weakens the case for buying Dualizer, especially if each deployment requires custom engineering. Earlier generalization failures and a calibration demo based on synthetic data leave repeatability across plants open. This supports Watch. The overlap shows a substitute for part of the workflow, while the memo leaves both a structural closing barrier and a below-hurdle sale price unestablished. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Dualizer demonstrate a repeatable advantage over the incumbent workflow in paid production that could justify a strategic purchase above the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Customer-verified reductions in engineering time or improvements in bottleneck identification across two paid deployments, with deployment costs low enough for the benefit to carry across a buyer’s installed base, would move the action to Reach out if those economics support a price that clears the hurdle after deal deductions.
