# Bayworks -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Bayworks has demonstrated that dealerships will try another application. Has it discovered something their existing software vendors cannot supply? The candidate secret is that capturing repair evidence directly from technicians can materially improve warranty reimbursement. But the dossier establishes voice capture, missing-information prompts, and narrative generation—not improved reimbursement. A controlled comparison of claim acceptance could falsify that proposition. No such result, or named competitor rejecting the proposition, appears here. The secret remains unproved.

The more serious problem is ownership of the resulting value. Technician repair narratives are a concrete beachhead, but Tekion already offers native voice documentation inside the dealership system of record. Bayworks retrieves previous repair stories, yet its privacy policy says dealership operational data belongs to the dealership; exclusive access and cross-customer learning rights are unverified. That does not establish that learning is impossible. It establishes that accumulated records cannot yet be counted as a proprietary advantage. The incumbent controls the workflow on which Bayworks depends, while the entrant supplies a feature the incumbent already overlaps.

The strongest objection to passing is substantial: Bayworks reports reaching 80+ dealerships in 110 days, including an unnamed top-50 US dealer group. That is unusually rapid organizational access for a company this young. Employer-provisioned accounts and booked demos provide a concrete acquisition and deployment route. But the reported 4,000 daily repairs describe those dealerships’ activity, not verified Bayworks usage, and the dealer group’s $3B+ revenue belongs to the customer. These figures support distribution progress; they do not establish product dependence. Contract values, acquisition costs, sales cycles, and renewals remain unavailable.

The founders receive neither a prestige premium nor a penalty for short biographies. Shubham Goyal’s backend experience at Navi and internships at Scapia and Cisco support his ability to build the documented workflow. His Bayworks role dates to June 2026. Both founders attended BITS Pilani, but shared education does not establish prior collaboration. Full-time status, ownership, compensation, and the allocation of core engineering are unverified. There is no documented part-time founder, convenience pairing, or investor-dominated capitalization, so those founding red flags are not triggered. Alignment remains an open diligence question.

Bayworks also fits an aspect of my actual investing behavior that a caricature of “hard technology only” would miss: ordinary commercial workflows can support extraordinary companies. Its documentation could make disputed warranty facts more legible. Neither observation earns investment credit without evidence of value capture. The technical-breakthrough exception does not apply because no independently validated technical discontinuity is reported. The persistent-demand exception offers the stronger challenge, but dealership deployment without paid retention or measured dependence does not remove the substitution risk. An inferred subscription model, without prices or margins, also provides no arithmetic for a power-law outcome; the $5.72B dealer-software market proxy cannot supply it.

I would pass with my own money. Bayworks’s strongest evidence concerns reaching customers; its weakest concerns retaining economic power once it reaches them. That combination can produce a useful business while leaving the system-of-record vendor with the durable advantage. One result would reopen the decision: an independently verified 12-month paid renewal cohort showing dealerships retain Bayworks despite an included native DMS alternative because it measurably improves warranty-claim acceptance. That would begin to demonstrate a position the incumbent cannot erase by adding another button.
