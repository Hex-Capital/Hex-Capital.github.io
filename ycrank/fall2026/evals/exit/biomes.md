# Biomes -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest potential route is acquisition by an AI deployment platform serving private equity sponsors. Biomes says it embeds engineers with operating teams, builds agents around existing tools, and turns workflow failures into evaluations that could be reused. A buyer could have a reason to acquire those workflows and evaluations if they measurably reduce the cost of deploying across portfolio companies. The memo documents an engineering opening at $400,000 annually plus equity. It provides no production results, paid rollout, or evidence that the hiring plan is funded. Repeating a deployment at a second company under the same sponsor is a plausible seed-stage milestone. Its timing and capital needs depend on how much custom integration the second deployment requires. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership. The sale scenarios below are illustrative. Both assume a cash close within eight years, $5 million in combined debt and deal adjustments, no further senior claims, and an eligible holder selling the full stake pro rata. The memo supplies no valuation evidence, waterfall terms, or confirmation that customer contracts and workflow assets can transfer. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Topsail already offers embedded engineering and agent deployment to sponsors and their portfolio companies. Sponsor-backed competitors also have direct distribution, illustrated by DeployCo’s work with a TPG portfolio company. If legacy integrations keep each Biomes deployment bespoke, a prospective buyer can use an existing provider and has little reason to pay for Biomes’ workflow assets. That substitute weakens the acquisition rationale today. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible sale-price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle, so the Pass gate stays open pending evidence. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Biomes reuse the same paid production workflow at a second company under one sponsor and materially reduce deployment labor while maintaining reliability? A documented second rollout showing integration hours, live task performance, and customer savings would answer it. Move to Reach out if those results support a plausible buyer case for clearing the $83.3 million distributable equity hurdle. Stay on Watch if buyer economics remain unresolved.
