# Versine -- Paul Graham Evaluation

What did these founders do when something refused to work? That is the fact I most want about Versine, and the dossier doesn't contain it. Harshith’s engineering experience at Stripe and Param’s work at Databricks and Glean make the team worth meeting. They don't establish resourcefulness under resistance. There is no named obstacle, founder-built workaround, and observable result. I would leave that criterion at its missing-evidence default rather than turn employment history into a founder anecdote.

Versine changed its messaging from agent authentication in September–October to payments in October. This could be a useful correction. It could also be a search for something people want. We aren't told what observation caused the change, whether the underlying goal remained constant, or what customers did afterward. A running back changes direction because something is in the way. Seeing two positions on the field doesn't establish why he moved. Adaptability therefore stays at default too.

The strongest case for Versine is that these founders may understand something important about making agent payments usable. Its October terms describe passkey-controlled USDC wallets, capped payment rules, and bank payouts through licensed providers. Account-free recipient claim links address a concrete inconvenience: receiving a reimbursement shouldn't require adopting another financial application. Harshith’s payments background gives this design more substance than a generic agent pitch. But the payments product has not launched. The provider disclosures establish a proposed implementation, not a measured technical result. And prior work at Stripe doesn't establish that the idea arose from a problem the founders personally encountered. Technical proof and problem origin both remain at default.

Versine resembles an unstated pattern I like: giving developers a primitive that lets them build things the founders didn't anticipate. The missing part is developers actually doing that. The consumer and developer waitlists contain no disclosed counts or usage, and 1,076 LinkedIn followers don't identify anyone who urgently needs an agent to move money. The strongest bull case is that reusable wallet and payout infrastructure lets three capable founders reach those users unusually quickly. That possibility deserves attention. It doesn't yet qualify for an exception to my founder gate: there is no demonstrated working product, unmistakable user pull, or independently validated result strong enough to substitute for the missing evidence.

The difficult work here could become the opportunity. Agent mistakes, irreversible transfers, public transaction histories on Base, and providers that can restrict payouts are concrete problems to solve. Whether users tolerate those costs for reimbursements is untested. Free sending and bank payouts also leave the eventual revenue mechanism undisclosed, while Stripe already supplies competing agent payment infrastructure. Competition alone wouldn't stop me. The decisive question is whether Versine discovers a small group whose need makes all this trouble worthwhile. I would not infer partner-dependent distribution merely from its use of infrastructure providers, or a hastily assembled team from an undisclosed cofounder history. Those red flags are unproven.

I would pass on the present evidence. This is an unproven founder bet, with a plausible product direction; it is not an affirmative case of founders waiting or refusing to learn. Versine clears neither my resourcefulness gate nor its technical-proof-or-user-pull requirement. One verifiable event would change my decision: a dated customer case showing a blocked reimbursement workflow, a workaround the founders shipped within weeks, and that same customer repeatedly completing real payments through it.
