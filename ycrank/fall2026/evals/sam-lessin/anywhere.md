# Anywhere -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Anywhere’s financing is a blank: no proposed post-money, competing-term-sheet count, or evidence that this is the first scarce check. YC membership does not establish an auction. It also does not establish an attractive deal. The dossier names no capital-funded experiment with a metric and deadline, so I cannot tell what truth my money would purchase. Both gates receive the missing-evidence defaults. I’m passing at 31/100, with substantially more interest in the builder than in the investment currently documented.

Charly Delaroche has unusually relevant operating receipts. Building a cross-platform mapping engine at Zenly and rebuilding Life360’s map experience are concrete accomplishments, beyond recognizable employers. He understands infrastructure and the consumer experience it enables. That is Anywhere’s strongest evidence. But the proposed bridge now extends from social mapping into agent-controlled personal context, and the dossier establishes no comparable operating history on the agent side. His Zenly employment also was not a founder exit. I give the shipped work meaningful credit without manufacturing the missing half of the bridge.

The strongest argument against my pass is that Anywhere could own the context that makes otherwise interchangeable assistants useful. Places, visits, notes, trips, and friend relationships could become a private pool of information that users continually enrich. Delaroche knows how to build the map beneath that behavior, and the documented API lets connected agents read and modify it. This fits my pattern of backing translation layers between systems. The unresolved question is whether users entrust Anywhere with that context repeatedly. A privacy policy establishes supported data types and permissions; it does not establish an accumulated proprietary asset or customer behavior caused by it.

That distinction matters against Mapstr’s saved places and Wanderlog’s collaborative itineraries. Agent access adds functionality, but standardized connections also give competitors a route to offer it. Anywhere launched publicly on November 1, 2026; its November 5 Claude connector listing establishes availability, without demonstrating usage or conversion. Four App Store ratings averaging 2.0 are too small and early a sample to establish rejection, although the reported verification failure identifies a concrete obstacle to collecting any context at all. Neither the listing nor the founder’s roughly 2,000 followers establishes owned distribution. I assign the absent-evidence default rather than treating those numbers as traction.

Delete the next financing from Anywhere’s plan and the economic picture remains unanswered: the app is free, no paid tier is identified, and there is no disclosed margin, burn, or break-even plan. That earns the economics default, without assuming a four-day-old launch should already be profitable. My exceptions to the price and venture-moment gates require documented conversion, cash economics, or an unusually concrete financing structure; this dossier supplies none. Nor does it establish an auction, confused founder authority, or a moat dependent on superior models. The decisive gap is narrower: strong mapping execution has not yet demonstrated an owned customer relationship that pays. One verifiable result would reopen my decision: a six-month cohort test showing that users repeatedly authorize agent access and pay enough to cover the variable cost of serving them. That would begin to establish a functioning base business beneath the AI option.
