# screenpipe -- Elad Gil Evaluation

screenpipe’s 358 releases make the single-miracle architecture its strongest dimension, while Microsoft Recall’s bundling makes index-like distribution its weakest: screenpipe is a rapidly working product that has not yet proven it owns the workflow. **Color Genomics** is the high rung here—workflow infrastructure built on commodity sequencing rather than a bet on new chemistry. screenpipe shares that mechanism by assembling accessibility APIs, OCR, Whisper, SQLite, REST, and MCP into a usable context layer without requiring a technical breakthrough. screenpipe diverges because Color became embedded in an institutional healthcare workflow; 250,000 users and 18,900 GitHub stars do not yet show that screenpipe is similarly unavoidable.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism on distribution: being a credible number two in a market where the default captures disproportionate economics. screenpipe faces a version of that problem because Microsoft can bundle Recall into Windows, Otter.ai already reports $100 million ARR, and Granola owns a narrower meeting workflow. screenpipe sits above Lyft because cross-platform local capture, programmable pipes, and MCP access form a distinct surface, but below an index business like Stripe because every new AI agent does not automatically create screenpipe revenue. screenpipe must still win each user or enterprise against bundled substitutes.

Limitless/Rewind’s December 2025 acquisition and discontinuation created a real opening, while MCP-compatible agents and locally runnable Whisper changed what desktop history can do. Over the next 18 months, that catalyst can compound for screenpipe: more agent adoption increases demand for persistent context, more pipes increase screenpipe’s utility, and local-default storage addresses the privacy objection that killed many centralized memory products. The catalyst is partly inferred rather than demonstrated through named enterprise purchases, so screenpipe is closer to Mistral’s post-foundation-model window than Anduril’s unmistakable post-Ukraine procurement reset.

250,000 users, 6× active-user growth in two months, and tens of thousands of dollars earned within weeks put screenpipe well above the absent-growth default. The counterfactual is straightforward: verified paid revenue compounding above 20% monthly with six-month cohort retention would move screenpipe toward Harvey’s “just working” rung and a Strong Invest; evidence that the 250,000 figure is cumulative installs with weak paid conversion would reduce screenpipe toward Neutral. Today the curve is unusually strong for pre-seed, but founder-reported activity and unspecified “tens of thousands” are not yet the same signal as auditable ARR.

**Perplexity** is the relevant founder parallel: Aravind repeatedly turned conversations into shipped artifacts, and Louis Beaumont appears to operate at a similar product cadence. screenpipe began as a weekend build, has been developed since 2024, and now shows 358 releases, 1,800 forks, and a working Rust/TypeScript stack across three operating systems. screenpipe’s evidence is stronger than résumé pattern-matching—Beaumont has already created the artifact, distribution, and initial monetization—but I would still want release velocity tied to user feedback rather than GitHub activity alone.

Tens of thousands of dollars within weeks support a falsifiable forecast: by July 2029, screenpipe reaches $15 million–$30 million ARR, primarily from enterprise deployments of its programmable local context layer, but screenpipe does not yet become the universal memory rail for AI agents. The single-miracle architecture lets screenpipe keep shipping into demand quickly; the weak index position caps the outcome because Microsoft, meeting platforms, and agent vendors can each absorb portions of the workflow.

Microsoft Recall is the strongest bear case: Microsoft distributes local screen memory at operating-system scale, Otter.ai has $100 million ARR, continuous capture creates regulated-data exposure, and screenpipe’s move from MIT to a paid commercial license could alienate the developer channel responsible for 18,900 stars. That combination could turn screenpipe’s apparent usage curve into open-source tourism while incumbents capture the paid accounts. The evidence currently cutting against that case is screenpipe’s 6× active-user growth, early revenue, cross-platform reach, and 358-release execution rhythm. I would flip to Pass if screenpipe’s verified six-month paid-customer retention is below 60%.
