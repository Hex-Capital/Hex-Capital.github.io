### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** Caladh’s strongest case is that port and subsea operators may have access to drone detection while still lacking a reliable way to mitigate incursions. The Coast Guard’s request for information documents buyer interest in underwater mitigation. Caladh says Eclipse is designed to disrupt maritime drones and offers site pilots. The memo establishes no field performance or buyer commitment for Eclipse, and its relevance to the Coast Guard’s underwater requirement remains unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Scenario assumption:** If cheaper intelligence increases drone activity, site-qualified mitigation could become more valuable even when every supplier has access to the same models. Caladh could capture that value if it can supply a capability buyers qualify and competing suppliers struggle to match. For this Boston-based seed candidate, a representative buyer-backed pilot could test that premise. Site contracts are a plausible expansion unit, with installation costs, pricing, and repeatability still unknown. Path B remains unproven because the memo identifies no newly viable transaction enabled by lower delivery labor. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Incumbents already compete for maritime security budgets. Ultra Maritime has demonstrated underwater detection and classification, and Caladh claims compatibility with third-party detection. An incumbent that bundles equivalent mitigation could control buyer access and capture the economics. The memo establishes no like-for-like mitigation substitute or durable advantage for Caladh, so the Path A override remains untriggered and the outreach gate remains unmet. Kita’s documented undersea research and engineering experience supports execution credit, with company-level field delivery still unverified.
