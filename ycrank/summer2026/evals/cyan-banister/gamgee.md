# Gamgee -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032: a dog’s tumour is sequenced, a mutation-specific vaccine is designed overnight, manufactured locally, and administered by its veterinarian before the disease outruns the treatment. Gamgee has one wonderful little leak from that world. In December 2025, Rosie’s tumour and healthy DNA moved through computational neoantigen selection, university RNA manufacturing, and veterinary delivery; Gamgee reports roughly 75% tumour reduction. That is more interesting than a pet-oncology TAM slide because the entire machine actually moved once. The missing pieces are still enormous: today’s workflow is about four weeks, the promised 24-hour turnaround and $2,000 cost are development targets, and the dossier does not establish how manufacturing, regulation, or clinical attribution improve with scale.

Paul Conyngham has real technical tuition: an electrical-engineering thesis in neural networks and reinforcement learning, 17 years in machine learning, two earlier companies, and hands-on coordination with UNSW and the University of Queensland. He also recruited a former Meta engineer. But the biography does not yet show that cancer biology was a pre-company obsession or that this team independently accumulated several oncology artifacts before Rosie. Rizz is visible in the institutional coalition and public reach; technical Tizz is plausible; the Biz of turning one bespoke intervention into repeatable treatment economics remains unproven.

The strongest evidence is the working artifact, not the claimed platform. Independent ABC reporting confirms that Rosie’s tumours shrank, but she retained cancer after previously receiving surgery, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted drugs. UNSW called the intervention citizen science, not a clinical trial. That makes the result both legitimately strange and scientifically non-attributable. Open trial applications and a veterinarian portal show a route to subsequent cases, but undisclosed enrolment means I cannot yet see owners repeatedly paying, veterinarians repeatedly referring, or manufacturing surviving real demand.

The bull case stings because pre-revenue biotechnology can deserve a check when a functioning prototype, institutional collaborators, and a deeply obsessed founder compress an apparently impossible workflow. That was the relevant exception at CosmicBrain AI. Gamgee shares the prototype and collaboration mechanism; the material difference is that its single result is clinically confounded and its founder record demonstrates long-term machine-learning depth rather than lifelong oncology or mRNA-building obsession. One more press story does not close that gap. A prospective cohort could.

I also cannot find the broken rule. Pet cancer is painful and expensive, but the dossier does not identify the fee, licensing structure, wait, or gatekeeper extracting rent, nor explain which durable agency Gamgee returns to owners or veterinarians. Meanwhile Torigen, ELIAS, and FidoCure already operate personalised-treatment or genomic-selection workflows with more than $61 million collectively raised. Gamgee may eventually become the concealed infrastructure—a canine-specific response dataset joined to rapid custom manufacturing—but today it is a direct therapy assembled partly through university facilities, with one recorded patient and no demonstrated control of a scarce bottleneck.

The red-flag check is unresolved rather than failed. Gamgee collects genomic, clinical, outcome, and adverse-event data into an anonymised research dataset, yet the dossier does not describe consent boundaries, custody, deletion rights, or access architecture. It also gives no co-founder history or governance structure, so I cannot verify who breaks a two-person tie. My August 2029 base case is therefore a small investigational veterinary program with under $1 million in annualised treatment revenue, not yet a scaled personalised-cancer platform. I would not write the check today. The verifiable flip condition is a prospective cohort of at least 20 dogs showing independently adjudicated response attributable to the vaccine, alongside repeatable sub-seven-day manufacturing and explicit genomic-data consent and custody terms.
