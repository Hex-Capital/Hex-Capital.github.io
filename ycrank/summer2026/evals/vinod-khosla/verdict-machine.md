# Verdict Machine -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is not yet large enough. Verdict Machine could prevent irreversible transactions and make banks more willing to hold digital assets, but the dossier quantifies neither today’s institutional loss pool nor the compliance labor it could eliminate. A $6.37 billion blockchain-security market is vendor revenue, not a 100x societal consequence. Billions moving through customer systems each week establishes exposure; it does not establish an order-of-magnitude change in cost, access, or capability.

The product thesis is an institution-specific model spanning inventory, posture, screening, and pre-execution enforcement. That integration may be useful, but no evidence shows a technical discontinuity. Blockaid processes more than 180 million transactions monthly; Hexagate covers over 75 chains with Chainalysis data; Check Point already demonstrated an AI-assisted blockchain firewall under Dan Danay. Verdict Machine discloses no comparative detection rate, false-positive rate, latency, chain coverage, proprietary model architecture, or production dataset. Calling the system AI does not separate it from the prevailing architecture. On current evidence, this is a control-plane integration competing against better-capitalized data networks.

The founders are the real asset. Danay and Grossman built keyTango together, Kogan ran blockchain-security product inside Danay’s Check Point group, and the team combines security engineering, infrastructure, and product experience. POCs initiated within weeks with asset managers and infrastructure providers moving billions weekly, plus evaluations by large-bank digital-asset teams, demonstrate unusual access for a pre-seed company. They do not demonstrate the criterion I care about most in founders: a material belief revised after evidence or a risk-specific recruit brought in to repair a weakness. This is credible domain repetition, not yet demonstrated learning velocity.

The strongest objection to passing is the relevant **Block** exception: a four-person team can begin without a 5–10x technical breakthrough when it attacks an enormous financial value pool and can expand from a narrow control point into infrastructure. Verdict Machine shares the small experienced team and transaction-layer wedge. The material difference is that its disclosed market is a crowded $6.37 billion security category, while Fireblocks, Blockaid, Hypernative, Hexagate, and Check Point already occupy adjacent control points. The broader, execution-heavy part of my investing history says this team could still build a valuable security company. It does not justify treating competitive integration as an uncapped option.

The POCs are sensible, but the dossier gives no risk-removal plan. The largest kill risk is whether AI enforcement can stop policy violations without either blocking legitimate institutional transactions or missing irreversible ones. There is no cheapest experiment, numerical threshold, cost, deadline, or stop condition. Economics are equally unproven: no pricing, implementation burden, loss-reduction model, or evidence that Verdict Machine replaces an existing stack rather than becoming another paid layer on top of it.

One condition would flip me: a paid production deployment at a named bank processing at least one million proposed transactions, with under 0.05% false-positive blocks and evidence of a 5x reduction in combined security-loss and compliance cost versus that bank’s existing stack. Until then, I expect Verdict Machine to reach roughly $1–3 million ARR by August 2029 as a specialized institutional-crypto security product, potentially attractive to an infrastructure acquirer, but not to establish a category-defining technical advantage. I pass today.
