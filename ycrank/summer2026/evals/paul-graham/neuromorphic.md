# Neuromorphic -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The most informative number is five days. Neuromorphic changed strategy during YC and signed its first customer contract five days later. That is evidence of founders who can change direction and make something happen, not merely describe a market. But the dossier never says what failed in the previous facility-automation strategy or what the founders physically changed to win the contract. I see the running back changing lanes; I cannot yet see the tackle he avoided. That keeps an unusually promising resourcefulness signal below the top anchor.

The technical evidence is real but thin. One biotechnology customer went from an initial conversation to daily robot operations within a week. A robot performing useful work in a lab is a much stronger artifact than an architecture diagram. The team is also unusually well matched to building it: Ege Doğanay researches contact-rich manipulation and policy generalization, Vatan Aksoy Tezer has navigation and deployment experience, and the founders report prior work involving more than 1,000 robots. Their relationship predates the application through high-school FRC competitions and championships. But Neuromorphic discloses no task-success rate, human-intervention rate, safety record, or cross-body benchmark. Daily operation proves that something works; it does not yet prove the advertised embodiment-agnostic brain.

Wet labs are nevertheless a credible narrow well. A customer signed a contract and tolerated the crude product in daily operations, while the service absorbs hardware, software, monitoring, and support. The previous $5,000–$10,000 per-robot monthly price indicates plausible economics even though current lab pricing and revenue are undisclosed. One customer cannot establish retention or repeatable demand, but it is far more informative than the three press articles, NVIDIA Inception membership, or a $24.23 billion robot-software TAM. The question is whether a second laboratory wants the same workflow without the founders rebuilding the system around it.

The move from energy, construction, security, and facility automation into wet labs preserved the underlying goal while changing the route. Signing a contract five days later suggests the change improved contact with demand. It does not reveal the observation that prompted the change, so I cannot distinguish a falsified hypothesis from opportunistic vertical shopping. The founders have lived inside robotics deployment, but the dossier does not show that they personally encountered a wet-lab workflow problem before choosing the segment. Their strongest founder-origin signal is therefore the general integration schlep—navigation, manipulation, monitoring, and support—not the specific laboratory wedge.

The bear case is formidable. FieldAI, Skild AI, Physical Intelligence, and Generalist AI have raised more than $4 billion collectively, while Neuromorphic has no identified moat, proprietary data scale, or measured advantage. This fails my recurring pattern of a difficult substrate or numerical proof spike assembled before the financing story. The classic partnership red flag is not yet active—NVIDIA Inception appears ornamental rather than necessary—and the founders seem to be selling and deploying personally. Still, “embodiment agnostic robot brains” is a much larger claim than one undisclosed customer can support. If the wet-lab workflow is mostly bespoke integration, the team has built a capable robotics consultancy with venture-shaped language.

No known exception is needed to rescue the investment. Neuromorphic clears the operating gate through a fast strategy change, a shipped deployment, and daily customer use. I would invest, principally in the founders’ speed and technical contact with reality, while treating the present product noun as provisional. My flip condition is simple: if Neuromorphic has no second paying wet lab operating the system daily by August 2027, I would reverse the decision, because the first deployment would then look like a custom project rather than the start of user pull.
