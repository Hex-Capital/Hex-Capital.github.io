# hardware intelligence -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence is real but not yet nonlinear. Verification consumes expensive engineering time inside a $20 billion EDA industry, and Wave could compress the loop from inspecting multi-gigabyte waveforms to tracing a signal, proposing a fix, rerunning the simulator, and auditing the diff. But hardware intelligence has not quantified hours eliminated, tape-out delays avoided, or the population of engineers affected. This is a potentially valuable productivity tool, not yet evidence of a 100x societal consequence.

The technical case is weaker than the workflow description. Wave is terminal-native, on-prem, and compatible with customer-controlled model keys, but Synopsys Verdi already offers natural-language interaction, failure analysis, signal tracing, and waveform comparison. ChipAgents adds root-cause analysis across designs, testbenches, logs, and waveforms and reports deployments at more than 120 semiconductor companies. Hardware intelligence shows demonstrations, not a measured 5–10x improvement in debugging time, accuracy, or compute cost against either incumbent. Convenience is not a discontinuity.

The founders are the reason I would keep watching. Athreya Anand combines agentic-AI work at Google with exposure to AWS Inferentia and Tesla; Rishov Sarkar built AMD AI Engine simulators, worked on LLVM flows at Siemens EDA, and maintains the 77-star LightningSim project. That is unusually relevant technical composition for two people. It does not, however, demonstrate rapid belief revision or magnet recruiting: the dossier records no thesis changed after customer evidence and no risk-specific recruit won from a center of excellence.

The largest immediate kill risks are obvious—FSDB coverage, incumbent feature overlap, and whether automated fixes survive real designs—but the company has not attached pass/fail thresholds to them. Pilot onboarding opened in July 2026, yet there is no named pilot, repeat usage, paid deployment, or experiment such as “reduce root-cause time by 5x on ten production failures.” Capital should buy that evidence before it buys sales capacity. Pricing is also absent, so there is no proof that on-prem deployment and onboarding can undercut the fully loaded incumbent workflow without becoming consulting labor.

The strongest bull case is the known small-team, giant-value-pool exception: like Block’s initial narrow wedge, Wave could evolve from a debugger into the control layer for semiconductor verification. The material difference is that hardware intelligence enters after both a dominant incumbent and a heavily financed startup already offer overlapping intelligence. The on-prem architecture also limits the cross-customer learning loop that might otherwise let the product compound faster. The founders’ domain depth makes a broader verification platform plausible; the present product evidence does not make it probable.

I pass today. My verifiable flip condition is one named semiconductor team completing at least 50 production debugging cases with Wave, showing a measured 5x reduction in median root-cause time versus Verdi, including FSDB workflows, and converting to a paid annual deployment. That would turn an attractive interface into a technical option worth purchasing.
