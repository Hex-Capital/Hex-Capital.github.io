# Osseus -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Osseus’s permissioned, version-aware engineering graph makes Osseus an architecturally coherent workflow-infrastructure bet with no visible commercial curve. The highest signal is the single-miracle structure: Osseus assumes existing models can parse engineering artifacts, then builds evidence, permissions, and workflow around them. The lowest signal is revenue velocity: invited workspaces and custom rollout pricing reveal neither revenue nor repeat usage. That pairing describes a plausible product searching for proof that the market is “just working.”

**Color Genomics** is the high rung because it built workflow and data infrastructure on top of commodity sequencing instead of betting on new chemistry. Osseus shares that mechanism: Osseus connects CAD, electronics, code, tests, supplier constraints, and decisions without requiring new robotics hardware or model research. Osseus diverges because connecting all those systems is itself a dependency chain—custom connectors, mirrored permissions, reliable cross-domain inference, and human approval—so the nominal single miracle risks decomposing into several execution miracles.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism: activity can be a false signal when structural market position does not translate into superior economics. Osseus does not yet offer even that ambiguous activity signal; Osseus reports invited-team access and demo-led onboarding but no customer, revenue, retention, or usage curve. Pre-seed status excuses small numbers, not the absence of one informative buying signal, particularly when CoLab has already financed direct AI-review overlap with a $72 million Series C.

**CoLab’s July 2025 AutoReview launch** and Duro’s June 2025 AI-native relaunch provide Osseus with a real but weak market catalyst. Over the next 18 months, better multimodal models should make engineering-document parsing cheaper and more useful, which could compound Osseus’s coverage across every revision and failure. The same catalyst also compounds incumbent capability: without a robotics-specific distribution wedge, Osseus may be skating for the puck directly behind vendors that already own design-review or PLM workflows.

**Anish Mariathasan’s prior compliance-AI startup** and Ayush Patel’s Apple agentic-QA work put Osseus above a credential-only founding profile, while Ayush’s self-driving robot is a relevant shipped artifact. Osseus would move into my invest range on founder execution if Anish and Ayush could show that Osseus shipped three customer-requested connectors or review capabilities between successive pilot meetings. The dossier instead shows exposure to the problem and credible technical preparation, not the Perplexity pattern of repeatedly building what users requested one week earlier.

**Stripe** became an index on e-commerce because every growing internet business eventually touched payment infrastructure. Osseus could become an index on robotics development if every design revision, test failure, supplier constraint, and software dependency flows through Osseus. Today Osseus remains an invited, forward-deployed application competing with CoLab, Bild, Duro, and Five Flute; customer-specific rollout pricing suggests services-assisted adoption rather than automatic category-level distribution.

**Duro’s AI-native PLM expansion** makes my 36-month forecast specific: by July 2029, Osseus will have narrowed into robotics-focused AI design review, remain below $2 million ARR, and be acquired by Duro for less than $25 million rather than becoming an independent engineering system of record. Osseus’s evidence graph is useful acquisition material, but Osseus’s connector burden, human-validation requirement, and lack of a visible usage curve favor a feature-scale outcome.

**CoLab’s $72 million Series C** also sharpens the strongest bull case: robotics is compounding, horizontal PLM products do not understand software-hardware dependencies, and Osseus could become the Color Genomics exception—a workflow layer on top of a hard technical category without accepting the underlying science risk. Osseus has the permission model, VPC deployment, and cross-domain graph needed for that outcome. The evidence still loses to direct incumbent overlap and zero disclosed customer behavior; I would flip to Invest if Osseus signs three named robotics customers and sustains greater than 20% monthly paid-usage expansion for six consecutive months.
