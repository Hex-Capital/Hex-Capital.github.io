# Amulet -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Amulet’s content-addressed commits, millisecond copy-on-write forks, and conflict-checked publishing make Amulet a technically coherent single-miracle infrastructure bet with no visible usage curve. That pairing is the signature: Amulet may have built the right primitive for disposable agent compute, but private-beta admissions, 115 LinkedIn followers, and zero disclosed revenue do not show that the market is pulling. This looks more like a potentially valuable subsystem than one of the dozen or two independent companies that matter ten years from now.

**Color Genomics** is the high rung on single-miracle architecture: workflow and data infrastructure sat on top of already-working commodity sequencing. Amulet shares that mechanism because Amulet assumes agents and cloud compute already exist; the remaining task is making large datasets persistent, forkable, and publishable across ephemeral jobs. The divergence is that Color’s underlying workflow demand was legible, while Amulet’s 1.9-ms p99 reads, 8.4-GB/s throughput, and 3-ms forks are company-published benchmarks attached to a recent pivot, with no independent workload proving that partial POSIX support is sufficient.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism on economic velocity: activity can exist without proving attractive category economics or a path out of the subordinate position. Amulet shows less than that—Amulet discloses private-beta partners but no active GiB, repeat usage, revenue, customer names, or month-over-month curve. The $0.20-per-GiB-month price is a rate card, not buying behavior. In an AI infrastructure market, silence on growth is a negative signal; this is not yet Harvey-style “just working.”

Archil’s April 2026 financing identifies the strongest version of Amulet’s market-reopening case: parallel stateful agents are creating disposable compute environments that still need durable shared data. For Amulet to move up a full tier, that inferred 2025–2026 catalyst must appear in customer behavior—agent workloads growing faster than ordinary training storage and selecting Amulet specifically for forks, provenance, and conflict-safe publishing. Without that evidence, Amulet’s “why now” may describe a feature cycle that Modal, E2B, or cloud filesystems can absorb rather than a newly opened independent market.

**Perplexity** is the relevant founder-shipping parallel because the decisive signal was not pedigree; Aravind repeatedly returned with the discussed product built. Nithik Bala and Varun Puru have relevant Meta, Rockset, Amazon, and backend-infrastructure experience, and Amulet’s working mount, fork, commit, and publish surface is a real artifact. Amulet’s recent pivot from customer-success agents weakens the parallel, however: one fast product build demonstrates velocity, but it does not yet demonstrate repeated iteration against user feedback or prior independent shipping at scale.

Amulet’s index position compounds only if every agent platform eventually needs a neutral data plane across compute providers. Over 18 months, stored histories, workspace APIs, provenance records, and committed datasets could turn Amulet into an index on agent execution: more agents create more forks, more active GiB, and more switching costs. Amulet currently lacks the distribution control required for that loop, while Modal can bundle storage with more than $300M in annualized revenue, E2B owns the sandbox boundary, and Archil has $18M to pursue the adjacent filesystem layer.

Amulet reaches $1–3M ARR by July 2029 and is acquired by E2B for under $50M to become E2B’s persistent workspace layer. Amulet’s technically bounded miracle makes the product plausible, while Amulet’s missing revenue curve and weak standalone distribution make acquisition more likely than independent category leadership. The buyer already controls the agent-sandbox entry point; the filesystem becomes more valuable inside that distribution than beside it.

Amulet’s strongest bull case is that Archil’s $18M round validates the category, the Meta-trained founders have already shipped unusually strong infrastructure, and sub-100-ms mounts plus zero-copy writable forks become the Stripe-like default beneath every agent runtime. Amulet could then be skating for the puck before usage is publicly visible, exactly when consensus metrics are least useful. The dossier evidence that wins today is the combination of a recent unrelated-product pivot, unverified benchmarks, unnamed beta partners, partial POSIX coverage, and three well-funded routes to bundling. Six consecutive months above 20% month-over-month paid active-GiB growth, including three named agent-platform customers, would flip my verdict to Invest.
