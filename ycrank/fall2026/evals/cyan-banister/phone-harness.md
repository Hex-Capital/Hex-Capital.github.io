# Phone Harness -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I want to tell my agent to handle an errand without caring whether the business offers an API. Phone Harness gives that future something concrete to touch: an actual phone. But the distance matters. Reliable execution, authentication, and permission to act must all come together, and the README excludes Face ID, camera, and multitouch flows. The dossier establishes a working control mechanism; it does not yet establish the sequence that turns phone access into dependable delegated agency.

Why should someone need Xcode just to let software operate their own iPhone? Removing that requirement is a useful little rebellion. Phone Harness uses iPhone Mirroring without phone-side installation, reducing setup labor and returning some control to the device owner. The economic claim is narrower than dismantling an institutional tollbooth, though. There is no measured customer burden or identified rent being eliminated. Hosted access at $0.05 per phone-minute creates a purchasing option; whether it saves users meaningful time remains unmeasured.

Shawn Pana’s experience leading growth at Browser Use matters because developer infrastructure needs someone who can recruit developers into a new habit. Product launches and developer partnerships are relevant operating experience, and the separately listed smux project, with approximately 1,500 stars, provides another public artifact beyond this company. What I cannot find is the biography of an obsession: dated experiments, a problem he repeatedly encountered, or evidence that this particular pursuit preceded the market’s enthusiasm. That is missing evidence, not evidence of opportunism. His Founder/CEO role also gives me an identifiable accountable person; there is no documented split-control problem here.

The strongest argument against passing is that Phone Harness has already escaped the slide deck. Approximately 4,000 stars and 403 forks as of October 27, 2026, plus an available cloud beta, create a credible route from a free tool into hosted infrastructure. This fits my pattern of finding an enabling business beneath a visible wave. The harness supplies controls to external agents across iPhone and Android rather than selling one narrow agent application. But a fork does not establish repeated workflow execution, and published pricing does not establish payment. MIT licensing helps distribution while letting developers take the implementation elsewhere; Droidrun/Mobilerun and iphone-use make that competitive alternative concrete. There is useful infrastructure here, with limited evidence of control over a scarce dependency.

The September 16 telemetry report is where my willingness to experiment stops short of a check. It alleges transmission of scripts, OCR output, and unredacted typing arguments by default. An instruction for disabling telemetry does not establish that the reviewed behavior has been corrected or that users knowingly consented. Phone Harness could give an individual more control over their phone while exposing what that individual does with it. I can make exceptions for missing revenue when a prototype, demonstrated obsession, and customer commitments carry the bet. Those supporting facts are not established here, and repository enthusiasm does not resolve a reported sensitive-data path. There is also no evidence of a devoted fringe whose unusual behavior would justify overriding the concern.

I pass with my own money today. The infrastructure direction interests me; the evidence of durable user behavior and trustworthy custody does not yet support the commitment. My flip condition is one independently verified, 30-day production pilot in which three unrelated customers renew paid recurring workflows and an accompanying data-flow audit confirms that scripts, OCR, and typing stay local unless users explicitly authorize transmission. That would turn developer curiosity into demonstrated dependence while addressing the issue that currently keeps me from inhabiting the product myself.
