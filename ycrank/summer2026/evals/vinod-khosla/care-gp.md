# Care GP -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Care GP has proved a valuable workflow, not yet a consequential technology. Moving document allocation from six hours to under 30 minutes is a measured 12x improvement inside an undeniable healthcare labor shortage. More than 3 million documents processed, 400 clinic operators, and 50% weekly usage growth soon after launch show that clinics want the result. But the dossier never converts Australia’s 7,000 clinics or the US$37.5 billion global administration market into an orders-of-magnitude societal consequence. A large cost pool is not automatically a Black Swan.

The strongest evidence is operational. Care GP reached US$200,000 ARR after 3.5 months, US$5,000 ACV, 85% pilot conversion, zero reported churn, and profitability within six months. Word of mouth tripled the business over 90 days without cold calls. This fits my unstated willingness to fund execution-heavy software when a small team attacks an expensive existing workflow. It also creates a credible learning loop: every processed document can improve identifier matching and clinic-specific allocation. These are substantially better signals than a pre-seed demonstration.

The technical claim remains incomplete. Samantha delivers a 12x workflow improvement against manual allocation, but Care GP reports a 5%–20% human edit rate and supplies no independent accuracy, cost, or retention comparison against Facere. Facere already offers overlapping document and voice automation inside Bp Premier. A broader roadmap across email, billing, referrals, and records transfer is product breadth, not proof of architectural novelty. Care GP may become the operating layer for primary-care administration, but today it could also become a well-executed integration business exposed to commoditizing models and incumbent distribution.

The largest kill risk is whether accuracy and correction data compound faster than competing products. Yet the dossier gives no threshold such as error rate by document class, improvement per million documents, automated exception rate, or clinic retention after twelve months. ISO 27001, Australian data residency, and identifier cross-checking reduce security and filing risk, but they do not establish a risk-removal plan. Hiring a senior engineer while this uncertainty remains unquantified could fund scale before proving the moat. I want the next dollars buying a decisive comparison with Facere, not another workflow added to the menu.

The bull case stings. This could follow the exception in which an initially undifferentiated operational wedge expands into a much larger infrastructure platform: Care GP already has strong conversion, profitable economics, distribution through Bp Premier, and usage across hundreds of operators. The material difference is that the dossier does not establish either a uniquely adaptable founder team or proprietary capability that competitors cannot reproduce. Melvin Chen brings company-building and commercial experience, but no documented clinical expertise, belief revision, or risk-specific recruiting; the disappearance of two previously named co-founders is also unresolved.

I would not write the check today. This is a Neutral, evidence-rich company with an Invest path, not a generic AI-wrapper dismissal. The verifiable flip condition is a blinded, third-party benchmark showing Care GP autonomously completes at least 95% of document and voice workflows at half Facere’s fully loaded clinic cost, while retaining at least 90% of a twelve-month customer cohort. That would turn three million processed documents from a usage statistic into proof of a compounding technical and economic advantage.
