# SE3 Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a robot model developer is the strongest supported route. SE3 reports access to industrial, processing, and hospitality sites and offers standardized workcells that run customer policies and capture human corrections. A buyer could gain a repeatable way to test policy updates and collect training data from failures. The value of owning that capability depends on whether it improves deployment outcomes or reduces testing costs enough to justify buying it. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The memo records 500 released teleoperation episodes across 10 tasks. SE3 also reports zero full-task completions in 100 zero-shot Dream-1 trials. A paid partner-site pilot that measures how much corrections improve completion on held-out tasks would strengthen the acquisition case. The pilot would require workcells, operators, site access, and recording permissions. The memo leaves the cost and funding of that work open. A sale could convey the workcells, while continued site access and reuse of recordings would depend on permissions and contract terms. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Using the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% and dilutes to 3% without follow-ons. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, which requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity. The sale scenarios assume full holder eligibility, an all-cash purchase of the entire stake by year eight, and pro rata distribution after net debt and deal adjustments, with no senior preferences.

- **Modest sale.** $50 million in enterprise value less $5 million in adjustments leaves $45 million in equity and pays the holder $1.35 million, or 2.7×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value less $10 million in adjustments leaves $90 million in equity and pays the holder $2.7 million, or 5.4×. At 2.5% ownership, the same sale pays $2.25 million, or 4.5×.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with reinvested cash yields $0 in realized proceeds by year eight.

The sale prices and deductions are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no basis for pricing an acquisition or underwriting an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Robot developers can use RoboDojo to run their own benchmarks or turn to Enact for physical rollouts and recovery data. SE3’s reported site access could give a buyer a reason to own the workflow, but partner-site sandboxes are described as a next step and raw recording requires authorization. Those facts leave a testable gap in buyer economics and transferability. The Pass gate requires a supported structural block or materially inadequate proceeds under the strongest credible route, and the memo establishes neither. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid partner-site pilot show that owning SE3’s testing and correction workflow would create enough value for a robot model developer to support an acquisition above the proceeds hurdle? Repeatable gains on held-out tasks, combined with the buyer’s planned rollout volume and cost of building or contracting for the same capability, would move the action to Reach out if the resulting economics support that price.
