# Axonnic -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Axonnic has built something. What I cannot yet see is what the founders did when building it became difficult. Williams’s EMG research and hardware work, and Patankar’s robotics software experience, make them plausible people to attempt this. But the dossier contains no specific obstacle followed by a founder-built workaround and an observable result. That leaves resourcefulness unproven. It also contains no rejected hypothesis and subsequent product change. Neither absence establishes passivity or stubbornness; both receive the missing-evidence defaults.

The strongest evidence is the product demonstration: company-reported finger tracking with 4.58° average joint-angle error, 50 predictions per second, and 78 minutes of training data. Connecting the software to a Proception robotic hand gives the claim more substance than an architecture diagram. This fits a pattern I like: technical founders making a difficult proposition inspectable. But the benchmark is self-reported, and reconstructing human finger positions is only an intermediate result. Axonnic still has to show that recording muscles teaches a robot something useful that video and hand pose alone cannot teach it.

Force-sensitive sewing is a good place to discover that. It is a specific job where the difference between seeing a movement and understanding its force could matter. The Italian manufacturer LOI and separately reported Perloro Packing Srl pilot provide some evidence of customer intent. They do not establish repeat use or improved autonomous performance. Construction recordings show data collection, not customer demand. I give Axonnic credit for finding an industrial application, while discounting the LOI until someone repeatedly needs the resulting product. Missing revenue at this stage is not the objection.

The strongest case for investing is that these founders are already crossing the unpleasant boundary between a laboratory signal and a factory task. Williams’s background makes that case credible. Yet the dossier does not establish that he encountered this particular customer problem through his prior work, so I cannot infer an organic origin from relevant credentials. Hemlock also offers EMG-based robot-training data. Axonnic therefore needs an advantage in what robots learn, beyond collecting this modality. Dataset licensing could become a business, but pricing and cross-customer data rights remain unestablished; a proprietary data advantage cannot yet be assumed.

I would pass at 40. The showcase prize and investor backing do not answer the founder question. Nor would I treat the public launch as evidence of a launch-dependent strategy, or the Proception integration as proof of dependence on one partner. Unknown cofounder history is missing evidence, not an adverse finding; a working prototype can justify looking past that uncertainty. The stronger exception—technical validation or unmistakable user pull sufficient to replace a weak founder prior—is not established here. My flip condition is one documented deployment cycle at Perloro: a specific task failure, a dated correction built by the founders, and repeated paid use after that correction measurably improves autonomous task success. That would connect the promising demonstration to both resourcefulness and need.
