# Greypoint Industries -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Darcy Zhang’s infantry experience and the $55.25 billion projected counter-UAS market make Greypoint Industries a domain-native hardware bet in a vast market, but Greypoint Industries has no network in which the next user makes every prior user more valuable. That is the signature: high pivot-room, low compounding-user value. LEGION may coordinate machines, but coordination is not a network effect when every military customer buys through a separate permissioned contract.

**Uber** is the high rung on market optionality: black cars became UberX, logistics, delivery, and a transportation platform because a huge market allowed the original idea to be wrong. Greypoint Industries shares that room—LEGION sensing, ARROW interception, Arctic surveillance, electronic warfare, and battlefield command are credible adjacencies inside a market growing at 22.4% annually. Greypoint Industries diverges where the analogy becomes load-bearing: Uber could test pivots through software and existing users, while controlled-goods registration, export permits, and competitive military qualification make every Greypoint pivot slower and more capital-dependent.

**Perplexity’s** low-rung weakness was a single-sided product whose users did not directly increase one another’s value; extraordinary founder knowledge and permissionless software distribution overrode it. Greypoint Industries has the same missing network but lacks the override. The signed Arrow contract adds one customer, not liquidity; the technical-briefing CTA starts another sales cycle, not a flywheel. Greypoint Industries could eventually create a data advantage across deployments, but no deployment volume, shared threat-data layer, or third-party developer ecosystem is public today.

Canada’s Defence Drone Initiative puts Greypoint Industries inside an 18-month mechanism governed by capital and permission: qualify hardware, manufacture interceptors, obtain controlled-goods approvals, integrate with military systems, and win contracts. Each success may produce operational data and manufacturing learning, but each additional Greypoint Industries customer still requires procurement labor and government consent. Code sits inside the product; code is not the distribution. “Product and Media are New Leverage” distinguishes software replication from software embedded in an aircraft that must be built, exported, and approved.

Jordan Khoo’s UAS and control-systems work, Steven Xu’s Sailbot controls and embedded-systems background, Nic Chu’s General Dynamics experience, and Darcy Zhang’s infantry service give Greypoint Industries the strongest founder-specific signal in the dossier. **Stack Overflow** was developers building for developers; Greypoint Industries is operators and embedded engineers building for drone defense. The parallel stops short of the top rung because Greypoint Industries publishes no performance artifact—no interception accuracy, range, test volume, field deployment, patent, or verified repository—so specific knowledge is credible from biographies but not yet proven through an exceptional shipped system.

Jordan Khoo, Nic Chu, and Steven Xu were already building together through UBC Applied Science in 2025, which gives Greypoint Industries evidence of energy and working history. The signed Arrow contract improves the intelligence read; no contested exits or integrity concerns appear. Greypoint Industries would move into the top founder tier if reference calls showed the four founders allocating authority cleanly, honoring commitments under failed tests, and operating together through a year of procurement pressure. Until then, integrity is appropriately unproven rather than presumed, and four co-founders leave more relationship surface area than the dossier resolves.

Fortem’s 4,500 reported captures and Anduril’s Lattice-cued Anvil make my 36-month forecast concrete: by July 2029, Greypoint Industries will have $3 million–$8 million in annualized Canadian defense contracts and will be acquired by Anduril for under $150 million, rather than becoming an independent compounding platform. Greypoint Industries can create strategic value through scarce engineering and Canadian qualification, but the low-network, permissioned-distribution architecture routes value toward a scaled prime contractor that already owns manufacturing, procurement relationships, and command software.

Canada’s first Defence Tech Hackathon win, the signed Arrow contract, and the July 2026 low-cost-interceptor initiative form the strongest objection: Greypoint Industries could be an **Anchorage Digital** exception, where permission becomes the moat and early regulatory position compounds. That bull case stings because sovereign procurement can lock in vendors for decades. Greypoint Industries still discloses no range, accuracy, field interceptions, production yield, or structural barrier while Fortem, Airobotics, and Anduril disclose operational histories. I would flip to Invest if Greypoint Industries verifies at least 100 successful field interceptions and demonstrates LEGION integrating third-party sensors and interceptors across two named military customers; then qualification would be turning into a platform rather than merely a contract.
