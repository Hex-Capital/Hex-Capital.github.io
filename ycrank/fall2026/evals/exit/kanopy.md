# Kanopy -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition is Kanopy’s strongest supported route. ACRT introduced Arborcision, which integrates Kanopy’s video-based clearance measurements into its vegetation management services. That gives a utility contractor a concrete use for the product. Schneider Electric’s announced AiDASH acquisition shows strategic interest in an adjacent workflow. A buyer could acquire Kanopy to capture more of the margin from inspection and trimming work, but the memo provides no measured buyer savings or revenue uplift to support a purchase price. (The Idea; Key Risks)

The next milestone is a paid, recurring utility deployment that field-validates clearance measurements and quantifies the field visits or costs saved. Kanopy reports 3,093 miles mapped. Antler reports six LOIs or master service agreements covering 270,000 miles of potential scope, with committed order volumes undisclosed. The mapping activity makes a validation test plausible at seed. The budget, runway, and time required to reach repeat paid deployment remain unverified. Kanopy’s hosted models and credentialed SDK make a software acquisition plausible, with IP ownership, footage rights, and contract consents still open. The memo supplies no evidence for an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction; Key Risks; Defensibility)

Using the benchmark $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons, the holder needs $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years. That requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3% pro rata participation. The following figures are sensitivity assumptions. The memo supplies no transaction price or holder terms. (Key Facts; Key Risks)

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge and holder cash |
|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40 million enterprise value less $5 million in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million in equity and $1.05 million in holder cash, or 2.1×. |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value less the same $5 million leaves $95 million in equity and $2.85 million in holder cash, or 5.7×. If only 80% of that allocation is realized in cash within eight years, proceeds fall to $2.28 million, or 4.56×. |
| Prolonged private | Continued operations with cash reinvested and no funded payout or eligible share sale yield $0 in realized holder proceeds within eight years. |

The full-cash sale cases assume the holder can sell their entire stake, the deal closes within eight years, and the waterfall pays the holder pro rata with no senior preference reducing that allocation. The strong case clears the hurdle under those assumptions. The disclosed acquisition and operating evidence do not establish that a buyer would pay that price. (Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Countercase

ACRT already offers the capability through a partnership, so it can sell the combined service without buying Kanopy. Schneider Electric’s announced AiDASH deal also points to an overlapping product in a strategic buyer’s portfolio. If utilities retain substantial field checks, the savings from Kanopy’s measurements may be too small to justify ownership at a price that clears the holder’s hurdle. The memo establishes neither a comparable substitute for Kanopy’s specific clearance workflow nor a supported sale-price ceiling. The Pass gate stays open pending evidence on buyer economics. (The Idea; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could a strategic buyer justify from the incremental economics of owning Kanopy over continuing to license its technology? Paid, recurring deployments with field-validated accuracy and measured savings could support a buyer-level estimate. A credible price range that clears $83.3 million in distributable equity under plausible closing and waterfall terms would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that even the strongest credible bid leaves the holder materially short would move it to Pass.
