# Last Accounting Company -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The founders have acted close enough to the problem that their knowledge is real, but not yet exact. Riki Karjalainen and Sami Laakkonen built petabyte-scale financial-data infrastructure at Token Terminal; Veikko Pere brings finance, legal, consulting, and private-equity exposure. More importantly, this four-person team has already put a proprietary ledger into production for dozens of Finnish companies and processed more than $80 million in transaction value. That is shipped evidence, not résumé inference. The remaining gap is that none of the founders is documented as having operated an accounting firm, owned statutory filings, or sold this service before LAC.

The product is more substantial than an AI accounting wrapper. Björn collects documents, reconciles transactions, prepares filings, and maintains books inside LAC’s own general ledger; accountants review the output, and the system preserves evidence links, permissions, controls, and customer-separated records. If a competitor copies the interface tomorrow, it does not immediately inherit those reviewed histories or integrations. But the dossier only infers that corrections and design-partner data become a proprietary learning loop. Pilot, Truewind, and Zeni already combine automation with human expertise, while the underlying models and computer-use capabilities are broadly available. A possible data asset is not yet a compounding asset.

The large-market path is credible but incomplete. LAC begins with a €1.4 billion Finnish accounting market and can point toward Europe’s $174.2 billion market, growing 7.7% annually. The shared capability—automating the movement from source documents through ledger entries to reviewed filings—could travel across the Nordics and eventually Europe. The constraint is localization: tax rules, payroll systems, bank integrations, liability, and accounting practice change by jurisdiction. Europe is not one adjacent market merely because it appears as one number.

The weak point is leverage. Each new customer currently creates more private accounting work, security exposure, and human-review obligations; the dossier supplies no referral, contribution, liquidity, or customer-to-customer utility evidence. This consolidates fragmented software and professional services, but it does not yet convert fragmented accountants or customers into a permissionless network. The Notion exception is only partially relevant: both companies put a developed product language ahead of an observable user loop, but LAC’s output still requires recurring professional review while its competitors can buy access to the same foundation models. The proprietary ledger must therefore compound faster than the service burden.

The strongest bull case is that “dozens” of companies and $80 million processed are unusually concrete for pre-seed, and fixed-price replacement of both accounting software and the accounting firm captures value directly. The bear case still wins on current evidence: no published price, revenue, retention, gross margin, or automation rate shows that transaction throughput becomes scalable economics rather than supervised labor. The company’s direct monetization model is sensible; its value capture is not yet proven.

I see no disclosed integrity problem, artificial financing deadline, founder instability, or other behavioral red flag. The childhood co-founder relationship may support a durable operating unit, but it is not evidence of integrity under pressure. I also find no known-exception mechanism strong enough to override the missing leverage: there is neither a self-reinforcing network nor a demonstrated proprietary model. My verifiable flip condition is twelve months of cohort evidence showing paid customers retaining while human-review minutes per completed monthly close fall by at least 50%, with corrections becoming exclusive training or evaluation data. Until then, this is a credible accounting firm built around agents, not yet an agent-native compounding system.
