# AnyAlpha -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

AnyAlpha’s founders have chosen a problem their reported experience actually touches. Shirley built trading systems at Coinbase and LSEG; Jerry describes six years spanning quantitative data, models, backtesting, and execution. Their published app supplies an observable artifact of shipping together. But Jerry’s reported growth from $50K to seven figures and the pair’s prior $500K ARR remain founder-reported claims, not independently corroborated operating records. I give them credit for action without underwriting the full résumé.

The decisive question is what AnyAlpha accumulates after it ships. A trader supplies a thesis; the software finds contracts, tests conditions, and executes. That can save real attention. But Composer and Capitalise.ai already demonstrate adjacent versions of this workflow, and Polystrat operates directly in prediction markets. If a competitor reproduces the workflow, the dossier identifies no exclusive data, proprietary model, distribution right, or customer-created asset that remains with AnyAlpha. This earns the missing-evidence default for durability. There is insufficient evidence to call its architecture a commodity wrapper, and equally insufficient evidence to buy a durable advantage.

The product fits my preference for tools that let individuals turn knowledge into scalable output. It has not yet demonstrated the next step: one participant making the system more useful for another. Private agents trading separate accounts do not automatically create a network. Shared strategies or execution records might eventually do so, but neither contribution behavior nor rights to reuse those records are established. Likewise, broader automated investing is a real adjacent category, but competitors serving equities and crypto do not establish AnyAlpha’s own route into those buyers. I cannot award a network or a large expansion path on resemblance alone.

The strongest argument against passing is that these founders may already possess the scarce asset: practical judgment about trading systems, combined with three years of building together and reported experience monetizing consumer apps. I can back a working prototype before network effects appear when the founders demonstrate unusual product insight and capital efficiency. Here, the public evidence does not yet establish that exception. The 42 iOS ratings span an earlier product, and the $53 earned by a $100 demonstration account measures one trading outcome, not repeat demand or company economics. Listed purchases of $4.99–$79.99 show a possible collection mechanism, but the BetAlpha labels and unspecified entitlements leave current-agent value capture unproven.

I would pass with my own money. The sports-information terms alongside trading-agent positioning create a concrete disclosure question; they do not establish dishonesty. Nor does the dossier show unfair dealing or manufactured fundraising urgency. The obstacle is structural: the team’s knowledge has not yet become an asset that compounds beyond its labor. My one verifiable flip condition is a prospective, independently audited test showing that an exclusively licensed dataset accumulated through AnyAlpha deployments materially improves out-of-sample execution quality over the identical system without that dataset. That would turn a plausible future advantage into something I could own.
