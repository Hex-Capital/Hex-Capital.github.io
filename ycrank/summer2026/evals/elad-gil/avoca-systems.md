# Avoca Systems -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: voice models can now handle patient access, radiology networks are buying automation to recover abandoned bookings, and Avoca Systems becomes large only if the contribution from recovered appointments materially exceeds the cost of integration, exception handling, and clinical errors. The dossier establishes the first two pieces but does not quantify the controlling economics. A 90% reduction in call abandonment is promising; the missing variable is how that translates into durable incremental revenue per clinic after deployment costs.

Demand is Avoca’s strongest evidence. The product is live across several radiology networks, including groups operating more than 150 clinics. Jobfit reports 86% call resolution and 4.6/5 satisfaction, while another customer reports 30 bookings converted within half a day. Lumus Imaging, Western Radiology, ForHealth, and other named logos make this more informative than a waitlist or pilot announcement. Revenue, retention, expansion, and contract scope remain undisclosed, so I cannot yet distinguish repeatable product-market pull from a few successful enterprise deployments.

This is also more substantial than a voice wrapper. Avoca combines calls, online booking, e-referrals, longitudinal communications, clinic rules, human escalation, and real-time RIS/EHR write-back. Epic, Oracle Health, MEDITECH, Karisma, Aura, and Visage integrations place the product inside the operational loop. Better models should improve call handling while Avoca retains workflow state, permissions, exceptions, and responsibility for completing the booking. That architecture is directionally durable.

The strongest bear case is market structure. openDoctor and AbbaDox already cover radiology scheduling, referrals, and integrations; Assort Health and Prosper AI have raised $222 million and $35 million respectively to pursue broader patient-access workflows. The measured radiology-scheduling market was only $780 million in 2025, growing 10.32% annually. Avoca has not shown that AI changes margins or expands the category rather than intensifying competition inside an existing software budget. Australia-only hosting also creates real work for U.S. expansion, while missing athenahealth and eClinicalWorks integrations constrain distribution.

The founders raise the probability of navigating those constraints. Max Shand was Afterpay’s first employee and sold Serenade in 2024; Campbell Mercer-Butcher has led engineering across insurance, property software, and a prior startup. This is credible second-time learning, although neither biography supplies radiology expertise or evidence of personal desperation around the problem. No known-exception override is needed or available: this is not frontier research carried by uniquely scarce technical talent. Its better fit is the pattern of software performing institutional work, but the red flags around reproducible integrations, installed competitors, and unquantified economics remain decisive.

I would monitor rather than invest today. The verifiable flip condition is Avoca reaching $3 million in U.S. ARR across at least ten radiology networks, with deployments completed inside 90 days and net revenue retention above 120%. That would show the recovered-booking benefit survives integration costs and that the first workflow creates reusable distribution. Without it, Avoca may become a useful radiology product without becoming a large independent company.
