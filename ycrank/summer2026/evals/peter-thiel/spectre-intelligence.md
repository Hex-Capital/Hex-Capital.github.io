# Spectre Intelligence -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The contested truth is precise enough to matter: sector-specialized AI agents trained on live decisions and ground-truth P&L can eventually outperform funds that scale by hiring additional human analysts. The falsifier is equally clear—after enough trades, the agents fail to produce persistent risk-adjusted returns outside their training period. Spectre Intelligence has begun this experiment with real semiconductor and biotech positions and reports profitability. But it discloses neither the return series nor AUM, drawdown, benchmark, dataset size, or out-of-sample performance. This is a secret under investigation, not yet a discovered secret.

The deeper problem is value capture. The two sector pods form a bounded beachhead, and their decision traces, expert inputs, and realized outcomes could become proprietary training material. Yet Spectre itself says successful strategies will be copied and their alpha will decay. Abundance, Nof1, Numerai, and established quantitative funds can access similar models, market data, and execution infrastructure. A dataset is not automatically a monopoly mechanism; if its labels describe opportunities that disappear once exploited, accumulated experience may measure yesterday’s market more accurately without controlling tomorrow’s.

The founders make this more credible than an ordinary “AI hedge fund” pitch. Michelle Li brings biology research and personal trading experience to the biotech pod; Joshua Zyzak brings reinforcement learning, AI engineering, and exposure to Optiver. They met at Harvard and studied evolutionary biology together, which explains the competing-agent architecture. Li is on leave, but the dossier does not establish that both founders are full-time, that they previously shipped together, or that they retain meaningful economic ownership. This is missing evidence rather than a damaged foundation, but it prevents confidence in the founding structure.

The bull case is unusually sharp: two students have already deployed real capital, created live feedback loops, and become profitable before building a conventional fund organization. Proprietary trading also avoids the normal SaaS distribution problem—good returns attract capital. This resembles the exception for a zero-to-one technical event only superficially, however. The material difference is verification: the dossier supplies a company assertion where the exception supplied an independently observable breakthrough. YC admission and a $5.6 trillion hedge-fund market cannot substitute for audited performance.

There is one coherent revenue engine—proprietary trading P&L, followed potentially by management and performance fees—but no arithmetic connecting it to a power-law outcome. Capital capacity, gross and net exposure, fee terms, investor commitments, and scalable strategy capacity are all absent. The compensated expert network may improve inputs, but it is not yet a demonstrated route to capital, and adviser obligations would appear if outside money becomes central. Spectre could become an excellent small trading partnership without becoming a defining technology company.

I pass today. The company has a real experiment but no demonstrated monopoly: secret or slogan remains unresolved, while alpha decay is already acknowledged. The verifiable condition that would flip me is an independently audited 12-month record showing at least a 20% net return with less than a 10% maximum drawdown on at least $25 million of capital, while maintaining performance as AUM grows. Without that proof, I expect Spectre Intelligence to remain a small proprietary desk or pivot toward selling financial-model training and benchmarking infrastructure within 36 months.
