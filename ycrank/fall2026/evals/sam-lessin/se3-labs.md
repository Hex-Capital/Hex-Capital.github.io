# SE3 Labs -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

SE3 Labs has shown that it can run robots. What my check would buy remains undocumented. Post-money valuation, competing offers, and the proposed capital-funded experiment are all unknown. YC membership establishes neither cheap entry nor an auction. The October 2026 Dream evaluation is a completed research exercise; it does not tell me which otherwise-unfundable hypothesis this round would test, by when, or what success would change about the business.

The interesting asset is the physical workflow between a model developer and deployment reality. SE3 published 100 Dream evaluation episodes across 10 tasks, with recordings, telemetry, and scoring sheets. Its September study recorded 6 completed placements using code-as-policy versus 18 using agentic control, from 20 attempts each. Those are operating receipts. This fits my unstated preference for businesses that translate between software and difficult physical systems. But who owns the resulting advantage? Customer raw data requires written authorization for general-purpose training reuse. SE3 could build valuable operating knowledge, but a cross-customer data flywheel is still a hypothesis. Public documentation establishes availability; it does not establish customer conversion.

The strongest argument against passing is that better robot models could make independent physical evaluation more valuable. SE3 already evaluated Dream through a closed-source API, demonstrating that access to model weights is unnecessary for this workflow. A model release might create another testing assignment. That makes the opportunity more interesting than a generic application dependent on temporary model superiority. Yet Poke & Wiggle already offers physical evaluation, and laboratories can operate their own stations. SE3’s published work establishes measurement capability; the proposed fine-tuning and comparative retesting remain subsequent work. Neither paid demand nor improved deployed policies has been demonstrated in the dossier. My exceptions for AI businesses require observed conversion, retention, or customer economics. These research results do not supply that override.

Delete the next financing from SE3’s plan. Contract-specific project fees could support a useful business, but the terms establish a billing mechanism rather than realized revenue. There is no disclosed margin, burn, runway, or seed-funded break-even plan. I use the missing-economics default; physical hardware alone does not prove bad economics. The founder dimension also stays at default: the three-person team has produced working evaluations, but founder identities and individual operating histories are unverified. Paige Xu’s reported OpenMind role cannot establish that a verified founder has shipped on both sides of this bridge. Nor does an empty founder section establish a leadership problem.

I pass on the evidence available. SE3 has a credible capability whose commercial leverage remains unproven, while both my price gate and venture-moment gate are unanswered. My one verifiable condition for reopening is a customer renewing a failure-directed evaluation engagement at positive contribution margin after a measured improvement in its deployed policy. That would connect the physical workflow to repeat purchasing and cash generation. I would still need to underwrite the round’s price and capital plan before committing my own money.
