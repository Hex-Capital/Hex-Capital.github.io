# Deploy -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Third-party logistics operators staff mixed-item returns, kitting, and packing stations manually because changing items and task sequences make fixed automation difficult. Deploy proposes to make automation economical at those stations by installing robot arms in existing workspaces and routing exceptions to remote operators. The memo records a booth demo in which Deploy packed more than 800 pens after roughly 30 minutes of fine-tuning data collection, along with a public demonstration of RoboJudge. That shows the team can adapt a robot to a narrow packing task. The founders’ experience in field autonomy and robot-model adaptation supports a customer-site test. Manual staffing is an indirect demand signal, and the memo documents no paid pilot or repeat order. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

**Cost scenario.** Assume a station costs the buyer $100 in manual labor for a fixed volume of accepted work. If Deploy delivers the same volume and quality at its advertised discount, the fee is about $50. The buyer’s retained labor, rework, and downtime costs must stay below the remaining $50 for savings to hold. Deploy must also cover hardware recovery, installation, maintenance, inference, and remote operator time within its $50 fee to earn positive contribution. The station is a plausible unit of expansion, but the memo supplies no production measurements for either side of that bridge. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Under model parity, buyers could still pay Deploy to install, operate, and reliably recover a station. That advantage depends on Deploy executing the service better than a credible alternative. Intervention data could reduce operator effort across sites if recoveries transfer, which the memo leaves unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility)

### Countercase

A repetitive booth task can succeed while a live station with changing items still needs enough intervention and rework to erase the savings. Even if the station saves money, Plus One already offers remote exception handling in adjacent warehouse workflows and could extend it to the same tasks. The memo leaves both required conditions open. It shows neither a production cost comparison nor a buyer switching to an equivalent service, so the case stays at Watch. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent logistics operator pay to expand Deploy after a representative mixed-item station trial? Move to Reach out if the trial shows lower full cost per accepted job than manual staffing at matched throughput and quality, positive contribution for Deploy, and a paid expansion based on a demonstrated service advantage over a credible alternative with equivalent model capability. Move to Pass if representative results show that exception handling erases the savings or that the buyer can readily switch to an equivalent service on comparable terms. An inconclusive trial keeps the action at Watch.
