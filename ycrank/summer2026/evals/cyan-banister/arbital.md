# Arbital -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032 and I can imagine markets becoming continuous, asset boundaries dissolving, and traders expecting one programmable account across equities, tokens, and derivatives. Arbital has not yet shown me the steps from here to there. The dossier’s “why now” is $6.7 trillion of 2025 perpetual-DEX volume—a large present wave, not a leak from a distinct future. The real leak is behavioral: 2,600 traders moved $1.3 billion through Arbital in seven months, including $377 million in current monthly volume. People are already trusting this machine with costly actions.

That usage is extraordinary for pre-seed, but I cannot tell which machine earned it. The website sells a decentralized market-making engine; the YC page describes a forthcoming social, mobile-first terminal spanning perps, memes, and stocks. The reported volume is not separated between those products. If the existing engine generated nearly all of it, Arbital has validated automated execution—not the consumer migration underlying its broader story. That discontinuity matters more than the headline number.

The founders are the strongest part of the bet. Karon Pangestu helped grow Aevo from roughly $1 billion to $100 billion in cumulative volume and previously built Launchy to more than 20,000 crypto participants. Steven Mulyawan onboarded more than 10,000 traders through KOL, agency, and high-net-worth relationships. Vincent Owen Toniwan built market-making infrastructure operating below two milliseconds and handling 100,000 events per second. This is complementary commercial and technical tuition earned inside the exact machinery they are rebuilding. What is missing is evidence that the obsession predates that employment or produced independent artifacts over several years.

I keep asking what rule Arbital breaks. “Traders use too many applications” is friction, but it does not identify the rent, artificial restriction, or gatekeeper responsible for the fragmentation. Reduced fees and boosted exchange points may simply redistribute venue incentives. The product also inherits jurisdictional restrictions around perpetuals and tokenized equities without disclosing licenses or eligible markets. I cannot yet say whose agency is structurally restored rather than temporarily subsidized.

The bull case stings: few teams can combine established trader distribution, production-grade execution experience, multi-venue integrations, and $377 million of monthly behavior this early. Cross-venue data could eventually make routing better and turn the interface into the trader’s primary relationship. But today Arbital sits above the scarce infrastructure. It depends on DEX liquidity, fee schedules, and incentive programs while Axiom, Fomo, Backpack, and Hyperliquid attack overlapping surfaces. A terminal can be copied; controlled order flow or demonstrably superior execution economics are harder.

My 36-month base case is that Arbital retreats from the broad social multi-asset promise and becomes a narrower B2B cross-venue execution and market-making product producing $3–8 million in annualized net revenue. That could be a good company, but it is not yet the future-defining company implied by the pitch. I am Neutral. I flip to Invest if one cohort of at least 500 traders shows 40% 90-day retention, repeatedly trades across two or more asset classes, and generates positive net revenue after exchange incentives.
