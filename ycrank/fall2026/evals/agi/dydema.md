# Dydema -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Dydema has a narrow industry-enabled path in hands-free confirmations for computer-agent users. The memo describes lab equipment and individual setup as barriers to EEG control, with existing programmable headsets requiring users to train commands. Dydema’s research-cap demo showed three imagined movements mapped to actions, and the company reports that its pretrained decoder works on new users after brief calibration. The seed-stage assumption is that less time spent training commands could make occasional hands-free confirmations practical for users who currently skip EEG. Under model parity, buyers would still need a wearable that reliably captures their signals and an integration that turns those signals into actions. Setup time, false triggers, and cost per successful confirmation on the intended consumer headset remain unmeasured in the memo. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The building-blocks path scores zero. The headset supplies a user input at the application layer, and the memo identifies no AI workload whose deployment is constrained by access to EEG data or controls. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Dydema’s reported EEG standardization and internal benchmark gains suggest early operating know-how, with limited evidence that it can sustain an advantage over competing headsets. The founders’ EEG and machine-learning backgrounds and the research-cap demo support prototype execution. Launch attention and listed API prices establish visibility and an asking price. Buyer commitments, repeat paid use, and the cost to deliver and support the next headset remain unknown. (Defensibility, Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Dydema could be making an existing EEG feature easier to set up. Emotiv and Neurosity already offer programmable commands, keyboards and mice handle the underlying actions, and Dydema’s demo uses a research cap with a small command set. If buyers find the calibration gains convenient while the same users and uses remain viable with existing options, the industry-enabled score falls to zero and the action becomes Pass. The current demo leaves that gate unresolved, which supports Watch. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will agent users who previously skipped EEG because of setup burden pay for and repeatedly use hands-free confirmations on Dydema’s intended consumer headset? A paid pilot with those users would move the action to Reach out if it shows shorter setup than an existing programmable EEG option and repeat use at a price that covers delivery and support, with false triggers low enough for users to keep the feature enabled.
