# Organizely -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to an adjacent manufacturing ERP vendor is the strongest supported route. Organizely combines maintenance and production planning in one factory model. A vendor such as Fulcrum could use that capability to extend its scheduling product. That buyer rationale is an inference. The memo documents the factory ERP launch and reports that Kinetic Labs, the founders’ prior business, uses Organizely for demand planning and supplier approvals. Kinetic Labs reported a 22% higher operating margin and 10 hours saved per employee per week. Organizely reported deployments across six states, with installation counts and customer identities undisclosed. (*The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks*)

A seed-stage milestone would be multiple unaffiliated, paying plants renewing after demonstrating measurable savings from the combined workflows, with implementation time and cost recorded. The reported deployments and on-site setup make that test plausible. Factory ERP pricing, onboarding costs, and runway remain unknown, leaving the capital and time needed to reach the milestone uncertain. (*The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks*)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale scenarios are illustrative. Both assume $10 million in combined debt and deal deductions, no further senior claims, and an eligible holder receiving cash for the full stake pro rata within eight years.

- **Modest sale.** A $50 million enterprise value leaves $40 million in distributable equity and pays the holder $1.2 million, or 2.4×.
- **Strong sale.** A $100 million enterprise value leaves $90 million in distributable equity and pays the holder $2.7 million, or 5.4×. At 2.5% ownership, the same sale pays $2.25 million, or 4.5×.
- **Prolonged private operation.** Continued reinvestment with no funded payout or eligible share purchase produces zero realized cash within eight years.

The memo leaves realizable sale prices, transaction rights and consents, and independent liquidity routes unevidenced. Those dimensions receive their defaults.

### Countercase

A strategic buyer could build or source overlapping functionality. Sutton already offers AI-based manufacturing ERP, and Bizmark’s Otto automates procurement and production planning. With the quantified factory results coming from a founder-affiliated operation, a buyer has limited evidence that owning Organizely would deliver repeatable gains across its customers. That weakens the case for paying a price that clears the return hurdle. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the memo establishes a substitution risk while leaving structural barriers and achievable prices unresolved. (*Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Would an adjacent ERP vendor’s build-versus-buy analysis support a price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity within eight years? A buyer-side valuation range grounded in repeatable savings at unaffiliated, paying plants, with enough room for deal deductions and the holder’s waterfall, would move the action to Reach out.
