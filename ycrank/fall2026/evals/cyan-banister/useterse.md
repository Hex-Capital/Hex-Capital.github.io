# Terse -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years forward, I can imagine agents negotiating shared work, waiting for human permission, and resuming days later without forgetting what they promised. Someone has to make that ordinary. Terse’s actor-owned SQLite storage and serialized requests address part of that machinery. But the dossier does not connect today’s runtime to that future through specific intervening unlocks or an application already exhibiting the new behavior. A counter demo establishes a primitive. I still need the small, surprising thing someone builds with it that makes the destination visible.

The plumbing attracts me. My preference for concealed infrastructure puts Terse squarely on the interesting side of the application boom: persistent state and coordination can serve agents, collaborative software, and realtime applications. Its MIT-licensed, self-hostable runtime also gives developers deployment control without requiring one closed model supplier. Open source is compatible with this bet. The unresolved question is which difficult dependency Terse becomes exceptionally good at owning. Cloudflare and Rivet already supply overlapping actor primitives; generated clients and decorated fields establish a programming approach, but the dossier does not demonstrate a scarce technical advantage.

Why should a developer have to surrender deployment control to obtain convenient stateful objects? Self-hosting gives Terse a concrete answer and returns some operational autonomy to the developer. That earns credit with me. Yet there is no documented fee, imposed wait, or restrictive rule whose removal transforms a customer’s economics. Hosting and support revenue remain hypotheses. I cannot establish from this evidence whether developers gain enough freedom to assume the operating responsibilities that accompany it.

Thomas’s seven years working on Siri and Apple Intelligence, his prior acquisition, and the published RustGPT project with 3.2K stars make this team more interesting than a pair of résumés chasing an agent label. Olivier brings substantial backend experience and publishes runtime releases. Those are credible building credentials. The missing piece is a dated history showing that this particular coordination problem kept bothering them before Terse. The earlier workflow-platform description leaves the origin unresolved; it does not establish opportunism. CEO and CTO responsibilities are named, while actual control arrangements remain unknown. Neither a governance violation nor a severe privacy problem is established here.

The strongest argument against passing is that useful infrastructure can look painfully small before other people build their lives around it. Terse has shipped, attracted 256 stars and 19 forks, and generated a substantive launch response—53 points and 25 comments on Hacker News. My pre-revenue exception for **CosmicBrain AI** applies to the financing stage: enabling technology can deserve a check before sales. The material difference is that its prototype came with signed industrial proofs of concept and a documented lifelong founder obsession. Terse’s public engagement does not yet establish repeated operational reliance. Meanwhile, the October 26 release documents potentially reordered or dropped socket messages, and persistence requires careful mutation handling. These limitations do not prove the runtime unsuitable, but they make observed application behavior especially valuable.

I would pass with my own money today. Terse fits my infrastructure pattern, but its strongest evidence concerns where the product sits; its weakest concerns the future behavior it uniquely enables. I see no evidence of incumbent-dismissal bravado or dependence on one closed model owner, so this is an unproven advantage rather than an active thesis violation. My flip condition is one independently referenceable team running a shared-agent application on Terse for 90 days, with logs demonstrating recovery and concurrent state correctness, and a concrete account of why Cloudflare or Rivet could not meet its deployment requirements. That would turn deployment freedom into a demonstrated reason to depend on Terse.
