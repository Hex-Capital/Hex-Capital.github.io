# TryNearby -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

TryNearby may become a good business. I do not yet see the consequence large enough to make it my kind of option. The dossier describes a $399-per-month replacement for agencies, influencer negotiation, owner labor, or a marketing hire. It cites a $34.2 billion adjacent influencer-platform market, but never quantifies the restaurant cost pool being eliminated or a mechanism for making local commerce orders of magnitude more productive. Two customers reporting nearly doubled revenue and a 50% sales increase are promising anecdotes, not evidence of a 100x societal consequence or even causal attribution.

The commercial execution is unusually strong for pre-seed. TryNearby reached 120-plus paying restaurants in ten months, 37% month-over-month growth, over 90% monthly retention, and roughly half of new restaurants through owner referrals. Its thousands of creators produced more than 2,500 videos and 35 million views. This is real demand, not presentation theater. My 36-month base case is therefore not death: it is a $3 million–$8 million ARR regional SMB-marketing company whose operational quality exceeds its technical leverage.

The missing discontinuity matters. An iMessage agent handling more than 10,000 creator texts monthly is useful automation, but the dossier supplies no measured cost, conversion, reliability, or throughput advantage against an agency, full-time marketer, Hyper, or Tablevert. The accumulated preference and scheduling data might improve matching, yet “might become a data moat” is not a benchmark. Hyper and Tablevert already advertise automated campaign operations, and TikTok and Instagram still control the underlying distribution. Today the differentiation is packaging and execution around commodity models, not a demonstrated 5–10x capability.

The strongest bull case is the team. Yousef Abdelfattah helped build FaZe Clan from age 14 through its public listing; Obaida Albaroudi reportedly helped grow another consumer application from $360,000 to $2 million ARR; Ahmad Ibrahim built infrastructure tracking more than 10 billion video views. The founders combine creator supply, growth operations, and engineering, and two have previously built together. This resembles the exception where a modest initial product can expand into a much larger platform. The material difference is that TryNearby has shown neither that broader infrastructure path nor a quantified value pool it can capture beyond recurring creator visits. There is also no documented belief revision or evidence that the founders recruited specifically against a named existential risk.

Capital allocation remains opaque. Geographic liquidity, platform dependency, attribution, and endorsement compliance are all named risks, but there is no cheapest decisive experiment, pass/fail threshold, cost, owner, or stop condition for any of them. Similarly, the $399 price demonstrates willingness to pay but says nothing about creator compensation, scheduling labor, acquisition cost, city-launch expense, gross margin, or whether each new geography becomes economically self-sustaining. Marketplace density can produce operating leverage; it can also disguise a city-by-city services business.

I pass at this valuation-independent stage because strong execution cannot substitute for consequence and technical discontinuity. One verifiable condition would flip me: an independently measured cohort of at least 500 restaurants across three non-California cities showing at least 5x incremental gross-profit return on the subscription while TryNearby sustains over 90% monthly retention and greater than 50% contribution margin. That would establish a repeatable economic machine rather than an efficiently coordinated marketing service.
