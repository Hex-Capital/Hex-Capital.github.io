# Donkey -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Donkey has identified a real tax on commerce: US importers may pay trading companies 15–30% markups while remaining blind to the underlying factory. Removing that opacity could build a useful business. It does not yet describe an orders-of-magnitude consequence. The only measured customer outcome is one $110,000 pipeline week and a quote 15% below the buyer’s existing supplier from the same factory. That is credible evidence of savings, but it is a 1.15x commercial improvement—not a 10x technical discontinuity or a quantified transformation of a major cost pool.

The product is currently an unusually integrated trading operation. It combines customs-record parsing, factory identification, purchasing, inspection, freight, customs clearance, and delivery. Tan’s entity-resolution software may eventually become valuable infrastructure, but Donkey provides no classification-accuracy benchmark, proprietary-data depth, or comparison showing that its technology finds suppliers 5–10x faster or more accurately than Sourcify, Flexport, or an experienced sourcing agent. The moat described today—inspectors across seven provinces and order-level supplier data—is operational and prospective. Competitors can copy workflow; they have more difficulty copying a demonstrably superior learning system, but that system has not been demonstrated.

The founders are the best part of this case. Martindale speaks Mandarin, sold to Chinese factories, advised industrial manufacturers, and drove from Vermont to San Francisco to visit wholesale distributors. Prelude acquired 31 paying factories in 39 days and became cash-flow positive before the team pivoted. Tan is building the customs-filing classification machinery. This is evidence of resourcefulness, domain contact, and willingness to change tactics. It is not yet evidence of risk-tuned recruiting or belief revision after a specific result; the dossier does not explain what Prelude taught them or why the pivot was the correct response.

The strongest bull case is the known exception for a non-scientific wedge entering an enormous transaction pool: an adaptive team can begin with a modest tool and expand into financial infrastructure, as Block did. Donkey takes title, offers net-45 terms, and could accumulate pricing, reliability, defect, and credit data with every order. The material difference is that Block’s exception rested on platform evolution around a clearly enormous value pool. Donkey quantifies neither its addressable import spend nor a proprietary learning loop capable of changing its economics. At present, the company assumes inventory, quality, tariff, freight, and buyer-credit risk to earn an undisclosed embedded margin.

That economic exposure is the kill risk, yet no milestone attacks it directly. There is no threshold for entity-resolution accuracy, defect losses, gross margin after inspection and logistics, working-capital turns, or credit defaults. Worse, the website’s landed-cost example retained a 10% surcharge authorized only through July 24, 2026 absent extension. For a company selling tariff-sensitive pricing as expertise, stale duty logic is not cosmetic. Capital spent expanding sales and title exposure before validating these thresholds would trigger the red flag of scaling operations before removing the largest risk.

I expect Donkey to pivot by August 2029 from taking title across a broad product catalog into a narrower sourcing or trade-finance brokerage and remain below $5 million in annual net revenue. I pass today. The absence of exit talk, permanent subsidy dependence, or a harmful category helps, and execution-heavy marketplaces are not outside my revealed investing pattern. But execution alone does not purchase enough asymmetric upside. One verifiable result would flip me: across 100 completed imports for at least 20 repeat customers, Donkey must identify the true factory with over 90% independently audited accuracy and deliver at least 15% net landed-cost savings after credit losses, inspection, freight, duties, and its own margin.
