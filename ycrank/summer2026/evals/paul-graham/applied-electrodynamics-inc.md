# Applied Electrodynamics, Inc. -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The most impressive fact is that these founders appear capable of building the machine. Applied Electrodynamics reports completed radio-imaging hardware and reconstruction algorithms operating through sheetrock, tile, marble, quartz, brick, and insulation. The team has spent eight-plus years together building antennas, sensors, and consumer hardware at Apple, Nest, and Humane. This is much stronger than a diagram and a collection of credentials. But the claimed 1 mm accuracy has no independent benchmark, specification sheet, or field dataset. It is a measured-sounding claim, not yet measured proof.

What I cannot find is the obstacle story. Nothing in the dossier says what broke during development, what the founders improvised, how quickly they shipped the workaround, or what changed afterward. Their collective 250-plus patents do not answer this; some belong to former employers, and patents are evidence of invention rather than resourcefulness. The four founders may be unusually formidable, but I cannot infer the decisive trait from their résumés. On my controlling test, the evidence therefore remains at the absent-evidence default.

Nor is there yet a narrow well of users pulling WaveSight from them. Hundreds of Build Expo conversations, 148 LinkedIn followers, and an uncounted preorder list establish curiosity. They do not identify a contractor repeatedly scanning walls, paying to avoid rework, or tolerating an awkward prototype because the alternative is worse. Seeking introductions simultaneously to contractors, industrial inspectors, and defense organizations makes this fuzzier. Those groups have different jobs, validation standards, and sales channels. It suggests the team has built an interesting capability and is still searching for the user who needs it most.

The strongest bull case is the **Bindwell** exception: a technically exceptional team can earn an early hard-tech check by turning a difficult claim into numerical proof before conventional market evidence exists. Applied Electrodynamics has the relevant depth and a claimed 1 mm result. The material difference is that Bindwell had laboratory validation and direct exposure to the underlying problem; here, neither the performance result nor a founder-originated construction need has been independently established. The dossier also shows no rejected hypothesis or evidence-driven route change, so I cannot yet tell whether this team will behave like a running back when contractors reveal that accuracy, price, workflow, or certification matters differently than expected.

The market sharpens the concern. Through-wall radar was estimated at only $134 million in 2025, while Vayyar already sells a $199.95 wall scanner and has raised more than $300 million. Applied Electrodynamics may escape that boundary through a genuinely superior instrument, but construction, manufacturing, cargo screening, and defense are not one market merely because radio waves serve all of them. My 36-month expectation is that the company pivots from general construction imaging toward defense or specialized industrial inspection and remains below $1 million ARR while qualifying the hardware. That prediction could be badly wrong if the room-distance point cloud produces an urgent workflow competitors cannot match.

I would pass today with genuine interest, not because the technical ambition is insufficient, but because the founders have supplied the noun before supplying the behavioral proof. The unstated pattern behind my recent hard-tech checks is a concrete, inspectable pre-round result; this dossier contains a company-reported result without external validation. The usual partnership and launch-day red flags are not fatal here, and the founders’ long working history is affirmatively good. But the operational gate still fails: no named blocker followed by a shipped workaround, and no repeated user behavior. One verifiable flip condition would change my decision: a dated contractor field trial in which an initial scanning failure leads to a founder-shipped revision and at least ten crews subsequently pay for repeated use.
