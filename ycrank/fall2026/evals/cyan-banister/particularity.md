# Particularity -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years forward, I can imagine canceling a subscription without needing a small act of personal courage. Particularity’s interesting possibility is that a regulator could discover the trap before thousands of people complain. Getting there requires reliable agents, evidence that survives challenge, and agencies changing how they initiate investigations. The October 2026 launch describes checkout testing, cancellation testing, and statutory mapping. It gives me a concrete direction, but no demonstrated investigation showing that this future has begun.

Why does the cancellation trap survive? Particularity identifies a useful asymmetry: commercial websites keep changing while investigators depend on incoming complaints and manual work. A merchant can benefit from friction that costs each consumer too little to justify fighting individually. Automated investigation could change that calculation and return time and purchasing control to consumers. That mechanism earns my strongest interest here. The dossier does not quantify the extracted dollars or investigative delay, or identify a specific statutory requirement the product has successfully tested.

Kumaravel’s Senate Banking Committee work in consumer protection and Bannon’s AWS security and compliance engineering make this a credible pairing for the problem. Bannon’s published Sailor framework, with 19 stars in the cached GitHub profile, adds a modest signal that he builds independently. But these facts establish relevant preparation more clearly than pre-company obsession. I cannot find the earlier experiment, persistent investigation, or first agency believer that explains why these two had to build Particularity. The named CEO and CTO establish operating roles; their friendship supplies no evidence about voting control or a contractual tiebreaker.

The strongest argument against passing is that these founders may understand the exact seam between discovering misconduct and making it actionable. Timestamped, hashed records and statutory mapping show attention to the work after an agent clicks a button. That matters. My willingness to back pre-revenue technical founders applies when a working prototype and concrete institutional commitments make the missing revenue unimportant. Neither is documented here. The October 15 emergence from stealth and launch coverage establish public availability of the story, not repeated investigator behavior. I assign no adverse meaning to absent revenue at this stage.

Particularity fits my pattern of funding unglamorous workflow repair, but the concealed infrastructure opportunity remains hypothetical. Hashing preserves a captured record; it does not establish that the interaction proves a violation. UNCTAD’s account of existing agency mystery-shopping and recording tools makes that distinction commercially important: Particularity must improve the investigative result enough to change agency behavior. No controlled dataset, embedded deployment, or scarce technical asset is demonstrated. My privacy gate also remains unresolved: the dossier describes commercial website testing, without establishing severe surveillance problems or documenting limits on collection, access, and retention. That uncertainty earns neither a rejection for misconduct nor affirmative credit.

I pass with my own money today. The attraction is real: Particularity could make exploiting dispersed consumers harder by giving investigators greater reach. My one verifiable flip condition is a named consumer-protection agency completing a paid pilot in which investigators repeatedly use its evidence packages, agency counsel accepts those packages for investigative use, and documented collection and access controls satisfy my privacy gate. That would connect the proposed mechanism to an institution actually changing its behavior.
