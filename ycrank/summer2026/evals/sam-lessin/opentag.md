# OpenTag -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The post-money valuation is unknown, the competing-term-sheet count is unknown, and the dossier does not establish that this is a genuinely scarce first check. Those are not administrative blanks; they prevent me from knowing whether I am buying a non-consensus belief or merely arriving late to another YC AI auction. OpenTag therefore receives the missing-evidence default on my primary gate.

The potential venture moment is legible but unfunded on paper: prove that one shared coworker can turn Slack and Teams conversations into recurring, auditable work across more than 80 models. Yet OpenTag names no use-of-proceeds plan, dated milestone, or pass/fail metric. “Hire, launch, and grow” would not reveal a discrete truth. The meaningful experiment would be whether the original early-access cohort delegates repeated production workflows, pays, and stays—not whether another hundred teams activate a free trial.

The bull case is real. Ten teams adopted the product within two weeks, six organizations are publicly identified as doing work with it, and the system already combines sandboxed execution, company-specific memory, permissions, approval gates, and source-linked wiki updates. The three founders also shipped together at Lilac Labs and bring adjacent experience from Tesla, Flatiron Health, Roche, CertiK, HPE, and regulated securities. This resembles the translation-layer pattern I like: connecting messy enterprise conversation, operational tools, and multiple model providers through one controlled interface.

But translation is not ownership. No evidence shows that OpenTag’s workflow caused a purchase, survived repeated use, or produced switching costs. Model routing is supplied through Conifer and external providers; the interaction itself is already offered by Anthropic’s Claude Tag, while Dust, Lindy, and Glean attack overlapping enterprise workflows with vastly greater resources. More models is a feature, not a proprietary dark pool. Ten teams and six logos without paid conversion or retention risk becoming precisely the activity-metric theater that obscures the only question that matters: does delegated work generate durable cash?

The strongest objection to passing is that this is only two weeks old, the founders have already built together, and OpenTag’s approval, isolation, and audit architecture may be enough to win enterprises before incumbents accommodate multiple models. The known-exception path is Ando: both are enterprise AI workflows, but Ando earned its override through a year of operation, greater than 90% forecast accuracy, complete pilot-to-paid conversion, and complete retention. OpenTag currently has none of that causal evidence. Its $50 plan and $100 trial establish a pricing surface, not revenue, gross margin, burn, runway, or a route to profitability if no Series A arrives.

I pass today. At a disclosed sub-$10 million post-money valuation, my one flip condition is that at least eight of the original ten teams become gross-margin-positive paying customers and remain active for six consecutive months. That result would turn the current logos into a controllable cash cohort, demonstrate that accumulated workflow context matters, and justify financing the next finite experiment rather than another generic AI application.
