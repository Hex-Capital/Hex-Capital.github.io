# Viraj Aero -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B, repeatable sales with account expansion,** is Viraj Aero’s strongest path. The first buyer is an AI data center developer facing a long grid connection wait and limited cooling water. The memo documents developers procuring on-site gas generation to bridge grid delays. Viraj’s proposed advantage is a modular 10 MW turbine that supplies power and recovers exhaust water for cooling. If a buyer validates both outputs in continuous service, it could add modules as a campus grows and reuse the design at other sites. Founder-led outreach and production-slot reservations are the current route to buyers. Paid activation and repeat orders through that route remain unverified. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

Viraj reports more than 1.3 GW of demand under letters of intent and its first power-and-water turbine under construction. It also reports one paying engine manufacturer, with the scope and payment undisclosed. The memo establishes neither a paid GENA deployment nor budget committed by a data center buyer to a field test. The buying problem plausibly recurs across markets, but the memo gives no buyer locations or cross-market deployment evidence. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

Under an equipment-sale scenario, repeat orders could spread sales and engineering effort across more modules. Each module would still require manufacturing and working capital. Each new site would require gas access, air approvals, cooling integration, commissioning, and service. In a second geography, the same turbine would need to meet local air requirements and fit the site's gas and cooling infrastructure. The main uncertainty is how much engineering and capital Viraj must commit each time. The memo provides no measured rollout costs. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

The overlap between power-constrained and water-constrained sites may be narrow. Where power is the urgent problem, competing generators can win without offering water recovery. Where both constraints matter, site-specific approvals and integration could make each sale a separate engineering project. With its first unit still under construction, Viraj has yet to show that the water recovered in continuous service is valuable enough to justify that work. These risks support Watch. The memo does not establish the structural demand ceiling or proportional scaling costs required for Pass. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Viraj complete a buyer-funded GENA deployment at an independent data center using a design the buyer could reuse at its next site? Move to **Reach out** if the buyer accepts the delivered power and usable cooling-water yield under continuous operation, and the deployment records show which engineering work can be reused and what Viraj would spend on the next site. Stay on **Watch** while that result is pending.
