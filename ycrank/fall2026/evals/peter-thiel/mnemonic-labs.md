# Mnemonic Labs -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Mnemonic Labs’s proposed secret is that trading firms’ short profitability horizons leave a longer-horizon opportunity: training directly on market activity can discover profitable strategies through scale. What would prove it wrong? Larger models could improve prediction while producing no additional returns after transaction costs, market impact, and competition. The founders have articulated a testable proposition, but the dossier identifies no named firm rejecting it and reports no experiment establishing it. The November 2026 training run exceeding 25TB demonstrates an undertaking, not a scaling law.

The founding structure is more persuasive. Videh and Kalki Seksaria are brothers whose shared robotics research preceded this company; this is affirmative evidence against the convenience-matched founding team. Their quantitative backgrounds at Citadel, Walleye, and Tower Research make the technical ambition credible. Shared history earns credit for alignment; prestigious employers do not establish ownership or commitment. Full-time status, founder equity, and compensation remain undisclosed, with no affirmative evidence of part-time work, outsourced core engineering, or investor control.

The strongest argument against passing is that these founders may understand an institutional constraint outsiders mistake for a technical limit. Kalki’s company-attributed $20M annual PnL at Tower is relevant evidence of practical trading competence, although it is neither independently verified here nor Mnemonic revenue. A two-person team reporting its own architecture and training pipeline in under six weeks also deserves attention. My revealed interest in financial businesses and adversarial information systems makes this a plausible hunting ground. But the exceptions that justify backing an uncertain monopoly require a demonstrated technical discontinuity, persistent live demand, or realized economics. Mnemonic has not yet supplied that overriding evidence.

Even if the scaling thesis succeeds, who keeps the value? Prodigy already describes a quantitative-finance foundation model, so this direction is not an undiscovered category. That overlap does not establish that the products are interchangeable; it does make market ownership a separate question. Mnemonic identifies neither a bounded trading beachhead nor exclusive data, measured technical superiority, or an established execution-feedback advantage. In markets, successful discovery can finance competitors while trading itself erodes the discovered opportunity. A last-mover position would require evidence that the advantage survives deployment.

I would pass on a check today while remaining willing to investigate the result. The commercial model is still inferred: proprietary trading and licensing have different capital requirements, capacity limits, and distribution economics. An inbound founder inbox establishes neither route, and the broader $26.3B algorithmic-trading estimate cannot resolve the choice. These are unanswered questions rather than demonstrated failures; I am not penalizing a Fall 2026 company for lacking a revenue history. My one verifiable condition for reopening the decision is an independently audited, prospective live-trading study showing that additional training produces greater profits after transaction costs and market impact at fixed risk and disclosed deployable capacity. That would turn Mnemonic’s central proposition into evidence worth underwriting.
