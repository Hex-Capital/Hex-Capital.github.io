# Viraj Aero -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Viraj Aero addresses a specific constraint on AI compute deployment. The memo documents data center developers procuring on-site gas generation as construction outpaces grid-connected supply. Multiyear connection waits can defer the start of compute operations, and limited cooling water can further restrict some sites. GENA is designed to generate power on-site and recover water from turbine exhaust for cooling. The seed-stage case is a grid-delayed AI data center that can use modular generation, secure gas and air permits in time, and put the recovered water to use. Viraj reports 10 MW, 45% electrical efficiency, and 2 ppm NOx. The first unit was reported under construction, so sustained power and usable water output remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The industry-enabled path scores zero. The proposed change in delivery economics comes from turbine design and water recovery. The memo identifies no previously uneconomic use made viable by AI reducing the cost per successful outcome. (The Idea)

The reported condenser patent gives Viraj a specific potential advantage, with no field evidence yet that it protects pricing. The company reports more than 1.3 GW of demand under letters of intent and one paying engine manufacturer with undisclosed scope. Those signals support buyer interest, while GENA order conversion remains open. The aviation prototype ground test and the team’s power-project experience support execution credit. The memo supplies no usable price-to-cost bridge for the next installed unit, so scale economics receives the default. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

A developer could use a competing turbine or modular reciprocating engines and source cooling separately. That could be faster where water is available or where GENA faces the same gas and permitting delays. The memo establishes that alternatives exist and leaves their relative timelines open. The market evidence clears the seed-stage fit threshold, but the unresolved question of whether GENA can deliver an earlier start at a representative site warrants Watch. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Can GENA bring a grid-delayed, water-constrained AI site online earlier than its best feasible alternative? Move to Reach out if a buyer-witnessed continuous-duty test meets that site’s net power, usable cooling-water, and emissions requirements, and the buyer’s schedule comparison shows an earlier start after accounting for fuel access and permitting.
