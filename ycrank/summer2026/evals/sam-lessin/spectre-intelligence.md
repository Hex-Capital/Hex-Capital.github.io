# Spectre Intelligence -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I need are missing: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. Spectre Intelligence may be a good trading operation, but I cannot tell whether its round offers cheap uncertainty or expensive YC consensus. Financing silence earns little credit because price determines whether I am buying a non-consensus belief or merely funding the latest AI-capital-markets fashion.

The potentially real asset is not “AI traders.” It is the loop connecting expert input, agent reasoning traces, live decisions, and ground-truth P&L across semiconductor and biotech pods. That is a plausible proprietary dark pool: unlike public market data or commodity models, Spectre’s labeled record of why an agent traded and what subsequently happened can improve through use. The problem is that the company admits successful strategies will be copied and alpha will decay. Without a disclosed dataset size, return series, or benchmark, this is currently a mechanism for producing a moat, not evidence that one exists.

The strongest bull case is straightforward. Spectre already trades real capital through two pods and says it is profitable. That is unusually concrete for a Summer 2026 pre-seed company, and it means Michelle Li and Joshua Zyzak are testing the system against money rather than demos. Li brings biology research and personal trading experience; Zyzak brings reinforcement learning, AI engineering, and exposure to Optiver. If those live trades are producing repeatable risk-adjusted returns, the team could compound proprietary labels while competitors repeatedly restart from shared models and shared market data.

But “profitable” without profit, AUM, return, drawdown, or benchmark figures is dangerously close to metric theater. The company itself says LLM traders are not yet effective and identifies memory, robustness, learning, and context as unresolved. That makes the functioning base business impossible to separate from the out-of-the-money AI option. It also leaves the claimed economics ununderwritten: no gross return, operating cost, compute burden, burn, runway, or route to break-even has been disclosed, even though proprietary trading should permit an exceptionally clean cash metric.

I also cannot identify the venture moment. The dossier does not say what this round funds, which hypothesis will be tested, by what date, or what measurable result would dramatically change value. “Add pods, experts, models, compute, and capital” would be a capital factory, not one truth-revealing experiment. The founders have relevant ingredients, but neither has a documented trading track record or a shipped institution-scale system bridging domain experts and capital allocation. Their equal-founder structure is an additional unresolved red flag because the dossier does not identify who has final authority over risk, capital, and model deployment.

My base case is that within 36 months Spectre remains a sub-$25 million-AUM proprietary trading operation using founder and close-network capital, without launching a durable institutional fund product. The one condition that flips me is a third-party-verified 12-month live record showing positive net returns after all trading and compute costs, benchmark outperformance, and disclosed maximum drawdown. That would convert the proprietary trace loop from an attractive story into the kind of hard operating evidence capable of overriding missing seed terms. Today, I pass.
