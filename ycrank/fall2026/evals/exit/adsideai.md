# Adside.ai -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic sale to a paid-media agency group. Adside describes a workflow that combines competitor research, creative production, campaign setup, and monitoring across ad platforms. An agency could use it across client accounts to reduce staff time and improve margins. That buyer benefit is an inference. The memo provides no measured savings or evidence of acquisition interest. (The Idea)

Adside reports 30 companies live at launch, and the memo records published self-serve and managed-service pricing. Those signals make a paid, multi-client agency pilot plausible. A proposed 90-day pilot could measure staff hours and gross profit per account against the agency’s current workflow while tracking campaign performance. The memo lists a $125,000 seed entry and leaves the full round, runway, and pilot funding unconfirmed. A share sale is a plausible structure, with continuity of delegated account access, platform permissions, and customer consents still to verify. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and $2.5 million in gross cash proceeds within eight years. That requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale scenarios below assume a full cash acquisition within eight years, an eligible holder, and a pro rata waterfall with no further preference leakage. The prices and deductions are illustrative assumptions. The memo supplies no sale-price evidence or holder terms to substantiate them.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $35M distributable | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M distributable | $2.70M (5.4×). At 2.5% ownership, $2.25M (4.5×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or funded payout | $0 realized within eight years |

### Countercase

An agency could assemble enough of the workflow from native platform tools and competing products to make buying Adside uneconomic. The memo documents Meta’s direct agent access and Google’s connector and native campaign tools. It also leaves the value added by Adside’s account-level personalization unquantified. This weakens the case for a price that clears the holder’s hurdle. It does not trigger the Pass gate because the evidence establishes neither a structural closing block nor inadequate proceeds under the strongest credible route. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid agency pilot demonstrate repeatable gross-profit gains across client accounts large enough to justify a purchase above the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Account-level labor, cost, and campaign performance data, paired with the agency’s own rollout and buy-versus-build estimates, would answer it. If those economics support a price that leaves at least $83.3 million distributable under the benchmark, move to Reach out to test willingness to buy and access continuity.
