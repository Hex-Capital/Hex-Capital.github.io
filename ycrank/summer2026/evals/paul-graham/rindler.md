# Rindler -- Paul Graham Evaluation

**Coinbase** (2012) turned an ugly compliance grind into an asset. Rindler is the same species at an earlier stage: high on schlep, low on evidence that the idea grew from the founders’ own pain. Rindler maps authenticated sites individually, captures credentials or cookies, handles retries, and sometimes relies on proxies, profile rotation, and CAPTCHA solving. That is real unpleasant work. It is not an AI wrapper. The divergence from Coinbase is that Rindler publishes no mapping count, customer count, or independent reliability history, and Rindler lacks SOC 2, HIPAA, and PCI certification. The schlep is visible; the appetite to endure years of it is not yet proved.

**Loopt** (2005) survived weak product timing because Sam Altman was such an animal that I made an exception. Rindler presents the analogous weakness without evidence for the exception: neither Michael Serrano nor Arthur De Los Santos is documented encountering broken browser automation in a personal workflow and building Rindler for himself. The dossier shows aligned expertise—Roblox ML, MIT CSAIL, vision-language navigation—but expertise is not organicity. Rindler therefore gets the absent-evidence default here. A clever answer to a fashionable problem is weaker than Drew Houston repeatedly losing his USB stick.

**Three production workflow categories—ATS, major-bank read/export, and state registries—show that Rindler shipped more than a diagram.** Over 18 months, those mappings could compound into a library of failure cases, authentication recipes, and recovery logic that makes each subsequent Rindler deployment easier. Or Rindler could become two founders manually repairing brittle integrations while charging $1 per successful session. The reported 3x reduction in failures and 4x faster completion are promising tracks, but they are company benchmarks with no named customer or revenue attached. Rindler passes the artifact test and only partially passes the animal test.

**Michael Serrano’s MIT physics and computer-science degrees, Roblox ML work, and CSAIL LLM research make Rindler technically credible.** Arthur De Los Santos adds CSAIL vision-language navigation work, so Rindler is not waiting to hire engineers after funding; the standard manager-mode red flag is absent. But the unstated hacker pattern is only partly present: their highlighted GitHub repositories have two stars and one star, with no Hacker News trail, open-source adoption, or competitive-programming artifact. If Rindler’s verifier or mapping language attracted independent developers and hundreds of real integrations, this score would move into the Stripe-like range. Without that, the evidence says accomplished researchers, not yet unmistakable hackers.

**Stripe** (2010) was two brothers who had already hacked on commerce together before Stripe existed. Rindler has the right numerical shape: Serrano and De Los Santos met at MIT in 2022 and shared the CSAIL environment before Rindler’s Summer 2026 batch. Four years of proximity is much better than founders paired weeks before applying. Rindler lacks evidence that they repeatedly shipped together during those four years, so the Collison parallel is structural rather than complete, but the usual solo-founder and startup-speed-dating red flags do not appear.

**Rindler’s $100 and $1,000 monthly plans price a horizontal product, while its production evidence clusters around ATS, banking, and registries.** By July 2029, I predict Rindler will have narrowed into managed authenticated workflows for recruiting and financial operations and reached $2 million to $5 million in ARR, rather than becoming the default translation layer for the whole web. Rindler’s schlep strength should win enterprise work; Rindler’s missing organic origin should make customer pull, rather than founder obsession, choose the eventual vertical.

**Browserbase’s $67.5 million, Kernel’s $22 million, and Browser Use’s $17 million form the strongest bear case against Rindler.** Kernel already supplies Rindler’s managed browsers, Browserbase is the fallback, and either supplier could absorb typed outputs, verification, and MCP endpoints while Rindler bears the mapping labor. The tiny public GitHub footprint sharpens that objection: Rindler may be an integration layer between better-capitalized primitives, not the next default. What keeps me on the investing side is that Rindler already claims production workflows across three unrelated hostile site categories, evidence that the founders are running toward the schlep rather than merely describing it. If Rindler cannot show ten paying organizations by July 2027 whose mappings operate for 90 days without founder repair, I would flip to Pass.
