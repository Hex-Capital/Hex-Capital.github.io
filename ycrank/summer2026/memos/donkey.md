## The Idea

- **Problem:** US importers buying through trading companies face company-reported container markups of 15–30%, undisclosed factories, and duties calculated on marked-up invoices, while their alternatives are retaining the intermediary or managing factories, quality control, freight, and customs directly ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/donkey), Jul 2026).
- **Approach:** Donkey analyzes public shipment records and a submitted product photo, identifies the manufacturer, quotes delivery-duty-paid within 72 hours, buys from the factory, inspects before final payment, takes title, clears customs, and delivers to the importer’s dock ([Donkey website](https://donkey.trade), Jul 2026).
- **Differentiation:** ImportYeti exposes customs records without executing orders, Sourcify operates sourcing and production workflows without taking factory cuts, and Jiga connects engineers directly with manufacturers, whereas Donkey becomes the US counterparty and importer of record for an existing reordered product ([ImportYeti](https://www.importyeti.com/faqs), [Sourcify](https://www.sourcify.com/startups-small-brands/), [Jiga](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/jiga), [Donkey](https://donkey.trade)).
- **Business Model:** Donkey embeds its margin inside one delivered price and states that it charges no fees, retainers, or additional percentages, with net-45 terms available to qualified buyers ([Donkey website](https://donkey.trade), Jul 2026).
- **TAM/SAM:** The adjacent global procurement-as-a-service market was estimated at $7.43 billion in 2025 and projected to grow at 10.8% CAGR from 2026–2033, while no public SAM estimate was found for US importers sourcing physical goods from China ([Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/procurement-as-a-service-market), 2026).
- **GTM / Distribution:** Founder Benjamin Martindale reported driving from Vermont to San Francisco to visit independent wholesale distributors, complemented by the website’s photo-submission and supplier-check calls to action ([LinkedIn](https://hk.linkedin.com/in/long-yan-james), Jul 2026, via search snippet; [Donkey website](https://donkey.trade)).

## Defensibility

- **Moat today:** Company-reported assets comprise inspectors covering seven Chinese provinces and order-level pricing and reliability data that the company says accumulates after settlement, with no independent scale verification found ([Donkey website](https://donkey.trade); [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/donkey), Jul 2026).
- **Future moat:** [Inferred]: Repeated orders could create product-to-factory, pricing, defect, and delivery-performance records that improve quoting and supplier selection, but no current order volume or prediction-accuracy data is public.
- **Market structure:** No structural barrier identified at this stage.
- **Commoditization risk:** ImportYeti already searches 70 million bills of lading and reports more than two million users, while Sourcify reports 3,000-plus factories and Jiga automates manufacturer matching and production monitoring ([ImportYeti](https://www.importyeti.com/about), [Sourcify](https://www.sourcify.com/), [Jiga](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/jiga)).

## Key Risks

- **Customs-valuation compliance:** Donkey’s savings proposition relies partly on duty assessment using the factory transaction, while CBP requires an arm’s-length bona fide sale, US destination evidence, and supporting documentation for first-sale valuation; Donkey states that it takes title and acts as importer of record ([CBP](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/548494); [Donkey website](https://donkey.trade)).
- **Working-capital and credit exposure:** Donkey buys from factories, holds title through delivery, and offers net-45 terms, creating inventory, transit, and buyer-default exposure; the company says buyer terms are credit-insured and limited to qualified customers ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/donkey), Jul 2026).
- **Supplier-attribution and quality risk:** Donkey’s terms state that photo-based supplier checks are assessments rather than guarantees or audits, while its mitigation is factory-floor inspection before payment and replacement or credit for incorrect deliveries ([Donkey terms](https://donkey.trade/terms), Jul 2026; [Donkey website](https://donkey.trade)).
- **China policy concentration:** The current product is centered on Chinese factories while tariff exclusions are scheduled for another decision after November 10, 2026; Donkey mitigates buyer administration by quoting duty-paid and serving as importer of record ([USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2025/november/ustr-extends-exclusions-china-section-301-tariffs-related-forced-technology-transfer-investigation); [Donkey website](https://donkey.trade)).
- **Product-transition clarity:** LinkedIn’s cached company description still referenced Prelude weeks before its current Donkey description, creating stale-channel and metric-attribution risk; the current website and YC profile consistently describe Donkey ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/thedonkeytrade); [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/donkey), Jul 2026).
