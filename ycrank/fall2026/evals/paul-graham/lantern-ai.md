# Lantern AI -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The useful fact about Lantern AI is that customers asked it to move upstream. They had an interviewer, but wanted help finding the people to interview. That is how a recruiting company can discover what to build next: do one job, then notice where users keep dragging you. The founders reportedly expanded the product in response. This earns credit for changing direction on evidence. Without an exact pivot date or a documented obstacle-and-workaround sequence, it does not yet tell me how quickly they move when something breaks.

There is a real business underneath the new description. The founder reports crossing **$1M ARR**, though its allocation between products is undisclosed. Crypto.com, Hyatt and Gammon are named customers of the original interviewer. Hyatt reports screening three times as many candidates weekly; Gammon reports a 40% reduction in time-to-shortlist. These are specific jobs for identifiable users. They are much more informative than the size of the recruiting-software market. The strongest argument for investing is that Lantern AI already has customers paying for one part of hiring and asking it to solve the adjacent part. That could make expansion unusually cheap.

The technical evidence is narrower than the product ambition. A deployed interview module with **100K+ cumulative interviews** is a functioning artifact, even allowing for the figure being company-reported. It does not establish that the World People Model finds better candidates or that the Company Hiring Model preserves judgment across handoffs. Those are separate claims. Lantern resembles the pattern I like of founders assembling something useful before calling it a platform, but the demonstrated substrate here is interviewing. Evidence of developers adopting a programmable primitive, another recurring pattern in my investments, is absent. Agent connections and a trial do not establish that behavior.

Chan’s two previous recruiting startups and both founders’ Career Hackers experience matter because they put the team near the problem before this version of the company existed. I would discount the prior “2M+” sales figure because its currency is unspecified. There is also no concrete account of the founders themselves losing hiring criteria across handoffs. Their experience supports problem familiarity; it cannot substitute for that account. The potentially valuable schlep is learning each employer’s judgment through integrations, feedback and manual correction. Gem already shares recruiting context across agents, and Juicebox adapts sourcing to feedback. Lantern must show that doing this work produces better hiring results, rather than merely a broader product.

I would remain **Neutral** and would not write a check from this dossier alone. My founder gate is unresolved: there is no independently described instance of a named blocker, a founder-built workaround and the time it took. That is missing evidence, not evidence of passivity. My known exceptions allow a working product or unmistakable pull to override an uncertain founder prior, and the interviewer makes that a serious possibility here. But the revenue allocation is unclear, and the demonstrated customer benefits do not yet validate the upstream expansion. There is no affirmative evidence of the partner-dependent distribution or premature delegation red flags; the consequential risk is expanding faster than customer learning justifies. One verifiable event would flip me: an existing customer documents a sourcing failure, the founders ship a workaround within two weeks, and that customer renews a paid contract covering the revised workflow.
