# Trepo -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Trepo’s investment case turns on a possible human secret: households will maintain accurate food inventories if the interface lives in the kitchen rather than inside an app. The dedicated device makes that proposition testable. If consumption and disposal routinely go unrecorded after the initial enthusiasm, replenishment will become unreliable. But this is my reconstruction of the opportunity; the dossier supplies neither a named prevailing belief the founders reject nor a longitudinal test establishing their opposing claim. Item check-ins measure recording activity. They do not establish that Trepo knows what remains in the cupboard.

The more immediate problem is ownership of the opportunity. Cooklist already addresses inventory and meal planning; Samsung and Instacart combine food recognition with replenishment. Trepo’s external device works with existing refrigerators, which removes an appliance-replacement requirement. That is a useful product distinction. It does not yet establish proprietary technology, a network effect, or a bounded customer segment Trepo can dominate. Household consumption histories could eventually make predictions better, but collecting data creates no monopoly unless its accumulated advantage becomes measurable and difficult to reproduce. The documented overlap makes this gate adverse; missing retention data alone would merely leave it unresolved.

The strongest argument against passing is that the founders have persuaded people to try something competitors have struggled to make habitual. Six beta hardware batches reportedly sold out, the app has 4.9 stars from 56 ratings, and the founders report more than 150,000 item check-ins. A kitchen device could become the persistent household interface that phone applications never became. That possibility deserves attention. Yet undisclosed batch sizes cannot establish broad demand, and cumulative check-ins cannot distinguish durable household use from repeated onboarding. My exceptions for demonstrated technical breakthroughs, persistent transaction demand, or a validated path into physical production do not presently remove this risk: Trepo’s evidence demonstrates engagement without establishing dependable inventory or an enduring advantage.

The founding relationship is the strongest part of the dossier. Taylor and Slaughter met in college and played water polo together; this is affirmative evidence of shared history rather than a team assembled for an application. Taylor’s Tesla and Nuro hardware experience also makes delivery more credible, while Slaughter has held a marketing leadership role. Those credentials do not establish economic alignment. Full-time status, ownership, compensation, and prior joint product work remain undisclosed. I see no documented founding red flag, but I cannot infer the complete foundation from complementary résumés.

Distribution has more substance than a channel slogan. Trepo has an App Store presence, founder TikTok activity, a hardware waitlist, and reported grocery-partner pilots. Slaughter’s background gives customer acquisition an identifiable founder owner. However, the pilots have no named counterparties or commercial terms, and the dossier does not connect acquisition spending to hardware contribution margin or subscription retention. The free app, preorder hardware, and contemplated subscriptions also leave the dominant revenue engine unspecified. This is an unresolved business model at pre-seed, not evidence of bad economics; it nevertheless supplies no arithmetic for a power-law outcome.

I would pass on investing my own money today. Trepo fits my willingness to back consumer businesses and physical products, but those unstated preferences cannot override an unresolved monopoly mechanism in a market with demonstrated substitutes. My single flip condition is a controlled six-month household study showing that Trepo’s accumulated private inventory history produces a sustained replenishment-accuracy advantage over an otherwise equivalent system without that history, alongside continued household use. That result would turn the proposed data advantage into evidence and justify reopening the investment question.
