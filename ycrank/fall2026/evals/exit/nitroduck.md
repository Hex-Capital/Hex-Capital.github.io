# Nitroduck -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition by a specialty-crop breeder** is the strongest path. The memo records Nitroduck’s public launch and Enza Zaden’s license of Pairwise’s editing tools. The license shows that a breeder will contract for editing capabilities. Nitroduck proposes fitting editing into breeders’ existing crosses and avoiding a separate plant-regeneration step. A buyer could have a reason to own the platform if exclusive control of the workflow and crop-specific know-how delivered a repeatable time or cost advantage across breeding programs. That advantage remains an inference pending validation. (*The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction*)

The first milestone is reproducibly producing edited seed in a commercially relevant specialty crop within an existing breeding program. The founders’ prior plant-engineering work and a reported $500,000 YC convertible note support the plausibility of an initial test. The memo leaves the test budget, runway, timing, and crop-level performance open. It also leaves IP ownership and transfer rights unverified. The applicable crop approval pathway depends on classification. (*Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Risks*)

The reported note leaves the evaluated investor’s ownership and waterfall terms unknown, so the bridge uses the thesis’s illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation. It assumes 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash. Under a simple pro rata waterfall, that requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The memo supplies no usable acquisition pricing or measured buyer economics to support a sale range. (*Market & Traction, Key Facts*)

For the illustrative sales below, enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. The assumptions are zero net debt, fees, other price adjustments, or senior claims, with any note conversion reflected in diluted ownership. All consideration is cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and transaction-linked compensation are each $0. Proceeds are before investor taxes.

- **Modest sale, year 6.** At $50 million, the holder receives $1.5 million, or **3.0×** the total check.
- **Strong sale, year 8.** At $100 million, the holder receives $3 million, or **6.0×**, at 3% ownership. At 2%, the same sale pays $2 million, or **4.0×**.
- **Prolonged private, through year 8.** Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. The holder realizes **$0, or 0.0×**, and retains the shares. The memo supplies no separately supported fallback that pays this holder. (*Market & Traction, Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Breeders may be able to get enough of the benefit through a license or a per-project fee. Enza Zaden already licenses editing tools, and Nitroduck itself proposes a flat-fee service. If either arrangement delivers comparable results, a buyer has little reason to pay an acquisition premium. Plantik’s published patent application adds a potential constraint on what Nitroduck could transfer. The memo establishes neither equivalent performance in Nitroduck’s target crop nor an applicable IP restriction. The structural Pass override remains untriggered, and the evidence supports Watch pending a result that makes ownership economically credible. (*The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a breeder-backed pilot reproducibly produce edited seed in an existing specialty-crop program and quantify a time or cost advantage large enough to make ownership economically credible? Move from Watch to Reach out if the pilot measures that advantage against the breeder’s current workflow and the breeder can explain why owning the capability would create enough incremental value over licensing or buying edits to make the modeled acquisition hurdle plausible.
