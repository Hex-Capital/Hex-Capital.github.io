# Streamline Systems -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a drone manufacturer. Streamline reports that its retrofit module demonstrated autonomous flight without satellite positioning. If the module qualifies across several of a buyer’s platforms, owning the technology and integration work could give the manufacturer control over a capability it would otherwise have to source or develop. That ownership advantage is an inference. The memo leaves buyer willingness to pay, IP ownership, and transfer terms unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The operating milestone is independently validated production qualification followed by repeat paid shipments. The memo records $500,000 in financing and a founding-engineer hire. Streamline reports more than $80,000 in contracted revenue and deposits reserving more than $87,000 worth of modules. Overlap, deposit amounts, cash collected, and shipments are undisclosed. The Q1 2027 availability target provides a checkpoint, while the cost and runway to reach production qualification remain unclear. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** With investor terms undisclosed, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in holder cash, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The memo supplies no usable acquisition valuation evidence. For illustration, assume zero net debt, transaction costs, and other adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. Assume an all-cash sale allocated pro rata with no preference adjustment and $0 each in stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and transaction-linked compensation. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

- **Modest sale, year 5.** $40 million in enterprise value yields $40 million in distributable equity and $1.2 million to the holder, or 2.4× gross.
- **Strong sale, year 7.** $100 million in enterprise value yields $100 million in distributable equity and $3 million to the holder, or 6× gross. At 2% ownership, the same sale yields $2 million, or 4×.
- **Prolonged private, through year 8.** Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The holder realizes $0, or 0×, and retains the shares.

The memo supplies no independent fallback. It leaves sponsor-supporting cash flow, demand for this holder’s shares, a path to public resale, and funded distributions or buybacks unsubstantiated. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The strongest countercase is that a manufacturer can buy Streamline’s module under an ordinary supply agreement or source navigation hardware from an established vendor. The memo identifies alternative suppliers, leaves requalification costs unmeasured, and reports that third-party flight-test results were unavailable as of November 1, 2026. Disclosed demand also depends on customers competing in one procurement program. Together, these facts weaken the case that ownership commands a premium. Whether an alternative delivers equivalent production performance at a price that caps acquisition value remains unresolved, so the structural Pass gate has yet to be met. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
