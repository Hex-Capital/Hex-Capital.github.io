# Reason Machines -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a small engineering team assigning work to persistent agents, closing their laptops, and returning to tested changes. Who keeps the work moving when a machine disappears or a model changes? Reason Machines has an interesting answer: separate the controller from the execution environment. But that future is my extrapolation. The dossier establishes a functioning coding product, without establishing the intervening transitions that make Reason an essential part of that future. I would not give my daydream the weight of company evidence.

The founders give me a better reason to pay attention. Adi Singh previously co-founded the Rust IDE company Dereference; Sven Myhre brings production ML experience and a 2025 Norwegian AI Championship win. Building and living together across four cities is more informative than simply sharing a university. These people have repeatedly chosen to build together, in a domain they already know. That earns meaningful founder credit. The record is thinner on dated, independently pursued experiments and evidence that they can recruit others into their particular vision.

Why does an engineer still babysit an allegedly autonomous teammate? Reason identifies real work: interruptions, environment management, testing, and review. Removing that work could return attention and control to developers. Yet the dossier identifies no specific institutional restriction, rent recipient, or quantified toll that Reason removes. Its $50 and $200 monthly plans establish what access costs, not whether customers recover enough time to change how they work. This is credible workflow repair, which fits one of my less glamorous investing patterns; the mechanism for transferring economic agency remains unproven.

The strongest objection to passing is the October 22 report of 10,000 users and 300 active customer Slack channels over fourteen days. Getting a coding agent into a team's working conversation is a more interesting behavior than collecting followers. Reason also has a functioning product and roughly 1,600 repository stars. But the founder-reported totals do not distinguish installation from repeated delegation, or free experimentation from payment. My pre-revenue exception at **CosmicBrain AI** rested on a working prototype combined with lifelong robotics obsession and signed industrial proofs of concept. Reason shares the working-artifact signal; the dossier does not establish that same combination of founder history and customer commitment. Missing revenue itself is not the objection.

I also see the concealed-infrastructure possibility: the useful company beneath the coding assistant might be its persistent controller. Parallel tool execution, deferred discovery, and cache-preserving prompts show actual engineering. Still, the reported 22% cost advantage over Codex is company-tested, and the contribution of each mechanism is unresolved. A Pi-based harness and permissively licensed messaging component do not yet establish control over a scarce dependency. Devin already offers shared context and external agents. Reason must show why customers keep its controller when competing products absorb the same conveniences.

I pass today. This is a credible builder team with an early usage signal, but my strongest future-facing criterion remains unsupported. I do not infer exclusive model dependence from third-party dependencies, a governance deadlock from two founders, or a severe privacy violation from autonomous execution; documented isolation and workspace controls matter. The decisive gap is whether Reason becomes something customers organize their work around. **My flip condition is an independently verified 90-day paid cohort that continues using Reason's shared controller while switching underlying models or execution hosts.** That would demonstrate that the enduring relationship belongs to Reason.
