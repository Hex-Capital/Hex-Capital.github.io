# Shielded -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

James Simon’s Balyasny commodities work and methane-emissions research make Shielded a domain-adjacent founder bet wrapped in an artifact I cannot yet touch; that is Shielded’s signature. The biography registers more strongly than the product: Simon has stood near commodity exposure, but Shielded’s inaccessible website and unnamed customer examples leave me observing a spreadsheet-shaped shadow. Biz and Tiz are plausible. Rizz, lived urgency, and a visible object remain unproven.

Crusoe is the high rung because Chase Lochmiller belonged on the well pad before anyone stapled stranded gas, Bitcoin, and infrastructure together. Shielded shares the useful part of that mechanism: Simon studied oil-and-gas methane and then worked on Balyasny’s commodities desk, so Shielded did not begin with a TAM slide alone. Shielded diverges where it matters most—interviewing hundreds of finance and procurement professionals demonstrates disciplined discovery, but the dossier never identifies the physical moment Simon personally suffered the late tariff or margin calculation. He has visited the problem; I cannot yet prove he is *of* it.

DeepMind’s low rung on physical verifiability was abstraction without an ordinary artifact, rescued only by Demis Hassabis’s extraordinary polymath compounding. Shielded shows the same axis weakness without equivalent proof of exception: Shielded’s website was inaccessible, its three customer examples are unnamed, and its claimed 48-hour ERP deployment cannot be independently used or observed. A bridge chart generated from a restaurant’s 200 SKUs is more tangible than a raw API, but I still have to accept the founder’s description instead of watching the product operate.

Interos’s $192.35 million in funding and existing tariff-cost and revenue-impact tools expose how Shielded’s non-consensus score could decay over the next 18 months. Shielded is not magically weird today; Shielded is entering a funded category beside Altana, Prewave, and Everstream with Susa Ventures already on the cap table. The compounding path would be customer-specific links among ERP records, commodity movements, margins, and actions becoming a proprietary operating graph. Without retention or integration-depth evidence, the opposite compounds faster: incumbents absorb the workflow while Shielded pays $1,000 for referrals into a market they already serve.

James Simon is the only founder named, but Shielded calls him “Founder,” not unambiguous CEO, and provides no evidence about equity, operating authority, or conflict protocol. Shielded’s governance score flips upward if Simon is the sole controlling founder or holds documented tiebreaker authority; it flips downward if an undisclosed co-founder shares control 50/50. GameCrush taught me that ambiguity waits quietly until investors or crisis make it expensive. No evidence means I apply the governance default rather than inventing a clean cap table.

Vigil Labs is the single-anchor parallel for Shielded’s regulated and unsexy potential: financial decisions become defensible when regulatory complexity is part of the product rather than a news feed pasted onto software. Shielded monitors the White House, USTR, Federal Register, and CBP, and Shielded touches sensitive supplier origins, volumes, and margins. But tariffs are regulated inputs, not a regulated moat; no certification, proprietary dataset, license, hardware, or security posture is public. This is adjacent to the ugly machinery I like, not yet embedded inside it.

Shielded’s three first-party use cases suggest a narrower future than its supply-chain-risk language implies. By July 2029, Shielded will have pivoted toward commodity-margin intelligence for CPG and industrial finance teams and will reach $2 million to $5 million in ARR, rather than becoming an Interos-scale supply-chain network. Shielded can get there because a restaurant auditing 200 SKUs and a manufacturing CFO tracking metals and energy share one repeatable job: translate external price movement into an immediate margin action.

Susa Ventures, a 48-hour integration claim, and three distinct customer workflows form the strongest bull case for Shielded: Simon may be the DeepMind exception—a computer-science, economics, methane, and commodities polymath whose cross-domain compression matters more than a tactile product. Shielded could use tariff volatility as the wedge, then turn every deployment into the proprietary graph the incumbents lack. The case still loses today because Shielded discloses no revenue, named customer, retention, moat, or independently accessible product, while Interos already markets the central outcome. Three named customers renewing above $100,000 ACV after twelve months would flip my verdict from Neutral to Invest. Until then, this is a rebuttal, not a hard no—but it is not my own-money check.
