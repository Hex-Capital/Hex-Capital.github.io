# Palette -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first useful number is missing. Palette reports a $500,000 YC-led pre-seed, but not the post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or whether OpenAI’s backing means capital, credits, or branding. A small check does not prove a cheap round, and YC plus generative video is hardly evidence that the belief remains unfashionable. I cannot underwrite capital scarcity from financing silence.

The proposed venture moment is also undefined. Palette has built real machinery—130-plus regression tests, capability-gated routing, failure refunds, and claimed improvements of 35% in speed and 50% in generation cost—but those are product benchmarks, not a finite experiment funded by this round. “Build an AI-native media platform” contains several simultaneous bets: model routing, creator workflow, enterprise security, multimodal editing, and distribution. I need one card the $500,000 flips, with a metric and deadline that separate a valuable workflow from another model aggregator.

The bull case is the team. Josephine Lee brings competitive programming and AI-music work; Serena Pei has diffusion and multimodal research across CSAIL, the Media Lab, and LIDS, plus production exposure at Amazon. They met through art before studying computer science, and two people have already shipped a public studio and API. That is unusually coherent technical preparation. It is not yet proof that they have operated inside an enterprise creative organization, controlled a media budget, or earned access to the buyers whose workflows they propose to reorganize.

The harsher issue is that Palette’s reported advantage lives where competition is fastest. Runway and Luma already aggregate models inside creative workspaces, while upstream providers can substitute, retire, or improve the models Palette routes. The internal claim of 100% task-capable routing measures technical availability, not customer value. There is no documented purchase, retained workflow, proprietary dataset, or brand history that becomes more valuable with use. Palette may eventually create that dark pool, but an inferred future moat earns no credit today.

Nor can I delete the next financing from the model. The company publishes a $0.01-per-credit price and custom enterprise quotes, but no revenue, gross margin, burn, paid conversion, or workload-level contribution economics. Failure-credit refunds improve customer experience while increasing the importance of inference economics. Copyright exposure, unfinished security certifications, and legal documents labeled as starter templates add enterprise costs before Palette has demonstrated enterprise cash generation.

I am passing. The strongest objection is that a technically exceptional two-person team has launched into a market forecast to grow from $788.5 million in 2025 to $3.44 billion by 2033, and its routing system could accumulate valuable workflow data before incumbents focus on cost optimization. But the known-exception logic for an AI application requires measured conversion, retention, and customer economics—not internal performance claims—and Palette supplies none. One verifiable flip condition would change my decision: five named enterprise creative teams converting from 90-day pilots to paid contracts, renewing after six months, and producing greater than 50% gross margin without requiring another round.
