# Mirrors -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Conditional on strong product-market fit, Mirrors’ strongest path is bottom-up adoption by developers running customer-facing AI agents. The recurring job is to fix repeated mistakes in production. Developers can scope a rule, preview it against past conversations, deliver it through the TypeScript or Python SDK, and compare outcomes. The same workflow and SDKs plausibly serve developers across markets, with integration work tied to each agent stack. Public SDK distribution and self-serve signup give developers a way to start. Independent production setup and checkout remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Growth pricing gives retained accounts a concrete way to spend more as their agents handle more turns, at $299 per month plus $0.005 per turn. Rule histories and outcome comparisons could make Mirrors part of the team’s operating workflow. Expansion could offset churn, though that remains an inference. The runtime product launched October 30, and Mirrors reported 63,052 turns in November. The counting period, paid share, and commercial growth rate are unknown, so initial traction takes the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

For an existing Growth account, each additional billed turn adds $0.005 in revenue. Incremental contribution depends on the cost of rule matching and delivery, trace storage and outcome measurement, plus any support or capacity the extra volume requires. The monthly fee can help cover account-level costs, and reusable SDK setup could limit onboarding labor. The memo leaves per-turn contribution at production workloads unresolved. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Native steering hooks and middleware could give teams enough control within their existing frameworks. Mirrors has to earn its place in the live request path through rule preview and outcome measurement. Models can ignore delivered rules, and Mirrors reports that turns proceed without its guidance after a 750 ms timeout. If those limitations reduce the value of its corrections, teams may revert to native controls before their bills expand. The structural downside gate clears because the documented SDK path supports portable deployment and the cost of scaling remains unresolved. (Defensibility, Key Risks)
