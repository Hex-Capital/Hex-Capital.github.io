# Alkera AI -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a data platform is Alkera’s strongest exit route. Alkera describes a product that traces column-level dependencies across Snowflake, dbt, and Hex and tests changes before they reach downstream dashboards. Fivetran’s acquisition of Tobiko Data shows that a buyer in this market has acquired adjacent transformation-validation tooling. The inference is that owning Alkera could give a platform broader safeguards across customer systems. Buyer demand, production savings, and the Tobiko purchase price remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A paid production pilot could measure downstream errors caught and review time saved against the customer’s existing tools. The product launch and reported $500,000 YC financing make a pilot plausible. Alkera’s reported benchmark advantage over Claude Code is an early technical signal. A funded pilot, its cost, and the runway to complete it remain unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale scenarios below are illustrative. Both assume cash at closing within eight years, eligibility to sell the full stake, and a pro rata waterfall after the stated deductions, with no further senior claims. The reported YC note’s conversion and priority terms are unknown. (Market & Traction)

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Holder cash |
|---|---|---|
| Modest acquisition | $40M less $5M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments = $35M | $1.05M, or 2.1× |
| Strong acquisition | $100M less $10M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments = $90M | $2.70M, or 5.4× |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or payout | $0 realized |

At 2.5% ownership, even the strong case yields $2.25 million, or 4.5×. The memo leaves realizable pricing, transfer rights, and required consents unverified. It provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route.

### Countercase

Snowflake’s Cortex Code already overlaps with Alkera in dbt development and catalog-aware SQL, and Altimate offers warehouse connectors, dbt tests, and column lineage. A buyer could extend its own tooling or use an available substitute, especially if gaps in Alkera’s integration coverage limit the errors it can catch. That weakens the reason to buy and the case for a price that clears the holder’s hurdle. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor materially inadequate proceeds under the strongest credible route, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What purchase price could a data-platform buyer justify from Alkera’s incremental value in production? A paid pilot showing repeatable reductions in downstream errors and review time versus existing tools, paired with buyer feedback supporting a price range that clears $83.3 million in distributable equity under the benchmark, would move the action to Reach out.
