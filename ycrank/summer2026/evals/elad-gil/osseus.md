# Osseus -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Robotics development is fragmenting across CAD, ECAD, code, requirements, and suppliers just as agents become capable of acting across those systems; the customer is a robotics R&D team, and the controlling variable is whether Osseus becomes the trusted dependency record used for real engineering changes. The dossier does not yet measure that variable. There is no accuracy threshold, customer count, or evidence that teams rely on the graph when approving a design change. That leaves the primary market gate unproven.

The product is more interesting than a generic engineering copilot. Osseus proposes persistent workflow state across geometry, electronics, GitHub, Jira, tests, decisions, and supplier constraints. Agents can identify affected requirements, prepare fixes, and rerun linked tests or simulations, with responsible owners approving the result. Better models should improve those actions while Osseus retains permissions, dependencies, and engineering history. This is the strongest part of the company: it could own an operational loop rather than merely format model output.

The economic step change is much less clear. The cited 2025–2026 improvements in model architectures and GPU costs come from PhysicsX, not from measured Osseus deployments. The dossier does not show that the product reduces redesign cycles, engineering hours, certification costs, or time to production. Meanwhile, Trace.Space already addresses AI-assisted traceability, Duro owns hardware lifecycle data, and Altium has combined Valispace with an established electronics distribution channel. A $58.36 billion engineering-software market growing 9.5% annually is context, not evidence that this cross-domain layer captures meaningful spend.

The bull case is that category fragmentation is precisely the opening: incumbents each own one artifact class, while Osseus can connect the causal chain among them. Mariathasan brings biomedical hardware, machine learning, and prior startup experience; Patel brings software, agentic QA work at Apple, and hands-on robotics. If this team builds a reliable graph before the adjacent vendors cross their boundaries, the first integration can become a distribution channel for testing, compliance, supplier coordination, and eventually procurement. The permissioned dependency record is a credible architecture for that outcome.

But current pull does not distinguish that bull case from a well-designed pre-seed thesis. “Onboarding R&D teams” supplies neither a count nor repeat usage, and 418 LinkedIn followers do not establish product-market fit. The marketplace adds a second liquidity problem before the core system of record is proven. The shift from a broad “research brain” to a robotics platform also suggests that the market boundary is still being discovered. No known exception applies: the founders have relevant breadth, but the dossier does not document frontier research, uniquely scarce technical capability, or a capital-intensive breakthrough that justifies overriding the missing market and adoption gates.

I would pass today. The verifiable flip condition is three named robotics teams paying and expanding after 90 days because Osseus repeatedly catches consequential cross-domain dependencies their existing tools miss. That would validate the controlling variable, demonstrate pull, and show that the dependency graph is becoming infrastructure rather than a feature incumbents can bundle.
