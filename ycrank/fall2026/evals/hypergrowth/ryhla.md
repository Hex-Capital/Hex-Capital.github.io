# Ryhla -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with developers of 1–50 MW projects, Ryhla’s most plausible growth path is to screen successive portfolios for the same developers and reuse calibrated feeder models across buyers in each covered territory. Ryhla reports live models in 11 utility territories across eight states and a live Midwest solar deployment. It also reports that screening out eight of ten planned battery projects saved a customer about $160,000 in fees. That outcome illustrates the value of portfolio screening. The memo gives no evidence of repeat paid use or account expansion. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The screening workflow looks reusable, but the current product relies on territory-specific feeder models and backtesting against utility studies. Filing management also depends on the receiving utility’s process. Those dependencies support a low portability score. The memo documents referrals and a website form for individual projects. The form makes intake repeatable, while the path from submission through payment and a usable screen remains unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The two-person team’s public launch and reported deployments establish early use. The memo gives no first paid date, realized revenue, or paying-customer trend, so the commercial traction default applies. Cost economics also takes the default. In a **per-project pricing scenario**, contribution would be the project fee less attributable selling, onboarding, computing, support, and filing labor. New territories would also need enough paid volume to recover feeder-data and calibration costs. The memo supplies neither a price nor measured cost inputs. (Ryhla, The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Ryhla may have to build and validate a local model for each utility territory and provide hands-on filing support for each project. Strong fit in covered territories would do little to accelerate growth if those costs recur with every expansion. The reported 91% approval rate covers only recommended sites, with no disclosed sample size or outcomes for screened-out sites. It gives limited evidence that model performance will carry over. The structural gate remains open because the reported domestic footprint shows replication across utilities, and the memo leaves the incremental work and cost of that replication unresolved. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Ryhla serve an ordinary paying developer in a utility territory outside the US using its existing screening and filing workflow with bounded adaptation? A paid deployment that documents the data and model changes, staff time through first value, and contribution at the actual price would answer it. Bounded adaptation and positive contribution would support rescoring for Reach out if the score reaches 65 and confidence reaches Medium. Evidence that each market requires substantial bespoke rebuilding with no credible near-term transfer path would change the action to Pass.
