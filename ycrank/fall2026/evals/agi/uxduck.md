# UXDuck -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

UXDuck’s strongest seed case is an integrated ground station for custom-drone teams. The product combines browser simulation, vehicle connectivity, configuration, mission planning, and flight replay. A CTO quoted in the memo reports using UXDuck for demos and flying after getting a custom aircraft flying within five minutes of download. UXDuck reports more than 500 simulated flight hours. These are early use signals. Business pricing is quote-based, and the memo provides no paid deployments or delivery-cost data. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

**Building blocks of superintelligence.** The product serves drone development and operations. The memo identifies no AI workload that depends on scarce ground-station capacity and no evidence that rising AI deployment would make this input harder to obtain. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

**Industry enabled by superintelligence.** The founder reports repeated integration and testing work, and the product offers permission-controlled AI assistance. The reported fast setup shows workflow utility. The memo leaves open how much of that result came from AI, how much engineering time AI saves per successful integration, and whether those savings make a previously unaffordable use viable. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The Apache-licensed core, overlapping incumbent tools, and support for parallel use make baseline workflows easy to substitute. Saved configurations could create switching costs if teams rely on them as engineering records, but retention is unproven. The founder’s prior drone-software work and the shipped fix for a documented flight-control regression support execution at seed, with dependable delivery across vehicle configurations still open. (Defensibility, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

If engineering time forces small teams to skip validation on some custom configurations, AI assistance inside the ground station could make that testing affordable at the frequency they need. Vehicle connectivity, simulation, and saved configuration history could still give buyers a reason to use the integrated product when comparable models are available. The custom-aircraft testimonial makes that scenario plausible, but it establishes neither prior exclusion nor an AI-driven cost change. Both paths score zero, triggering Pass. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a small commercial drone team use UXDuck’s AI to validate custom configurations at a cadence it previously could not afford? A representative pilot would change the action to Reach out if it showed that AI lowered the cost per successful validation, including engineer review and rework, enough for the team to adopt that cadence and prefer UXDuck’s integrated workflow over its existing tools with a comparable model.
