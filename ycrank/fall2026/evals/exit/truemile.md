# TrueMile -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is TrueMile’s strongest path. An established carrier could buy the fleet-planning solver and dispatch workflows to reduce empty miles and handle more trucks per dispatcher. The memo documents that the founder-built solver runs in TrueMile’s own operation. TrueMile reports 9× dispatcher capacity and 17% higher dry-van revenue per mile on AI-selected loads. Ownership could give a buyer control over how the solver learns from actual trips and integrates with dispatch across its fleet. That advantage remains an inference. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is a repeatable contribution-margin gain across several recurring lanes after accounting for empty miles and manual approvals. TrueMile reports $57,000 in freight booked over three weeks, and the memo documents outreach for two dedicated-lane partnerships. Realized revenue, carrier margins, and funding for a multi-lane test remain unverified. A sale would also depend on rights to transfer the solver and operating data, continuity of key contracts, and how the buyer handles leased-equipment obligations. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks, Defensibility)

**Proceeds bridge.** Using the thesis’s illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership dilutes to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The $2.5 million cash target within eight years requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios below are assumptions. They use zero net debt, transaction fees, and preference adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity and the holder receives 3% pro rata. All consideration is cash at closing before investor taxes. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each $0.

| Scenario | Timing and assumed outcome | Holder cash and gross multiple |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | Year 5, $40 million enterprise value | $1.2 million, 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7, $100 million enterprise value | $3 million, 6× at 3% ownership. At 2%, $2 million, 4×. |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested | $0 realized, 0×. Shares remain held. |

The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction price to support either sale value. Cargofy’s secondary shows that a peer’s shareholders received liquidity. The memo supplies no evidence of demand for TrueMile shares or of another route that could pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A carrier could combine its existing dispatch operation with commercially available freight matching and route planning. TrueMile still requires a person to approve every load and carries the operating responsibilities that come with leased equipment. If a buyer can capture similar margin gains through vendors, the value of owning TrueMile could fall below the proceeds hurdle. The memo establishes that buyers can source parts of the workflow. It leaves open whether those parts can match the integrated result or cap the price, so the structural Pass gate remains unmet. (Defensibility, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a funded carrier pilot show a repeatable contribution-margin gain across recurring lanes that makes owning TrueMile worth enough to clear the holder’s proceeds hurdle? A carrier-validated comparison with its best available dispatch alternative, including manual approvals and integration costs, would answer it. Move to Reach out if the measured gain and the carrier’s addressable fleet support a cash acquisition above the hurdle under plausible holder terms.
