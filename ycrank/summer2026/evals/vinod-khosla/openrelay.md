# OpenRelay -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

One hundred billion tokens weekly sounds large, but tokens are throughput, not consequence. OpenRelay addresses a real $8.21 billion GPU-services pool growing toward $26.62 billion, yet the quantified outcome is one B300 deployment costing 25% less than reserved cloud capacity. That is useful infrastructure optimization. It is not yet evidence that an unlikely breakthrough makes inference 10x cheaper or radically expands who can afford intelligence.

The technical evidence is more substantial than the ambition statement. Two founders operate across 22 locations, four continents, and 11 accelerator SKUs, continuously benchmarking heterogeneous capacity behind one endpoint. This is a working system, not a slide-deck wrapper. But the only disclosed comparison is a customer testimonial claiming greater than 25% savings. RunPod, Vast.ai, Together AI, and hyperscalers can all add routing; an OpenAI-compatible interface makes adoption easier while also making departure easier. There is no measured 5–10x discontinuity and no identified structural barrier.

The bull case stings. Patel helped build Amazon Bedrock and managed 10,000-plus-GPU clusters; Wang built a half-megawatt data center and worked across TensorDock and Voltage Park. Their skills cover both orchestration and physical capacity, and achieving this operating footprint with two people is an unusually strong pre-seed signal. The relevant **Block** exception is a tiny team entering a giant existing value pool through a modest initial product that could expand into infrastructure. The material difference is that OpenRelay has not shown a proprietary compounding loop comparable to a financial network: its routing data may improve, but provider counts, customer retention, and exclusive supply are undisclosed.

The largest kill risk is not whether the founders can deploy GPUs. They have proved that. It is whether cross-provider routing produces durable economics after competitors respond. The dossier contains no cheapest decisive experiment, numerical kill threshold, cost, or stop condition for that question. Dedicated VMs still lack automatic migration, SOC 2 Type II remains unfinished, and distributed third-party hardware creates a security burden precisely where enterprise customers demand predictability.

Economics are promising but incomplete. Published token and GPU-minute prices, plus one 25% savings result, show that OpenRelay can sometimes beat reserved-cloud pricing without subsidy. They do not provide a bottom-up path through provider payments, utilization, networking, failover overhead, and gross margin. Cheap capacity is not enough if the aggregator captures no durable margin.

I would not write the check today, but I would keep the meeting. One verifiable result flips me: a controlled production benchmark showing at least 5x delivered price-performance versus RunPod or Together AI at the same latency and availability target, sustained across three accelerator families. Without that discontinuity, OpenRelay is a capable broker inside an increasingly commoditized market; with it, the routing layer could become consequential infrastructure.
