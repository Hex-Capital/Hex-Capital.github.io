# Definite -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Definite’s most useful founder signal is a failed sale. Earlier compliance-product LOIs did not become purchase commitments; the team moved to contract-first selling, as described in the September 2026 interview. That is knowledge acquired through contact with buyers. The launched regulatory-reporting product and verification engine add evidence of building in the problem domain. BitGo controls and Boosted.ai financial-analysis experience provide relevant context. Together, these earn meaningful founder credit, although the company-reported FinBalance benchmark does not establish that finance teams depend on the current product.

The investment turns on what accumulates after implementation. Definite retains customer-specific mappings, deterministic rules, historical snapshots and source-to-cell lineage. Copying the application would not automatically reproduce a customer’s installed financial definitions. That is the strongest bull case: each completed reconciliation could make the next workflow easier to deploy and the existing installation harder to replace. But the dossier establishes those technical mechanisms, not their economic durability. There is no demonstrated migration cost or reuse advantage, and Workato already supplies connectivity, semantic context and orchestration. Custom implementation can produce either a durable asset or a continuing service obligation.

The market offers room to maneuver. A shared financial model can connect an initial reconciliation task to reporting and other back-office workflows for the same finance buyer. Numeric’s expansion across close, reporting and cash management supports the existence of adjacent demand, without proving Definite can capture it. The missing mechanism is customer-created value across accounts. Nothing shows that one customer’s participation improves another’s product or acquisition. Scoped connector development instead leaves an unresolved dependence on company labor. Definite fits my pattern of turning knowledge into scalable output only partially; the dossier does not establish the permissionless contribution or shared supply that makes that output compound.

Monthly contracts, no deployment fee and a two-month opt-out make the offer concrete, but establish little about value capture. No monetary price, named paying customer or realized revenue is disclosed. Absorbing implementation cost while allowing an early exit could work if deployments become repeatable; the economics remain unproved. The 297 LinkedIn followers and Dynamics Roadshow activity show visibility and selling effort, not purchasing behavior. I see no evidence of an integrity problem or artificial financing pressure. Nor does changing direction after failed LOIs establish the red flag of abandoning conviction whenever someone objects.

I would keep Definite under observation and decline a check today. My exceptions permit unresolved monetization when a network already compounds, or absent distribution when an exceptional prototype and capital efficiency carry the case. Definite has a shipped predecessor and useful commercial learning, but neither exception is established strongly enough here. The founder case deserves more credit than the durable-asset case. **My flip condition:** a documented cohort of three paying customers that each adds a second workflow using retained mappings and rules with materially fewer implementation hours than its first. That would show the financial model accumulating reusable value inside deployments—the mechanism on which this investment depends.
