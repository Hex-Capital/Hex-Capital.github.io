# Canos -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Canos has a $699 test price and no disclosed investment price. Those answer very different questions. The dossier gives me neither post-money valuation nor competing-term-sheet count, so I cannot establish whether my check buys an overlooked risk at an attractive price. YC participation proves neither scarcity nor an auction. I would pass on the investment described here while taking the founders seriously.

The strongest evidence is that Tsai and Li have already worked across the systems Canos needs to connect: veterinary diagnostics and computational pathology. Tsai’s prior ZYTCA pathology product reportedly reached 10,000-plus clinics; the founders previously built veterinary AI pathology together. That is an operating receipt, beyond their Oxford credentials. Canos fits my preference for founders who can translate between scientific capability and an inconvenient physical workflow. Blood collection, sample handling and veterinary interpretation are substantial operational work, but complexity itself is no objection when the team understands both sides.

The capital question is what exact uncertainty this round retires. Canos reports 90.6% sensitivity at 95.1% specificity in a 303-dog diagnostic analysis under nested cross-validation. That gives the prospective experiment a substantive starting point. It does not establish performance in routine screening or prove that a longitudinal result changes a veterinarian’s treatment decision. OncoTrack’s free early-access testing could generate useful evidence, but the dossier supplies no round-funded hypothesis, deadline or pass/fail milestone. I cannot credit an experiment I have designed on the founders’ behalf.

The strongest bull case is that repeated monitoring creates an unusually valuable private dataset: molecular changes linked to treatment and outcomes, collected through veterinary relationships. The founders’ prior deployment experience makes that possibility credible. But Canos has not documented exclusive data rights, repeat paid testing or a purchase caused by its owned workflow. Nationwide kits are collection infrastructure; they do not yet establish distribution leverage. CanCan already markets sequencing-based canine blood testing, making proprietary longitudinal evidence more consequential than the AI label. My exceptions to normal financing discipline require demonstrated conversion, operating economics or unusually hard external validation. The research benchmark alone does not meet that standard.

At $699 including the veterinary visit, I want to know what remains after collection, sequencing, interpretation and support. Revenue, gross margin, burn and runway are undisclosed; that leaves the economic question open rather than proving an uneconomic business. There is also no evidence of an auction, financing treadmill or margin plan predicated on disappearing AI errors. My pass rests on unestablished underwriting gates, alongside genuine founder fit. One verifiable flip condition would be a signed sub-$10-million post-money financing package whose costed proceeds fund a prespecified prospective OncoTrack study through paid, contribution-positive repeat testing and cash break-even without another equity raise. That would connect the price, the experiment and the business.
