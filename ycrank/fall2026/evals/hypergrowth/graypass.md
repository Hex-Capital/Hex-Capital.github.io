# GrayPass -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

GrayPass’s strongest path is to sell application teams an API that checks who controls a session before a payout, data export, or permission change. Assuming strong fit with those teams, the same policy decisions and action-bound proofs could serve the same recurring job across markets. The documented Illinois consent requirement constrains biometric collection, and the core API is in beta. The portability case rests on the core API, with experimental behavioral recognition confined to sandbox and shadow evaluation. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The evaluation form gives buyers a concrete entry point, and shadow mode gives them a reusable way to test one action before enforcement. Each customer still has to wire the decision into its server-side authorization path. The memo documents the evaluation flow, with limited evidence that ordinary buyers can complete purchase and production setup with minimal help. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The published starting price is $500 per month, and the terms describe annual contracts and allowances metered by assured actions or active Brainprint months. Protecting more workflows could increase usage within an existing account. Higher recurring spend would depend on customers paying for additional capacity, which the memo leaves unresolved. Separately, the two-person company reported $50,000 in October revenue as of launch and more than 900,000 customer-app sessions. That gives GrayPass an early commercial signal. Revenue mix, concentration, and growth pace remain unclear, and the session count leaves billable volume unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Facts, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

At the advertised starting price, an illustrative deployment brings in $500 per month before decision processing, proof signing, any behavioral data storage, and attributable sales, onboarding, and support. Reusing the API and shadow test across customers could limit GrayPass’s implementation labor. The memo leaves workload costs and vendor hours unmeasured, so incremental contribution remains uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Shadow evaluations could remain easier to start than paid enforcement is to deploy. Customers must change authorization code for every protected workflow, and teams drawn by behavioral recognition would be evaluating an experimental capability. Biometric consent can add rollout work. If ordinary deployments also require sustained GrayPass engineering support, the same work could slow acquisition and erode contribution. The structural gate stays open because the memo establishes customer-side integration and a specific consent constraint, while leaving unavoidable vendor work and market-by-market rebuilding unestablished. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of ordinary application teams from one repeatable developer channel purchase GrayPass and move its first sensitive action from shadow mode to paid production enforcement with minimal GrayPass engineering help? A documented path from discovery through payment, backed by invoices, live enforcement records, and low support hours across the cohort, would move the action to Reach out. If the cohort still needs substantial founder-led integration, keep GrayPass on Watch.
