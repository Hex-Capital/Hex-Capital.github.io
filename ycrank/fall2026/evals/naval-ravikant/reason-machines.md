# Reason Machines -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Adi and Sven have earned the right to build this. Dereference, Ara, and now Reason show repeated work on the same underlying problem: helping developers turn intent into running software. The October technical disclosure exposes actual engineering decisions—parallel tools, deferred discovery, cache-preserving prompts. That is specific knowledge expressed through artifacts. Their universities matter much less.

The difficulty is what Reason Machines gets to keep. Its reported 22% cost advantage over Codex could attract customers, but the disclosed mechanisms are reproducible. The harness builds on Pi; the messaging engine permits competing developers to reuse it. Open source can create distribution, but permission to contribute does not establish a network effect. The dossier identifies no exclusive learning asset that becomes stronger with each deployment. A competitor could copy the techniques without inheriting the founders’ experience, but experience must keep winning every subsequent race.

The strongest objection to passing is substantial: these founders may already be winning that race. The October 22 claim of 10,000 users and 300 active customer Slack channels is enough to take seriously, even though it is founder-reported. A functioning product with this much early attention could develop exceptional taste through rapid feedback. My exceptions for unusually capable builders and demonstrated prototypes deserve consideration here. But the dossier does not show repeated customer preference or a feedback process whose improvements compound beyond what competitors can reproduce. Three hundred channels also do not establish that one customer improves the product for another.

There is ample demand. The cited $9.8 billion coding-tools market and Cognition’s reported $1 billion revenue run rate establish existing spending. Reason’s controller, interchangeable execution environments, and Slack interface offer a plausible route from individual coding tasks into team coordination. The adjacent budget remains unspecified, however, and Devin already supports shared context and external agents. Calling the product a teammate for “work and code” does not yet demonstrate room to expand beyond this contested buying decision.

Published prices of $50 and $200 per person per month establish a direct charging mechanism. They do not establish paid conversion or gross profit after model and execution costs. Likewise, repository stars demonstrate attention without proving proprietary distribution. Reason fits my interest in tools that multiply an individual’s output; it has yet to demonstrate my stronger preference for customers creating reusable value for one another. There is no documented integrity problem or artificial financing pressure, and the Ara-to-Reason transition alone supplies no evidence of unreliable conviction.

I would pass with my own money today. Founder capability is the strongest evidence; an advantage that survives copied code is the weakest. The single condition that would reopen the decision is a controlled deployment study showing that accumulated customer-authorized workflow learning materially improves task completion over an otherwise identical fresh installation, with those learning rights exclusive to Reason. That would establish a concrete asset behind the promising execution.
