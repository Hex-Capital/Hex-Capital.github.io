# Ailit -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is Ailit’s strongest private-company exit path. A locomotive supplier could buy Superwagon to offer battery power across existing diesel-electric fleets. Wabtec’s dedicated battery locomotive shows product overlap. Ailit proposes retaining diesel backup and operating the packs and swap sites itself, with a tariff about 20% below the diesel equivalent. That discount is a company target. The ownership case is an inference: if coordinating packs, charging sites, and train schedules produces reliable service across routes, a supplier could gain more by controlling the system and its rollout than by building a retrofit or partnering with Ailit. The memo leaves buyer willingness to pay unverified. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

The observed prototype evidence is a full-scale unit positioned behind a locomotive ahead of commissioning. Ailit targets its first railroad customer deployment in 2027. Prior funding of at least SEK 10 million and YC backing support a path to testing, while the pilot budget, available runway, and funding for packs and utility connections remain unknown. The Swedish patent filing documents an application by Ailit Rail AB. Its scope and whether a sale of the invested entity could transfer the necessary IP and site rights remain open. (*Market & Traction*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*, *Defensibility*)

**Proceeds bridge.** With investment terms undisclosed, the illustrative seed case uses a $500,000 total check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, or 5×. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, no other price adjustments or senior claims, and a simple pro rata waterfall. Enterprise value therefore equals distributable equity consideration, paid entirely in cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. Returns are before investor taxes.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Holder cash | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $50M → $50M | $1.5M | 3× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100M → $100M | $3.0M | 6× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested | $0, shares retained | 0× |

The hurdle is $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, and $62.5 million at 4%. The modeled $100 million sale clears at 3% and yields only 4× at 2%. These prices are sensitivities. The memo supplies no usable acquisition valuation or buyer economics to support them. The Swedish entity’s reported FY2025 turnover of SEK 0 gives no basis for underwriting a cash-flow buyout or payout, and the memo supplies no independent evidence of holder liquidity through a secondary or listing. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A railroad could buy Ailit’s proposed per-kWh service without buying the company, and a locomotive supplier could continue selling its own battery locomotive or partner with Ailit. The service also leaves Ailit carrying battery and site costs as cheaper diesel could compress its margin. A successful pilot could therefore win a customer while leaving the acquisition rationale weak. The memo does not establish that these substitutes would cap a buyer’s price below the hurdle or that prolonged private ownership is the probable outcome. The Pass override does not apply. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid freight-route deployment show enough value from owning Superwagon to justify a locomotive supplier paying above the holder’s hurdle? The trigger for **Reach out** is a supplier’s preliminary economic case grounded in loaded-haul performance, repeatable swaps, and route margins after pack and site costs. It would need to explain why owning the system captures more value than building or partnering, and support a price that could leave this holder $2.5 million after dilution and the waterfall. A pilot that establishes customer savings alone leaves Ailit on **Watch**. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)
