# GodHands -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

GodHands launched a desktop product that, according to the company, captures workflows in approved apps and turns them into source-linked playbooks for employees and agents. One potential input to AI deployment is company-specific knowledge about exceptions that desktop agents must handle. The memo describes how teams lose that knowledge and rebuild it through documentation and shadowing. It supplies no evidence that acquiring it is a scarce input limiting AI deployment or that alternative workflow-capture products are slow to supply it. The building-blocks path scores zero. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

The strongest evidenced business is faster workflow documentation and handover. Company-published testimonials report that a support team documented its escalation process within two weeks and that a replacement controller closed the books on time in month two, compared with two quarters previously. These accounts suggest benefits within existing workflows. The memo identifies no buyer, use, or operating cadence that became economically viable because of AI. Agent-proposed changes still require human approval, and the memo gives no cost per correct outcome after review and rework. The industry-enabled path scores zero, triggering Pass. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Retained, source-linked playbooks could make switching costly as exceptions accumulate. That is an inference, with no retention or migration evidence and with overlapping products already on the market. The testimonials indicate concrete use, while paid conversion, repeat demand, and revenue remain unverified. The October launch and the CTO’s prior systems work support seed-stage execution credit. Reliable handling of exceptions remains unproven. Quoted subscription terms establish a pricing model, and the memo gives no basis for estimating contribution from the next deployment. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

GodHands could make low-volume support exception handling economical when a team currently leaves those cases to specialists because authoring and maintaining automation costs too much. That scenario assumes screen capture can learn enough exception detail to cut specialist time after approvals and corrections. The reported escalation documentation and a separate testimonial that coding agents began following internal conventions make the mechanism plausible. Under model parity, buyers would still need to capture and maintain their own procedures and integrate approval into the workflow. The memo documents neither a support queue previously excluded from automation on cost grounds nor a deployment that crossed that threshold, so the scenario leaves the Pass gate in effect. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid deployment make a support exception queue that the buyer previously found too expensive to automate economical at the buyer’s required quality? A buyer-verified comparison would measure fully loaded cost per correctly resolved case, including setup, playbook upkeep, approvals, and rework, against the buyer’s prior workflow and a comparable-model setup without GodHands. A deployment that meets the buyer’s cost and quality thresholds and shows that GodHands’s workflow capture materially contributes would change the action to Reach out.
