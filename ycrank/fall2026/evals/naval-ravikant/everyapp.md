# EveryApp -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Ben Senescu has earned specific knowledge by building the thing he now wants other people to build. The January Gateway, subsequent OpenSEO application, and October 26 platform launch establish a sequence of working artifacts. OpenSEO’s 25.3K GitHub stars corroborate that his work reached developers beyond his immediate circle. That is stronger evidence than his Klaviyo title. I credit the founder’s demonstrated understanding of application deployment; I do not transfer OpenSEO’s audience into EveryApp’s adoption numbers.

EveryApp’s hardest problem is that its promise also describes its competitive exposure. Applications run in the customer’s Cloudflare account, and the MIT license permits another provider to sell the same platform. Shared authentication and permissions solve useful problems, but the dossier establishes no exclusive asset that survives copying the implementation. Cloudflare supplies the infrastructure; EveryApp must establish what it owns economically. Possible migration friction earns no credit without evidence, particularly while backward compatibility remains unpromised.

The expansion path is intelligible: start with developers deploying applications, then serve businesses replacing proprietary subscriptions through the same identity and deployment layer. That creates room to maneuver, although Cloudflare-specific applications constrain the reachable catalog. Cloudron and Coolify demonstrate existing alternatives to this approach; they also make hosting convenience a contested purchase. EveryApp’s announced $10 monthly managed service identifies a payer and a direct charging mechanism. It establishes neither completed purchases nor the margin available after infrastructure and support. OpenSEO’s separate pricing cannot answer those questions for the platform.

The strongest objection to my skepticism is that Senescu has already used an application to recruit attention for the infrastructure beneath it. EveryApp fits my pattern of tools that let individuals turn knowledge into independently owned output. A useful app catalog could give developers distribution and customers more reasons to adopt the Gateway. My prelaunch-product exception allows investing before a user loop appears when a demonstrated prototype and unusual capital efficiency justify it. The prototype is here; unusual capital efficiency is unestablished. The founder-led exception also deserves consideration, but domain competence alone does not establish an exceptional recruiting or operating advantage. And the exception for an emerging network cannot apply yet: 351 platform stars, 16 forks, and 128,561 launch-post views do not show one customer improving another customer’s experience.

I see no documented integrity problem, artificial financing deadline, or evidence of reassurance-dependent founders. The reservation is structural. EveryApp has a builder with relevant knowledge and a useful product, while the permissionless ecosystem that could make it durable remains hypothetical. That leaves me neutral, without a check today. My flip condition is one measured ecosystem result: independently authored apps cause existing customer cohorts to increase retained Gateway usage over six months, with developer participation growing without proportional company support. That would show the platform beginning to compound beyond Senescu’s own output.
