# Conifer -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Conifer’s 194.6-token-per-second Apple Silicon benchmark makes Conifer a single-miracle infrastructure bet whose weakest signal is the absence of a buying curve. **Color Genomics** is the high rung here: workflow infrastructure built on commodity sequencing, avoiding a bet on new chemistry. Conifer shares that architecture by building a Rust routing and inference layer on capable local models rather than inventing a foundation model. Conifer diverges because routing accuracy—the one required miracle—remains unbenchmarked, while hardware performance is verified only on selected Apple Silicon configurations. This is still the cleanest part of the deal: one hard product problem followed by ordinary GTM execution, not a science project with three independent breakthroughs.

**Lyft’s** low-rung failure mechanism in my Series C pass was demand without proof that the available curve would produce winning economics. Conifer has 100-plus waitlist developers and 268 GitHub stars, but Conifer has no disclosed revenue, repeat usage, retention, or named customer. Those signals establish curiosity, not “just working.” OpenRouter’s approximately $50 million in annualized revenue makes the silence more consequential: buyers demonstrably spend in this category, yet Conifer has not shown that local-first routing converts interest into paid behavior. At pre-seed I do not need scale, but I need one legible buying signal; otherwise I apply the absent-evidence prior almost intact.

Gartner’s 2026 identification of AI gateways provides Conifer with a real, if weaker-than-ideal, market catalyst: model proliferation, rising inference spend, and on-device hardware collectively changed the routing equation. Over the next 18 months, Conifer’s mechanism can compound as better local models increase the percentage of requests handled without API fees, which improves savings and generates routing data. The problem is that Conifer’s catalyst is inferred rather than singular—the way Ukraine reopened defense or ChatGPT reopened foundation-model applications—and every improvement in local inference is equally available to OpenRouter, Portkey, and model providers. Conifer is skating for the puck, but the rink is already crowded.

Charles Muehlberger’s edge-inference research and Michael Jeffords’s reported ML work explain why Conifer could ship Sage and a Rust engine that reportedly decodes up to 60% faster than llama.cpp. Conifer therefore sits above the credential-only founder profile, but the dossier does not show the Perplexity pattern: a sequence of products built, tested with users, and visibly improved between conversations. The founder score flips into the Invest range if Conifer can document three rapid releases tied to measured customer feedback—especially routing-quality improvements from production workloads—because that would turn technical capability into demonstrated iteration velocity.

**Stripe** became an index on e-commerce because every new internet business eventually touched the payments rail. Conifer could become an index on heterogeneous AI inference if every enterprise request passes through its cost, privacy, and model-selection layer, but Conifer currently distributes a direct-download application into a control point already contested by OpenRouter, Portkey, Martian, and Not Diamond. Local execution is a useful wedge, particularly for sensitive data, yet no structural barrier or proprietary routing dataset exists today. Conifer must win share against gateway incumbents before it can ride category growth automatically; that is not yet an index position.

OpenRouter’s approximately $50 million annualized revenue makes my 36-month forecast falsifiable: by July 2029, Conifer will have pivoted from a broad least-cost router into a local-inference and security gateway for regulated enterprise engineering teams, reaching $2 million to $6 million ARR but remaining outside the top two general-purpose AI gateways. Conifer’s strong single-miracle architecture can produce a viable product, while Conifer’s missing buying curve and weak index position make a broad routing winner less likely. Local-only handling of sensitive data is the wedge that survives commoditization; generic token savings will not.

Sage’s 268 GitHub stars support the strongest bull case: Conifer may resemble the **Mistral** exception, where a technically exceptional group entered a crowded, capital-intensive category after the foundational miracle had already occurred and used differentiated performance to establish relevance. Conifer’s 60%-faster reported decoding, edge-inference expertise, and local-only security mode could produce a developer default before enterprise revenue becomes publicly visible. The comparison breaks because Mistral’s founders had shipped Llama at Meta, while Conifer has neither comparable prior shipping evidence nor a routing-quality benchmark; the current moat is a benchmark, not distribution. Ten named enterprise customers, at least $50,000 MRR, and growth above 20% month over month would prove Conifer is “just working” and flip my verdict to Invest.
