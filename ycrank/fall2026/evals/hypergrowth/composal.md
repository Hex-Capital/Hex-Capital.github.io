# Composal -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Conditional on strong initial product-market fit, Verify has a credible path to serving engineering teams across markets. The same browser agents can exercise a running web app, save user journeys as repeatable scenarios, and return failure evidence in the pull-request workflow. That release-testing job and the core product appear portable. The clearest access barriers are app-specific authentication and private networks. Verify is the basis for this assessment. Sparks is at the beta waitlist stage, leaving its commercial path uncertain. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Verify’s October launch provides a concrete discovery channel, and its documented scan before checkout gives buyers a way to try the product independently. Repeatable paid activation is less established because production setup can require founder help. The $500 monthly Team plan and $1 prepaid minutes give retained accounts a way to spend more as test coverage or run frequency grows. Saved scenarios and failure history support repeat use. Expansion could offset churn if enough teams consistently exceed the included 3,000 minutes. Actual renewals and expansion remain unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Composal reports finding more than 1,500 bugs in customer apps since mid-October. That indicates use and leaves commercial traction uncertain. The memo gives no realized revenue, paying-customer count, or confirmation that Verify is pre-revenue, so initial traction takes the default. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

At full use of the included minutes, the Team plan yields about $0.17 per browser minute before browser infrastructure, agent inference, and attributable acquisition, setup, and support costs. Additional prepaid minutes yield $1 each before incremental costs. Reusing saved scenarios could spread setup effort over more runs. Positive contribution remains uncertain because the memo provides no measured serving costs or support effort. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Verify could attract teams with a quick scan yet require founder-led implementation for routine paid use. Composal reported that five of ten setup calls stalled on authentication or network controls, and it said nine teams it spoke with had already built QA agents in-house. If access work recurs account by account and customers can substitute internal tooling, acquisition costs could stay high while paid usage stalls. The structural gate stays open because Verify documents dedicated test accounts and private-network connections as ways through those barriers, and the reported calls leave the prevalence and ongoing cost of required intervention unresolved. (Key Risks, Defensibility)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a representative cohort of new Verify buyers move from the discovery scan to paid, recurring release checks without founder-led setup and with positive incremental contribution? Checkout and run records, paired with attributable onboarding, support, and serving costs, would answer it. A cohort that demonstrates this would move the action to Reach out.
