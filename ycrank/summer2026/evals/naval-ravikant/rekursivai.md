# rekursiv.ai -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Joshua Dillon and Dan Kondratyuk have unusually strong specific knowledge. They did not arrive at autonomous ML research through a market map: they created TensorFlow Probability, built Veo and VideoPoet, led foundation-model and world-model work at Luma, and then built a system that completed hundreds of experiments. The founders have already performed nearly every component of the job they want to automate. This is top-quartile founder evidence.

The system is more than a demo. It coordinated 235 ARC experiments across 15 directions and 684 Sudoku experiments in four days, producing 100% reported Sudoku-Extreme accuracy and competitive ARC results at sharply lower compute cost than selected peers. The important artifact is the feedback loop—hypothesis, experiment, criticism, repair—not any single benchmark result. But technical complexity is not durability. Public evaluation may overstate ARC performance by four to six points, the Sudoku method did not transfer directly to ARC, and hundreds of experiments remain dependent on commodity models and compute.

If a competitor copies the code, rekursiv.ai has not yet shown what survives. Some components and discoveries are Apache-licensed, Sakana AI publishes competing code, and the inferred corpus of experiment histories is not documented as exclusive or improving experiment selection. The company could convert individual researchers’ knowledge into scalable output, which matches a real pattern of permissionless leverage. It has not yet created a permissionless system: 16 GitHub followers, 29 stars on its leading repository, and an access-request gate do not show researchers contributing value or distribution to one another.

The market is potentially enormous but presently unbounded rather than mapped. Autonomous ML experimentation can plausibly expand from benchmark optimization into model training, evaluation, and eventually broader scientific discovery. Yet Periodic Labs, Lila Sciences, and Edison Scientific are pursuing physical or domain-specific systems with proprietary deployment environments, while rekursiv.ai remains a software cockpit. Large adjacent markets exist; the dossier does not establish the shared asset that unlocks them.

The strongest exception is **Notion**: a demonstrated prototype and unusually capable builders justified investing before an observable user loop. The shared mechanism is founders developing a new product language before the category becomes legible. The material difference is that rekursiv.ai’s output currently depends on repeatable external compute and copyable software, while neither capital efficiency nor a compounding proprietary substrate has been established. The $5 million financing validates access to capital, not customer value; there is no disclosed payer, price, revenue, or named customer.

I see no founder-integrity, artificial-scarcity, or morale red flag. I also see no reason to manufacture a moat from impressive benchmarks. I am neutral today: exceptional founders have built a credible research engine, but the company has not converted intelligence into ownership. One condition flips me to invest: three independent research organizations each paying at least $100,000 annually while contributing exclusive experiment traces that improve held-out research performance by at least 20%.
