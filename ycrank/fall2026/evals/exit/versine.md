# Versine -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest exit route is a strategic sale to a consumer payments platform. Versine describes a product that lets assistants initiate person-to-person transfers under passkey approvals or standing rules, with bank claim links for recipients without accounts. A platform could buy that workflow if it brings in repeat payment volume that would be costly to capture by building in-house. The memo records 182 Product Hunt upvotes and an Android listing showing 5+ downloads, with no data on repeat transfers, payment volume, or buyer interest. A pilot with a payments platform could measure incremental repeat transfers after launch credits expire. The launched product makes that test plausible, while pilot funding and runway remain unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The shared benchmark is a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at full pro rata participation. The sale scenarios below assume the holder is eligible and cashes out the entire stake within eight years, with no preference changing its 3% share of the proceeds. The memo provides no sale-price or waterfall evidence, so these figures illustrate the hurdle.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $10M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $30M for equity holders. | $900K (1.8×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M for equity holders. | $2.7M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | The company reinvests cash for eight years, with no distribution or eligible share sale. | $0 (0×) |

Even the strong case falls short if the holder can sell only 90% of the stake, yielding $2.43 million in cash. The memo leaves Bridge payout and identity-verification continuity after a sale, shareholder eligibility, and closing terms unverified. It supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Defensibility, Key Risks, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

A payments incumbent could build the workflow using its own wallet and approval infrastructure. Venmo and Cash App already handle person-to-person payments, and Stripe Link shows that an incumbent can extend its approval infrastructure to assistant-initiated merchant purchases. Versine’s launch metrics leave open whether it can bring a buyer recurring payment volume or a capability worth acquiring. The evidence supports a credible build alternative, while leaving the cost of replicating Versine’s specific workflow and the strongest credible sale price unresolved. Neither Pass gate is triggered. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a payments-platform pilot show enough incremental, repeat assistant-initiated payment volume after launch credits expire to support an acquisition that leaves at least $83.3 million distributable to equity holders? Cohort data and a buyer’s quantified comparison of acquiring versus building would answer it. Move to Reach out if that evidence supports a price that clears the hurdle after deal adjustments, and to Pass if it establishes that the strongest credible sale leaves the holder materially short.
