# OneCLI -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Jonathan Fishner and Guy Ben-Aharon make OneCLI a hacker-built company whose weakest signal is that the idea may be adjacent to their experience rather than organic to their own pain. OneCLI has 320K claimed downloads, 2.9K GitHub stars, and a working gateway, but the dossier never names the incident that forced either founder to build it for himself. The analytical signature is strong hackers, less certain organic idea. That combination usually produces an excellent product; it does not always produce a great company.

**Stripe** (2010) is the high rung for hacker genealogy: the Collisons had shipped commerce software before turning their accumulated frustration into an API. OneCLI belongs close to that rung because Fishner and Ben-Aharon previously built ChartDB to 22.7K GitHub stars and more than $10K MRR, while Ben-Aharon was the first engineer at Argon Security before its acquisition. OneCLI diverges because Stripe’s genealogy included an unusually clear sequence of childhood hacking, Auctomatic, and commerce pain; OneCLI shows impressive artifacts, but not yet the same inevitability.

**Kiko** (2005) failed partly because capable hackers built a plausible product without the idea being sufficiently rooted in a problem that pulled the market toward them. OneCLI does not repeat the whole Kiko pattern: Fishner worked in network security, Ben-Aharon worked in supply-chain security, and both served in Unit 8200. But OneCLI’s origin is described only as the 2025–26 rise of agents creating a credential-boundary problem. OneCLI has deep founder-market fit; it lacks the Dropbox-style sentence about the founders personally suffering the problem before deciding to sell a solution.

ChartDB’s 22.7K stars and $10K-plus MRR show how OneCLI’s resourcefulness could compound over the next 18 months. Fishner and Ben-Aharon already know how to turn open-source attention into a product, and OneCLI has repeated the first half of that motion with a 160-point Show HN launch, 170 forks, and NanoClaw making OneCLI its default credential layer. The animal test is not merely whether founders work hard. It is whether they make reality yield. OneCLI has left tracks, though converting 320K downloads into paid security deployments remains the missing stunt.

OneCLI’s $1,499-per-month managed VPC tier points toward a real schlep: forcing agent traffic through a gateway, maintaining network policies, satisfying security reviews, and handling credentials that briefly exist decrypted in memory. OneCLI would move into Coinbase territory if named customers were already enforcing proxy-only egress through Docker or Kubernetes policies and surviving enterprise audits. Without that evidence, OneCLI may still be selling elegant proxy software while customers bear the ugliest operational work themselves. The score flips upward when OneCLI proves it personally owns the deployment and compliance grind.

**Twitch** (2007) is the useful parallel for OneCLI’s co-founder configuration. Kan and Shear’s prior failure at Kiko made their next collaboration more informative, not less; they had already discovered how each behaved when the first idea stopped working. Fishner and Ben-Aharon similarly built ChartDB together before pivoting to OneCLI, and ChartDB reached both substantial developer adoption and revenue. OneCLI therefore has something better than two impressive résumés: OneCLI has a pair that has already shipped, monetized, and changed direction together.

Infisical’s April 2026 launch of a similar open-source HTTP credential proxy is why I predict OneCLI will be acquired by **1Password within 36 months after reaching roughly $3–7M ARR**, rather than becoming an independent category giant in that period. OneCLI’s high hacker-genealogy signal should produce fast integrations and developer adoption; OneCLI’s weaker organic-pain signal and Apache-2.0 core make durable independence less certain. A larger secrets platform can buy the developer default, distribution, and founders faster than OneCLI can build an enterprise identity suite around them.

OneCLI’s 320K claimed downloads form the strongest bear case too: downloads can be empty calories, Infisical can copy the architecture, traffic can bypass the proxy, and root access to the gateway host can expose decrypted credentials. OneCLI could become an Apache repository surrounded by better-funded vendors—Oasis has raised $195M—without ever becoming a business. What defeats that case today is that Fishner and Ben-Aharon have already converted an open-source product into more than $10K MRR once, while NanoClaw’s default integration gives OneCLI distribution inside an actual agent workflow. I would invest, but failure to reach $1M ARR or disclose several paying managed-VPC customers within 18 months would flip me to Pass.
