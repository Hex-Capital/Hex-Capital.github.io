# TareBio -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. The interesting future is not merely that cancer vaccines improve; it is that a physician can sample the immune response already fighting a disease, recover the antigens that response has discovered, and produce a vaccine in weeks. TareBio has one artifact leaking from that future: an end-to-end platform run reportedly produced a vaccine that cured lymphoma in mice. The path backward is legible—human antigen recovery, safety and efficacy validation, an IND, reproducible patient-specific manufacturing, then broader disease programs—but only the first step has been demonstrated. Antigen escape and the undisclosed manufacturing and regulatory plan leave several difficult transitions, not unnamed miracles, between the mouse and the institution.

Eta Atolia is the strongest part of this company. Her 2023 UCLA-Caltech dissertation was literally titled “Empirical Reverse Engineering of Vaccine Neoantigens,” and TareBio incorporates eight years of work spanning MIT, the Koch Institute, Harvard Medical School, Weizmann, and UCLA’s Butte Lab. Gates Foundation support and the completed lymphoma experiment are first-believer proof: this is not a recent biotechnology costume stitched together for Summer 2026. The missing piece is recruitment. A solo founder can be wonderfully accountable—there is no 50/50 governance knot here—but clinical development, manufacturing, regulatory work, and partnerships require evidence that she can attract people who are exceptional outside her own scientific lane.

The broken rule is antigen selection by prediction: developers nominate what ought to provoke immunity, then discover whether biology agrees. TareBio reverses that loop by beginning with immune cells already responding to disease. That is a real systems insight, but the dossier does not quantify the rent created by today’s trial-and-error process, identify who captures it, or show whose economic or occupational agency is returned. Patients may receive better vaccines, but “better therapeutic outcome” is not yet the same mechanism as transferring control away from a gatekeeper.

The mouse cure is substantial technical proof and almost no behavioral proof. No patient, clinician, or pharmaceutical partner has yet spent money, reorganized a workflow, supplied repeated samples, or trusted the platform with a treatment decision. The relevant known exception is CosmicBrain AI: a functioning prototype and lifelong technical obsession outweighed missing revenue, but signed industrial proofs of concept showed that an outside institution would cross the laboratory boundary. TareBio has the obsession and prototype; it does not yet have that external act of belief.

This does fit one of my unstated patterns: the immune-imprint system could become concealed infrastructure beneath many visible cancer and infectious-disease vaccines, with disease-specific datasets compounding across HIV, tuberculosis, hepatitis C, and oncology. Today, however, that layer is a possibility rather than a controlled bottleneck. There are no disclosed patents, dataset scale, or human outcomes, while Nouscom, Enara Bio, and Geneos have raised roughly $142 million, $50 million, and $49.5 million respectively, and the open pVACtools suite keeps adjacent antigen-selection methods accessible. The claim “any cancer or infection” is much wider than the lymphoma evidence.

My red-flag review does not find a leadership shell game or equal-control deadlock, but it does find an unresolved custody question: tumor samples, blood, immune-cell profiles, and treatment outcomes can become extraordinarily sensitive datasets, and no limiting consent or data architecture is described. It also finds a one-year first-patient ambition without a disclosed IND, trial, manufacturing-validation plan, or human safety data. I expect that by August 2029 TareBio will have narrowed to one oncology indication and either entered or received clearance for an initial Phase 1 study, rather than operating a general vaccine platform across cancers and infections. I would not write the check today. One verifiable event flips me: independent human-sample validation that recovers clinically relevant antigens, paired with a named clinical or pharmaceutical partner committing to the first regulated program.
