# Definite -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Definite could make weekly site-level margin analysis economical for operators that currently trace operating changes to financial results only at month-end or when a problem warrants days of controller work. That buyer behavior is a scenario assumption. The memo reports a manual spreadsheet workaround, which is indirect evidence of demand for the underlying job. Definite says its software proposes mappings between operating and finance records, then applies deterministic rules to produce results tied to source records. The economic test is whether it lowers the full cost per controller-approved analysis after accounting for setup, rule maintenance, computing, review, and rework. The memo gives no measured comparison or buyer commitment at the proposed cadence. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

With equivalent models available to rivals, buyers might still pay Definite to preserve site-specific finance rules and replay analyses with their approval history intact. The company describes those capabilities. The switching cost is an inference, and the memo provides no migration or retention evidence. The memo records a prior bank-reporting launch and a subsequent shift in positioning. The founders’ prior work on financial approval controls and analyst tools supports early execution capacity, while the bank deployments remain a company claim about the prior product. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

### Countercase

Customer-specific connector work and recurring mapping exceptions could absorb the controller time Definite aims to save. A deterministic calculation can carry a misclassified record through to the result, leaving finance accountable for review. For Dynamics customers, Microsoft’s native finance agents offer an overlapping option. Together, these risks could leave Definite selling a more convenient way to do an existing job, with buyers able to switch once a rival reproduces the workflow. The memo contains neither a representative cost comparison nor observed substitution, so both Path B gates remain open and unproven. (Key Risks, Defensibility)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent operator that previously skipped weekly site-level margin analysis pay to repeat it with Definite after a side-by-side pilot? The pilot should show that Definite delivers controller-approved results below the buyer’s weekly spending threshold, including setup and exception handling, and that the buyer chooses Definite over an alternative with equivalent model quality and comparable output accuracy because it can preserve and replay the buyer’s rules and approvals. A paid repeat commitment on those terms would change the action to Reach out. A representative result showing that weekly analysis still costs too much or that the buyer readily switches would close Path B and change the action to Pass.
