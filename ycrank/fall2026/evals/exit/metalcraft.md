# Metalcraft -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition by a life-science model developer. Metalcraft’s public catalog shows six tasksets, sample cases, and a way to request full access. Metalcraft reports task-level model results. A buyer could value owning the catalog if it measurably improves model selection and would be costly to reproduce. That benefit is an inference. The memo leaves paid demand, workflow savings, and exclusive data rights unverified. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*)

The catalog and the founder’s computational biology and applied AI experience make a paid customer pilot plausible at seed. The recent pivot and lack of a disclosed revenue signal leave the funding and timeline for that milestone unclear. The memo provides no basis to assess data and IP transfer rights, holder eligibility, or an independent liquidity route. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Facts*, *Defensibility*)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale cases assume an eligible holder sells the full stake for cash within eight years and shares pro rata after net debt and closing adjustments, with no senior preference reducing the payout. The prices and adjustments are illustrative assumptions. The memo supplies no buyer economics or comparable transaction prices to anchor them. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

- **Modest sale.** $40 million in enterprise value with no net debt or closing adjustments leaves $40 million in distributable equity and pays the holder $1.2 million, or 2.4×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value less $10 million in net debt and closing adjustments leaves $90 million in distributable equity and pays the holder $2.7 million, or 5.4×. If those deductions reach $20 million, proceeds fall to $2.4 million, below the objective.
- **Prolonged private.** If Metalcraft keeps operating and reinvests available cash, the holder realizes $0 within eight years. The memo provides no evidence of a funded payout or an eligible share purchase. (*Key Facts*)

### Countercase

OpenAI and Benchling already build overlapping evaluations. A prospective buyer may be able to cover the workflows it values internally, especially if Metalcraft’s benchmark scores have limited predictive value in live research and its data can be reproduced. That weakens the case for paying an acquisition premium. The memo leaves open whether those substitutes cover the work a buyer would pay Metalcraft to own and provides no supported price ceiling, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Metalcraft show a life-science model developer enough value from owning its tasksets to justify a price that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? A paid pilot showing a repeatable improvement in live model-selection decisions, together with a buyer’s estimate of the cost and time to replicate that benefit internally, would test the case. Buyer economics that plausibly support the hurdle would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that the strongest credible buyer can readily replicate the benefit and would pay materially below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
