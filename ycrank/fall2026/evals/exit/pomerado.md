# Pomerado -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A browser automation or enterprise agent platform could buy Pomerado’s hosted service and maintained catalog of website integrations to shorten deployment on portals that lack APIs and reduce ongoing repair work. The memo records catalog growth from 46 tools across 23 sites to 584 tools across 447 sites. The founders report a 2.2% run error rate and say 82% of errors were repaired automatically. These are early operating signals. The milestone is repeat paid enterprise use with measured deployment and maintenance savings that an acquirer could capture through ownership. The reported $500,000 convertible note supplies a financing signal. Runway, burn, and the capital needed to reach the milestone are unknown. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

**Illustrative proceeds.** Investor terms are unavailable. Model a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3% and no follow-ons. The 5× gross cash target within eight years is $2.5 million. A simple pro rata allocation before investor taxes requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. At 3%, assuming $5 million of net debt and no other deductions, the enterprise-value hurdle is $88.3 million. At year eight, a $25 million enterprise-value sale yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), while a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). Neither price is supported by buyer economics in the memo. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

If growth plateaus and Pomerado spends eight years privately reinvesting its available cash, with no buyer interest and public scale out of reach, the holder realizes $0 and retains the shares. The memo shows no cash generation to support a sponsor buyout or company-funded payout, no eligible secondary demand, and no operating scale to support an IPO. That leaves the acquisition case without an evidenced fallback. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A potential buyer can use Pomerado’s MIT-licensed core, license the hosted service, or build a competing version. Skyvern has announced a similar approach to compiling and repairing workflows. The most plausible reason to buy is the value of the maintained catalog in paid production, and the memo has no retention or switching evidence to establish that value. Customers must also maintain portal authorization, and portal operators can change access policies. Those risks could erode what a buyer would pay for. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported sale price that leaves the holder materially short, so the Pass gate does not apply. (*Defensibility*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a credible strategic buyer justify paying about $88.3 million in the modeled 3% ownership case based on the incremental value of owning Pomerado’s hosted integrations? A buyer-validated comparison of ownership, licensing, and internal development, grounded in measured savings across repeat paid deployments, would move the action to Reach out if it supports a price that clears the holder’s target within eight years. Until then, Watch.
