# Gamgee -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The downside is one pre-seed check. The upside, as documented, is not yet 100x. One in four dogs develops cancer, but the entire global pet-cancer therapeutics market was only $400.9 million in 2024. Gamgee could materially improve treatment for individual animals without creating the societal or economic consequence I require. The dossier never quantifies how mutation-specific vaccines change survival, treatment cost, or the number of treatable dogs by an order of magnitude.

The product is more interesting than the market arithmetic. Gamgee sequences tumour and healthy DNA, ranks somatic mutations with canine-specific models, and manufactures a personalised mRNA construct. That is a distinct mechanism from Torigen’s whole-tumour-cell vaccine, ELIAS’s vaccine-plus-T-cell protocol, and FidoCure’s selection of existing drugs. But distinct is not discontinuous. The approximately 75% tumour reduction occurred in one dog that had already received surgery, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted drugs; cancer remained, and UNSW called the work citizen science rather than a clinical trial. There is no controlled evidence of 5–10x better response, speed, or cost.

The Australian trial is the correct next expenditure because clinical attribution is the largest kill risk. Yet Gamgee discloses no enrolment target, response threshold, budget, control arm, or stop condition. Its current workflow takes roughly four weeks. The proposed 24-hour turnaround and $2,000 price are targets without a bottom-up manufacturing model, so I cannot tell whether the product becomes globally affordable veterinary infrastructure or permanently remains bespoke medicine for wealthy owners.

The strongest bull case is the team’s ability to assemble capabilities it did not possess internally. Paul Conyngham brings 17 years of machine-learning experience, recruited an engineer from Meta, and coordinated the University of Queensland, UNSW Ramaciotti Centre for Genomics, and UNSW RNA Institute to produce a real treatment. Normal usage could create a valuable longitudinal dataset connecting mutations, vaccine designs, outcomes, and adverse events. Founders Fund and YC add credibility. This is meaningful pre-seed execution, but it does not invoke the exception logic: Gamgee has demonstrated neither a giant existing value pool nor an exponential technical curve large enough to override weak comparative evidence.

I pass today. In 36 months, the evidence presented points more toward a small veterinary clinical program than a scalable treatment platform: competitors already possess manufacturing or distribution networks, while Gamgee has one confounded case and no disclosed dataset scale. One result would flip me: a prospective study of at least 30 dogs showing a materially superior objective response rate against a named standard-of-care cohort while delivering each vaccine within 72 hours at an unsubsidized cost of $2,000 or less. That would convert an impressive scientific coordination exercise into an auditable technical and economic discontinuity.
