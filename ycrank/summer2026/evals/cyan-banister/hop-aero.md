# Hop Aero -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

$1.25 million from the U.S. Air Force makes Hop Aero a maximum-frontier, minimum-directly-observable company: Rook demands rocket-scale conviction, yet Hop Aero has only shown a tethered small-scale flight and engine hot-fire. I like that signature. Hop Aero is not a software tourist visiting defense; Hop Aero is six people attempting to put 250 kilograms through hypersonic re-entry and onto an unprepared surface. This is the hidden frontier-hardware sleeve of my portfolio, with one foot in love and the other checking whether the rocket actually lands.

SpaceX is the high rung: capital intensity and technology risk were the reason conventional investors stayed away, not defects to be optimized out. Hop Aero shares that load-bearing mechanism—a reusable, container-launched vehicle operating inside defense procurement, flight qualification, and launch regulation—but Hop Aero diverges where SpaceX had an integrated machine visibly progressing toward orbit. Hop Aero has a tethered prototype, one hot-fire, and no demonstrated 750-kilometer flight, hypersonic re-entry, or autonomous landing. Rook is magically weird, but today I can touch the engine more readily than the promised system.

DeepMind’s physical-verifiability failure was abstraction: a normal person could not install the research, watch it land, or photograph it working in the wild. Hop Aero is above that low rung because Hop Aero built an engine, fired it, and flew a small-scale vehicle at the Oklahoma spaceport. Hop Aero remains below the tactile immediacy of Niantic or Flock Safety because no soldier or offshore operator can yet place cargo in the 40-foot container and receive it 750 kilometers away. My unstated sourcing pattern—believing what appears in my physical environment—has only partial evidence here.

Jacob Balaj worked on Marine Corps MV-22 avionics, Falcon 9 and Heavy, LauncherOne, Masten lunar-lander integration, and SpinLaunch testing; Matija Milenovic sold propulsion IP, led a 20-plus-person supersonic-UAV group, and scaled Ukrainian loitering-munition production from 10 to more than 100 units monthly. Hop Aero compounds that lived experience over the next 18 months because propulsion, avionics, manufacturing, and military-user knowledge shorten each build-test loop together. Hop Aero passes my anti-pedigree test despite KTH and SpaceX logos: these founders have scars from the machinery, not merely credentials describing it. This is much closer to Anduril’s founder-product continuity than to founders discovering a market in a memo.

Inversion’s $54 million, Grid Aero’s $26 million, and Sierra Space’s $1.95 billion make rapid contested logistics less non-consensus than Hop Aero’s rocket suggests. Hop Aero would move to my highest magically-weird rung if credible aerospace investors were rejecting Hop Aero specifically because container-launched regional rockets looked aesthetically absurd—and the round remained priced as though those investors were right. Instead, the Air Force has formalized Point-to-Point Delivery while several capitalized competitors pursue adjacent architectures. Hop Aero is weird in implementation, not lonely in thesis; the missing public moat keeps the weirdness from becoming mispricing evidence.

HQ Trivia is the single-anchor warning on governance: a compelling artifact temporarily hid a co-founder tiebreaker problem until the disagreement became the company. Hop Aero names Matija Milenovic as CEO and Jacob Balaj as CTO, which is materially better than shared authority, but Hop Aero discloses neither co-founder history nor a vesting, deadlock, or exit-conflict protocol. The HQ Trivia exception does not rescue that omission because Hop Aero has no explosive consumer pull capable of overwhelming governance risk. “CEO” is a title; I still need to know who makes the irreversible call when the rocket and the contract demand opposite things.

Rook’s stated 250-kilogram payload and 750-kilometer range produce my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Hop Aero will have accumulated $10 million–$25 million in U.S. defense prototype awards and completed an untethered, payload-bearing flight, but Hop Aero will not yet have demonstrated the full 250-kilogram mission over 750 kilometers or exceeded $1 million in commercial ARR. Government qualification, environmental review, re-entry, and unprepared-surface landing are sequential gates, not parallel software features. Hop Aero can become valuable before becoming operationally complete because the Air Force pays to retire those gates.

Sierra Space’s $1.95 billion and Hop Aero’s absent public moat form the bear case that stings: Hop Aero may be a technically ambitious subcontractor trapped between better-capitalized vehicles, environmental opposition, concentrated procurement, and two founders whose tiebreaker is undocumented. Hop Aero could exhaust seed capital integrating five breakthroughs while the $1.25 million Air Force contract proves to be paid curiosity rather than procurement pull. The evidence that wins—for now—is Balaj’s launch-and-military integration history, Milenovic’s demonstrated defense-production scaling, a company-built hot-fire, and an actual government check. I invest, but one verifiable condition flips me to Pass: no untethered payload-bearing flight by July 2028.
