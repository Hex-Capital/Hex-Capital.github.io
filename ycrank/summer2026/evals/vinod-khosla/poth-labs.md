# Poth Labs -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence is too small as currently specified. Poth Labs addresses a real $4.38 billion customer-feedback software market, but the dossier does not quantify a resource bottleneck, population reached, or cost pool transformed by 10x—much less 100x. Searching fragmented support tickets, CRM records, surveys, and analytics is inefficient. Making that search easier is useful software, not yet a world-changing option.

The technical case is weaker. Enterpret already offers entity resolution, a customer context graph, cited answers, and more than 50 integrations. Poth adds adaptive interviews when evidence is missing, but supplies no benchmark for entity-resolution accuracy, research validity, answer quality, or analyst hours eliminated. Without a measured discontinuity, this is overlapping workflow orchestration around widely available models. The absence of a proprietary learning loop or defensibility signal makes commoditization the base case, not merely a risk.

The founders are the reason I hesitate before dismissing it. Matthew Wong worked on commercial data systems as a Palantir forward-deployed engineer; Mojmír Horváth brings data-science and multimodal-model experience. More importantly, they changed direction after working with Summer 2026 YC teams, moving from adaptive surveys toward cross-system customer intelligence. That is observable belief revision, and Inkle, Sol, Composio, UnCircuit, and Illume provide credible pre-seed usage evidence. A two-person team learning this quickly could find a sharper wedge.

The bull case is that execution-heavy enterprise software can grow from a modest tool into infrastructure. Poth’s cross-system mappings could accumulate switching costs, while adaptive research could convert static customer data into a continuously improving decision system. But the known exception requires a giant value pool or a platform-shaped expansion mechanism. A crowded $4.38 billion category, five unnamed usage relationships, and no revenue or retention evidence do not establish either. Strong founders cannot manufacture asymmetric upside from an incremental mechanism.

Capital allocation is also undisciplined in the evidence available. Entity-resolution errors and invalid automated research are correctly identified as kill risks, yet Poth publishes no decisive experiment, numerical threshold, cost, timing, or stop condition for either. The 280-fold decline in industry inference costs helps every competitor; it is not Poth’s unit-economic model. There is no pricing, gross-margin profile, utilization assumption, or demonstrated path to delivering research at a fraction of human or incumbent-software cost.

I pass. My 36-month base case is that Poth either pivots into a narrower enterprise customer-intelligence workflow or is absorbed as product talent before reaching $3 million ARR. I would flip only if a blinded, third-party test across real multi-system datasets showed at least a 10x reduction in analyst hours versus Enterpret, with equal or better entity-resolution accuracy and paid customer renewals. That would turn the graph from an implementation detail into a technical discontinuity.
