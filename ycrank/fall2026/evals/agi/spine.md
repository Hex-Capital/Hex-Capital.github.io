# Spine -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Spine offers early-stage startups a specialist-run HR back office inside their existing systems. Its specialists handle work founders and operations leads previously did themselves, including payroll exceptions, carrier coordination, and compliance filings. Spine says it charges employers $0 for administration and earns carrier commissions as broker of record. The service supplies no identified scarce input for AI deployment, so the building-blocks path scores zero. (*The Idea*)

The enabled-industry path also scores zero. Spine says AI drafts responses for specialist review. That could lower labor costs, but the memo leaves drafting time, review and rework costs, and cost per completed workflow unmeasured. It also leaves open whether any customer previously went without outsourced HR because it was unaffordable. The $0 employer price establishes the offer, while carrier commissions provide a plausible explanation for that price on their own. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

Spine reports adoption by 30+ startups, a meaningful seed-stage demand signal with renewal and repeat use still open. The launch and the founders’ prior experience handling these workflows support early execution credit. Broker-of-record status gives Spine a role in carrier selection and renewals, while competing HR support and brokerage offerings, reliance on third-party HR systems, and exposure to losing a broker appointment limit durable capture. Reported commission growth leaves contribution per added account unverified because the memo gives neither absolute commissions nor specialist delivery costs. These gaps limit confidence in the economics. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Spine could make outsourced HR viable for small startups that otherwise leave payroll exceptions and compliance work to founders. AI drafting could reduce specialist time enough for commissions to cover the full service. Even with equally capable models, customers would still need someone to coordinate carriers, resolve exceptions, and execute filings. Spine’s reported adoption and roughly 15% average reduction in customer healthcare costs support the appeal of the offer. Healthcare savings measure a different outcome from the cost of delivering HR administration. The evidence leaves the AI affordability threshold unverified, so both path scores remain zero and the Pass gate applies. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Does AI-assisted delivery bring the fully loaded cost of outsourced HR below what carrier commissions can support for startups that previously handled it in-house because outsourcing was too expensive? A representative cohort through renewal, compared with similar specialist-only accounts on comparable commission terms, could answer this by measuring cost per correctly completed workflow, including review, exceptions, and rework. Buyer confirmation that outsourcing was previously unaffordable, sustained use through renewal, and evidence that AI moved delivery from above to below the commission threshold would change the action to Reach out.
