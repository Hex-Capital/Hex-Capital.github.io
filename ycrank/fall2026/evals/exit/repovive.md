# Repovive -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an AI lab is the strongest supported route. The memo documents Repovive’s formal-verification launch and describes private Lean tasks, machine-checked reference proofs, and training environments. Repovive reports $100,000 in signed contracts across two formalization environments and a separate $200,000 letter of intent for broader coding data. The contracts suggest willingness to pay for access. A lab could have a reason to buy Repovive if owning a continually refreshed private task library materially reduced the cost of building and maintaining its own. The memo provides no measured buyer benefit or basis for an acquisition price. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The seed milestone is a paid renewal tied to a measured improvement in post-training performance or proof-authoring cost against the lab’s best alternative. The signed work gives Repovive a setting in which to test that result. The capital and time required to replenish the task library at the quality and volume a buyer would need remain unknown. The memo leaves asset transfer rights, holder participation, and independent liquidity routes unevidenced. (Market & Traction, Defensibility)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-ons. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, which requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at full pro rata participation. The sale prices and deductions below are scenario assumptions. Both sale cases assume an eligible holder sells its entire diluted stake for cash by year eight, with the remaining equity proceeds distributed pro rata and no preference diversion.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Holder cash |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40 million enterprise value less $5 million in assumed debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million in distributable equity | $1.05 million, or 2.1× |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value less $10 million in assumed debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million in distributable equity | $2.70 million, or 5.4×. If only half the stake can be sold, $1.35 million, or 2.7× |
| Prolonged private | The company reinvests available cash and there is no eligible share sale or distribution within eight years | $0 realized |

### Countercase

Labs could keep buying environments as needed or build them in-house. LeanDojo offers an open training environment, Reinforce Labs offers checked Lean proof data, and DeepMind’s research demonstrates internal verification capability. Those alternatives could limit both the incentive to acquire Repovive and the price a lab would pay. The memo leaves open whether they can match Repovive’s private task sets at the quality and refresh rate a lab needs. The evidence establishes neither a structural block nor a credible ceiling on holder proceeds, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a renewing lab’s measured advantage from Repovive over its best in-house or open alternative justify an acquisition price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A paid renewal paired with a lab-validated comparison and credible acquisition economics supporting at least $83.3 million in distributable equity under the benchmark would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that substitutes cap the strongest credible route materially below that hurdle would move it to Pass.
