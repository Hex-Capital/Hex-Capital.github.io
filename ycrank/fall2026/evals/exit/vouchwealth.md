# Vouch -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is Vouch’s strongest path in Fall 2026. An Indian wealth platform or mutual fund distributor could pay cash to acquire an active base of households whose approved investments generate recurring distribution commissions. The reason to buy would be to acquire those relationships and their earnings faster and at lower cost than building a comparable customer base. That ownership advantage is an inference. The memo supplies no buyer interest, measured retention, or evidence of the cost to replicate the business. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Facts*)

The memo documents a published AMFI distributor registration and reports a $500,000 pre-seed raise. Vouch says it has launched early access and signed up 27+ fund houses. Those signals support a pre-seed test of whether households keep using Vouch, approve investments, and generate recurring net contribution after onboarding and servicing costs. The memo gives no commission collections, burn, or runway figures to establish how quickly Vouch can reach that milestone. It also leaves open whether customer data access and fund-house arrangements would carry over in a sale. There is no separately supported route to cash for this holder. (*Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

**Proceeds bridge.** The reported raise supplies no terms for the check under evaluation, so the scenarios use the thesis’s illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation. Initial ownership of 5% falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. For both sale scenarios, assume no net debt, working-capital adjustments, or transaction fees, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. Assume simple pro rata allocation with no preference adjustment and all cash at close. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. At 3%, the holder needs about $83.3 million in distributable equity to receive $2.5 million gross before investor taxes within eight years. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% ownership and $62.5 million at 4%.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing and distributable equity | Gross holder cash at 3% |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | Year 5 at $40 million | $1.2 million, 2.4× total checks |
| Strong sale | Year 8 at $100 million | $3.0 million, 6.0× total checks. At 2% ownership, $2.0 million, 4.0×. |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. | $0, 0×. Shares remain held. |

The sale prices are scenario assumptions. The memo provides no usable valuation evidence to establish whether a buyer could justify a price above the hurdle. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

PowerUp Money already offers household fund aggregation, personalized rebalancing, and AI-assisted monitoring. Vouch also depends on external holdings records. A buyer could replicate much of the workflow and pay little for the software itself. Vouch says its adviser registration is pending, and client-level segregation limits the ability to collect both advisory fees and distribution commissions from the same relationship. That narrows the potential value of cross-selling. The memo still leaves open whether a retained, commission-generating customer base would be expensive enough to recreate to justify an acquisition. The ownership and price gaps warrant Watch. The evidence does not establish a structural price cap or make the prolonged-private scenario probable enough to trigger a Pass override. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a retained early-access cohort show enough recurring net contribution from customer-approved investments to make acquiring Vouch economically preferable to building an equivalent customer base? Cohort retention, commission collections, and servicing costs would answer this if they supported a buyer-specific case for clearing the $83.3 million distributable equity hurdle under plausible scaling assumptions. That would move the action to Reach out to test those assumptions and transferability. Until then, Watch.
