# TrueMile -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** TrueMile’s strongest seed case is that fleet-wide planning and automated broker calls could make recurring full-truckload service viable on thin lanes. The scenario assumes manual coordination and empty return miles make those lanes uneconomic today. TrueMile reports that a dispatcher can manage 9× as many trucks, while a person still approves every load and signs its rate confirmation. That is an early throughput signal. The memo leaves open how much human work goes into each completed shipment at comparable service quality and whether the savings open a new lane or frequency. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Under model parity, a shipper could still pay TrueMile to provide a truck, coordinate the trip, and take responsibility for delivery. TrueMile centrally dispatches leased-on owner-operators and says its service covers safety and compliance. That gives it some operating control. The durability of its access to trucks and shipper preference for its service remain unproven. TrueMile reports $57K of freight booked over three weeks. Completed shipments, collections, and repeat demand are unverified. The two dedicated-lane partnerships were solicitations. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Dhaliwal’s family-fleet experience and the solver he built support seed-stage execution. For scale, the relevant unit is the next recurring lane. TrueMile’s reported 10% increase in monthly miles per truck makes utilization worth testing. Lane contribution depends on collected freight charges after operator payments, empty-mile costs borne by TrueMile, inference, dispatch and exception labor, insurance, and compliance. The memo provides too little cost or lease-term detail to establish positive contribution. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

TrueMile may be improving dispatch for freight that existing carriers already serve profitably. Cancellations can unravel linked loads and add manual work. Existing providers already offer AI matching or dispatch, and a competing carrier with equivalent models and ample capital could recruit operators and reproduce the service. The memo identifies no structural barrier that would keep scarce lane capacity under TrueMile’s control, leaving Path A unsupported. Neither Path B condition clears the outreach gate, but the evidence leaves both open to a lane trial. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can TrueMile win repeat paid shipments at positive contribution on a lane that conventional dispatch could not serve economically at the same frequency and service level? A matched trial should log human time per completed shipment, including approvals, retries, and exceptions, and measure empty miles and lane contribution. Give the shipper a credible alternative with comparable AI dispatch and test whether it keeps paying TrueMile for dependable truck capacity and delivery. Evidence that both conditions hold would support Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 with at least medium confidence. Evidence that either condition fails would move the action to Pass.
