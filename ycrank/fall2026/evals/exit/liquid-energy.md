# Liquid Energy -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a managed-inference operator is the strongest supported route. NanoDC combines GPUs, fold-out solar panels, batteries, cooling, and satellite connectivity in a factory-built module. An operator such as Crusoe could have a reason to own the platform if it can bring reliable inference capacity online faster and at a lower delivered cost than available alternatives. The memo records the November launch. Liquid Energy claims four-week deployment, reports agreements with unnamed inference platforms, and reports 20 units on hand. The memo contains no confirmed go-live, measured customer benefit, or acquisition interest. Liquid Energy’s ownership of the hardware makes a fleet acquisition plausible, with IP rights, liens, and contract consents unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

**Proceeds bridge.** The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale scenarios assume an all-cash close within eight years, $10 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, no senior waterfall claims, and an eligible holder selling its entire stake at closing.

| Scenario | Distributable equity | Cash to holder |
|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale at an assumed $40 million enterprise value | $30 million | $900,000 (1.8×) |
| Strong sale at an assumed $100 million enterprise value | $90 million | $2.7 million (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private operation with cash reinvested and no share purchase | No distribution | $0 (0×) |

These sale prices are sensitivities. At an illustrative 5× annual revenue, the strong case would require $20 million in annual revenue, equivalent to roughly 13,300 revenue-generating units at the company’s modeled $1,500 per unit per year. Realized rates, utilization, and the capital needed to deploy and operate that fleet remain unverified. The strong case clears the objective by $200,000, so additional debt, senior claims, or a partial sale could erase the cushion. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Key Facts, The Idea, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

An inference operator could buy capacity or deploy competing modules without acquiring Liquid Energy. Greenference markets solar-and-battery inference containers, and Armada already manufactures deployable GPU modules. Liquid Energy’s proposed advantage depends on sustained off-grid throughput through nighttime battery operation and satellite-link interruptions. The reported inventory gives the company a starting point for a field test, while the cost of scaling remains open. These risks leave the buyer’s reason to acquire and the likely holder payout unresolved. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported price ceiling that would trigger Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid inference-platform deployment demonstrate a durable cost and time-to-capacity advantage over the customer’s next-best option? A 90-day operating record covering delivered tokens, overnight uptime, fully loaded cost, and a paid repeat order would test that advantage. Move to Reach out if the measured economics and capital required to scale support a credible strategic sale above the $83.3 million distributable-equity hurdle. Otherwise, remain on Watch.
