# ProvenMetal -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is too small as presently constructed. ProvenMetal addresses a real $16.12 billion North American PCB market, but changing an overseas wait of “weeks” into a five-day managed order does not rewrite electronics manufacturing economics. The dossier quantifies neither the affected engineering cost pool nor the value of faster iteration, and it identifies no mechanism for a 10–100x consequence. This is procurement improvement, not yet infrastructure transformation.

The technical comparison is worse. Network PCB, the company’s own assembly partner, advertises three-day turnkey PCBA and accepts direct RFQs. Sierra Circuits advertises one-day fabrication. ProvenMetal’s one-minute KiCad sourcing check is useful workflow integration, but there is no measured improvement in cost, yield, inspection accuracy, or throughput. Managing suppliers, inspection records, and component procurement may produce a good service business; it is not a technical discontinuity.

The serious objection to my pass is the **Block** exception: a four-person, adaptable team can enter a large transaction pool through a narrow wedge and expand into infrastructure. Doyle and Carkner have already built hardware together, achieved a reported 95% reduction in phantom energy during Syncra’s 17-day pilot, abandoned that product, conducted more than 30 industry conversations, and obtained five LOIs for an early ProvenMetal concept. The KiCad plugin could become distribution, and ordinary orders could eventually generate proprietary supplier, revision, and inspection data. But the material difference is control. ProvenMetal currently outsources the core operation to a supplier offering an overlapping, faster direct service, while the five LOIs cannot be tied to the current turnkey configuration. Adaptability is affirmative evidence; platform ownership is still hypothetical.

Capital is not yet being spent against a clearly ranked kill risk. The existential question is whether customers repeatedly choose ProvenMetal rather than ordering directly from Network PCB, MacroFab, or Sierra—and whether that choice survives beyond founder-led program management. The dossier gives no retention experiment, pass/fail threshold, acquisition cost, order-level margin, or evidence that inspection data compounds. High-reliability certifications are available through selected partner routes, but ProvenMetal has not shown that these permissions belong to its own durable system.

The unstated pattern that could rescue the company is a scarce manufacturing workflow becoming a repeatable production platform. The current economics do not establish that pattern. Prices and gross margins are undisclosed, fabrication may remain overseas, and the faster-turn claims trail named competitors. My 36-month base case is that ProvenMetal pivots from turnkey PCBA brokerage into defense-electronics inspection and traceability software rather than exceeding $3 million ARR in board manufacturing.

I pass with my own money. One verifiable condition would flip the decision: ten named repeat customers demonstrating at least a 5x reduction in design-to-inspected-board time at equal or lower landed cost than buying directly from Network PCB. That would convert workflow convenience into measurable technical leverage and show that the partner cannot simply disintermediate the company.
