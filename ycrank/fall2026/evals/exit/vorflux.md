# Vorflux -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Vorflux’s strongest path. An enterprise developer-tool vendor could buy its recurring Slack roles, approval routing, and repository-specific test harnesses to bring governed coding workflows to its installed customers faster. The memo records the AutoBots launch and Cursor’s acquisition, which establishes that a strategic transaction occurred in the category. Customer testimonials describe a feature shipped from Slack and fixes across six repositories. Those accounts show how teams have used the platform. The buyer benefit remains an inference because the memo gives no comparative savings, retention data, or indication of what a buyer would pay. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A proposed seed-stage milestone is three paying enterprise deployments that renew after two quarters and show lower engineering time per accepted change against their prior workflow, with comparable rework rates. The announced $15 million seed and the launch make that test plausible. Runway, burn, and repeatability across customers remain open. Customer-hosted AWS deployments and customer-supplied model keys suggest a buyer could acquire the orchestration software while customers retain their infrastructure and credentials. IP ownership, contract assignment, and change-of-control rights remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The memo gives no investor terms or usable sale-price range. Under the illustrative eight-year scenario, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. A $25 million enterprise-value sale with assumed $5 million net debt returns **$600,000 gross (1.2×)**. A $100 million sale on the same terms returns **$2.85 million gross (5.7×)**. Both assume pro rata distribution before taxes and no additional fees or senior claims. The $2.5 million cash target requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, or $88.3 million of enterprise value at the assumed debt. At 2% and 4% ownership, the enterprise-value hurdles are $130 million and $67.5 million, respectively. The reported Cursor acquisition supplies no usable price benchmark for Vorflux. (Market & Traction)

If growth plateaus, buyer interest fails to materialize, public scale stays out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes **$0 over eight years** and retains the shares. The memo gives no evidence of cash generation to finance a sponsor buyout or company-funded payout, demand for an eligible secondary, or Vorflux’s readiness for an IPO. Any wind-down recovery would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Devin already handles event-triggered work, and Cursor already returns browser test evidence. Those capabilities give potential buyers credible ways to cover parts of Vorflux’s workflow by extending their own products or integrating with a partner. Vorflux’s reported Codex integration illustrates the partnership option. Without evidence that its configured workflows produce durable savings or retention, a buyer has little demonstrated reason to pay an acquisition premium. That gap supports Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural block nor a credible sale price that would leave the holder materially short. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would an enterprise developer-tool vendor pay enough to clear the holder’s $2.5 million cash target once Vorflux meets the proposed deployment milestone? A buyer-backed indicative price range tied to measured deployment gains and a quantified advantage over building or partnering would answer it. A credible range that clears the roughly $88.3 million enterprise-value hurdle under the modeled terms, adjusted for actual ownership and senior claims, would move the action to Reach out.
