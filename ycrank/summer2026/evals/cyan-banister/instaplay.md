# Instaplay -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032 and I can see personalized games becoming disposable social objects: someone describes an inside joke, an agent builds it, and a group chat plays it minutes later. Instaplay has one real leak from that future—20,000 creators have already turned prompts into browser games shared through Discord and group chats. But the dossier does not map the intervening transitions from generation to durable social behavior. It gives me the first artifact, not yet the six-year chain.

The uncomfortable number is not zero revenue. It is median engaged play of 3.0–7.9 seconds. Creation has become easier, but Instaplay has not shown that the resulting games hold attention or cause creators to return. The 100,000 monthly users and 3,000 blinded FunBench votes prove that people will touch the artifact. They do not yet prove the costly or weird behavior I want: repeatedly building games, inviting friends, maintaining worlds, or forming rituals around them.

The bull case stings because this is not a waitlist attached to an AI slogan. The team shipped a functioning multiplayer product, attracted meaningful organic usage, and built FunBench from real player judgments. Gary Wu previously founded an LLM-evaluation company, Frank Liu brings Instagram engineering experience and top-1,000 Teamfight Tactics fluency, and Akhil Aggarwal adds globally deployed AWS security work. Their combined experience plausibly explains both the evaluation harness and the social-product ambition. What is missing is evidence that games were a pre-company obsession shared by the team rather than a well-executed move into a fashionable category.

Instaplay returns creative agency by removing coding, asset production, and engine setup, but it has not identified a rent-producing rule or gatekeeper. More importantly, it currently sits on top of the model wave rather than beneath it. Delivered games cost $0.86–$7.11 in model spend while the product is free to start, and no proprietary model advantage or paid loop is demonstrated. Prompt and engagement telemetry could become valuable, but “could” is doing the work. Astrocade already reports 20 million users, Verse8 reports 3.5 million monthly users, and general coding agents keep compressing the same creation cost.

The social layer also creates two gates I cannot wave away. Publishing, profiles, comments, and multiplayer rooms exist without detailed indexed governance policies, leaving consent, custody, and moderation architecture unclear. The dossier identifies Frank as CTO but does not identify the ultimately accountable CEO or explain decision control among three co-founders. Neither fact proves misconduct or deadlock; both require resolution before my money moves.

My base case is that by August 2029 Instaplay has pivoted toward B2B game-generation or evaluation tooling and remains below $3 million ARR, because its strongest asset is presently the creation-and-ranking machinery rather than sustained play. I am Neutral, not dismissive: the product has arrived before its economics and ritual have. One result would flip me to Invest—a dated cohort showing that at least 25% of first-time creators return and publish another game 30 days later without paid acquisition.
