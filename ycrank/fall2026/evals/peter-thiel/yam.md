# Yam -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Yam’s interesting hypothesis is that women who consume fictional relationships will spend substantially more time participating in them. The reported three hours per daily active user makes that hypothesis worth examining. But what prevailing belief does it overturn? The dossier identifies neither a named skeptic nor a test distinguishing Yam’s insight from the established demand for character roleplay. A comparison showing that interactive relationships consistently displace conventional dating sims could establish something important. Today, the engagement claim has no measurement period or denominator. There may be a secret here; it has not yet been stated precisely enough to test.

The more decisive problem is ownership. Women-focused romance supplies an intelligible initial audience, but Character.AI already offers character interaction and Dorian offers creator-authored games. Yam combines capabilities buyers can obtain elsewhere, without documented exclusive content, proprietary technology, or a measured network effect. This is affirmative competitive overlap, beyond the ordinary absence of a pre-seed moat. If another platform can offer the same characters and scenarios, why should the value accumulate to Yam? Popular fictional characters may attract players while leaving control of the attraction with someone else.

The strongest objection to passing is that Yam may already be discovering distribution through play. Its leading games display 1.8 million and 516,400 plays; creators publish shareable links, and referrals reward activated friends. Those are concrete product mechanisms, although plays cannot establish unique audience size or acquisition efficiency. My revealed willingness to back consumer networks matters here: romance is no less investable because it looks less consequential than industrial technology. The bull case is that creators bring audiences, audiences attract more creators, and the resulting network becomes difficult to reproduce. The dossier establishes the publishing machinery and visible activity, but not that reinforcing loop.

The founders make that bull case more credible. Yu Qin’s work on TikTok creation tools and the reported growth of Effect House’s creator community to 800,000 are relevant operating experience. Shan Zhong reports having grown a previous consumer fintech to 100,000 users and 2 million ARR; Crystal Shan brings product-design experience from Webex and Amesite. These are substantive backgrounds, with the prior-company metrics kept separate from Yam’s results. They do not establish the founding structure I require: prior work together, full-time commitment, ownership, and control of core engineering remain undocumented. I assign the stated missing-evidence prior rather than inventing either alignment or a founder red flag.

Yam has a coherent potential revenue engine: recurring consumer spending on interactive romance, expressed through subscriptions and currency purchases. At $9.99 per month, one million full-price subscribers would produce approximately $120 million in annual subscription revenue before costs. That arithmetic describes the required scale; it does not establish a route to it. Paid conversion, renewal, and inference costs are unknown. Three hours of daily use could produce exceptional attachment or expensive consumption. The dossier’s report that Character.AI entered Chapter 11 in October 2026 makes the distinction concrete, without proving that Yam shares its economics.

I pass with my own money today. No disclosed founder behavior triggers my organizational red flags, but the competitive gate remains adverse. My exceptions for a demonstrated technical breakthrough or persistent transactions under legal pressure do not resolve it: Yam shows neither an independent technical discontinuity nor disclosed paid behavior sufficient to override the ownership problem. Character-rights exposure also means popular fan content cannot simply be counted as proprietary supply. I would reopen this pass after one verifiable result: a controlled cohort study showing that Yam-exclusive creator content materially improves 90-day paid retention, at positive contribution margin, over otherwise comparable nonexclusive content. That would begin to demonstrate that the platform keeps value its creators generate.
