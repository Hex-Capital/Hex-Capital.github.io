# Keres -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Keres could change the economics of defending against repeated drone attacks if destroying the target stops consuming the interceptor. That is a consequential proposition: preserve aircraft, preserve inventory, and keep defending after the first wave. The dossier identifies a $12.6 billion counter-UAS spending pool in 2026, but that includes much more than reusable interceptors. It supplies neither today’s relevant engagement-cost baseline nor the improvement Garmur can deliver. I see the consequence mechanism. I cannot yet quantify the consequence.

The important distinction is recovery **after a kill**. Anduril’s Roadrunner-M can return when an engagement does not require destruction; Keres proposes bringing the jet home after destroying its target. That could be a genuine capability discontinuity. But the [public description](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/keres) does not establish the destruction mechanism, a completed interception-and-recovery sequence, or a measured advantage. Fortem already demonstrates that recoverable aircraft can address some drone threats through capture. Keres must establish which target envelope it can serve economically that those alternatives cannot. A proposed capability earns a technical question, not a technical benchmark.

The strongest argument against passing is Christopher Palazzolo. More than four years spanning maintenance, avionics, flight testing, and XB-1 production at Boom, followed by operations leadership at H3X, puts relevant aircraft-building experience inside this three-person team. Coupled with DIU’s demonstrated interest in counter-UAS alternatives, that makes this a credible physical-systems bet worth investigating. My actual investing pattern accommodates manufacturing, qualification, and long development timelines. But the dossier documents neither belief revision nor complementary recruiting against specific weaknesses. The exceptional-founder override remains possible; relevant employment alone does not establish it. There is also no documented extraordinary commitment or quantified consequence sufficient to override the missing technical evidence today.

The economic kill risk is whether post-engagement reuse survives inspection, damage, turnaround, and aircraft losses. Returning once is insufficient if refurbishment consumes the savings or aircraft availability collapses between attacks. Keres discloses no operating-cost model, service life, or costed experiment with a pass/fail threshold. Those omissions receive the rubric’s missing-evidence defaults; they do not prove bad economics or undisciplined spending. Likewise, the October 12 media profile establishes visibility, not customer validation. I see no evidence of premature scaling, permanent subsidy dependence, or exit-first behavior. A proprietary flight-data learning loop is plausible, but remains an inference.

I pass with my own money today. Keres has a serious problem and relevant engineering experience, but the investment case currently asks me to assume both reusable lethality and favorable lifecycle economics. I would accept considerable uncertainty around procurement after those assumptions became evidence. My single flip condition is an independently observed repeat-engagement trial demonstrating successful destruction and recovery at a fully burdened cost per successful intercept at least 10x below Roadrunner-M against a matched target class, including losses, maintenance, and turnaround. That would turn the central promise into an option I could underwrite.
