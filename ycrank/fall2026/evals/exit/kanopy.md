# Kanopy -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a grid software vendor is Kanopy’s strongest private-company path. ACRT’s Arborcision launch shows that Kanopy’s video-derived clearance measurements can be integrated into a vegetation-management service. Schneider Electric’s announced AiDASH acquisition shows strategic interest in overlapping software. A grid software buyer could gain control of the reconstruction technology and integrate it across its utility customers. That ownership case depends on field-validated savings from fewer repeat inspections and on how much of that benefit the buyer could already capture through a license. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Kanopy reports 3,093 cumulative miles mapped. Six utilities and contractors reportedly signed LOIs or master service agreements covering 270,000 miles of potential scope. Committed order volumes and revenue are undisclosed. A paid, repeat deployment with clearances validated against field measurements is a plausible next milestone. Disclosed funding figures vary, and the memo leaves the cost, runway, and time needed to reach that milestone open. IP assignments and transfer rights for relevant customer contracts and data are unverified. There is no separately supported fallback that could pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts, Defensibility)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** With investment terms undisclosed, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The 5× cash objective requires $2.5 million within eight years, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. For both sale scenarios, assume zero net debt and zero other price adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. Assume simple pro rata allocation with no preference deductions and all consideration paid in cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. The memo supplies no usable transaction price or buyer economics to substantiate either sale value. (Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

ACRT already gets Kanopy’s analysis through a partnership, and Kanopy’s hosted SDK gives customers a way to integrate it into their own workflows. If licensing delivers the same savings, a buyer has little reason to pay for control. Competing inspection software and continued field checks could further limit the savings a buyer can capture. The memo supports waiting for evidence of an ownership advantage. It does not establish a binding price ceiling or make prolonged private ownership probable enough to trigger a Pass override. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid utility deployment that renews show field-validated savings large enough for a grid software buyer to justify an acquisition near the ownership-adjusted proceeds hurdle? Move to Reach out if measured savings and a credible comparison of buying, building, and licensing support that price. Keep watching if the evidence establishes only the value of using the product.
