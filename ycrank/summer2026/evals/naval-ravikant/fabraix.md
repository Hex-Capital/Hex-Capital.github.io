# Fabraix -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Ahmed and Ibrahim have specific knowledge earned through action. Ahmed ran international-payments fraud systems handling more than $1 billion annually; Ibrahim built production-debugging agents at Meta and compiler and database systems at TradingHub. Those credentials matter because they culminated in Nyx: a working attacker that reports a 78% AgentHarm success rate, versus 67% for GPT-5.6 Sol, and typically finds its first exploit within an hour. The benchmark-to-production transfer remains unproven, but this is shipped technical work in the exact neighborhood of the problem, not résumé theater.

The decisive counterfactual is simple: copy Nyx’s code tomorrow. Fabraix still has a library of more than 10,000 attack strategies and a self-learning attack loop, which are the beginnings of a proprietary asset. But the dossier does not show that the strategies are exclusive, that deployments generate unavailable regression data, or that attack performance improves as the corpus grows. Virtue AI, HiddenLayer, SPLX, Garak, and PyRIT already automate overlapping work. The 78% benchmark demonstrates execution; it does not yet demonstrate durability.

The strongest bull case is hidden in the Playground. More than 50,000 attempts suggest that outsiders are willing to attack agents for sport, and an open adversarial network could turn each participant’s ingenuity into better protection for every customer. That would be permissionless leverage. Today, however, the evidence stops at participation: 71 GitHub stars, 50,000 attempts, and no measured contribution, referral, or improvement loop. The prototype-before-network exception is real, especially for a two-person team, but it does not override a crowded category unless the prototype is already creating a scarce asset.

The market is broad enough to matter but not yet mapped tightly enough to support the option. Generative-AI cybersecurity is projected to grow from $8.65 billion in 2025 to $35.50 billion in 2031, and continuous retesting becomes necessary as agents acquire tools, permissions, files, and changing models. Yet no Fabraix-specific SAM is established, while Check Point and Zscaler have already acquired overlapping products and can bundle testing into existing security distribution. A large parent market does not prove that standalone red-teaming captures the transaction layer.

The economic evidence is thinner still. Fabraix offers custom-priced scans and monthly usage pricing, but names no payer, price, completed contract, recurring revenue, retention, or unit economics. Finding vulnerabilities at dozens of Fortune 500 companies is technically useful; it is not evidence that those companies bought anything. My 36-month base case is that the team shifts from standalone scans toward continuous agent-security monitoring and reaches roughly $2 million to $8 million in ARR, but remains vulnerable to bundling by a larger security platform.

There is no disclosed integrity, artificial-deadline, or founder-stability red flag; the problem is structural, not personal. I would not invest my own money today because excellent attackers have not yet become a compounding business. One verifiable condition flips the decision: three named enterprises renew continuous CI testing and Fabraix demonstrates that their deployments add proprietary attack data that measurably improves exploit discovery for subsequent customers.
