# Pomerado -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Pomerado turns a task on a website into a reusable tool and offers hosted execution with automated repair. The strongest market-opening scenario is a small service business that skips some recurring client follow-ups because manual portal work takes too much staff time and a custom integration costs too much to build and maintain. The founders report that a personal trainer uses Pomerado to automate workouts and client messages. Whether the trainer previously skipped those tasks, pays for the product, or uses it repeatedly remains unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

In that scenario, compare manual labor or the cost of building and maintaining a custom integration with Pomerado’s full cost per correctly completed workflow. Reuse spreads setup across successful runs, while hosting, retries, human review, exceptions, and compliance still affect delivery cost. The company’s benchmark used prebuilt integrations and one attempt per task, leaving setup and ongoing maintenance economics unresolved. The founders separately report a 2.2% run error rate and automatic resolution of 82% of errors. Those figures leave the cost of handling the remaining failures unknown, so contribution at the listed $50 monthly Premium price remains unverified. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The public catalog grew from 46 read-only tools across 23 sites to 584 tools across 447 sites, showing that the team can ship integrations. Manglik’s prior enterprise-agent work is relevant to the next milestone of keeping authenticated workflows running through portal changes. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Pomerado could make existing automation cheaper while opening few new workflows. Even if it opens a market, customers might get the same outcome from a rival once models are widely available. Pomerado’s core is MIT-licensed, Skyvern has announced similar script reuse and repair, and portal operators retain control over access. Maintaining configured workflows could give buyers a reason to stay, but the memo records no retention, switching, or willingness-to-pay evidence under functional parity. The competing approach is concrete, while actual substitution remains untested. Both Path B gates remain open, and the memo provides no independent Path A case for scarce capacity Pomerado controls. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent small service businesses repeatedly pay Pomerado a price that covers delivery for recurring portal work they previously skipped, when a functionally equivalent alternative is available? A paid field trial should document the prior economics, measure all-in cost per successful outcome through ordinary failures and repairs, and give buyers a choice at renewal. Repeat payment for newly viable work and a demonstrated reason to choose Pomerado under parity would move the action to Reach out. A representative trial showing that the work remains uneconomic or that buyers readily switch while preserving the outcome would close Path B and move the action to Pass.
