# Context.dev -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Yahia Bakour previously built two acquired software products, and StockAlarm.io reached 225,000 users and 100,000 daily notifications. Context.dev is therefore a proven-shipping founder attached to a weakly differentiated infrastructure position: founder execution is Context.dev’s highest dimension, while index-like distribution is its lowest. The 5,000 developers and 280 customer companies show that Bakour can get software into users’ hands; Firecrawl’s 48,000 GitHub stars and Browserbase’s $67.5 million in funding show that Context.dev does not yet own the workflow those users must touch.

**Perplexity** is the high rung for founder iteration: Aravind Srinivas repeatedly returned a week later with the discussed feature built. Context.dev shares the load-bearing mechanism because Bakour shipped Essense.io, StockAlarm.io, and web-scale extraction infrastructure before launching Context.dev; these are operating artifacts, not ex-Amazon credential theater. Context.dev diverges from Perplexity because the dossier provides no evidence of week-to-week product iteration or a customer-feedback cadence, so I place Bakour below the Perplexity rung despite unusually strong pre-seed shipping history.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism for index position: being number two in infrastructure governed by scale or network effects can leave respectable usage with structurally inferior economics. Context.dev enters behind Firecrawl, Tavily, and Browserbase, and Context.dev has no documented proprietary coverage, retention advantage, historical corpus, or distribution lock. Combining extraction, screenshots, brand intelligence, and transaction identification is useful packaging, but packaging does not make Context.dev an index on agentic web activity; absent a must-touch data layer, Context.dev must keep winning share feature by feature.

Agentic web traffic reportedly grew nearly 8,000% during 2025, creating a real recent catalyst for Context.dev: production agents now need current, machine-readable information rather than model-cutoff knowledge. Over 18 months, more deployed agents should create more retrieval calls, more monitored domains, and higher consumption revenue for Context.dev, which is the right compounding mechanism. The discount is that Context.dev’s “why now” is inferred, competitors raised substantial rounds during 2025, and web scraping itself is forecast to grow only 17.39% annually; Context.dev is skating for the puck, but not alone and not especially early.

Context.dev reports 5,000 developers, 280 customer companies, and 2,000 monitor runs in 24 hours, yet Context.dev discloses no ARR, paid-customer count, retention, or month-over-month curve. That is the red flag in an AI infrastructure deal: visible usage can be “just working,” but Product Hunt’s 1,029 upvotes and a free tier can also manufacture a false signal. The score flips into the Harvey range if Context.dev shows more than 20% monthly paid-revenue growth, expanding cohorts, and named customers such as Klarna moving onto the $499 Scale or enterprise plans; without that evidence, I will not translate developer registrations into revenue velocity.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow infrastructure on top of a hard capability already supplied by others is often better than funding the hard capability itself. Context.dev does not need new model science, new browser physics, or regulatory approval; Context.dev assembles validated crawling, extraction, and language-model capabilities behind one API. The single miracle is becoming the default context layer, while robots.txt changes, quarterly cache refreshes, and 10–30-second first-domain latency are serious operating dependencies rather than independent scientific miracles. Bakour as a solo founder also avoids the equal-equity and unclear-decision-maker red flag.

Context.dev’s 280-company footprint leads me to forecast that Context.dev reaches $8–15 million ARR and is acquired by Cloudflare within 36 months, rather than becoming the independent “Stripe for web context.” Context.dev’s founder-shipping strength should produce a valuable developer product quickly, while Context.dev’s weak index position makes distribution, global crawling infrastructure, and compliance more valuable inside Cloudflare’s network than as a standalone moat. That outcome follows directly from the signature: strong product execution, limited structural ownership.

Firecrawl’s 48,000-star repository is the bear case that stings: open-source extraction commoditizes Context.dev’s core, Tavily owns agent search, Browserbase owns hosted browsing, target websites can revoke access, and Context.dev could die between three better-capitalized layers without ever converting free developers. Context.dev’s counterevidence is unusually strong for pre-seed—5,000 developers, 280 companies, two prior founder exits, and a unified API spanning extraction through transaction data—so the Color exception applies: workflow on top of hard infrastructure can compound without owning the underlying technology. I will invest, but if fewer than 25% of the reported 280 companies are paying or 90-day gross retention is below 70%, I flip Context.dev to Pass because the apparent curve is distribution noise rather than buying behavior.
