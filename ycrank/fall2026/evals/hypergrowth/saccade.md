# Saccade -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with people seeking assessment before memory symptoms, Saccade could expand by distributing the same headset exam through longevity clinics. The memo documents a working headset design, and the company solicits U.S. clinic referrals and accepts appointment inquiries. The exam is the most portable part of Acuity. The full service needs local capacity for in-person testing, blood draws, and physician consultation. Referrals give Saccade a repeatable acquisition channel, with staff involvement required to deliver first value. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The company lists $897 per test on a quarterly billing schedule. Follow-up testing gives customers a reason to renew, while the fixed bundle offers little evidence that retained customers will spend more. The company reports testing at San Francisco clinics and describes Research participation as free. Paid Acuity use and revenue remain unknown, so initial traction takes the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

At the listed price, contribution per paid test would be $897 less lab processing, blood collection, physician time, headset capacity, scheduling, and attributable acquisition and visit costs. Partner clinics could improve contribution by using existing staff and equipment capacity, an operating assumption the memo leaves untested. Labor and lab costs recur with each test, and the memo provides too little cost data to establish positive contribution. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Even with strong local fit, Acuity could grow only as fast as Saccade can add clinical capacity market by market. The company describes its assessment as investigational and lacking validation for individual risk prediction. The treatment criteria cited in the memo also limit the immediate clinical value of an asymptomatic result, which could weaken quarterly renewals. The structural downside gate stays open because a partner clinic could plausibly deliver the same protocol using its existing staff and lab relationships. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a partner clinic in a second country deliver Acuity at the listed price through a paid first visit and quarterly retest using the same protocol and its existing staff and lab relationships? A completed rollout with positive contribution after acquisition and delivery costs would warrant rescoring for Reach out. Evidence that each new market requires bespoke implementation or that partner delivery remains uneconomic would change the action to Pass.
