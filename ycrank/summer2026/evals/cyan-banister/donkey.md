# Donkey -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Benjamin Martindale speaks Mandarin, advised Sany and Sinoboom, and drove from Vermont to San Francisco visiting wholesale distributors. Donkey is an ugly, regulated, balance-sheet-heavy trading operation built by founders who have touched Chinese factories, but Donkey’s weakest point is the unanswered question of who breaks a founder deadlock. That high-capital-intensity, low-governance pairing is the signature: Donkey looks less like an AI wrapper and more like a physical supply-chain machine whose operational stress may reveal its organizational fault line.

**Crusoe** is the high rung because ugly industrial operations, commodity exposure, and capital intensity created an accumulating advantage that software tourists could not easily copy. Donkey shares that load-bearing mechanism: Donkey takes title, becomes importer of record, inspects goods before final payment, clears customs, and offers qualified buyers net-45 terms. Donkey diverges because the capital currently finances inventory and credit exposure rather than proprietary energy infrastructure; no structural market barrier or independently verified order-level data moat exists yet. Still, first-sale customs documentation and seven-province inspection coverage make Donkey the kind of unsexy regulated swing other investors may misunderstand.

**HQ Trivia** is the low governance rung because explosive product love temporarily concealed an unresolved co-founder fault line until the fault line consumed the product. Donkey names Martindale as founder and Tan as co-founder and CTO, which suggests functional separation, but Donkey discloses no operating agreement, equity split, or explicit tiebreaker. The HQ Trivia exception does not rescue Donkey: there is no current-product revenue or viral usage powerful enough to override this red flag. Donkey therefore sits above HQ Trivia but below a founder-led operation with documented final authority.

**Prelude OS’s 31 paying Chinese factory customers in 39 days** make Martindale’s founder-market fit more than biography wallpaper. Over the next 18 months, Donkey can compound the founders’ Mandarin factory relationships, Tan’s customs-filings parser, and Martindale’s willingness to visit distributors in person into proprietary supplier-reliability and landed-cost data. Donkey passes my unstated environmental-sourcing test: the founders previously operated among the factories and are now physically hunting buyers. The missing piece is a specific autobiographical moment when Martindale personally suffered the importer problem, so Donkey is closer to the lived-experience high rung than the market-analysis middle, but not sitting on top of it.

**ImportYeti’s 70 million bills of lading and two million users** show that supplier discovery alone is neither weird nor scarce. Donkey becomes magically weird by converting a product photo into a 72-hour delivered-duty-paid quote and then putting its own balance sheet behind the answer. Donkey would flip into my top non-consensus range if credible investors passed specifically because China concentration, first-sale valuation, or importer-of-record exposure looked aesthetically or regulatorily unacceptable. Without evidence about competing investors or round price, Donkey is plausibly underappreciated, not demonstrably underpriced.

**Flock Safety** is the single-anchor parallel for physical verifiability: an abstract data layer becomes investable when it produces an artifact observable in the world. Donkey’s artifact is a reordered product moving from an identified factory, through inspection and customs, to an importer’s dock; Donkey can be judged by the delivered carton, defect rate, invoice, and arrival date. That is tactile in a way Jiga’s matching workflow is not. Donkey falls short of Flock’s immediacy because a normal person cannot experience the full product within 48 hours, but an importer can verify every important claim through one completed shipment.

**Donkey’s claimed 15–30% trading-company markup opportunity** produces my falsifiable forecast: within 36 months, Flexport will acquire Donkey for less than $75 million after Donkey reaches $20–40 million in annualized merchandise volume but cannot independently finance the importer-of-record balance sheet at venture scale. Donkey’s physical execution and regulatory expertise should create real buyer value, while net-45 credit, title ownership, and China policy concentration pull Donkey toward a larger logistics balance sheet.

**ImportYeti’s scale, Sourcify’s 3,000-plus factories, and Jiga’s $12 million Series A** make the strongest bear case a commodity-margin trap: Donkey has no disclosed current orders, its photo-based supplier checks are explicitly not guarantees, and competitors can imitate the software while lenders capture the economics. Donkey survives that objection because Prelude already demonstrated rapid paid adoption, Donkey controls execution rather than merely exposing data, and factory-floor inspection plus importer-of-record responsibility gives buyers one accountable US counterparty. I invest with a smaller check because governance is a hard no versus a rebuttal issue: a verifiable 50/50 founder agreement lacking any deadlock mechanism would flip my verdict to Pass.
