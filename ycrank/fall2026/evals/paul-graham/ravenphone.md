# Raven -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Isa Usmanov has built a phone prototype that does things. The organizer-confirmed demonstration includes flight search, generated interfaces, and calendar actions. That is Raven’s strongest evidence. But the founder question remains unanswered: what broke, what did Isa do about it, and how quickly did the situation change? The dossier contains an artifact without the obstacle-and-response history that would make me confident about backing its builder.

His previous businesses make that question worth pursuing. Droov reportedly reached $3.4M in GMV and 32,000 users; an unnamed education SaaS reached $1.1M ARR; Reinio was sold to a laundromat chain. These are concrete accomplishments, although the figures come from his biography. They suggest someone who has dealt with customers and operations. They do not establish how he overcame resistance. I cannot turn those outcomes into a resourcefulness anecdote that the dossier does not contain, or count their revenue as Raven’s traction.

Who wants this phone badly enough to change phones? “Employees” is too broad an answer. The illustrated Gmail-to-Google-Drive-to-Slack workflow supplies a plausible job, but no identified occupational niche repeatedly performing it. Initial shipments are more encouraging than a request form, though quantities and payment status are undisclosed. The reported 160,000 demo views and hundreds of community signups show curiosity. They provide limited evidence of demand, because watching a phone execute a command costs much less than trusting it with tomorrow’s work. Rabbit’s existing-device software makes that distinction consequential: Raven must solve a problem painful enough to justify adopting another device.

The strongest case against passing is that Isa has already crossed from an idea into functioning software, has previous operating experience, and reports shipping the result. Raven fits my unstated preference for concrete proof before fundraising much better than a presentation-only company would. But the demonstration does not yet establish the other pattern I look for: technical users repeatedly appropriating a new interface for useful work. My prototype-based exceptions address uncertainties that unusually strong working products or unmistakable demand can overcome. Here, the prototype is meaningful, but neither independently measured performance nor repeated user behavior supplies an override for the missing founder evidence. There is also no affirmative evidence of the launch-dependent, partner-dependent, or premature-delegation red flags; I would not invent those from a small public record.

I would pass on the present evidence. Raven’s Android and workflow-software background is relevant, but the dossier never connects the idea to a problem Isa personally encountered or a neglected operational burden he has undertaken. Nor does it document an evidence-driven route change; the Sintem-to-Raven identity change proves no such thing. My flip condition would be one documented deployment in which Isa removes a named adoption blocker within a week and five paying users in the same occupational niche then use the resulting workflow daily for four weeks. That would connect the builder, the obstacle, and the need. Today those remain separate pieces.
