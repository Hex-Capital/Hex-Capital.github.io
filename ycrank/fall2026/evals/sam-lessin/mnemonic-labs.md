# Mnemonic Labs -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Mnemonic Labs has a proposed scaling law and an undisclosed price. I can evaluate the research thesis; I cannot yet underwrite the deal. Post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital are all unknown. The September 2026 database entry for $125K of YC funding establishes none of those things. YC membership does not prove an auction, but it does not establish a cheap, discarded opportunity either.

The founders are the strongest reason to take this seriously. Videh Seksaria worked in quantitative research at Citadel and Walleye; Kalki Seksaria worked at Tower Research, where the company attributes $20M of annual PnL to his work. That is an operating claim worth verifying, beyond an MIT credential. Building their own training pipeline and architecture in under six weeks adds evidence that they can execute outside an established desk. My outsider-insider pattern has some support here: they understand trading operations and have begun building the learning system. The missing bridge is whether that system produces an economic result. Kalki’s prior-employer PnL belongs in founder underwriting, not Mnemonic’s revenue.

The potentially investable hypothesis is precise enough to be interesting: more training on market activity generates additional profitable strategies. But a run exceeding 25TB of tokens measures an input. What card does this check flip? Mnemonic has disclosed no round-funded experiment with a deadline and a pass/fail threshold for live returns after transaction costs. Nor is it established why existing trading profits or a commercial partner could not finance the test. Prodigy’s overlapping foundation-model work makes exclusive data and execution feedback particularly consequential. Neither is established here, and inbound contact from a public launch supplies no evidence that owned access already causes a purchase.

The bull case stings because these founders might need very little conventional distribution. If their model discovers durable trading profits, Mnemonic could monetize directly and finance further research from cash generation. That would fit my preference for a business that can delete the next financing from its plan. But proprietary trading and licensing are both inferred business models in this dossier. There are no disclosed current returns, customer conversions, margins, burn, or runway to choose between them. My exceptions to missing financing gates require operating receipts, proprietary access, or demonstrated conversion. The founders’ backgrounds justify attention; they do not yet establish those company-level overrides.

I pass at this point. Mnemonic’s core underwriting risk is that successful model training never becomes cash available to its owners. The relevant AI red flag is mistaking technical progress for an economic advantage; the dossier does not establish that the founders themselves have made that mistake. There is also no evidence of financing pressure tactics or an equal-control committee. My one verifiable condition for reopening the investment is six consecutive months of independently reconciled live trading profit that covers execution, data, compute, and payroll at disclosed deployed capital. That would establish a functioning base business and materially change how I price the research upside.
