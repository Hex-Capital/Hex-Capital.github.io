# Poth Labs -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The important event is Poth Labs’s soft pivot from adaptive surveys to a knowledge graph that answers questions across operational systems. That suggests the founders are attached to understanding user behavior rather than to their first implementation. But the dossier omits the decisive middle: what broke, which customer exposed it, what Wong and Horváth changed, and how quickly the change improved usage. “Soft pivot” is evidence of motion, not yet evidence of relentless resourcefulness.

There is nevertheless a real product here. It resolves identities across CRM, support, analytics, transcripts, and surveys; returns cited supporting and contradicting evidence; and can launch follow-up research. Several Summer 2026 YC teams reportedly used it, while Inkle, Sol, Composio, UnCircuit, and Illume appear as trusted teams. That is more than a diagram. But Poth publishes no entity-resolution error rate, research-validity benchmark, data volume, or concrete result produced for one customer. The technical proof is working software, not measured superiority.

The narrow well remains blurry. “Product leaders with data across at least three tools” describes a segment, but not an urgent user whose behavior proves the product is necessary. The five named teams are useful evidence, yet there is no payment, repeated-query frequency, retention, referral, or example of a team changing a roadmap or preventing churn because of Poth. A demo funnel and 270 LinkedIn followers do not answer the simple question: who comes back when the founders stop asking them to?

The strongest case for investing is the team’s proximity to the problem. Wong worked on commercial data systems at Palantir, and Horváth was a data scientist who coauthored work on a 4.5-billion-parameter multimodal model. Cross-system identity resolution is ugly, consequential work, and their pivot toward it may have come from watching early users struggle with fragmented evidence. If those users are already bending Ask Poth into recurring decisions, the dossier understates the company. But this is still an inferred origin story. It does not document the founders encountering the problem themselves or name the integration, governance, or accuracy obstacle they personally conquered.

The bear case wins for now because Enterpret already offers entity resolution, cited answers, a customer context graph, and more than 50 integrations after raising $25.1 million. Poth’s adaptive follow-up research is a plausible difference, but no concrete pre-round proof spike shows that customers value the combination. Nor is there an exception-quality override: the product works, but there is neither unmistakable pull nor independently measured technical evidence. I see none of the launch-day, partnership, or premature-hiring red flags, but absence of those mistakes cannot substitute for founder behavior under resistance.

I would not write the check today. The company is too substantial for a simple pass, but it fails my operational gate because the dossier never shows a named blocker becoming a shipped workaround. One verifiable condition would flip me: a named customer documents that Poth solved a specific cross-system identity or research failure within two weeks, then used the resulting workflow weekly for eight consecutive weeks as a paying customer. That would simultaneously reveal the founders and the narrow well.
