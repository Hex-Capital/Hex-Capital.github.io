# Ooak Data -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Seven-figure contracts with the two largest AI labs make **Ooak Data** a revenue-validated supplier without yet making **Ooak Data** an index on agentic AI. That is the signature: unusually strong buying behavior at pre-seed, paired with a weak structural distribution position. The contracts say Ooak Data may be “just working”; dependence on two unnamed buyers and competition from Scale AI, Surge AI, Mercor, and Snorkel say Ooak Data could still be a high-value feature supplier rather than one of the dozen enduring companies in the category.

**Harvey** is the high rung on revenue velocity because its valuation moved from $3B to $5B to $8B as customer revenue visibly compounded. Ooak Data shares the load-bearing mechanism: sophisticated buyers are already writing seven-figure contracts, not touring pilots or signing soft LOIs. Ooak Data diverges because the dossier provides no ARR, renewal, contract-duration, or month-over-month growth data, and the two customers remain unnamed. Seven figures at pre-seed is exceptional, but without a curve I cannot distinguish recurring demand from two bespoke procurement events.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism on index position: being a credible number two in a market whose economics concentrate around the leader. Ooak Data is not yet an index on AI workflows because Scale AI, Surge AI, Mercor, and Snorkel already possess lab relationships, capital, or expert networks that can absorb Ooak Data’s product category. Ooak Data could escape that pattern if reusable rights to hundreds of acquired company ecosystems create a scarce workflow library, but no dataset count, exclusivity provision, or cross-customer reuse rate is disclosed. Today Ooak Data must win share against category leaders rather than automatically compound with every new agent.

The 2025 migration of established training-data vendors into RL environments is a real catalyst for Ooak Data: static benchmarks saturated just as labs began demanding interactive, multi-tool environments. The counterfactual that would move Ooak Data into the highest catalyst tier is evidence that frontier-model progress created a newly indispensable data bottleneck that synthetic environments cannot solve, rather than merely prompting vendors to sell a new dataset format. Ooak Data’s own research argues this case, but published performance comparisons between its acquired-workflow environments and Snorkel-style simulations would turn the reopening from inferred to demonstrated.

**Perplexity** is the relevant founder parallel because Aravind Srinivas repeatedly converted conversations into shipped artifacts. Pierre-Louis Vouteau previously built ExamSolutions to a reported 15,000 users, Grégoire Lamy progressed from Epsor’s first product manager to Head of Ops & Data, and Thomas Aubry led data work at PayLead before shipping Ooak Data’s anonymization pipeline and APEX Explorer. Ooak Data therefore has more operating evidence than credential-only founders, and Ooak Data’s pivot plus current lab contracts imply iteration. The gap from Perplexity is direct evidence of cadence: APEX Explorer contains 480 Mercor tasks, not Ooak-owned environments, and the dossier names no sequence of customer feedback followed by weekly product releases.

Up to $300,000 per acquired ecosystem and a target of more than 300 acquisitions create Ooak Data’s 18-month single-miracle test. If anonymization preserves task fidelity, reusable rights allow one acquisition to serve several labs, and renewals follow, Ooak Data’s library compounds: every ecosystem improves coverage while marginal revenue outruns procurement cost. If any one of privacy approval, fidelity, reuse rights, or acquisition economics fails, Ooak Data requires several miracles simultaneously. The **Color Genomics** exception partly applies because Ooak Data is workflow infrastructure on top of a hard technical layer, but unlike Color’s commodity sequencing substrate, Ooak Data has not shown that its raw workflow supply is standardized or reusable.

Two seven-figure lab contracts lead me to forecast that within 36 months **Ooak Data** reaches $25M-$40M ARR and is acquired by **Scale AI**, rather than becoming an independent category default. Ooak Data’s revenue signal can support a substantial data business quickly, while its lowest-scoring dimension—index-like distribution—makes consolidation the more probable endpoint. Scale AI can distribute Ooak Data’s real-company workflow library across existing lab relationships and internalize the acquisition, privacy, and processing machinery more efficiently than Ooak Data can build a broad standalone channel.

Scale AI’s $14.3B strategic backing and Surge AI’s reported $1.2B revenue make the bear case sting: Ooak Data could be squeezed between well-capitalized incumbents and two concentrated buyers that internalize environment creation after one contract cycle. Ooak Data is also paying as much as $300,000 per ecosystem before disclosing reuse economics, while removing names, dates, financial figures, addresses, and PII without publishing fidelity results—a genuine multi-miracle red flag. The evidence that currently wins is actual seven-figure purchasing by the two largest labs; pre-seed buyers do not spend that amount merely to be polite. I would reverse to Pass if either contract proves non-recurring and Ooak Data cannot document at least three independent renewing customers within 12 months.
