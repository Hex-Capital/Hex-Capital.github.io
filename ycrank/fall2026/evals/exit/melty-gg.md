# Melty -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Melty’s strongest exit route is a strategic acquisition of Gym by an AI lab or simulation platform that repeatedly builds game-based evaluations. The launch demo shows a simulated robot driving in GTA V and interacting with racks in a simulated data center. Melty reports building custom tasks with unnamed teams. A buyer could gain reusable tooling for changing mechanics, objectives, and agent roles if Gym materially cuts the time and cost of building successive tasks. The demo and reported projects make a paid repeat-use pilot plausible at seed. Setup savings, the cost of supporting additional games, and the capital needed to reach repeatable deployment remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and zero follow-on investment. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3% pro rata. Gym pricing and company revenue are undisclosed, leaving the sale prices below as scenario assumptions. (Key Facts)

| Scenario | Assumed transaction and holder cash |
|---|---|
| Modest sale | $30 million enterprise value yields $30 million in distributable equity and $900,000 to the holder, or 1.8×. |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value yields $100 million in distributable equity and $3 million to the holder, or 6×. A $20 million reduction for debt and deal adjustments leaves $80 million and cuts the holder’s cash to $2.4 million, or 4.8×. |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale yields $0 realized within eight years. |

The sale cases assume an all-cash close within eight years, full participation by the holder, and a pro rata waterfall with zero debt, deal adjustments, or senior claims except in the stated sensitivity. At the unadjusted strong-case price, a sale limited to half the holder’s stake would deliver $1.5 million at close. The memo leaves Gym’s transfer permissions and the holder’s participation rights unverified. The free consumer launcher and reported player base provide no evidence of a funded payout or eligible secondary. (Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer may be able to meet its needs with curated game tasks or an existing simulator. Good Start Labs already offers tasks with verifiable rewards, while Isaac Lab provides a physics simulator for robot learning. Melty’s consumer terms also document publisher restrictions, creator permission requirements, and takedowns. Those restrictions make the rights to commercialize and transfer Gym’s game-derived environments a material diligence issue. The memo establishes neither a substitute for Gym’s full workflow nor a restriction that blocks a Gym acquisition. It also gives no supported sale-price ceiling below the proceeds hurdle. The Pass gate is therefore untriggered. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a prospective strategic buyer show that owning Gym would be worth enough to clear the holder’s proceeds hurdle? A paid repeat-use deployment that measures setup costs against the buyer’s current workflow, documents transferable rights for the environments used, and informs a buyer-side valuation consistent with at least $83.3 million in distributable equity would move the action to Reach out.
