# Ailit -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a rail equipment supplier is Ailit’s strongest potential route to liquidity. Wabtec already sells a dedicated battery locomotive. If Superwagon can electrify existing locomotives while preserving uptime, acquiring it could give a supplier a retrofit offering for customers that want to keep their fleets. That buyer rationale is an inference. Ailit’s proposed tariff of about 20% below diesel-equivalent cost is a company target. The memo reports a full-scale prototype positioned behind a locomotive ahead of commissioning, with loaded-haul performance and repeatable swap times still to demonstrate. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

A paid railroad deployment that measures loaded-haul uptime, swap times, and route margins after pack and site costs is the next milestone. Reported prior funding and the completed prototype make a field test plausible, and YC says Ailit is preparing for a first deployment in 2027. The memo leaves the test budget, funding coverage, and site readiness open. A purchase of the pack and swap-site business is a plausible structure, with IP ownership and assignment, site and customer consents, and holder participation unverified. The memo supplies no independent liquidity route. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. To receive $2.5 million gross in cash within eight years, the holder needs about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The two sale scenarios assume a close within eight years, eligibility to sell the entire stake, cash paid at closing, and a pro rata distribution after debt and deal adjustments with no senior preference reducing the holder’s share. The prices illustrate the hurdle. The memo supplies no transaction comparables or measured route economics to support them. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

- **Modest sale.** $50 million in enterprise value less $10 million in assumed debt and deal adjustments leaves $40 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $1.2 million, or 2.4× gross.
- **Strong sale.** $110 million in enterprise value less $15 million in assumed debt and deal adjustments leaves $95 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $2.85 million, or 5.7× gross. At 2.5% ownership, the same sale yields $2.375 million, or 4.75×.
- **Prolonged private operation.** If Ailit reinvests available cash and there is no eligible share purchase or distribution within eight years, the holder realizes $0.

### Countercase

Wabtec can keep selling its own battery locomotive, and Voltify already offers a competing electrification approach and holds a patent covering a dual-source locomotive and tender. Ailit also plans to carry the pack and site costs itself. Low utilization or falling diesel prices could squeeze margins and weaken a buyer’s reason to pay enough to clear the proceeds hurdle. The memo establishes neither a ready substitute for Ailit’s specific retrofit use case nor a supported ceiling on sale proceeds. The Pass gate is therefore unmet. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid railroad deployment demonstrate enough value to a rail equipment supplier to support a sale that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Railroad-verified loaded-haul uptime and repeatable swaps, measured route margins after pack and site costs, and a supplier’s valuation of buying versus building would answer it. Move from Watch to Reach out if those results support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity under the benchmark terms. Move to Pass if the evidence puts holder proceeds materially below the objective even under the strongest credible sale.
