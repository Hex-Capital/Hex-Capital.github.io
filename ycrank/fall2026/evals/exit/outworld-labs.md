# Outworld Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A robotics platform vendor could buy Horizon to integrate hardware-calibrated dexterous manipulation, scene reconstruction, and policy evaluation into its training stack. Outworld says Horizon fits simulated behavior to physical measurements and reports agreement. The memo documents a public launch and demo, with buyer-side savings and purchase interest still unverified. Ownership could give a buyer control over the engine and calibration workflow if using them across its platform materially reduces physical testing and offers more value than licensing or building the capability. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

The demo and the founders’ robotics research make a comparative pilot plausible. The memo provides no funded pilot, burn, or runway figures to underwrite the cost and timing of reaching that milestone. It also leaves ownership and transfer rights for the engine and any calibration data unverified. The buyout case lacks predictable cash flow and financing evidence. A secondary lacks eligible share demand, an IPO lacks evidence of operating scale and reporting readiness, and a dividend or buyback lacks evidence of distributable cash. (*Founders & Team*, *Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Facts*)

Under the shared scenario, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target requires $2.5 million in gross cash, or $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3%. The equity hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% ownership and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% hurdle is $88.3 million of enterprise value. A sale by year eight at $25 million in enterprise value yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), while a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million gross (5.7×). Both assume a pro rata distribution before investor taxes. Actual ownership, debt, fees, and senior claims are unverified, and the memo supplies no basis for a realizable sale-price range. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

If growth plateaus, buyers stay absent, public-market scale remains out of reach, and Outworld reinvests available cash for eight years, the holder realizes $0 and retains the shares. Any wind-down recovery would come from the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo cannot quantify. (*Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A buyer can already get GPU-accelerated manipulation simulation from Isaac Sim / Newton, hardware-trajectory fitting from MuJoCo Playground, and open-source tactile-contact tooling from SuperDex. If those tools deliver comparable hardware results, or if Horizon requires substantial recalibration for each robot and task, building with available components or licensing Horizon could capture most of the benefit. The memo establishes overlap and calibration risk while leaving end-to-end substitutability unresolved. It also leaves sale economics unverified, so the evidence falls short of either Pass gate. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid pilot with a robotics platform vendor show that Horizon reduces physical test hours and total calibration cost across two robot platforms while matching on-hardware task success against the vendor’s current stack? A buyer-validated result would move the action to Reach out if the vendor can quantify an ownership benefit over licensing that supports a sale range clearing the modeled proceeds hurdle. Otherwise, keep Watch.
