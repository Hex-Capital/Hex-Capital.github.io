# ORO AI -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path A, scarce complements, is the strongest path. If cheaper models let AI teams test more purchasing policies, the scarce input could be a trusted way to detect costly errors that surface weeks after an order. ORO’s SMB Bench simulates a 90-day produce-buying desk and compares matched scenarios with and without delayed customer and supplier effects. ORO reports that no tested model beat the fixed buying rule in every run and that the best model averaged $7,749 in simulated profit against the scripted expert’s $10,795. That is seed-stage evidence that the prototype can expose weaknesses inside the simulation. Whether it detects failures that buyers would otherwise miss in production remains open. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Wholesalers helped calibrate the simulation and scripted expert, and ORO controls access to the grader. That gives ORO something specific to supply today. Durable value capture would depend on continued access to operator knowledge that competitors struggle to reproduce. The memo establishes no exclusive data rights. (Defensibility)

The memo records 670 dataset downloads in a month. ORO reports 859 runs across 16 models, with the buyer count and paid repeat use unverified. The founders’ evaluation and distributed-systems experience, together with the benchmark release, support seed-stage execution. Hosted access and custom environments are plausible products, but pricing, calibration labor per new workflow, and serving costs are unknown, so scale economics receive the absent-evidence default. The seed-stage profile is plausible, with the operating base unclear given the Vancouver and San Francisco listings. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A well-funded competitor could use public benchmark methods, recruit its own wholesale experts, and offer a customer-specific simulation. Cheap models could also make it easier for buyers to build their own tests. Veris already builds enterprise replicas, and SMB Bench covers one purchasing workflow while other business functions follow fixed rules. The memo offers no like-for-like evidence that a substitute detects the same delayed failures, so the Path A override is untriggered. Path B remains unproven because the memo provides neither a same-quality labor comparison showing a newly viable transaction nor evidence that buyers would keep paying ORO under model parity. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent AI team pay to use SMB Bench again after a head-to-head qualification against its best alternative? Use the same models and held-out wholesale cases with known delayed outcomes. If ORO reliably catches costly failures the alternative misses and the buyer pays to repeat the evaluation on its next model release, that would support Reach out if the updated score clears 65 and mandate fit is confirmed. A readily available substitute that matches ORO at a viable cost would close Path A and move the action to Pass on the current Path B evidence.
