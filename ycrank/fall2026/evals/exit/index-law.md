# Index Law -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic sale to a legal-workflow platform. Mike‑1 combines company-specific contract history, redlining, and handoffs to independent counsel. A buyer could use that workflow to reach startups that engage counsel as needed. Index Law reports one contract turnaround under 60 minutes and quotes up to 80% below comparable law-firm quotes. Those claims suggest a benefit worth testing. The memo documents adjacent competitors and their financings, which leave acquisition interest and pricing unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The public preview gives Index Law a way to test whether free users convert to paid plans and keep sending contracts after the first matter. A paid cohort with repeat usage and measurable savings after model and support costs would strengthen the buyer case. The preview and announced pricing make that test plausible. Its funding and timing remain open, and the memo identifies no realized revenue. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A software acquisition is plausible because Index Law routes licensed advice to independent lawyers. A buyer would still need to verify software ownership, permissions to transfer and reuse customer matter history, and continuity of counsel referrals. The memo leaves independent liquidity routes unevidenced. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years. The sale scenarios assume $5 million in combined debt and deal adjustments, a pro rata waterfall with no senior claims, eligibility to sell the entire stake, and full cash payment within eight years.

| Scenario | Bridge to holder cash |
|----------|-----------------------|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M leaves $35M in distributable equity. At 3%, the holder receives $1.05M, or 2.1×. |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $5M leaves $95M in distributable equity. At 3%, the holder receives $2.85M, or 5.7×. At 2.5% ownership, this falls to $2.375M, or 4.75×. |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or payout yields $0 in realized proceeds. |

The base case requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity, or $88.3 million in enterprise value under the assumed deductions. The memo gives no basis for underwriting either sale price, so proceeds receive the absent-evidence default. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A strategic buyer could build basic contract review itself. The memo identifies an existing configurable legal plugin, and Index Law’s retained matter history and independent-counsel workflow would carry less buyer value if customers rarely return or transfer permissions limit their use. The free preview and company-reported outcomes leave both questions open. The evidence establishes neither a structural closing block nor a price ceiling below the proceeds hurdle, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a legal-workflow platform pay at least $88.3 million in enterprise value for Mike‑1 if paying customers repeatedly used it and verified meaningful workflow savings? Credible buyer feedback tying that price to measured paid-cohort retention and savings would move the action to Reach out. A supported price ceiling that leaves the holder materially below $2.5 million would move it to Pass.
