# AnyAlpha -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

AnyAlpha’s proposed post-money valuation is unknown. Competing term sheets are unknown. Whether my check would fund something otherwise unfundable is unknown. I will not turn YC membership into evidence of an auction, but I cannot underwrite cheap uncertainty without a price. The dossier also names no capital-funded experiment with a deadline and a measurable result. Automating a trading thesis describes the product; it does not establish what truth this round buys.

The founders are the strongest reason to keep reading. Shirley Peng built trading systems at Coinbase and LSEG, and Jerry Miao reports six years building quantitative data, backtests, and execution. Together they previously built apps reaching $500K ARR. That is meaningful evidence of shipping across the two systems AnyAlpha joins: trading infrastructure and consumer software. Their three years building together further strengthen the operating case. I credit those receipts to the team; the prior apps’ revenue does not establish this product’s economics.

The investment problem is who owns anything scarce once thesis-to-agent creation becomes commonplace. AnyAlpha connects research, market discovery, backtesting, and execution, but Composer and Capitalise.ai already demonstrate adjacent workflows, while Olas offers autonomous prediction-market trading. The team could create a valuable translation layer between traders and fragmented venues—one of the patterns I like—but the dossier establishes neither exclusive data nor embedded customer access. The 42 cumulative iOS ratings and 100-plus Android downloads span an earlier positioning. They cannot tell me whether the current agent product causes purchases or repeat usage.

The strongest bull case is that these founders can sell useful automation without needing to sell persistent trading alpha. A customer might pay to execute their own rules reliably, even without beating the market. Their prior consumer revenue makes that possibility more credible than a generic agent pitch. But the reported $53 gain on a $100 demo account measures a trading outcome, not subscription demand, execution reliability, or company profit. Purchases listed at $4.99–$79.99 have unspecified billing periods and entitlements. The known exceptions to my pricing and AI skepticism require demonstrated conversion, retention, cash economics, or unusually strong external validation. AnyAlpha’s current-product evidence does not yet supply that override.

Delete the next financing from the spreadsheet. There is no disclosed margin, burn, or seed-funded break-even plan to evaluate, so economics receive the prescribed missing-evidence score rather than an invented failure case. Likewise, CEO and CTO titles give me no basis to allege an equal-founder decision committee, and the dossier does not prove that margins depend on agent errors disappearing. The actual concern is narrower: no demonstrated paid workflow yet offsets a readily reproducible product category. April 2026 sports-information terms alongside October agent positioning also leave the commercial offering insufficiently clear.

I pass with my own money. AnyAlpha has credible builders, but their operating history cannot substitute for the missing deal and current business. My verifiable flip condition is six consecutive months of positive operating cash flow from retained, paying agent subscribers, excluding trading gains and financing proceeds. That would demonstrate owned workflow causing purchases and a business capable of surviving without another round—the kind of hard evidence that could justify revisiting my missing-gate defaults.
