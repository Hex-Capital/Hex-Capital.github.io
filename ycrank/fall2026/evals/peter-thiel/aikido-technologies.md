# Aikido Technologies -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Aikido Technologies has a testable proposition: putting compute beside offshore wind can overcome terrestrial infrastructure constraints while preserving competitive cost and reliable service. What would prove it wrong? An integrated installation whose backup power, maintenance, and connectivity requirements erase the offshore advantage. The proposed 15MW+ turbine, batteries, and hull-based cooling make the proposition concrete. But the dossier establishes neither a disputed belief that named peers reject nor an operating result testing the complete proposition. I assign the secret the absent-evidence default; I would not manufacture contrarian conviction from an unusual location.

The founding structure is more encouraging. Kanner and Yu worked at Principle Power before Aikido, and the founders occupy the CEO and two chief-engineer positions. This supplies evidence of shared working history and technical responsibility inside the founding team. It does not establish Reeves’s prior alignment with the others, everyone’s full-time status, or their economic exposure. Those are unanswered questions, not founder red flags. The team earns affirmative credit for an existing relationship, rather than for collecting three impressive biographies.

What could Aikido own? Its [granted floating-wind patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12571372B2/en) is a tangible asset, but the dossier does not establish that competing offshore datacenters must practice its claims. Accessible data halls distinguish the architecture from submerged compute; they do not establish exclusive access to customers or superior economics. Nor does an identified UK site establish a beachhead monopoly without site rights, customer commitments, or a demonstrated cost advantage. There is proprietary engineering here. Ownership of a valuable market remains unproved.

The commercial uncertainty concerns the revenue engine itself. Contracted powered capacity and compute services are dossier inferences, with different capital requirements and operating responsibilities. Without a chosen offering, price, or margin structure, I cannot construct the arithmetic of a power-law outcome; that dimension receives its stated default. Distribution has slightly more substance: Aikido reports inference-customer interest and commercial discussions around a UK installation targeting 2028. That identifies a potential buyer class and project route, but no named purchasing customer, sales cycle, or implementation economics. NVIDIA Inception membership does not close that gap.

The strongest objection to passing is that Aikido has already retired some physical risk. ABS witnessed a quarter-scale upending demonstration, and the founders possess relevant offshore experience. Its move into AI power infrastructure also fits the broader pattern of financing the physical production stack behind software. The **Panthalassa** exception matters: both companies connect ocean energy with constrained compute demand, and a credible pilot-factory and deployment path can justify substantial coordination risk. The material difference is that Aikido’s demonstrated milestone concerns its floating-wind platform; the integrated datacenter remains a planned retrofit. Norway deployment plans and UK discussions are meaningful intentions, but they do not yet remove the risk of synchronizing generation, batteries, cooling, compute, servicing, and customer delivery.

I pass on Aikido Technologies today. The objection is the distance between a validated platform component and a business that captures the value of offshore compute—not the absence of ordinary pre-seed revenue. No documented part-time founder, broken technical claim, or regulatory shutdown warrants an adverse gate score. My one verifiable flip condition is an independently monitored, paid integrated deployment demonstrating that Aikido meets a customer’s contracted availability requirements at a lower fully loaded delivered-compute cost than that customer’s terrestrial alternative. That would turn the central proposition into evidence worth underwriting.
