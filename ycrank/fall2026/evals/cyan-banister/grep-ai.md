# Grep AI -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

By 2032, I can imagine a compliance team teaching its software a judgment once, then spending its days on genuinely unfamiliar cases. Grep AI’s interesting idea is the transition between those two modes: repeated agent reasoning becomes executable procedure, while exceptions still receive reasoning. That gives me a mechanism to work backward from. First capture expert judgment, then make routine execution cheaper, then earn permission to handle more work. The dossier documents that product approach; it does not yet demonstrate the learning loop improving cost and reliability over successive generations. There is a future here, with some distance still unmeasured.

The founders have paid relevant operating tuition. AJ Asver and Miguel Ríos worked together at Brex beginning in 2020, and Parcha was tackling compliance in 2023, before Grep’s broader preview opened in December 2025. Asver’s prior acquisition and Ríos’s platform-engineering leadership add evidence that they can build and operate. This fits my pattern of an operating wound becoming a product specification. But I would distinguish that substantial domain history from a documented, independently pursued obsession: the dossier provides fewer early experiments or first-believer artifacts than would earn my highest founder score.

Why should a legitimate business wait days for someone to investigate it? Grep names a concrete obstruction: the founders describe 2,000 businesses awaiting manual verification at Brex. At IG, flagged-customer activation reportedly fell from two or three days to under ten minutes. That returns time to applicants and operating capacity to reviewers. I like that transfer of agency. The limit is that this appears to repair necessary review work; the dossier does not identify an artificial restriction or a gatekeeper collecting rent that Grep can remove.

IG also did something considerably more persuasive than join a waitlist. It spent months shadow-testing the workflow, encoded its review criteria, and obtained governance approval. Its testimonial reports automated screening reviews increasing from 10% to 30%, with accuracy above 99%. That is institutional effort and reputation committed to a functioning product. I give this more weight than hundreds of preview signups or the company-wide 150,000-hours-saved claim, whose allocation between product generations is unclear. Missing ARR is no reason to dismiss that behavior.

The strongest argument for writing a check is that this regulated beachhead could finance a reusable execution layer. Grep already names Airwallex, Worldpay, and Citizens Bank as customers, and AgentRun is a tangible software artifact. But my preference for concealed infrastructure requires a dependency the company controls. Customer procedures may become sticky; the dossier labels that an inference. Apache-2.0 infrastructure is reusable by competitors, and n8n’s SAP integration gives an overlapping platform enterprise distribution. Asver’s reported nearly $80,000 monthly AI bill makes the optimization problem painfully real. The reported redesign does not establish current margins or a scarce asset.

I would hold my check at **Neutral**. My willingness to back companies before revenue applies here; revenue is not the missing permission slip. The unresolved issue is whether Grep AI becomes essential infrastructure or remains useful software facing abundant substitutes. The dossier establishes no founder-control violation or exclusive model dependency. Sensitive compliance data does make consent and custody architecture necessary diligence, without establishing a privacy breach. **My flip condition:** one named customer documents six months of Grep automatically converting repeated cases into procedures, with declining cost per completed review and stable independently audited accuracy. That would show the future teaching itself to become economical.
