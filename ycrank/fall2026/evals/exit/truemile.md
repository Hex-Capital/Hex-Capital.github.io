# TrueMile -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

TrueMile’s strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a carrier that could deploy its fleet solver across more trucks. The memo describes a founder-built solver already used in TrueMile’s leased-on fleet to plan outbound loads and backhauls together. TrueMile reports 17% higher dry-van revenue per mile and 8% higher refrigerated revenue per mile on AI-selected loads versus the market. Those claims suggest a potential buyer benefit. The ownership case depends on whether combining the solver with transferable lane data yields a durable margin gain a buyer could capture across its own fleet. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A milestone would be a repeatable contribution-margin gain on recurring shipper lanes, measured against comparable dispatch and including empty miles, driver payments, and manual approvals. TrueMile reports $57,000 in freight bookings over three weeks and has solicited two dedicated-lane partnerships. That supports a path to testing the milestone, with signed lane commitments, burn, and funding needs still unknown. The solver’s use in TrueMile’s fleet makes a software asset purchase plausible. IP title and data transfer rights remain unverified, and a broader carrier acquisition would also have to address lease terms and carrier liability. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

**Illustrative proceeds.** A $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, falling to 3% after 40% relative dilution with no follow-ons. The 5× gross cash target is $2.5 million within eight years. It requires $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%, versus $125 million at 2% or $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% hurdle is $88.3 million in enterprise value. At year eight, a $25 million sale yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million gross (5.7×), assuming pro rata cash distribution before investor taxes and any fees or senior claims. The memo provides no realizable sale range or actual holder terms. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public-market scale remains out of reach, and all available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains the shares. A sponsor buyout or company-funded payout lacks evidence of cash generation after reinvestment. The memo gives no basis for an IPO resale or an eligible TrueMile secondary. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A carrier can already buy route planning and dispatch from CloudTrucks or source broker-carrier matching from FleetWorks. TrueMile’s selected-load comparisons leave fleet-wide margin gains unverified, and every load still requires human approval. If existing tools capture most of the benefit, a buyer has little reason to acquire TrueMile’s software or assume its carrier obligations. The substitutes weaken the ownership case. The evidence leaves both a structural block and materially inadequate proceeds unestablished, so the Pass gate does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could a carrier justify from the incremental margin it would gain by owning TrueMile’s solver and lane data? A matched recurring-lane test, paired with buyer economics that account for licensing and internal build costs, would answer it. Move to Reach out if the resulting price range credibly clears the holder’s eight-year cash hurdle after dilution and claims. Remain on Watch while that range is unsupported.
