# Ailit -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — repeatable sales and deployment with account expansion.** The initial buyer is a freight railroad looking to reduce diesel spend on regularly scheduled routes while preserving hauling power, range, and uptime. Ailit proposes powering existing locomotives with swappable battery tenders, retaining diesel backup, and selling electricity per kWh at roughly 20% below diesel-equivalent cost with no upfront equipment purchase. If a standard installation can serve recurring departures, a railroad could buy more energy by adding trains at existing swap sites, then extend the service to other routes. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The memo documents a full-scale prototype positioned behind a locomotive in Richmond ahead of commissioning. A first customer deployment is planned for 2027. The supplier-directory listing identifies a possible route to buyers, while the memo documents no customer acquisition through it, paid activation, repeat use, or buyer budget commitment. The reported zero FY2025 turnover applies to the Swedish entity for that period. The prototype is a concrete engineering milestone, and buyer fit remains untested. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Facts)

**Second-market scenario.** If another freight operator has compatible locomotives and routes that can use scheduled swap stops, Ailit could reuse the tender design and per-kWh service. It would still need to establish locomotive compatibility, secure suitable stops and utility capacity, and price against local diesel economics. The memo gives no second-market buyer evidence or measured localization costs. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Ailit may have to finance a separate infrastructure project for each corridor. An additional train may need another tender, more packs, and more swap labor. A new corridor may also require separate railroad procurement and integration work, new sites, and utility connections. At the proposed tariff, per-kWh revenue must cover electricity, pack wear and replacement, site operations, and recovery of battery and site capital. Higher utilization could spread fixed costs across more paid kWh if schedules and grid capacity allow. Lower diesel prices could compress the tariff while those costs persist. The memo provides no measured rollout costs or utilization from loaded service, so proportional capital requirements remain an unresolved risk and the structural Pass gate is untriggered. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can the planned 2027 deployment deliver a buyer-funded test on a recurring loaded route, using a tender and swap design that can be reused on a second corridor? Move to **Reach out** if the railroad pays the proposed tariff, operating records show reliable swaps and scheduled departures, and measured power, labor, battery, and site costs show that additional departures can contribute after capital recovery. That result would test the main constraint at this stage while leaving cross-market demand open. (The Idea, Key Risks)
