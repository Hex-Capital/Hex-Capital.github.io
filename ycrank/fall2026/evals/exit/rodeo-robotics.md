# Rodeo Robotics -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Rodeo’s strongest exit path is a strategic sale to a vehicle inspection or shop-equipment vendor. The memo documents a PitPro tire-changing installation and describes UVeye’s drive-through inspection product. Rodeo says its system combines inspection, diagnosis, and routine service. An inspection vendor could acquire Rodeo to turn detected issues into service jobs within the same workflow. That rationale depends on Rodeo demonstrating that its broader service capabilities improve shop economics. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

Rodeo reports 96% autonomous execution time on initial tasks and targets January 2027 for customer deployments. The memo lists a $500,000 convertible note, with terms and deployment funding unverified. The deployment milestone is concrete, but the memo leaves throughput across vehicle models, technician time after interventions, and the capital needed to reach repeatable deployments open. It also leaves acquisition funding, IP and contract transfer rights, and required consents unverified. There is no evidenced independent liquidity route. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

The proceeds benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale cases below are sensitivity tests. Buyer pricing and the actual waterfall are unknown. Both cases assume a sale of 100% of the equity for cash within eight years, with the holder eligible to receive 3% pro rata after net debt and deal adjustments and no further reduction from senior preferences or holdbacks.

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Gross cash to holder |
|-----------|-------------------------------------------|----------------------|
| Modest sale | $40M less $5M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments = $35M | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M less $10M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments = $90M | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested, with no funded payout or eligible share sale within eight years | $0 (0×) |

The strong case falls to $2.25 million (4.5×) at 2.5% ownership. The memo provides no acquisition pricing or measured buyer economics to establish that Rodeo can reach the hurdle. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A strategic buyer could assemble a narrower offering from existing inspection and tire-service systems. PitPro already has a tire-changing installation, and Automated Tire’s SmartBay covers tire changes, wheel balancing, and inspection. If Rodeo’s broader coverage requires frequent technician intervention, shops may gain too little throughput to justify the cost, weakening a buyer’s reason to acquire the company. The company’s initial-task autonomy figure leaves that question open. The evidence supports Watch because the deployment gap is testable and neither a structural closing block nor inadequate proceeds under the strongest credible route is established. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can paid deployments across multiple shops and vehicle models show enough net labor savings and added service throughput to support a strategic sale that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Logs comparing technician time and completed jobs against each shop’s baseline, together with installed and ongoing costs, would answer the operating question. Move to Reach out if those results are repeatable and support a plausible buyer case for at least $83.3 million in distributable equity under the benchmark terms.
