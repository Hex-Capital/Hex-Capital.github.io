# Particularity -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with the first consumer protection agency, Particularity’s strongest path is to reuse the same browser investigation and evidence capture software across regulators. The company describes agents that test checkout and cancellation flows, timestamp and hash captures, match them to complaints, and map findings to statutory elements for staff review. The EU’s online pricing sweeps show that a comparable buyer performs a similar job abroad. The browser tests and evidence capture appear reusable. Whether complaint intake and statutory mapping can transfer through bounded configuration remains open. *(The Idea, Market & Traction)*

Saved captures could become more valuable as investigators match them to later complaints. That supports repeat use, while recurring fees and expansion revenue remain hypothetical. In an agency contract scenario, incremental contribution would be the contract revenue less attributable sales and procurement work, setup, browser execution, evidence storage, and support. Reusing the browser and capture software could limit setup costs, but the memo gives no contract price or deployment data to test that inference. The reported October 15 launch establishes when the product became public. The memo leaves paid use, commercial growth, and pre-revenue status unknown. *(The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)*

### Countercase

The documented acquisition path starts with a conversation. Its call booking and shared inbox provide reusable lead intake, while purchase, implementation, and first use remain unproven. If each regulator requires bespoke statutory mapping, evidence validation, and security or hosting work, strong fit with one agency may do little to accelerate the next deployment. The product’s statutory mapping and the security and residency requirements in the cited New York solicitation make that risk concrete. The structural downside gate remains open because the memo leaves the extent of required rebuilding uncertain. *(The Idea, Key Risks)*

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paying regulator outside the U.S. produce evidence its staff can use in an investigation with the current product, documented local configuration, and limited vendor effort? A paid production rollout that records the changes, staff time, and direct costs would test portability and the economics of repeating the deployment. Move to Reach out if the resulting evidence clears the rubric’s score and confidence thresholds. Move to Pass if the rollout shows that comparable regulators would require substantial bespoke rebuilding market by market.
