# Meteoric -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first problem is the deal, because there effectively is no deal to underwrite. Meteoric discloses neither valuation nor competing-term-sheet count nor whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. YC admission is not evidence that the belief remains non-consensus; weather modification may be unfashionable, but financing silence earns no scarcity credit. I am not paying for the romance of drones weakening hurricanes without knowing the strike price.

There is a potentially excellent venture moment buried inside the company: fly over one operating solar farm, alter a natural cloud, and measure incremental power at the meter. Success would convert a 13% artificial-cloud chamber result into field evidence that an operator can buy. But Meteoric has not tied round proceeds to that experiment, supplied a deadline, or defined a pass threshold. The 2028 hurricane operation is a second, vastly harder objective contradicted by the World Meteorological Organization’s finding that tropical-cyclone modification lacks generally accepted evidence. It dilutes the clean solar-farm test.

The founders are the strongest part of the case. Mete Karslioglu worked on cloud-reflectivity nozzles and atmospheric measurement; Eric Nilsson worked on digital microfluidics, droplet removal, controls, and atmospheric equipment. They have built technical receipts, not merely collected climate credentials. But the bridge has two sides: atmospheric droplet control and commercial solar operations. The dossier shows work on the former and no shipped deployment, utility procurement experience, airspace approval, or operating access on the latter.

The bull case stings. Electric drones at a claimed $30–$60 per hour could turn an experiment previously burdened by aircraft costing more than $2,000 per hour into something cheap enough to repeat. A 10–30% modeled annual generation uplift would be enormously valuable against a 773 GW interconnection queue, and the direct competitive set for chemical-free solar-cloud clearing appears small. This is exactly the sort of weird physical-world hypothesis where a small check can reveal a lot.

But the economic chain currently stops before the customer. Meteoric has no natural-cloud result, measured recovered MWh, paid pilot, proprietary field dataset, permit portfolio, or evidence that its outreach caused a purchase. Tens to hundreds of drones flying at 1–5 km also collide with baseline FAA limits on altitude, visual line of sight, and one-drone-per-pilot operation. The company publishes neither drone-hours per MW nor fleet capital, maintenance, price, gross margin, burn, or a route to break-even without another round. This is still a laboratory effect attached to modeled value, not a functioning base business.

I am passing. My flip condition is one paid utility-scale field trial that repeatedly measures incremental meter-level generation net of fleet operating cost under a documented authorization path. That single result would establish customer action, physical efficacy, and the beginnings of cash economics; absent it, the likely outcome is a pivot toward more localized fog-clearing applications rather than a scalable solar or hurricane business.
