# Rational -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot underwrite Rational’s round before knowing the proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. YC affiliation and a fashionable “AI employees” category make auction risk material, while the dossier supplies no terms. That is not proof of a bad deal; it is failure of the first gate. More importantly, no use-of-funds plan names the finite experiment this check purchases—only a broad product spanning document collection, approvals, reconciliation, communications, and close.

The strongest evidence is not the 606 LinkedIn followers or 300 advertised integrations. It is that Rational appears embedded in real accounting workflows. Tenace’s approximately 50-person team reports reclaiming 20 hours per person each week, 98% accuracy on configured reconciliations, and roughly doubling its revenue run-rate. An anonymized Fortune Global 500 customer reports halving close time, automating 80% of routine communications, and doubling transaction volume without adding finance headcount. Those are consequential operating outcomes, but the dossier does not establish that either customer paid, renewed, expanded, or selected Rational over an incumbent.

The technical case is better than a generic model wrapper. Rational reports 92% strict exact-return accuracy and 96.3% strict line accuracy from a deterministic tax engine combined with specialized models, versus 58% for the tested GPT-5.6 configuration. Human approval gates also acknowledge that accounting errors compound rather than magically disappearing at scale. The bear case still stings: this is a company-run benchmark with private API access, no structural market barrier, and five heavily financed competitors attacking overlapping workflows. Firm-specific rules and review histories might become a proprietary data pool, but “might accumulate” is not the same as already owning one.

The founders explain the engineering but not the translation layer. Christ Xu has shipped software at Tencent, Jane Street, and OpenAI and previously achieved a $400,000 exit; Jibril Moinuddin built STEMplicity, reportedly connecting 20,000 students with mentors. Those are operating receipts on the product-building side. Neither founder has documented accounting practice, tax operations, or ownership of the buyer relationships on the other side. The current design partners may be supplying that missing domain access, but rented customer expertise is weaker than founder-owned access.

The exception case is Ando: an AI application deserved unusual latitude when measured accuracy sat beneath complete pilot-to-paid conversion and retention. Rational shares the mechanism—task-specific accuracy plus documented customer operating impact—but not the decisive commercial proof. There is no public revenue, pricing, gross margin, burn, runway, or evidence that the business survives without another round. Delete the Series A from the model and Rational currently has impressive customer claims, not a demonstrated cash engine.

I am passing at the present evidence level. One verifiable fact would flip the decision: proof that both cited design partners converted to paid annual contracts and renewed after at least six months. That would connect the owned workflow to purchase and retention, provide a harder override for missing deal data, and turn the round’s obvious experiment into scaling a repeatable accounting deployment rather than financing another broad AI application.
