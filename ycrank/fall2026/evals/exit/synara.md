# Synara -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a wholesale monitoring provider is Synara’s strongest path to liquidity. Synara markets integrator-branded monitoring that uses existing camera recorders and combines AI flagging, human verification, and live intervention. A buyer with a dealer network could roll that workflow out across its installed base. Buying Synara could make economic sense if the workflow reduces operator labor while preserving response quality and helps dealers sell monitoring to more sites. Those benefits are inferred. Synara advertises under 30 seconds from alert to action, and the memo gives no measured deployment results. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

A paid production pilot across multiple integrators could measure operator minutes per site and verify that response quality holds. The public launch, YC backing, and Boris Ploix’s prior work on false-alarm filtering make that test plausible at seed. Conflicting funding figures and the absence of paid deployment data leave the test budget and runway uncertain. The memo also leaves IP ownership and contract assignment unverified and provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Facts*)

**Proceeds bridge.** Use the benchmark $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-ons. The eight-year, 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. Both sale scenarios assume the holder is eligible to sell their full stake by year eight, receives cash at closing, and shares pro rata with no preference reducing their payout.

- **Modest sale.** The memo reports that Motorola bought Calipsa for $39 million net of cash acquired. Using $39 million as an illustrative enterprise value for Synara and assuming zero net debt or deal deductions gives $39 million of distributable equity and $1.17 million to the holder, or 2.34×. (*Founders & Team*)
- **Strong sale.** An assumed $100 million enterprise value, less $10 million in assumed net debt and deal deductions, leaves $90 million of distributable equity and pays the holder $2.7 million, or 5.4×. At $20 million in deductions, the payout falls to $2.4 million, or 4.8×. The memo offers no Synara-specific buyer economics to support the $100 million price.
- **Prolonged private.** If Synara continues operating and reinvests its cash throughout the eight years, the holder realizes $0 in this scenario.

### Countercase

Becklar already sells remote guarding through dealers, and Immix AI Link lets monitoring centers add AI filtering while retaining their operator workflows. A prospective buyer could therefore capture much of the apparent benefit through its existing offering or by adding filtering. The memo gives no measured savings or dealer retention that would establish a reason to buy Synara, and the Calipsa price illustrates a sale well below the holder’s hurdle. These facts support Watch. The Pass gate remains open because the substitutes do not establish a structural block and the single transaction does not establish a ceiling on Synara’s proceeds. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*, *Founders & Team*)

### Decisive diligence question

Would the savings from owning Synara’s full monitoring workflow, measured against adding AI filtering to an existing operation, justify a price that leaves at least $83.3 million for equity holders? Paid deployments across multiple integrators showing lower operator labor per site at comparable verified response quality, with savings that a credible buyer could achieve across its dealer network, would move the action to Reach out if those economics support clearing the hurdle. If the results show buyers can achieve comparable savings through existing tools and the strongest supported sale price leaves the holder materially short, move to Pass.
