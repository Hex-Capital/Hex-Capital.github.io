# Raven -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with people who manage email, calendars, and calls from their phones, Raven has a plausible path to serving the same recurring job across markets. Its Gmail, Slack, and Google Calendar integrations are reusable across buyers who already use those tools. The memo leaves open whether Raven can deliver and activate the same handset and bundled cellular service across markets with limited changes. The public launch led to a direct checkout for a refundable reservation. The path from checkout to device activation and a first completed task remains unverified. (The Idea; Key Risks)

Raven reports that test handsets reached early adopters in October and that more than 1,000 people contacted the company after the prototype debut. That is early adoption and interest for a one-person team. The memo establishes neither earned revenue nor growth in paying customers. Recurring work and saved workflows could support continued use, while the projected $99–$199 monthly price leaves open whether customers will renew or what would cause an existing member’s bill to rise. (Market & Traction; Founders & Team; The Idea; Defensibility)

In a scenario where a customer activates, keeps the phone for six months, and pays the projected rate after the included three months, gross billings would be $496–$796. Contribution depends on the handset, shipping and returns, cellular service, inference, acquisition, and support costs. The return requirement gives Raven a potential way to spread handset cost across memberships if returned units can be redeployed economically. (The Idea; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Even with strong initial fit, each new member needs a physical handset and ongoing cellular and AI service. Raven is still working with hardware partners and has published neither final specifications nor a commercial ship date. If each market requires substantial custom rollout work, or if each activation consumes more cash than it can earn with no credible improvement lever, the structural downside gate would apply. The memo leaves both outcomes unresolved, so the gate stays open. (The Idea; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Raven take a paid cohort in two markets from direct checkout through the first monthly renewal on substantially the same handset and setup flow, with minimal staff help and positive contribution per activation? Cohort records showing delivery, independent activation, paid renewal, and attributable costs would answer it. A repeatable result would support Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 and confidence rises to Medium or High. A rollout that demonstrates unavoidable bespoke work by market or structurally negative contribution would change the action to Pass.
