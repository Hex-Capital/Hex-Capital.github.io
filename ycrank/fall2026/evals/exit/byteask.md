# ByteAsk -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A **strategic acquisition** is ByteAsk’s strongest route. Harness is a plausible buyer because it has already acquired Augment Code’s assets to integrate codebase context into its delivery platform. ByteAsk could add build-aware C/C++ indexing and native patch verification, including for air-gapped environments. In ByteAsk’s own same-model test, it matched Codex CLI at 129 successful runs out of 144 while reporting 68% lower cost. The ownership case is that controlling the verification engine could let a buyer integrate it into its delivery platform and capture the value across customers. The memo establishes an adjacent purchase and a product-level cost signal. Buyer interest and the value of owning ByteAsk remain unverified. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is repeatable paid production use across several C/C++ environments, with customers measuring the cost per accepted patch after verification and human review. In ByteAsk’s case study, Nodus reported 2.3× co-located decode throughput and 52% higher co-located training throughput during its pilot. Those results show the potential value of the resulting code changes. The reported $1 million pre-seed gives ByteAsk capital to pursue further deployments, while burn, runway, and paid conversion remain unknown. An asset sale is a plausible structure, with ownership and transferability of the core engine and customer configurations still unverified. (Market & Traction; Key Risks; Key Facts)

**Illustrative holder proceeds.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and pro rata cash proceeds before investor taxes. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million, requiring $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. Using the illustrative $5 million of net debt, the 3% hurdle is $88.3 million in enterprise value. ByteAsk’s actual debt, preferences, and transaction fees are unknown. The adjacent purchase gives no usable price for ByteAsk, and the memo gives no collected revenue or quantified buyer economics to support a sale range. (Market & Traction; Key Facts)

- **Modest sale.** At $25 million in enterprise value after eight years, the holder receives $600,000 gross, or 1.2×, after illustrative net debt.
- **Strong sale.** At $100 million in enterprise value after eight years, the holder receives $2.85 million gross, or 5.7×. At 2% ownership, the same sale returns $1.90 million, or 3.8×.
- **Prolonged private operation.** If growth plateaus, buyer interest fails to materialize, public scale stays out of reach, and available cash is reinvested for eight years, the holder realizes $0 and retains the shares. A sponsor buyout or company-funded payout lacks evidence of sustainable cash generation. A secondary lacks evidence of eligible demand, and an IPO lacks evidence of operating scale or readiness. Any wind-down recovery would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo does not quantify. (Market & Traction; Key Facts)

### Countercase

Harness already has Augment’s context engine, Cursor already orchestrates agents, and ByteAsk offers its context layer as an add-on. A buyer could integrate the add-on or build C/C++ verification in-house. The Nodus pilot used target H100 hardware and required 150 hours of human review over 30 days, leaving open how much integration and review work a buyer would inherit across customers. This weakens the ownership premium without establishing a structural block or a supported sale price below the holder’s hurdle. The Pass gate is therefore untriggered, and the price gap supports Watch. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

After ByteAsk demonstrates repeatable paid production use, can a strategic buyer justify paying roughly $88.3 million in enterprise value to own the verification engine? Buyer-specific economics that quantify the benefit across deployments, account for integration and review costs, and show an advantage over building or licensing would support Reach out if the resulting price range clears the holder’s target on plausible ownership terms.
