# Kairos Health -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Kairos could make it economical for short-staffed dental practices to call overdue patients more often. Its software identifies patients from practice records, contacts them, and books directly under each practice’s scheduling rules. The strongest case is a recall appointment that the practice would otherwise forgo because staff time costs too much. Direct booking makes that mechanism plausible at seed. The memo leaves labor per completed appointment, booking and eligibility accuracy, and the volume of previously uneconomic recalls unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The memo records 16 live U.S. locations at the October launch. Pecan Dental’s testimonial reports more than 1,200 calls handled and more than $21,000 in average monthly dental production. The incremental production attributable to Kairos is unknown. The founder reports 13 paying practices and over $4,000 in monthly recurring revenue. Renewal, realized pricing, and payment specifically for newly viable recall work remain unclear. The signed LOIs are pipeline. The team’s dental front-desk, patient call-routing, and scheduling integration experience supports its ability to ship the workflow. Its ability to reduce exceptions across practices remains untested in the memo. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Founders & Team)

**Scale scenario.** At the reported $1,200 monthly list price, the claimed 85% gross margin would imply $180 in monthly direct costs per location if both figures applied to the same location. At the $600 launch price, holding those costs constant would leave $420 per location per month before acquisition and any onboarding costs outside gross margin. Actual inference, review, rework, and support costs for an additional location are unknown, as is the number of practices that would buy at a viable price. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Kairos may be selling a more efficient front desk to existing buyers. Staff may still spend enough time correcting scheduling and eligibility errors to erase the savings on marginal recalls. With cheap frontier-quality models, practices could choose Arini’s overlapping recall and verification workflows or a bundled front desk from Weave or Peerlogic. Kairos’s scheduling integrations and practice rules could make switching costly, but the memo shows no buyer preference under functional parity. With abundant competing capital, the memo identifies no durable integration rights or scarce operating capacity that Kairos controls, leaving Path A unsupported. The evidence leaves both Path B conditions open, and a comparative pilot can test them. That supports Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Kairos win paid renewals at a viable price from practices that previously skipped the same recall work, when those practices can choose a functionally comparable alternative? A multi-practice paid pilot should compare usual care, Kairos, and the alternative on incremental attended appointments at comparable booking and verification quality, counting staff review, retries, exceptions, and compliance work. Repeat paid use with positive contribution and a demonstrated preference for Kairos would support Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 with at least medium confidence. A result showing no newly viable recall work or ready substitution at parity would close Path B and move the action to Pass.
