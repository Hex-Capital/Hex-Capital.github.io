# Almanac -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I want are missing: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and what this check specifically buys. YC participation does not prove an auction, but it does not prove scarce capital either. With no terms, financing history, dated milestone, or use-of-funds plan, I cannot tell whether Almanac is a cheap experiment or another AI company raising money to discover its experiment. Both gates remain at their absent-evidence defaults.

The strongest asset is that the founders have shipped adjacent software. CodeAlmanac reached 791 GitHub stars and 71 forks before the team expanded from coding knowledge into company-wide context. That is a real product receipt, not merely three technical résumés spanning Harvard, IIT, ML research, and Arcesium. But it demonstrates developer interest on one side of the bridge, not operating authority inside the Slack-Gmail-Calendar workflows Almanac now proposes to join. The dossier identifies no enterprise deployment, buyer relationship, or founder who has owned company knowledge operations at scale.

The product thesis is legible: continuously assemble a source-linked wiki, then let an assistant act through integrations and a managed browser. The problem is that legibility is not defensibility. Glean already has 250 connectors and more than $100 million ARR; Dust serves more than 3,000 organizations with 300,000 deployed agents. OpenAI and Anthropic supply the underlying computer-use capability. Almanac currently owns neither a proprietary distribution channel nor a unique technical input. Its possible workflow data moat is explicitly inferred, with no retention, correction, migration, or accuracy evidence behind it.

The bull case is that CodeAlmanac proves this team can attract technical users, while the $250-per-user monthly price could turn a handful of retained teams into meaningful pre-seed revenue. A wiki that learns from source relationships and corrections might eventually become the proprietary dark pool. But 791 stars and 846 LinkedIn followers have not been tied to a purchase, pilot, referral, or repeat use of the current product. Treating those attention metrics as customer validation would be precisely the kind of metric substitution that obscures the cash driver.

The economics are similarly unresolved. A posted price is not revenue, and the dossier gives no gross margin, inference cost, burn, runway, or route to break-even. This matters because Almanac combines expensive model usage, 2,500 shared requests per user, browser operation, integration maintenance, and liability around actions that may transmit or delete customer data. Remove the next financing and there is no evidence yet that the $250 price supports the operating burden. The three-founder structure also leaves a specific governance question unanswered: who has final authority over product, hiring, and financing?

My 36-month base case is that Almanac either shuts down or pivots back toward a developer-focused knowledge assistant, where the team has its only demonstrated adoption. I am passing at this evidence level. The known-exception route for a generic enterprise AI application requires hard operating proof strong enough to override model commoditization and crowded financing; Almanac has none yet. One condition flips me: ten non-YC companies paying the published price, with at least 80% retained after six months and gross margins demonstrating that browser-based action is economically sustainable.
