# Halmos Labs -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is **scarce complements**. If AI research tools generate more viable experiments than qualified labs can run promptly, reliable access to those labs could become more valuable. Physical Validation launched in November to source external labs for AI research software. Halmos reports two physical tests with a December 10 readout scheduled. The launch and reported tests support the feasibility of arranging lab work. Scarcity, AI-driven demand, and Halmos’s ability to secure capacity remain unverified. The team’s drug-discovery and quantitative modeling backgrounds, together with the launch, support seed-stage execution credit. Delivery for an independent buyer remains open. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Founders & Team)

Halmos restricts catalogue redistribution and reviews supplier corrections, giving it some control over catalogue quality. The memo records no independent buyer commitments or repeat orders for Physical Validation. The unit of expansion is a fulfilled lab order. Project-specific quotes establish a pricing format, while Halmos’s fee, lab payout, coordination time, and rework costs remain unknown. (Defensibility; The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

The earlier Halmos Bio simulation offers limited support for a New Economies path. Halmos reports 30% fewer simulated evaluations to match a prior campaign’s best result. The memo leaves prior delivery labor costs, wet-lab cost per successful outcome, previously excluded buyers, and willingness to pay Halmos under model parity unmeasured. (Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Halmos could be a sourcing intermediary in an existing market. Science Exchange already combines supplier relationships, procurement, and an AI assistant. Medra and Ginkgo operate their own automated labs. Physical Validation relies on external providers and advises buyers to verify provider information directly. Catalogue controls offer limited leverage when lab access and fulfillment depend on suppliers. These alternatives make substitution credible. Whether they can match the capacity, turnaround, and quality Halmos can offer remains untested, so the scarce-complement path stays open at Watch. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Halmos fulfill a repeat order for an independent AI research team that pays Halmos for qualified lab access it cannot reliably obtain elsewhere? A completed repeat order, buyer confirmation of the alternatives it tried, and supplier terms showing Halmos can reserve comparable capacity again would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that the buyer can routinely secure equivalent capacity, turnaround, and quality through existing channels would close the path and move the action to Pass.
