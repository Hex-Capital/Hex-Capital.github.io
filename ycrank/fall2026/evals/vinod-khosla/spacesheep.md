# Spacesheep -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Spacesheep’s consequential opportunity is to remove human review as the bottleneck in agent-generated work. If agents produce ten times more material but people still inspect every line, generation has become cheaper while the scarce resource remains human attention. Interactive documents could change that equation. The dossier, however, supplies no baseline review cost, measured reduction, or demonstrated expansion in what teams can accomplish. I cannot price an extreme-outcome option from the existence of a better publishing surface alone.

Michael Makarov is the strongest reason to take the possibility seriously. He built internal deployment and the first CC Artifacts version at Anthropic, and his Pinecone experience maps directly to retrieval. This is unusually relevant experience for Spacesheep’s publishing-and-search problem. The strongest bull case is that someone who built the incumbent workflow understands its next bottleneck before outsiders do. But relevant experience does not establish the particular founder behavior I am scoring: material belief revision and recruiting complementary people against named risks. His acknowledgment that the launch video needed to demonstrate the product sooner is responsive; it does not document either capability. I keep that dimension at its missing-evidence default.

The product already offers multi-agent publishing, location-specific comments, versions, and semantic search. James Briggs reportedly uses it for plans, explanations, and reviews. Those are concrete signs of a usable product. The unresolved question is whether these features improve comprehension enough to make a separate workspace necessary. Notion’s July 2026 external-agent and interactive-HTML functionality makes that question urgent. Spacesheep supplies no comparative benchmark against Notion or Claude Artifacts. Chrome’s 22 users and Product Hunt’s live #4 position with 180 upvotes establish early exposure, without establishing repeat team dependence. My interface-differentiation red flag is relevant here, although the dossier does not establish that Spacesheep merely wraps a standard model.

I also invest in execution-heavy software and platforms that begin with small technical communities; a scientific breakthrough is not compulsory. Spacesheep could become the independent collaboration layer across competing agents. But the inferred organizational-context advantage remains unverified, and sessionpipe’s 14 stars do not yet demonstrate a committed platform community. The exception to demanding technical discontinuity would require stronger evidence of an instigator converting an enormous existing spending pool into a different business. Here, neither that consequence nor a proprietary learning loop is documented. Low launch counts are acceptable at pre-seed. They simply cannot establish the exception.

I would pass with my own money today. The cheapest decisive experiment is a controlled comparison of real team review work against the existing workspace; the dossier provides no such experiment, budget, threshold, or stop condition. Team pricing of $10 per member per month makes purchase accessible, but says nothing about delivered-work cost after model allowances, hosting, and support. I assign the missing-evidence defaults to both risk sequencing and economics, without assuming poor execution or subsidy dependence. **My flip condition is a paid-team trial demonstrating 10x more correctly reviewed agent output per human hour than the teams’ existing Notion or Claude Artifacts workflow, with positive contribution margin at the advertised price.** That would connect the product to a consequential bottleneck and give the next check a specific risk to remove.
