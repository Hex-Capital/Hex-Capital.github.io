# Yam -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic sale to a character-chat platform such as CHAI. Yam’s persistent cast and evolving relationships could give a buyer a romance game format it could distribute to an existing audience. Yam’s public catalog spans 112 fandoms and displays 2.4 million plays for its leading title. Yam reports one million beta play hours and more than three hours of average daily playtime per daily active user. These signals support testing whether the format can attract a durable paying audience. Buyer interest and the economics of owning the product remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, which requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3% pro rata. For both sale scenarios, assume a full cash acquisition within eight years, $5 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, and eligibility to sell the entire stake in a pro rata waterfall with no preference reducing the holder’s payout. Yam’s actual terms are unknown.

| Scenario | Assumed enterprise value | Distributable equity | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | $30 million | $25 million | $750,000 (1.5×) |
| Strong sale | $100 million | $95 million | $2.85 million (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private operation with cash reinvested | No sale or distribution | No cash distributed | $0 (0×) |

The strong scenario has a narrow cushion. At 2.5% ownership, the same deal pays about $2.38 million and misses the objective. At an illustrative 4× annual revenue multiple, a $100 million sale implies $25 million in annual revenue. Yam’s listed subscription and currency prices show how it can charge players, and the memo reports $5.5 million in annual platform revenue for adjacent romance-game platform Dorian. Yam’s realized revenue and a supportable acquisition multiple remain unknown. The memo supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The next operating milestone is repeat paid play in original or licensed worlds at positive contribution after inference and moderation costs. Yam has a live paid offering, a reported $125,000 YC investment, and an announced angel investment of undisclosed size. Those signals make a cohort test plausible, while runway and the capital needed to scale remain unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

An incumbent could add romance scenarios using available models and persistent-character tools. Yam’s most-played title is a fan game, and rights-holder takedowns can remove fan games from the catalog. If engagement depends on those titles, a buyer could acquire the company and still lose part of the audience that justified the price. Model costs and moderation also bear on the value of that audience. The memo leaves open whether Yam can carry paid engagement into transferable content, so neither a structural closing block nor a proceeds shortfall on the strongest route is established. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Yam show repeat paid engagement in original or licensed worlds at economics that could support a strategic sale above the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Several months of title-level paid retention, net revenue, and contribution after inference and moderation costs would answer it. Move from Watch to Reach out if those cohorts support a credible path to the required sale value. Move to Pass if the evidence shows that paying demand depends on content that cannot transfer and leaves the strongest sale case materially below the hurdle.
