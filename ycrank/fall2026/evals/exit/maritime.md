# Maritime -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Maritime’s strongest path. A cloud infrastructure provider serving AI-agent developers could buy Maritime’s scheduler and hosting stack to add persistent, per-customer agent fleets. Keiki’s documented use of Maritime microVMs shows the product runs a customer workload. Maritime reports 1,107 agents with 29 awake in one snapshot and describes a scheduler that resumes VMs without reading a full snapshot. Those signals support a production test of wake latency and cost per agent. The acquisition case depends on whether owning and integrating the stack would deliver a material advantage over building it or partnering. (Market & Traction; Defensibility)

A head-to-head test on a paying production fleet is a plausible pre-seed milestone. The announced pre-seed investment and Keiki deployment support a path to running that test. Funding amount, runway, test budget, and margins under realistic awake loads remain unknown. IP ownership and infrastructure contract transferability are also unverified. The memo supplies no usable acquisition valuation evidence or independent route to cash for this holder. (Market & Traction; Key Risks; Key Facts)

The illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-on checks. The eight-year, 5× objective requires $2.5 million in holder cash, or **$83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds** at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale scenarios below are assumptions for testing the hurdle. They assume zero net debt and other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. They also assume all consideration is paid in cash at closing, with no stock, escrow, earnout, rollover, or compensation, and a simple pro rata waterfall with no preference adjustment. Actual terms and buyer-supported prices are unknown.

| Scenario | Timing and outcome | Holder cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5, $50M enterprise value and equity cash | $1.5M | 3× |
| Strong sale | Year 7, $100M enterprise value and equity cash | $3.0M | 6× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. Shares remain held. | $0 | 0× |

The strong scenario clears the hurdle at 3% and yields only $2 million, or 4×, at 2%. There is no buyer evidence supporting the modeled price. (Key Facts)

### Countercase

Fly.io already offers idle sleep and persistent checkpoints, and Google Cloud offers isolated agent sandboxes with persistent workspaces. A buyer could replicate enough of Maritime’s functionality or obtain it through a commercial agreement. Keiki’s deployment establishes use, while buyer-specific savings and the premium for ownership remain unmeasured. The overlap supports Watch. The Pass override would require evidence that these substitutes cap credible proceeds below the hurdle or that a prolonged-private outcome with no credible liquidity route is probable. The memo establishes neither. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a buyer-validated benchmark on a paying production fleet show that owning Maritime yields enough incremental value over building or partnering to support a price above the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Measured wake latency and all-in cost per agent under realistic concurrent loads, translated into buyer economics that support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%, would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that available substitutes cap credible pricing below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
