# Vouch -- Elad Gil Evaluation

AI could make personalized advice affordable for Indian middle-class households; Vouch becomes a large company only if recurring revenue per household exceeds the cost of acquiring that household and delivering accountable advice. That is the controlling variable. Connecting fragmented finances creates usefulness, but usefulness does not establish an economic buyer. The dossier provides no pricing, service-cost breakdown, or demonstrated threshold at which the business works. I can formulate the market hypothesis, but I cannot give Vouch credit for validating it.

The December 2024 SEBI amendment establishes responsibility for AI-generated advice and client-data protection. It does not establish that advice becomes cheaper to deliver. Human experts could resolve difficult cases efficiently, or remain the principal cost of serving every customer. Neither outcome is documented. Meanwhile, Novelty Wealth already offers SEBI-registered AI advisory across portfolios, taxes, and goals, and CRED-owned Kuvera combines portfolio analysis with execution. This market is already crystallizing around overlapping capabilities. The estimated $2.3 billion wealth-platform market includes brokerage and distribution; it does not establish the revenue available to Vouch or an economic advantage from entering now.

The strongest argument against passing is Arnav Ghatiwala’s experience building Robinhood’s Advisor Network. That is unusually relevant experience for deciding where automation ends and human advice begins. His Jane Street work adds financial-operating context. A founder who understands those boundaries could discover a much better delivery model than the public description reveals. But this biography supplies neither documented prior startup learning nor costly personal commitment, the evidence my founder criterion actually rewards. It also does not establish the rare frontier-research capability that would justify overriding absent commercial pull.

Vouch’s proposed workflow has more potential than a financial question-answering interface: connected holdings, goals, investment selection, tax optimization, and expert involvement could create a recurring operating relationship. Better models could improve that relationship. However, the dossier does not verify integrations, execution permissions, retained planning state, or feedback from completed actions. I would therefore use the missing-evidence default for workflow durability. Competitor overlap raises the burden of differentiation without proving that Vouch is technically disposable. Similarly, the risk of retaining advisory-service costs deserves investigation; it is not evidence that Vouch already has poor margins.

I would pass with my own money today. The reported $500,000 YC investment funds exploration; it is not customer demand. The WhatsApp waitlist has no disclosed size, and the November 2026 launch remains licensing-dependent. Those are normal pre-seed circumstances, so I apply the stated absent-evidence defaults rather than treating them as failed adoption. My preference for investing across a technology discontinuity does not resolve the missing economic mechanism, and there is no demonstrated customer channel through which adjacent products could compound. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: **a licensed customer cohort showing repeat paid use and positive contribution after acquisition costs and all human advisory labor over six months**. That would directly test whether Vouch has changed the economics of wealth advice.
