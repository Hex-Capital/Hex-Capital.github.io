# Prodigy Research -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Specific knowledge is the entire case—and presently the central uncertainty. Prodigy says Michael Wang trained Gemini 3.1 Pro at Google DeepMind and generated billions in P&L as a Jane Street quant and licensed trader; Yuhua Wang reportedly built AI infrastructure serving billions of Apple users after scaling Salesforce systems to millions of daily queries. Those are almost perfectly matched capabilities. But every material credential comes from Prodigy’s own website. Without employment confirmation, a strategy artifact, or an independently attributable result, I cap founder evidence at strong self-description rather than mistake an extraordinary résumé for demonstrated action.

The product could possess a real substrate. A proprietary finance model deployed in live markets can accumulate trade outcomes, execution data, and market-impact observations unavailable to general-purpose models. That asset would survive someone copying the agent harness. Today, however, the feedback loop is inferred from two months of operation. The claimed victories over Claude Fable, GPT-5.6 Sol, and a 90th-percentile Jane Street trader use unpublished datasets and protocols. EquiLibre already trades billions of dollars daily, while Standard Signal and Numerai attack related territory. “Proprietary” identifies ownership, not defensibility; the missing fact is whether Prodigy has scarce data that improves the model faster than better-capitalized competitors can reproduce it.

The strongest bull case is that verification will arrive after the return stream, not before it. A delta-neutral system reporting 108% in two months, no down week, and performance against experienced human traders may be exactly the strange early signal that conventional diligence underweights. The founder-specific known exception could override an illegible wedge when rare recruiting or technical capability is independently demonstrated. But Prodigy has not yet earned that exception: there is no audited daily series, capital base, volatility, drawdown, or statistical significance. A two-month result can reveal skill, leverage, selection, or luck; the dossier cannot distinguish them.

The economics are direct but opaque. Trading company capital captures value without waiting for software pricing, and completed trades are more informative than an LOI. Yet 108% has no disclosed dollar denominator, so it does not establish durable profit, capacity, or revenue. The market is enormous in the abstract, but Prodigy names neither an initial external buyer nor an adjacent path into fund management, model licensing, execution infrastructure, or institutional software. Market breadth therefore receives no imagined credit.

My unstated-pattern test weakens the case further. Prodigy is internally operated: outside researchers do not contribute models, customers do not improve utility for one another, and no fragmented supply becomes permissionlessly productive. Nothing compounds while the team sleeps except the still-unproven proprietary data loop. There is no disclosed integrity problem, artificial deadline, or founder instability, but the fashionable-AI red flag is active: internal benchmarks and celebrity employers are standing where independently measurable adoption or audited performance should stand.

I pass with interest rather than reject the possibility. By August 2029, my base case is that Prodigy remains a small proprietary trading operation with less than $1 million in external software ARR, rather than becoming a broadly distributed finance-model company. One verifiable condition flips the decision: twelve audited months showing positive net returns on at least $10 million of capital, with disclosed drawdown and capacity, alongside evidence that proprietary deployment data causes out-of-sample model improvement. That would convert claims into specific knowledge and code into a compounding asset.
