# The Agentic Data Co. -- Paul Graham Evaluation

A thousand hours of recorded conversation is something the founders actually made. That matters. But my first question is what happened when making it became difficult. The dossier describes a collection app, separate speaker tracks, and human review. It does not describe a specific obstacle Christian or Matias encountered, the workaround they shipped, or how quickly it worked. I can credit the artifact without inventing the founder behavior behind it. Christian’s previous company and Matias’s operations experience make them worth meeting; they do not answer that question.

The technical evidence is the strongest part of The Agentic Data Co. Its released corpus contains **1,000.245 hours across 1,602 conversations and 239 speakers**. This is beyond a proposal. The team also trained a turn-taking model on a larger, 1,984-hour corpus and reports first place among 24 end-of-turn prediction entries in an October 17 leaderboard snapshot. Those results deserve a discount because they are company-reported and do not establish performance in noisy or mixed-channel environments. But the functioning collection system and released data remain substantial evidence even after that discount.

The strongest argument for investing is that these founders have already assembled a useful substrate before asking anyone to believe in a platform. That fits a pattern I like. Recruiting acquaintances, obtaining consent, separating audio, and reviewing conversations are tedious jobs that someone must do. Better conversational timing could make this data valuable to a small group of speech-model developers. The missing piece is why these particular founders discovered the need. Neither Christian’s engineering background nor Matias’s growth role establishes that they personally encountered it. The operational burden is concrete; the founder-originated insight is still undocumented.

Who wants it badly enough to come back? The **3,022 monthly Hugging Face sample downloads** suggest discovery, but they measure a sample repository, not repeated training use or commercial demand. Matias’s acknowledgment of customers adds a little evidence, with no names, usage, or contract details to establish its strength. Free approved access could be an effective way to recruit users. It could also attract researchers who never need a private license. David AI and Appen already offer overlapping services, so the decisive question is whether a buyer returns because this collection method produces something the alternatives cannot supply.

I would pass today. There is no documented dismissal of customer feedback, dependence on a major partner, or plan that stops at launch. There is also no documented evidence-driven route change; that remains unknown rather than evidence of rigidity. I considered the exception for unusually strong working products or validated proof. The released corpus makes that exception worth considering, but the company-reported benchmark and sample downloads do not yet replace the missing evidence of resourcefulness and urgent demand. **One customer case would change my decision:** a speech-model team documents a data requirement existing suppliers failed to meet, the founders deliver a working collection change within weeks, and that customer makes repeated paid purchases. That would connect the impressive artifact to both founder behavior and a narrow well of need.
