# Plover Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest path. A developer platform or enterprise code-review vendor could buy Plover to integrate its system for turning agent failures into repository-specific regression tests and merge checks. Ownership could shorten the buyer’s build and rollout time if Plover can transfer and reuse that capability across customers. Fieldguide reported 40% higher merged PR throughput in Forge’s first production quarter and 1.3–1.6× faster merges for Forge-authored PRs, with flat rollback rates and defect severity. Those results measure the combined Forge deployment. Plover separately claims that Fieldguide tripled PRs per engineer and that both Fieldguide and Envoy signed five-figure monthly contracts. The memo supplies no buyer-side valuation evidence. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is a second production deployment that measurably reduces engineer review time, maintains defect and rollback rates, and requires limited repository-specific work. Forge produced merge-ready patches on 37 of 40 retrospective tasks, giving Plover a baseline for a repeat test. The memo lists a $125,000 financing entry and leaves runway and rollout costs unverified. Ownership and portability terms for the repository-specific checks are also undisclosed. The memo supports no independent fallback that could pay this holder. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The illustrative seed check is $500,000 at a $10 million post-money valuation. With no follow-on and 40% relative dilution, ownership falls from 5% to 3%. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in holder cash, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. For both illustrative sales below, assume zero net debt, no other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, and a simple pro rata waterfall with no preference adjustments. Cash is paid at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each modeled at $0.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Investor cash | Gross multiple |
|----------------------|--------|---------------|----------------|
| Modest sale at $40 million enterprise value, yielding $40 million in distributable equity | Year 5 | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value, yielding $100 million in distributable equity | Year 7 | $3 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private ownership with growth plateauing, buyers staying away, public scale out of reach, and cash reinvested | Through year 8 | $0, with shares still held | 0.0× realized |

The modeled strong sale clears the hurdle at 3% and yields only $2 million at 2%. The sale prices are scenario assumptions. The memo gives no basis for estimating what a buyer would pay. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A platform buyer could assemble required merge checks and automated review with native tools, or license Plover if the product adds enough value. Fieldguide co-built its rollout and retained engineer approval after a patch passed component tests while missing a required integration. If each repository needs comparable customization, Plover’s results may support an enterprise software contract while offering little acquisition premium. The memo establishes overlap with native controls and leaves their adequacy as a substitute untested. That gap warrants Watch. Neither a binding price ceiling nor a probable prolonged-private outcome is established, so the Pass override does not apply. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a strategic buyer show that owning Plover’s transferable verification system creates enough incremental value over building or licensing it to support a price that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? A second production deployment with measured review savings, stable defect and rollback rates, and limited custom work would give a buyer a basis for that comparison. Buyer-supported economics that clear the hurdle would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that a readily available substitute delivers comparable results and caps the price materially below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
