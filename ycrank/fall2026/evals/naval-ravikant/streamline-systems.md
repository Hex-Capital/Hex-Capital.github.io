# Streamline Systems -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Streamline Systems has done something more informative than assembling an impressive résumé: the team reports flying without satellite positioning and handing control back to GPS. That is action in the exact problem. Poraj’s software background and Jessett’s RAF reserve affiliation provide context, but neither establishes navigation expertise by itself. The working system is the founder signal. Its strength remains limited by company-reported demonstrations without independently documented outcomes ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/streamline-systems)).

The investment turns on what is inside the module. If a competitor copied all the software tomorrow, what would remain? A proprietary sensing component could remain. An exclusive calibration process could remain. Neither is established here. ArduPilot, INAV and PX4 compatibility makes the product easier to adopt; it does not establish ownership of a scarce asset. The advertised absence of an RF signature is relevant to the mission, but without accuracy, drift, duration and operating-envelope results, I cannot distinguish a technical breakthrough from a useful integration. Hardware satisfies my interest in physical infrastructure only when the difficult part is demonstrably owned.

The strongest argument against passing is that public disclosure may lag genuine engineering progress. Streamline reports signed LOIs and scheduled government trials, and its recruitment listing describes flying hardware awaiting hardening and manufacturability work. That is a credible progression from experiment toward product, and it makes dismissing the team as merely credentialed a mistake ([recruitment listing](https://uk.linkedin.com/jobs/view/founding-electrical-engineer-at-streamline-systems-incorporated-4467858822)). My exceptions for unusual prototypes before commercial validation are relevant. But those exceptions require unusually convincing product or founder evidence; undisclosed architecture cannot supply that conviction by itself. LOIs establish reported interest, while undisclosed prices, counterparties and purchase commitments leave direct value capture at its default.

The market evidence also constrains the option. The cited estimate puts GPS-denied drone navigation at $154.38 million in 2025 and $386.10 million in 2035. That estimate is uncertain, but the dossier supplies no supported path into a substantially larger adjacent market ([market estimate](https://www.strategicrevenueinsights.com/thought-leadership/gps-denied-drone-navigation-systems-market)). Commercial and military UAV operators diversify buyers within the wedge. They do not yet establish expansion beyond it. Nor does another module buyer demonstrably improve navigation or distribution for existing buyers. My pattern of backing physical bottlenecks applies; my patterns of permissionless contribution and customer-created networks do not yet appear.

I pass at this evidence level. Nothing presented triggers an integrity or artificial-deadline veto, and a two-person pre-seed team need not already have revenue or production capacity. The specific issue is that Streamline Systems has demonstrated reported execution before demonstrating a durable asset or an expansive economic destination. My verifiable flip condition is a paid production design win with a named UAV manufacturer whose qualification report establishes a repeatable accuracy-per-dollar advantage attributable to a proprietary sensing component. That would change the evidence behind this decision.
