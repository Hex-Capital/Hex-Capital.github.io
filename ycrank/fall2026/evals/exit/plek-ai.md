# Plek AI -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Plek’s strongest exit route is a sale to a sponsor-backed workforce software platform. Main Capital Partners’ announced majority acquisition of Orgvue shows that a sponsor has pursued an adjacent asset. A platform could acquire Plek to add employee-level EBITDA attribution to its PE diligence workflow and sell that capability to existing customers. Plek reports that its analysis of a 3,600-person business matched an adviser’s six-week estimate of $30 million in potential annual EBITDA improvements. That comparison suggests the workflow could save diligence time. Realized savings, repeat demand, and a buyer’s willingness to pay remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

A concrete seed milestone is a paid expansion from the initial fund deployment to a second portfolio company, with the customer verifying realized savings against a baseline. Plek reports four completed pilots, a deployment at a $2 billion PE fund, and onboarding with an advisory firm. Those projects give Plek a starting point for the test. The memo gives conflicting funding figures and leaves runway and deployment costs open. Plek describes separate customer databases, which makes a sale of the software with limits on data transfer plausible. Rights to the research-derived technology, contract assignment, and permissions for workforce data remain unverified. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks, The Idea)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, dilution from 5% to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at full pro rata participation. Both sale scenarios assume $5 million in combined net debt and deal adjustments, no senior claims on the remaining equity, and an eligible holder selling the full stake for cash at closing within eight years. The sale values are illustrative. The Orgvue deal price was undisclosed, and the memo gives no Plek revenue figures or buyer valuation to anchor a price. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $45M enterprise value less $5M yields $40M in distributable equity | $1.2M (2.4×) |
| Strong sale | $105M enterprise value less $5M yields $100M in distributable equity | $3.0M (6.0×). If the holder can sell only 75% of the stake, $2.25M (4.5×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share purchase or distribution | $0 |

### Countercase

Orgvue already sells M&A workforce restructuring tools, and Reejig already maps work at the task level. A buyer could extend an existing product if Plek’s employee-level attribution proves easy to replicate. Errors in mapping employees to tasks could also undermine the savings estimates that make the product attractive. These risks weaken the rationale to buy, but the memo does not establish a ready substitute for Plek’s specific workflow, a structural closing barrier, or a credible sale price that falls short of the hurdle. The Pass gate is untriggered. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What price could a workforce software buyer justify after Plek demonstrates a paid expansion to a second portfolio company and customer-verified realized savings? Buyer-side economics that support at least $2.5 million in cash to the holder within eight years under plausible sale terms would move Watch to Reach out. A supported upper bound materially below that objective would move the action to Pass.
