# Dualizer -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Dualizer’s founders have done work that could produce specific knowledge. Chris Donahue worked at AVEVA and taught simulation certification; Nathan Liu researched controls and chemistry. More importantly, the team built something that won first place among **531 teams**, earning **$100,000 in September 2026** ([Texas A&M](https://mays.tamu.edu/news/2026/09/29/chemical-engineering-students-win-100k-ai-challenge/)). That independently corroborates execution beyond the biographies. It earns substantial founder credit. It does not establish industrial accuracy or customer demand.

The decisive question is what Dualizer owns after a competitor reproduces the conversational interface. KinetIQ’s deterministic engine and engineer-approved changes are a sensible architecture, but “proprietary” describes ownership, not an economic barrier. The website excerpts acknowledge synthetic calibration histories without measured-plant validation. Meanwhile, researchers have already demonstrated natural-language control of AVEVA through MCP ([research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11650)). Dualizer could possess valuable numerical methods; this dossier does not establish them. I give the missing durable asset its default score rather than assume the engineering is trivial.

The strongest bull case is that these founders understand where existing simulators fail and can turn customer deployments into a reusable industrial modeling asset. That fits my pattern of backing infrastructure bottlenecks. My exceptions for exceptional founders and working prototypes before public adoption also apply: revenue need not precede conviction. But the advertised deployment work involves proprietary customer data, custom unit models, and kinetics ([YC role](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/dualizer/jobs/DKxXnTw-forward-deployed-process-simulation-engineer)). Those inputs could remain isolated inside each customer. There is no demonstrated mechanism by which one deployment improves another customer’s product, nor evidence that Dualizer can retain and reuse the resulting knowledge. The bull case turns on that distinction.

There is an identifiable buyer and existing spend: process engineers already use simulation software, and the cited market estimate is **$1.77 billion for 2025**. That supports a commercial wedge, but the dossier does not establish a route into a much larger adjacent market. Nor does it establish direct value capture. A first paid industrial pilot was still a stated next step in September; the competition prize is financing, not customer revenue. Missing pricing and user-loop evidence receive their defaults. They are uncertainties appropriate to this stage, not proof of failure.

I am **Neutral** and would keep my money uncommitted today. The dossier supplies no documented integrity, artificial-deadline, or founder-instability red flag; the constraint is structural conviction. Strong domain builders could create a valuable engineering business here, but customer-specific work has not yet become a demonstrated compounding asset. My one flip condition is a paid second-customer deployment showing that Dualizer can legally reuse a first deployment’s calibrated model component, achieve measured-plant accuracy, and materially reduce implementation work. That would connect the founders’ specific knowledge to an advantage the next customer helps strengthen.
