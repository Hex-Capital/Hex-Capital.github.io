# Telamont -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I want a construction drawing revision to arrive with its consequences attached: which installed work becomes wrong, whose obligation changes, and what must happen before someone pours concrete. Telamont’s continuous monitoring points toward that world. But comparing documents, interpreting visual changes, and reliably connecting them to site decisions are separate steps. The dossier documents advertised capabilities without a completed example tracing that chain. I can imagine the destination; I cannot give Telamont credit for having demonstrated the road.

Why does a contractor discover a conflict only after doing the work? Telamont identifies a specific institutional failure: contracts, drawings, specifications, and subcontracts change separately, while people must reconcile their consequences manually. Source-cited discrepancies and notices drafted for human approval could return some control to the party otherwise left absorbing the mistake or pursuing payment afterward. The reported $1.4 million in avoided rework and $2–3 million recovered on subcontract packages make that mechanism economically concrete. Those are unnamed customer outcomes reported by the company, however, not verified savings or Telamont revenue. This is the strongest part of the bet, with an important limit on confidence.

The objection to passing is substantial. Charles Goulet brings construction exposure through his family and employers including EBC and RGI Electrical, alongside engineering experience at 1Password. William Gagnon brings industrial AI experience from Poka. Named customer logos, an Autodesk marketplace listing, and more than ten reported integrations suggest a product reaching actual workflows. My portfolio’s unglamorous workflow-repair pattern fits here. But the founder record establishes relevant experience rather than dated, independently pursued obsession before Telamont. Likewise, “billions” in covered project value and 300 client touchpoints do not establish repeated customer dependence. I would not turn either missing observation into an accusation; neither earns affirmative credit.

The decisive question is what Telamont comes to control. Its construction playbooks and professional feedback provide substance, but the dossier does not establish a scarce asset that other products must depend on. Document Crunch already offers project-level risk workflows, and Trimble can distribute overlapping capabilities inside existing systems. The concealed-infrastructure pattern would become compelling if expert corrections produced a demonstrably better understanding of project obligations; today that remains an inferred possibility. My exception for pre-revenue technical bets does not resolve this gap: the evidence here lacks both the documented lifelong building record and the demonstrated technical artifact that would justify overriding weak behavioral proof.

I pass with my own money today. External AI processing deserves scrutiny, but organizational isolation, Canadian document storage, exclusion from model training, and human approval are explicit safeguards; the dossier does not establish a severe privacy violation or dependence on one closed supplier. The issue is conviction in Telamont’s particular advantage. **One verifiable flip condition:** a customer-audited, six-month deployment showing contractors repeatedly acted on material cross-document conflicts that their existing review process missed, with correction history demonstrating that Telamont’s own playbooks drove the advantage. That would connect the useful workflow to something the team is actually accumulating.
