# Florin -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: robotics, space, advanced-energy, and defense companies need coverage that legacy class codes cannot price, while Florin believes external data and simulation can make them insurable without human underwriting. The controlling variable is not quote speed. It is whether Florin can maintain an acceptable loss ratio on these nonstandard risks. A sub-one-minute quote that attracts mispriced risk destroys value faster than week-long underwriting.

There is enough product substance to make the thesis falsifiable. Florin says it combines ACORD forms, satellite imagery, permits, public records, and social-media evidence, simulates tens of thousands of policy-year outcomes, and produces a bindable quote. This owns a consequential decision rather than merely summarizing documents, and better models could improve the workflow. But the dossier contains no actuarial backtest, policy count, reinsurance arrangement, coverage limit, or projected loss-ratio threshold. “Zero underwriters” is therefore an architectural claim, not evidence that the economic variable works.

The technology shift is real but already crystallizing. Corgi received regulatory approval for an AI-native carrier in 2025; Shepherd automates submission intake; Coterie already offers sub-one-minute binding; and four named competitors have collectively raised more than $800 million. This validates production readiness while weakening the claim that automation alone creates a differentiated opening. Florin is also targeting several risk classes at once, even though robotics, space, energy, and defense have different loss histories and regulatory constraints. Breadth before calibration compounds the hardest part of the business.

The strongest bull case is the team. Shaurya Aggarwal built TPUs and encryption deployed across more than 100,000 hosts; Amol Pant worked on perception, Autopilot compute, rover prototypes, and flight simulators; Aydin Sorensen reports scaling TerraFirma’s realized revenue from $500,000 to $30 million and previously selling Snacci. Those capabilities map unusually well to extracting risk signals from physical systems. Florin could become stronger with every policy and claim if it obtains sufficient capacity before competitors reproduce its data. The material problem is that none of the founders has disclosed actuarial, carrier-management, or insurance-regulatory experience, and the company’s banking-to-insurance pivot appears to have occurred only weeks before launch.

The closest exception would be Anduril: both pursue physical-technology categories underserved by legacy institutions and attempt to replace labor-heavy processes with product economics. The difference is decisive. Anduril entered a strategic vacuum; Florin faces heavily financed specialists and has not shown that automation changes insurance economics rather than merely accelerating intake. Its undisclosed licensing and capacity structure, use of potentially discrimination-sensitive public and social data, and absence of any customer or policy evidence prevent that exception from carrying the investment case.

I pass today. My base case is that the technical team eventually narrows Florin into underwriting infrastructure or an MGA for one specialty vertical rather than operating a broad, zero-underwriter carrier. One condition would flip the decision: a named, established reinsurer committing capacity for at least $100 million of annual written premium after independently validating Florin’s pricing model. That would turn the central claim from an AI workflow demonstration into evidence that a sophisticated risk counterparty believes the loss economics work.
