# Epsilab -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A, scarce complements.** Epsilab’s strongest case is that frontier labs will need more long-horizon training environments with reliable graders as their models take on longer tasks. The memo documents METR’s expansion of its long-duration test set. Epsilab says existing benchmarks sometimes set unattainable success criteria or misgrade outcomes, and says it builds resettable enterprise simulations using real workflows and practitioner input. A representative test showing that agents trained in these simulations perform better on held-out real work would establish bottleneck relief at seed. The memo reports no such result. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Epsilab reports working with unnamed frontier labs. That is a limited demand signal pending evidence that a lab committed research time, integration work, or budget. The founders bring relevant RL, language-model, and infrastructure experience, and the research role describes concrete checks for reward hacking and generalization. The memo describes those checks as planned work. Epsilab’s San Francisco location and recent Fall 2026 launch make seed-stage assessment appropriate. (Epsilab, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

The expansion unit is one additional qualified environment. **Scenario assumption:** If Epsilab can reuse workflow data and validated graders across environments, the next delivery could require fewer engineering and practitioner hours. Pricing, reuse rates, and incremental delivery costs are unknown. Path B remains unproven because the memo shows neither a newly viable transaction opened by lower delivery labor nor a reason buyers would keep paying Epsilab under model parity. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

With abundant capital and equally capable models, labs could use open-source rollout and grading tools, build their own evaluations, or buy from competing environment suppliers. The memo documents OpenAI building GDPval and identifies suppliers already offering agent environments. Epsilab claims access to practitioners and real workflow data, but the memo establishes no durable rights to those inputs. Epsilab could therefore face bespoke production costs while labs retain credible alternatives. Simulation gains could also disappear on real work. The alternatives establish a credible substitution risk, while equivalent performance and easy switching remain unproven. That leaves the Path A override untriggered and supports Watch at low confidence. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will a frontier lab place a paid follow-on order after an equal-budget comparison shows that Epsilab’s environment yields materially better performance on held-out real workflows than the lab’s best available alternative? The qualifying result would include a lab-verified improvement, a paid order for a second environment, and enforceable reuse rights to the workflow inputs that explain the advantage. That would support Reach out if the updated evaluation clears the score and confidence gates. Comparable real-work results from a readily available substitute at similar delivered cost would move the case to Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
