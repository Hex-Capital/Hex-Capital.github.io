# Petrichor -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The secret worth testing is whether scientist-executed experiments, with failures and intermediate actions preserved, produce better biological training data per dollar than automated laboratories. Petrichor describes photographs, raw instrument files, and timestamped execution records that could support that proposition. But this is my reconstruction of a possible secret: the dossier supplies neither named peers rejecting it nor a comparative result. Equivalent model improvement from ordinary laboratory outputs would undermine the claim. A different workflow alone does not establish a different technological future.

The founders provide the strongest affirmative evidence. Wilhelm Hedenskog, Jesper Johansson, and Elias Reinfeldt were high-school classmates and previously worked together under Qokedas. That is meaningful evidence of a relationship preceding the current opportunity. Their transition from instrument data to experimental execution also supplies continuity of purpose. Full-time status, ownership, and responsibility for essential product work remain undocumented; I cannot award those points. Equally, there is no evidence of convenience matching, part-time founders, or investor-dominated ownership. The founding structure deserves investigation.

What would Petrichor own if its customers succeeded? Frontier AI laboratories are an identifiable initial buyer group, but the service has no demonstrated monopoly mechanism. Customer ownership and the prohibition on shared-data training specifically prevent me from assuming that commissioned experiments accumulate into a proprietary training corpus. Execution expertise could still compound, but the dossier provides no measured advantage or retention evidence. ECL and Strateos offer overlapping remote experimentation capabilities; their existence establishes alternatives, although it does not prove Petrichor is interchangeable with them. The decisive uncertainty is whether each experiment strengthens a position Petrichor can keep.

The strongest bull case is that the human execution layer is precisely what makes the business possible. Scientists may accommodate changing protocols before automation becomes economical, while granular records make their work useful to models. Petrichor consequently fits my unstated interest in the physical infrastructure behind AI. Its earlier instrument-data work gives that ambition some substance. Yet scientist labor could also make capacity expand roughly with staffing. My known exceptions require technical proof, persistent customer behavior, or a concrete deployment path strong enough to overcome the relevant risk. None is documented here. Conversely, the dossier does not establish the simultaneous licensing, manufacturing, and financing dependencies that would justify declaring a fatal coordination failure.

The commercial route is at least specific: the website addresses frontier laboratories and directs inquiries to the CEO. That earns modest distribution credit, but no repeatable acquisition process is demonstrated. Commissioned experiments suggest one coherent revenue engine; the model remains inferred, and prices, margins, and customer expansion are unknown. I therefore use the stated default for outcome magnitude. Fall 2026 YC participation and October 11 founder coverage establish visibility, not customer demand. Missing pre-seed revenue is ordinary; neither visibility nor the scale of AI spending supplies the missing business arithmetic.

I pass on writing a check today. Petrichor has a credible founding relationship and a potentially consequential experiment to run, but insufficient evidence that supplying valuable data produces durable value capture. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: a named frontier laboratory renews a paid engagement after a controlled comparison demonstrates superior model improvement per dollar from Petrichor’s granular records versus its existing experimentation provider. That would connect the proposed secret to actual purchasing behavior.
