# Topos -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Topos is attacking a consequential bottleneck: how much scientific reasoning can we trust without consuming another expert’s time or running another experiment? Making that expertise radically cheaper could matter enormously. But this dossier does not establish which biological workflow changes, its present cost, or how much of it becomes computationally verifiable. I cannot price the upside from “biology” alone. The consequence is plausible; its magnitude remains unmeasured.

The decisive technical distinction is between agents agreeing and an independent checker establishing correctness. Topos describes agents formalizing problems and checking one another, but discloses neither a checker specification nor a benchmark. A proof can establish that a conclusion follows from biological assumptions while those assumptions remain wrong. The company must identify precisely where its guarantee ends. Harmonic’s reported 96.8% result on VERINA code-verification problems establishes progress in an adjacent domain; it supplies no evidence that Topos has crossed the biological boundary.

The founders give me a reason to investigate. Ryan Min June Shin has Harvard mathematics and Dana-Farber experience; Sohum Sukhatankar combines mathematics/computing with seizure-localization research at Boston Children’s Hospital. Their joint computational prime-progression project is evidence of working together on mathematical problems. That is more relevant than their follower counts or YC admission. The strongest bull case is that this combination lets them formalize a narrow biological workflow that general scientific-agent vendors cannot reliably handle. It fits my preference for scarce scientific work becoming a repeatable production platform. But the dossier documents neither that platform nor a proprietary experimental learning loop, and it contains no observed belief revision or recruiting against a named technical weakness.

Topos’s missing commercial history is normal for a two-person Fall 2026 company. I am not subtracting points for undisclosed revenue, customers, or hiring. I am withholding affirmative credit where the investment mechanism remains unspecified. There is no disclosed experiment with a pass/fail threshold, budget, or stop condition, and no model comparing inference, formalization, and expert-review costs with the existing workflow. Edison already covers literature, analysis, and experimental planning. A separate verification layer must remove enough expensive work to justify entering that workflow; additional checking could otherwise add cost.

I pass with my own money today. My exceptions permit investing before a product or business model when a documented instigator and an enormous consequence make the option compelling. Topos’s university departures and relevant research experience warrant attention, but do not establish that override. Nor does the dossier establish the opposite: there is no evidence here of liquidity-first founders, refusal to learn, or a commodity wrapper. My single reconsideration trigger is an independently replicated, held-out benchmark on one named biological workflow showing at least 10x lower fully loaded verification cost than expert review at matched error-detection performance, with empirical assumptions explicitly separated from machine-checked conclusions. That would turn the central promise into evidence worth underwriting.
