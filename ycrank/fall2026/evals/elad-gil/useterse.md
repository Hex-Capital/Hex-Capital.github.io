# Terse -- Elad Gil Evaluation

AI agents make long-running, concurrent applications more common; developers now need durable coordination, and Terse becomes a large business only if those developers adopt an independent state layer that can capture meaningful hosting spend. That last step controls the investment. Cloudflare, Rivet, and Restate already address overlapping requirements. Self-hosting and a different programming interface establish product choice, but the dossier does not identify a measurable condition under which Terse wins that choice repeatedly. I would pass on a personal check today.

The timing has substance. Restate’s September 30, 2026 announcement describes agents bringing retries, callbacks, and human approvals into mainstream application development. Terse’s serial request handling, actor-owned SQLite storage, and generated Python and TypeScript clients address that complexity directly. But the economic step change remains unmeasured: there is no comparison showing engineering time saved, operating costs reduced, or deployments enabled relative to existing approaches. Growing infrastructure demand can support several useful projects while concentrating commercial value in platforms that already own distribution.

The strongest case for Terse is that better models should increase demand for its underlying function. More capable agents can take more actions over longer periods; they still need persistent identities and coordinated state. The runtime implements those functions rather than merely describing a future integration. That fits my pattern of investing across the infrastructure supporting a technology shift. However, applications accumulating state does not automatically mean Terse captures the customer relationship, particularly with an MIT-licensed, self-hostable implementation. The October 26 release also documents socket ordering and delivery limitations, while nested mutations require explicit persistence discipline. These are concrete constraints on how much operational responsibility the runtime currently absorbs.

The founders make the opposing case harder to dismiss. Thomas Karatzas previously founded a company acquired by RateMyInvestor and spent seven years working on Siri and Apple Intelligence; Olivier Simard-Morissette brings backend experience from Pigeon, Squarespace, and The New York Times. The prior acquisition earns credit for having gone through startup mechanics, without assuming its size or inventing a motivation story. Together with the working runtime, this is a credible team for the problem. But 256 GitHub stars, 19 forks, and a Show HN launch with 53 points establish developer attention, not repeated production reliance. The dossier supplies no evidence of failed adoption either; product-market pull remains unestablished.

Terse clears the concern that a stronger model simply reproduces its feature. Its unresolved issue is commercial ownership in an already competitive infrastructure category. The research-stage exception does not resolve that issue: this dossier establishes relevant engineering experience, rather than frontier research or a scarce technical capability that justifies overriding absent demand. My verifiable flip condition is a cohort of five independent production customers renewing a paid Terse hosting service after 90 days. That would begin to connect the promising technical asset to the controlling market belief: developers want Terse itself operating their durable state.
