# Markov -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Markov’s 10,000 hours of computer-use recordings reveal a schlep-rich, origin-poor startup. Markov has already done an unreasonable amount of unpleasant work, but the dossier never says Harish Ashok or Dev Mandal built Markov to solve a problem either founder personally suffered. That pairing is Markov’s signature: strong animal tracks around data collection, with a weaker organic-idea story underneath them.

**Scale AI** is the high rung because collecting human-generated training data becomes an operational machine disguised as software. Markov shares that mechanism: 48,478 videos, roughly 12,300 hours, and workflows spanning Salesforce, AutoCAD, Blender, Photoshop, Excel, and VS Code. The divergence is load-bearing. Scale converted the schlep into customer revenue, while Markov released Computer Use Large under CC-BY-4.0 and has disclosed no paying contract. Markov has proved willingness to do the work, not yet ownership of its economic output.

**Loopt** is the low rung because weak product timing was carried by Sam Altman’s force of personality rather than an obviously organic founder pain. That was a known exception, and Markov does not yet show the exception’s override signal. Dev Mandal worked at Sarvam AI and Harish Ashok built robotics and hardware workflows, so Markov is adjacent to the problem. But adjacency is not the same as Drew Houston repeatedly losing his USB stick. Markov’s dossier says “experience with human-computer interaction,” not “we needed these trajectories ourselves and nothing adequate existed.”

Markov’s 48,478-video release is the strongest animal evidence in the dossier. Two founders assembled more than 10,000 hours of recordings and reached 45,000 downloads in the first month; animals leave tracks, and these are tracks. Over the next 18 months this resourcefulness compounds only if Markov turns dataset production into a repeatable loop—recruit contributors, clean sensitive screens, annotate actions, deliver environments, then use customer failures to design the next collection. If Markov merely publishes another large corpus, the mechanism compounds reputation rather than revenue.

Dev Mandal’s doubt.ai reached 700 users and resolved 5,000 questions in four weeks, while Harish Ashok previously built Zenith. Those are shipped artifacts, but the unstated hacker-genealogy test remains mixed: Harish’s visible GitHub repositories have two stars each, and no public repositories were found for Dev. Markov would move up a full tier here if diligence uncovered a non-trivial capture, synchronization, or RL-environment system the founders personally built before YC—especially code or technical work with users predating Markov. Without it, Markov looks technically capable but not yet like the Collisons or Aaron Swartz, whose hacker trails existed before anyone called them founders.

**Stripe** is the useful single-anchor parallel for founder configuration: two founders with complementary ability can move extraordinarily fast when the relationship predates the startup and the problem. Markov has the correct count—Harish Ashok and Dev Mandal—but no public evidence about how long Markov’s founders have worked together or whether they survived a hard project together. I will not manufacture a red flag from missing biography, but I also will not award Markov the Collison-brothers advantage merely because the YC page lists two names.

Markov’s 150,000-plus Hugging Face downloads lead to a falsifiable prediction: by July 2029, Markov will reach $8 million to $15 million in annual recurring revenue selling custom GUI-trajectory datasets and evaluation environments to frontier-model developers, rather than becoming a general developer-software platform. Markov’s mechanism is the schlep itself: collecting rights-cleared human workflows, handling privacy, and reproducing failures across desktop applications faster than laboratories want to build those operations internally.

Computer Use Large’s CC-BY-4.0 license, the absence of disclosed contributor-consent procedures, and zero named paying customers form the strongest bear case. Scale AI, Turing, and Surge AI can all copy the category, and Turing has already delivered more than 10,000 supervised GUI tasks; Markov could become a free-data project feeding better-capitalized vendors. The evidence that keeps me on the invest side is that Markov’s two founders already produced 12,300 hours and 150,000 downloads before showing a revenue machine—the sort of disproportionate artifact I trust more than a deck. I would flip to Pass if diligence cannot verify commercially usable contributor rights and sensitive-information filtering for Markov’s 10,000-plus-hour corpus, because then the schlep is not an asset. It is a liability.
