# Native -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Native’s founders wound down Wepost and started again in January 2026. That is the episode I would want to understand. Wepost had reported NOK80,000 MRR in September 2025, so they abandoned something that people were already paying for. But the dossier doesn’t establish what broke, what they learned, or why Native was the answer. Changing companies can reflect resourcefulness or impatience. Without the observation that forced the change, I give neither resourcefulness nor adaptability more than its missing-evidence default.

The strongest evidence comes afterward: Native reports $2M ARR within six months and more than 2,200 paying customers ([YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Vdd-native-artificial-intuition-for-marketing), [About](https://native.no/about-us)). Those figures deserve attention. At the advertised $79 monthly price, 2,200 subscriptions would produce roughly $2.1M annualized revenue. The figures are broadly compatible, although discounts and enterprise contracts prevent that calculation from verifying either. Small businesses are paying to have marketing work taken away. That is a much better starting point than businesses saying they would like better AI. I score demand highly, with room left for evidence that customers keep paying.

Native has also shipped a functioning system. Its reported 40,000 published posts in one month provide evidence of operational output, independently of the customer count. But there are two technical claims here: that Native can produce and publish content, and that Norn can choose unusually effective ideas. The first has substantial support. The second remains provisional. Briggs on Homes achieved 5.5% engagement on Native posts versus 2.9% on its own posts, but that comparison doesn’t isolate Norn’s contribution. Native’s research also acknowledges selection and survivorship biases ([Native](https://native.no/), [Research](https://native.no/research/the-taste-layer)). The technical score rewards the deployed product while leaving the claimed predictive advantage unproved.

The uncomfortable bear case is that customers are buying inexpensive relief from a chore, and Native mistakes that demand for evidence of a proprietary intelligence layer. Blaze already offers a similar strategy, creation, publishing, and learning workflow. Collecting approval signals establishes a training mechanism; it doesn’t establish that the resulting predictions beat competitors. This matters to my preference for concrete technical proof before platform claims. Native’s proposed developer interface is still an API waitlist, so it supplies no evidence of developers building around a new primitive. The existing business could succeed while that larger thesis fails.

The founders nevertheless have relevant experience: Bekken produced content, Hauga operated a marketing company, and Gråsberg worked in AI/ML. They also built Wepost together, which answers some concern about an untested cofounder relationship. Nothing here establishes the launch-dependent distribution or premature delegation that would make me actively negative. But prior work in a field doesn’t establish the specific personal frustration that produced this product, or an ugly barrier the founders have already mastered. I leave the founder-origin dimension at its default rather than invent that story.

I would keep talking, but I would not write a check on this dossier. Native falls short of my founder-action gate. Its paid adoption makes an exception worth considering, but the documented exceptions require compelling proof strong enough to replace the missing founder evidence; aggregate company-reported revenue without customer persistence doesn’t quite do that here. My one flip condition is a dated, customer-corroborated account of a specific failure the founders overcame with a shipped change within weeks, followed by those affected customers continuing to pay. That would connect the impressive commercial result to the behavior I am actually trying to buy.
