# Viraj Aero -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** AI data center developers have begun procuring on-site gas generation to bridge grid delays, and some sites also face cooling-water constraints. Viraj Aero’s GENA is designed to supply on-site power and recover usable cooling water from the same turbine exhaust. The investment case is strongest where both constraints bind and recovering water advances the date a developer can bring compute online. Cheap frontier models would leave the developer dependent on qualified physical equipment and site integration. Viraj could capture a premium if its system solves both constraints better than the available alternatives. The company reports that its first power-and-water unit is under construction, so that advantage remains unverified. The memo supplies no separate evidence of a newly viable transaction under Path B. *(The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)*

Viraj reports more than 1.3 GW of interest under letters of intent, with terms and conversion undisclosed. It also reports one paying engine manufacturer, with the scope and payment undisclosed. These are early demand signals, with no confirmed GENA purchase by a data center buyer. The aviation ground test and subsequent aircraft integration show relevant prototype execution. The team also brings power-project and turbine-production experience, while continuous-duty site qualification remains ahead. The seed stage fits the strategy. The memo lists a San Francisco office and a team in Toulouse, leaving U.S. investment fit open. *(Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)*

The proposed expansion unit is a 10 MW module. **Illustrative scenario:** At 80% utilization and the published 45% electrical efficiency, one module would deliver roughly 70 GWh of electricity per year on 156 GWh of fuel energy, assuming rated output and efficiency during operating hours. The economic test is whether usable recovered water and any earlier start date justify the installed and operating cost relative to separate power and cooling. The memo gives no measured water yield, installed cost, or deployment throughput to test that bridge. *(The Idea, Key Risks)*

### Countercase

With abundant capital, competing turbine and engine suppliers could add on-site generation while developers address cooling separately. If that configuration reaches the same operating date at lower cost, Viraj’s combined system loses its premium. Viraj reports a condenser patent, but the memo leaves its scope and susceptibility to design-arounds open. Gas access and air permitting could also erase the schedule advantage. The memo identifies credible power substitutes and leaves their equivalence at a site constrained by both power and water unresolved. That keeps Path A open for a test and leaves the outreach gate unmet. *(Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)*

### Decisive diligence question

Can Viraj pass an independent, buyer-funded continuous-duty qualification at a U.S. data center constrained by both power and cooling water? The buyer should verify net power, usable water yield, efficiency, emissions, and uptime, then compare installed and operating costs and time to service against its best feasible separate power and cooling option. A verified advantage that converts a conditional commitment into a firm order at a margin Viraj can credibly protect would support **Reach out** if the updated case clears the score and mandate gates. If the best alternative matches the result and the buyer declines to pay for Viraj’s combined system, move to **Pass**. *(The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)*
