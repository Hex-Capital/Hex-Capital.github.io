# Nebula Security -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

$400,000 in Google bug-bounty rewards makes Nebula Security a missionary security operation wearing a commercially unproven AI-software wrapper. Nebula Security’s highest ladder placement is founder conviction: Eten Zou, Yuan Tan, Frank Wu, and Xiaochuan Yu accumulated 1,162 validated findings, 98 public CVEs, and multiple DEF CON and Black Hat credentials before anyone needed a YC pitch. Nebula Security’s lowest placement is round mispricing because no valuation, round size, investor demand, or visible pass is disclosed. That pairing matters: exceptional illegible researchers can build a valuable asset while still offering me no evidence that the SAFE is a valuable security.

**Drop.io** is the high rung for asymmetric conviction: years spent building a dismissed primitive against free incumbents made the pre-pitch behavior inseparable from the founder thesis. Nebula Security shares that mechanism because Eten Zou’s kernel research, Yuan Tan’s Linux contributions, and the founders’ $400,000 in bounty earnings existed before VEGA’s enterprise private beta. Nebula Security sits just below Drop.io rather than on the same rung because the founders have demonstrated obsession with finding vulnerabilities, not yet an opinionated commercial belief about why researcher-validated repository scanning becomes the durable system of record. The rizz is real; the business tizz remains mostly implied.

**Robinhood’s** later consensus markup is the low-rung failure mechanism on round discipline: a great outcome can still become a bad new check once access replaces underwriting. Nebula Security sits between Sublime Security’s pre-consensus enterprise-security entry and Robinhood’s consensus phase, but closer to the latter risk: AI security is already occupied by OpenAI’s Codex Security, XBOW’s $120 million financing, Semgrep’s $204 million capitalization, Gecko, and ZeroPath. Nebula Security provides no round-dynamics evidence — applying absent-evidence default — and no operator-network access or demonstrably early price comparable to the exceptions that justified Pinterest or Robinhood. “AI security engineer” is exactly the sort of 2026 YC label capable of turning a differentiated research group into an undifferentiated auction.

Nebula Security’s pay-as-you-go Researcher plan could make the capital structure better over the next 18 months, because per-repository subscriptions can finance usage before enterprise sales mature. Nebula Security’s human-reviewed reports and human-written patches, however, create a regatta with an awkward first tack: every additional scan may increase both revenue and expert labor unless VEGA’s automation improves faster than demand. That is potentially a good Wizard-of-Oz sequence, but without revenue, customer counts, or reviewer-efficiency data, I cannot tell whether Nebula Security is default-alive software or a high-end security consultancy being force-fit into a SaaS cap table.

Nebula Security’s 1,186-star exploit repository and 6,000 X followers are meaningful owned distribution, but Nebula Security has not yet converted technical reputation into a founder-as-brand asset comparable to a creator business. The tier-flipping counterfactual is straightforward: if Eten Zou’s audience or the CyberMeowfia community produces repeat paid repository adoption without demo-led acquisition, the community belongs on the cap table as an economic asset. Today, 109 LinkedIn followers, an enterprise private beta, and no disclosed paying customers place Nebula Security above a zero-distribution enterprise startup but below a John Fish-style business whose audience already supplies demand.

**Sublime Security** is the useful single-anchor parallel for category fit: both Sublime and Nebula Security turn deep security judgment into enterprise software before the category’s next consolidation wave. Nebula Security earns credit for exploit-focused researchers, 98 CVEs, and proofs of concept rather than merely wrapping an LLM around a scanner. Nebula Security still lives uncomfortably close to the sleeve I avoid because OpenAI, Gecko, ZeroPath, XBOW, and Semgrep overlap the workflow, while no proprietary customer-derived dataset yet compounds. Security fits; foundation-model-dependent application software does not automatically fit.

Semgrep will acquire Nebula Security for less than $100 million by July 2029 after Nebula Security reaches roughly $3 million to $6 million in ARR, primarily to buy the exploit researchers, validation corpus, and VEGA workflow rather than a dominant standalone distribution channel. Nebula Security’s founder-conviction advantage should create technically credible findings and a small base of serious customers, while weak round legibility, human-review throughput, and platform commoditization should prevent the independent curve from escaping. That is a respectable operating outcome and potentially a mediocre seed return depending entirely on today’s undisclosed price.

**Ro** is the exception that makes the bull case sting: apparently inefficient human and regulatory operations can become the moat when competitors cannot clear the operational schleps. Nebula Security’s human validation could similarly turn 1,162 findings, eight Chrome zero-days, and DARPA-level repair expertise into a proprietary vulnerability engine that OpenAI cannot reproduce from models alone; Nebula Security could then become the next Sublime rather than Semgrep’s acquisition target. The dossier still says no customer-derived compounding advantage exists, no revenue is disclosed, and every report remains human-reviewed, so expertise currently scales as labor more clearly than as software. I would flip to Invest if Nebula Security documents $1 million ARR across at least 20 paying repositories while human review falls below 10% of findings.
