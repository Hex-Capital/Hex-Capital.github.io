# Cesara -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Cesara’s strongest path. A revenue-cycle platform could buy its claim-pricing and clause-backed appeals workflow to offer health systems more recoveries through an existing distribution channel. The memo records a public launch and demo. Cesara’s CEO reports that pilots identified over 90% of valid underpayments and recovered over 70% of those identified. Customer names, sample sizes, and recovered dollars are undisclosed, so the results leave the buyer’s economic benefit unquantified. Ownership could be worth paying for if Cesara’s integrations and workflow give a buyer a measurable advantage over building or licensing the capability. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*)

The operating milestone is repeatable paid production use across multiple health systems, with measured incremental dollars recovered and implementation costs. The service-line pilots give Cesara a way to test that milestone. Broader deployment requires reliable contract and claims feeds, security review, and business associate agreements. Cesara says it signs those agreements. The memo leaves assignment and continued data access after a sale unresolved. Conflicting financing records and undisclosed deployment costs leave runway and additional capital needs uncertain. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

A sponsor buyout lacks evidence of predictable cash generation after reinvestment. A secondary lacks evidence of demand for eligible shares. An IPO lacks evidence of public-market scale or readiness. A dividend or buyback lacks evidence of excess cash available to holders. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

**Illustrative proceeds.** With actual holder terms undisclosed, assume a $500,000 investment at a $10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million gross before investor taxes. At 3%, that requires $83.3 million of distributable equity, or $88.3 million of enterprise value assuming $5 million of net debt. At 2% and 4% ownership, the equity hurdles are $125 million and $62.5 million, respectively. The memo supplies no basis to estimate transaction fees or senior claims.

| Eight-year scenario | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---:|
| Modest sale at $25 million enterprise value, less $5 million illustrative net debt | $600,000 (1.2×) |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value, less $5 million illustrative net debt | $2.85 million (5.7×) |
| Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and Cesara reinvests available cash without a secondary or payout | $0 realized, with shares retained |

These scenarios show the required economics. Cesara’s disclosed pilot results leave the realizable sale price open. In a wind-down, shareholder cash would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo leaves unquantified. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Adonis and Trek Health already monitor reimbursement variances. An incumbent could extend that workflow to clause-backed appeals or license the capability, especially if Cesara’s deployments remain costly and its incremental recoveries remain unverified. The memo documents workflow overlap and leaves switching costs unproven. That supports Watch. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the evidence leaves both transferability and achievable holder proceeds unresolved. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Could a revenue-cycle platform justify paying enough to clear the holder’s cash target based on the incremental value of owning Cesara’s workflow? Customer-verified recovery dollars and implementation costs from repeat paid deployments, tied to buyer economics that support a sale around or above the modeled hurdle, would move the action to Reach out.
