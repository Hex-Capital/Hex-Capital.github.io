# Applied Kinetics -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Applied Kinetics is selling access to manufacturing capacity. The investment question is whether that access is scarce enough to own, and whether my check buys evidence of it. Proposed post-money: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. Whether this is the first genuinely scarce check: unknown. I will not infer an auction from YC membership, but I cannot underwrite cheap entry without a price. The dossier also names no capital-funded experiment, budget, or deadline. Those are missing underwriting inputs, not evidence that the founders are running a financing factory.

The strongest bull case is physical and specific: developers facing equipment delays could pay for someone who gets a compliant transformer built, tested, and delivered faster. Applied Kinetics claims seven-week production against up to 65 weeks on the open market, supported by access to 40-plus partner plants. That fits my pattern of backing software that reorganizes difficult physical operations. A company can create considerable value by translating between developers’ specifications and factories’ capabilities. It does not need to invent a transformer to deserve investment.

But whose asset is this network? The reported 10-plus GW belongs to the manufacturing base’s history, not Applied Kinetics’ deliveries. No named completed shipment establishes the seven-week claim, and the advertised ranges remain nonbinding until specifications and approvals are settled. Forty factories could represent valuable relationships or a supplier directory available to the next intermediary. There is no documented purchase, repeat usage, exclusive capacity, or contractual access that resolves that distinction. The 253 LinkedIn followers do not help. The potential dark pool is identifiable; its economic ownership is unproven.

Rohit’s GPU telemetry work at Crusoe and Adhik’s AI-agent development at IBM provide relevant software experience. Their shared Georgia Tech background and prior hackathon collaboration establish that they have worked together. Neither establishes operating receipts in factory qualification, electrical-equipment acceptance, customs, or warranty service. The CEO and CTO roles give no basis to allege an equal-founder decision committee. Likewise, the dossier does not claim that agent errors disappear at scale. The concern is narrower: Applied Kinetics proposes to manage consequential physical obligations, while the documented founder experience covers only part of that bridge.

Delete the next financing. Can customer deposits fund production through acceptance, freight, and service, leaving cash after failures and rework? Project quotations and separately licensable agents offer possible revenue mechanisms, but the dossier supplies no payment terms, margins, burn, or working-capital cycle. My exceptions for expensive rounds or AI-heavy businesses require demonstrated conversion, retention, cash economics, or unusually strong external validation; none is documented here. I would pass today. My verifiable flip condition is a documented transaction pairing a sub-$10 million post-money round with one named customer’s transformer order: proceeds fund a dated delivery-and-acceptance test, and contracted payments cover the full cash cost through acceptance. That would establish what the money proves and whether this business can begin financing itself.
