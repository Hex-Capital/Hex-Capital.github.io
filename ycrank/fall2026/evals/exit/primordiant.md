# Primordiant -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a lab instrument or automation vendor. Bruker’s investment in Atinary shows strategic interest in instrument-linked automation, with no disclosed price. Primordiant says its robots can adapt experiments as results come in. A vendor could buy that capability to make its installed instruments more useful across changing protocols, provided Primordiant can demonstrate repeatable performance and savings across labs. [The Idea, Market & Traction]

Primordiant reports two client engagements, including a wet-lab deployment after building its first robot in under three weeks. That supports a plausible next milestone of paid deployments that reproduce autonomous results across labs and quantify savings after integration costs. Published pricing shows billing disabled during beta, and the memo leaves the capital and timeline for that milestone unverified. An acquisition of the core robotics and software appears feasible in principle. Ownership and assignment rights remain unverified, and any value attributed to training on customer content depends on opt-in or written agreement. [Defensibility, Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks]

**Proceeds scenarios.** The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity. Both sale scenarios assume $10 million in net debt and deal adjustments combined, a pro rata waterfall with no senior preference diversion, and full eligibility to sell the entire stake for cash by year eight.

- **Modest sale.** $50 million enterprise value yields $40 million distributable equity and $1.2 million to the holder, or 2.4×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million enterprise value yields $90 million distributable equity and $2.7 million to the holder, or 5.4×. At 2.5% ownership, the same sale yields $2.25 million, or 4.5×.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested yields zero realized proceeds in the scenario.

The sale values are illustrative. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction pricing that supports either value, and it evidences no independent liquidity route. The proceeds and independent alternatives scores use their absent-evidence defaults. [Market & Traction, Key Facts]

### Countercase

Instrument vendors can partner for AI-enabled automation. Bruker already collaborates with Atinary, and Thermo Fisher is working with NVIDIA. If Primordiant’s robots require substantial integration at each site, partnering may be cheaper than buying, and the reported engagements leave cross-protocol autonomy unverified. These risks justify Watch. A structural block or a credible sale ceiling below the proceeds hurdle remains unestablished. [Defensibility, Key Risks]

### Decisive diligence question

Would owning Primordiant’s automation create enough incremental value for an instrument vendor to justify a purchase above roughly $93.3 million in enterprise value under the stated assumptions? A buyer-validated business case based on repeatable savings and measured integration costs across paid deployments would move the action to Reach out if it supported that price.
