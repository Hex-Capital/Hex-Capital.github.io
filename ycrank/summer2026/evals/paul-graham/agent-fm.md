# Agent FM -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The useful fact is that the founders encountered the problem themselves. They were juggling parallel Claude Code and Codex sessions, then shipped a separate process that observes those sessions without wrapping them. That is a real workaround, not a market diagram. But the dossier does not say what broke during development, how quickly they overcame it, or whether the workaround changed anyone else’s behavior. I see resourcefulness, but not yet the unusually legible episode of resourcefulness that makes me bet primarily on the founders.

The strongest evidence is the artifact. Agent FM detects Claude Code and Codex, produces audio and chat summaries, exposes token and tool-error metrics, and has shipped on macOS and Windows 10/11. Cross-tool, cross-platform observation is materially harder than making a demo around one API. It is nevertheless an unmeasured proof: there is no accuracy rate for detecting blockers, no reduction in supervision time, and no evidence that the product remains reliable as upstream CLIs change.

The missing fact is who wants this now. Agent FM has 809 LinkedIn followers, but followers are spectators. Starter and Pro are waitlisted at $48 and $144 per year, while the dossier reports no active users, payments, repeat usage, or named engineering teams. Those prices also leave an awkward economic question: a product supervising expensive engineering work should either become much more valuable or remain a small utility. The founders have built a fishing rod but have not shown a narrow well containing fish.

The bull case stings because the market is moving unusually quickly. Codex exceeded one million developers, developers are running more parallel agents, and Agent FM already works across vendors rather than depending on a single model provider. Passive observation may also survive upstream changes better than wrappers do. This could qualify for the working-product exception to otherwise thin founder and traction evidence. But that exception needs unusual product behavior or user pull. Agent FM currently has neither in the dossier, while Codex, Conductor, and Omnara can absorb much of the command-center, progress, and voice surface.

There is also no running-back evidence yet. We have one route, no rejected hypothesis, and no dated change caused by usage. The founder-originated need earns credit, as do the compatibility and privacy schleps implicit in supporting multiple tools and enterprise session data. But SSO/SAML and Privacy Mode are pricing-page promises, not proof that the team has learned enterprise procurement or personally converted reluctant buyers. No public cofounder history makes the absence of an obstacle story more consequential.

My base case is that by August 2029 Agent FM has pivoted from ambient audio into enterprise coding-agent observability and remains below $1 million ARR. I would not write the check today. One result would flip me: five named engineering teams each paying at least $500 per month and using Agent FM weekly for eight consecutive weeks. That would show both a narrow well and that the audio interface is the start of a system rather than a feature waiting to be copied.
