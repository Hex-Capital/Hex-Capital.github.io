# Ploid -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Ploid has shipped several ways to ask about a person. I want to know what the founders did when the answer was wrong. The dossier contains no specific obstacle followed by a founder-built workaround and an observable result. Hernandez’s recommendation systems work at Wander gives him relevant technical experience. Karic calling his cofounder his best friend is encouraging. Neither establishes how they behave under resistance. Resourcefulness remains unproven, which earns the missing-evidence default rather than an adverse judgment.

The product supplies the strongest affirmative evidence. An app, API, CLI, and MCP server represent something more substantial than a proposed architecture. Ploid also reports 81% accuracy across 1,000 queries, against 63% for Exa and 23% for Apollo. That is a testable claim. But the company published the comparison and used Gemini 3.1 Pro to grade it; I would give the claimed advantage partial credit until someone reproduces it. Likewise, two billion indexed people could represent considerable engineering, but the number does not establish accurate identity linking or a proprietary advantage. Third-party contact suppliers make that distinction consequential ([documentation](https://ploid.com/documentation), [privacy policy](https://ploid.com/privacy)).

The strongest argument for investing is that developers could adopt Ploid as a primitive before investors understand the category. That matches a real pattern in my investing. The September 4 Monid integration shows an outside connection, and the claim that thousands of companies use Ploid deserves some demand credit. But neither identifies a small group whose work already depends on it. Monid also combines competing providers, making substitution easy unless Ploid produces answers the others cannot. The $200 and $600 monthly plans establish what customers can buy, not what they have bought repeatedly. I would ask which five users would be most upset if Ploid disappeared ([pricing](https://ploid.com/pricing), [company posts](https://teapawt.org/ploid_ai)).

The prototype-based exception to my founder screen deserves consideration here. A sufficiently impressive working product can outweigh an incomplete founder history. Ploid’s interfaces make that case plausible, but an internally graded benchmark and an unspecified usage claim do not make it compelling. There is also no documented rejected hypothesis followed by a changed product, so adaptability stays at its default. Hernandez’s retrieval experience does not establish that he personally encountered this problem. Identity maintenance, provenance, and data rights contain plenty of schlep; the dossier does not show the founders discovering their wedge by doing that work. The origin criterion stays at its default too. I see no evidence of passive founders, premature delegation, or dependence on a promised partnership.

I would pass today. Ploid clears my technical-proof threshold but misses the resourcefulness gate: the available evidence cannot support the required 18/31. This is a specific unresolved founder test, not a penalty for being early or lacking disclosed revenue. One verifiable result would change my decision: a dated customer record showing the founders repaired a specific identity-matching failure within two weeks, after which five recruiting teams paying at least $200 monthly used Ploid weekly for eight weeks. That would connect founder action to sustained need. At present, the product is ahead of the evidence for writing my own check.
