# Kiwi -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Kiwi has a concrete account of why agents create work for their supposed beneficiaries. It has no disclosed price for the equity or explanation of what this round will prove. Post-money valuation: unknown. Competing term sheets: unknown. Use of proceeds: unknown. I cannot call the deal cheap, crowded, or a financing treadmill from that silence. But I also cannot underwrite my personal check without knowing the purchase price and the experiment it buys.

The founders are the strongest evidence. Melanie Chen combines data-platform work at Plaid with founding FDE and post-sales experience at Chalk; Bill Qin built Chalk SQL. Chen’s report of spending 20 hours correcting agent work in one week supplies direct operating exposure to the other side of that infrastructure. This fits the translation layer I look for: people who understand both data systems and the operator stuck repairing their outputs. Qin’s precise founder status remains unclear, but the dossier identifies Chen as CEO; there is no evidence of an equal-founder committee or concealed decision authority.

The strongest case against passing is architectural. Versioned records, SQL traces, replayable snapshots, and stopping when sources are stale address failures that a smarter language model alone cannot eliminate. Kiwi could become the evidence layer customers retain while swapping the agents above it. That would give the product a reason to survive model improvements. But customer-specific history becomes a proprietary asset only when customers depend on it. The dossier documents the mechanism, not that dependence. Clay already describes company context and learning from workflow outcomes, so Kiwi must demonstrate that its controls change purchasing behavior.

More than 100 discovery conversations establish substantial listening, not conversion. Likewise, order-form language establishes a way to charge for pilots, not that anyone has paid. There is no documented purchase, retained deployment, or exclusive access supporting an exception to my financing gates. The two historical company-account followers and GitHub star counts tell me essentially nothing about this enterprise product. The missing evidence that matters is whether a buyer entrusts a recurring workflow to Kiwi and keeps paying because its historical record is costly to lose.

Delete the next financing from the model. Kiwi could conceivably support a small, valuable business, but the dossier gives me no price, support burden, margin, burn, or route to break-even. Human checkpoints are sensible reliability controls; they may also consume expensive labor. I will not assume those costs disappear with scale. Nor will I assume the founders intend endless fundraising: no capital plan is disclosed. The known exceptions require demonstrated conversion, cash economics, proprietary access, or a financing structure that changes the dependency on future rounds. Kiwi’s technical and operating credentials are promising, but they do not supply those receipts.

I pass at 32/100. This is a team with relevant shipped work and a plausible product mechanism, while the primary investment gates remain undocumented. My verifiable flip condition is a documented round below $10 million post-money funding one 90-day experiment: three independent customers renew at positive contribution margin after measured agent-correction hours fall by at least half. That would connect the equity price, the capital-funded test, customer dependence, and operating economics. Today, that connection is still a hypothesis.
