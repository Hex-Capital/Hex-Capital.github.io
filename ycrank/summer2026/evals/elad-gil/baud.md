# Baud -- Elad Gil Evaluation

A 50M-parameter model trained on 5M tokens at 1,000+ tokens per second makes **Baud** a technically credible shipping organization whose weakest dimension is architectural probability: **Baud** has an FPGA cluster, compiler, distributed stack, and GlobalFoundries 12 nm design, but success requires the representation, production silicon, and customer adoption to work together. Sarang Zambare and Eric Taylor are substantially better builders than the median pre-seed founders; the investment still resembles a science project with capitalization risk, technical risk, and market risk. I would not write the check today.

**Perplexity** is the high rung on founder shipping because Aravind built the discussed artifact between meetings; **Baud** shares the bias toward output over credentials. Zambare shipped ML through 100,000+ Peloton devices and helped build Caper before its Instacart acquisition, while Taylor completed four tape-outs before **Baud** produced a working FPGA-emulated cluster. The divergence is feedback cadence: Perplexity’s weekly releases met active users, while Baud discloses an unnumbered design-partner program and no evidence that partners repeatedly drove compiler or architecture iterations. Baud belongs below Perplexity but well above a credential-only founding profile.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism: the investment required an external market miracle—share inversion inside winner-take-all dynamics—rather than execution alone. **Baud** similarly needs several independent events: multiplier-free training must preserve frontier-model quality, the first ASIC must deliver acceptable yield and economics after its planned 2026 tape-out, and developers must retrain models whose existing weights cannot run directly. **Baud** therefore fails my single-miracle test more sharply than its polished demo suggests; “bit-exact in most cases” is not enough when representation risk, fabrication risk, and behavior change compound.

A reported 6,144-H100, 4.3 MW training run is a real catalyst for **Baud**, because power and cluster costs have crossed a threshold where arithmetic–hardware co-design can matter more over the next 18 months. The mechanism also compresses **Baud**’s window: Etched already reports more than $1B in customer contracts, Cerebras generated $510M in 2025 revenue, and Groq generated a reported $500M. The market is reopening, but Baud is skating for a puck with several well-capitalized skaters already there; the catalyst is stronger than TAM arithmetic and weaker than the post-ChatGPT opening that made Mistral immediately legible.

No public revenue, paid reservation, or repeat-usage curve exists for **Baud**; 94 YC Launch votes and an open early-access program are false signals until money or sustained workload follows. **Baud** would flip this dimension into invest territory if two named model developers paid for FPGA capacity, expanded usage for three consecutive months, and converted into non-cancelable ASIC reservations. Until then I apply the absent-evidence default: pre-seed silence is not fatal, but an AI-infrastructure claim this ambitious needs buying behavior to distinguish “just working” from a technically impressive experiment.

**Stripe** became an index on e-commerce because every new internet business could adopt the same rails without redesigning its product. **Baud** could theoretically become an index on AI compute, but Baud currently asks developers to train in a proprietary representation and cannot run existing weights directly; that is a migration bet, not automatic category exposure. PyTorch export reduces friction, yet NVIDIA, Cerebras, Groq, Etched, and d-Matrix can capture category growth without requiring the same model-format reset. Baud must win share against the category leaders before it can ride the category.

Four prior tape-outs by Taylor make my 36-month base case an IP outcome rather than a dead laboratory: by July 2029, **Baud** will be acquired by **NVIDIA** as a compiler-and-arithmetic tuck-in before reaching $5M ARR, rather than operate a scaled independent training cloud. **Baud** can create valuable representation, compiler, and ASIC intellectual property even if the integrated commercial architecture fails; scarce hardware execution talent makes the parts more financeable than the whole. That forecast follows directly from strong shipping capacity paired with a multi-miracle standalone plan.

**Mistral** is the strongest objection because I accepted a capital-intensive, pre-revenue infrastructure bet after ChatGPT reopened the market, and **Baud** similarly combines Taylor’s four tape-outs with Zambare’s 100,000-device ML deployment. The bull mechanism is that the FPGA result already isolates the remaining work to capitalization and fabrication, making Baud another Known Exception where experienced builders remove science risk. The comparison breaks because Mistral’s foundational miracle—transformer capability—had already happened, while Baud still must validate frontier-scale representation quality, production silicon economics, and retraining adoption against competitors with $450M–$3.9B raised. One condition flips my verdict: a named frontier-model developer signs a paid, non-cancelable capacity reservation after reproducing Baud’s advantage on production silicon.
