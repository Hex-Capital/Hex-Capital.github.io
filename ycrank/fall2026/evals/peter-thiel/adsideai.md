# Adside.ai -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

What does Adside.ai believe about advertising that Omneky, Madgicx, and Motion would reject? The dossier supplies no answer. It documents a working combination of competitor research, creative generation, and campaign operations, but no disputed proposition or experiment establishing an unexpected advantage. Shipping AdsSpy in days using Apify demonstrates execution speed. Because competitors can access the same infrastructure, it also exposes how quickly the technical starting point can be reproduced. The secret remains undisclosed; that is missing evidence, not a refuted claim.

The monopoly question is more troublesome. Adside serves startups and marketing teams across several advertising platforms without identifying a bounded segment it can dominate. Its own comparisons describe capabilities already sold by named vendors. Collecting more than 133,000 ads across 6,055 YC companies establishes functioning research infrastructure, but those records come from public advertising libraries. No exclusive access, measured performance advantage, or demonstrated switching cost protects the resulting product. The privacy commitment against training shared models on customer data also prevents me from assuming that each additional account improves a common proprietary model. Adside has built useful capabilities; the evidence for retaining exceptional profits is weak.

The founders are the strongest reason to reconsider that judgment. Robin Choy and Guillaume Miara shared a coworking space for four years, built Botdog together, and sold it before starting Adside. This is an established operating partnership with experience buying advertising for its own business. That fits my preference for companies formed through durable operator relationships. It does not establish a proprietary founder-discovery network or the broader production-stack pattern behind some of my recent investments. Their shared execution history earns substantial credit, while current ownership, compensation, and full-time commitments remain unverified. The previous exit supplies no evidence of an adverse current capitalization or acquisition objective.

The strongest bull case is that these founders can replace an agency relationship and capture its budget. Adside reports 30 companies live, attracts inbound interest through its own advertising, and brought more than 40 founders and growth practitioners to a workshop. Those are meaningful early signals. At the managed-service minimum of $3,500 per month, 1,000 customers would generate $42 million in annual revenue. But paying status for the 30 companies is unspecified, and neither delivery margins nor human workload establishes how that engine scales. The workshops identify a concrete acquisition route; absent conversion, acquisition cost, and sales-cycle evidence, they do not yet establish repeatability. A capable team could build a substantial business here while competition limits the value it captures.

I would pass with my own money. The decisive problem is the combination of overlapping substitutes and no demonstrated monopoly mechanism, rather than the ordinary absence of pre-seed financial history. My known exceptions require evidence that directly removes that concern: an independently verified technical breakthrough, persistent transaction behavior establishing a defensible position, or a concrete deployment advantage. Thirty live companies establish none of those yet. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: an independently audited, six-month cohort of a clearly defined customer segment showing Adside delivers at least 10 times the gross profit per advertising dollar, after fees, of the best available alternative. That would give the claimed technology advantage substance.
