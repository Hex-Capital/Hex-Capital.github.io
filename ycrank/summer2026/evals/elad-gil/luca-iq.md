# Luca IQ -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: AI-assisted ingestion and IRS-authorized filing APIs now let CPA firms replace desktop-era preparation, and the controlling variable is whether firms will move meaningful return volume—not merely trials—onto an independent calculation engine. Luca IQ has evidence for the mechanism but not yet the threshold. Roughly 95% of firms reportedly use four legacy vendors, yet April and Column Tax already offer authorized filing infrastructure. The falsifiable belief is that direct ownership of calculation, auditability, and e-filing can overcome incumbent workflow inertia.

The product is materially more than an AI tax assistant. AI and human review determine inputs, but a deterministic rules engine maps source documents to return lines, preserves an audit trail, validates schemas, and transmits through IRS Modernized e-File. Passing Assurance Testing in July 2026 proves a difficult technical and institutional milestone. The reported 57% reduction in administrative preparation and 30-minute intake-to-review-ready time suggest a real economic change, although one testimonial does not establish category-wide margins.

Demand is promising but incompletely resolved. Hundreds of firms applied for the pilot, customers completed a tax season, and one firm reports 1,100 hours saved. Those are unusually informative pre-seed signals because tax preparation is seasonal, consequential work rather than casual product testing. The missing facts are paid return volume, repeat usage, and conversion from applicants to production customers. Fixed per-return pricing indicates monetization, but undisclosed revenue prevents me from calling this demonstrated product-market fit.

The strongest bear case is market structure. April has raised $78 million and offers national coverage; Column Tax has been acquired by Aiwyn; Filed and TaxGPT can bundle automation into software firms already use. Luca IQ currently covers federal Form 1040, lacks completed SOC 2 Type II certification, and must continuously maintain changing tax rules. Its inferred filing-data moat receives no credit: a funded competitor could reproduce rules, corrections, and integrations unless Luca IQ first captures substantial workflow state and distribution. The open outbound-sales internship also suggests that adoption remains sold rather than pulled.

The founder-market fit is credible but not dispositive. Angelo Policicchio brings CPA, EY, and FBI forensic-accounting experience, while Akash Sadashivapeth has led data science and has engineering training. That explains why the team could build the system, but the dossier documents neither prior startup learning nor a costly commitment demonstrating desperation, so founder evidence remains at the default. No fundraising, secondary-liquidity, or broad-hiring red flag is documented. The research-stage exception is unnecessary and would not apply: Luca IQ should be judged on commercial adoption because the technology already works.

I would write a small pre-seed check. My 36-month base case is $3 million to $7 million in ARR from CPA firms, with Luca IQ valuable but not yet the category winner. The product owns a consequential workflow and has crossed the regulatory gate; distribution is now the constraint. One verifiable flip condition: if fewer than 20 firms pay to process a second tax season through Luca IQ by October 2027, I would pass because the controlling market belief—workflow migration—would be falsified.
