# OpenScaffold -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Deployment engineers currently piece together requirements from calls, tickets, and GitHub. OpenScaffold says it turns those inputs into a source-cited plan, briefs coding agents, and checks their changes against customer rules. **Scenario assumption:** If that workflow reduces total engineer time per successful deployment, it could make smaller implementations economical when manual scoping and review would put them out of reach. The memo provides no measured cost comparison or example of a deployment that became viable. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The memo records a public launch and Faraday’s CEO publicly identifying himself as a customer. The founder reports testing the product on an unnamed pilot partner’s calls. These are early demand signals, with payment and repeat use unverified. Both founders have prior deployment experience, and the launch and reported pilot show progress toward field testing. Per-deployment costs and repeat volume remain unknown. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The founder reported that in a pilot test the product failed to distinguish customer requests from the deployment team’s requests 82% of the time. Source citations can help reviewers trace errors, but correcting them could absorb the expected labor savings. Under model parity, approved playbooks and deployment history might give teams a reason to stay if they reuse them. The memo offers no observed preference under parity, and Harness already overlaps in shared context and integrations. The pilot result is adverse, but it leaves net labor savings unresolved, and the platform overlap leaves actual substitution untested. Neither finding closes the path. (Key Risks, Defensibility)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent buyer pay to repeat a small deployment it previously skipped on cost grounds after trying OpenScaffold and a functionally equivalent alternative using the same model? A matched trial should compare full cost per accepted deployment against manual delivery, including attribution corrections, review, retries, compliance, and fulfillment. Move to **Reach out** if the savings make the deployment viable and the buyer chooses OpenScaffold for a paid repeat at a price that covers delivery. Move to **Pass** if a representative trial shows that correction costs erase the savings or that the buyer retains the same outcome by switching.
