# Grep AI -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies** is the strongest path. Grep converts recurring screening steps into reusable workflows and routes exceptions to a reasoning model. IG reported that automated alert review rose from 10% to 30% and that flagged applicants went from waiting 2–3 days for activation to under 10 minutes. Grep’s internal benchmark reported a 91% reduction in model cost on a screening workload. That benchmark leaves total delivery labor and equivalent screening quality unmeasured, including human review, retries, exceptions, and governance. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Scenario assumption.** A smaller fintech could buy near-real-time review for low-risk flagged applicants whose expected account value previously made expedited manual review uneconomic. IG’s results make the delivery mechanism plausible, while the memo leaves demand from that segment unproven. Shopmonkey’s 64 research jobs in 30 days show early repeat use in an existing underwriting workflow. The memo gives no payment or renewal evidence for that use. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

IG’s customer-specific rules and months of shadow testing suggest that switching to an equivalent workflow could require revalidation. That friction offers limited evidence of value capture because buyer preference under model parity remains untested. The founders’ prior risk and operations work at Brex and the progression from a research preview to a managed-agent launch support seed-stage execution. Repeatable rollouts across regulated buyers remain unproven. (Defensibility, Founders & Team, Market & Traction)

For scale, the relevant unit is a successfully resolved alert. Grep lists usage-based and subscription pricing, but contribution per alert depends on realized revenue after inference, human review, retries, and fulfillment, with customer-specific validation costs spread over actual volume. The memo gives neither those unit economics nor the number of reachable buyers and their repeat alert volumes. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Grep may simply make an existing compliance queue faster. IG still uses manual oversight for high-risk cases, and months of customer-specific approval could absorb much of the savings at lower volumes. With cheap models and ample capital, buyers and competitors can use hosted execution and Grep’s open-source runtime to build overlapping workflows. The memo identifies no scarce resource Grep controls for Path A. Those facts leave both Path B gates open but unproven. The San Francisco company’s seed-stage profile fits the mandate, while the evidence falls short of the outreach gate. (Grep AI, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will fintechs that previously could not justify expedited screening repeatedly pay for Grep when a credible alternative uses equally capable models? A paid, head-to-head pilot should show that Grep delivers reviews at the same screening quality and governance standard, at a viable price after counting all human labor, retries, and exceptions, and that buyers choose Grep again at that price. Move to Reach out if those results establish both Path B conditions and bring the reassessment to at least 65 with medium or high confidence. Pass if a representative trial shows that all-in costs erase the market opening or buyers readily switch under parity.
