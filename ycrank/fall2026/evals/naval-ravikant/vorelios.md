# Vorelios -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Vorelios has produced something more informative than credentials: a model that predicts aerodynamic surface quantities, with published comparisons against Transolver-3 and AB-UPT. That is evidence of specific knowledge acquired through building. Daniel McNeil’s physics background and Krish Kapadia’s 2025–2026 autonomy research provide context, but the technical artifact earns the founder credit. It does not yet establish that either founder understands where aerospace engineers will trust a prediction enough to change a design. Nothing in the dossier establishes an integrity problem, artificial fundraising pressure, or dependence on investor reassurance.

The important question is what a competitor would have to reproduce after copying the code. Vorelios trains its own model on internally generated simulations. Training and validation represent real work; this is more substantial than an interface over a commodity API. But a reproducible simulation factory can produce a temporary lead without producing a compounding advantage. Luminary already generates simulation data and offers aircraft models. Vorelios’s reported 1.1–31 millisecond inference times on an H100 demonstrate technical capability, while leaving the scarcity of its underlying asset unresolved.

The strongest argument for investing now is that two people may have built the beginning of an engineering model whose usefulness expands far faster than its operating costs. Reported NASA Common Research Model errors of 6.37% for pressure and 3.81% for skin friction make that possibility concrete. My pattern of backing infrastructure bottlenecks fits this work. My willingness to back an unusual prototype before distribution is established also applies. But that exception requires unusually persuasive product evidence: agreement with solver outputs does not yet establish reliable performance on unfamiliar customer geometries. A two-person team alone does not prove exceptional capital efficiency. I would preserve interest without converting benchmark promise into investment conviction.

Aerospace is a legible initial buyer, and the estimated $14.2 billion CAE market establishes substantial surrounding expenditure. It does not establish Vorelios’s route into that expenditure. Heat transfer and structural mechanics require capabilities beyond the published aerodynamics results. Nor does the dossier show my preferred customer-created compounding: another aerospace customer does not automatically improve anyone else’s predictions. Internal simulation generation remains company-produced supply until evidence demonstrates otherwise. Pricing and revenue receive their absent-evidence defaults; missing commercial history is ordinary at this stage, but it supplies no affirmative value-capture evidence.

I land at Neutral and would keep my money uncommitted today. Vorelios has demonstrated technical initiative, with a plausible proprietary-model substrate, but has not demonstrated why its advantage strengthens as competitors enter. My verifiable flip condition is an independently documented cross-customer learning loop: validation data that Vorelios has exclusive rights to use measurably improves predictions on a second customer’s previously unseen geometries. That would turn deployments into an accumulating asset and establish the customer-created value this dossier currently lacks.
