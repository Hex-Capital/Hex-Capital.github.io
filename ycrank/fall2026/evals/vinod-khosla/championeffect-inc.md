# ChampionEffect Inc. -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

ChampionEffect Inc. could make scarce employee expertise available throughout an organization. That is the consequential version of this idea: one person discovers a valuable workflow, and everyone else can execute it without consuming that person’s time. But the dossier does not quantify the expertise bottleneck, the cost displaced, or the improvement achieved. The cited $7.49B corporate-training market measures category spending. It does not establish an orders-of-magnitude consequence. I cannot price the upside merely by multiplying “every employee” by an assumed benefit.

The technical question is whether employee walkthroughs produce reliable, reusable capability. Slack, Teams, and call participation provide access to work; the learning mechanism must supply the advantage. Section already offers personalized workplace-AI coaching. ChampionEffect supplies no comparative result showing that its accumulated workflow knowledge makes guidance substantially more accurate, cheaper, or easier to deploy. That leaves technical discontinuity unproven. It does not establish that the product is merely a wrapper, either. My red flag around interchangeable AI interfaces remains a diligence question, not a demonstrated offense.

The strongest argument against passing is the team’s ability to turn this wedge into something much larger. Kevin Law co-founded Cambly and led its technology; Emre Simdi reports helping grow Cambly from zero to $100M+ revenue. That revenue belongs to their previous company, but the operating experience is relevant. Califrais signed before launch, according to Simdi. A customer buying before delivery is meaningful pre-seed evidence. Technical and commercial founders who have already worked together could discover a much bigger opportunity through deployment. My actual investment pattern includes execution-led software businesses, and my exceptions allow an exceptional instigator to pursue a giant existing value pool without an initial scientific breakthrough. ChampionEffect deserves that consideration. The dossier nevertheless establishes neither the quantified consequence needed for that override nor observed belief revision or difficult, risk-specific recruiting.

The next dollar should test whether coaching transfers expertise successfully without creating another supervision burden. Launching Coach and signing Califrais are commercial milestones; neither establishes a deliberate attack on that kill risk. There is no disclosed experiment with a budget, threshold, and stop condition. There is also no pricing or delivery-cost model showing what remains after workflow capture, inference, support, and human correction. Software does not need a factory cost curve, but it still needs to make expertise cheaper after counting the labor hidden around the model. Missing process and economic evidence receive the stated defaults, rather than an assumption of poor execution.

I pass on ChampionEffect Inc. today. The commercial foundation is more convincing than the demonstrated transformation. Company-specific walkthroughs could become a proprietary learning loop—an important unstated pattern in what I fund—but collecting them alone does not prove that loop exists. One verifiable result would reopen this decision: an independently auditable Califrais deployment showing at least a 10x reduction in total cost per correctly completed workflow against its existing champion-assisted process, including setup and human correction. That would demonstrate an expertise multiplier and give the larger consequence case something concrete to build on.
