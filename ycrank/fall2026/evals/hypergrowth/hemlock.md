# Hemlock -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Conditional on strong product-market fit, Hemlock could sell the same force-annotated demonstrations to robotics labs in multiple markets. The memo describes a wearable capture system and shows synchronized video samples with contact, hand-pose, and depth overlays. Labs training force-sensitive manipulation models could use the same type of dataset across markets. The open question is how much of a collection transfers across buyers’ tasks, robot hands, and training pipelines. Hemlock reports that adding force data raised success from 10% to 100% for egg placement and from 30% to 80% for lightbulb screwing. Those two demonstrations support the use case, with limited evidence of transfer to other tasks. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Hemlock has a direct inquiry and referral channel and can reuse emailed samples for initial evaluation. The documented path relies on a conversation about buyer needs, and the memo gives no repeatable purchase or production onboarding flow. New training tasks could create repeat demand for additional datasets, but recurring purchases, pricing, and same-account expansion remain unverified. The October 27 public launch establishes a date for launch attention. Commercial launch timing, paid adoption, and revenue are unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

In a licensing scenario, incremental contribution would be the fee from the next lab less the sales, onboarding, delivery, and support needed to serve it. Reusing a collection across labs could spread its capture costs. If each sale instead requires a fresh collection, the fee also has to cover worker time, equipment, any required calibration, and label quality checks. The memo gives no realized fee or measured inputs for either path. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The strongest countercase is that Hemlock becomes a custom data collection business. If the gains vary by task or robot hand, each lab may need new demonstrations and dedicated capture work. The memo identifies worker-specific calibration as a risk by analogy to published wrist-EMG research. It also identifies direct tactile capture and public force-data collection methods as substitutes that could limit what labs will pay. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo leaves both cross-buyer dataset reuse and the amount of unavoidable collection work unresolved. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Hemlock sell the same force-annotated dataset package to independent labs in two markets through a self-serve purchase and setup flow, then earn a paid expansion from one lab at positive incremental contribution? Paid orders, activation records, and attributable labor and delivery costs would answer it. A successful result would support Reach out if rescoring reaches 65 and confidence rises to at least Medium. Evidence that each sale requires bespoke capture that consumes its contribution, with no supported way to lower that cost, would change the action to Pass.
