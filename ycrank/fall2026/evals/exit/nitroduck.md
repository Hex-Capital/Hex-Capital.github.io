# Nitroduck -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Nitroduck’s strongest path. A specialty-crop breeder could buy the platform for exclusive control of a workflow that, if validated, would let it use its existing crosses and skip a separate plant-regeneration step. Nitroduck estimates that conventional variety development takes 8–16 years and a new trait costs $3 million. Those are company estimates. The memo documents a public launch and an adjacent licensing deal between Pairwise and Enza Zaden, which shows a breeder will pay for editing access. The case for ownership depends on measured advantages across enough crop programs to make exclusive control worth more than buying access. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*)

The first operating milestone is independently confirmed edited seed in a commercially relevant crop within an existing breeder program, with enough repeatability to measure time or cost savings. The founders’ prior plant-engineering work and a reported $500,000 convertible note make an initial test plausible. Crop-level results, a test budget, and runway remain unverified. (*Founders & Team*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

Under the shared scenario, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million gross before investor taxes. At 3%, that requires $83.3 million in distributable equity, or $88.3 million in enterprise value with illustrative net debt of $5 million. The reported note’s conversion and repayment terms are unknown. Using the same illustrative debt in both sale scenarios:

- A $25 million sale yields $600,000 gross, or 1.2×.
- A $100 million sale yields $2.85 million gross, or 5.7×. At 2% ownership, the same sale yields $1.9 million, or 3.8×.
- If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public-market scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested for eight years, the holder realizes $0 and retains the shares. Any wind-down recovery would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo gives no basis to estimate.

The sale prices are sensitivities. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction evidence to support a realizable price range. It also supplies no cash-generation evidence for a buyout or payout, no eligible secondary demand, and no operating-scale evidence for an IPO. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Even if the technology works, breeders may prefer a license or a per-project service. Pairwise’s licensing deal demonstrates that alternative, and Nitroduck itself proposes a flat-fee service. Plantik’s published patent application adds a concrete diligence issue for a buyer, while claim overlap and Nitroduck’s transferable rights remain unresolved. The Pass gate remains open because the memo establishes neither a structural block nor a supported sale price that would leave the holder materially short. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a breeder-run pilot show a repeatable advantage large enough to justify buying Nitroduck rather than licensing access? Reach out if a breeder confirms edited seed and measured savings in its own program, and buyer-specific economics show how those gains could support a sale near or above the $88.3 million illustrative hurdle within eight years.
