# Dock -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Mike Molinet and Govind Kavaturi worked together at Branch for six years, then co-founded Thena, before starting Dock. That makes Dock an unusually strong founder pair attached to an unusually weak hacker trail. Dock looks like an operator-built company: the founders know how organizations grow, but the dossier does not yet show the technical artifact or hacker obsession that made the earliest versions of Dropbox and Stripe feel inevitable.

Stripe is the high rung for co-founder configuration: two brothers who had been building together for years, with technical and commercial abilities fused rather than divided. Dock has the same load-bearing continuity. Molinet hired Kavaturi as Branch’s first international employee, they helped scale Branch past $100 million ARR, and they later built Thena together. Dock diverges from Stripe because neither founder has a documented personal repository, programming achievement, or developer-community footprint. The relationship is exceptional; the evidence of complementary hacking is not.

Loopt is the dangerous low-rung precedent on hacker genealogy: weak product timing was excused only because Sam Altman displayed such unusual force that he became a Known Exception. Dock supplies credentials—Bucknell engineering, a Stanford MBA, two prior B2B companies—but Dock supplies no equivalent exception-making artifact. Six organizational repositories with one star at the high end are tracks, but faint ones. Dock risks the red-flag pattern of experienced managers entering a fashionable AI category and believing organizational skill can substitute for technical taste.

Branch’s rise past $100 million ARR is nevertheless real evidence of animal resourcefulness. Over the next 18 months, that ability could compound inside Dock through integrations, templates, pricing experiments, and the unpleasant work of persuading companies to entrust agents with API keys. Molinet and Kavaturi have already navigated international expansion and built two companies together. But Dock’s dossier names no pre-funding stunt, unexpected customer win, or manual operation comparable to Airbnb’s cereal boxes. Dock has demonstrated persistence at prior companies, not yet animal behavior in the creation of Dock itself.

Molinet and Kavaturi spent years operating B2B software across functions, which puts Dock above a merely researched idea. The missing fact is whether either founder personally ran enough agents to become enraged by fragmented chats and hand-managed state. Dock would move into the top tier on organicity if its attributed activity history showed that the founders had first used Dock to operate Thena or Dock itself before packaging the workspace for others. Without that evidence, Dock may be an intelligent extrapolation from the AI-agents wave rather than something the founders could not avoid building.

Coinbase is the single useful parallel for Dock’s schlep. Coinbase’s visible product was simple; the real work was identities, permissions, compliance, and everything that could go wrong when software moved money. Dock has a smaller version of that shape in separate API keys, role checks, signed webhooks, actor attribution, and event history. But Dock remains a commercial beta, native WebAuthn is still on the roadmap, and Dock explicitly leaves connected-agent behavior to third-party providers. That is early contact with the schlep, not yet proof that the founders enjoy it.

Dock’s $19 Pro and $49 Scale plans make my 36-month forecast concrete: by July 2029, Dock will have narrowed from a general multiplayer workspace into an agent-identity and audit-control product and reached $2 million to $6 million ARR. Dock’s strong repeat-founder pair should be better at selling operational trust than winning a developer-framework contest, while the weak hacker genealogy makes it unlikely that Dock becomes the universal substrate implied by the current pitch.

Dust has raised more than $60 million, Relevance AI $34 million, and CrewAI $18 million, so the stinging bear case is that Dock is a thin collaboration layer built by operators while better-capitalized technical products absorb shared state, permissions, and audit history. Dock’s leading repository having one star supports that case. The fact that Molinet and Kavaturi previously scaled Branch to $100 million ARR prevents me from dismissing Dock as an ordinary wrapper, but prior-company execution is not a Dock artifact. I would flip to Invest if Dock showed at least 20 paying organizations each sustaining five or more agents and 10,000 attributed actions per month for three consecutive months; that would prove the permission-and-history schlep is becoming indispensable rather than merely well packaged.
