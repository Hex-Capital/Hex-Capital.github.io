# Hickory & Company -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong initial product-market fit, Hickory’s strongest path is to sell investigation and case-documentation software to bank and fintech compliance teams. Hickory describes agents that retrieve cited evidence, draft cases against institution-specific criteria, and log actions for staff review. Evidence retrieval, drafting, and logging are potentially reusable across markets. How much local policy work and data adaptation the current product would need remains uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The memo documents demo booking, conference distribution, and organization-provisioned accounts under written agreements. That gives Hickory a repeatable way to reach buyers, with a human-led path to purchase and limited evidence about setup. Recurring investigations, configured policies, and accumulated audit histories could support renewals if customers adopt the product in production. Licensing is inferred, and billing cadence and expansion pricing are unknown. The October 30 launch establishes a public launch date. Paid production use, realized revenue, and commercial growth remain unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Assuming institution-level licensing, incremental contribution would be the fee from the next institution less attributable sales, setup, model processing, storage, and support. Reusing the investigation workflow could reduce setup effort. Pricing and delivery effort are unquantified, leaving contribution uncertain. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Hickory could win U.S. BSA customers and still need substantial work for each new market. Institution-specific acceptance criteria may require expert configuration, and the documented restrictions on financial-account information and hosted processing could limit which cases the product can handle. Qualified staff review remains required, and Hickory’s claim of 10× faster queue processing comes without a benchmark method in the memo. The structural downside gate remains open because the memo leaves a credible route for the investigation workflow to transfer through configuration and does not establish that bespoke rebuilding is required. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paying institution outside the U.S. run the same investigation workflow in production using standard policy configuration and acceptable data handling? A deployment record showing what changed and how much Hickory staff time it required would answer this. If the rollout requires a bespoke rebuild that would recur in each new market, move to Pass. A bounded rollout would support continued Watch while Hickory establishes repeatable sales and onboarding.
