# Armorer Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest supported route. Check Point’s acquisition of Lakera shows that a security platform has bought an adjacent capability. SnapLogic executives’ collaboration discussions show interest in working with Armorer. The acquisition rationale is an inference: a platform could add Armorer’s workflow-tested fixes to its existing detection and enforcement products. Armorer’s reported accuracy and latency advantage comes from its own 50-document comparison, leaving production performance and the value of the remediation workflow unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

A paid production deployment that measures time to verified remediation and tracks regressions in existing workflows would strengthen that rationale. The Just Eat Takeaway testimonial credits Armorer with resolving a security problem, and the founders’ prior work together on AI security supports their ability to run a scoped test. The memo leaves the test budget, runway, and deployment timeline unknown. Customer contract assignability and other closing conditions are also unverified. The memo supports no independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Facts)

Under the benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and dilutes to 3%, with no follow-ons. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity. The sale cases assume a full cash acquisition paid within eight years, $10 million in combined debt and deal adjustments, no senior waterfall claims, and full pro rata participation by the holder.

| Scenario | Distributable equity | Cash to holder |
|----------|----------------------|----------------|
| Modest sale at $60 million enterprise value | $50 million | $1.5 million (3.0×) |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | $90 million | $2.7 million (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private operation with cash reinvested | No distribution or share purchase | $0 |

At 2.5% ownership, the strong sale would yield $2.25 million (4.5×). The sale values are sensitivity assumptions. The memo gives neither a price for the Lakera acquisition nor an Armorer revenue figure that would support a realizable price range. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Check Point already bundles agent discovery, testing, and runtime controls, and NVIDIA offers open-source runtime enforcement. A strategic buyer could meet enough of its needs with existing tools that acquiring Armorer adds little value. That makes the incremental economics of workflow-tested remediation decisive. The evidence shows a credible substitute for runtime controls, while leaving both the buyer’s need for the full workflow and the attainable sale price unresolved. Neither Pass gate is established. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could a strategic buyer justify from Armorer’s measured improvement in time to verified remediation over its existing tools? A paid production comparison and a buyer-backed price range that supports at least $83.3 million in distributable equity within eight years would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that the strongest credible sale would leave the holder materially short would move it to Pass.
