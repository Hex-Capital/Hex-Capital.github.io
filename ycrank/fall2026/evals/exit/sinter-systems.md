# Sinter -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a digital manufacturing platform such as Xometry. Xometry already quotes molding jobs and fulfills them through external suppliers. Sinter says its printed photopolymer tools work in existing presses and can deliver parts in 48 hours at one-tenth the time and cost of traditional methods. If that cost advantage holds on repeat, low-volume jobs after accounting for scrap and tool replacement, owning the workflow could let a platform serve more of those jobs profitably. The buyer benefit is an inference, and the performance figures are Sinter’s claims. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Public prepaid prototype ordering and molding pilot recruitment give Sinter a way to test the process on customer jobs. Sinter reports producing more than 500 kg of customer parts in its first month, with the share produced by pilot molding unspecified. Using existing presses could limit the equipment needed for a repeat-run test. The memo leaves the test budget and the capital needed to scale unverified. Sinter’s documented quoting and mesh-repair workflow gives a buyer a software asset it could plausibly integrate. IP ownership and transfer terms remain open. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. Both sale scenarios assume an eligible holder sells its entire stake for cash by year eight, with a pro rata waterfall, zero senior preference deductions, and no deferred consideration. The prices and adjustments are illustrative assumptions. Sinter’s financials and buyer pricing remain unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Gross cash to holder |
|-----------|-------------------------------------------|----------------------|
| Modest sale | $30 million less $5 million in net debt and deal adjustments = $25 million | $750,000 (1.5×) |
| Strong sale | $100 million less $10 million in net debt and deal adjustments = $90 million | $2.7 million (5.4×), falling to $2.25 million (4.5×) at 2.5% ownership |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested through year eight, with no eligible share purchase or funded payout | $0 realized |

### Countercase

A platform could source quick-turn molding or replicate enough of the process to avoid buying Sinter. Protolabs advertises one-day aluminum tooling, and existing mold shops can trial commercially available photopolymer tooling. Sinter’s tool life by resin and geometry remains unverified. Frequent insert replacement and scrap could erase the claimed savings even if the first sample arrives quickly. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the memo leaves both the economics of buying over building and the achievable sale price unresolved. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can repeat paid molding jobs show a cost advantage large enough for a manufacturing platform to justify buying Sinter? Matched runs against quick-turn aluminum tooling should measure cost per accepted part at the same quality and lead time, including scrap and insert replacement. Move to Reach out if a platform’s addressable job volume and savings support a purchase price that could clear the holder hurdle. Move to Pass if those economics support a price materially below it. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
