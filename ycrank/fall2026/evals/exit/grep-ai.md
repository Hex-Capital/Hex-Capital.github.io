# Grep AI -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition by an established financial-crime software vendor. IG reported that automated screening-alert review rose from 10% to 30% and flagged-customer activation fell from 2–3 days to under 10 minutes. A buyer could acquire validated workflows and shorten deployment for its own customers if Grep can reproduce those gains across institutions. The memo supports the workflow benefit and leaves buyer willingness to pay unverified. (Market & Traction, Defensibility)

A proposed 12–18 month milestone is two additional paid, governance-approved screening deployments with comparable improvements and measured implementation costs. Shopmonkey’s reported use of 64 underwriting research jobs in 30 days shows adoption in a second workflow. The memo records $5.5 million in cumulative financing, including a $500,000 YC convertible note. Current runway, the capital required for those deployments, and the note’s conversion and payout terms are unknown. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3% with no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. That requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. Both sale scenarios below assume $5 million in combined net debt and deal costs, no preferential diversion of proceeds, and an eligible holder selling the entire stake for cash within eight years. The prices are illustrative assumptions. The memo provides no buyer valuation or transaction evidence to support either price. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Distributable equity | Gross holder cash |
|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale at $50 million enterprise value | $45 million | $1.35 million (2.7×) |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | $95 million | $2.85 million (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share purchase | No distribution | $0 |

The strong case falls to about $2.38 million at 2.5% final ownership. The memo leaves transfer permissions and holder eligibility unverified and provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

A buyer could use Grep’s Apache 2.0 runtime and existing managed agent infrastructure to build overlapping capabilities. IG’s months of shadow testing, customer-specific governance approval, and continued manual oversight also suggest that much of the value may have to be rebuilt for each customer. That weakens the case for an acquisition premium. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible price ceiling below the holder’s hurdle, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would an established financial-crime software vendor value Grep’s validated workflows and customer deployments at a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? Buyer-side build-versus-buy economics grounded in repeat paid deployments would answer it. A supported price range that clears the hurdle after deal adjustments and the holder’s waterfall would move the action to Reach out. A supported ceiling materially below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
