# Decawork -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Sarthak Aggarwal built enterprise AI at NVIDIA and Ema, while Aman Raj built Barclays compliance tooling used by 240-plus teams: Decawork is strongest as a lived-domain founder bet and weakest as a two-founder structure with no visible tiebreaker. Decawork has Biz and Tiz—the founders have already built enterprise systems—but the dossier cannot tell me who gets the last word when two intelligent people disagree. That is the signature: authentic contact with the problem, ambiguous custody of the company.

Niantic is the high rung because John Hanke spent a decade inside location technology before Pokémon Go materialized. Decawork shares that biography-before-market mechanism: Sarthak worked on enterprise AI, Aman deployed compliance tooling across 240-plus Barclays teams, and their prior autonomous-IT product ran across multiple enterprises. Decawork diverges because that production evidence belongs to the human-employee incident product presented in June 2026, not the current agent control plane. They are of the neighborhood, but they have changed houses.

GameCrush is the low rung because unresolved control let investors strip away the product’s DNA. Decawork names Sarthak and Aman only as “Founder,” provides no co-founder history, and identifies neither a CEO nor a tiebreaker. Decawork therefore walks directly into my “multiple founders, always a recipe for disaster” scar tissue. The HQ Trivia exception does not rescue Decawork: no current-product usage displays the kind of tactile, explosive pull that can temporarily overwhelm a governance defect.

Oasis Security’s $195 million, Astrix Security’s $85 million, and Okta’s 8,200 integrations turn Decawork’s “magically weird” possibility into an 18-month compounding test. If Decawork accumulates proprietary policy templates, integrations, and attributed run histories, every governed agent makes the control plane harder to remove. If Decawork does not, Okta bundles identity controls while better-capitalized startups erase the distinction between governance and run monitoring. A credential-holding gateway is daring, but a crowded AI-security label is consensus wearing a slightly stranger hat.

Okta’s central kill switch shows what Decawork would need to transcend ordinary software infrastructure: Decawork would have to assume—and survive—regulated operational responsibility for agent actions, not merely expose approvals and audit logs. No public security certification appears on Decawork’s trust page even though the gateway stores credentials and mediates production tool calls. A SOC 2 badge alone would not flip the score; Decawork would need a liability-bearing deployment in healthcare, finance, or critical infrastructure where policy correctness compounds into a real moat.

Flock Safety could be discovered through a Wi-Fi name and verified by looking at a camera in the world. Decawork can be demonstrated in 30 minutes, and an IT operator can observe approvals, failures, and audit logs, but Decawork remains an administrative abstraction whose value is largely founder-reported. The prior autonomous-IT product running across multiple enterprises earns more than the floor, yet no public evidence shows a normal operator repeatedly using the current agent-control product.

Decawork’s June 2026 autonomous-IT product already had production deployments before Decawork recentered on agent governance. My 36-month forecast is that by July 2029 Decawork will have pivoted back toward autonomous IT incident resolution and will remain below $3 million ARR. Decawork’s founders know that workflow well enough to win some enterprise use, but incumbent convergence, absent current-product traction, and unclear founder authority make the broad control-plane position difficult to hold.

Aman Raj previously built a fintech serving 52,000 families and processing $1.5 million, then led a consumer-AI product past 100,000 MAU; the bull case is that Decawork has a DeepMind-style polymath exception hiding in plain sight. Sarthak’s NVIDIA and Ema experience strengthens the claim that Decawork can move faster than Okta and turn prior enterprise deployments into distribution. The objection stings, but DeepMind’s cross-domain brilliance produced unmistakable technical differentiation; Decawork presently discloses no moat, revenue, current-product adoption, or attributable public engineering artifact. One verifiable condition flips my verdict: Decawork publicly names one founder as CEO with majority operating authority and a documented conflict-resolution protocol.
