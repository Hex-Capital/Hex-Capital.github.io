# Wondersearch -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Wondersearch’s founders have earned a serious technical conversation. They built Polygres, secured an enterprise POC for that prior product, and launched Wondersearch on September 28, 2026. PostgreSQL retrieval development is relevant action in the problem they are attacking. Dalton’s research and technical presales experience adds context. Dale’s 5,000 customers at Swig Energy demonstrate operating experience, but those customers do not transfer into search demand. I give substantial founder credit without manufacturing an installed base.

The decisive question is what survives a competitor reproducing the interface. Wondersearch has its own search model and an October 4 benchmark reporting 80.06 nDCG@10 on SciFact for production Large. That is affirmative technical evidence; dismissing this as a commodity API wrapper would ignore the dossier. But a relevance result does not establish an accumulating advantage. There is no demonstrated exclusive training resource or deployment-feedback mechanism that makes the next version progressively harder to reproduce. Pinecone already automates embedding generation, so Wondersearch must sustain an advantage beyond removing an inconvenient setup step.

The strongest argument against waiting is that these founders may already possess the difficult technology, with public benchmarks exposing only a small part of it. My exception for backing a demonstrated prototype before a user network forms applies here. Missing revenue at this stage does not invalidate that case. What limits the exception is the evidence of durability: independent reproduction is absent, production latency and concurrent-load economics remain unresolved, and no customer behavior establishes an advantage that the benchmark misses. I would investigate the model before assigning it the economics of a moat.

Wondersearch also lacks the customer-created compounding I prefer. Its 128,943 launch-post views measure attention. The 1,074 GitHub stars belong to pgGraph, a separate prior product. Neither demonstrates that one Wondersearch customer improves another customer’s experience or acquisition. Self-service access gives developers permissionless access to a tool, but there is no observed contribution loop. The dossier does not yet support my patterns of users contributing useful work or turning individual knowledge into output that benefits subsequent participants.

There is room to maneuver commercially. The same passage-retrieval capability can serve an agent developer initially and enterprise document-search buyers later; API, MCP, and custom deployments provide concrete routes. IDC’s market exceeding $6 billion supports existing adjacent spend, although Wondersearch’s reachable share remains undefined. Pricing at $0.001 per query plus $0.33 per GB per month establishes a direct charging mechanism. It establishes neither completed purchases nor gross profit. H100 benchmark hardware cannot tell me whether those prices produce attractive margins.

I would keep Wondersearch under consideration and decline to write a check today. The documented product changes do not establish the reassurance-seeking or indiscriminate abandonment I avoid, and the dossier supplies no integrity or financing-pressure red flag. The unresolved issue is whether strong technical founders have built an asset that compounds. One result would change my decision: an independently reproduced deployment study showing that feedback Wondersearch has contractual rights to train on improves its proprietary model’s retrieval for subsequent customers relative to a frozen-model control. That would turn deployment into accumulating advantage.
