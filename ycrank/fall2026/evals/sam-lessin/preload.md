# Preload -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Preload’s sensor stack is more legible than its investment proposition. Proposed post-money: unknown. Competing term sheets: unknown. Whether my money would be the first genuinely scarce check: unknown. The database-reported $125,000 YC investment establishes none of those things. I cannot call this an auction because it is in YC, but I cannot underwrite cheap uncertainty without knowing the price. The dossier also supplies no capital plan identifying what this round proves and by when. Those are my two largest criteria, and both receive their missing-evidence defaults.

There is a real technical question worth financing here: does EMG add economically useful information beyond video and tactile sensing when demonstrations transfer onto an actual robot? Preload combines all three modalities, but combining sensors and improving customer outcomes are different accomplishments. Touch2Robot identifies human-to-robot contact mismatch; TACROSS addresses differences between sensor systems. Neither establishes that Preload has solved those problems. A finite experiment could measure incremental robot-task completion against a video-plus-touch baseline. That is a proposed venture moment I can articulate, not a documented use of Preload’s proceeds.

The strongest argument against passing is the team’s unusually relevant preparation. Li brings computational neuroscience and robotics training plus Neuralink experience; Pandiaraju has worked across neural-interface companies; Wang has EMG, motion-tracking, and neural-hardware fabrication experience. This is a plausible bridge between biological sensing and robot learning, and operational complexity itself does not scare me. It fits my pattern of looking for people who can translate between systems. The research and fabrication work earn affirmative founder credit. The dossier does not establish a deployed robotics result or customer-side operating access sufficient to put that criterion near its ceiling.

The commercial mechanism remains unproven. Preload’s launch video attracted 246,186 tracker-reported views, but no purchase, paid pilot, or retained usage is documented. That is attention without demonstrated conversion; it does not establish an owned distribution channel. The possible dark pool is a corpus of difficult-to-reproduce demonstrations with enforceable reuse rights. Neither those rights nor collection volume is verified. Meanwhile, Axonnic already offers EMG capture and Human Archive collects multimodal wearable data. The familiar AI red flag would be assuming that an available technical input becomes durable ownership. Preload has not demonstrated that ownership, although the dossier does not establish that its founders are making the assumption.

Delete the next financing from the model and I still cannot calculate what remains. Dataset collection and licensing are inferred business models, with no verified pricing, delivery costs, margins, burn, or runway. Missing economics are normal at this stage; they earn the specified default rather than an invented adverse finding. I also checked the exceptions to my gates: there is no documented exceptional conversion, retention, acquisition offer, prior founder exit, or alternative financing structure here to override them. My decision is **Pass**. One verifiable event would reopen underwriting: a named robotics customer placing a repeat paid order after independently measuring an improvement attributable to Preload’s EMG data. That would turn the central claim into customer behavior. I would then have something concrete to price.
