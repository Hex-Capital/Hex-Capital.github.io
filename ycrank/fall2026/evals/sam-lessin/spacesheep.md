# Spacesheep -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Spacesheep’s post-money valuation is undisclosed, competing term sheets are undisclosed, and I cannot establish whether my money would be a scarce first check. YC affiliation proves none of those things. I also cannot identify the finite experiment this round funds: what hypothesis, by what date, with what pass/fail result? Those two missing gates receive my stated defaults. They are not evidence of an auction or a bad capital plan, but they leave me unable to underwrite the deal.

Michael Makarov gives me a substantial reason to care. He built internal deployment tooling and the first CC Artifacts version at Anthropic; he encountered the review problem inside the system producing it. Spacesheep connects that experience to a working product where multiple agents publish interactive surfaces and teammates comment, request edits, and retain versions. This is shipped work and direct operating context. It fits my pattern of backing people who translate between systems: agent production on one side, human comprehension and coordination on the other.

The strongest bull case is that independence becomes the product. If teams use several agents, the shared review surface could own the organizational workflow while individual models become interchangeable suppliers. James Briggs’s reported use for coding plans, explanations, and reviews gives that claim a concrete foothold. But the causal chain stops there. Payment and repeat usage are unspecified, and the dossier does not demonstrate that accumulated discussions or relationships caused a purchase or retained usage. Product Hunt’s 180 upvotes and the Chrome extension’s 22 users do not close that gap. I cannot yet identify the proprietary access that keeps customers here.

The competition makes that distinction consequential. Notion already supports external agents and interactive HTML blocks; Anthropic already hosts and shares interactive applications. Spacesheep therefore touches my AI application red flag: useful functionality can converge into an incumbent workspace before an independent business owns the customer relationship. Makarov’s experience makes execution credible, but does not itself establish distribution. The strongest exceptions to my AI and pricing screens involve demonstrated conversion, retention, or unusually hard external validation. The named user is encouraging; it does not yet provide comparable grounds for an override.

Delete the next financing from Spacesheep’s plan. Pro at $4.50/month, Max at $9, and Team at $10/member/month establish asking prices, not cash economics. There is no disclosed margin, burn, or seed-funded break-even plan, so economics receives its default rather than an assumed penalty for model costs. I would pass with my own money today: the builder is substantially better evidenced than the investment mechanism. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision—ten independent teams renewing paid subscriptions after 90 days at positive contribution margin, with those teams acquired through existing-user referrals. That would begin to show that Spacesheep owns a purchasing relationship and a functioning base business.
