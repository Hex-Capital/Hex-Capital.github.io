# Aviern -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Aviern’s strongest path. An aircraft manufacturer could pay to own Aviern’s purpose-built cargo airframe and AviernOS if acquiring the integrated design and software would get it into regional feeder service faster or at lower cost than building or partnering. Aviern reports more than 150 autonomous miles on a scaled demonstrator and targets a 1,000 lb payload for the planned A1000. Its estimated $100 per flight hour is a company projection for aircraft operating cost. The memo records outreach to FedEx, UPS, and DHL, with carrier demand and the economic benefit of ownership still unverified. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

The relevant milestone is a repeatable, regulator-authorized feeder trial with the purpose-built aircraft that measures payload, dispatch reliability, and route cost against a retrofit alternative. The scaled flights and a larger 50 lb test aircraft in development support a staged test plan. Aviern targets a retrofit flight by Q1 2027 and a purpose-built aircraft flight by Q1 2028. The memo records seed financing entries, while the cost, runway, and funding for a feeder trial remain unknown. Aviern’s reported in-house development supports a possible transfer of the core assets, with IP title and transfer terms unverified. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

**Illustrative proceeds.** A $500,000 check at $10 million post-money buys 5%, diluted to 3% after 40% relative dilution and no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million gross before investor taxes. It requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. Using $5 million of assumed net debt and simple pro rata allocation before any additional fees or senior claims, a $25 million enterprise-value sale by year eight returns $600,000 (1.2×). A $100 million sale returns $2.85 million (5.7×), falling to $1.9 million (3.8×) at 2% ownership. The memo’s financing entries leave actual ownership, debt, and preference terms unresolved, and its operating evidence leaves both sale prices hypothetical. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

If feeder growth plateaus, buyer interest fails to emerge, public-market scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and retains illiquid shares. The memo supplies no independent evidence of an eligible secondary, distributable cash for a dividend or buyback, cash generation to finance a sponsor buyout, or operating scale for an IPO. In a wind-down, holder proceeds would depend on residual value after obligations and applicable preferences. The memo provides no basis to estimate that residual. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

A manufacturer could partner for autonomous cargo capability, as the memo documents with Grid Aero and Oshkosh Defense. Carriers could also source retrofit capacity from a provider such as Reliable Robotics, which was selected for an FAA pilot program that includes regional cargo operations. Aviern faces a separate regulatory risk. The draft rule’s 110 lb total-weight cap for certificated package delivery would rule out the planned 1,000 lb payload if that classification applies to its feeder service. The rule is proposed and the classification remains open. The Pass gate remains open because the evidence establishes neither a structural block nor a credible acquisition price that leaves the holder materially short. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a funded, regulator-authorized feeder trial establish an operating advantage over retrofit capacity large enough to support a manufacturer’s case for buying Aviern? Measured payload, dispatch reliability, and route cost would answer the operating question. Move to Reach out if those results support a specific buyer’s build-versus-buy economics at a price that could clear the holder’s $2.5 million target after expected dilution and senior claims.
