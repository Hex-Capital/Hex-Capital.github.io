# antimattr -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest private-company path. An industrial workflow platform could buy antimattr’s voice-isolation technology to make hands-free troubleshooting reliable in noisy factories. The Geocarbonite IntelliPro integration establishes a concrete use case. antimattr reports below 5% word error versus 30% for AirPods in its own comparison, with no disclosed test protocol. The acquisition case depends on a production rollout showing that owning the technology would give a buyer durable control over command reliability and deployment economics that it could not secure through an integration or by building its own solution. (*The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks*)

The company-reported first shipment and two enterprise pilots, together with reported YC funding and grants, make a paid production test plausible at seed. Pilot payment terms, test budget, runway, and the capital needed to scale manufacturing remain unknown. The memo describes privacy controls for conversation capture but provides no evidence on IP assignments or the transferability of relevant agreements. It supplies no usable acquisition-price evidence or independent fallback that could pay this holder. (*Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks*)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** The proposed check terms are unavailable, so the seed case assumes one $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. Under simple pro rata allocation, the required distributable equity value is $83.3 million at 3%, $125 million at 2%, and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume all cash at closing. Net debt, other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, preference leakage, stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and transaction-related compensation are each modeled at $0. Under those assumptions, enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. The prices and dates are scenario assumptions.

| Scenario | Timing and assumed outcome | Gross holder cash | Gross multiple |
|----------|----------------------------|------------------:|---------------:|
| Modest sale | Year 5, $40 million enterprise value | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7, $100 million enterprise value | $3.0 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested | $0, shares retained | 0× |

The strong sale clears the objective at 3% ownership and falls short at 2%, where it returns $2 million, or 4.0×. The memo gives no buyer economics to establish whether either sale price is plausible. (*Market & Traction, Key Facts*)

### Countercase

The existing IntelliPro integration shows that a platform can already access the product through a partnership. Subtle offers voice-isolating dictation earbuds, and Aina offers ring-based agent controls. If a partner can get comparable factory performance through an integration or a competing device, it has little reason to pay an acquisition premium. The memo does not establish performance parity or a price ceiling, so the countercase and the modeled $0 prolonged-private outcome leave the decision at Watch. Confidence is Low because production performance, the advantage of ownership, and buyer economics remain unverified. (*The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid IntelliPro production rollout show that owning antimattr’s voice-isolation technology would create substantially more value for an industrial workflow platform than integrating it or using a substitute? A customer-verified comparison that measures failed commands and rework, then translates the improvement into buyer economics consistent with the modeled exit hurdle, would move the action to Reach out. Comparable results from an ordinary integration would favor Pass if the resulting price ceiling leaves the holder materially short.
