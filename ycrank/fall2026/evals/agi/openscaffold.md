# OpenScaffold -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

New Economies is the strongest path. OpenScaffold aims to reduce the engineering labor spent reconstructing customer requirements from calls, tickets, and GitHub, briefing coding agents, and checking their work. One plausible scenario is that a small implementation that cannot justify dedicated deployment engineering becomes affordable. The memo describes the manual workflow but gives no baseline labor cost or cost per successful deployment. Both are needed to test whether the workflow opens that market. (The Idea)

Faraday’s CEO publicly identified himself as a customer. The founder also reported testing on an unnamed partner’s calls. Those are early demand signals, with payment, repeat use, and uptake by previously excluded buyers still unverified. The founders’ prior deployment work together at Usul and the public demo support their ability to ship and test the product. At scale, contribution per deployment will depend on connector setup, model processing, and human review and rework. The memo gives no measured costs or realized pricing for that expansion unit. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

OpenScaffold may become a feature for existing deployment teams. The founder reported that the product failed to distinguish customer requests from deployment-team requests 82% of the time in one pilot test. Source citations and human review provide a way to catch errors, but the time spent correcting them could erase the labor savings. Open MCP integrations and Harness’s overlapping context features make substitution plausible. Approved playbooks could give teams a reason to stay if they reuse them, but the memo gives no evidence of reuse or buyer preference at functional parity. The pilot leaves the market-opening condition unresolved, substitution remains untested, and the memo supports no independent scarce-complement path. Watch applies. (Key Risks, The Idea, Defensibility)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent buyers pay for and repeat deployments they previously declined on labor cost, and still choose OpenScaffold when a comparable alternative uses equally capable models? A paid field trial should document why those deployments were previously declined, measure labor and total cost per accepted deployment including attribution corrections, and let buyers choose which tool to pay for on the next deployment. Repeat paid use with verified cost savings and preference at parity would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that review erases the savings or that buyers readily switch at parity would move it to Pass.
