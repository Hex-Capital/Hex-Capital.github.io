# Snowstone -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Snowstone’s market opens if recent AI improvements let PE operating teams diagnose portfolio-company problems without reproducing the labor costs of consulting. The controlling variable is the cost of producing a financially validated recommendation that management actually implements. Reading more documents matters only if that cost falls enough to make repeated diagnostics attractive across a sponsor’s portfolio. Snowstone connects operational evidence to EBITDA and cash impact, which is the right mechanism. The dossier does not yet establish that the mechanism produces economical, executable recommendations.

The accuracy evidence makes that distinction concrete. Snowstone reports that dollar estimates fall within 20% of ground truth in 66% of cases on a private benchmark. That may be useful for screening opportunities, especially when calculations have inspectable sources. It does not establish how much expert review is required before someone reallocates capital or changes operations. The founder attributes feasibility to AI advances during the preceding six months, but there is no measured reduction in diagnostic cost or delivery time. I see an identifiable technology opening with unproven economics.

The strongest case for investing is distribution through the PE sponsor. One successful diagnostic could create access to several portfolio companies without reacquiring each buyer independently. The company-reported $2B+ of transaction value reviewed and $50M+ of opportunities identified suggest exposure to substantial financial work. The announced collaboration with Corporate Lifecycles MD and Grey Matter Global offers a possible route into that market. But transaction value measures analysis scope, and identified opportunities measure estimated upside. Neither establishes repeat purchasing or realized savings. I give those figures modest credit for activity, rather than treating them as product-market fit.

Snowstone’s strongest product evidence is the persistence of reviewed entity matches and source/version histories. Those create useful workflow state: better models could improve subsequent analysis while preserving prior validation work. This is more substantial than a disposable financial-summary feature. The boundary matters, though. Access is read-only, and management remains responsible for implementation. Celonis and Skan already address adjacent process-intelligence workflows, so Snowstone needs its PE-specific financial interpretation to justify a distinct purchase. The service-economics red flag would become material if every engagement required substantial bespoke reconciliation; the dossier does not establish whether that is happening.

The founders supply relevant starting knowledge, particularly Lucas Baur’s Winterberg PE diligence and LBO-modeling experience. Moreland’s agentic-systems work also fits the product. Those facts do not establish costly personal commitment or second-time startup learning, so that dimension stays at its default. My pattern of investing across a technology discontinuity makes this category interesting, but does not settle which layer captures value. The research-stage exception also does not apply: the dossier contains neither field-defining frontier research nor a documented capital path that would justify overriding weak demand evidence.

I would pass today. Snowstone could become a useful diagnostic business, but the evidence does not yet distinguish scalable software economics from AI-assisted advisory work. One verifiable flip condition would reopen the investment: a PE sponsor renews a paid deployment across multiple portfolio companies after independently validating realized savings greater than the full diagnostic and implementation costs. That would connect the product, economic value, and reusable distribution in one observable result.
