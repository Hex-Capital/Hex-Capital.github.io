# COACH -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

COACH’s contested truth should be precise: in-person sales performance can be improved systematically by converting previously invisible conversations into individualized behavioral feedback. The falsifier is equally clear: controlled cohorts fail to outperform ordinary manager coaching after selection effects are removed. COACH reports a 27%–32% conversion lift, but publishes neither cohort definitions nor methodology, and Rilla and Siro already pursue substantially the same thesis. The evidence therefore supports customer demand, not a secret named peers reject.

The founders are the strongest part of the company. Mathieu and Thomas Perez previously operated a 44-rep field-sales business that generated €3.5 million in 14 months, so this product emerged from an operating problem they experienced rather than a fashionable search for an AI application. They are brothers who built that business together, and Nicolas Sebag brings in-house mobile engineering experience. His recruitment through LinkedIn weakens the evidence of long technical alignment, while ownership, salaries, and secondary liquidity remain undisclosed. Still, there is no evidence of part-time founders, outsourced core engineering, or investor-majority ownership.

The bull case stings because COACH is not merely a prototype. It reports €1 million ARR within eight months, an earlier $500,000 ARR figure followed by 120% monthly growth, 100% pilot conversion, zero churn, and thousands of reps at five large French customers. Its demo-led, forward-deployed sales motion fits organizations with at least five field reps, and the founders have already sold into the workflow they once managed. This could become a substantial business. Yet pricing, gross margins, sales-cycle length, and customer expansion are absent, so the evidence does not establish that one revenue engine can produce a power-law outcome.

The decisive problem is value capture. COACH’s 74,473 analyzed meetings across 200 companies may improve customer-specific benchmarking, but no evidence shows exclusive outcome data, superior model performance, or retention caused by the corpus. Anthropic supplies the underlying AI and transcription, while two heavily financed competitors already offer mobile recording, analysis, and coaching. “Organizations with five or more field reps” is not a narrow beachhead; it is a broad category definition. COACH does resemble the pattern of making hidden organizational reality legible, but the information is not yet proprietary enough to create a monopoly rather than another interface over commodity models.

The known-exception logic does not rescue the investment: live customer behavior can override execution or regulatory uncertainty, but revenue alone cannot turn a shared product thesis into a zero-to-one technical event. My 36-month base case is that Gong acquires COACH after it reaches €10 million–€20 million ARR, because this team can build and sell faster than it can establish an independent last-mover position. I pass on the angel investment today. I would reverse that judgment if COACH captured more than half of a named field-sales vertical and sustained over 130% net revenue retention for 12 months through exclusive, outcome-linked conversation data.
