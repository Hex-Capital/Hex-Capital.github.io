# Saccade -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition is the strongest path.** A diagnostics company with a downstream blood-testing business could buy Saccade to control an earlier patient referral channel. The memo documents a working eye-tracking headset design and cites a strategic investment in adjacent retinal imaging. Saccade describes its attachment as patent-pending and its model as proprietary. The ownership case depends on Saccade showing that its assessment identifies enough additional asymptomatic patients who benefit from confirmatory testing, and that exclusive control of the model and usable study data is worth more to a buyer than a license or partnership. Buyer interest in Saccade and the economics of those referrals remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is a prospective readout showing that the assessment improves referral decisions against a defined comparator. Saccade reports clinic testing and an IRB-approved study with an advertised enrollment figure of 2,000. Completed enrollment, study costs, and runway are undisclosed, so the memo supports a path to a readout while leaving its timing and funding uncertain. The company plans an FDA submission after publication. Ownership and transfer rights for the university-developed attachment and study data remain unverified. Acuity’s listed price establishes an offer, with realized revenue and paying-customer counts undisclosed. The memo supports no independent route to shareholder cash. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** With no terms for the evaluated check, assume one $500,000 investment at a $10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3%, and a target of $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, transaction fees, and preference adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. All consideration is cash at closing and allocated pro rata. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation each contribute $0. The sale prices are scenario assumptions, and the memo supplies no usable acquisition pricing evidence. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

- **Modest sale in year 5.** $40 million of enterprise value yields $1.2 million to the investor, or 2.4×.
- **Strong sale in year 7.** $100 million of enterprise value yields $3 million, or 6.0×, at 3% ownership. At 2%, it yields $2 million, or 4.0×.
- **Prolonged private through year 8.** Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested. The holder keeps the shares and realizes $0, or 0.0×.

### Countercase

A buyer could get comparable referral value by partnering with another provider. ViewMind already combines VR, eye tracking, and machine learning, with a funded evaluation against amyloid and tau in patients with mild cognitive impairment. Saccade’s terms say its assessment lacks validation for individual risk prediction. The cited Leqembi indication also requires early symptomatic disease and confirmed amyloid pathology, limiting the immediate use of an asymptomatic result. Together, these facts weaken the reason to buy Saccade outright. The memo leaves open whether a validated Saccade assessment could outperform substitutes enough to justify the required price, so the structural Pass override does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
