# Metalcraft -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements** is the stronger case. As life-science models improve, developers could need benchmarks that distinguish which models are reliable enough for particular research workflows. The memo reports that a domain-specific model passed 36.1% of tasks on a separate benchmark versus 25.7% for a general model. That gap illustrates why evaluation can matter for model selection. Metalcraft’s six listed tasksets are observed product evidence. Their published model scores are company-reported, and their ability to predict performance in live research remains unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The November pivot and San Francisco listing make a U.S. seed assessment plausible. Kiely’s computational biology research and applied AI engineering experience are relevant to building the product. Buyer commitment and revenue remain unknown. Under a licensing scenario, scale would depend on reusing validated tasksets across buyers at prices that cover expert authoring, validation, and refresh costs. The memo gives no measured inputs for that calculation. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

OpenAI built an overlapping expert-authored benchmark in-house, and Benchling offers evaluations drawn from laboratory experiments. Even as demand grows, customers and well-funded competitors could supply enough of the same evaluation coverage to limit Metalcraft’s pricing power. The memo leaves exclusive rights and the cost of reproducing Metalcraft’s coverage unknown. Existing alternatives make substitution credible, while equivalence on a buyer’s actual workflow remains untested. Path A therefore falls short of the outreach gate, with a feasible seed-stage test still available. Path B lacks evidence that automation opens a previously uneconomic transaction or that buyers would keep paying under model parity. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
