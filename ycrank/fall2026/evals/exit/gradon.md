# Gradon -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition by an AEC software platform such as Autodesk. Gradon’s Windows agent works across existing design tools and applies each firm’s project history and standards. A platform could acquire those workflows to accelerate automation across disciplines. Gradon reports one project completed in 180 hours against a 750-hour manual estimate. That comparison suggests potential customer value, with independently measured savings after licensed review still open. The memo gives no indication that a buyer has valued the workflows or assessed the cost of building them internally. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The CEO reports reaching mid-six-figure contracted ARR in four weeks. The modeled sale prices depend on sustained revenue growth, and the sale multiple is a sensitivity assumption. Using the shared $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, the holder starts at 5%, dilutes to 3%, and makes no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. Both sale scenarios assume a 100% cash acquisition within eight years, $5 million in net debt and other deal deductions, an eligible holder paid 3% pro rata at close, and no senior claims or escrow. The memo leaves those terms unverified. (Market & Traction)

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $5M ARR × 6 = $30M enterprise value, less $5M = $25M distributable equity | $750,000 (1.5×) |
| Strong sale | $15M ARR × 6 = $90M enterprise value, less $5M = $85M distributable equity | $2.55M (5.1×) |
| Prolonged private | Gradon reinvests available cash through year eight, with no eligible share sale or funded payout | $0 |

At 5× ARR, the strong case yields $70 million in distributable equity and $2.1 million to the holder. The modeled return therefore depends on both revenue scale and the buyer’s willingness to pay.

Live-project pilots and the CEO’s report of civil engineering use give Gradon a starting point for demonstrating paid renewals, repeatable savings after licensed review, and less on-site setup per deployment. At least a week of on-site work per deployment constrains the rollout, and the available capital to scale it is unclear. An acquisition of the software business is plausible, with customer contract assignment and continued access to firm-specific data and workflows still unverified. The memo provides no evidence of an independent route to holder liquidity. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Autodesk already has an assistant in technical preview for Revit and Civil 3D, two tools Gradon uses. If Autodesk can reproduce the workflows customers value inside those applications, its reason to buy weakens. Gradon’s on-site setup and licensed review requirements could also limit the scale and savings a buyer would underwrite. The preview establishes credible substitution risk. The memo leaves both full substitution and a below-hurdle sale price unproven, so the Pass gate is open and Watch is appropriate. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What would a named AEC platform buyer pay to acquire Gradon’s cross-tool workflows after validating the savings that survive licensed review? A buyer’s build-versus-buy assessment that uses results from repeat paid deployments and supports roughly $88.3 million or more in enterprise value under the modeled deductions would move the action to Reach out. If the strongest credible buyer economics cap distributable equity materially below $83.3 million and no independent route emerges, move to Pass.
