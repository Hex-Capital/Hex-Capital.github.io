# Metalcraft -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by an expert-evaluation vendor such as Mercor. Metalcraft’s catalog shows sample tasks and an access request flow, and the company reports model performance by taskset. A vendor already selling expert evaluations could use the catalog to expand its life-science offering. The acquisition rationale depends on whether owning and refreshing the tasksets would be faster or cheaper than building comparable evaluations in-house. That advantage remains unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The six published tasksets and the founder’s computational biology and applied AI experience support a concrete next milestone, a paid production pilot that measures the cost or time saved against the buyer’s internal process. The recent pivot and lack of disclosed revenue leave pilot funding and timing uncertain. The memo provides no evidence on whether data and contributor rights can transfer in a sale or whether an independent route could pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Defensibility, Key Facts)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3% with no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. Both sale scenarios assume closing within eight years, an eligible holder selling its entire stake for upfront cash, and a pro rata waterfall with no preference diversion. The prices and deductions are illustrative assumptions. The memo provides no pricing evidence for either case. (Key Facts)

- **Modest sale.** $30 million in enterprise value less $5 million in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $25 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $750,000, or 1.5×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value less $10 million in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million in distributable equity. The holder receives $2.7 million, or 5.4×. A preference diversion, escrow, or rollover that reduces cash received within eight years by more than $200,000 would put the holder below target.
- **Prolonged private.** Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share purchase or distribution yields zero realized proceeds within eight years.

### Countercase

OpenAI and Benchling have already built overlapping evaluations. A prospective acquirer could build its own or license Metalcraft’s tasksets without buying the company, especially while exclusive data rights and the tasksets’ value in live research remain unverified. This weakens the case for paying an acquisition premium. The evidence leaves the price and transferability unresolved, so neither the structural-block nor the inadequate-proceeds gate triggers Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would owning Metalcraft’s tasksets save an evaluation vendor enough time or money to justify a sale that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A paid production pilot showing a repeatable advantage over in-house development, paired with evidence that the relevant rights can transfer and buyer economics that support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity, would move the action to Reach out. Credible buyer economics that cap proceeds materially below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
