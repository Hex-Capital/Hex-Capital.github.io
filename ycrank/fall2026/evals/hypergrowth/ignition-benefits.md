# Ignition Benefits -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit, Ignition’s strongest path is a software-assisted U.S. benefits brokerage for employers with 25–500 employees. A free renewal review, paid referrals, and CFO webinars offer repeatable ways to find employers when they are ready to shop. Ignition says its risk analysis helps licensed brokers compare carrier quotes. Its Light Labs case study reports a completed plan switch that saved $113,115 against the renewal quote while the employer kept Gusto for payroll. The intake and analysis are reusable, while licensed brokers still handle placement and enrollment. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Annual renewals and ongoing eligibility work give employers a reason to stay. Ignition’s Mango case study describes that ongoing work inside the customer’s payroll system. Ignition says carriers pay the brokerage, but the memo leaves commission terms and same-account expansion economics unclear. The reported plan switches establish production adoption. Realized carrier payments and the pace of commercial growth remain unverified, so initial traction receives the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Incremental contribution per placed employer depends on carrier compensation less acquisition, broker time for quoting and enrollment, and ongoing support. If each qualified referral triggers the stated $1,000 payout, referral cost per placed account is $1,000 divided by the referral-to-placement conversion rate. Automating renewal analysis could reduce broker time, but the memo provides no conversion rates, commission amounts, or broker hours to establish contribution. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The same renewal problem could recur abroad, and the risk-scoring software may be reusable. The strongest current product, however, is the full brokerage service. It depends on carrier access, usable claims and workforce data, licensed placement, and local enrollment and compliance workflows. Even with strong U.S. fit, serving comparable employers abroad would likely require substantial market-by-market work. The memo identifies no credible near-term way to transfer that service with bounded changes, which triggers the structural downside gate. Required broker labor limits onboarding scalability, while its effect on contribution remains uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

A completed placement with documented, bounded changes to carrier integration and compliance would move the action to Watch by addressing the portability gate.
