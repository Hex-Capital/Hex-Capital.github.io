# Raven -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Raven’s consequential possibility is making routine knowledge work disappear into delegated execution. Reading Gmail, updating a Drive document, and sharing through Slack could remove human coordination from entire workflows. But how much labor disappears, for whom, and at what cost? The dossier does not establish that baseline. Gartner’s $393.3 billion smartphone-spending forecast measures device purchases, not the work Raven could eliminate. I can imagine a large consequence; I cannot underwrite an imagined one.

The working prototype earns attention. An organizer-confirmed demonstration of flight search, generated interfaces, and calendar actions is evidence of building. It is not yet evidence of a technical discontinuity. Brain already has SoftBank distribution for an intention-based phone; Rabbit executes across existing devices. Raven must establish why its execution capability is substantially better and why that capability requires another phone. Employee positioning alone does not resolve either question. The standard-model interface red flag remains an unresolved diligence question: the dossier establishes neither proprietary capability nor that Raven is merely a wrapper.

The strongest argument against passing is Isa Usmanov himself. His reported prior record includes $3.4 million in GMV and 32,000 users at droov, an ed-tech business with $1.1 million ARR, and the Reinio sale. Now he has built a demonstrated prototype and reports initial shipments. That is a doer with commercial experience. My actual investing pattern includes execution businesses; scientific novelty is not compulsory. Yet those prior results do not document fast belief revision or recruiting complementary people against specific risks. Raven’s 160,000 demo views and hundreds of signups show attention, while shipment quantities, payment status, and repeated usage remain undisclosed. They cannot establish that employees will delegate consequential work.

The first dollar should attack reliable execution. A calendar deletion can succeed technically and still be the wrong action. Raven needs a bounded workflow test that measures completed work, human correction, and recovery from mistakes. The dossier supplies no threshold, experiment budget, or stop condition, so I assign the process default rather than treating a hackathon win as risk retirement. Economics receives its default too: there is no device price, inference-cost model, support burden, or useful-work cost comparison. Those omissions do not prove bad economics. They leave the route to globally affordable automation unestablished.

I pass with my own money today. The known exceptions require either a documented instigator addressing a giant established value pool or extraordinary evidence of transformative consequence. Usmanov’s operating record makes that possibility credible, but Raven has not supplied the mechanism-specific evidence to invoke an override. My one verifiable flip condition is an independently observed, paid workplace deployment demonstrating at least 10x lower human handling time on a defined recurring workflow, at matched completion quality and lower fully loaded cost including the device. That would connect the prototype to both consequence and economic value. Another viral demonstration would leave the decision unchanged.
