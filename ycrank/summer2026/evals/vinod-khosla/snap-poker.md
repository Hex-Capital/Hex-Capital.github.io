# Snap Poker -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The maximum consequence here is not large enough. Snap Poker could make online poker more social and improve discovery inside a $4.7 billion global market, but it does not collapse the cost of an expert service, unlock a resource bottleneck, or extend essential infrastructure to billions. Worse, real-money gambling is a category I explicitly avoid. A successful follower graph attached to wagering is still engagement optimization around gambling, not a 100x societal consequence.

The product discontinuity is also absent. GGPoker already offers video, voice, reactions, and shareable Hand Moments; PokerStars already offers hand replay and sharing. Moving those artifacts into a native public feed may reduce sharing friction, but the dossier supplies no measured improvement in retention, liquidity, creator growth, or acquisition cost. This is a feature-and-distribution hypothesis against named incumbents, not a technically auditable 5–10x breakthrough. No proprietary learning loop or present moat changes that conclusion.

The strongest bull case is that the founders are unusually young technical doers who have already abandoned CueBench, Pennsylvania expanded the shared interstate player pool beyond 38 million Americans, and zero rake could attract liquidity while player-created reels provide organic distribution. A native social graph might then turn a commodity poker table into a network. That possibility is real, and the pivot shows tactical flexibility. But no evidence establishes why they pivoted, repeat product usage, poker expertise, gaming-compliance competence, or a differentiated monetization mechanism. The known exceptions to my technical-discontinuity requirement involved enormous value pools and founders or platforms capable of expanding far beyond the opening product. Snap Poker establishes neither override.

Capital should first remove the regulatory and liquidity kill risks. The decisive experiment is not another feed feature: it is obtaining a lawful operating route in one named jurisdiction and showing that a bounded player cohort reaches sufficient simultaneous liquidity without paid subsidies. Snap Poker discloses no license, geolocation plan, experiment threshold, budget, or stop condition. It also inherits a prior “Snap Poker” name used by 888poker and adds voice-content moderation to an already regulated operation. These are several expensive dependencies being combined before the central behavior has been proven.

The economics remain equally ungrounded. Zero rake is attractive to players but removes the incumbent revenue engine; “monetize like a game” is not a scale model. There are no disclosed virtual goods, subscriptions, conversion assumptions, moderation costs, compliance costs, or evidence that monetization can support liquidity without exploiting gambling behavior. Free play subsidized by venture capital is not economic parity.

My 36-month base case is a pivot from regulated real-money poker into free-to-play social poker or another gaming product after licensing and liquidity prevent the current network from reaching scale. I would reverse this decision on one verifiable result: a licensed cohort showing at least a 10x improvement over a named incumbent in organic acquisition or 30-day retention, with positive contribution economics under a disclosed non-rake monetization model. Until then, this is 1x downside attached to neither a 100x consequence nor a Black-Swan mechanism. Strong pass.
