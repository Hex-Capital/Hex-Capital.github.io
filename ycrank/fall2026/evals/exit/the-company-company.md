# The Company Company -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an agent automation platform such as Gumloop is the strongest plausible route. The Company Company’s shared sessions and ticket-to-pull-request workflow could give an acquirer a way to add engineering execution to its existing automation and company knowledge products. The public beta is open for subscriptions, and a named customer reported using the product for months to coordinate multiple agents. The founder’s reported internal pull-request volume shows claimed use at scale within the company. The memo gives no measured customer savings or evidence of buyer interest. At seed, the next milestone is repeatable time savings and sustained paid use across several external teams. The beta makes that test practical, while the solo team and undisclosed runway leave its timing uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years for a 5× return, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale cases below are illustrative assumptions. Both assume $10 million in net debt and deal deductions, an all-cash close within eight years, and an eligible holder selling the entire stake with pro rata proceeds and no preference diversion.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Cash to holder |
|----------|-----------------------------|----------------|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $10M yields $30M in distributable equity | $900K, or 1.8× |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M yields $90M in distributable equity | $2.7M, or 5.4× |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with available cash reinvested and no eligible share purchase or payout | $0 realized |

In the strong case, selling half the stake at closing would yield $1.35 million. The memo supplies no pricing evidence for a sale, holder waterfall or eligibility terms, or evidence of an independent liquidity route. The figures show what a deal would need to deliver. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Gumloop already offers agents and a company knowledge layer, and OpenAI Frontier offers shared business context and agent execution. A prospective buyer could extend its own product, especially while customer retention and switching costs remain unverified. Repository access and the model-training opt-out also leave customer consent and data transfer open in diligence. These issues weaken the case for a premium acquisition, but the memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible price range that would trigger the inadequate-proceeds gate. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would owning the ticket-to-pull-request workflow create enough value for a specific strategic buyer to justify a price that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Measured time savings across several retained, paying teams, paired with buyer feedback quantifying the value of acquisition over an internal build, would move the action to Reach out if it supported roughly $93.3 million in enterprise value under the modeled deductions and payout terms.
