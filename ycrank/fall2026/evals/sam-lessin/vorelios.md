# Vorelios -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Vorelios has published model results, but I cannot yet identify the investment I would be buying. Post-money valuation: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. Whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital: unknown. YC participation proves neither an auction nor a bargain. The dossier also names no experiment the round finances, no deadline, and no result that would settle it. Those are missing underwriting inputs; I would not turn them into accusations about the founders.

The interesting possibility is that internally generated simulations let Vorelios cheaply explore designs that aerospace engineers currently leave unexplored. The company reports **1.1–31 millisecond inference on an H100**, with **6.37% pressure error and 3.81% skin-friction error** on the NASA Common Research Model ([results](https://vorelios.com/results/)). That is concrete technical work. The strongest argument against passing is that even an imperfect predictor could change engineering economics if it screens candidates before expensive conventional simulation. It could earn its place without replacing the final solver. But agreement with solver outputs does not yet establish useful decisions on unfamiliar customer geometries, and the dossier documents no repeat engineering usage.

That distinction determines whether the simulation corpus becomes a proprietary asset. Generating data internally gives the team control over an input; it does not establish that the input causes purchases or remains difficult to reproduce. Vorelios currently offers a founder contact form, with no documented customer conversion or embedded workflow. Meanwhile, **Luminary already offers simulation-data generation, model building, and aircraft-wing models** ([announcement](https://luminary.ai/resources/luminary-cloud-secures-72m-series-b-to-lead-the-physics-ai-era/)). The relevant AI red flag is dependence on a performance lead that competitors can contest. I see a potential fit with my preference for software reorganizing difficult physical systems, but the customer access that makes those businesses durable remains unproven.

McNeil’s physics background and Kapadia’s **2025–2026 autonomy-lab research** provide relevant technical grounding; the published model results add an execution receipt beyond their university affiliations. That earns modest founder credit. There is no documented aerospace deployment or operating access on the buyer’s side of the bridge. Nor does the dossier provide pricing, gross margin, burn, or a seed-funded route to break-even. Fast inference alone cannot establish profitability when training-data generation, validation, and customer support costs are unknown. These are unanswered questions at pre-seed, not evidence of bad economics.

I pass at **29/100**. I checked the exception case: unusually strong paid conversion, independently demonstrated demand, or a proven founder paired with an explicit financing structure could outweigh missing gate evidence. Vorelios supplies technical benchmarks, but none of those overrides. My verifiable flip condition is **a paid aerospace deployment on previously withheld customer geometries that renews at pricing covering simulation generation, inference, and engineering support**. That would connect the technical result to owned workflow and cash economics, giving me a reason to reopen the investment and underwrite its terms.
