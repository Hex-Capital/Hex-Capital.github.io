# Definite -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Definite’s shift from end-to-end bank regulatory reporting to a broader finance-agent context layer makes Definite an adaptable workflow-software company whose strongest signal is founder learning and whose weakest is a capital profile too ordinary for my edge. **Slash** is the high rung on learning rate because its documented Kanye West pivot exposed evidence, changed the thesis, and produced a new business. Definite may have made a similarly meaningful update, but the dossier supplies neither the founders’ reasoning nor customer evidence showing that the change was learned rather than merely repositioned. A changed website is not yet a demonstrated learning loop.

**DoorDash** was the low rung on required capital: capital-light execution would not have triggered my science-investing framework, and only the prospect of reorganizing consumer commerce supplied the override. Definite has the same capital mismatch without DoorDash’s network-scale consequence. Definite’s three-person software operation, read-only integrations, and absence of any disclosed training, infrastructure, or deployment requirement above $50 million mean any competent generalist can finance it. I am over-resourced for a business whose apparent milestone is enterprise ARR rather than a technical miracle.

The **$10.84 billion** accounting and financial-close market defines how Definite’s consequence mechanism compounds—and where it stops. Over 18 months, Definite can accumulate connectors, mapping rules, and reusable reconciliation logic across NetSuite, Workday, Stripe, Bill.com, and Ramp; that could reduce onboarding costs and make Definite operationally useful. But agents that only flag, draft, and notify do not break the finance-labor mechanism because humans or other systems must still execute every consequential action. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself.” A better close workflow may make a good company; it does not yet make accounting labor disappear.

Immutable snapshots, per-value lineage, deterministic verdicts, and hash-chained receipts would move Definite into a different technical tier if Definite could show proprietary mapping accuracy across thousands of heterogeneous ledgers, an accumulating reconciliation dataset, and safe autonomous write-back. The dossier instead reports no patents, dataset scale, switching evidence, or mapping-accuracy measurements, while Numeric, Rillet, FloQast, n8n, and Workato attack adjacent layers with far more capital. Definite currently requires integration persistence and trust engineering, not a falsifiable scientific breakthrough. That is software execution risk—the risk every Tier-1 generalist already funds.

**Physical Intelligence** paired Stanford’s Chelsea Finn and Berkeley’s Sergey Levine with a public intellectual break from conventional robot programming. Definite presents the opposite founder pattern: Mazin Al-Ani brings Optiver and finance-AI experience, Farhan Ur Rehman brings Meta recommendation systems, and Gurshabd Singh Varaich brings BitGo approval controls. Those are relevant credentials, but Definite applies familiar expertise to familiar finance infrastructure; no founder has publicly contradicted the field, abandoned an incumbent playbook, or named an expert consensus that must be wrong. Experts can build excellent products. They rarely generate the non-consensus distribution I seek.

Definite’s YC launch described regulatory reporting for banks before Definite’s website broadened the product into customer-built finance agents. My 36-month forecast is that by July 2029, Definite will have narrowed back to audit-ready regulatory reporting for banks and fintechs, reaching **$1 million–$3 million ARR**, rather than becoming the universal live model of enterprise books. Definite’s strongest attribute—willingness to reposition—should help it find a sellable wedge, while its weakest—no capital-intensive proprietary stack—will keep that wedge inside conventional enterprise software.

**Glean** is the strongest objection because a context layer can begin as ordinary enterprise plumbing and become the control plane for economically valuable work. Definite’s canonical entities, source lineage, deterministic checks, and hash-chained receipts directly attack Deloitte’s named trust barrier; if those primitives become the trusted substrate for autonomous finance, Definite could replace labor rather than optimize it. The objection fails today because Definite is read-only, reports no customers or proprietary data advantage, and faces Numeric, Rillet, and FloQast before demonstrating switching costs. I would flip to Invest if Definite produces at least **10 named enterprise customers, $5 million ARR, and audited evidence that write-enabled agents complete reconciliations or regulatory filings without human execution**.
