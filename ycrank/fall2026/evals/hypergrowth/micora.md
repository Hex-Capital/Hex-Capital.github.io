# Micora -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with remote industrial operators, Micora’s strongest growth path is leasing the same modular solar and battery system across markets. Remote manufacturing, R&D, and infrastructure sites share an ongoing need for power where grid access is limited. Stackable solar blocks and containerized batteries give Micora a reusable core, and the company reports deployments underway at Pacific island and manufacturing sites. How much redesign, approval work, and delivery effort each new market would require remains unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The configurator documents a repeatable inquiry flow and 12- or 24-month lease options. A lease can generate recurring revenue while a site needs power. Additional sites could increase an account’s spend, though renewals and paid expansion remain unverified. The projects under construction are company-reported, and the memo leaves payment status and realized revenue unknown, so Initial traction uses the 7-point default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Every order still requires site review for final pricing, plus design and commissioning. Terrain, weather, and continuous-power requirements can add work or backup equipment. For a lease, the cost bridge starts with contracted payments and subtracts attributable sales, engineering, freight, installation, commissioning, support, and the cost of financing and allocating the hardware. Robotic placement could reduce field labor, and redeployment could spread hardware cost across leases. Both are assumptions whose impact depends on field performance and utilization. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo leaves the extent of market-specific rebuilding and the economics of required site work unresolved. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
