# NebuSec -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

NebuSec demonstrates that its founders can find vulnerabilities. What important truth have they discovered that competing security companies reject? The dossier supplies no such proposition. Its September 2026 benchmark reports 88% coverage against 80% for Codex Security and 74% for Claude Security across 76 known vulnerabilities. That is a measurable performance claim, but an eight-percentage-point lead on a vendor-selected benchmark does not establish a secret about security. I assign the absent-evidence default here; the absence of a stated secret is not evidence that the founders have none.

The stronger objection is that NebuSec could create considerable value without retaining much of it. Semgrep already combines static analysis, LLM reasoning, and remediation; Aikido offers overlapping detection, validation, and repair. NebuSec has not identified a bounded customer segment where it possesses exclusive data, dominant adoption, or a demonstrated technical advantage large enough to prevent substitution. Published exploits establish competence, but publishing them does not establish exclusive ownership. This is an adverse competitive structure for my framework, rather than merely an undisclosed moat.

The strongest case against passing is substantial: these are researchers with operating credibility, not founders borrowing cybersecurity vocabulary. The team reports more than $400,000 in Google vulnerability rewards, publishes working exploits, and includes experience in program analysis, automated repair, and commercial security research. More importantly, PingCAP independently confirms engaging NebuSec for an investigation completed by October 23, 2026. That customer behavior could be the beginning of a business in which difficult investigations expose repeatable problems that ordinary scanners miss. It fits my unstated preference for adversarial information systems that make contested facts legible. But the technical-breakthrough exception requires independent proof of the product’s exceptional capability; the persistent-demand exception requires demonstrated continuing use. Neither is established by one investigation and a benchmark of known bugs.

The founding structure remains an open question. Zou, Tan, and Wu share UC Riverside affiliations, while Wu and Yu share r3kapig membership. Those connections are consistent with an existing operator network, another pattern I favor, but affiliations do not document years of shipping together. Tan’s departure from doctoral studies supports his commitment; it cannot establish every founder’s employment status or economic exposure. There is no documented part-time founder, outsourced core engineering, or investor-dominated ownership to trigger my founding red flags. I therefore use the stated default rather than rewarding credentials as alignment.

Distribution is the most concrete business signal. Research publications, public exploit releases, and conference outreach provide plausible routes to technical buyers, while PingCAP establishes that at least one organization entrusted the team with consequential work. The dossier does not connect that engagement to a particular acquisition channel or disclose repeat purchases, sales cycles, or delivery costs. Likewise, fixed scan quotes and several subscription tiers describe ways to charge, but provide no pricing or margin arithmetic for a power-law outcome. At this stage, missing revenue is ordinary; the unresolved investment question is whether each additional customer buys scalable software or consumes scarce researcher time.

I would pass on NebuSec today. The team has earned credibility as investigators; the product has not yet earned a monopoly thesis. Neither the research reputation nor the named engagement directly resolves that gate, and the deployment-based exception supplies no override for competitive software economics. My single flip condition is an independently audited, paid deployment across a defined customer segment showing that NebuSec repeatedly discovers exploitable vulnerabilities missed by competing tools, with recurring software use and minimal researcher intervention. That would connect its demonstrated expertise to an ownable business.
