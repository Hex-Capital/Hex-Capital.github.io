# Agent 37 -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Agent 37’s most important number is the $4.94 Boon reportedly spends per agent each month. That is an observed customer expense, down from $85, and it explains why somebody would move. It does not tell me whether Agent 37 makes money serving that customer. My proposed post-money, competing-term-sheet count, and evidence that this is the first scarce check are all missing. The reported $500,000 YC investment establishes funding, not an attractive entry price. I would pass on the evidence available.

There is a functioning business underneath the agent enthusiasm. Boon reportedly deployed its fleet in three days after spending six months building on Render; ETEC+ migrated more than 30 existing clients onto its branded product. Those are concrete workflow decisions. They make the distribution case substantially stronger than the 651 GitHub stars or June 2026 Product Hunt award. But what does Agent 37 own after onboarding? The dossier establishes useful deployment infrastructure, with some potential switching friction. It does not establish exclusive customer access, proprietary data, or an acquisition channel competitors cannot reproduce.

The strongest objection to my pass is that inexpensive infrastructure can become a good business before it becomes a defensible platform. Vishnu has shipped managed hosting, attracted more than 30 paying customers on the earlier product’s launch day, and now reports over 100 companies running agents. His Amazon and Quantcast experience supplies relevant operating context, although the dossier identifies no specific prior infrastructure deployment or result attributable to him. The current product is the stronger receipt. A founder translating infrastructure complexity into something customers can deploy in days fits my preference for businesses joining two operating systems that otherwise communicate badly.

The difficulty is underwriting the price advantage. E2B already offers state-preserving pause/resume, and Daytona offers broader environments while also supplying Agent 37’s template builds and image imports. Published low prices therefore need an explanation in operating economics. Memory-pressure restarts and polling agents that must remain awake make utilization and support costs particularly consequential. Neither proves negative margins, but neither earns credit toward profitability. The company-wide “100k ARR” claim lacks a currency and product split; gross margin, burn, and runway are absent. I cannot turn customer savings into a seed-funded route to break-even.

Agent 37 also has no documented capital-funded experiment with a metric and deadline. That leaves both primary gates at their missing-evidence defaults; I am not inferring an auction or a financing treadmill from silence. I checked the exceptions: demonstrated customer migration makes this more substantial than a model-dependent application, but the dossier supplies neither the measured conversion and retention nor the cash economics that would justify overriding those gates. My verifiable flip condition would be six consecutive months of positive operating cash flow from Cloud, including infrastructure, support, and engineering payroll. That would demonstrate that the low price supports an independent business. Today, Agent 37 has proved that customers will move workloads; it has yet to prove why my capital belongs in the company.
