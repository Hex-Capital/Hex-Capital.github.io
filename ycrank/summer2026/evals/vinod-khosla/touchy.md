# Touchy -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence-of-success case is too small as currently stated. Touchy eliminates manual app switching for iPhone users; the dossier does not quantify hours recovered, expert labor displaced, or a resource bottleneck changed by orders of magnitude. An $18.60 billion market growing 18.8% annually describes spending, not consequence. This is convenience until proven otherwise.

The product combines voice, camera input, memory, and more than 1,000 integrations, but supplies no measured discontinuity. Apple’s Siri AI already claims personal context, visual understanding, and system-level cross-app actions. Touchy offers neither a task-completion benchmark against Siri nor proprietary architecture, data, or a demonstrated learning loop. Falling inference costs of 5–10x annually benefit every competitor. They do not constitute Touchy’s advantage.

The founders are the reason to keep watching. Rohit brings agent-alignment research, Andrew built educational software used by roughly 500,000 students, and Kevin has credible infrastructure experience plus a 2,500-star repository. Their rapid move from Tokenless infrastructure to a consumer assistant could reflect useful tactical flexibility. It could equally reflect two months of thesis instability. The dossier gives no customer evidence that caused the revision and no recruit brought in specifically to own safety, consumer distribution, or privileged-data risk.

The largest kill risk is already visible: an assistant touching messages, photos, location, and third-party applications must act reliably enough to deserve trust. The founders themselves say current models are insufficient and “airtight safety guarantees” are required. Yet there is no decisive experiment, failure threshold, budget, or stop condition attached to that risk. The live app has too few ratings for an App Store overview, while the $14.99 Touchy Plus listing does not disclose even its billing interval. Neither usage nor economics currently demonstrates that users value the capability enough to offset inference, integration, and support costs.

The strongest bull case is the known exception of **Block**: a tiny technical team can begin with an undifferentiated interface and expand into a platform when it attacks a giant existing value pool. Touchy shares the small-team adaptability. The material difference is that Block had an expensive financial workflow to reorganize, while Touchy documents a $2.363 billion U.S. assistant-software segment and an interface controlled by Apple. No proprietary transaction network, learning loop, or adjacent value pool yet supplies the override.

I pass today. This is a capable team building on a commodity intelligence layer beneath a platform owner with overlapping functionality. My view would flip on one verifiable result: a held-out benchmark showing that real users complete cross-app tasks at least 5x more reliably with Touchy than with Siri AI, with fewer than one unauthorized action per 10,000 executions. That would convert an attractive interface into a technical option worth buying.
