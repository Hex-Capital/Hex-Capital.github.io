# Deploy -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Deploy’s strongest case is that adaptable robot arms and shared remote supervision could make per-station automation viable for third-party logistics operators whose changing mix of returns, kitting, and packing makes fixed automation impractical. The public demo packed more than 800 pens after about 30 minutes of fine-tuning data collection. That shows adaptation to a single task. Labor per successful job, including exception handling, remains unmeasured on a representative customer workflow. The San Francisco team is early enough to assess against a seed-stage field trial. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks; Founders & Team)

Deploy advertises a monthly fee roughly half the cost of manual labor, with no setup fee or equipment purchase. **Scenario assumption:** If that price holds at equivalent throughput and quality, the fee must cover equipment amortization, installation, maintenance, inference, and remote staffing at realistic utilization. Under model parity, buyers could still pay Deploy for installation and reliable recovery. Whether Deploy can deliver those services better than another integrator depends in part on whether intervention data improves recovery across sites. The memo leaves that transfer and buyer commitment unverified. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks; Key Facts)

### Countercase

Mixed-item failures could cluster and require enough remote staffing to erase the advertised savings. Plus One already offers remote exception handling in adjacent workflows, so a comparably capable rival could reproduce much of Deploy’s service if its recovery know-how proves easy to replicate. Under abundant competing capital, the memo identifies no separately controlled bottleneck that supports Path A. Both Path B conditions remain open, which keeps Deploy below the outreach gate. The narrow booth demo and adjacent competition leave room for a field test. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will a logistics operator that previously kept a mixed-item station manual pay to expand Deploy after a production pilot with a comparably capable alternative? A paid expansion would support Reach out if the pilot shows lower human labor per correctly completed job at matched quality and throughput, positive station contribution after observed peak exception staffing, and a measured recovery or integration advantage that drove the buyer’s choice. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)
