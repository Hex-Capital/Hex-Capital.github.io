# Micora -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales with account expansion.** The clearest first buyer is an operator of a remote manufacturing site that needs ongoing power where grid access is limited and diesel depends on deliveries. Micora’s configurator captures system size, location, and schedule before a site review. Micora reports projects under construction, including deployments at Pacific island and manufacturing sites. The island work is an early signal of geographic reach, though the memo leaves the locations, buyer identities, and payment status unclear. It records no buyer-confirmed benefit, realized revenue, or repeat orders. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

If the first installation delivers reliable power at an acceptable price, the same operator could add capacity or order systems for more sites. That expansion is a scenario assumption. The proposed repeatable rollout uses the same modular solar blocks, containerized batteries, and robotic placement process. In a second geography, shipping, terrain, weather, permitting, and backup power requirements could change the time and cost to commission the same system. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Micora could scale like a site-by-site engineering business. Each additional installation adds equipment, freight, a survey, design, and commissioning, with backup generation where the load requires it. Robotic placement could reduce installation labor, but the memo contains no independent field results or deployment costs to show how much it saves. If Micora funds the leased equipment, each new lease also ties up capital, and any redeployment benefit depends on the cost of moving the system and securing its next customer. The site-dependent scope and timing make this countercase credible. The structural Pass gate requires evidence that these commitments scale proportionally or worse without a supported path to improvement. The memo leaves that test unresolved. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Micora win and commission a paid installation for an independent remote manufacturing buyer in a second geography through the same sales and deployment process? A paid invoice and commissioning record, paired with comparable records from the first site, would show whether engineering and crew hours stayed bounded and whether the price covered hardware, freight, installation, backup where needed, and any lease financing costs. A successful repeat on those terms would move the action to Reach out.
