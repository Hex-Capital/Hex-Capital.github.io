# Plek AI -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition by a workforce-planning platform seeking to add task-level underwriting to its PE diligence product. Plek says its software traces modeled EBITDA improvements to individual employees and reconciles overlapping savings. It reports that one pilot matched an advisory firm’s six-week estimate of $30 million in potential annual EBITDA improvements. That comparison gives a buyer a reason to test whether Plek can shorten diligence while preserving the quality of the analysis. Main Capital’s announced majority acquisition of Orgvue shows transaction interest in adjacent workforce software. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The proceeds case depends on what a buyer could earn by selling Plek’s product to its existing customers or save by acquiring it instead of building it. Orgvue’s £34 million in FY2025 UK entity turnover gives some evidence of commercial scale in the adjacent category. The acquisition price was undisclosed, and Plek’s order-form pricing leaves its own revenue and buyer economics unknown. Under the benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. A 5× return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. Assume the holder is eligible to sell the full stake for cash within that period, with zero net debt, deal costs, or senior claims. A modest $40 million enterprise-value sale yields $1.2 million (2.4×), and a strong $100 million sale yields $3 million (6×). At the strong price, $20 million in combined net debt, deal costs, and senior claims would reduce the common equity pool to $80 million and the payout to $2.4 million (4.8×). In the prolonged-private scenario with cash reinvested, realized proceeds are zero. The sale prices are scenario assumptions. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Plek reports four completed pilots, a deployment at a $2 billion PE fund, and advisory onboarding. A plausible 12–18 month milestone is for the fund deployment to convert into paid use across multiple diligence engagements, with measured turnaround and customer validation of the estimates. That would test repeatability on work Plek already performs. Paid conversion, runway, and the cost of supporting repeat deployments remain unknown. Customer database isolation could help a buyer take over the product, while IP ownership, contract assignment, and permissions covering employee survey data remain open. The memo supplies no evidence of an independent liquidity route for this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Orgvue already sells M&A workforce modeling, and Reejig already maps work at the task level. A platform could extend its existing tools and avoid acquiring Plek. The reported $30 million represents modeled customer savings, and the memo shows neither realized savings nor repeat paid demand. That makes the price hurdle uncertain. The evidence supports Watch because it establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible ceiling below the holder’s objective. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What would a workforce-planning platform pay for Plek once a PE customer has used it on multiple paid diligence engagements? A buyer-side valuation range grounded in those engagements, quantified cross-sell potential, and the cost of building the capability internally would answer it. Move to Reach out if the range credibly supports at least $83.3 million in distributable equity value within eight years. Move to Pass if the strongest credible range leaves the holder materially short.
