# Decent -- Elad Gil Evaluation

AI could make continuous benefits advice economical for small employers, but Decent’s opening depends on how many employers have claims data it can actually use. That is the controlling variable: usable data allows intervention before renewal, which could turn an annual insurance transaction into a recurring service. The restriction matters. Decent excludes fully insured and PEO arrangements from its claims-report process even though it shops across those arrangements. The dossier identifies the mechanism and its boundary, but does not establish what proportion of the addressable customer base clears that boundary.

The economic discontinuity is plausible. Decent reports that AI executes 89% of brokerage work, while its cited industry figure puts 51% of covered workers at employers with 10–199 employees in self-funded or level-funded plans in 2025. Those are meaningful reasons to investigate this market now. They do not establish software economics. Automating routine tasks can leave most costs concentrated in exceptions, licensed advice, and customer support. Provider commissions and bonuses remain the revenue model; there is no disclosed servicing-cost or contribution-margin evidence showing how much of the automation benefit Decent retains.

The strongest argument for writing a check is that customers appear to be getting something concrete already. Within roughly a month of the September 29, 2026 announcement of the new entity, Decent reports 67 companies working with it. Serif Health attributes $60,000 in annual savings to its comparisons; Locate reports more than $100,000 against a renewal proposal. These are company-hosted testimonials and an ambiguous customer count, but they carry considerably more information than registrations. The limitation is causal: the testimonials support better insurance shopping. They do not yet validate year-round claims intervention as the reason employers choose or retain Decent.

The product also has more substance than an AI-generated recommendation. Licensed brokerage operations and carrier/PEO quoting relationships put the team inside an actual purchasing workflow. Better models could improve that work. However, the dossier does not document integrations, accumulated operational state, or a completed claims-to-renewal feedback loop. Nava already offers year-round advice, AI tools, and bill audits, and now advertises its acquisition by Alliant. This market has established distribution and active consolidation. Decent must demonstrate an economic advantage that survives competitors adopting similar tools; presumed proprietary claims data earns no credit without evidence of exclusive access or reproduction cost.

Soman’s earlier health-plan business, acquired by Nice Healthcare, and experience operating a PEO make this a credible second attempt in a market he knows. Budker brings production AI experience from Meta, and the founders have known each other since working on Kindle approximately 16 years ago. That combination fits my preference for repeat operators applying technology to an existing service market. I would give credit for prior operating experience without assuming desperation, a small prior exit, or lessons the dossier does not describe. Production AI credentials also do not establish the frontier-research exception that could justify overlooking weak commercial evidence.

I would monitor Decent rather than write a personal check today. The bull case is uncomfortable to dismiss: experienced founders, named customer savings, and substantial claimed automation could produce an unusually efficient brokerage before its public metrics mature. The unresolved issue is whether continuous service changes the economics enough to create a large independent company. My flip condition is a completed-renewal cohort demonstrating at least 50% lower fully loaded servicing cost per retained employer than comparable conventional brokerage accounts, including claims review and licensed human work. That would turn the automation claim into evidence that technology changes the business’s margin structure.
