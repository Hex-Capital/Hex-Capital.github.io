# Caveman -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Caveman has 115,500 GitHub stars. What does my check buy? The dossier gives no proposed post-money, competing-term-sheet count, or capital-use experiment. Those are unknowns; YC backing does not establish an auction. But developer enthusiasm cannot answer whether I am buying a cheap, unresolved belief or paying for attention already acquired. I pass at the evidence available.

The product’s most interesting move is from compression tricks toward proving which optimizations actually work. Recording workloads, replaying evaluations, then using shadow tests and approved canaries could make Caveman useful even as models improve. That matters because the earlier skill produced **8.5% fewer output tokens but 11.6% higher aggregate billed cost** in [JetBrains’ test](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/07/speak-to-ai-agents-like-cavemen-tosave-tokens/). The outlier does not invalidate the new product, but it exposes the underwriting problem: fewer tokens need not mean money saved. A finite experiment around provider-billed savings at unchanged task success would reveal something valuable. The dossier does not establish that this is what round proceeds will fund.

The strongest objection to passing is Julius Brussee’s demonstrated ability to ship and attract users. His agent-runtime and retrieval work connects directly to the product; the earlier extension reached **20,000 users**, and the repository accumulated **6,700 forks**. This is substantially more evidence than a student résumé. Yet Cloud launched only on **October 13, 2026**. Adoption of the earlier product cannot establish demand for the new commercial offering. The viral-product exception needs corroborating enterprise demand or acquisition validation; neither appears here. No documented cash economics or alternative financing structure supplies another override.

Caveman also touches two patterns I like: translating between systems and helping customers own their workflows. Its potential bridge joins agent engineering to accountable spending decisions. But Brussee’s shipped technical work establishes only part of that bridge; the dossier provides no operating receipts from enterprise budget owners. Likewise, customer workload histories could become proprietary access, but that remains a hypothesis. Apache-2.0 code and gateways such as LiteLLM and Edgee make overlapping functionality accessible. The defensible asset would have to emerge from customer-specific evidence and embedded usage. Stars do not establish that asset, and GreenPT’s referral arrangement supplies no reported paying conversions.

Delete the next financing from the model and the answer remains unknown. Caveman publishes consumption prices and **$250/$750 monthly governance packages**, but prices establish neither collected revenue nor gross profit, burn, or runway. I would track gross profit from retained production deployments after all optimization costs. My single condition for reopening this pass is **three independent Cloud customers renewing after 90 days with verified provider-billed savings, unchanged task-success rates, and positive contribution margin for Caveman**. That would connect technical attention to an economic transaction. Until then, I see a capable builder with substantial reach and an unproven investment mechanism.
