# Tsenta -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Nearly 8,000 users make Tsenta a tactile consumer product with almost no hard substrate: people can install Tsenta, watch Tsenta submit applications, and feel the saved hours, but the MVP was built with Claude Code in three months and has no patents, exclusive data, or switching costs. That high physical verifiability paired with low frontier difficulty is the analytical signature. Tsenta is a working vending machine placed inside a crowded bazaar—not yet a strange new machine only these founders know how to build.

**Niantic** is the high rung because I could play Ingress and experience the location graph before Pokémon Go became “the closest we ever came to world peace.” Tsenta shares that load-bearing loop: 1,000 Chrome users, 500+ Android downloads, 29 iOS ratings, and nearly 8,000 total users let me touch Tsenta rather than trust a slide. Tsenta diverges where the sensation matters most: Niantic could observe play directly, while Tsenta cannot verify whether its applications produce employment outcomes. An application counter is observable; a better career is still founder testimony.

**HQ Trivia** is the low rung on durable technical difficulty: intoxicating consumer software briefly concealed a shallow substrate and organizational fragility. Tsenta shows the same dangerous lightness—Jobright already performs end-to-end applications, and Tsenta’s own MVP took three months with generated code. Tsenta has policy friction around LinkedIn, Indeed, sensitive resumes, and email OTPs, but borrowed platform restrictions are not the compounding regulatory moat of Mindbloom or the physical risk of SpaceX. The DeepMind exception does not rescue Tsenta yet; no evidence shows polymath technical compounding beyond assembling available agentic tools.

Agnay Srivastava and Pulkit Gupta submitted more than 3,000 applications as international students before launching Tsenta from their dorm. That is lived contact, not a TAM memo, and it activates my strongest unstated pattern: the founders are *of* the workflow rather than visiting it. Over 18 months, that origin can compound into thousands of small product decisions about sponsorship filters, ATS failures, and applicant anxiety. Tsenta weakens the analogy because Agnay and Pulkit experienced job seeking, not recruiting operations, and Tsenta has no direct access to the employment outcomes required to learn the other half of the market.

$500,000 of YC funding does not make Tsenta consensus by itself, but Jobright, Simplify, Teal, and Clera make the category feel busy rather than magically weird. For Tsenta to flip this dimension into my invest range, Agnay would need to show that sophisticated investors dismiss mass auto-application as ugly or harmful while a hidden cohort produces materially better interviews—not merely more submissions. Tsenta instead sells up to 4,500 applications for $99, a volume proposition that risks becoming an arms race every browser agent can join. The red flag is not AI; it is defensibility residing in the model and automation stack rather than the founders.

**GameCrush** taught me that named titles do not settle power when pressure arrives. Tsenta identifies Agnay as CEO and Pulkit as CTO, which is better than shared leadership, but Tsenta discloses no equity split, operating agreement, vesting structure, or explicit tiebreaker. The HQ Trivia known exception offers some mercy because palpable consumer pull can temporarily override governance risk, and growth from roughly 1,100 to nearly 8,000 users is real pull. I still invest my own money, so “CEO” on a YC page is a rebuttal—not proof that the founders have held the hard conversation.

Jobright’s $1 million of 2024 revenue shows that applicants will pay for automation, but Jobright also shows how little territory Tsenta owns. My 36-month forecast is that Tsenta pivots from high-volume consumer auto-apply into university-sponsored international-student placement software and remains below $2 million ARR. Tsenta’s lived founder insight and multi-surface product should preserve a user base; Tsenta’s weak outcome data, platform dependency, and interchangeable automation should prevent the current product from becoming a standalone category leader.

Fifty thousand monitored career pages, 15+ ATS integrations, and growth from 1,100 to nearly 8,000 users form the strongest bull case: Tsenta could accumulate an application-response graph before competitors notice, turning today’s browser artifact into tomorrow’s matching network. That case stings because consumer pull once let HQ Trivia override my governance rule. Tsenta’s dossier nevertheless says the founders cannot directly observe employment outcomes, which breaks the proposed flywheel at its most valuable edge; coverage alone is plumbing, not learning. I stay Neutral and do not write the check today. A verified cohort showing at least a 2× interview-rate lift alongside six-month paid retention above 50% would flip me to Invest.
