# Egoist Machines -- Elad Gil Evaluation

David Khachaturov’s 4.7K-star BetterJoy project and Egoist Machines’ zero disclosed revenue define the signature: Egoist Machines is a technically credible, fast-building product searching for evidence that anyone will buy. Founder shipping is Egoist Machines’ strongest dimension; revenue velocity is its weakest. That pairing can produce a useful developer tool, but it does not yet look like one of the dozen or two durable companies that matter ten years from now.

**Perplexity** is the high rung on founder iteration: Aravind Srinivas repeatedly built the discussed product within a week. Egoist Machines shares the artifact-driven part of that mechanism—Khachaturov shipped BetterJoy, worked on OpenAI PaperBench, and contributed to LodeDB’s 178 commits—so Egoist Machines is materially above a credential-only founder profile. The divergence is that BetterJoy and LodeDB are adjacent artifacts, not evidence that AI Passport itself is being rebuilt around user feedback; no Passport usage, retention, or iteration cadence is disclosed. Erin McGurk’s audience of more than 1 million gives Egoist Machines an unusually inexpensive feedback channel, but the dossier does not show that channel closing the build-measure loop yet.

**Lyft** is the low rung’s failure mechanism: activity was insufficient when it could not demonstrate share inversion in a structurally difficult market. Egoist Machines is earlier and weaker on this axis—its consumer waitlist, 268 LinkedIn followers, four regional or business press mentions, and 89 GitHub stars for adjacent LodeDB are attention signals, not a usage curve. Egoist Machines discloses no revenue, repeat Passport usage, live developer customer, or named integration; no evidence—applying the 8/25 absent-evidence default. In an AI market, that is the difference between “just working” and a false signal.

OpenAI’s April 2025 cross-conversation memory expansion and Google’s August 2025 Gemini personalization are the credible recent catalysts for Egoist Machines: personal context is accumulating rapidly, but inside incompatible provider silos. Egoist Machines would flip from Neutral to Invest on this dimension if those silos began exposing portable memory interfaces—or if regulation required practical context portability—because Egoist Machines could then skate for the puck as the neutral permission layer. Today the catalyst creates the problem while strengthening the incumbents that can block the solution, so Egoist Machines sits below **Mistral’s** post-ChatGPT reopening and above a steady-state consumer-data pitch.

The claimed 800-plus connector sources can compound Egoist Machines over 18 months only if integrations create a loop: more applications make the passport useful, more users attract developers, and more approved context increases switching costs. Egoist Machines currently has no named live developer customer, integration count, or retention evidence, while OpenAI and Google already own both distribution and native memory. Without application-side adoption, Egoist Machines must win users and developers separately; that is not yet an index on AI applications, but a two-sided marketplace with cold-start risk.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel because workflow infrastructure on top of commodity sequencing isolated the hard technical layer from the product and distribution problem. Egoist Machines similarly relies on already-working foundation models and builds consent, receipts, revocation, and scoped context above them; Egoist Machines is not inventing new model science. The analogy breaks because AI Passport still needs several independent outcomes: consumers must trust it with health and identity data, developers must integrate it, incumbents must tolerate portability, and paid demand must emerge despite a free-for-life consumer product. That is a multi-miracle dependency chain, not Color’s cleaner workflow-layer architecture.

Egoist Machines will, by July 2029, have pivoted AI Passport toward developer-facing consent, audit, and memory-governance infrastructure and remain below $2 million ARR rather than becoming a broadly adopted consumer passport. Egoist Machines has the technical shipping capacity to build the control plane, but the absent buying curve and integration cold start make B2B compliance tooling a more natural destination than consumer identity infrastructure. The receipts, scoped permissions, revocation, and sensitive-data controls already point toward that adjacent market.

Erin McGurk’s 1-million-plus audience is the bull case that stings: Egoist Machines could seed consumer distribution cheaply while Khachaturov’s security background and 4.7K-star open-source artifact attract developers, creating a neutral rail before OpenAI and Google agree on portability. That would resemble early **Stripe**—incumbents technically capable of solving the problem but structurally disinclined to make cross-provider use easy. Egoist Machines still loses that comparison because no named application has integrated, no Passport retention exists, and no developer pricing demonstrates buying behavior. I would flip to Invest if Egoist Machines signs three named AI applications and reaches $1 million ARR while sustaining more than 20% month-over-month growth.
