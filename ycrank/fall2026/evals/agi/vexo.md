# Vexo -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

New Economies is Vexo’s strongest path. Professionals currently spend time reconstructing commitments from memory, entering context into an assistant, and preparing follow-ups. Vexo’s Band captures conversations, makes them searchable, and drafts actions for approval. The scenario to test is whether this makes it worthwhile to follow up on informal conversations professionals previously let slide. The relevant cost is human time per correctly completed follow-up, including consent, review, and corrections. The memo leaves both the baseline and the savings unmeasured. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The memo records a $199 checkout charge refundable before shipment, and the CEO reports that some preorder customers received prototypes. That gives Vexo an early demand signal, with order volume, refunds, and repeat use still unknown. The founders’ embedded systems and consumer software experience supports their ability to deliver a prototype. At scale, each Band’s net receipts would need to cover hardware, fulfillment, support, and ongoing cloud processing. The memo supplies the price and identifies recurring costs, but leaves contribution per Band unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Vexo may improve an established workflow without opening a new market. Bee already offers wrist-worn capture and follow-up workflows, while Omi publishes reusable capture and assistant-integration components. Vexo’s permissioned memory connector returns source references and works with existing assistants. An accumulated conversation history could give users a reason to stay, but the memo leaves retention and preference under model parity untested. The existing alternatives undermine a scarce-complement case for wearable capture. Whether Vexo opens a new use and earns buyer preference remains testable, which supports Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will a paid cohort repeatedly complete informal follow-ups they previously skipped, at materially lower human time per correct outcome, and still choose to pay $199 for Vexo when offered a comparable wearable using equally capable models? A field comparison that measures review and correction time and records actual purchase choices would move the action to Reach out if both conditions hold. If usage stays confined to existing follow-ups or buyers readily switch at parity, move to Pass.
