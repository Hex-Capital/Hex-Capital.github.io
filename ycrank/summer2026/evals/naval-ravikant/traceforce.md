# Traceforce -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The strongest evidence is not the founders’ credentials; it is what they have already shipped. Xia Hua led cloud-data-protection products at Clumio, Varun Wadhwa built distributed backup and RAG infrastructure, and together they placed Traceforce on more than 1,500 employee devices across five enterprises. The 500-plus-employee deployment turns relevant experience into action-revealed specific knowledge. This is a security team that has crossed the distance from architecture to an installed enforcement product.

The harder question is what remains when Zenity copies the endpoint hooks. TraceGraph, the 600-plus-MCP Atlas registry, and MCP X-Ray’s 80 GitHub stars are real artifacts, not deck language. But Traceforce has published no detection benchmark, exclusive telemetry advantage, or retention evidence showing that deployments compound into a proprietary model. Zenity already discovers prompts, commands, MCPs, and tool calls; WitnessAI enforces MCP controls at the network layer. Code can be copied. An activity graph becomes an asset only when accumulated observations make the next decision materially better.

The timing is credible: local agents such as Claude Code and Cursor can reach files, credentials, and tools that gateways were not designed to inspect. The cited agentic-security market grows from $1.65 billion in 2026 to $13.52 billion in 2032, and Jamf, JumpCloud, NinjaOne, Iru, and PORT1 give the company plausible enterprise and MSP distribution. But this establishes a growing parent category, not yet Traceforce’s room to maneuver. Endpoint enforcement could become the control plane for agent activity, or it could remain one feature inside broader security platforms financed with hundreds of millions of dollars.

The bull case stings. Two founders with directly adjacent infrastructure experience have secured five enterprise deployments, Fortune 500 proofs of concept, fleet-management integrations, and announced SOC 2 and ISO 27001 compliance before building a larger team. This is unusually dense execution for pre-seed. It also matches my recent preference for infrastructure bottlenecks over disposable application software. The counterweight is structural: funded competitors already cover endpoints, networks, posture management, and runtime protection. Traceforce may have found an important layer without owning it.

There is no disclosed integrity problem, artificial deadline, founder instability, or other personal red flag. The missing pattern is permissionless leverage. Each new protected enterprise does not currently make another enterprise’s product better, and every deployment appears to require sales, integration, and policy work. The 1,500 devices prove usage, not a user-created network. There is also no public payer, contract value, recurring revenue, or take rate. I sometimes waive missing monetization when behavior is already self-reinforcing or a strange, capital-efficient product has created its own category. Traceforce demonstrates capital-efficient execution, but not the compounding mechanism required for that exception.

I am neutral and would not write the check today. The founders have earned attention; the substrate has not yet earned conviction. One verifiable condition flips me to Invest: an independently reproducible benchmark showing that Traceforce’s accumulated endpoint telemetry improves detection recall by at least 20% at a fixed false-positive rate versus policies trained without that deployment data. Then the installed base becomes leverage rather than merely distribution.
