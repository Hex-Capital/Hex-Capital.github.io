# Keres -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Keres’s proposed post-money, competing-term-sheet count, and use of proceeds are undisclosed. I cannot establish whether my check buys an overlooked experiment at a sensible price. YC membership does not establish an auction, either. The interesting underwriting question is precise: can Garmur destroy a target, survive, and return cheaply enough that reuse changes the economics of air defense? That could be a venture moment. The dossier does not establish what this round spends to answer it, or by when.

The distinction between recovery after a kill and recovery without an engagement matters. Anduril’s Roadrunner-M returns when destruction is unnecessary; Keres proposes preserving the aircraft after destroying the target. If that works repeatedly, the team could change the expenditure required to maintain interceptor inventory. But the relevant metric is fully burdened cost per successful interception, including aircraft losses, inspection, repair, and turnaround. No public flight-test sequence or operating-cost data establishes that mechanism. I will not give a proposed experiment credit as an executed capital plan.

The strongest argument against passing is Christopher Palazzolo. More than four years across Boom’s maintenance, avionics, flight testing, and XB-1 production operations, followed by operations leadership at H3X, is relevant experience for making aircraft survive repeated service. Keres fits my pattern of taking physical complexity seriously when a neglected operating system can be reorganized. The missing half of that bridge is demonstrated military operating access: the dossier establishes neither founder-led procurement results nor a buyer relationship. A CEO is identified, so I also have no basis to invent an equal-founder decision problem.

Customer access could determine whether technical progress becomes a business. DIU’s 2025 counter-UAS activity shows an evaluation pathway exists; it does not give Keres access to it. Fortem already has disclosed Army and DHS contracts and Lockheed Martin integration. Against that, Keres’s October 12, 2026 media profile and public inquiry email establish visibility, not customer action or an owned distribution advantage. None of my exception grounds is established here: no documented conversion, proprietary buyer access, cash economics, or prior-exit financing structure overrides the missing gates. That is an evidence limit, not proof that customers have rejected Garmur.

I pass on the present record. Aircraft sales and maintenance are inferred business models, and without pricing, margins, burn, or runway I cannot underwrite survival after deleting the next financing. I am not asserting that Keres requires a capital factory; I cannot establish the alternative. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: a signed, funded military trial contract specifying repeated post-kill recovery and an all-in cost-per-successful-interception acceptance threshold. That would connect the technical claim to a buyer’s money and make the next underwriting conversation concrete.
