# Orvixo -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Kolya Schwinge was a senior machine-learning engineer at IFS, but neither founder has worked in factory planning. That makes Orvixo a schlep-rich, pain-poor startup: Orvixo has chosen an exceptionally unpleasant problem without showing that the problem chose the founders first. The ERP integrations, broken source data, machine constraints, and planner overrides are real. The organic origin is not.

**Coinbase** is the high rung for schlep blindness because Brian Armstrong ran toward the compliance machinery everyone else avoided. Orvixo has the same structural opportunity in miniature: connecting ERP, MRP, shop-floor data, labour, tooling, and material constraints will repel founders who merely want to wrap an API. But Coinbase had Armstrong’s working wallet and visible appetite for the grind. Orvixo has published no customer, pilot, production schedule, or reliability measurement proving Orvixo has endured even one complete factory deployment.

**Loopt** shows the failure mechanism at the low end of organic founder pain: weak product timing can be survived only when exceptional founder force overrides it. Orvixo’s founders studied physics at Cambridge, Ethan Phillips worked in analysis and growth, and Kolya Schwinge worked in enterprise ML, but neither founder is documented as having rebuilt factory schedules at 2 a.m. Orvixo therefore reads as an intellectually discovered opportunity, not a tool the founders desperately wanted for themselves. No equivalent of Sam Altman’s animal quality is yet visible to justify the known exception.

Ethan Phillips’s founder-led scheduling reviews and Orvixo’s UK automotive-supplier map are small animal tracks. Over 18 months, that behavior could compound: every manual integration could expose another hidden constraint, and every scheduling review could become reusable product knowledge. But Orvixo currently has 86 LinkedIn followers and no public revenue, named pilot, or deployed artifact. Content and consultation are not yet the equivalent of Airbnb selling 1,000 cereal boxes at $40 each. Orvixo has approached the factory door; the dossier does not show the founders forcing it open.

Kolya Schwinge’s JWST research and Ethan Phillips’s quantum-computing work at Hitachi Cambridge Laboratory establish technical intelligence, not yet hacker genealogy. Orvixo would move up a full tier if the founders showed a working constraint compiler, an unusually fast replanning engine, or a public benchmark beating Siemens Opcenter or Asprova on a messy production case. No public GitHub repositories or comparable pre-startup artifacts were found. Orvixo may contain serious code, but hackers leave artifacts, and the public evidence presently stops at credentials.

**Stripe** had two brothers who had already built and sold Auctomatic before simplifying payments together. Orvixo also has the preferred two-founder shape, and both founders studied physics at Cambridge during 2023–2024. But shared enrollment is weaker than years of building together, and Orvixo provides no evidence of a joint project predating the startup. The configuration is promising rather than proven: two technical founders, complementary experience, uncertain accumulated trust.

IFS is the clue to Orvixo’s next 36 months. By July 2029, I expect Orvixo to have narrowed into scheduling software for UK automotive suppliers, with less than $1 million in annual recurring revenue, rather than operating as a general system that “optimises and runs factories.” Orvixo’s integration schlep will force specialization, while the founders’ distance from factory-planning pain will make customers—not an original internal insight—choose the vertical.

Ethan Phillips’s scheduling reviews, Kolya Schwinge’s IFS experience, and Orvixo’s factory-language modeling form a serious opposing case. The bull mechanism is that the manual work is already Orvixo’s cereal-box phase, merely hidden by enterprise confidentiality; Airbnb likewise looked implausible until one unscalable fact revealed the founders. But Airbnb had the fact. Orvixo has no named manufacturer, paid deployment, repeat usage, or quantified scheduling improvement. Two named manufacturers renewing after twelve months and reporting measurable production gains would flip my verdict, because that would prove Orvixo’s founders are actually doing the schlep rather than describing it.
