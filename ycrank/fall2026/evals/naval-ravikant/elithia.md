# Elithia -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Elithia has built something a physician can inspect: a chronology whose findings link back to the source pages. That is useful evidence of execution. Sarthak Jain’s unstructured-data engineering at Winton supplies relevant technical experience; his 2026 Medico-Legal Conference speaker listing suggests contact with the actual profession. Oxford and MIT do less work in my decision. The remaining founder question is what he has learned about expert review that another capable engineer would discover only after serving these customers.

My central concern is what accumulates after the chronology is delivered. Elithia removes duplicates, organizes records, and exports findings to Word or PDF. DigitalOwl already offers medical chronologies and evidence-linked record chat. Product overlap does not prove Elithia is trivial to reproduce, but the dossier identifies no asset that survives reproduction. Expert corrections could become valuable training or evaluation data. Without collection rights, ownership, and measured improvement, that remains a possibility rather than a moat. There is also no demonstrated mechanism by which one physician’s use improves another physician’s experience.

The strongest case for Elithia is that clinician review is a distinct job inside a market competitors approach more broadly. Reported sales to expert witnesses and medical-legal firms suggest a payer exists. The first 1,000 pages free gives practitioners a concrete way to evaluate the output. Chronology and evidence reconciliation could extend to insurers and third-party administrators; EvenUp’s reported $150 million of recurring revenue under contract establishes substantial adjacent legal-workflow spending, although it says nothing about Elithia’s revenue. This could become a useful business before it becomes a defensible one.

That distinction matters to my exceptions. Elithia could earn an early check without substantial revenue through exceptional founder insight, an unusually original prototype, or a user network already forming. The dossier establishes a working workflow, but not those stronger mechanisms. Its small team fits my preference for direct operating structures; headcount alone proves no operating advantage. Nor does this product yet turn fragmented professional expertise into shared supply or give independent contributors a system that compounds their work. There is no documented integrity issue, artificial financing deadline, or other founder veto. I am withholding conviction because the durable advantage is unproven, not because an adverse founder story exists.

I would pass on a personal check today. My base case is that by November 2029 Elithia remains a specialist medical-review vendor below $2 million ARR: customers can pay for the immediate utility while competing products constrain differentiation. Undisclosed pricing and margins leave the strength of value capture unresolved. One result would reopen the decision: a controlled evaluation showing that physician corrections Elithia has contractual rights to reuse materially improve accuracy on unseen records relative to the same system without those corrections. That would demonstrate an asset growing with deployment.
