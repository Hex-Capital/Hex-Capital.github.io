# Risklytics -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is weak. Risklytics points to a $335.87 billion global brokerage market, but it does not quantify the frontier-technology premium pool, the losses created by AI exclusions, or the number of companies left uninsurable. Better policy matching could prevent expensive coverage gaps. That is useful. Nothing in the dossier shows an orders-of-magnitude change in cost, access, or risk-bearing capacity.

The technical case is weaker. Structured intake, policy-form analysis, carrier routing, and exclusion review are brokerage workflow improvements, not a demonstrated discontinuity. Alton Risk already markets policy-gap detection and carrier matching, while Corgi controls both underwriting and distribution. Risklytics provides no measured accuracy, turnaround-time, placement-rate, or premium-cost benchmark against either competitor or a generalist broker. A continuously updated forms database could become a learning loop, but without placements or disclosed database scale it is currently an assertion, not a moat.

The founders have shown one valuable behavior: they called Ember, their natural-catastrophe model, a non-starter and changed course. That is evidence of belief revision rather than attachment to a fashionable original plan. Risio also combines ML research with an insurance licensing examination, and both founders left Harvard to work full-time in May 2026. But this remains a two-person, same-institution team with no disclosed brokerage operator, carrier underwriter, regulatory specialist, or recruit explicitly assigned to the access risk. Learning rate is visible; risk-tuned recruiting is not.

The largest kill risks are correctly named. Risklytics cannot place coverage until producer licensing is complete, and carriers may demand placement history that a new broker cannot possess. State-by-state licensing and wholesale-broker or MGA partnerships are reasonable routes around that circularity. Yet there is no falsifiable milestone: no target state, carrier commitment, submission cohort, placement threshold, budget, or date. Capital could easily be consumed building policy-analysis software before proving that carriers will quote the risks.

The strongest bull case is that ISO’s 2026 optional AI-liability exclusions create rapidly changing complexity, the founders revised their plan quickly, and ordinary brokerage activity could generate proprietary carrier-appetite and placement-outcome data. The known exception is the software wedge into a giant financial value pool: **Block** began without a scientific discontinuity and expanded far beyond its first device. The material difference is that Risklytics has not yet processed a transaction, demonstrated customer pull, or shown a product wedge that generalist brokers cannot copy. Today it is closer to a specialized brokerage with software than a new risk infrastructure layer.

I pass. The downside may be only one pre-seed check, but the upside has not been made uncapped: no quantified consequence, no 5–10x result, and no proven distribution or underwriting leverage. One condition flips the decision: bind at least 20 frontier-technology policies and publish cohort evidence that Risklytics reduces submission-to-bind time or uncovered AI-exclusion incidence by at least 5x versus the customers’ prior brokers, while adding repeat carrier capacity from those outcomes.
