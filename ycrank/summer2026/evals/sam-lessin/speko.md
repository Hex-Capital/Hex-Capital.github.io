# Speko -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Post-money: unknown. Competing term sheets: unknown. First scarce check: no—Speko reportedly raised $1.1 million before YC and $1.6 million including YC. That does not prove an auction, but it prevents me from treating this as cheap, abandoned optionality. Without valuation or competition data, I am underwriting blind on the dimension that matters most to me.

The product is real enough to sharpen the question. Speko tracks 23 speech-to-text models, finds different winners across nine languages, and exposes STT, LLM, and TTS routing through one API with failover. But the financing has no named venture moment. “Build the voice OpenRouter” is a strategy, not a finite experiment. I need one workload, one dated threshold—production minutes, latency-adjusted accuracy, or paid conversion—and a result that changes the company’s value. The dossier supplies none.

The strongest asset is Beknazar Abdikamalov. He has worked on voice technology since 2019, spent four years manually comparing providers at prior products, served as Hupo’s co-founder and CTO, and now ships open SDKs and LiveKit and Pipecat adapters. That is credible experience on the developer side of the translation layer. It is not yet proof that he owns access on the provider or enterprise-buyer side, but this is more than résumé fit.

The bull case is that the first voice-company partners and one commercial pilot are the beginning of embedded workflow ownership. Frequent model releases create real selection pain, and Speko’s language-specific benchmarking is more useful than a generic model leaderboard. If the pilot converts into recurring production traffic, routing decisions could become sticky. This is also the only plausible known-exception case: hard customer behavior could override unclear financing. Today, 12 Launch YC votes, two stars on each principal SDK, and one undisclosed pilot do not clear that bar.

The darker mechanism is commoditization. LiveKit already routes among inference providers, OpenRouter supports speech and transcription, and Vapi permits provider swapping while owning more of the deployment workflow. Speko publishes its benchmarks and does not train on customer audio or transcripts, so it currently owns neither proprietary data nor distribution. Its 5% routing markup and $0.09-per-minute managed price establish a billing model, not a profitable path; there is no revenue, gross margin, burn, retention, or runway evidence. Delete the next round and I cannot identify the controllable cash metric.

My 36-month base case is that Speko pivots from standalone routing into voice-agent evaluation and observability and remains below $3 million ARR, because benchmarking is useful while an independent router without private workflow data is structurally thin. I am passing. The one verifiable flip condition is a signed 12-month enterprise contract routing production traffic with disclosed positive gross margin and renewal terms; that would turn the claimed workflow into owned, cash-producing access rather than another public benchmark wrapped in an API.
