# Amulet -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Amulet’s July 2026 pivot from customer-success agents to an AI filesystem makes Amulet a fast-learning infrastructure experiment with a small-capital ceiling. Founder learning rate is Amulet’s strongest dimension; required-capital fit is its weakest. The economic mechanism Amulet might break is repeated copying of large datasets across disposable agent compute, but a useful filesystem primitive is not yet a civilizationally distinct world-state. “Consequence over probability” does not mean consequence by association with AI.

**Slash** (2024) is the high rung for learning rate because its documented Kanye West pivot exposed founders who could discard their own thesis and follow reality. Amulet shares the load-bearing mechanism: Nithik Bala and Varun Puru abandoned customer-success agents for content-addressed commits, copy-on-write forks, and sub-100-ms Linux mounts. Amulet diverges because the dossier supplies neither the reasoning behind that pivot nor current-product validation beyond a private beta. A pivot demonstrates motion; it does not automatically demonstrate learning. Amulet sits below Slash because the causal lesson remains invisible.

**DoorDash** (2013) occupies the low rung on required capital: little scientific capital risk, but extraordinary ambition ultimately overrode the mismatch. Amulet shows the same low-capital mechanism without DoorDash’s evidence of a vast commerce network emerging. Amulet’s software-only product charges $0.20 per GiB-month, names no capital-intensive milestone, and faces cloud platforms capable of replicating its primitives. I am over-resourced for Amulet unless Amulet proves that building the authoritative state layer for agents requires a scale of storage infrastructure that ordinary venture capital will not finance.

Amulet’s private beta and free 10-GB tier could compound over 18 months if every agent run creates another immutable history, provenance record, and operational dependency. That would turn Amulet from a storage tool into the system of record for machine work. Today, however, Archil, E2B, Modal, and cloud vendors can bundle neighboring primitives, while Amulet identifies no structural barrier and excludes live same-file multi-writer access. The consequence score stays modest because Amulet currently improves an emerging workflow rather than naming the incumbent economic regime it will collapse.

Amulet’s company-published 1.9-ms p99 reads, 8.4-GB/s throughput, 3-ms forks, and conflict-checked publishing show legitimate systems engineering, not a GPT wrapper. The technical-risk score would flip upward if Amulet demonstrated that these results persist across petabyte-scale, geographically distributed workloads while preserving full-enough POSIX semantics and conflict correctness. Without independent benchmarks, Amulet resembles an ambitious distributed-systems product that any Tier-1 generalist can finance—not the three-falsifiable-miracle profile of Commonwealth Fusion.

Nithik Bala’s Meta and Rockset experience, alongside Varun Puru’s Meta, Amazon, and J.P. Morgan infrastructure work, parallels **Glean** on one axis: domain experts using incumbent experience to build a better infrastructure layer. Glean earned the exception because enterprise information asymmetry created enormous consequence and capital scale. Amulet has relevant competence but no documented contradiction of filesystem orthodoxy, public technical thesis, or controversial position. Amulet’s founders presently look like experts extending their field—the precise profile my TED argument warns may extrapolate the past.

Modal’s more than $300M in annualized revenue validates spending around AI infrastructure, but it also shapes my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Amulet will be independent at $2M-$5M ARR and will have repositioned from a standalone filesystem into an integrated state-and-provenance layer for agent sandboxes. Amulet’s high learning rate makes another thesis refinement likely; Amulet’s low capital requirement and weak structural barrier make category ownership unlikely. The probable outcome is a valuable infrastructure product, not a new economic regime.

Archil’s $18M financing supplies the strongest bull case against that forecast: agent compute may need a “Git for data,” and Amulet already claims zero-copy writable forks, immutable commits, per-run provenance, and 8.4-GB/s throughput. DoorDash proves that a capital-light Known Exception can become enormous, while Amulet’s abrupt pivot may reveal unusually fast founders. The objection fails today because Amulet has no public retention, revenue, independent benchmark, or market barrier, while Modal and E2B can integrate the same workflow. I would flip to Invest if Amulet reaches $5M ARR with three named AI customers each managing at least one petabyte through Amulet and shows independently reproduced performance under production concurrency.
