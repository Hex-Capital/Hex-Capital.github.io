# screenpipe -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I need are missing: proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and size of the scarce first check. That matters because screenpipe already carries the social markers of a potentially auctioned deal—YC, 20.9K GitHub stars, 300K claimed users, and 20–30% weekly revenue growth—but none proves that the price still leaves uncertainty for me to underwrite. I will not pretend silence means scarcity.

The real asset is not the audience count. It is the observed path from an owned technical workflow to payment. By February 2025, screenpipe had acquired 202 subscribers and $3,480 MRR organically, without paid marketing. The repository’s 12,176 commits support a functioning cross-platform capture system rather than a landing-page AI wrapper: screenshots, accessibility data, audio, local SQLite storage, search, MCP, and programmable pipes. Twenty-one thousand stars are soft evidence; subscribers paying for continuous capture are hard evidence that some users entrust the product with a consequential part of their work.

What capital flips, however, remains undefined. The two visible hires—Head of Virality and Forward Deployed Engineer—suggest simultaneous consumer distribution and enterprise implementation, not one finite experiment with a metric, deadline, and truth-revealing result. Is the venture moment proving that ten regulated enterprises will deploy continuous capture across 1,000 seats? Is it converting the open-source funnel into $1 million ARR? “Hire and grow” is a capital factory, not an experiment.

The economics are promising but incomplete. Annual-equivalent pricing of $250 for Basic and $500 per Business seat, organic acquisition, and real MRR create a plausible route to a self-sustaining software business. But there is no gross margin, burn, runway, churn, or current revenue base, and 320 weekly active users against broader claims of 300K users is precisely why I refuse to underwrite the vanity denominator. Delete the Series A: the dossier does not yet show whether paid retention and support costs produce enough cash to finance continued capture reliability, privacy work, and enterprise deployment.

Louis Beaumont is unusually credible on the building side. Prior infrastructure and machine-learning work, satellite-intelligence engineering, and maintaining his own AI second brain since 2020 connect deep capture infrastructure with an experienced user’s workflow. The shipped system and paying users are receipts. The missing side is enterprise administration: the dossier does not show that he has deployed security, consent, retention, and device controls inside large organizations where recorded screens and microphones implicate coworkers, customers, credentials, and regulated data.

The bear case stings because the components are available, local histories are exportable, and Microsoft can bundle Recall into the operating system. Granola and Otter already own narrower workplace-context wedges, while screenpipe must solve cross-platform reliability and a much larger privacy surface. Its June 2026 source-license change may also weaken the community funnel that generated its early distribution. My 36-month base case is an independent developer-and-prosumer business at $3–8 million ARR, not yet the enterprise context layer; the evidence could prove me embarrassingly conservative, but only if organic adoption survives paid conversion and enterprise controls without paid-growth economics.

I am neutral today. The product has earned more belief than the financing has. My one flip condition is a financing memo documenting a sub-$10 million post-money round dedicated to a dated test that converts the existing organic workflow into at least $1 million ARR with measured retention and positive contribution margin. That would turn impressive activity into a scarce, truth-revealing check I can buy.
