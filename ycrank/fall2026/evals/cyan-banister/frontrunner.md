# Frontrunner -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, a salesperson should be able to change tools without giving the business amnesia. Account history, customer promises, and the reasons a deal stalled should travel with the work. Frontrunner’s business graph points toward that world: connect CRM, email, calendar, product data, and calls, then let agents act on retained context. But getting there requires reliable memory, permissions that survive across systems, and learning that transfers between workflows. The dossier establishes the product’s intended architecture, without a demonstrated artifact showing those transitions underway. I can imagine the future. I cannot award Frontrunner points for my daydream.

The strongest evidence is what Maya Nayyar and Carl Bager did before this company. Maya ran growth at Console and Encord; Carl built growth systems at Encord, Instawork, and The Org, and previously founded Diciv, which was acquired. More useful than the employer names is a former colleague’s specific credit for their website flows and account-intent routing at Encord. These two have already worked together on the machinery they now want to sell ([founder profiles and colleague account](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/frontrunner)). That is meaningful first-believer evidence. It supports commercial and technical fluency, although it does not establish a long, independently pursued obsession or exceptional recruiting ability.

Why does an account need to be reconstructed every time someone opens another tool? Frontrunner identifies a real operating wound: context gets stranded, and people supply the missing connections with their own time. Restoring that time fits my preference for unglamorous workflow repair. The economics remain less specific. There is no measured reconstruction burden, identified gatekeeper collecting a rent, or demonstrated transfer of customer ownership to an individual. Contract-specific pricing establishes how Frontrunner can charge, without showing how much economic control its customers recover ([terms](https://usefr.com/terms-of-service)). A useful convenience can become a business; my stronger interest begins when it changes who controls the work.

The bull case has teeth. Customers reportedly action more than 100 signal-based opportunities daily, and one testimonial claims 40–50% outbound-to-meeting conversion. If people repeatedly trust this product with live sales opportunities, the founders may already have crossed the difficult boundary between an impressive demo and consequential use. But the company-authored testimonials omit customer identities, measurement windows, and cohorts, limiting that behavioral evidence to modest credit ([launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/VXB-frontrunner-cursor-for-gtm)). More importantly, Clay’s September 2026 announcement describes overlapping shared context and autonomous workflows. Frontrunner has not demonstrated control of a scarce underlying asset. The attractive possibility is infrastructure beneath many agents; the evidenced position is an application competing to assemble context from systems whose APIs others control.

I pass at this evidence level. My pre-revenue exceptions depend on unusually strong founder obsession and technical artifacts; missing revenue alone is no obstacle, but those compensating signals are incomplete here. The CEO and CTO roles are identified, with no documented leadership shell game or equal-control problem. Connecting email and call recordings raises a genuine custody question, though the dossier does not establish a severe privacy violation. Nor does it establish dependence on one closed model supplier. Those red flags should not be invented to justify the pass. My one verifiable flip condition is an independently observed customer deployment where a second distinct GTM workflow uses the existing graph without rebuilding context and remains in weekly use for six months. That would begin to show Frontrunner becoming durable business memory—the part of this opportunity I want to own.
