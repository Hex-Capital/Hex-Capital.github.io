# Topos -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Scientific agents create a potential buyer for Topos: biology teams that need to trust computational reasoning before spending expert time or experimental budget. The controlling variable is how much costly review Topos can eliminate while preserving correctness. Agents agreeing with one another does not establish that variable. The dossier describes formalization and cross-agent checking, but supplies neither an independent correctness criterion nor a measured threshold at which a buyer benefits. I can formulate the market thesis; I cannot yet credit Topos with demonstrating it.

The technical discontinuity is plausible but remains outside the proposed application. Harmonic reported solving 96.8% of VERINA code-verification problems in December 2025. That establishes progress in code verification, not transferable performance in biology. A biological conclusion can follow correctly from assumptions that are empirically wrong. Topos must identify which valuable tasks have assumptions and outputs that can actually be checked computationally. There is no measured reduction in review time, experimental cost, or delivery time here, so the technology-shift dimension stays at its missing-evidence default.

The strongest argument for investing is that these founders may discover that narrow task before a larger platform does. Ryan Min June Shin has Dana-Farber experience; Sohum Sukhatankar researched seizure-localization models at Boston Children’s Hospital. Their shared computational prime-progression project, credited in September 2026, adds evidence of technical collaboration. Both left Harvard, which is a concrete commitment and earns founder credit beyond the default. A focused product validating computational biological models could become useful without solving biological truth generally. But the research-stage exception requires demonstrated frontier research, unusually scarce technical capability, and a credible capital path. The dossier does not establish that combination.

Market structure makes the scope decision consequential. Harmonic and Axiom have disclosed $295 million and $264 million in financing respectively, while Edison has raised $70 million around scientific workflows. Those figures establish competing development resources, not customer demand. My pattern of backing several layers of a technology shift makes verification interesting, but Topos still needs an independently valuable position. The product description does not document integrations, retained workflow state, evaluation feedback, or responsibility for a completed research task. Better models might strengthen its agents; they might also make cross-agent checking a standard platform feature. That uncertainty earns no workflow premium, and the inferred future dataset earns no moat credit without evidence of ownership or reproduction cost.

I would pass with my own money today. YC participation and zero job postings say nothing about product-market pull; absent revenue and usage receive the stated default, without treating normal pre-seed silence as failure. The decisive market gates remain unmet, rather than contradicted. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: a named biology customer repeatedly paying for a workflow in which an independently checked, held-out evaluation shows Topos cuts expert review time by at least 50% without increasing material errors. That would connect verification capability to a buying decision—the connection currently missing.
