# TovenAI -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. Institutional compliance teams no longer spend four hours reconstructing a trade; machines continuously assemble communications, orders, policies, and regulations into cited evidence, while humans adjudicate the strange cases. TovenAI has one credible leak from that world: six agents already operating across multiple workflows at a top-10 U.S. institutional trading firm. What is missing is the backward map. The dossier does not show why TovenAI’s intelligence layer compounds into that future instead of becoming a feature inside NICE Actimize or Behavox.

The artifact is unusually convincing for pre-seed. A regulated institution let this two-person company connect sensitive communications and trading records across more than 30 systems. Trade reconstruction reportedly fell from four hours to minutes, roughly 70% of false positives are automatically dispositioned, and another evaluation produced about seven times more value per reviewed alert than an incumbent. That is costly user behavior: installation, permissions, security review, and organizational trust—not a waitlist pretending to be traction. The missing revenue and unnamed customer metrics limit certainty, but they do not erase the behavior.

Why did reconstruction take four hours? Because the evidence is fragmented across calls, chats, trades, regulations, internal policies, and legacy surveillance products. The SEC’s $63.1 million electronic-recordkeeping enforcement action makes the institutional cost concrete, while TovenAI returns analyst time by assembling cited checks and escalating judgment-bearing cases. I see the broken workflow and its rent in expert labor, but not yet a deeper artificial rule or gatekeeper whose removal transfers durable control to users.

Batu Balci has the useful kind of proximity: he built AI systems inside Citadel’s quantitative organization. Sam Turchetta contributes production ML experience from Meta, and his mother’s 15 years in risk and compliance supplied an early reality check. An eight-year relationship and explicit CEO/CTO roles reduce my leadership-shell-game concern. Still, the biography begins with Stanford and employers, not dated pre-company experiments, independently shipped compliance tools, or years of obsession. Their customer deployment proves the product; it cannot also be counted as founder first-believer evidence.

This hits my unglamorous-workflow-repair pattern and partially fits the concealed-infrastructure pattern: TovenAI sits inside existing systems rather than asking banks to replace everything. But the enabling asset is not yet scarce. Its connectors depend on Bloomberg, Cloud9, CME, and incumbent APIs; isolated client data is not used for training; and several well-capitalized competitors can bundle overlapping agents, surveillance, case management, and audit trails. The privacy red flag is real because the product processes voice, email, chat, and trades, but on-premises deployment, single-tenant isolation, encryption, audit logs, and human escalation are serious limiting architecture. The fashionable-AI red flag remains unresolved at the moat level, not the safety level.

My August 2029 base case is $4 million to $8 million in ARR as a valuable embedded compliance-automation vendor, rather than an independent system of record. The bear case stings: Behavox or NICE Actimize could copy the workflows, restrict connector access, and sell the same capability through existing procurement relationships before TovenAI accumulates switching costs. The pre-revenue exception does not fully override that risk because the working artifact is not paired with a lifelong founder obsession or controlled technical bottleneck. I am neutral today. I flip to invest if three independent institutions each renew and expand from one workflow to at least three while subsequent deployments remain under five days; that would show the connector-policy layer compounds rather than merely integrates.
