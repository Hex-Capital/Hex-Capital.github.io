# Robocurve -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Robocurve is measuring a bottleneck, not yet collapsing one. If independent evaluation accelerates trustworthy deployment of general-purpose robots, the eventual consequence could be enormous. But the dossier never quantifies the labor cost pool, affected population, evaluation burden, or deployment delay that disappears. A large robotics future is not itself a 100x consequence case. Today I am being asked to buy an option without knowing the size of the prize.

There is real product work. Inspect Robots has approximately 400 commits, 134 GitHub stars, 19 forks, 13 related repositories, and a completed frontier-model pilot on physical hardware. The framework’s cross-embodiment design is more ambitious than a single Franka bin-picking leaderboard. But Robocurve has not demonstrated a 5–10x improvement in evaluation cost, reproducibility, throughput, or predictive validity against laboratory-built pipelines, Positronic, Instance, or ReSim. Breadth and openness are useful product properties; they are not yet a technical discontinuity. The MIT license makes that distinction especially important because competitors can reuse the software.

The first real-robot pilot at least spends effort against benchmark validity, the correct kill risk. Unfortunately, its results were still pending, the API remains in early development, and no pass/fail threshold is disclosed. The cheapest decisive experiment is not another benchmark catalog. It is a blinded replication showing that several laboratories obtain the same model ordering and that this ordering predicts failures in deployment. Without that evidence, hardware maintenance, controlled resets, and domain-specific setups can turn the company into a bespoke testing service before it becomes infrastructure.

The founders are the strongest part of the case. Jay Chooi brings AI-evaluation work from MATS and the UK AI Security Institute alongside publications at ICML, ACL, and EMNLP; Aris Zhu brings robotics experience from Amazon Robotics, Amazon AGI Labs, and Yondu Robotics. That is unusually complementary gene-pool construction for a two-person pre-seed team. It earns credit for risk alignment, but the dossier documents neither a material belief revision nor the recruitment of an exceptional third owner for benchmark science, hardware operations, or commercial adoption.

The bull case stings: 134 stars, 19 forks, rapid movement to version 0.21.1, and one physical pilot within weeks of public releases suggest that a tiny team may be forming the neutral measurement layer for embodied AI. Normal usage could generate a valuable cross-model, cross-robot history, and open source could become distribution rather than leakage. This is precisely the kind of scarce technical workflow that can become a repeatable platform. But three overlapping products launched around the same problem in 2026, no structural barrier or revenue is visible, and the proposed paid-engagement model has no disclosed unit economics. None of my exception logic overrides those gaps because Robocurve has not yet quantified either a giant existing value pool or an extreme consequence that makes weak differentiation irrelevant.

I pass today. The option could become attractive, but right now it looks more likely to become evaluation software plus labor than a compounding physical-AI institution. One result flips my decision: a blinded benchmark replicated across three independent robotics laboratories that preserves model rankings within 5% while reducing evaluation cost per policy by at least 5x versus each laboratory’s internal process.
