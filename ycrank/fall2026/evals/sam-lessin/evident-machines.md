# Evident Machines -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Evident Machines gives me a technical proposition, but the investment proposition is still undefined. Proposed post-money: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. Whether my money would be the first scarce check: unknown. YC participation establishes none of those things. I will not infer an auction from the batch badge, but I cannot underwrite a cheap, non-consensus investment without the price. The dossier also names no capital-funded experiment with a deadline and a pass/fail result. “Real-world benchmarks for electrical engineering” describes a product category; what truth does this check buy?

The physical experimentation is the part I would pursue. Simulation can establish that a design satisfies a model while missing what happens on a bench. If Evident Machines can measure that gap reliably, it could own something frontier labs cannot obtain from another public leaderboard. That fits my preference for businesses translating between systems: scientific AI on one side, physical engineering on the other. But the distinction becomes an asset only if the team owns reusable tasks, experimental results, and scoring procedures that buyers trust. The dossier establishes the intended approach, with no published validation or ownership evidence. A possible dark pool is still a possibility.

The strongest argument against passing is that these founders have already approached both sides of that bridge. Trishala Jain worked on Gemini for Science and studied EECS; Mark Nour has search-augmented reasoning research and a documented assistive-cane robotics project. Their earlier Buildbox product launched publicly on August 3, 2026, and they reportedly spent three years building together. Those are useful receipts beyond employer names. They do not yet establish a deployed physical-evaluation system or privileged access to lab purchasing. The CEO/CTO roles also give me no basis to invent an equal-founder decision-making problem.

The commercial question is whether physical proof commands a recurring payment sufficient to fund physical proof. Atopile already finances EEBench itself and says it does not sell benchmark scores; PCBBench supplies another overlapping evaluation approach. Evident therefore needs to establish why a lab pays rather than consults those alternatives or builds internally. There is no documented purchase, pilot, retained usage, pricing, margin, or burn. I assign the stated missing-evidence defaults rather than treating ordinary pre-seed silence as failed conversion or bad economics. But deleting the next financing still leaves me without a demonstrated cash engine.

I pass at present. The exceptions I would consider require unusually hard operating evidence, documented conversion, or a financing structure supported by prior founder outcomes; none appears here. Equally, I see no evidence of auction tactics, dependence on disappearing model errors, or a plan explicitly designed around another raise. The problem is narrower: the potentially valuable experimental asset has not yet become an underwritable business. One verifiable condition would reopen my decision: an executed, prepaid frontier-lab contract that covers delivery at a positive contribution margin and leaves Evident Machines ownership of reusable experimental tasks. That would connect the technical distinction to proprietary access, a purchase, and cash.
