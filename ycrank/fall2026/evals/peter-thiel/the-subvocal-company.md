# The Subvocal Company -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The Subvocal Company’s proposed secret is that deliberate internal articulation can become a practical, silent replacement for keyboard input. What would prove that wrong? If decoding requires extensive retraining whenever the user, session, or sensor placement changes, the laboratory achievement does not become a general interface. The team explicitly identifies all three as unresolved challenges. Its reported **200-plus words per minute** is therefore interesting but incomplete: speed without corresponding accuracy and calibration requirements cannot establish usable communication. AlterEgo’s pursuit of the same objective also means the ambition itself is not a truth that competitors reject.

There is nevertheless a serious technical bull case. The earlier prototype reportedly achieved **45% accuracy across English words with under two hours of training data**; the later continuous-speech claim suggests an experiment worth examining. These are different measurements, so I cannot infer an accuracy improvement from the speed increase. But this is physical invention with a working prototype claim, fitting my preference for neglected technical frontiers. The strongest objection to passing is that a small team may have discovered an unusually efficient way to extract a rich physiological signal. My exception for a demonstrated technical breakthrough could apply here. Company-reported results without an independently characterized test do not yet establish that exception.

The decisive commercial question is what The Subvocal Company would own if silent speech works. “Knowledge workers” identifies a population, not a bounded market the company can dominate. AlterEgo directly overlaps; whisper dictation offers an adjacent substitute for some situations. Neither establishes that Subvocal is a commodity, but the undisclosed sensing architecture does not establish proprietary superiority either. The proposed cross-user dataset remains hypothetical. A useful interface could create substantial value while several hardware vendors compete away the return.

Yadav and Kumar built the initial prototype together at **TreeHacks in February 2026**, with sensing and firmware work attributed to Yadav and consumer-software experience to Kumar. That is evidence of collaboration and internal construction. It does not establish their earlier working history, current full-time status, or economic exposure; I would retain the cautious founding-structure default rather than invent either commitment or a red flag. GreenSat’s reported **$2 million annual revenue** belongs to Yadav’s separate company. It supplies neither Subvocal revenue nor evidence that these founders have built a durable organization together.

I would pass today. Planned pre-orders identify an intended transaction, but there is no disclosed price, margin structure, or tested acquisition route from which to construct a power-law business. The hardware posting also places portable implementation ahead of the team: decoding robustness, power, and mechanical integration must converge. This raises the coordination concern in my hard-technology red flags, although the dossier establishes no fatal technical reversal or regulatory barrier. Neither verified technical proof nor a concrete customer deployment path currently supports an exception. **My flip condition is an independently audited paid pilot in one defined customer segment demonstrating at least 95% word accuracy at 150 words per minute across users and sessions after no more than five minutes of calibration, with a measured advantage over the best available substitute.** That would begin to establish both the secret and a reason this company could own its first market.
