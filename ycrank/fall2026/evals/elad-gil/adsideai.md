# Adside.ai -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Adside.ai’s opportunity is to turn paid-advertising management from purchased human time into software-delivered work: startups buy campaign outcomes, and the controlling variable is how much human intervention remains necessary as managed spend grows without degrading acquisition performance. That is a potentially large business. The dossier establishes the workflow fragmentation but does not establish the automation threshold or economics that make this market work for Adside. I would keep that distinction explicit rather than award conviction for an attractive market map I supplied myself.

The pricing makes the test concrete. Managed service costs 10% of advertising spend with a $3,500 monthly minimum, implying the minimum stops binding at $35,000 of monthly spend. That creates room to fund account management, but a price is not evidence of software margins. The strongest documented technology improvement is narrower: reusable Apify infrastructure let the team ship AdsSpy in days rather than months. That is a real reduction in development time. It does not yet establish a comparable reduction in campaign-management labor or customer acquisition costs. The supplier case study supports some why-now credit, without proving a change in industry economics.

The product has more substance than a creative generator. Adside connects advertising accounts and operates campaigns alongside research, strategy, and creative generation. Better models could improve the work performed through those integrations. That earns meaningful workflow credit. However, the documented research assets—133,000-plus ads across 6,055 YC companies—come from advertising libraries and reusable scraping infrastructure. A competitor’s reproduction cost appears limited; those records are not a proprietary customer-learning loop. Omneky already overlaps in creative generation and publishing, while Madgicx overlaps in optimization. Cross-platform execution could become valuable ownership, but the dossier does not yet demonstrate persistent state or feedback that makes Adside increasingly difficult to replace.

The strongest argument against passing is the founders. Choy and Miara built and sold Botdog together; Miara reports a seven-figure exit within 18 months. They also operated paid ads for their previous business. This closely matches my preference for founders who have learned startup mechanics through a smaller prior outcome. Their four-year working relationship makes the execution case stronger still. But that evidence belongs primarily in the founder dimension. It cannot also establish market pull or margin expansion. The documented team does not supply a frontier-research exception, and the development-speed improvement does not establish an industry-economics exception. There is also no evidence of the fundraising-status or premature-liquidity red flags.

Adside reports 30 companies live in October 2026, which is useful early deployment evidence for two founders. Paying status, repeat usage, and expansion are unspecified, so I would discount the claim rather than translate it into revenue. The workshop with more than 40 attendees suggests access to an initial customer community; it does not yet prove repeatable distribution. My 36-month expectation is a functioning, service-led business generating $1–3 million in annual revenue, with human account management still material to delivery. The founders’ demonstrated execution supports that outcome more strongly than the current evidence supports a software category winner.

I would pass on investing my own money today. The overall score is Neutral, but Adside does not clear my controlling-market-variable gate. My one verifiable flip condition would be a 90-day cohort of ten paying managed-service customers showing acquisition performance at least as good as their prior baseline while Adside earns at least 70% gross margins after all delivery labor. That result would directly connect the advertised workflow to the economic transformation I need to underwrite.
