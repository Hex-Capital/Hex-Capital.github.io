# Verdant -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Verdant’s stated 80–90% AI accuracy makes Verdant a technically plausible, human-in-the-loop workflow product whose **single-miracle architecture is its strongest dimension and absent revenue curve is its weakest**. Verdant does not need new science: current models can identify ordinance conflicts while government personnel retain authority. The problem is whether municipalities will buy Verdant quickly enough to justify replacing established workflows. Two hundred interviews establish discovery, not a curve; no named jurisdiction, recurring usage, or revenue establishes that Verdant is “just working.”

**Color Genomics** is the high rung: workflow and data infrastructure built on commodity sequencing, avoiding a direct bet on the hard science. Verdant shares that mechanism by building ordinance mapping, intake, review, and staff-report generation on top of existing foundation models, with AWS, OpenAI, Anthropic, Supabase, and Reducto supplying the hard layer. Verdant diverges because Color controlled the operating environment, while Verdant must replace municipal workflows and achieve acceptable accuracy across jurisdiction-specific rules without a named deployment. The 80–90% accuracy disclosure and retained staff authority isolate the technical miracle, but procurement and implementation remain independent execution risks.

**Mistral’s** low revenue rung—pre-revenue, capital-intensive, and without a visible usage curve—was investable only as a Known Exception because the category had reopened after ChatGPT and the founders had already shipped Llama at Meta. Verdant has no equivalent override: private order forms prove Verdant can charge, but no customer, ARR, repeat usage, or signed LOI proves anybody is buying. The 444 LinkedIn followers and 200 interviews are false signals until converted into procurement. For an AI product launched July 3, silence on usage deserves the absent-evidence default.

**Seattle’s June 2026 CivCheck study** is a credible recent catalyst: municipal AI moved from exploration toward sanctioned deployment during 2025–2026. Over the next 18 months, that catalyst can compound for Verdant if early cities create referenceability, reusable ordinance mappings, and shorter procurement cycles for adjacent jurisdictions. It can also compound against Verdant because Clariti already owns CivCheck, GovWell has over 150 agencies, and Accela is adding AI to an installed platform. Verdant is skating for the puck, but the puck is already surrounded; a Seattle pilot is evidence of market reopening, not evidence that Verdant owns the window.

**Jason Yi’s prior work at Holly and Aidy**, plus 24 public repositories, puts Verdant above a credential-only founding profile. Verdant’s shift from grant-management positioning in March 2026 to a permitting launch in July shows iteration, although it may equally show unsettled product selection. The score flips into the Perplexity range if Yi and Aidan Ng can demonstrate that three product changes requested by municipal users shipped into repeat production use within weeks. Without that feedback loop, Verdant has shipping artifacts but not yet the “show up a week later with it built” cadence that triggered my Perplexity check.

**GovWell’s 150-plus agencies and $35 million raised** show why Verdant is not yet an index on municipal modernization. Stripe benefited whenever e-commerce grew because developers naturally passed through its payment rails; Verdant must win procurement jurisdiction by jurisdiction against GovWell, Clariti, Govstream, and Accela. Verdant could eventually become the ordinance-and-review layer every local-government system touches, but Verdant currently markets an end-to-end replacement in a permit-software market estimated at only $307 million in 2025. That is a share fight, not yet an index position.

**GovWell’s deployed-agency advantage** drives my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Verdant will have pivoted from end-to-end system replacement to an ordinance-checking and applicant-intake copilot integrated with incumbent municipal platforms, remaining below $2 million ARR and becoming a plausible Clariti acquisition target. Verdant’s core AI mechanism should work; slow procurement, limited index-like distribution, and incumbent bundling should force Verdant toward the narrower overlay model already demonstrated by Govstream.

**Verdant’s 200-plus municipal interviews** make the strongest bull case sharper than a generic pre-seed story: Aidan Ng understands government buyers, Jason Yi has built government software, and Seattle’s executive-order-backed adoption could mark a genuine category reopening. The Color Genomics exception suggests that workflow infrastructure on top of a hard layer can beat the hard layer itself, while Verdant’s rapid pivot may reflect disciplined customer discovery rather than drift. The evidence that wins today is the gap between those interviews and GovWell’s 150 agencies: Verdant has disclosed neither a deployment nor repeat use while three funded vendors already offer overlapping review workflows. Three named jurisdictions producing at least $500,000 of contracted ARR, with quarterly revenue growth above 20%, would flip my verdict to Invest.
