# HillStar -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by an enterprise sourcing platform is HillStar’s strongest path. A platform could buy HillStar to add utility-specific bid evaluation and traceable award decisions to its existing product. HillStar reports that it evaluated 22 bids covering about 19,800 pages in nine minutes and estimates that the utility saved $2.5 million on that procurement. The possible ownership advantage is faster deployment of a proven workflow that reuses customer-specific scoring criteria and award history across sourcing events. Repeat use, buyer demand, and the rights to transfer or reuse that history remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The seed milestone is repeat paid utility deployments that verify mandatory-criteria accuracy and show that prior scoring decisions improve later procurements. HillStar’s reported detection of a missing signed price certification in a separate municipal solicitation gives that test a concrete starting point. The memo gives no confirmed runway or cost to reach the milestone, no usable evidence of IP or contract transfer terms, and no separately supported route to cash for this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** With no terms for this check in the memo, assume a $500,000 investment at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, or 5×. At 3%, that requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds, versus $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The reported customer savings give no usable basis for estimating an acquisition price. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, no fees or other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, and a pro rata waterfall with no senior preference claims. All consideration is cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each $0. Prices and timing are illustrative assumptions.

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value → distributable equity | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $50M → $50M | $1.5M, or 3× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100M → $100M | $3.0M, or 6× at 3% ownership. At 2%, $2.0M, or 4×. |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. | $0, or 0× realized. Shares remain held. |

### Countercase

Enverus already offers AI bid evaluation inside its sourcing suite. A platform may be able to build the remaining utility-specific features or license HillStar’s workflow, especially if confidential bid histories cannot move with the business. The overlap weakens the rationale for paying an acquisition premium. The memo establishes neither equivalent performance on utility compliance work nor a binding transfer restriction, so the structural Pass override does not apply. The decisive gap is buyer-side evidence that ownership could justify a price that clears the holder’s hurdle. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

After repeat paid utility deployments, can a sourcing platform substantiate an acquisition price that would leave at least $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership? A buyer-backed comparison of acquiring, building, and licensing that quantifies the value of HillStar’s transferable workflow and supports clearing the hurdle within eight years would move the action to Reach out.
