# Altair Health -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Altair Health could make insurance billing require radically less human work. That is the consequence worth buying an option on. The dossier identifies a $7.73 billion U.S. billing-outsourcing market, but collecting money owed to a practice is different from eliminating the cost of collecting it. Altair has not quantified how much labor disappears, how much cheaper billing becomes, or how broadly its mechanism applies. The spending pool is established; the orders-of-magnitude consequence remains unproven.

The theorem prover is where I would concentrate diligence. Altair says agents propose claim actions, formalized insurer policies constrain those actions, and human billers handle verification failures. This architecture addresses a real weakness in autonomous billing. But a proof establishes consistency with encoded rules; it does not establish that those rules faithfully represent the insurer’s requirements. Incorrect policy translation can produce a perfectly verified mistake. Against athenahealth or Anomaly, the dossier supplies no measured improvement in accuracy, throughput, or cost. I would neither dismiss this as a generic wrapper nor award technical-discontinuity points for an architectural description.

The strongest argument against passing is that the founders have already crossed into actual operations. An unnamed substance-use-treatment practice reportedly receives several hundred thousand dollars in monthly insurer collections through Altair. Those are client receipts, not company revenue, but they are more informative than a demonstration. Aouss brings enterprise selling experience from Grafana Labs and AppDynamics; Jad brings healthcare ML research and founding engineering experience. Clinic visits to secure a design partner show willingness to do the work. However, the reported year to full deployment does not reveal which existential uncertainty was retired, at what cost, or against what threshold. Likewise, the transition from co-pilot to managed billing documents a changed offering, without establishing what evidence changed the founders’ beliefs. Their complementary backgrounds do not establish a record of recruiting people to own specific weaknesses.

Altair’s managed operation could fit my preference for turning scarce expert workflows into repeatable production platforms. Handling the entire workflow may expose exceptions that a software vendor never sees. That is the bull case that matters. Yet the dossier’s proposed payer-response learning loop is explicitly inferred, and no measured advantage establishes that accumulated cases make subsequent deployments cheaper or more reliable. Human escalation could supply valuable training evidence; it could also leave labor costs growing with collections volume. The free EHR and separate $395/year authorization listing do not resolve that question. Neither establishes managed-billing pricing or cost per successfully resolved claim.

My exception cases require something concrete to override missing commercial or milestone evidence: demonstrated capability progress, an exceptional instigator, or a quantified consequence large enough to justify extraordinary uncertainty. Altair’s deployment is encouraging, but it does not yet establish that override. Nor does the dossier establish the opposite red flags: buying growth before resolving technical risk, refusing disconfirming evidence, or competing solely through distribution. I pass because the supplied evidence does not yet distinguish a scalable technical breakthrough from a capable billing operation. The missing economics matter more than missing pre-seed revenue history.

One result would reopen my decision: an independently audited deployment across three payers showing at least fivefold lower fully loaded billing cost per collected dollar than the same practices’ baseline, with no deterioration in denial rates or compliance outcomes. Include policy-maintenance labor and every human escalation. That single experiment would test whether the verification system removes work or merely relocates it. Spend the next dollars making that answer decisive.
