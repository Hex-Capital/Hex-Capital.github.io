# Edviro -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years from now, a building should notice that an air handler is drifting, calculate whether software or new equipment is the economical fix, execute the safe intervention, and prove the savings without waiting for a facilities manager to reconcile five dashboards. Edviro has one little leak from that future: seven school sites are already feeding operational data into models that can issue work orders or change controls and then check the result against meter data. But I constructed most of that future myself. The dossier does not date the transition, identify the intermediate technical unlocks, or explain why Edviro rather than an existing building-controls vendor owns the resulting system.

The origin is much better than the market story. Hursh Shah found approximately $360,000 in school utility overbilling and turned that ordinary, irritating artifact into a company. Why did nobody catch it? Because bills, meters, BMS data, alarms, and work orders live in separate systems, while the person accountable for the budget lacks a closed loop from diagnosis to action. Edviro returns some agency to that operator: test an intervention, execute it, and verify the outcome. The mechanism is concrete, although the dossier does not establish whether the rent is captured by utilities, contractors, software vendors, or simply organizational neglect.

The product has survived more contact with reality than most pre-seed software. Edviro reached $20,000 ARR in three months, has seven school sites live and 29 described as expanding, and is running a construction-company measurement-and-verification pilot. Allowing a two-person startup to connect operational systems and influence schedules or setpoints is costly behavior: customers are putting budgets, comfort, and professional reputations behind the product. The claimed $400,000 in savings is compelling but still company-reported, aggregated, and unsupported by named case studies or a published baseline methodology. I believe the behavior before I believe the precise savings number.

Shah brings a prior aviation startup, NeurIPS workshop research, and the school-facilities wound; Tanuj Siripurapu brings ML work at RTX, infrastructure experience, and evidence that he can sell through a prior $20,000 agency. That is real Tizz and some Biz. What I cannot yet see is a dated, pre-company energy obsession, why these two founders chose each other, or how long they have operated together. Shah is identified as CEO, so there is no demonstrated leadership shell game, but the missing co-founder history and control arrangement matter before I invest my own money.

Here is the objection that stings: Siemens already offers AI-driven HVAC adjustment, Trane acquired BrainBox AI, and GridPoint, 75F, and Clockworks have capital, installed channels, or adjacent functionality. “The cost of building has collapsed” helps those companies too. Edviro currently controls neither proprietary hardware nor a demonstrated cross-building model advantage; seven per-building twins may be customer value without becoming a defensible shared asset. Integration also creates the ugly work—legacy protocols, cybersecurity, permissions, and savings disputes—that a two-person team cannot wish away. This is the unglamorous workflow-repair pattern I like, but it has not yet become the concealed enabling layer I look for beneath a visible wave.

My 36-month base case is that Edviro reaches $1 million to $3 million ARR in schools and adjacent public facilities but remains an integration-heavy optimization vendor rather than becoming the energy world-model layer. The working artifact makes outright failure too pessimistic; the absent controlled bottleneck makes category ownership too optimistic. None of my known exceptions resolves that gap: Edviro does not need forgiveness for being pre-revenue, and early customer proof cannot substitute for technical leverage. I am not writing the check today. One result would flip me: an independently verified cohort of at least 25 live sites showing that intervention accuracy or savings improves measurably as Edviro’s cross-building dataset grows, while deployments retain without bespoke integration work.
