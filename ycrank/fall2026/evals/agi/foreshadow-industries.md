# Foreshadow Industries -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** Foreshadow’s strongest case is that naval operators seeking longer missions with less crew exposure will need qualified autonomy retrofits for vessels they already own. If cheaper intelligence makes autonomy software easier to supply, buyers could still pay Foreshadow for vessel integration and qualification work that carries over to the next vessel. Foreshadow reports repeated overnight demonstrations, which support the plausibility of extended operation at prototype stage. The memo provides no buyer-verified comparison of mission duration or crew burden. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Foreshadow reports integrating three military vessels. That gives limited support to its ability to adapt the product across vessels, while reuse of integration or validation work remains unverified. Its separate claim of simultaneous operation on two vessels is an early execution signal. The memo documents a Navy procurement decision in the broader autonomous vessel market and leaves Foreshadow’s own buyer commitments unknown. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under a licensing-plus-integration scenario, the expansion unit is one more vessel. Contribution would depend on license and integration fees after hardware, installation, qualification, and support costs. The memo gives no pricing, per-vessel costs, or estimate of the reachable retrofit fleet. Path B remains unproven because the memo identifies neither a previously excluded buyer or transaction made viable by lower delivery labor nor a demonstrated reason to pay Foreshadow under model parity. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Sea Machines already sells vessel retrofits, and purpose-built autonomous vessels offer buyers another procurement route. With abundant capital and equal model access, competing retrofit suppliers could fund their own integration and qualification. If they meet the same buyer requirements with comparable effort, Foreshadow has no demonstrated advantage that would protect its economics. The memo establishes the presence of alternatives and leaves equivalent performance and switching costs unresolved. The evidence therefore supports Watch while a buyer-led comparison remains feasible. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Foreshadow secure a funded follow-on retrofit from a naval buyer after a qualification trial shows it meets the buyer’s requirements with less integration and validation work than a credible alternative, using reusable work Foreshadow controls? That result would support Reach out if rescoring clears the 65-point threshold. A credible alternative matching the result and effort would support Pass if the trial leaves no other durable advantage.
