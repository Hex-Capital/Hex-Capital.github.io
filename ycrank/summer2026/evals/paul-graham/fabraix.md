# Fabraix -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is not a benchmark. It is what broke. Ahmed Aly and Ibrahim Abdu have built a functioning attacker, but the dossier never names a specific obstacle, what they did when they encountered it, or how quickly their workaround changed a customer or technical outcome. Their four-year working relationship and experience at Monzo, Two, and Meta make resourcefulness plausible. They do not prove it. I cannot substitute résumés for the running-back test.

The technical evidence is much stronger. Nyx performs black-box, multi-turn attacks across websites, files, messages, and SaaS replicas without source-code integration. Fabraix reports a 78% AgentHarm attack-success rate against 67% for GPT-5.6 Sol, first exploits in under an hour, and a library exceeding 10,000 strategies. The Playground attracted more than 50,000 attempts. This is a real artifact built before permission, not a diagram of something the founders intend to build. But the benchmark is company-reported, and finding failures in agents operated by Fortune 500 companies does not establish that those companies hired Fabraix.

The founders did encounter adjacent versions of the problem before starting the company. Ahmed led payments-fraud work at Monzo, while Ibrahim built production-debugging agents at Meta and previously worked on compiler and database systems. That gives the idea an organic origin and makes the ugly surfaces—permissions, indirect prompt injection, changing data sources, enterprise certification—more than invented complexity. Still, the dossier does not show the moment when their own work produced Nyx, nor a rejected product route revised after evidence. Founder-market fit earns credit; a retrospective origin story and pivot history cannot be inferred from it.

The narrow well is not yet visible. Researchers will play with an adversarial challenge, as 50,000 attempts demonstrate, but the customer is supposedly an AI team that repeatedly inserts Nyx into CI and pays custom monthly usage fees. There is no named paying team, repeat scan history, revenue, or retention. Product Hunt rankings, 934 followers, and 71 GitHub stars are useful distribution experiments, not proof of pull. This distinction becomes more important because Virtue AI, HiddenLayer, and security platforms assembled through the Lakera and SPLX acquisitions can bundle similar testing into broader enterprise products.

The strongest bull case is that Fabraix has already built the difficult substrate and attracted enough attackers to improve it continuously. If Playground submissions create an attack-data flywheel, Nyx could become a developer primitive rather than another compliance scanner. That matches my preference for concrete proof and programmable infrastructure. The objection that changes my decision is that none of this yet demonstrates the founders can cross the enterprise schlep themselves. Demo-led sales, custom pricing, and SOC 2 “in progress” could become a long process in which technically impressive exploits produce demonstrations rather than recurring revenue.

I would not write the check today. My 36-month forecast is that Check Point acquires Fabraix for its attack corpus and team before the company reaches $3 million ARR. One verifiable condition would flip me: a named enterprise blocker followed by a founder-shipped workaround within two weeks, after which five production teams each run paid weekly CI scans for eight consecutive weeks. That would simultaneously show resourcefulness and the crude product being pulled back by real users.
