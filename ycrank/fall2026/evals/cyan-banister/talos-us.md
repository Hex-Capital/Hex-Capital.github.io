# Talos -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I want a utility technician to ask a transformer where it hurts and get an answer early enough to do something useful. Talos is building toward that interaction with external sensors and equipment-specific anomaly alerts. But a warning becomes valuable only after someone establishes which physical condition it represents, how much time remains, and what maintenance changes the outcome. The dossier does not yet connect those steps. I can imagine the future; I cannot give Talos credit for having demonstrated the path.

The strongest present-day mechanism is the replacement queue. Transformer delivery takes years, according to Wood Mackenzie’s October 2025 research. Earlier diagnosis could return some scheduling control to operators who otherwise inherit an emergency. Why wait for an asset to fail before learning its condition? That is a useful systems question. Still, this queue reflects physical supply constraints; Talos has not identified an artificial gatekeeper or quantified the inspection expense it removes. Monitoring could help operators navigate scarcity, but it does not manufacture the replacement transformer.

Kohli’s Constellation work in power engineering and predictive maintenance gives the team relevant contact with the problem. Hwang’s research on physiological waveforms across more than 100 TB of ICU data suggests experience extracting signals from messy measurements, while Musku’s research includes a first-author IEEE paper. These are substantive credentials. What I cannot establish is the chronology of obsession: experiments before Talos, repeated attempts to solve this particular problem, or a first operator persuaded to entrust equipment to the founders. A named CEO provides visible accountability; the dossier leaves ownership and contractual decision rights unestablished, rather than documenting a governance violation.

The little artifact I would pick up is the FieldSense demonstration mapping **304 magnetic readings around a condensate pump**. Someone has made an invisible field visible. Now I want to follow that picture into a maintenance decision. The demonstration establishes neither transformer diagnostic performance nor an operator spending money, labor, or reputation on its conclusions. Talos fits my preference for unglamorous repair beneath a larger technological wave, but the controlled enabling asset remains uncertain. Oktogrid already offers magnetic-adhesion sensing, and Camlin covers several established diagnostic modalities. External sensor fusion needs to reveal something consequential those alternatives miss. Talos’s described encryption and device-identity checks address relevant security concerns; the dossier establishes no severe consent or surveillance violation.

The bull case stings because a cheap retrofit that reliably buys maintenance time could become indispensable well before transformer supply catches up. Revenue is unnecessary to recognize that possibility. **CosmicBrain AI** supplied a precedent for backing pre-revenue physical technology, but its signed industrial proofs of concept and lifelong founder building record supplied conviction that Talos’s public evidence does not yet establish. I pass with my own money today. My flip condition is a paid utility deployment, independently verified, in which Talos localizes a subsequently confirmed transformer fault at least 30 days before intervention becomes necessary, with the operator documenting that the alert changed its maintenance plan. That would turn a compelling picture into evidence that someone can act on.
