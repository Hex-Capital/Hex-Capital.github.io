# OneCLI -- Elad Gil Evaluation

**OneCLI’s 320K downloads against no disclosed revenue curve make OneCLI a shipping-heavy security-infrastructure bet whose strongest signal is founder execution and whose weakest is proof that usage converts into buying behavior.** Jonathan Fishner and Guy Ben-Aharon already built ChartDB to 22.5K stars and more than $10K MRR, then took OneCLI from a January 2026 start to 2.9K stars, 170 forks, and a default NanoClaw integration. That is unusually legible pre-seed execution. The open question is whether OneCLI is “just working” commercially or whether developer attention is a false signal.

**Perplexity is the high rung on founder shipping: Aravind Srinivas repeatedly turned conversations into working product.** OneCLI shares that mechanism because Fishner and Ben-Aharon have shipped two open-source infrastructure products together, while OneCLI’s 4,523 weekly SDK downloads show that the second artifact is actually being used. OneCLI diverges from Perplexity because the dossier shows product iteration across projects, not yet the week-over-week customer-feedback loop or named enterprise adoption that made Perplexity’s cadence decisive. I place OneCLI just below that high rung rather than in the credential-only founder bucket.

**An AI startup with no disclosed growth curve is the low-rung failure mechanism: narrative can conceal a product that is not working.** OneCLI has more evidence than that anti-pattern—320K claimed downloads, NanoClaw bundling, and a priced $199 Team plan—but OneCLI discloses neither ARR, paid accounts, retention, nor month-over-month usage. Downloads can come from CI activity, experimentation, or one integration; GitHub stars are not procurement. At this point OneCLI sits between a pre-revenue exception such as Mistral, where extraordinary founder and catalyst evidence carried the deal, and the generic AI-security pitch whose “traction” never becomes revenue.

**The 2025–26 shift from chat interfaces to agents executing code and API calls is the mechanism that can compound OneCLI’s market position over the next 18 months.** Every additional autonomous action expands the credential attack surface, and prompt injection turns conventional secret delivery into a newly visible failure mode; that is a real recent catalyst, even though the dossier labels the timing inference rather than documents a regulatory or customer-driven discontinuity. OneCLI benefits if agent deployment keeps compounding, but OneCLI’s window is less clean than Anduril after Ukraine because Oasis, Astrix, Entro, and Infisical were already moving toward the same security buyer.

**Infisical’s April 2026 Agent Vault defines the counterfactual for OneCLI’s index position.** OneCLI becomes an index on agentic computing only if its gateway protocol becomes the default neutral credential boundary across frameworks, with NanoClaw as the first of many embedded integrations; three or four additional high-volume defaults would move the score into Stripe territory. If OneCLI remains an optional Apache-2.0 proxy sold beside Infisical’s nearly identical MIT-licensed proxy, OneCLI must win feature-by-feature through the same DevSecOps channel rather than benefiting automatically from category growth.

**Color Genomics is the relevant single-anchor parallel because it captured workflow value above a hard technical layer rather than betting on new science.** OneCLI similarly assumes working HTTPS proxies, existing secrets stores, and enforceable network policy; OneCLI does not need new cryptography or a model breakthrough. The miracle is adoption of an inline identity gateway as a standard control point. Proxy bypass, root-level gateway compromise, and permitted-action misuse are serious product constraints, but they are engineering and deployment problems rather than three independent miracles.

**OneCLI’s 2.9K stars and absent paid curve imply a falsifiable 36-month outcome: by July 2029, OneCLI will reach $5M–$10M ARR as an agent-access gateway, driven by managed VPC and enterprise policy deployments, but will not yet be the category’s independent winner.** OneCLI’s mechanism is open-source developer adoption feeding security-team conversion into the $1,499-per-month managed tier and larger custom contracts. That forecast follows the signature: unusually credible builders create distribution quickly, while monetization lags until enterprises standardize how agents receive authority.

**Infisical’s matching proxy architecture is the strongest bear case because Apache-2.0 makes OneCLI’s core mechanism copyable while Oasis and Astrix already possess enterprise security distribution.** OneCLI could discover that 320K downloads represent credential experiments rather than durable workloads, leaving OneCLI as a popular feature that a secrets incumbent bundles for free. The founders’ ChartDB history and NanoClaw default status keep that precedent from winning today: Fishner and Ben-Aharon have already converted open-source attention into revenue once and are embedding OneCLI at the framework layer. I would flip to Pass if OneCLI cannot disclose at least $25K MRR or five retained enterprise deployments by July 2027.
