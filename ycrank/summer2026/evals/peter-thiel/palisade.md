# Palisade -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The contested truth is that a marketplace loses more transactions from absent salesmanship than from inadequate search, and that persistent agents representing buyers, sellers, and staff can recover those transactions. The falsifying event is simple: after controlling for existing search and merchandising tools, Palisade fails to produce durable incremental conversion. More than $2.7 million in GMV driven since the Summer 2026 batch, with Shorebreak and Vaulted Atelier live in production, is meaningful evidence that the proposition is more than an AI slogan. But the dossier does not report the counterfactual conversion lift, repeat usage, or fees earned, so it has demonstrated an effect without yet measuring the secret precisely.

The harder question is whether Palisade discovered a company or merely a feature. Rep AI already engages shoppers from behavioral intent; Constructor already turns clickstream data into product discovery; Nosto already personalizes search and merchandising. General-purpose computer-use APIs further reduce the scarcity of interface navigation. Buyer, seller, and staff agents make Palisade broader, but breadth is not monopoly. Persistent histories and integrations may create switching costs, yet there is no evidence of exclusive data, proprietary performance, dominant share in a bounded segment, or a technical result competitors cannot reproduce. This is explicit commodity risk, not merely missing evidence, and it fails a primary gate.

Jonathan Salama is unusually coherent with the problem: Amazon Traffic & Marketing, founding engineering work on consumer-shopping agents at Sage AI Labs, and the million-subscriber Lulus TV project all connect software, commerce, and attention. A solo technical founder also avoids the pathology of artificially assembled co-founders. But the dossier establishes neither that he is full-time nor his ownership, salary, economic exposure, or whether all essential engineering remains in-house. Founder-market fit cannot substitute for founder-company alignment, so this remains an unanswered foundation question rather than an affirmative signal.

The strongest bull case is that Palisade’s early production result is already the exception: one founder deployed a product that influenced $2.7 million of GMV within a batch, and one script tag opens access to catalogs, booking engines, PMS systems, and CRMs. If those integrations cause proprietary histories to accumulate and improve conversion, today’s feature could become tomorrow’s control point. But the evidence currently runs the other way. Palisade sells through an unspecified demo process, discloses neither pricing nor sales-cycle length, and has no reported ARR, gross margin, retention, or customer-acquisition economics. GMV touched is not revenue captured.

The business may be large if one agent layer becomes the operating system for marketplace transactions, but the present market definition is horizontal conversational commerce, where several well-funded companies already compete. The dossier provides no arithmetic connecting monthly or annual plans to a multibillion-dollar outcome. My 36-month expectation is therefore that Palisade narrows into a vertical marketplace product and remains below $3 million ARR: useful conversion technology, but not yet a last-mover position.

I would pass today. The known exception for an uncertain monopoly requires an independently verified zero-to-one technical event; $2.7 million of influenced GMV across two named deployments is promising commercial evidence, not such an event. The verifiable flip condition is twelve-month cohort data from a narrowly defined marketplace segment showing at least a 20% incremental conversion lift, net revenue retention above 130%, and declining acquisition cost as Palisade’s proprietary interaction history grows. That would convert the central inference from “replicable agent feature” to “compounding data monopoly.”
