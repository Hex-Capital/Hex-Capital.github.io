# Fabraix -- Elad Gil Evaluation

A 78% AgentHarm attack-success rate makes **Fabraix** a catalyst-rich but revenue-unproven security-infrastructure bet. **Fabraix** is skating for the puck created by agents gaining tools, memory, files, and production permissions, but “vulnerabilities found in dozens of Fortune 500 agents” is not the same as customers buying. The dimensional signature is therefore a strong market re-opening paired with a weak economic curve: technically timely, commercially unresolved.

**Anduril** is the high rung on market re-opening because Ukraine converted defense-software inadequacy into procurement urgency. **Fabraix** shares the load-bearing mechanism: production agents create attack surfaces that static prompt testing cannot exercise, and the acquisitions of Lakera by Check Point and Prompt Security by SentinelOne confirm that the security window is real. **Fabraix** diverges because its catalyst remains inferred from deployment patterns rather than tied to a dated regulatory mandate, budget shift, or incumbent failure comparable to Anduril’s post-Ukraine opening.

**Harvey-clone legal AI pitches** are the low rung on revenue velocity because category heat without a disclosed buying curve is usually a false signal. **Fabraix** reports 50,000 Playground attempts, Product Hunt rank #6, and vulnerabilities across dozens of Fortune 500 agents, but **Fabraix** identifies none of those organizations as paying customers and reports no revenue, retention, or repeat-assessment rate. I give modest credit above the absent-evidence default because the usage is real, but public hacking participation is not Harvey’s “$3B → $5B → $8B” version of just working.

Ahmed Aly built fraud systems covering more than $1 billion in annual B2B transactions, while Ibrahim Abdu built production-debugging agents at Meta. Over 18 months, **Fabraix** can compound that relevant shipping history through Nyx’s 10,000-plus attack library: every successful exploit becomes training material for variants, which improves discovery and attracts harder targets. **Fabraix** has shipped a concrete product and challenge environment, but the dossier does not show the Perplexity pattern of weekly customer-driven iteration or independently document what Aly and Abdu repeatedly shipped outside their prior employers.

Lakera and Prompt Security were absorbed by incumbent security platforms, which defines the counterfactual for **Fabraix** becoming an index on agent deployment. The score flips upward if Nyx becomes a default CI or production-security gate used whenever an agent’s model, prompt, tool permissions, or data sources change, with proprietary attack trajectories accumulating across customers. **Fabraix** currently sits below that position: Mindgard already sells automated red-teaming, open-source Promptfoo, Garak, and PyRIT reduce switching costs, and **Fabraix** has disclosed no structural distribution barrier.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow infrastructure on top of a validated hard layer, not a bet on inventing the hard layer. **Fabraix** does not need new cryptography or a scientific breakthrough; **Fabraix** needs adaptive models to find economically meaningful agent exploits more reliably than fixed-payload tools, then needs ordinary product and GTM execution. The complication is that benchmark superiority, enterprise trust, and differentiation from bundled security products must all persist, but those are coupled execution risks rather than three independent miracles.

A $25.35 billion broader AI-cybersecurity market will create strategic demand even if standalone red-teaming economics remain thin. My 36-month forecast is that by July 2029 **Fabraix** is acquired by CrowdStrike for less than $250 million before **Fabraix** exceeds $10 million ARR. The catalyst makes adaptive agent testing strategically necessary, while the missing revenue curve and weak index position make platform absorption more probable than an independent breakout.

Lakera’s $30 million of funding and Prompt Security’s reported $300 million acquisition make the strongest bull case: agent security may be an open market where **Fabraix** resembles the Color exception—workflow infrastructure built by founders with unusually relevant fraud, agent, compiler, and database experience. Nyx’s 10,000-plus attacks and 78% AgentHarm result could become a proprietary learning loop, turning **Fabraix** into Decagon for adversarial testing. The evidence that wins today is attribution: no named buyer, no remediation outcome, and no recurring revenue separates the Fortune 500 claim from security tourism. Three named enterprises each paying at least $250,000 ARR, combined with greater than 20% monthly ARR growth for six months, would flip my verdict to Invest.
