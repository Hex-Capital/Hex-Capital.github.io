# Prized -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I start with three blanks: no proposed post-money valuation, no competing-term-sheet count, and no evidence this is genuinely the first scarce check. That is not proof of an auction, but it means I cannot underwrite the round as cheap, non-consensus risk. The second blank is worse: Prized names no use of proceeds, dated milestone, or finite experiment. “Lovable for internal tools” describes a product category; it does not tell me what truth my money buys.

The product itself is more serious than the slogan. Per-tool database roles, brokered credentials, outbound-host controls, read-only integrations, administrator approval, and audit logs address the real failure mode of letting non-engineers point agents at production systems. Griffith and Eliades also shipped fleet-management software together at Gander before its acquisition, so this is not two résumé tourists assembling a weekend wrapper. But they have receipts on the application-building side, not on the security-administration side where enterprise adoption will be won or lost.

Prized potentially joins two mistrustful systems: employees who want software immediately and administrators who must constrain what that software can do. That translation layer fits me. The problem is that Superblocks already offers natural-language internal-app generation with permissions, auditing, policy enforcement, and private-cloud deployment. Prized’s narrower controls may be better, but technical resemblance to a dark pool is not ownership of one. There is no proprietary enterprise workflow, customer data, or embedded distribution demonstrated yet.

The ten hand-delivered toolboxes are energetic founder-led outbound, not conversion evidence. Neither 223 LinkedIn followers nor a public YC launch shows that an administrator approved a connector, an employee repeatedly used a generated tool, or anyone paid. This is exactly where activity metrics can disguise the only question that matters: did controlled access to company data cause a purchase or retained workflow? No public revenue, pricing, gross margin, burn, or runway answers what happens when the next round disappears.

The strongest bull case is that the founders have identified the governance bottleneck created by shadow AI development before enterprises fully recognize it, built unusually concrete safeguards, and are personally manufacturing target-specific tools to cross the adoption gap. A few design partners could turn approved connector configurations and forked internal tools into real switching costs. But the known exception for an AI application requires hard conversion or retention evidence; Prized has none, while four well-capitalized platforms already overlap with the category.

My base case is that by August 2029 Prized has pivoted from generating internal tools to selling a governance control plane for employee-built AI applications, because the security layer is more differentiated than the builder. I pass today. The verifiable flip condition is three named companies paying after security review, with at least 80% six-month workspace retention and evidence that approved connectors produce multiple repeatedly used tools per customer. That would prove both the translation layer and the purchase-causing workflow; another launch metric would not.
