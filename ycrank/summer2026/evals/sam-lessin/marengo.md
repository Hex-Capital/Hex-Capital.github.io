# Marengo -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The proposed post-money valuation is unknown, the competing-term-sheet count is unknown, and the dossier does not establish whether this is Marengo’s first genuinely scarce check. YC admission and a 16-of-1,614 Onstage selection show institutional attention, not cheapness or non-consensus belief. I will not invent an auction, but I also will not treat financing silence as favorable evidence when price determines whether the uncertainty is still mine to buy.

“Twice the design speed at half the cost” could become a clean venture experiment. It is not one yet. Marengo has disclosed no use of proceeds, named pilot, dated milestone, or acceptance test showing what this round will prove. The important card is not whether AI can generate a plausible data-center concept; it is whether a builder will approve, purchase, and construct from one under measurable reliability constraints. A broad plan to automate engineering would finance activity. A paid design passing an agreed validation threshold would reveal truth.

The founders give the company its strongest case. Emil Ares has worked in physics, control systems, and systems research; Gad Marconi built the HITEN orbital-dynamics library and has aerospace-design experience. They have shipped technical work on the simulation side of the bridge, and the April 2026 space-mission product suggests the underlying capability preceded the data-center positioning. But neither founder has documented data-center design, construction, commissioning, or approval experience. This is an engineering-software team entering an operational system, not yet an outsider-insider team proven on both sides of it.

The bull case stings because the physical workflow is difficult, economically important, and growing: data-center electricity use rose 17% in 2025, large technology companies spent more than $400 billion in capex, and Marengo says it is already working with some of the world’s largest builders. If that work is an embedded pilot, the company could accumulate proprietary designs, simulation results, and approval feedback faster than horizontal competitors. But today those customers are unnamed, no purchase or repeat use is disclosed, and the proposed data layer is explicitly inferred. The 329 LinkedIn followers, UKspace membership, and finalist selection do not cause a customer action.

The AI exception therefore does not apply yet. Ando earned that exception through measured forecast accuracy, paid conversion, and retention; Marengo supplies none of the analogous proof. It also lacks accuracy benchmarks, certifications, completed-project cases, and disclosed CAD or BIM integrations, while SWAPP already writes into Revit and ArchiCAD and Entangl occupies the data-center workflow. The risk is not merely another competitor. It is that Marengo remains a demonstration layer while incumbents own the workflow and builders retain the economically valuable project data.

There is also no pricing, revenue, gross margin, burn, or route to break-even, so deleting the next financing leaves no operating model to underwrite. My base case by August 2029 is that Marengo has pivoted back toward aerospace or general engineering-design services and remains below $1 million ARR, because the team has technical receipts but no demonstrated data-center purchase loop. I am passing. One verifiable condition flips me: a signed, paid pilot with a named data-center builder that includes a dated engineering-acceptance benchmark and converts into production deployment.
