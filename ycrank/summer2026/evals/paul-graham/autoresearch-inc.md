# Autoresearch, Inc. -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Autoresearch, Inc. has shown that it can run experiments. I want to know what happened when an experiment failed in a way the founders hadn't anticipated. The reported 4,188 runs over two days on 50 B200 GPUs demonstrate execution, but the dossier doesn't identify a specific obstacle, the founder who overcame it, and the time it took. That distinction matters when I'm investing in people. I give the experiment system substantial credit without turning its output into an invented story about resourcefulness.

The strongest evidence is the [released training code](https://github.com/hiloopai/search-is-enough) and the reported median validation result of 0.9016 bits-per-byte across 25 confirmation runs. Interleaved testing suggests the team understands that searching thousands of alternatives can produce apparent improvements through noise. This fits my preference for technical founders who produce an inspectable artifact before asking anyone to believe them. It remains a company-reported result on a hardware-sensitive benchmark. It establishes more about their ability to build an experiment engine than about its usefulness on a customer's problem.

There is a serious bull case here. Karan and Thomas built production ML systems at Reducto and elsewhere; Jad brings formal-verification expertise. Running research inside customer infrastructure entails precisely the integrations, credentials, isolation, and operational work that can hide a good business. Their shared work at Reducto also supplies some evidence behind the founding relationship. But relevant employment doesn't establish that these founders personally encountered this particular need. Nor does the hiloop rename establish an evidence-driven change of route. Both remain unanswered rather than adverse.

The missing commercial fact is simple: who asked them to run another campaign? [Paid-plan terms and enterprise MSAs](https://autoresearch.inc/terms/) describe how a customer could pay, not evidence that one has paid or returned. The small following doesn't concern me. The absence of demonstrated repeat use does, because customers can extend an open-source research loop themselves. Autoresearch must discover which painful part they will repeatedly pay to avoid. Its programmable-infrastructure resemblance fits an unstated pattern in my investments, but the corresponding developer appropriation and daily use aren't documented. Founder-led outreach is sensible; I see no disclosed dependence on a ceremonial launch, a major partner, or prematurely hired salespeople.

I would pass at 41. My exceptions allow working products or exceptional validated results to overcome uncertain founder priors, so the published recipe deserves serious consideration. Here it doesn't yet replace the missing evidence of action under resistance or customer pull. This is a promising technical team whose strongest proof concerns its machinery. One documented customer engagement would flip my decision: a named team presents a blocker, the founders ship a workaround within weeks, and that customer pays for a second campaign after verifying the improvement on held-out production tasks.
