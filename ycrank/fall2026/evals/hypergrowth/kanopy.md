# Kanopy -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with electric utilities, Kanopy has a plausible path to serving the same recurring vegetation inspection job across markets. Its hosted software reconstructs grid assets from ordinary drone, vehicle, or handheld video. The footage-to-clearance workflow looks reusable, while the work needed to meet local clearance standards and secure utility acceptance remains uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

ACRT’s Arborcision launch establishes a contractor distribution channel. Kanopy’s credentialed SDK gives authorized buyers a reusable way to upload footage and retrieve results. The likely sales motion is enterprise contracting, with the effort required for a typical utility to buy, validate, and start using the product still unclear. Repeat surveys and accumulated inspection records could support continued use. How those surveys are billed and whether customers spend more as coverage expands remain unknown. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Kanopy reported 3,093 cumulative miles mapped, including over 300 miles across four states in two weeks. The six reported LOIs or master service agreements cover 270,000 miles of potential scope. The billing status of mapped miles and committed order volumes are unknown, so Initial traction uses the absent-evidence default. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

Under an illustrative per-mile contract, contribution would be revenue per billed mile less reconstruction and storage costs and the attributable costs of sales, setup, support, footage capture, and field validation that Kanopy bears. Buyer- or contractor-supplied video could limit Kanopy’s field costs. Pricing, the division of fieldwork, and operating costs are unknown, so Cost economics uses the absent-evidence default. Those gaps and the limited evidence of paid repeat use keep confidence Low. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Kanopy could scale as a contractor-led inspection service whose field workload grows with every utility and every survey. The California utility plan cited in the memo calls for field validation of remote methods and retains ground inspections where those methods fall short. If that work remains extensive on repeat surveys, it could slow onboarding and absorb the contribution from each deployment. The memo establishes a validation risk in one utility workflow. The structural downside gate remains open because the extent and economics of that work across ordinary deployments are unresolved. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can ACRT take a utility in a new jurisdiction from its first paid survey to a larger paid repeat order using the existing workflow, with bounded validation work and healthy incremental contribution? A paid repeat order, a record of the adaptations required, and actual delivery costs would answer it. Move to Reach out if that evidence brings the score to at least 65 with Medium or High confidence. Move to Pass if the deployment shows that unavoidable fieldwork makes repeat surveys uneconomic and there is no credible way to reduce that work.
