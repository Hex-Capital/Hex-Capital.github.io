# Tire Swing -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

The interesting future here is a family getting the keys while its eligibility paperwork is still warm. By 2032, affordable-housing compliance could happen alongside an application rather than becoming another queue between a person and a home. Tire Swing’s source-linked findings and requests for missing documents suggest a possible route. But that future requires reliable rule maintenance, reviewers trusting the results, and operators actually shortening the wait. The dossier establishes the proposed workflow; it does not yet establish that transition. I can imagine the destination more clearly than I can see it arriving.

Why does the queue exist? Operators must establish income and eligibility, staff inspect documents, and outside consultants sell additional review capacity. Tire Swing attacks identifiable administrative labor and vacancy costs. That gives this more substance than “AI for real estate.” The agency returned could be meaningful: staff spend less time chasing documents, and applicants spend less time waiting. But the dossier supplies no measured delay, consultant bill, or completed move-in accelerated by the product. Eligibility rules also serve a purpose; automating their administration does not itself remove an artificial restriction. I give the company credit for locating the friction, with limited evidence of how much control it returns.

Paul Witten’s leadership of a memory-forensics working group and Lucas Irvine’s Cloud Custodian experience make security and rules-based software credible territory for this team. Those are useful skills for handling consequential documents. They do not establish a longstanding affordable-housing obsession or an independently discovered operating wound. The earlier healthcare-compliance product makes me curious about what customers taught them before this change. It does not prove opportunism. Founder-led demonstrations and AHF Live attendance show outreach, but the dossier does not document the pre-company experiments or first-believer recruitment that would make me unusually confident in these particular founders.

The strongest case against my pass is sitting inside the product description: thousands of deterministic and AI checks, property-specific configurations, source-linked findings, and qualified human approval. An unglamorous workflow can become essential infrastructure by getting difficult details right. HUD’s January 1, 2027 HOTMA enforcement date gives operators a concrete reason to examine their processes. This fits my pattern of backing mundane operating repairs. Yet Yardi already combines an installed property-management relationship with direct payroll and banking verification, while Pronto addresses qualification and annual compliance. Tire Swing needs to demonstrate that its document-review layer earns a durable place between those systems and the reviewer. A reported check library is a useful asset; it is not yet a demonstrated bottleneck others depend upon.

I would not require revenue to make this bet. My exceptions for pre-revenue companies depend on unusually strong prototype evidence and founder obsession, and neither is documented here at that strength. An anonymized prospect-file demonstration does not show customers repeatedly entrusting live certifications to the product. The reported $500,000 financing cannot substitute for that behavior. Tenant financial documents also make privacy a real gate: reported encryption, role-based access, and SOC 2 Type II certification are constructive evidence, although they do not establish tenant consent or downstream data custody. There is no documented severe privacy violation, concealed leadership, or dependence on one closed model supplier that warrants an adverse deduction.

I pass with my own money today. Tire Swing has found consequential paperwork, but the evidence has not connected competent automation to a different lived housing experience. My one verifiable flip condition is an independently audited longitudinal deployment showing that households receive keys sooner because Tire Swing reduces certification time, without increasing eligibility errors. That result would make the family’s shorter wait the artifact worth following.
