# Spacesheep -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Spacesheep’s strongest plausible liquidity route is a strategic sale to a collaboration software vendor. Its cross-agent publishing, inline review, and searchable history could give a vendor such as Gamma a way to bring work created in Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor into its product. That buyer rationale is an inference. Spacesheep showcases an AI Foundry dashboard and a live thermal-camera stream, and the memo records a public launch, small browser-extension install counts, and a reported $125,000 seed investment. Those signals support testing the workflow with paying teams. Paid adoption, repeat use, buyer economics, and the capital needed to reach that milestone remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Under the benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity for a full pro rata payout. The sale cases below are sensitivities. Both assume an all-cash close by year eight, $5 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, and an eligible holder who sells their entire stake and receives 3% of the remaining equity proceeds after the waterfall, with no preference leakage or holdback. Actual rights and closing terms are unverified.

| Case | Assumed enterprise value | Distributable equity | Holder cash |
|------|-------------------------:|---------------------:|------------:|
| Modest sale | $40 million | $35 million | $1.05 million (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100 million | $95 million | $2.85 million (5.7×) |

Even in the strong case, an eligible sale of only 80% of the holder’s stake would yield $2.28 million. The memo provides no transaction price evidence or independent route through an eligible secondary, public resale, buyback, or distribution. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Claude Artifacts and ChatGPT Sites already provide native publishing, and Type.com already connects Claude and Codex to a shared workspace. Spacesheep also faces default domain restrictions in Claude Code on the web, while export to Drive or GitHub could make its archive easier to leave. A buyer could decide its existing workflow is sufficient or that cross-agent review is cheaper to build than buy. That weakens the acquisition rationale. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported sale-price range that falls short of the hurdle, so the Pass gate is untriggered. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could a collaboration vendor justify from the customer value of Spacesheep’s cross-agent review workflow? A paid pilot showing sustained use across multiple agents, paired with buyer-side estimates of incremental revenue or retention, would give that question a measurable answer. Move to Reach out if those economics credibly support an enterprise value near or above the modeled $88.3 million hurdle.
