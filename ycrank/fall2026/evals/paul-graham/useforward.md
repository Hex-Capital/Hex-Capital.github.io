# Forward -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The useful fact about Forward is that the founders reportedly made more than 200 cold calls a day. They went looking for customers themselves. But I want the next part of that story: what kept a particular advisor from getting a deal funded, what the founders changed, and how quickly it worked. The dossier documents effort without documenting that sequence. I cannot infer resourcefulness under resistance from call volume alone.

These founders have a plausible reason to be doing this. Daniel Yoon worked in private credit at Kain Lawyers; Riley Janjis worked on credit diligence at Rixon Capital and Longreach Alternatives. They also previously operated a tutoring business together. That makes the team more credible than three strangers assembling an “AI for finance” application. Turning borrower documents into opportunities that fit actual lender mandates involves tedious work worth understanding. But prior employment establishes proximity to the problem, not yet the specific collision that caused them to build Forward.

The strongest argument for investing is the investor-reported $180K annualised revenue within two months. Even with currency and revenue composition unspecified, that deserves substantially more attention than the fundraising or grant. Someone apparently found the service worth paying for. The founder-reported 50+ funds onboarded also identifies a reasonably narrow initial audience. But $300M of “processed” deals is not $300M funded, and 500% weekly growth without a metric or period tells me almost nothing. Forward could have found an urgent need while still lacking evidence that the same customers come back.

The important technical question is whether each transaction makes the next one easier. A useful matching system could accumulate knowledge about which lenders actually execute particular deals. A service business could instead accumulate more documents and require more people. Darryl Luo’s prior CTO role supports technical competence, but the dossier supplies no measured matching result or inspectable software proof. Forward therefore does not yet exhibit my recurring pattern of a difficult technical substrate or numerical proof preceding the investment. Finitive already operates in origination, while adjacent vendors such as Heron handle financial documents; Forward needs evidence that its accumulated knowledge improves outcomes. There is also no documented product revision tied to customer evidence, so I cannot award credit for adaptability merely because the founders are busy.

The Fidelity Bank, Hudson Cove, and iPartners announcements could help recruit users. They would become a red flag if the growth plan depended on those partners doing the recruiting. Likewise, the November 6 launch placeholder does not establish a ceremonial-launch obsession when earlier revenue is reported. The serious objection to passing is that these founders may already be doing exactly the manual work from which good software emerges. I take that seriously. But my exceptions require a working prototype, unmistakable user pull, or validated technical proof strong enough to replace missing founder evidence; the current reports do not establish that. I would pass on this dossier. One verifiable flip condition would be a documented repeat customer whose initially failed placement caused the founders to ship a specific workaround within weeks, followed by a funded transaction and another paid submission. That would connect effort, learning, and demand.
