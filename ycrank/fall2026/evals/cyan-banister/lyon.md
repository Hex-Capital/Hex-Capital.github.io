# Lyon -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Six years forward, I can imagine a bank learning a customer’s financial patterns once, then using that understanding across products without commissioning another feature-engineering project every time. Lyon’s customer embeddings and downstream adapters give that future a technical shape. But the important transition is from bespoke prediction projects to reusable institutional memory. The dossier establishes an approach to building that memory; it does not yet establish that reuse works. I will not turn my daydream into evidence for the company.

Why does each prediction take months today? Lyon points to rules and task-specific feature engineering. That identifies a real queue and the labor creating it. Moving that work into a reusable model could give customer teams more control over what they build. But whose agency ultimately increases? The demonstrated application identifies premium-card converters. That can improve a bank’s marketing economics without returning meaningful control to the person whose transactions trained the model. There is operational friction here, but no quantified rent or artificial restriction being removed.

The strongest argument against passing is substantial: Lyon reports training on **28 billion transactions** for a fintech serving tens of millions of active users, with **4× greater precision** identifying premium-card converters than existing rules. Getting an institution to commit that data and implementation effort is meaningful behavior at pre-seed. I can back a company before revenue when a functioning artifact and committed counterparties make the destination credible. Here, however, the customer is unnamed, the benchmark methodology is undisclosed, and credit deployment remains underway. These facts support a working prediction product; they do not establish repeat adoption across applications. The insurer engagement adds interest without resolving that distinction.

The founders have made things. Noya’s TreeHacks 2025 Edge AI win and Höhn’s separate Deaflingo project, with **152 GitHub stars**, carry more weight for me than Stanford affiliations alone. Noya’s NVIDIA and finance experience also gives the team relevant context. What I cannot recover from those facts is a sustained, pre-company obsession with transaction modeling or evidence of recruiting others into that specific belief. Noya is identified as CEO; Höhn’s absence from the YC listing warrants clarification, but it does not establish concealed leadership or equal-control dysfunction.

Lyon does fit my less obvious pattern of looking beneath applications for enabling infrastructure. Training customer-specific models from scratch, inside the customer’s VPC, is substantive technical work rather than merely renting a model endpoint. Customer ownership also matters. Yet proprietary customer data is not automatically Lyon’s defensible asset, and SAP’s acquisition of Prior Labs puts overlapping technology inside an established enterprise distribution channel. The VPC architecture addresses custody meaningfully, while leaving consent, permitted uses, and sensitive financial inference unresolved. I see no demonstrated severe privacy violation; I also cannot treat deployment location as complete privacy clearance.

I would pass with my own money today. The decisive gap is whether Lyon owns a repeatable enabling method or delivers a succession of skilled customer projects. My one verifiable flip condition is a customer-confirmed production case showing the same trained representation reused across three distinct financial workflows, with measured reductions in implementation time and documented permission for those uses. That would connect the working artifact to the future I can presently only imagine.
