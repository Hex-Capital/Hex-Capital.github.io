# Orca Aerospace -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest private-company path. A spacecraft-bus supplier could buy Orca’s onboard autonomy software and flight-stack integrations to control how fault recovery is qualified, maintained, and deployed across its customers’ spacecraft. Ownership could save integration and qualification work across deployments and give the buyer control over updates. Those benefits are inferred. The memo provides no measured savings or evidence that a buyer values ownership above a license. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*)

The memo records a signed Orbital letter of intent with a ceiling of $20 million, contingent on fleet rollout, and a separate Lyra concept study. The letter describes an integration fee and per-satellite licenses, with amounts and payments undisclosed. Orca’s planned milestones are a possible Pathfinder test in July 2027 and use on Orbital-1 in 2028. A flight test showing faster fault recovery outside ground-contact windows, safe command execution within avionics limits, and a reusable Reflex integration would strengthen the acquisition case. The founders’ relevant experience supports execution, while test funding, integration costs, and runway remain unknown. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*)

**Proceeds bridge.** Using the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership dilutes to 3% with no follow-on checks. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The scenarios assume zero net debt, no senior preferences or other waterfall adjustments, and pro rata distribution before investor taxes. Enterprise value therefore equals distributable equity proceeds in the modeled sales. Both assume cash at close, with stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation each set to zero.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Holder cash | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest acquisition at $50 million enterprise value | Year 5 | $1.5 million | 3.0× |
| Strong acquisition at $100 million enterprise value | Year 7 | $3.0 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private ownership with growth plateauing, buyers staying away, public scale out of reach, and cash reinvested | Through year 8 | $0, shares retained | 0.0× |

The sale prices are scenario inputs. The strong case clears the hurdle at 3% but falls to $2 million, or 4.0×, at 2%. The memo supplies no usable acquisition pricing or buyer economics to support either price, and IP ownership and transfer rights are unverified. Lyra adds a second use case for the same software. The memo supplies no independent path to cash for this holder through a buyout, secondary, public resale, or company-funded payout. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Orbital’s proposed license gives it a way to use Orca’s software without buying the company. Shield AI and Sedaro have demonstrated onboard AI with independent command checking in flight, and AIKO has demonstrated onboard replanning. If licensing or a competing system delivers comparable performance and qualification costs, a strategic buyer has little reason to pay an ownership premium. The memo leaves that substitution question open and establishes neither a binding price ceiling nor a probable prolonged-private outcome. The structural Pass override remains unmet. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

### Decisive diligence question

After flight validation, can a spacecraft-bus supplier quantify enough value from owning Orca’s software and integrations, compared with licensing them, to support a price that yields $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds? A buyer-backed estimate tied to measured flight results and reusable integration work would move the action to **Reach out** if it supports that hurdle under the modeled terms. A successful flight test alone would leave the action at **Watch**.
