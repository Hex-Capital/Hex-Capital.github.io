# Forward -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with its initial commercial real estate advisors, Forward has a plausible path to serving the same recurring job abroad. Advisors need to find lenders with current mandates, and lenders need to screen incoming deals. Forward’s documented workflow for preparing financing briefs, matching mandates, and coordinating submissions appears reusable. Each new market would still need an active lender network, and the memo leaves the extent of local workflow changes uncertain. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

Forward used cold calls and LinkedIn outreach before starting a controlled rollout with one exclusive advisor. It reports that 100 U.S. funds signed its fee agreement within three months of its July 2026 U.S. arrival. That shows early reach for a seven-person team, while the ordinary path from outreach to first funded loan remains unproven. A customer testimonial describes lender introductions and term sheets. Investor reports cite $180,000 in annualized revenue and $545,000 in contracted revenue, with currency and collections unspecified. These are early commercial signals. The memo establishes neither the amount of fees earned nor their growth across comparable periods. (*Forward*, *The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

Lenders pay 25 basis points on funded loans. Repeat deal flow and current mandate data give lenders a reason to return, and a lender can spend more by funding more principal through Forward. Repeat paid use and expansion within existing lender accounts remain unverified. In an illustrative scenario of five $10 million submissions with one funded loan, Forward would earn $25,000, or $5,000 per submission on average. Positive contribution would require attributable acquisition, onboarding, document processing, and deal coordination across all five submissions to cost less than $25,000. Automation offers a concrete way to reduce that cost, with savings still unmeasured. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Even with strong initial fit, scaling could require rebuilding lender relationships market by market and spending substantial staff time on deals that never fund. The exclusive first advisor, outbound acquisition, and closing-dependent fee support that concern. The structural downside gate stays open because the memo does not establish that each market needs a bespoke rebuild or that unavoidable deal work makes contribution uneconomic. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Forward use its current product and a standard onboarding flow to generate repeat, contribution-positive funded loans through multiple independent advisors in a second country? A dated cohort showing collected fees, repeat funding by the same lenders, limited local adaptation, and positive contribution after counting staff time and failed deals could move the action to Reach out if the rescored case reaches 65 with at least Medium confidence. Evidence that rollout requires substantial bespoke rebuilding or that unavoidable deal work makes growth uneconomic would move the action to Pass.
