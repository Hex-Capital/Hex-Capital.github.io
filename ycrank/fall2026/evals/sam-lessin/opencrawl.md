# OpenCrawl -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

OpenCrawl’s proposed post-money, competing-term-sheet count, and remaining financing need are all undisclosed. I cannot call this a cheap, scarce check, and YC participation does not establish an auction either. The dossier also names no capital-funded experiment with a deadline and a pass/fail result. Those two gates remain at my missing-evidence defaults. The underwriting question is concrete: what does my money prove beyond the founders’ ability to build another collection-and-search service?

The founders’ ability is the strongest affirmative evidence. Shalin Patel built transcript-ingestion infrastructure at Pocus; Deep Utkarsh built GTM agents there and worked on Sigma’s query compiler. They have shipped on both the data-production and data-consumption sides of the proposed bridge. Their shared Pocus experience makes the translation-layer thesis credible: these are engineers who have encountered the machinery an agent needs behind its answer. That earns substantial founder credit. It does not establish that customers will buy this particular bridge.

The strongest bull case is that OpenCrawl can turn bespoke customer data into an embedded workflow that general search cannot serve. Its source discovery, hosted crawlers, maintained indexes, and MCP/API retrieval could become valuable operating infrastructure. But who owns the resulting advantage? Customer-specific data may strengthen the customer while leaving OpenCrawl replaceable. Firecrawl’s September 2026 Alexandria release already combines providers, custom connectors, and indexes. No disclosed purchase, renewal, or migration difficulty shows that OpenCrawl owns privileged access. The founders’ reported 32 TB ingested and 600-plus incidents belong to OnePatch’s observability product; they demonstrate prior operation, not current search demand. The 108 LinkedIn followers establish neither.

Delete the next financing and the cash model is still blank. The $25 starter grant, $10 monthly allowance, and $10 minimum top-ups describe an acquisition mechanism, not collected revenue or contribution profit. Crawling, OCR, transcription, and index maintenance incur costs; the dossier supplies no margin, burn, or seed-funded break-even plan. I therefore use the economics default rather than assume the business is uneconomic. OpenCrawl also deserves a more precise objection than “AI wrapper”: maintaining source infrastructure can retain value through model improvements. The unresolved risk is whether overlapping vendors can supply the same service before the team establishes customer dependence.

I pass at 32. The demonstrated engineering bridge fits my preference for founders who connect systems, but the exceptional-case overrides require observed conversion, durable operating economics, or a financing structure that captures an established asset. None is documented here. Nor is there evidence of an auction or founder-governance problem; those are not reasons for this pass. One verifiable flip condition would reopen underwriting: a signed sub-$10-million post-money financing plan funding a 90-day experiment in which three unrelated customers renew paid bespoke indexes at positive contribution margin. That would connect price, a finite venture moment, and customer behavior. Today, OpenCrawl has credible builders and an unproven investment proposition.
