# Kara -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The contested truth is that diamond’s failure to become an engineering material reflects a manufacturing problem, not an economic one. What would prove this wrong? If Kara can grow and process material with the required dimensions, defect density, and yield, yet semiconductor customers still choose silicon carbide, gallium nitride, or copper after qualification, then superior bulk properties do not create a superior product. The dossier establishes diamond’s theoretical advantage—2,400 W/m·K thermal conductivity and a 10 MV/cm breakdown field—but reports none of Kara’s own wafer dimensions, yields, defect rates, or independently measured results. This is a plausible secret, but it remains an assertion rather than a falsified-and-surviving experiment.

The founders make the claim more credible than a fashionable advanced-materials pitch. Aarin Jhaveri is a fifth-generation diamantaire who has sold more than $5 million of diamonds; Anuveer Chadha worked on diamond quantum-sensing systems at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory; Arjun Lakhanpal adds computing and data experience. The combination of materials knowledge, customer familiarity, and in-house growth, processing, and characterization is unusually coherent. Yet Berkeley attendance is not evidence that the three previously built together, and the dossier supplies no ownership, compensation, or complete full-time-status record. Anuveer’s public profile still identifying Berkeley may be stale, but founding structure is too important to fill with optimism.

The larger problem is monopoly versus commodity. Kara offers controlled orientation, roughness, impurity profile, and isotope fraction, but established suppliers already sell overlapping wafers and heat spreaders, while more than a dozen firms are developing single-crystal diamond semiconductor wafers. Diamond Foundry has raised $315 million, Diamfab €8.7 million, and Akash Systems has already announced commercially available diamond-cooled AMD MI350X servers. In-house production can become proprietary process knowledge; it is not evidence that such knowledge presently delivers a 10x result. Kara has entered a technically difficult market, but difficulty alone does not abolish competition.

The timing is real. NVIDIA’s designs reach 120 kW per Blackwell rack, and the March 2026 Akash launch suggests that diamond thermal materials may finally have a qualification path. This aligns with the neglected physical layer of the AI production stack. But the strongest bull case also exposes the weakness: if demand is urgent and Kara’s integrated process is already producing customer-grade material, a single qualified heat-spreader specification could become a narrow beachhead with substantial expansion into RF, power electronics, aerospace, and quantum systems. The dossier instead shows no customer, pilot, production shipment, price, gross margin, or repeatable sales cycle. The known exception for a coordination-heavy physical frontier does not apply without a validated unit or deployment path.

The economics remain indefinite. A $10.2 million substrate segment cannot support a power-law outcome by itself, while the $2.12 billion broader synthetic-diamond market does not show what share Kara can capture or retain. Substrates, engineered layers, membranes, heat spreaders, submounts, and custom fabrication may share a production platform, but they do not yet constitute one demonstrated revenue engine. NDA-gated specifications and contact-led technical sales describe how industrial materials are commonly sold; they do not establish that Kara’s acquisition cost, qualification burden, or margins fit the contract value.

I would pass today. The decisive flip condition is a paid, repeat production order from a named semiconductor or compute-hardware customer, supported by independent measurements showing that Kara’s material delivers at least a 10x system-level thermal or electrical advantage at a disclosed manufacturable yield. That single result would simultaneously test the secret, define the beachhead, reveal proprietary process value, and turn a generic qualification route into distribution evidence. Until then, Kara is a capable team pursuing a consequential material in a crowded category: natural secret unproved, monopoly absent.
