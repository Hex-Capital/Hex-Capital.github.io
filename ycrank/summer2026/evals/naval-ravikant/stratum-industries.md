# Stratum Industries -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Raj Jagiasi averaged 220% of quota while Vihaan Jagiasi shipped as Cadastral’s founding engineer number three. Stratum Industries is a high-energy founder pair attached to a low-leverage distribution architecture. Intelligence and energy are visible; integrity has no negative signal. But Stratum Industries must win introductions, procurement, security approval, and agency trust before its code can compound. The founders are the strongest asset. Permission is the weakest.

**Stack Overflow** is the high rung: practicing builders created a developer product, shipped publicly, and let users pull the product into the market. Stratum Industries shares the complementary-builder mechanism—Raj sells and Vihaan builds—but Stratum Industries diverges where the analogy matters. Neither brother has disclosed government adjudication experience, Vihaan has no confirmed public repository, and Raj’s 220% quota performance proves sales ability rather than founder-product-market fit. I can see intelligence and energy. A 10-year partnership still requires evidence of judgment under consequential failure.

**Anchorage Digital** is the low rung on permissionless leverage because regulatory permission is the architecture, not an inconvenience. Anchorage survived that inversion through pre-consensus crypto timing and an OCC charter that became a moat. Stratum Industries also depends on permission: Stratum Industries explicitly asks for introductions to agency adjudication leaders while disclosing no FedRAMP posture, security certification, pilot, or proprietary dataset. Salesforce already holds FedRAMP-High and DoD IL5 authorization. Stratum Industries has the permission bottleneck without Anchorage’s permission-as-moat exception.

**ClearOS** already markets AI caseworkers for disability claims, permits, FOIA requests, and licensing. Over 18 months, each Stratum Industries agency deployment would produce isolated rubrics, integrations, and audit histories; one agency does not automatically make another agency’s workflow more valuable. Stratum Industries could accumulate switching costs, but switching costs are not a network. The unstated portfolio pattern is missing: users are not adding value for users, and distribution is not compounding through code or media.

**Federal agencies’ reported generative-AI use cases increased ninefold from 2023 to 2024, while Stratum Industries can address permits, patents, drug approvals, grants, licenses, and benefits. That gives Stratum Industries real pivot-room across federal, state, and local workflows. The market score would move into the highest tier if one reusable adjudication engine could cross categories without bespoke procurement and implementation. If every rubric instead requires new security reviews, integrations, and agency-specific services, the apparent horizontal market collapses into a collection of labor-intensive verticals.

**Replit** was built from Amjad Masad’s decade-long obsession with code environments; the artifact preceded the financing story. Stratum Industries has no equivalent evidence of non-trainable government knowledge: Vihaan’s Cadastral experience and Certuary project show technical ability, while Raj’s Deel record shows exceptional commercial energy, but neither founder has disclosed adjudication, procurement, administrative-law, or public-sector security experience. Stratum Industries may learn the domain. Specific knowledge that must be learned after incorporation is not yet specific knowledge.

**Salesforce’s authorized Government Cloud makes my 36-month forecast concrete: by July 2029, Stratum Industries will have pivoted from direct government adjudication to application-review infrastructure for government contractors or regulated enterprises, with less than $3 million ARR. Stratum Industries can move quickly because Raj can sell and Vihaan can build, but direct agencies impose slow, permissioned distribution while adjacent contractors can purchase faster. High founder energy preserves the company; low permissionless leverage changes the customer.

**GovWell’s 130 government customers across 34 states are the strongest objection to that forecast. The bull case is that government modernization has crossed the adoption threshold, Stratum Industries spans more adjudication categories than PermitFlow, and Raj’s 220% quota performance converts a hard sales motion into an advantage. Anchorage Digital proves permission can become a moat. The objection loses today because ClearOS already describes nearly the same workflow, Salesforce owns the authorization layer, and Stratum Industries discloses no pilot, customer, certification, or government-native founder history. A named agency deployment processing at least 10,000 consequential cases with independently measured accuracy and a reusable procurement package would flip my verdict.
