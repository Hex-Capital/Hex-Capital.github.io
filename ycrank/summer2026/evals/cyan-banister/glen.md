# Glen -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Nikos Dritsakos is a solo founder building an invisible organizational memory layer, making Glen a high-authority, low-tactility bet: one person can steer Glen decisively, but no normal human can touch or observe the product outside an MCP call. That pairing describes founder-controlled agent infrastructure, not a lived artifact. Glen has Biz and some Tiz—the $500,000 SalesBop exit and Composio agent-tooling experience matter—but the dossier cannot show me the unstructured human signal that supplies Rizz.

**SpaceX** is the high rung for unambiguous authority: one founder unmistakably owns the mission and the final decision. Glen shares that structural clarity because Nikos Dritsakos is the sole founder and Glen has a team size of one; no 50/50 stalemate is hiding behind a friendly co-CEO photograph. Glen diverges from the high rung because no public evidence establishes founder vesting, succession, or an exit-conflict protocol. Fourteen points feels right: the tiebreaker is obvious, while the governance underneath him remains unwritten—or merely undisclosed.

The consensus YC AI-agent darling is the low rung because software abstraction can look impressive while remaining impossible to experience without founder narration. Glen exhibits that failure mechanism: agents call Glen through MCP, while customers enter through a work-email waitlist and founder-led demo. Glen’s Slack integration offers something installable, but it is a doorway into invisible retrieval behavior, not the product-level physical proof I found in a ride, camera, or location game. Retrieval quality has no public benchmark, so I cannot independently feel whether Glen remembers the right thing.

The **$500,000 SalesBop exit** gives Glen a more authentic founder mechanism than a freshly minted enterprise-search thesis. Nikos previously built AI sales coaching, managed AI products at FliteHouse, and worked on agent tooling at Composio; over 18 months, those repetitions could compound into unusually good instincts about what agents forget and what organizations need preserved. Glen still lacks the autobiographical moment—the specific failure Nikos suffered that made Glen inevitable—so the founder sits above the absent-evidence default but below the founders who were unmistakably *of* the problem.

**Glean’s $300 million ARR** makes Glen’s category legible, funded, and dangerously fashionable. Glen becomes magically weird only if Nikos can prove that read/write organizational memory is a different primitive from enterprise search—and that sophisticated investors dismiss it because autonomous retention feels unsafe, not because Glen lacks differentiation. A tier-flipping counterfactual would require named customers repeatedly choosing Glen over Glean, Dust, or internal RAG, alongside evidence that the round is underpriced after informed passes. Today Glen is an AI-memory product in the center of consensus, not three uninvestable ideas stapled together.

**Flock Safety** compounded through physical deployment, regulatory friction, and an artifact visible in the wild. Glen faces sensitive-data aggregation, OAuth 2.1, row-level security, and permission questions, but Glen is not yet an unsexy regulated swing; it is software serving organizations with an optional private mode. The current rule that learned information is shared organization-wide unless a chat is private could become a serious enterprise constraint, yet the constraint is presently a liability rather than a moat earned through deployment.

**MCP’s November 2024 release** gives Glen a distribution surface across agent clients, while Glen’s solo-founder authority allows rapid narrowing when horizontal organizational memory collides with Glean, Dust, and Unblocked. My 36-month forecast is that Glen pivots toward developer-agent memory, remains below $5 million ARR, and is acquired by Composio for $15–40 million. Glen can move quickly enough to find a useful wedge, but low physical verifiability and weak structural barriers make independent category ownership less likely than a tooling acquisition.

**Production deployment with teams admitted from a waitlist** supports the strongest opposing case: a founder-tooling flywheel. Glen has a repeat founder with a $500,000 exit, direct Composio experience, one clear CEO, Slack distribution, and a protocol riding broad industry adoption; the **DeepMind** exception says a cross-domain builder can outrun an imperfect origin story. Glen could quietly become the shared memory substrate before incumbents understand that search and learning are different. The evidence that wins today is harsher: Glen has no disclosed revenue, retention, accuracy benchmark, or source-permission fidelity, while three adjacent vendors already have $30 million to $623 million behind them. I would flip to Invest if Glen reaches $1 million ARR from at least 20 paying organizations within 12 months.
