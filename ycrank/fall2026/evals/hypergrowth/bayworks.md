# Bayworks -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with U.S. dealership service departments, Bayworks has a recurring job that could travel. Its documented workflow turns technician dictation and repair evidence into a manufacturer-formatted warranty narrative, then saves it to the repair order. Dictation, evidence capture, and technician review are reusable. How much work Bayworks would need to adapt warranty rules, language handling, and dealership-system integrations in another market remains uncertain. The dealership demo form and dealer-issued accounts point to a sales-led motion. The app and PBS extension give dealers reusable setup components, while independent purchase and activation remain unproven. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Bayworks reports nearly seven figures in cARR within four months and more than 80 dealer locations in 110 days. That is a fast reported rollout for a two-person team, and the Harvey Auto Group testimonial corroborates use at one group. The memo leaves live recurring revenue, paying-customer growth, and the paid share of deployments undisclosed, so the rollout figures cannot establish the realized commercial slope. Daily repair-order use and prior narratives give dealers a reason to keep using the product. More locations or repair volume could lift spend if contracts charge for either. Pricing, renewals, and paid expansion within existing accounts remain unverified. (Bayworks, The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Under a per-location pricing scenario, incremental contribution would be the recurring fee less the attributable sales, activation, support, audio and photo processing, and integration maintenance costs. Reusing an existing connector across locations could reduce activation effort. The memo gives no fee or measured delivery inputs, so the direction and size of incremental contribution remain uncertain. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

The reported reach across six dealership groups could reflect a few centrally won contracts whose locations still need hands-on activation. Manufacturer-specific warranty formats and the PBS-specific extension also raise the possibility that a new market requires substantial integration and workflow work. The memo establishes those dependencies but leaves their implementation burden open. The structural downside gate stays open because the evidence does not establish that a market rebuild or uneconomic per-dealer work is unavoidable. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Bayworks bring a dealership group outside the U.S. onto paid production across multiple locations using its existing workflow and a repeatable setup? A rollout record showing live billing, the changes required for local warranty formats and dealer systems, and the sales, activation, and support effort per location would answer it. Bounded changes and positive incremental contribution would support rescoring and a move to Reach out if the score and confidence thresholds are met. Evidence that substantial market-specific rebuilding is unavoidable without a credible near-term transfer path would move the action to Pass.
