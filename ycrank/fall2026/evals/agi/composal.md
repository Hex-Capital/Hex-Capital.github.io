# Composal -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Verify has the strongest commercial case. Engineering teams still manually check user journeys before release because CI and code review can miss customer-facing failures. Verify’s documented workflow runs browser agents in parallel, saves journeys as reusable scenarios, and returns recordings and reproduction steps. This supports a case for reducing manual QA. The memo leaves open whether labor costs previously kept a defined group of teams from testing at a particular cadence. Verify and the Sparks beta automate engineering work. The memo identifies no scarce input that either supplies to an AI workload, so the building-blocks path also scores zero. Both zero scores trigger the Pass gate. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Verify’s retained scenarios and failure history could create switching costs. Composal’s report that nine teams had already built internal QA agents is concrete evidence of substitution, and the memo gives no retention or pricing evidence that Verify’s workflow protects its economics. Reported setup calls and an early-access Recorder rollout support trial interest. Composal also reports finding more than 1,500 bugs in customer apps, with paid conversion and repeat use unverified. The Verify launch and the founders’ relevant delivery experience support execution credit. Authentication or network controls stalled five of ten reported setup calls, leaving dependable onboarding open. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

At the listed $500 monthly price, a team using all 3,000 included minutes would generate about $0.17 in subscription revenue per browser minute. That scenario leaves the cost per successful check unknown, including browser and model usage, reruns, support, and human review. The documented workflow and launch support medium confidence in the assessment. Commercial outcomes and unit costs remain weakly evidenced. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Verify could make end-to-end checks practical on every agent-authored pull request for teams that currently check user journeys only before release. The memo reports a rise in agent-authored pull requests and describes a concrete labor lever in parallel browser runs and reusable scenarios. Composal would still have work to do under model parity, including maintaining scenarios, handling test-environment access, and collecting evidence engineers can act on. The memo does not establish that manual cost previously ruled out that cadence for a defined buyer or that Verify made it viable. The Pass gate therefore applies. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Verify turn a team’s release-only manual QA into sustained, paid pull-request verification at a lower fully loaded cost per trustworthy check than the team could achieve in-house with comparable models? A paid pilot documenting that labor cost had previously ruled out that cadence, then showing repeated use at comparable coverage and tracking setup, reruns, and review costs, would change the action to Reach out.
