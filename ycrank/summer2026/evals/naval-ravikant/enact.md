# Enact -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The missing fact is not revenue. It is what these founders learned by doing. Enact names no founders, prior robotics work, shipped systems, research artifacts, or customer deployments. That is uncertainty rather than failure, but specific knowledge cannot be inferred from a polished workflow. There is no disclosed integrity or behavioral red flag; there is also no action record that earns founder conviction.

The product closes a real loop: find policy failures, generate targeted demonstrations and recoveries, retrain, and test again. The strongest case is that repeated deployments could turn failure states and recovery outcomes into proprietary training data. This is also the decisive weakness. Enact discloses no dataset scale, exclusivity, benchmark, customer, or cross-embodiment result. If a competitor copied the workflow today, the dossier identifies nothing it could not copy.

Physical AI is the right substrate, but participation in a hard market is not itself a hard advantage. Instance has tested success detection on more than 10,000 held-out episodes across seven robot platforms; Axis already combines distributed collection with human-gated post-training; NVIDIA and Runway can absorb evaluation capabilities into broader platforms. Enact’s integrated loop may ultimately outperform these components, but integration without measured improvement is an architecture diagram, not a moat.

The market figures—$3.2 billion for physical-AI training platforms and $1.008 billion for robotic datasets in 2025—show expanding spend. They do not establish Enact’s room to maneuver. The initial buyer appears to be a robotics-model developer requesting task-specific data, but the dossier does not name adjacent buyers, a transferable capability, or a path beyond bespoke deployments. Each engagement currently appears to require company-created data and direct sales; no user contributes value or distribution for another user.

The pre-seed exception would be founders with unusual demonstrated capability and a working prototype whose efficiency compensates for absent customers or network effects. Neither is documented here. My 36-month base case is that Enact pivots toward bespoke robotics-data services and remains below $1 million ARR rather than becoming the post-training platform. One result flips me: a named customer publishing that Enact improved policy success across at least three robot platforms, while the resulting recovery dataset remains exclusive and measurably improves subsequent deployments. Until then, I pass. The downside is bounded, but the nonlinear asset has not yet appeared.
