# OpenCrawl -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a private strategic acquisition by a coding-agent platform. OpenCrawl combines searchable session histories across tools with a shared coordination board. A platform could pay to own the integrations and bring established teams into its product if those teams use the shared history often enough to reduce duplicated work and make the workflow hard to replace. That ownership rationale is an inference. The November launch and 57 GitHub stars show early interest, and the CTO reported onboarding a user and seeing agents exchange messages. The memo leaves paid production usage, retention, and buyer demand unverified. The reported $125,000 YC seed entry predates the launch, and available runway for a production test is unknown. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

The memo supplies no applicable investment terms or usable acquisition-price evidence. The illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted by 40% relative to 3%, with no follow-on checks. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the investor and about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% ownership and $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale scenarios assume all consideration is cash paid at close, with zero net debt, transaction costs, or other adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity proceeds. They assume a simple pro rata waterfall with no senior preferences. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each $0. Actual holder rights and the transferability of IP and customer agreements remain unverified.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Gross cash to the holder at 3%, before taxes | Gross multiple on $500,000 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale at $40 million enterprise value | Year 4 | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | Year 7 | $3.0 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private ownership with growth plateauing, buyers staying away, public scale out of reach, and cash reinvested | Through year 8 | $0, with shares retained | 0× |

The strong scenario clears the hurdle at 3% but yields only $2 million, or 4×, at 2%. The sale prices are sensitivity assumptions. No independent route has memo evidence of meaningful cash reaching this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Agent Relay already offers cross-tool messaging and searchable sessions. Claude Code covers shared tasks and messaging for teams using its own tools, and OpenCrawl offers self-hosting. A buyer could capture much of the benefit through an existing product, an integration, or self-hosting. OpenCrawl’s default team visibility and listener-dependent push mentions could also limit adoption in mixed-tool teams. These are concrete reasons a buyer might pay little for ownership. The memo leaves open whether those substitutes can match a production deployment across multiple clients, so the evidence supports Watch without triggering a structural Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid production deployment show that a coding-agent platform gains enough from owning OpenCrawl to justify the proceeds hurdle? A platform’s quantified build-versus-buy assessment, grounded in measured reductions in duplicated work across multiple clients, would move the action to Reach out if it supported a price yielding roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership.
