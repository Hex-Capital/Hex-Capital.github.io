# Vestris -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The contested truth is that most title processing is not legal judgment but repetitive information work that an agent can compress from 8–10 hours to one while leaving only true exceptions for human approval. The obvious falsifier is not a clever demo; it is whether that time reduction survives across jurisdictions without increasing title defects, fraud exposure, or reviewer labor. Vestris has run beside one title shop settling millions of dollars monthly and reports roughly seven hours saved per file, so this is more than an AI slogan. But the result remains company-reported, covers only Washington, D.C., and Montgomery County, and does not identify the prevailing industry belief it disproves. The secret is plausible and testable, not yet independently proved.

The founders are the strongest evidence. Aahil Valliani and Joshua Tang were roommates, best friends, and coworkers at the same title desk before forming Vestris. Both processed the workflow they are automating; Valliani had already founded a software product and Tang had built live telemetry systems. This resembles a company assembled around an operating thesis rather than two people shopping for an AI market. It avoids the founding red flags: no convenience matching, outside core engineer, part-time co-founder, or investor-heavy capitalization is disclosed. But full-time status, founder ownership, salaries, and whether all essential engineering remains in-house are still unverified, so alignment is demonstrated more clearly than economic exposure.

The beachhead is appropriately narrow: small and midsize title agencies in two jurisdictions, performing pre-closing work across inboxes, documents, and county portals. Narrowness, however, is not monopoly. Vestris says its Schedule B workflow was trained on decades of approved and rejected titles, but the dossier cannot establish the dataset’s size, ownership, or exclusivity. Qualia owns ResWare, Flueid already provides underwriter-approved title decisioning, and Stavvy controls adjacent closing infrastructure. Each possesses distribution or integration advantages that could absorb comparable automation. Human-reviewed closing outcomes might eventually compound into proprietary jurisdictional data, but a possible flywheel is not an existing one.

The strongest bull case is that the apparent weakness is actually the secret: incumbents built systems of record, while these founders discovered that autonomous execution across fragmented county portals is a different product. The reported 8–10-to-one-hour improvement would be a genuine technical discontinuity if independent customers reproduce it. That could justify overriding today’s weak monopoly evidence, as a verified zero-to-one technical event sometimes does. The known exception does not yet apply, however, because one concurrent pilot and undisclosed training data do not establish either general accuracy or an advantage incumbents cannot copy. The same computer-use APIs enabling Vestris also reduce the cost of imitation.

Distribution and power-law economics remain mostly indefinite. Founder-led sales to small title agencies is a sensible initial route, and the live pilot proves access to at least one buyer. Yet there is no disclosed price, paid contract, conversion rate, sales cycle, gross margin, or expansion behavior. The $18.5 billion title-premium figure measures the surrounding industry rather than Vestris’s software revenue opportunity. Jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction implementation, security obligations in a market with $275 million of annual fraud losses, and human approval could produce a valuable services-heavy business without producing a last mover.

My 36-month base case is $2–6 million ARR from boutique title agencies, with Vestris still proving whether jurisdictional workflow data becomes a monopoly mechanism or merely an integration burden. I would investigate, not invest today. The verifiable flip condition is ten paying agencies across at least five counties showing sustained sub-two-hour processing, independently audited title accuracy, and net revenue retention above 120%. That would simultaneously prove the secret travels, the data compounds, and distribution expands economically.
