# Vyra -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a video-editing platform is Vyra’s strongest path. Vyra combines reference-guided short-form editing with an editable timeline, searchable footage, and reusable style references. A hands-on comparison and 14 iPhone ratings provide evidence of a shipped product with some usage. The company’s 104,982-creator counter is undefined, and the founders’ reported 40M+ views on edited content leave paid adoption and retention unknown. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

A buyer could pay to acquire the workflow and a base of repeat paying creators if ownership lets it capture retention or subscription gains that building or licensing would struggle to match. The seed milestone is sustained paid retention among creators who repeatedly use saved references, measured against a comparable native editing workflow. The shipped product makes that test feasible. The disclosed Launchpad SAFE program terms leave burn, runway, and the capital needed to reach the milestone unknown. IP assignment and the transferability of customer permissions remain unverified. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

The evaluated check has no disclosed terms, so the proceeds bridge assumes $500,000 invested at a $10M post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5M in gross cash, or 5×. Under a simple pro rata waterfall, that requires $83.3M in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125M at 2%, or $62.5M at 4%.

The sale scenarios assume zero applicable net debt, transaction costs, and preference leakage, so enterprise value equals distributable equity cash. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. The memo supplies no buyer economics or transaction prices to support these illustrative values. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Descript already offers MCP editing, Adobe previewed agent control inside existing Premiere projects, and OpusClip offers reusable editing recipes. A buyer could reproduce enough of Vyra’s workflow to avoid paying an acquisition premium. That risk warrants waiting. The memo leaves open whether native tools match Vyra’s reference-guided workflow closely enough to cap the price below the hurdle, so the structural Pass override does not apply. No independent fallback has evidence of cash reaching this holder. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an editing platform use a measured, sustained paid-retention lift from Vyra’s saved-reference workflow to justify buying it at a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? An indicative buyer-side analysis grounded in that cohort result, with quantified build and licensing alternatives and economics consistent with roughly $83.3M in distributable equity cash at 3% ownership, would move the action to **Reach out**.
