# Axonnic -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Muscle-signal decoding could give robotics developers demonstrations that cameras miss; Axonnic becomes a large company only if those additional signals materially reduce the cost of teaching robots reliable manipulation. That is the controlling variable. Recording a human hand more accurately has value, but the buyer ultimately needs successful autonomous tasks. The dossier explains why grip effort might improve training, without establishing how much improvement survives retargeting onto a different mechanical hand.

The technical evidence makes this worth investigating. Axonnic reports **4.58° average joint-angle error, 50 predictions per second, and 78 minutes of training data**, validated against held-out Apple Vision Pro recordings. Those figures support a functioning sensing and decoding implementation. The 2025 research on generalized sEMG interfaces also supplies a credible technical opening. Neither establishes lower robot-training costs, fewer failed grasps, or faster deployment. This distinction matters economically: a sleeve can make data collection easier while calibration, policy training, and customer-specific engineering consume the savings.

The strongest bull case is that Axonnic already reaches beyond sensing. Its synchronized EMG, video, and hand-pose pipeline, together with the demonstrated **Proception hand integration**, gives it a starting point for owning the path from demonstration to robot action. Better underlying models could increase the usefulness of that infrastructure. The company-reported Italian manufacturing LOI, founder-described pilot with **Perloro Packing Srl**, and construction recordings suggest access to real tasks rather than exclusively laboratory demonstrations. These are informative pre-seed signals. They establish customer interest and access; they do not yet establish repeat purchasing or autonomous production performance. I would not assume the LOI and named pilot represent separate customers.

Value capture is the harder issue. **Hemlock already offers overlapping EMG-based robot-training data**, so muscle sensing alone does not define an exclusive layer. Axonnic could build reusable deployment software and sell additional capabilities through its first manufacturer relationships, which fits my preference for infrastructure around a technical discontinuity. It could also become a collection and integration service whose costs recur with every customer. The dossier does not resolve that split. Cross-customer data rights are negotiated individually, and there is no evidence establishing the time or money a competitor would need to reproduce the dataset. I therefore give credit for the demonstrated integration, while leaving the proposed data advantage uncredited.

Williams’s EMG research and Voxblock hardware experience, alongside Patankar’s robotics software work, make the implementation credible. Those backgrounds do not document founder sacrifice, a prior founder exit, or specific lessons from running a startup, so that dimension stays at its absent-evidence default. I also do not see grounds for a frontier-research exception: the dossier establishes relevant technical capability, but not field-defining research or a demonstrated economic discontinuity sufficient to override weak commercial proof. Axonnic has an interesting enabling technology; I would pass on writing a personal check today because the market gates remain unproven. **My flip condition is a customer-verified, matched sewing deployment showing that adding Axonnic’s EMG data cuts total training and calibration cost per successful autonomous task by at least 50% versus the same pipeline without EMG.** That would connect the technical result to the variable that determines whether this market works.
