# Viraj Aero -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Viraj Aero has raised €4.5 million, but I cannot price my check from the amount somebody else invested. The August 2026 financing identifies IRDI and three other investors; it supplies neither post-money valuation nor competing offers. That establishes financing, not an auction and not scarce capital. My first gate remains unresolved. A technically interesting turbine can still be a lousy investment at the wrong price.

The engineering team gives me a reason to keep reading. Bouilloux developed six power projects totaling more than 120 MW; Risson brings propulsion experience from Ascendance and Beyond Aero. Company-reported full-scale aircraft-prototype integration adds a concrete execution receipt beyond their credentials. This fits the pattern I like: people connecting aerospace engineering with the practical business of developing power infrastructure. But their demonstrated experience does not yet establish access to data-center purchasing decisions. The reported Safran work lacks enough scope to close that gap.

What exact card does this round flip? A 2027 demonstrator flight is a dated milestone, and the condenser gives Viraj Aero a specific engineering proposition to test. Yet the dossier does not connect the proceeds to a bounded experiment, its budget, and a measurable acceptance threshold. GENA’s advertised 45% electrical efficiency and 2 ppm NOx are development specifications. The unresolved 30% versus 40% additional-power claims make a common test baseline especially important. A successful aircraft demonstration would also leave continuous hot-site water recovery for data centers to be established. Two applications can share technology while requiring separate commercial proofs.

The strongest objection to passing is substantial: €13 million of aviation LOIs, reported prototype integration, and an experienced power developer could support a narrower retrofit business before Viraj Aero manufactures complete generating systems. That would fit my tolerance for difficult physical infrastructure and translation between established industries. But the LOIs establish aviation purchase interest; they do not establish paid conversion, proprietary customer access, or demand for GENA. The disclosed evidence also falls short of the measured conversion and operating economics that have justified exceptions to my usual gates. LinkedIn followers and a sales inbox add nothing to that argument.

Delete the next financing from the plan. The dossier supplies no margins, burn, runway, or retrofit cash economics with which to determine what survives. I am not assuming the business is uneconomic, and a pre-seed hardware company need not already have revenue. I simply cannot underwrite a self-funding path from a 2029 commercialization target. Grid bottlenecks create urgency, but urgency does not tell me who pays Viraj Aero, when, or at what contribution margin. Nor do competitors’ enormous funding totals establish this company’s capital requirements.

I pass on the evidence available. The team is the affirmative signal; the deal and capital experiment remain undocumented. My single verifiable flip condition is a signed, customer-funded retrofit agreement whose acceptance test measures sustained water recovery and net power improvement, with payments covering the fully costed deployment. That would demonstrate customer access, finance a finite technical test, and give me a functioning business to price.
