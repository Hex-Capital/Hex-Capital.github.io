# Stoa -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Blackwell upgrades are creating secondary H100 supply while institutional buyers need faster price discovery; Stoa becomes a large company only if a meaningful share of RFQ notional converts into repeat, settled transactions without counterparties leaving the platform. That conversion rate is the controlling variable. The $300 million routed during Stoa’s first month is unusually strong evidence of demand for quotations, but it does not establish the threshold that matters: executed volume, revenue, or recurring liquidity.

The market change is real. AI data-center chip shipments are projected to grow from $123 billion in 2024 to $286 billion in 2030, data-center capacity from roughly 103 GW to 200 GW, and Blackwell deployments should release H100 inventory into secondary channels. Yet Stoa has not quantified how price discovery changes buyer costs, dealer margins, utilization, or transaction frequency. Compute Exchange already spans physical hardware, reserved capacity, and forwards across more than 100 providers, while Procurri couples resale with inspection and logistics. This is a crystallizing market, not an empty category.

The product goes beyond a listings page. Blind firm quotes, a 48-hour RFQ cycle, awards, shipping, inspection, and settlement give Stoa workflow state from which useful price histories could emerge. Contractual ownership of bid, offer, volume, and transaction data is helpful. But the terms disclaim inspection, title, authenticity, delivery, and payment responsibility. The 12-month non-circumvention clause is similarly revealing: relationships can migrate off-platform before the exchange becomes indispensable. A claimed data moat is weak until Stoa can estimate what it would cost a well-funded competitor with existing dealer access to reproduce it.

The bull case is that $300 million of first-month RFQs is the visible edge of latent institutional liquidity, and this team is unusually well composed to convert it. Kaan Yigit built trading and pricing systems at Uniper, Eren Berke Saglam traded derivatives at Citi, and Berat Celik is a repeat founder who left an xAI opportunity. The founders have known one another for more than ten years. If even a modest portion of those RFQs settles, the resulting transaction data could improve benchmarks, attract lenders, and create adjacent distribution into financing and risk products.

I do not underwrite that flywheel from RFQs alone. There is no disclosed executed value, take rate, repeat participation, or available H100 index value. Stoa therefore does not yet qualify for the physical-market exception: software has organized the transaction, but it has not demonstrated a change in hardware economics. Nor is this a frontier-research or scarce-technology exception. My 36-month base case is that Stoa pivots toward GPU pricing data or financing infrastructure rather than reaching $10 million ARR as a pure transaction exchange.

I would monitor rather than invest today. The single condition that flips the decision is third-party-verifiable quarterly settled volume above $50 million. That would show that Stoa is creating liquidity rather than cataloging interest, validate the price-data loop, and make the first product a credible distribution channel for financing and other institutional products.
