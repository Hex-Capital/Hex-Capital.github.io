# Tire Swing -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Tire Swing has built something more informative than a résumé: a certification workflow with source-linked findings and a reported library of thousands of compliance checks. That is evidence of learning through action. Witten’s forensic investigations and Irvine’s Cloud Custodian work make rules, security, and auditable systems credible territory for this team. Affordable-housing judgment is less established. The dossier identifies no operator deployment or customer outcome that demonstrates the founders understand where a technically correct rule fails in practice. I give them credit for building, with limited credit for having mastered the exact problem.

The decisive question is what Tire Swing keeps after a competitor reproduces its workflow. Public regulations are available to everyone. Thousands of checks represent accumulated work, but the count does not establish an exclusive asset. Property configurations and reviewer histories could become valuable; the dossier labels their switching costs as inferred. Meanwhile, Yardi already brings payroll and banking verification into an existing customer relationship. Tire Swing’s document-based approach addresses a different part of certification, but differentiation becomes durable only if deployments produce something competitors cannot readily acquire.

The strongest bull case is that affordable-housing compliance rewards accumulated judgment more than elegant code. Human reviewers encounter exceptions, correct interpretations, and expose interactions between rules. Tire Swing could turn those corrections into a system that becomes more reliable with every property. HUD’s January 1, 2027 HOTMA enforcement date gives covered housing authorities a concrete reason to update workflows. That could accelerate learning. But required human approval establishes oversight, not a learning loop. There is no evidence that one operator’s corrections improve another operator’s results, or that Tire Swing possesses the rights and mechanisms to compound that knowledge.

The buyer and immediate problem are clear: operators processing move-ins and recertifications. Expansion into broader housing operations is conceivable, but the dossier does not establish the shared capability and adjacent demand needed to underwrite that expansion. Subscription order forms establish an intended way to charge; they establish neither a paid transaction nor its economics. The reported $500,000 financing says nothing about customer value capture. Founder demonstrations and AHF Live attendance show direct selling effort, while evidence of customers creating distribution or utility for other customers remains absent. Tire Swing currently fits my pattern of automating individual work better than my stronger patterns of permissionless contribution or network formation.

I would pass with my own money. The earlier healthcare product does not establish indiscriminate pivoting, and the dossier supplies no evidence of dishonesty, coercive fundraising, or dependence on investor reassurance. My exceptions for exceptional founder behavior, a compelling capital-efficient prototype, or an emerging network do not override the missing durable asset here: the necessary evidence is not supplied. One result would change my decision: an independently verified rollout across multiple operators showing that reviewer corrections Tire Swing has rights to reuse reduce certification errors at subsequent properties without proportional growth in review labor. That would demonstrate that each deployment creates an asset for the next one.
