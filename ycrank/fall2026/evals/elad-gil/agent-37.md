# Agent 37 -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Agent 37’s market opens as developers move from demonstrating agents to operating a persistent environment for every customer. Those developers now buy provisioning, credentials, storage, and scheduling together. The controlling variable is gross profit per retained customer: can Agent 37 remove enough operational work to retain customers while earning attractive margins on very cheap compute? Boon’s reported six months building on Render, followed by deployment in three days with Agent37, identifies a concrete bottleneck. That supports the market thesis. It does not yet establish that Agent37 captures the economic value it creates.

The cost reduction is substantial. Boon reports monthly hosting falling from $85 to $4.94 per agent, approximately 94%. Even discounted as a company-published case study, that is a meaningful adoption mechanism. But customer savings and supplier economics are different things. Published prices of $0.80 per vCPU and $0.70 per GB of RAM per month establish affordability; they reveal little about gross margins after infrastructure and support. Memory-pressure restarts matter here because a low resource price can become expensive if customers need intervention or additional capacity to operate reliably.

There is more demand evidence than I would normally expect at this stage. ETEC+ migrated more than 30 existing clients onto its branded product, and Vishnu reported over 100 companies running agents in October 2026. The named deployment is more informative than Product Hunt rankings or GitHub stars. February’s 30-plus paying personal-hosting customers also show an ability to find buyers, although that demand cannot simply be transferred to Cloud. I would exclude the ambiguous company-wide “100k ARR” claim from underwriting. The evidence supports genuine early pull; its revenue composition and persistence remain unclear.

The strongest bull case is that customers are buying an operating layer, making raw sandbox comparisons increasingly irrelevant. Persistent files, preinstalled harnesses, application integrations, and a white-label starter kit could make the first deployment a distribution channel for subsequent customer workloads. Better models would increase the usefulness of those environments. This fits my pattern of investing across an emerging technology stack. However, E2B already preserves filesystem and memory state, Daytona has raised $91 million, and Agent37 itself uses Daytona for template builds and image imports. Composio supplies application integrations. Agent37 has assembled useful functionality, but the dossier does not establish how much recurring workflow control remains uniquely its own.

Vishnu’s earlier companies, Real Voxel and Dota2Connect, establish prior founding activity, but the dossier documents neither specific lessons nor costly commitment that would justify a founder premium. Employer credentials cannot fill that gap. Nor does the research-team exception apply: this is an execution and distribution contest in a category with established suppliers. The relevant red flag is claiming software value capture while retaining commodity infrastructure economics; Agent37 has not demonstrated that failure, but its low prices make the question consequential. I would monitor rather than write a personal check today. One result would flip me: a verified cohort of at least 20 paying Cloud customers producing more than 120% six-month gross-profit retention, with infrastructure, support, and outage credits included in the calculation. That would show the deployment wedge compounds economically.
