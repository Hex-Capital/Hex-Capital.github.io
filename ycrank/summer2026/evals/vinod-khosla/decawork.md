# Decawork -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Decawork moved from an autonomous IT administrator in June 2026 to an agent control plane by July 2026. That makes Decawork a high-learning-rate, low-capital-intensity enterprise software bet. The founders can change direction faster than incumbents, but the maximum success-state still looks like better governance of AI agents, not the destruction of a large economic regime. Identity administration changes hands; civilization does not. Consequence comes before probability. Decawork fails that first test.

**Slash** is the high rung on learning rate because its documented pivot revealed founders who could absorb reality and rebuild around it. Decawork shares that mechanism: Sarthak Aggarwal and Aman Raj abandoned the human-employee incident framing despite having the prior product live across multiple enterprises. Decawork diverges because Slash’s pivot generated a validated commercial engine, while all disclosed Decawork usage belongs to the abandoned product. A pivot demonstrates learning only when the new thesis earns evidence. Otherwise it may be motion confused with progress.

**HubSpot** represents the low-capital failure mechanism: a workflow feature that an incumbent can finance internally makes specialized venture capital strategically irrelevant. Decawork’s gateway, scoped credentials, approvals, monitoring, and audit logs resemble features Okta can distribute through 8,200 integrations. Decawork discloses no scientific breakthrough, proprietary data asset, or roadmap requiring more than $50M before commercialization. I am over-resourced for Decawork. “Make a great company and the money takes care of itself” does not mean put expensive capital into inexpensive problems.

**88% of surveyed organizations reported an agent-security incident, while only 22% treated agents as identities.** Decawork could compound over 18 months if every deployed agent creates another credential, policy, approval path, and audit record that IT must govern. Decawork would then become the tollbooth for machine labor. But the economic mechanism that breaks is merely fragmented identity administration; the $15T labor system is displaced by the agents, not by Decawork. Governance captures a tax on the revolution. It does not cause the revolution.

**Okta’s 8,200-integration catalog** pins Decawork near the low end of technical risk because Okta already announced discovery, registration, lifecycle management, and a kill switch. Decawork would flip a tier only if its gateway solved a falsifiable problem incumbents could not copy—for example, autonomous recovery from cross-system agent failures with provably safer execution than static IAM policies. No such breakthrough appears in the dossier. Decawork currently requires product execution and enterprise sales, not a **Commonwealth Fusion**-style miracle that repels consensus capital.

**Aman Raj’s Barclays compliance tooling reached 240+ teams**, while Sarthak Aggarwal previously built enterprise AI at NVIDIA and Ema. Decawork therefore resembles the **Glean** exception on founder background: expert continuity can be acceptable when experience exposes a large hidden failure in the incumbent system. Glean used search expertise to attack Fortune 500 information asymmetry. Decawork uses identity and compliance expertise to enter a market already occupied by Astrix, Oasis, Token Security, and Okta. Decawork has not documented a controversial technical belief those experts reject; the founders are moving with consensus, only faster.

**No public revenue or current-product adoption is disclosed.** By July 2029, I predict Decawork will have pivoted back toward autonomous IT remediation and reached $3M-$8M ARR, rather than remaining a standalone agent-identity control plane or becoming the category leader. Decawork’s demonstrated learning rate favors another thesis update, while its low required-capital profile favors a conventional enterprise-software outcome. The control plane becomes a feature inside the remediation product because selling governance head-on against $195M-funded Oasis and public-company Okta is the inferior distribution strategy.

**The prior autonomous-IT product was live across multiple enterprises**, which is the strongest bull case: Decawork may already possess the workflow access, founder learning rate, and enterprise credibility needed to unify deployment, recovery, and security. The **Slash** exception says a documented pivot can override modest consequence, while the **DoorDash** exception says capital-light businesses can still produce enormous outcomes. That case stings. It loses today because Decawork reports no adoption for the new product, no moat, and no mechanism by which Okta’s distribution becomes irrelevant. Three named Fortune 500 customers each routing more than 1,000 production agents through Decawork, with at least $2M ARR and independently verified autonomous failure recovery, would flip my verdict.
