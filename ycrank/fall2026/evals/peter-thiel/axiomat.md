# Axiomat -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Axiomat’s potentially important truth is that business-data agents can become reliable through mathematical verification rather than progressively better guessing. What would prove that wrong? An answer that passes verification while misinterpreting the customer’s definition of revenue. The company says it converts documentation and schemas into a logical business model, then verifies queries against that model. This is a concrete, testable proposition. But proving that a query follows a specification does not establish that the specification captures the business. That translation is where Axiomat’s “never hallucinates” claim must survive.

The proposed mechanism earns some secret credit; the absolute promise earns none by itself. The dossier identifies neither a named opponent rejecting Axiomat’s particular proposition nor an independent experiment establishing it. AWS’s August 2026 policy-refinement work also shows that formal verification for AI is already an accepted commercial direction. The secret therefore cannot simply be that mathematical checks improve generated answers. It must concern what Axiomat can verify that existing approaches cannot—and under which assumptions. That remains unproved rather than refuted.

The strongest bull case is Ethan Kharitonov’s work on formal methods and AI verification at AWS. He has worked inside the relevant technical problem. Jack Naimer’s semantic-safety research and Elad Perlman’s utility-control engineering add credible experience with systems where incorrect outputs matter. Perhaps this team can make business definitions executable and become the control point through which enterprise agents must pass. That would fit my unstated preference for owning an essential layer of the AI production stack. But technical backgrounds do not establish proprietary technology, and the three founders’ University of Toronto connection does not establish years of working together. Full-time status, ownership, and prior joint execution remain undisclosed; I would neither invent alignment nor infer a founding-team red flag.

Who would Axiomat monopolize first? “Employees and customers asking questions of data” is a product audience, not a bounded market. TextQL’s ontology, PromptQL’s shared context, and Defog’s SQL generation are adjacent approaches rather than demonstrated equivalents. Their existence does not prove Axiomat is interchangeable. Equally, the dossier supplies no exclusive asset, measured technical advantage, or customer behavior establishing a monopoly mechanism. Accumulating customer business rules could eventually create switching costs, but that is an inference. Axiomat must show that it owns something valuable beyond the work of configuring each installation.

The commercial evidence does not yet connect that possibility to a power-law outcome. Demo bookings and email/LinkedIn outreach identify contact methods, but no economic buyer, contract value, sales cycle, or implementation burden is documented. Without pricing or margins, I cannot distinguish scalable verification software from expensive semantic-modeling services. YC participation is not customer validation, and zero advertised jobs carries no adverse significance for a three-person pre-seed team. My relevant exceptions require an independently established technical breakthrough or persistent customer behavior that resolves a particular risk. Neither appears here. There is also no documented technical retraction, part-time founder, or damaging ownership structure that would justify treating uncertainty as failure.

I pass on investing my own money today. The decisive gap is the distance between verifying a formal model and owning a business built on trustworthy answers. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: an independently audited, paid production deployment showing that Axiomat catches deliberately introduced business-definition errors against customer-approved ground truth, with its coverage and abstentions disclosed. That would test the distinctive claim at its weakest point. Until then, I am being offered a plausible verification architecture whose commercial importance exceeds the evidence for its correctness and ownership.
