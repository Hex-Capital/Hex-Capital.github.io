# Armorer Labs -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Armorer Labs has credible builders; I cannot yet underwrite the investment. Proposed post-money, competing term sheets, and whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital are all undisclosed. YC backing and Paul Vixie’s involvement establish neither an auction nor a bargain. The dossier also supplies no round-funded experiment with a budget, deadline, and pass/fail result. “Fixes ready for approval in four hours” describes a product promise, not what my money proves. Those two missing gates receive my stated defaults.

The founders are the strongest affirmative evidence. Daniel Begimher’s reported AWS agent reached 100,000-plus customers, and Cristian Leo’s work includes ThreatForest and AI security science. They also report three years building together ([company background](https://armorerlabs.com/about)). That is operating experience across agent development and adversarial security, rather than two impressive résumés adjacent to a fashionable problem. Armorer fits my pattern of backing people who translate between systems: here, engineers trying to ship useful agents and security teams responsible for constraining them. AWS deployment scale belongs in the founder assessment; it is not Armorer’s customer traction.

The strongest argument against passing is that Armorer could own the authorization boundary regardless of which model wins. Its documented one-use authorization tokens and workflow-preserving remediation address an operational problem that better language models do not automatically eliminate ([Guard documentation](https://github.com/ArmorerLabs/Armorer-Guard)). But protection depends on wrapping tool calls and removing direct credential paths. The reported 1.9-millisecond checks and 98% accuracy come from a 50-document comparison; they do not establish reliable enforcement across production workflows. HiddenLayer, Zenity, and Noma already address overlapping controls. Armorer needs to demonstrate that its integration becomes a durable customer dependency before I treat the proprietary implementation as a proprietary economic asset.

The Just Eat Takeaway engineering-director testimonial is a useful counterweight: somebody identifiable endorses issue resolution. It does not establish a paid deployment, repeat usage, or that Armorer’s owned data caused a purchase ([company site](https://armorerlabs.com/)). Neither 45 SDK stars nor 451 LinkedIn followers supplies that missing causal link. The known exceptions to my pricing and AI skepticism rested on documented conversion, retention, enterprise demand, or an acquisition offer; this dossier establishes none of those overrides. Equally, it supplies no evidence of auction tactics or economics dependent on agent errors disappearing. My concern is unproven underwriting, not an affirmative accusation.

Delete the next financing from Armorer’s plan and the cash model is still blank: commercial runtime licensing is inferred, with no disclosed pricing, margin, burn, or route to break-even. I assign the economics default rather than invent losses. **I pass.** My verifiable flip condition is a documented financing package below $10 million post-money that funds one dated production experiment, supported by paid customer contracts and a budget showing that successful delivery reaches cash break-even without another raise. That would connect this team’s demonstrated capability to a scarce check with a defined economic destination.
