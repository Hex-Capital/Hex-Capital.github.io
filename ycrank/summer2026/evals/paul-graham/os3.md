# OS3 -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Rishabh Chanana and Chris Hailey built Observee in six hours, won three hackathon categories, and turned it into a reported $10K MRR business before OS3 existed. That makes OS3 an animal-founded robotics company with an inorganic-looking idea. OS3’s highest signal is resourcefulness; its lowest is whether either founder personally needed a hotel-kitchen robot. Animals can survive a pivot. But the best pivots preserve some stubborn founder pain, and OS3’s dossier does not show that connective tissue.

**Airbnb** is the high rung for animal resourcefulness because the cereal boxes proved the founders could manufacture money when ordinary financing failed. OS3 shares the important mechanism: Chanana and Hailey converted a six-hour hack into three prizes and then $10K MRR. But OS3 diverges from Airbnb at the point where the stunt must become evidence for the current product. Observee was archived in November 2025, while OS3 discloses no hotel, paid contract, robot count, or task-success rate. The founders have left tracks, but the tracks stop before the robot enters the kitchen.

**Loopt** is the low rung for an idea whose weak connection to usable infrastructure had to be overridden by Sam Altman’s force of personality. The analogous failure mechanism at OS3 is idea distance: Chanana has robotics and ML experience, but OS3 provides no evidence that Chanana or Hailey personally performed hotel labor, managed a property, or built OS3 to solve a problem they themselves suffered. OS3 may be an intelligent response to a market opportunity—65% of surveyed hotels report staffing shortages—but that is not the same thing as Drew Houston repeatedly losing his USB stick. No organic-pain evidence means I apply the absent-evidence penalty.

OS3’s schlep compounds if deployments are real. A wheeled, dual-arm robot with nine-hour battery life, 5 kg payload per arm, emergency-stop systems, and hotel-specific integrations creates eighteen months of ugly work: grease, carts, narrow corridors, frightened employees, broken grippers, and managers calling at 2 a.m. Figure and Apptronik can spend their billions on generality; OS3 can learn one unpleasant kitchen task until it becomes boringly reliable. If OS3’s unnamed hospitality deployment is merely a demo, the loop never starts. If it is an operating site generating intervention data every day, schlep blindness becomes OS3’s best asset.

Chanana’s UCSD robotics research and Hailey’s Coinbase production work would flip OS3’s hacker score into the top tier if the dossier showed a non-trivial robot built and deployed by their own hands, with logs, videos, or task-success data. OS3 instead publishes hardware specifications and an architectural story while Chanana’s pinned repository has zero stars and Hailey has no public repositories. Credentials say they can build. Observee says they can ship software. Neither proves OS3 has crossed the much harder boundary from a robot-shaped artifact to a machine that works repeatedly in a hostile physical environment.

**Stripe** had two hackers whose previous commerce work made both the partnership and the problem cumulative. OS3 also has two technical founders, and the six-hour Observee build suggests that Chanana and Hailey can move unusually fast together. But OS3’s founders met at the hackathon where they created Observee, precisely the short-history configuration I distrust. A pair is better than a solo founder, yet OS3 has not shown that disagreement, exhaustion, or a hardware failure at a customer site will leave the pair stronger rather than split it.

Figure’s $1.745B-plus funding and Apptronik’s nearly $1B make the next 36 months fairly easy to call: by July 2029, OS3 will have abandoned a general hospitality humanoid pitch and either pivoted to a narrower wheeled manipulation product for hotel kitchens and back-of-house logistics at $1M–$4M ARR, or be dead. OS3’s resourcefulness makes the narrow deployment business plausible; OS3’s missing organic origin makes sustained devotion to the broad idea unlikely. Hardware punishes tourists more severely than software does.

OS3’s strongest bull case is the **Airbnb** exception: the idea may look implausible while the founders’ earlier stunt reveals the fact that matters. A two-person technical pair, a prior $10K MRR hackathon business, an in-house robot, and even one real hospitality deployment could outweigh the missing founder-pain story. But Airbnb’s cereal sales directly demonstrated the same sales ingenuity needed to create its marketplace; Observee’s software revenue does not demonstrate that OS3 can achieve safe, economical physical labor. I would flip to Invest if OS3 names one paying hotel and shows at least 90 days of repeat operation with task-success, human-intervention, and uptime data. Until then, the animal signal earns attention, not a check.
