# Lyon -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Lyon’s market turns on whether one customer-specific transaction model makes the second prediction problem substantially cheaper to deploy than the first. Banks and insurers already buy prediction systems. The proposed change is replacing months of application-specific feature engineering with reusable customer embeddings and adapters. That could support a large infrastructure company. The dossier establishes a plausible mechanism and an initial application; it does not yet establish the reuse economics that would make this market attractive.

The strongest evidence is customer access followed by a measurable result. Lyon reports training on **28 billion transactions** for a fintech serving tens of millions of active users and achieving **4× greater precision** in identifying premium-card converters. For two founders at pre-seed, that is meaningful evidence of customer engagement. I discount it because the customer and evaluation methodology are undisclosed: precision depends on recall, population selection, and the comparison baseline. Credit deployment underway is encouraging, but it is not a second demonstrated outcome. The insurer engagement adds little without evidence of actual use.

The distinction between better predictions and better deployment economics matters here. A fourfold improvement against rules could justify buying the first application while telling us almost nothing about the cost of building the next one. Oak HC/FT’s February 2026 statement supports research maturity as a plausible why-now. It supplies no quantified evidence that Lyon can reduce implementation time, training expense, or ongoing support costs. Training from scratch for each customer could preserve the expensive customization layer. I therefore give the economic discontinuity the missing-evidence default rather than treating prediction lift as proof of software margins.

The architecture earns some separate credit. Customer-specific models, embeddings, and deployment inside the customer’s VPC create implementation assets that a generic model endpoint does not immediately replace. Better modeling techniques could improve this product. But customer-owned models and customer-held transactions do not establish an exclusive data advantage, and the dossier documents no decision orchestration, feedback loop, or operational actions owned by Lyon. Fundamental has raised **$255 million**, while SAP acquired Prior Labs in July 2026. This is already a competitive market in which useful prediction technology may accrue value to the vendor controlling distribution and the completed workflow.

The case against waiting is substantial: these founders have already persuaded a large fintech to expose a consequential dataset and report a useful result. That may be more informative than another early revenue announcement. Lyon also fits my pattern of investing across layers of a technology transition. But that pattern does not override the red flag of calling accessible customer data a moat without establishing reproduction cost. Noya’s NVIDIA and finance affiliations and Höhn’s mathematics and ML background support technical relevance; they do not document prior startup learning or costly personal commitment. Nor does the dossier establish the exceptional research record that would justify bypassing the commercial economics.

I would keep Lyon under observation and leave my personal check unwritten today. The next dollar should prove reusable deployment, before funding broad expansion. My flip condition is **one independently verifiable customer deployment where the existing transaction model powers a second paid production application within 30 days at a fully loaded gross margin above 70%**. Those are my underwriting thresholds, not reported results. Meeting them would connect the initial customer signal to the mechanism that could make Lyon a scalable company.
