# Blinka -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Blinka’s strongest path. A business banking or payments platform could buy Blinka to acquire customer relationships and payment workflows with U.S.-incorporated businesses operating in Latin America. Ownership could accelerate entry if those customers repeatedly use Blinka for payouts, invoicing, and accounting, and if acquiring those relationships costs less than building the product and winning the customers directly. That ownership advantage is an inference. The memo documents remote USD account opening, local payout routes, and the providers behind those services. It also reports more than 400 onboarded businesses and $18 million in cumulative payment volume. Those reported figures support testing repeat usage and contribution margin after provider costs, with retention, current-product net revenue, and funding for the next stage still unverified. (*The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Facts*)

The evaluated check’s terms are unavailable, so the proceeds bridge uses the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-on checks. A 5× gross return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The memo supplies no usable acquisition pricing or buyer economics. The sale values below are scenario assumptions. (*Market & Traction; Key Facts*)

| Scenario | Assumption | Cash to investor |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40 million enterprise value in year 5 | $1.2 million, or 2.4× |
| Strong sale | $100 million enterprise value in year 7 | $3 million, or 6×. At 2% ownership it yields $2 million, or 4×. |
| Prolonged private | Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested through year 8 | $0 realized, or 0×. The investor retains the full stake. |

Both sale scenarios assume zero net debt, transaction fees, and senior waterfall deductions, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration and the holder receives 3% pro rata before investor taxes. All consideration is cash at close. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and transaction compensation are each assumed to be $0. The disclosed prior SAFE leaves actual dilution and waterfall effects unresolved. The memo supplies no independently supported fallback that would pay this holder. (*Market & Traction; Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A buyer could assemble comparable rails through partners and build or license the workflow. Slash and Jeeves already combine cards and stablecoin payments with business finance software, while Blinka relies on third parties for accounts, card issuance, and settlement. A buyer could therefore value Blinka mainly for its customer relationships, whose durability remains unmeasured. Provider continuity after a sale is also unverified, and the memo identifies no binding transfer restriction. The overlap makes the ownership premium uncertain, but the evidence does not establish that a substitute fully serves Blinka’s customer segment or caps every credible sale below the hurdle. That supports Watch. (*The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a strategic buyer justify a cash price that clears the holder’s hurdle based on the economics of a transferable cohort of repeat customers? A buyer-backed valuation grounded in measured retention and contribution margin after provider costs, with a credible path to continuing those services after a sale, would move the action to Reach out if the applicable waterfall still delivers at least $2.5 million within eight years.
