# Control Seat -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Jack Grodnick built SCADA at Tesla and SpaceX, while Warren Shepard worked on Starlink manufacturing and flight software. Control Seat is a domain-native industrial tool with unusually strong founder-product-market fit and unusually weak user-added compounding. Control Seat may become valuable software, but today each Battex Chile deployment creates value for Battex Chile—not automatically for the next plant. Specific knowledge is the asset. The missing network is the ceiling.

**Stack Overflow** is the high rung: developers built for developers, with public artifacts and organic community pull proving specific knowledge before the equity story. Control Seat shares the load-bearing mechanism because Grodnick operated SCADA at Tesla and SpaceX, and Shepard encountered factory software at SpaceX. Control Seat diverges where Stack Overflow was strongest: Grodnick’s visible GitHub artifact has zero stars, Shepard has no public repository, and neither founder has technical writing or community adoption that independently demonstrates a long-running obsession. The résumés are domain-specific, not merely prestigious. That places Control Seat closer to Stack Overflow than to the generic ex-FAANG opportunity hunter, but the evidence is employment-deep rather than artifact-deep.

**Perplexity** is the low rung on network effects: each user initially received an answer without directly increasing every prior user’s value. I waived that weakness because permissionless distribution, Aravind Srinivas’s rare retrieval expertise, and pre-consensus AI timing all passed emphatically. Control Seat has the same single-sided geometry without those compensators. Control Seat sells custom-scoped managed-cloud, self-hosted, and integration deployments; Battex Chile does not create supply, demand, or content for the next customer. Plant histories may create switching costs, but captivity is not a network. A customer locked in is not a customer compounding the product for everyone else.

Battex Chile consolidated MQTT brokers, virtual machines, databases, dashboards, and APIs onto Control Seat, proving the software can absorb complexity. Over 18 months, however, Control Seat’s configurations and validated control logic compound inside each plant while distribution resets at the next factory gate. Systems-integration projects are the stated entry motion. That is labor leverage carrying code leverage across the threshold. The seven-day hosted trial could become permissionless distribution, but Control Seat currently violates my unstated portfolio pattern: no open-source adoption, no founder-media channel, no organic builder community, and no product-led motion identified as dominant. Code scales after installation; installation does not yet scale.

The $12.89 billion SCADA market gives Control Seat real room, with historians, anomaly detection, forecasting, virtual PLCs, and natural-language analysis providing adjacent directions. Control Seat nevertheless begins inside brownfield industrial systems where safety requirements, incumbent compatibility, and commissioning consume runway with every pivot. The market score flips upward if Control Seat’s Virtual PLC becomes a hardware-agnostic runtime that customers can adopt without founder-led integration, opening edge control, industrial data infrastructure, and automation software as self-serve adjacencies. Until then, Control Seat has a large market but expensive maneuvering room. A large ocean is less useful when every course change requires rebuilding the ship.

**Twitter** had operators who shipped and a clean prior-exit signal in Ev Williams; intelligence, energy, and integrity could be judged through completed work. Control Seat shows intelligence and energy through SpaceX, Tesla, Microsoft security, Amazon GenAI, and a working Battex deployment. Control Seat’s founders also met in their first year at Dartmouth, giving the partnership more history than a pitch-deck pairing. Integrity remains unobservable: no contested history, but no completed venture, prior-team record, or decade-long operating evidence either. I apply the absent-evidence default rather than invent virtue from credentials. Play long-term games with long-term people—but first establish that they play long-term games.

CODESYS already offers containerized virtual control and certified SIL3 safety capability, while Control Seat’s benchmark wins nine of ten IEC execution workloads but excludes I/O latency, scheduler jitter, motion, and safety certification. My falsifiable forecast is that by July 2029, Control Seat reaches $3 million to $6 million ARR across roughly 20 to 40 industrial sites and is acquired by Rockwell Automation for under $100 million because Control Seat remains integration-led rather than becoming a compounding network. Control Seat’s specific knowledge should produce a useful product and credible deployments; Control Seat’s distribution architecture should constrain independent scale. Expertise creates the wedge. Labor-bound deployment determines the terminal geometry.

**Anchorage Digital** is the strongest objection: permission can become a moat when regulation, trust, and integration make the installed position defensible. Control Seat could follow that exception—Battex validates the overlay strategy, proprietary SCADA and historian software deepen the footprint, and years of plant configuration could make Control Seat painful to remove. The objection stings because industrial incumbents are built from precisely these slow, sticky deployments. The dossier still gives the deciding evidence against it: only one named deployment, no disclosed production duration, no structural barrier, an incomplete CODESYS benchmark, and documentation requiring safety functions to remain independent. I do not write the check yet. Ten production plants, with at least 70% deployed without founder-led integration and documented customer expansion after installation, would verify code leverage over labor leverage and flip my verdict.
