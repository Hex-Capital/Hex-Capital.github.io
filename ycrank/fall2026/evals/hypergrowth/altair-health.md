# Altair Health -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Even assuming strong product-market fit with independent practices, Altair’s current billing service depends on encoded payer policies, connections to practice EHRs and clearinghouses, and business associate agreements that Altair says it signs. The rule-checking engine could carry over to other markets. Serving practices in a different reimbursement system would likely require substantial work to encode local payer rules, connect to local claims infrastructure, and adapt data handling. That dependency triggers the structural downside gate. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The memo documents a demo-led path and separate guidance for new practices and practices switching billers. Those give Altair a repeatable starting point, while access agreements and system setup likely require staff involvement before a practice can submit its first claim. Recurring claims and denials, integration into existing systems, and Altair’s reported reuse of payer-specific resolutions support continued use. The directory-listed annual price covers prior authorization, and the listed 5% starting rate has an unspecified fee basis. The billing service’s recurring terms and ability to grow revenue within retained accounts remain uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Initial traction takes the 7-point default. The dated launch and founder-reported clinic outcome concern the earlier co-pilot. The memo leaves paid production use and revenue growth for the current billing service unknown. For each additional practice, contribution depends on billing fees less attributable sales, integration, processing, and exception-handling costs. Automated checks could reduce routine handling, while failed checks go to in-house billers. Actual fees, exception frequency, and biller time are unresolved, so the evidence supports neither healthy contribution nor a separate economic gate. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Altair reports tracking policies across more than 3,400 payers and reusing prior claim resolutions. If that breadth comes from a configurable rules layer and reusable connectors, the same engine could support new markets with bounded changes. A recurring billing workload would then give each deployment repeated opportunities to reuse that work. The reported payer coverage supports the possibility, but the memo establishes no comparable transfer across reimbursement systems. The structural gate still applies. (Defensibility, The Idea)
