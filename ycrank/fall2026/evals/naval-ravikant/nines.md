# Nines -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Nines is attacking a physical bottleneck in an industry obsessed with software. Compute cannot earn its return while waiting for someone to connect a fiber. That is an attractive place to look. But I want to know what these founders learned by doing the work. Tiantian Li’s reported large-scale inference responsibilities at Meta and autonomous-underwater-vehicle experience provide relevant operating and robotics context. Taerim Eom’s Patreon livestream work provides evidence of software delivery. Neither establishes that the team has built reliable rack manipulation or sold into data-center operations. I give partial founder credit for relevant experience; the exact-problem knowledge remains unproven.

Commissioning is a sensible wedge. Connecting fiber before a facility enters service could offer a more controlled starting environment than manipulating an operating rack. The proposed expansion into optics reseating, drive swaps, and restarts reaches recurring maintenance demand at the same operators. Shared manipulation and control capabilities make that adjacency coherent, although each task still requires validation. The cited $16.2 billion data-center robotics market includes cleaning, inspection, and other activities Nines does not address. It establishes a broad spending category, not the company’s obtainable market.

The decisive question is what accumulates after the first installation. Hardware can embody an advantage, but purchasing and integrating robots does not automatically create one. Nines has not documented proprietary manipulation models, exclusive operating data, or a demonstrated recovery capability. Its website sequence is disclosed as simulated inspection. Microsoft’s November 2024 transceiver prototypes establish that overlapping tasks attract serious engineering effort; they do not establish Nines’s performance. The dangerous outcome is a business where every new facility requires another bespoke engineering project. Deployment data could eventually reverse that relationship, but the dossier supplies a hypothesis rather than an existing asset.

The strongest argument against passing is that these founders combine compute experience with robotics exposure at a genuine physical constraint. This fits my recent preference for infrastructure bottlenecks. A small team could learn something valuable before larger suppliers organize around the problem. I have room for exceptions before revenue or network effects when a working prototype or exceptional founder action supplies the conviction. Here, neither substitute is established. The approximately 252,000 launch views from November 13 measure attention, not repeated operator use. There is no observed customer contribution loop, and pricing and revenue remain undisclosed. I see no evidence of an integrity violation, artificial financing pressure, or concealed simulation; the reservation is evidentiary.

I pass with my own money today. Nines has a credible direction, but the dossier does not yet show that deployment produces reusable capability faster than it produces service obligations. My verifiable condition for reopening is one paid operator deployment demonstrating repeated autonomous fiber patching, with recorded success and intervention rates and reuse across different rack configurations. That would turn the proposed mechanism into evidence. Until then, the attractive market cannot supply the missing product proof.
