# isoquant -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Isoquant’s candidate secret is that continuously optimizing models for specific workloads can outperform general inference clouds on both quality and cost. The falsifying event would be a matched production comparison in which Together or Fireworks reproduces the advantage without materially greater expense. The dossier supplies useful experiments, but no prevailing belief that named competitors reject; those competitors already pursue customization and optimized serving. I give the secret the absent-evidence default. There may be an important discovery here, but the public evidence has not identified it.

The strongest argument for investing is that the engineering already produces observable results. Isoquant reports 452 ms median time to first token against Together’s 890 ms, and a customer reports approximately twice-as-fast agent inference after switching. Its banking experiment reports 91.8% accuracy against GPT-5.4 mini’s 74.7%, with estimated inference cost down 91.7%. These are substantial improvements if the comparisons hold. The technical-breakthrough exception deserves consideration: a sufficiently important result can justify investing before market ownership becomes clear. But a company-published comparison across 3,079 banking requests establishes a promising application of specialization; it does not yet establish a breakthrough competitors cannot reproduce.

Who keeps the savings? This is isoquant’s weakest dimension. Together, Fireworks, and Baseten already combine serving with customization or optimization. The dossier identifies no exclusive data, dominant customer segment, or demonstrated scale advantage. Endpoint compatibility helps customers arrive and also helps them leave. Even successful engineering can therefore become a continuing obligation to match competitors’ prices. The crowded-market red flag applies to this competitive structure; I am not treating the founders’ choice of an AI market as evidence against them.

The founding structure is more encouraging. Deshpande and Patankar report knowing each other for almost ten years, and they have already shipped Agent FM together. That is affirmative evidence of a relationship surviving actual product work. Full-time status, ownership, and economic exposure remain undocumented, so the founding gate earns intermediate credit. Isoquant also fits the unstated preference for businesses operating inside the AI production stack, and its origin in optimizations developed for Agent FM gives the product a concrete operating problem. Neither pattern supplies the missing monopoly mechanism.

The consumption model is coherent, but its power-law economics remain unproved. At the published GLM prices, 1.2 trillion monthly tokens would imply roughly $30,000–$180,000 in monthly billings **if** every token were paid and charged within that model’s listed rates. The actual paying share, model mix, discounts, and GPU costs are unknown. I therefore give partial credit for an identifiable revenue engine without treating token throughput as profitable revenue. Self-service onboarding and workload consultations provide a plausible distribution route; the reported 1,000-plus customers and switching testimonial show adoption. They do not establish acquisition cost, repeat purchase, or sales efficiency. Agent FM’s 60-plus paying users belong to the previous product.

I would pass on a personal check. The persistent-demand exception is the strongest remaining objection: rapid usage could reveal an underserved customer population before the economics become publicly visible. Yet neither reported demand nor the technical results directly removes the risk that customers capture nearly all the benefit through competitive switching. My flip condition is an independently verified production cohort showing that isoquant sustains an order-of-magnitude cost advantage at matched quality against competing providers for six months while retaining paying customers at positive gross margins. That would materially change the case for owning the advantage.
