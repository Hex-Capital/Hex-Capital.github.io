# OpenTrade -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. What are young investors doing differently? OpenTrade does not answer. Its dossier explains what consumer AI can do today—parse filings, earnings calls, sentiment, and market data—but supplies no sequence from short-form drills to a changed investing institution. Tickerdle, Runway, and Higher/Lower are working artifacts, yet none is demonstrably a leak from a six-year future. The product’s movement among an AI trade deck, execution language, fantasy leagues, and training games makes the destination less legible, not more.

The strongest evidence is behavioral, but it is still soft. OpenTrade launched its iOS app on July 1, 2026, built several playable web drills, reported 3x weekly-active-user growth in three days and 5x total-user growth in five days, and attracted 100,000-plus views to one launch animation. Those figures prove that this team can manufacture attention. They do not reveal the denominators, repeat cohorts, money committed, or any costly user behavior. Too few App Store ratings for a review overview is especially useful negative space: people may be sampling the wrapping without adopting a ritual.

The bull case stings because finance games can become scheduled social behavior rather than education. **HQ Trivia** earned an exception through the same possible mechanism—competition becoming a consumer ritual—but it had 2.1 million people arriving simultaneously. OpenTrade has ratios, followers, and video views, not comparable evidence of users returning together or changing how they invest. Prize-backed creator contests may further confuse acquired traffic with intrinsic pull.

The founders are more promising than the product evidence. Ojas Kandhare, Tario You, and Dylan Pak worked together through 67 Labs; Tario has Ai2 and UW research experience plus an ECCV 2026 paper, while Dylan reports generating more than 600 million content views. That is credible technical and distribution range, and the internship recruiting suggests some ability to attract help. What is missing is a dated pre-company obsession with market structure or investing behavior. There is also an accountability question: Dylan is described publicly as a growth co-founder but is absent from YC’s active-founder list. That is not proof of a leadership shell game, but I need to know who runs the company and who has the tiebreaker before my money goes in.

The system economics are similarly thin. “Stock research takes too long” names an inconvenience, not a broken rule, captured rent, artificial gate, or person whose agency is restored. OpenTrade neither executes trades nor acts as an adviser, and it has disclosed no subscription, referral, advertising, or transaction model. Earlier execution and position-sizing language followed by current disclaimers creates a trust boundary the team must manage carefully. A future dataset connecting game decisions to investment outcomes could become sensitive financial-behavior surveillance; because its consent, custody, and permitted uses are undisclosed, that possibility earns no moat credit.

Underneath the interface I find no controlled enabling layer. Alinea, Blossom, Invstr, and StockGro already combine pieces of AI research, education, quizzes, portfolios, and social investing, while OpenTrade has disclosed no exclusive data, regulatory asset, proprietary market access, or technical bottleneck. This looks like a capable consumer-growth team searching for the sticky version of a replicable product. I pass. I would reopen the decision on one verifiable condition: a cohort of at least 1,000 non-incentivized users with more than 40% week-12 retention and a median of three completed drills per week.
