# Taan.ai -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest private exit path is a strategic acquisition by a video-editing or video-creation platform. Music for Video AI identifies Taan as its scoring provider, showing that the technology can be integrated into another product. Taan says its model aligns music to cuts and lets users revise individual instruments or timings. Owning the model could give a platform control over timeline integration and exclusive access to precise revisions, if those capabilities materially reduce editor rework and an API license cannot deliver the same benefit. The memo gives no buyer-side measure of that benefit. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

A paid production rollout that measures editor time and repeat generations against the platform’s best alternative is a plausible seed milestone. The live integration and preview provide a starting point. The CEO reports $1 million raised, and the memo separately records a $500,000 YC investment. Available runway and the cost of reaching the milestone remain unknown. The founder lists two published patent applications and Taan says its training music is licensed. Ownership and transferability of the model IP and training rights remain unverified. The memo supplies no independent fallback that could deliver cash to this holder. (*Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*, *Key Facts*)

The illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. A 5× cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the investor, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, transaction fees, or preference adjustments, so enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration and cash is allocated pro rata before investor taxes. They assume all cash at closing, with zero stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, or compensation. The prices are scenario assumptions. The memo gives no buyer economics or transaction evidence to support them. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A platform could license Taan’s API or use an existing alternative. Premiere already offers scoring inside the timeline, while ElevenLabs and Sonilo offer video-to-music APIs. Taan’s own on-time figures differ between its research and launch materials, at 91.3% and 73.2%, and the benchmark uses short instrumental requests. If available alternatives meet editors’ timing needs in production, the case for paying an acquisition premium weakens substantially. The memo leaves that comparison unresolved, so the evidence supports Watch and does not trigger a structural Pass. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a video platform’s paid production rollout show enough value from owning Taan to justify a price that clears the investor’s hurdle? A buyer-side comparison of building, licensing, and acquiring, grounded in measured reductions in editor rework, would move the action to Reach out if it supports roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership and identifies a credible path to transfer the model and training rights.
