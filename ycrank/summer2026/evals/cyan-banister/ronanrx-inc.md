# RonanRx Inc. -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032 and the interesting version of RonanRx is not an online GLP-1 clinic. It is a control system that continuously matches therapies to a person’s labs, wearables, and observed response while physicians and pharmacists retain the consequential decisions. The fixed-dose schedule is a small leak from the old world, and RonanRx has assembled the intake and prescription machinery for a different one. But the dossier does not map the transitions between those worlds: no validated feedback loop, no longitudinal outcomes, and no evidence that peptide personalization extends beyond today’s very fashionable GLP-1 market. I see the outline of what is coming, but not yet the causal chain.

The founders are the strongest part of this bet. Lloyd Armbrust is a repeat YC founder who says he built a $50 million medical-manufacturing business and exited three companies. Emily Colman spent more than a decade in healthcare sales, including weight-management work at Eli Lilly across hundreds of physicians. That is real operating tuition: manufacturing, regulated distribution, clinic relationships, and commercial fluency live inside this five-person team. What I cannot find is the pre-company artifact showing that either founder was independently obsessed with individualized dosing before GLP-1s became a consensus market. Their biographies establish competence and recruiting complementarity, not yet the strange first-believer act that earns my highest founder score.

There is a real broken rule here. FDA 503A policy permits compounding when a prescriber documents a patient-specific significant difference, while ordinary GLP-1 care still begins with fixed doses and standardized titration. RonanRx turns that rule into software gates: the physician decides, the pharmacist releases, and prescription-level lot genealogy follows the medication. The white-label model may return control to clinics that otherwise cannot operate intake, compounding, shipping, and refills themselves. But the economic rent remains fuzzy. The subscription price is hidden until checkout, the pharmacy bills medication separately, and the company may be monetizing regulatory complexity without yet proving that patients receive better agency or outcomes.

The working artifact is legible but user behavior is not. RonanRx has text intake, Stripe billing, structured clinical records, release checklists, and Texas service availability. Those are more substantial than a rendering. Yet 65 Launch YC votes are not patients enduring treatment, physicians changing protocols, or clinics trusting the system repeatedly. There are no named clinics, paid-user counts, refill cohorts, outcome comparisons, or signed commitments. I will excuse missing revenue at pre-seed when a prototype produces an unusually costly behavior or institutional commitment; this dossier supplies neither.

The bull case stings because the visible GLP-1 program could be merely the beachhead. Code-enforced compliance, pharmacy coordination, and longitudinal response records could become the concealed operating layer beneath many personalized therapies, and this team has unusually relevant experience for building it. My contrary forecast is that within 36 months RonanRx will primarily sell clinic-and-pharmacy workflow software, remain below $5 million ARR, and stop leading with AI-personalized peptides. Hims and Ro already demonstrate that intake, customized dosing, labs, and fulfillment can be integrated, while RonanRx discloses no controlled technical bottleneck or dataset scale. Worse, collecting medical records, labs, wearables, and treatment responses without a disclosed consent, custody, or retention architecture touches my privacy gate directly.

I am Neutral at 48. This is capable workflow repair inside a hot present-day market, not yet a six-year future leaking through weird user behavior. The titles identify Armbrust as founder and Colman as COO, so I do not see evidence of a leadership shell game, but the undisclosed governance relationship should still be verified. I would flip to Invest on one auditable result: a prospectively defined cohort of at least 250 paid patients across three named clinics showing materially lower six-month discontinuation or adverse-event rates than standard titration, with documented patient consent for the data used.
