# Terse -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to a developer infrastructure platform is Terse’s strongest exit path. Terse says Durable Actors commit application state and SQLite writes together by default, and the runtime supports self-hosting. A platform could buy Terse to add managed agent coordination and bring over production workloads that need deployment control. That rationale depends on customer adoption and buyer economics that the memo leaves unverified (The Idea, Defensibility).

The memo records the hosted launch and 366 GitHub stars. Terse’s founders reported 1.4 million requests in one day and 32,752 VM launches coordinated in a demo. Separately, Doop’s creator reported a migration in progress. The milestone is repeat paid production use on Terse Cloud after initial deployment. The reported $500,000 YC financing gives Terse some capacity to test that milestone. Its cost, timing, and funding requirements remain uncertain (Market & Traction).

With holder terms unspecified, the benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. That requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. Both sale scenarios assume an all-cash close within eight years, $5 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, no additional senior waterfall claims, and an eligible holder selling the full stake pro rata. The prices are illustrative assumptions.

| Scenario | Gross cash to holder |
|----------|----------------------|
| Modest sale at $40 million enterprise value | $35 million distributable equity × 3% = $1.05 million, or 2.1× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | $95 million distributable equity × 3% = $2.85 million, or 5.7×. Selling half the stake on the same terms yields $1.425 million. |
| Prolonged private operation | $0 realized within eight years if cash is reinvested and no eligible share purchase occurs. |

The MIT license and self-hosting support the practicality of a runtime handoff. Hosted asset ownership, customer contract assignment, financing, and holder rights remain unverified. The memo gives no support for an achievable sale price or an independently funded liquidity route (The Idea, Market & Traction).

### Countercase

Cloudflare already bundles persistent state and WebSockets into Workers, and Rivet offers self-hosted actors with per-actor SQLite and WebSockets. A platform can also adopt Terse’s MIT-licensed runtime directly. If those options meet its needs, buying Terse would make sense only if the hosted business or customer relationships add material value. The memo shows early activity and published pricing, with paid retention still unverified. That gap supports Watch. The evidence establishes neither a structural block nor a credible price ceiling below the holder’s objective (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks).

### Decisive diligence question

What would make an infrastructure platform pay enough for Terse to leave at least $83.3 million in distributable equity value within eight years? Move from Watch to Reach out if a plausible buyer gives a valuation range that clears that hurdle after expected deal deductions, grounded in the value of retained, paying production workloads it would gain by acquiring Terse.
