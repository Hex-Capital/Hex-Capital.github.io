# Sona8 -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, an automation agent should be able to ask why an employee bypasses the official purchasing system and discover the exception that keeps the factory running. Sona8 is collecting the human explanations that enterprise software misses. Its multilingual interviews already feed themes into connected AI assistants; the live workflow graph remains a development goal. I can follow the path from interviews to operational memory to useful automation. The first transition is visible. Whether employees keep that memory accurate—and whether machines can safely act on it—is still unproven.

The founders make this worth investigating. Anton Hantel and Thilo Tamme encountered the problem while conducting employee interviews at BCG, and Tamme brings conversational-AI research experience from TUM. Madeleine Malmsten’s self-taught engineering background, previous founding experience, and principal-engineer role at QuantumBlack suggest someone who can build through enterprise complexity. This is an operating wound becoming a product specification, a pattern I like. But the dossier establishes relevant experience more clearly than longstanding obsession: it supplies little dated evidence of pre-company experiments, independent inventions, or recruiting people into an improbable mission.

Why are the workarounds invisible in the first place? Sona8 identifies a concrete constraint: transformation teams interview selectively, sometimes through interpreters, so the people operating the system never enter its diagnosis. Adaptive multilingual interviews could widen that aperture. But the economic transfer is less clear. A platform fee plus interview and query charges gives consultants a scalable research instrument; it does not yet show employees gaining control over their work. The unnamed top-three consulting customer could distribute Sona8 widely while leaving the same institution in charge of interpreting everyone else.

The strongest argument for writing a check is that this distribution is already producing contact with reality. Sona8 reports more than 10,000 completed interviews, and Pacific Southwest Container’s strategy director describes previously unseen efficiency and safety opportunities. Employees have spent time explaining their work through a functioning product. That matters at pre-seed. Still, the interview count lacks cohort and repeat-participation detail, and the testimonial establishes discovery rather than sustained operational change. I would want to inhabit the employee experience myself: would I volunteer the awkward workaround, then return because something actually improved?

That question also exposes the privacy gate. Voluntary participation, aggregation, minimum group sizes, and works-council agreements are affirmative architectural choices; this dossier does not establish a severe privacy violation. Yet the founder-coauthored September 2026 audit found singleton concerns omitted in 86% of 45 LLM summaries. That is not Sona8’s production error rate, but it challenges the mechanism directly: the unusual account may contain the most important exception. Fixed codebooks, ten coding agents, six-agent agreement, and excerpt traceability show methodological care. They do not establish that rare truths survive or that Sona8 controls a scarce enabling asset.

I land at Neutral. The concealed-infrastructure possibility is attractive, but today’s evidence supports an interview product more strongly than an indispensable context layer. Celonis already pursues enterprise context, and Salesforce’s announced Listen Labs acquisition could put interviewing inside a powerful distribution channel. My pre-revenue exception does not resolve this: missing revenue is acceptable; the missing proof here concerns what the product becomes. One verifiable flip condition would change my decision: a paid customer renews after a year of using Sona8’s employee-validated workflow graph to support two distinct production automation systems. That would demonstrate the bridge from useful conversations to infrastructure.
