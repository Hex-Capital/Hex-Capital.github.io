# Silica -- Paul Graham Evaluation

I want to know what happened when Enrico Milletti tried to make a robot joint measure force accurately and something broke. The dossier explains the problem: estimating contact forces from motor current leaves developers compensating in software. But it gives no specific episode in which Milletti encountered that limitation, built a workaround, and measured the improvement. That leaves my most important founder test unanswered. His Apple manufacturing experience is relevant. It cannot tell me what he does when blocked.

The strongest argument for Silica is that this is precisely the sort of problem a materials and manufacturing engineer might understand better than software founders. Milletti has a Berkeley materials engineering MEng, worked on manufacturing processes at Apple, and was Artefact’s founding engineer. An affordable actuator with output-side torque sensing could remove work from every robot developer who uses it. Calibration, durability, and production yield are also plausible places for difficult accumulated knowledge to matter. But the dossier does not connect those credentials to a documented personal encounter with this problem. The founder-origin criterion stays at its missing-evidence default.

The distinction that matters now is between a specification and a result. Silica labels its specifications as design targets. Customer prototypes are scheduled for December 2026 and production for January 2027; neither establishes an achieved sensing threshold today. My pattern of backing technical founders outside familiar sectors depends on numerical proof that makes their claims inspectable. Silica has identified something worth proving, but the dossier supplies no functioning artifact or measured experiment to score above the technical default. Nor does it document an observation that caused Milletti to revise his product route. That is unknown, rather than evidence of obstinacy.

Who needs the crude version badly enough to keep using it? Robot developers collecting physical-interaction data are a plausible narrow niche. Opening preorders does not establish that anyone ordered, installed, or returned for another unit. HEBI already sells series-elastic actuator modules, and Sensodrive offers torque-controlled joints, including a safety-certified version. Those alternatives make the affordability claim consequential: Silica needs a concrete reason for a developer to choose its joint. Public comparative prices and measured performance are unavailable. The $18.62 billion category estimate cannot answer that question. Demand therefore remains at its default too.

I would pass on the present evidence. Silica has no documented partner dependency, premature sales delegation, or dismissal of contrary feedback; I would not invent those red flags from a sparse dossier. But backing from YC, Founders Inc., and UP.Partners does not replace founder behavior or user response. The exceptions in my framework require a working prototype, unmistakable demand, or validated technical proof strong enough to override an unanswered founder test. None is established here. My one flip condition is a documented customer deployment showing Milletti overcoming a named integration blocker with a shipped revision that achieves measured output-torque performance and earns a repeat paid order. That would turn this from an attractive engineering proposition into evidence about the founder.
