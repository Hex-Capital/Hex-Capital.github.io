# Derya -- Elad Gil Evaluation

$80,000 of contracted first-month revenue makes Derya an execution-rich freight operator whose strongest signal is founder shipping and whose weakest is index-like positioning. Mert Turna previously deployed ship-operations software across 30-plus vessels representing $200 million of fleet value, while Oguzhan Karaca built a business to six-figure ARR. Derya can clearly ship and sell; Derya has not yet shown that each new shipment strengthens a distribution rail rather than merely enlarging a brokerage book.

**Perplexity** is the high rung for iterative shipping: Aravind Srinivas repeatedly built the discussed product within a week. Turna’s operating software and Karaca’s prior ARR business put Derya closer to that pattern than credential-only founders, and sourcing $3.2 million of opportunities in month one suggests rapid commercial iteration. Derya diverges from Perplexity because the dossier names prior artifacts, not repeated Derya product releases or measured automation improvements between customer conversations.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism: being another participant in a market where scale advantages accrue disproportionately to established leaders. Derya faces Flexport, Forto, Nuvocargo, and Zencargo while directly quoting and moving freight rather than supplying infrastructure every forwarder must use. Derya may accumulate useful pricing and carrier-performance data, but no exclusive relationships or structural barriers are disclosed; today Derya must win shipments against category incumbents rather than become an index on global freight.

Recent language-model improvements plausibly let Derya automate documents, carrier qualification, pricing messages, and customer updates, but that is a horizontal technology catalyst entering a freight market already populated by digital forwarders. The market-reopening score would flip upward if Derya showed that agents reduced quote or operating cost by an order of magnitude—same-day quotes are directionally useful, but no automation rate, accuracy figure, or gross-margin comparison is disclosed. Without that evidence, Derya looks like skating for the puck with the right technology but without proof that the rink itself has changed.

**Harvey** is the relevant revenue parallel because the important signal was not “AI legal software”; it was a curve that was just working. Derya’s $80,000 contracted in month one and TVO’s signed 20-container commitment are unusually informative at pre-seed, but Derya’s $3.2 million of sourced opportunities remains pipeline, not realized revenue. One month cannot establish Harvey-like velocity, yet Derya has crossed well beyond pilot tourism into actual buying behavior.

Derya’s operating scope compounds in two directions over the next 18 months: completed shipments can improve carrier selection and pricing, while customs liability, partner dependence, and financing above $20,000 can compound balance-sheet risk just as quickly. Derya does not require new physics or a new foundation model, so the technical miracle has already occurred; Derya still needs reliable agent execution, repeat freight demand, defensible carrier access, and disciplined underwriting to work together. That is more operational dependency than Decagon, though it is not a science project in the Color Genomics sense.

$3.2 million of first-month opportunities supports a falsifiable forecast: within 36 months, Derya will reach $8 million to $15 million in annualized net revenue and remain an AI-enabled freight forwarder rather than becoming a horizontal logistics-software platform. Derya’s shipping founders and early contracted demand can build a meaningful operator, but Derya’s nonexclusive partner network and transaction-by-transaction acquisition model constrain the index outcome.

**Flexport** supplies the strongest bear case: freight forwarding can absorb enormous capital while software differentiation collapses into service operations, and Derya adds its own financing exposure before disclosing underwriting performance or gross margins. Derya could therefore mistake $3.2 million of opportunities for durable demand and scale liabilities faster than contribution profit. The facts that win for now are $80,000 already contracted, a named 20-container customer commitment, Turna’s 30-vessel deployment history, and Karaca’s prior six-figure-ARR execution; I would flip Derya to Pass if fewer than 30% of first-booking revenue recurs within six months.
