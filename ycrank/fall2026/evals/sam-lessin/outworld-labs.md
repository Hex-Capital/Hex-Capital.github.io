# Outworld Labs -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Outworld Labs proposes measuring reality closely enough that robot developers can stop paying to rediscover it on hardware. That could be valuable. But what does my check buy? The dossier supplies neither a post-money valuation nor competing-term-sheet information, and it names no capital-funded experiment with a deadline and a pass/fail threshold. I cannot establish that capital is scarce or that this round reveals a discrete truth. Those are missing facts, so both financing dimensions receive their stated defaults. YC membership does not establish an auction.

The interesting mechanism is Horizon’s loop: measure hardware behavior, fit the physics, and report agreement with reality. My preference for software reorganizing difficult physical operations applies here. A simulator that reliably reduces physical trials could own an important workflow even while NVIDIA and DeepMind distribute free infrastructure. The distinction would have to survive a customer asking, “Why can’t I calibrate MuJoCo myself?” Outworld describes a custom dexterous-manipulation engine, but the dossier contains no comparative transfer results or calibration-time measurements. Accumulating hardware data could eventually create proprietary access; the dossier does not establish ownership rights or show that this access has caused a purchase.

The strongest argument against passing is the founders’ proximity to the actual problem. Matei Gardea brings BAIR robotics research; Daniel Grant’s work includes legged-robot control and computational fluid dynamics, alongside research experience at DeepMind, NASA, and Oxford. They may understand a failure mode that general simulation platforms underserve. I take that seriously. But this criterion asks for shipped operating results across the systems being joined. The dossier establishes relevant research backgrounds without documenting an external deployment or a customer operation they have run. That earns the founder default, rather than an invented operating track record.

The September 28, 2026 founder-reported joint first place at YC’s Group 1 showcase is evidence that the demonstration registered with that audience. It supplies no purchase, repeat usage, or cash economics. The 23 company-account followers carry no underwriting weight either. With pricing, revenue, calibration labor, and burn undisclosed, I cannot determine whether each new robot configuration expands a profitable software business or adds another bespoke engineering obligation. Delete the next financing: the dossier gives me no funded route to break-even. That is uncertainty, not evidence of negative margins, and the economics score stays at its default.

I would pass on Outworld Labs today. The exceptions in my framework require something concrete to override incomplete gates: demonstrated conversion and retention, external commercial validation, or an explicit capital structure and use plan. None is documented here. Nor does the dossier establish auction tactics, failed conversion, or an error-free automation claim; I am not assigning those red flags by association. My one condition for reopening is an independently documented, paid customer deployment showing that Horizon reduces physical training or evaluation costs after including calibration labor. That would connect the technical proposition to owned workflow and customer economics. It would give me a reason to underwrite the round rather than merely admire the research.
