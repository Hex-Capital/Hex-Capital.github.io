# Caution -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Confidential-computing hardware is becoming broadly available, security-sensitive teams now need independently verifiable deployments, and Caution becomes a large company only if source-to-runtime proof converts a difficult attestation problem into a managed workflow customers repeatedly pay for. The controlling variable is paid adoption of that workflow—not the forecast that confidential computing grows from $5.5 billion in 2023 to $153.8 billion by 2030. The dossier establishes the need: 84.5% of surveyed organizations cite attestation-chain validation as a barrier. It does not yet establish the threshold economics: pricing, revenue, retention, and expansion are undisclosed.

The technical mechanism is real. Caution reproducibly builds enclave images, provisions AWS Nitro infrastructure, and lets an independent party verify the live workload against reviewed source, configuration, kernel, and toolchain. This is more than an attestation dashboard. It owns a sequence from git commit through deployment and verification, and better enclave hardware should expand the workloads it can support rather than erase the workflow. Production deployments across five workload categories and TrustedStake’s public identification as an early user provide credible pre-seed evidence that the product is operational, although “production” without usage or revenue data does not yet demonstrate product-market pull.

The market change is directionally favorable but economically incomplete. Azure added Intel TDX VM availability in 2025, while 75% of surveyed organizations reported using or piloting confidential computing. More hardware support increases the number of workloads that could require Caution. Yet the company currently supports only AWS Nitro, depends on AWS’s trust root, and has not quantified whether its verification layer reduces audit cost, deployment time, insurance expense, or security staffing. A technical wave deserves credit when it changes adoption or margins; the dossier currently shows expanding feasibility, not the magnitude of the customer-level economic step change.

The strongest bull case is the team. Anton Livaja and Lance Vick encountered infrastructure security through Ledn, BitGo, Turnkey, OpenZeppelin work, and their security consultancy Distrust; Ksenia Lesko already handled strategy and operations alongside them. They have worked together since at least 2021 and appear to have converted repeated consulting problems into a product. That is meaningful second-time learning. It also explains why an unfinished private-beta product already reaches crypto infrastructure, lending, consumer hardware, post-quantum security, and private AI inference.

The opposing case is value capture. The complete platform is AGPLv3, cloud vendors control the hardware trust roots, and OPAQUE, Edgeless Systems, and enclaive already overlap with deployment, attestation, and compliance. Customer-approved baselines and multi-hardware integrations could eventually create switching costs, but those are inferred rather than built. Open source can create distribution; it can also leave Caution selling support around a capability absorbed by AWS or bundled by a better-funded platform. No known exception resolves this: the company is neither a uniquely scarce frontier research team nor reconstructing a market with demonstrated step-change margins.

I would monitor rather than invest today. The market gate narrowly clears because the attestation-chain barrier is measured and the product attacks it causally, but the evidence stops before showing durable monetization. My flip condition is verifiable: within twelve months, Caution should disclose at least five paying production customers, with three renewing or expanding managed deployments and at least one supporting a second hardware trust root. That would demonstrate both pull and movement from an open-source security tool toward owned infrastructure.
