# Edgerun -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers are missing: post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. YC and H.R. McMaster are credible backers, but they do not tell me whether I am buying a neglected belief or paying the Demo Day clearing price. Financing silence is not evidence of an auction; it is also not evidence of cheap convexity. Under my framework, that leaves the deal gate at its low prior.

The company evidence is much stronger than the deal evidence. Since May 2026, Edgerun has completed more than 100 field tests with Ukrainian infantry, special-operations, and National Guard operators, while Balistyka is integrating the ten-pound system with existing combat kit. That is an actual workflow producing repeated prototype usage and a potentially proprietary feedback dataset—not 247 LinkedIn followers masquerading as distribution. The limitation is causal conversion: no test has produced a disclosed purchase order, paid pilot, or retained deployment.

The technical card is legible. A waist-mounted system adds hip torque where Edgerun says roughly 60% of loaded-walking force originates, using one-pound actuators, swappable batteries, and a claimed twelve-plus-hour runtime. Proving 30–45% assistance independently under combat load could be a clean venture moment. But Edgerun has not said that this round funds that experiment, named a deadline, or defined the result that converts verification into procurement. A $1.16 billion military-exoskeleton market is not a capital plan, and “nothing exotic by design” makes rapid manufacturing plausible while also limiting component-level defensibility.

David Venegas has operated near the machinery: Durin operations, Hadrian engineering, and Stoke Space software are relevant receipts from industrial robotics and aerospace manufacturing. Edgerun’s Kyiv testing loop and Washington presence provide access to soldiers and procurement channels on the other side. What is missing is a prior shipped product, scaled operation, or defense deployment connecting those ecosystems. The sole-founder structure at least makes accountability clear; there is no concealed equal-founder committee.

The painful bull case is that I am underrating unusually fast physical-world learning. More than 100 combat-context tests in roughly three months, a named armor-integration partner, McMaster’s backing, and a product designed around newly capable actuators and batteries could turn third-party validation into procurement faster than ordinary defense hardware. That would justify overriding missing deal information. But the disclosed 30–45% assistance remains internally measured, and neither Balistyka nor any military unit has disclosed payment. Hard external conversion is what would earn the exception; field access alone does not.

My August 2029 base case is that Edgerun has one or more paid allied-military pilots and $1–5 million in annual product revenue, but remains dependent on another financing because procurement, manufacturing, and support have not yet become self-funding. I am passing today because the field-learning asset is real while the price, finite capital experiment, and cash path are undocumented. One condition flips me: a signed investment agreement at no more than a $9 million post-money valuation whose budget names a twelve-month, independently verified loaded-walking milestone tied to a paid military deployment.
