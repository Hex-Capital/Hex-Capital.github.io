# Grep AI -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Grep AI has already raised a $5 million seed round as Parcha. What would my check buy now? The dossier supplies neither today’s post-money valuation nor competing-term-sheet count, so I cannot establish that capital is scarce or that the price compensates for the risk. Prior backing does not prove an auction. Equally, converting repeated agent reasoning into code is a product hypothesis; it is not a documented financing experiment with a budget, deadline, and pass/fail result. Both primary gates remain unanswered.

The strongest evidence is inside IG’s compliance operation. Screening automation increased from 10% to 30%, flagged-customer activation fell from 2–3 days to under ten minutes, and reported review accuracy exceeded 99%. Months of shadow testing and governance approval make this substantially more informative than hundreds of preview users. Grep has crossed the boundary between an interesting agent and an embedded workflow people actually use. The potential dark pool is the customer-specific review criteria and operational history. But those assets sit inside the customer’s process; the dossier does not establish Grep’s exclusive access or whether this advantage transfers from legacy compliance deployments to its broader product.

The founders make the bull case uncomfortable to dismiss. Asver has an actual acquisition behind him, and the pair worked together at Brex before operating compliance agents through Parcha. This fits my preference for people who can translate between software infrastructure and a mistrustful operating system. Their approach also addresses a real economic problem: turn repetitive reasoning into procedures and reserve expensive reasoning for exceptions. If existing relationships with Airwallex, Worldpay, and Citizens Bank produce repeat purchases across workflows, the enterprise foothold could matter much more than the crowded agent category.

The cash evidence keeps me from making that leap. Asver reported nearly $80,000 in monthly AI spending and loss-making product runs before a harness redesign reduced costs. That is historical adverse evidence, with a plausible repair but no disclosed current margin to verify it. A $200 or $500 subscription is a price, not contribution profit. Delete the next financing: can customer receipts pay for inference, exception handling, implementation, and six people? Nothing here answers that. Meanwhile, n8n’s self-hosting and SAP distribution mean customer control and enterprise access are already contested. Cheaper execution must leave Grep with cash and durable ownership of the workflow.

I pass. The known exceptions require harder evidence than is available here: measured paid conversion and retention, compelling acquisition demand, or an explicit financing structure that makes repeated raises optional. IG supplies genuine operating validation, but not that economic override. Nor does the dossier establish the red flags of auction pressure or confused founder authority; I am not inventing them. The decisive issue is a demonstrated workflow bridge whose current financing purpose and cash engine remain unproven. One verifiable flip condition would reopen the decision: six consecutive months of positive company operating cash flow from customer receipts, including inference, human oversight, implementation, and payroll. That would show the redesigned system funds itself.
