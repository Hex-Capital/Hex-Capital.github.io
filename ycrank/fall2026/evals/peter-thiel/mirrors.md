# Mirrors -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Mirrors offers a candidate secret: recurring agent failures can be corrected more reliably by delivering situational guidance than by continually enlarging a global prompt. What would prove that wrong? A controlled comparison showing that conditional guidance introduces as many mistakes as it removes, or that ordinary prompt maintenance achieves the same result. The documentation establishes that Mirrors has implemented a delivery mechanism. It does not establish the disputed truth behind it: no named peer rejects the proposition, and no comparative result demonstrates its superiority. I can formulate the hypothesis from the product; I cannot credit the founders with having proved it.

The harder question is why solving this problem would create a company that keeps the value. Mirrors targets engineering teams maintaining customer-facing agents, but that describes a customer category rather than a bounded market it can dominate. Papaya ships trace-derived optimization through pull requests; Mem0 supplies persistent conversational memory. These are meaningfully different approaches, so calling them interchangeable would overstate the evidence against Mirrors. Yet reviewed context routing has no demonstrated proprietary technology, exclusive data, network effect, or scale advantage in this dossier. Customer-specific guidance might eventually create switching costs. A possible future mechanism receives no credit as an existing monopoly.

The strongest objection to passing is that Mirrors has already built a more substantial product than its sparse commercial disclosure suggests. Previewing matches, reviewing guidance, and supporting shadow or off modes address the actual difficulty of changing production behavior safely. The website’s **711,933 agent sessions** could conceal repeated use by teams with an urgent problem. My unstated preference for infrastructure embedded in important workflows makes this worth taking seriously; standalone software is not outside my investment universe. But the session count has no period, customer breakdown, or separation between the earlier replay product and today’s context product. It cannot establish a repeatable acquisition channel. Demo booking and public integration documentation provide a concrete route to engineering buyers, earning modest distribution credit, while conversion, sales cycles, and delivery economics remain unproved.

Aryan Singhal’s UT Austin and UCSB research supplies relevant technical context. It does not answer the founding questions. The team is listed as two people, but the dossier does not establish the other founder’s identity, their shared working history, ownership, or full-time status. Purdue studies are not evidence of divided commitment, and the founder explicitly says he has stopped working on Profine. I therefore apply the missing-evidence default rather than invoke the part-time-founder red flag. There is also no evidence of outsourced core engineering, investor-majority ownership, or substantial founder liquidity. Those red flags have been checked; none is established.

Mirrors has one coherent product, which is preferable to several unrelated revenue experiments. Nevertheless, custom pricing without a disclosed price, billing unit, margin structure, or customer expansion pattern gives me no arithmetic for a power-law outcome. The **$8.3 billion** context-layer estimate includes businesses beyond Mirrors and cannot supply that arithmetic. My known exceptions do not resolve this: the dossier presents neither an independently verified technical discontinuity nor persistent paid customer behavior that directly overcomes the uncertainty about value capture. A shipped SDK and an unattributed session counter are insufficient substitutes.

I pass on writing a check today. This is an unresolved investment proposition, not evidence that the company must fail: the founders have identified a plausible engineering problem, but the dossier has not shown the secret or the mechanism that lets Mirrors own its solution. One verifiable result would move me to investigate: a paid production deployment demonstrating a sustained, independently measured advantage over the customer’s strongest in-house conditional-context implementation, attributable to proprietary Mirrors routing technology. That would begin converting useful software into evidence of a business worth owning.
