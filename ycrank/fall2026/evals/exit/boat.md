# Boat -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a cloud or agent infrastructure provider is Boat’s strongest path to investor cash. The memo documents a live, usage-priced product that provides full Ubuntu VMs with Docker and a desktop, plus the ability to stop, resume, and fork environments. Boat’s CTO says the snapshot system was built in-house. A buyer could pay to control that runtime if owning it materially improved the cost or reliability of running persistent agent workloads at production scale. The memo supplies no buyer-validated economics or acquisition price evidence. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*)

The founder reports 350+ paying users in September and $1 million in ARR on November 7, up from $200,000 a month earlier. Those claims support testing the runtime with sustained paid workloads. Retention, margins after compute costs, and the capital needed to scale remain unverified. The memo also leaves ownership and transferability of the snapshot IP unverified and supplies no usable evidence of an independent route to cash for this holder. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Defensibility*)

The proceeds bridge uses the illustrative seed terms because the memo supplies no actual check terms. A $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-on checks. The eight-year, 5× objective requires $2.5 million in gross cash, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Holder cash | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---|---|
| Modest sale at $40 million enterprise value | Year 5 | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | Year 7 | $3 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private ownership | Through year 8 | $0, shares retained | 0× |

The sale prices are scenario assumptions. Both assume zero net debt, fees, or other price adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. The waterfall assumes pro rata cash at close before investor taxes, with zero senior claims, stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, or compensation. The strong case clears the hurdle at 3% ownership and yields only 4× at 2%. In the prolonged-private case, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested.

### Countercase

A buyer could rent Boat’s VMs, self-host E2B’s open-source runtime, or use AWS’s bundled browser and code execution for workloads those tools cover. The memo provides no production comparison showing that owning Boat’s stack creates enough incremental value to justify the required price. That gap warrants Watch. The memo leaves open whether substitutes would cap the price below the hurdle, so the structural Pass override is unproven. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a plausible acquirer validate, on sustained production workloads, that owning Boat’s snapshot and orchestration stack creates enough value over the best rental or self-hosted option to support a price yielding roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds? A buyer-validated comparison that quantifies operating savings or incremental margin after integration costs and supports that price would move the action to Reach out. A comparison showing that substitutes cap the price materially below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
