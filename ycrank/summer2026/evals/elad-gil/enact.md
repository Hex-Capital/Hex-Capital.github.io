# Enact -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: general-purpose robotics models are improving, robot developers now need deployment-specific post-training, and Enact becomes large only if its closed loop reduces real-world policy failures faster and more cheaply than customers, data vendors, or model platforms can do themselves. The controlling variable is measurable improvement per dollar of collected recovery data. Enact describes the right loop—identify failures, generate targeted demonstrations, retrain, and evaluate—but publishes no success-rate delta, collection cost, iteration-time reduction, or threshold at which customers outsource the work.

The technology shift is real but the economics remain unproven. Gemini Robotics and NVIDIA Cosmos arrived in 2025, while the physical-AI training-platform market was estimated at $3.2 billion with 41.35% annual growth. Better base models should expand the number of deployable policies and therefore the volume of edge-case remediation. But real-world collection remains expensive, task-specific, and difficult to reuse across embodiments. Enact has not shown that targeted recovery data changes this cost structure rather than creating a specialized services business inside a growing market.

The product architecture is the strongest part of the case. Enact is not merely labeling data or grading rollouts; it proposes to own the operational cycle from failure detection through corrective data, training, and verification. Better models could improve that system rather than erase it. Repeated deployments might also create useful mappings between failure states and effective recoveries. Today, however, this is a workflow description, not demonstrated infrastructure: there are no disclosed integrations, datasets, evaluation results, or evidence that the resulting state is exclusive and difficult to reproduce.

The bull case is that robotics post-training is still pre-crystallization and the winning company may be the one that closes this loop before model vendors internalize it. Competitor financing supports category formation: Axis Robotics raised $12 million, Dyna Robotics has raised $143.5 million, and micro1 reportedly reached $100 million ARR in the broader human-data market. Yet the same evidence makes Enact’s position less forgiving. Instance has tested success detection on more than 10,000 held-out episodes across seven platforms, while NVIDIA and Runway can incorporate evaluation and synthetic-data capabilities at the platform layer. Enact currently supplies neither rare documented technical talent nor frontier research sufficient for a no-traction exception.

There is also no product-market pull to resolve the market-structure question. The August 9, 2026 YC listing and “Request data” button establish availability, not usage, revenue, repeat demand, or even a named pilot. No founder histories are public, so I cannot credit robotics expertise, costly commitment, or second-time learning. My 36-month base case is that Enact pivots toward project-based robotics data and evaluation services and remains below $3 million ARR by August 2029, rather than becoming the independent post-training layer.

I would pass today. The single flip condition is three named robotics-model customers renewing contracts after independently reporting at least a 20% policy-success improvement from Enact-generated recovery data. That result would validate the controlling variable, demonstrate pull, and show that the integrated loop has value beyond components already offered by competitors.
