# Hubble -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Hubble’s 40+ live tools across Epic, Cerner, athenahealth, UnitedHealthcare, and Aetna make Hubble a healthcare-rail asset I already know how to like, trapped inside an oversubscribed pre-seed round I do not know how to price. That high category fit and low entry-price fit is the analytical signature: Hubble may become a useful business while still being a bad personal angel investment. Software is no longer investible—fine, that is deliberately overstated—but undifferentiated software bought in a hot process genuinely is difficult to make money on.

Ro is the high rung because regulatory complexity across prescription rails turned capital intensity into the moat and created a genuine venture moment. Hubble shares the load-bearing mechanism: patient authorization, protected health information, provenance, audit logs, and CMS-0057-F implementation beginning in 2027 make healthcare access infrastructure harder than another API wrapper. Hubble diverges because Ro owned a consumer relationship and monetization surface, while Hubble has no disclosed revenue, no proprietary dataset, no coverage benchmark, and four well-funded substitutes—Particle Health, Health Gorilla, 1upHealth, and Zus Health. The unstated healthcare-DTC and prescription-rail sleeve helps Hubble, but Hubble sits one layer farther from the patient economics that made Ro exceptional.

Pinterest exposed the low-rung pricing failure mechanism: consensus can eliminate entry alpha even when the underlying company is excellent, and only missionary temperament plus personal-network access justified the exception. Hubble reports an oversubscribed pre-seed financing while disclosing neither price nor investors, which is precisely the red flag my value-seed filter is designed to catch. Hubble offers no Pinterest-style override: no prior relationship to my operating network, no evidence that I have privileged allocation, and no founder artifact so unusually compelling that I should ignore the auction. An oversubscribed SAFE is not proof of quality; it is proof that someone else has already performed the legibility translation and is charging me for it.

CMS-0057-F can compound Hubble’s capital structure over the next 18 months if the 40+ retrieval tools convert regulatory deadlines into paid implementations before reliability work consumes the seed. Hubble could then become default-alive through implementation revenue and recurring data-access fees, with each Epic or payer workflow lowering the cost of the next deployment. The opposing mechanism is founder-drainage: browser automation, voice retrieval, consent controls, normalization, and per-call auditing across fragmented systems create an integration-services treadmill, while Hubble’s inferred demo-led B2B model and absent revenue evidence provide no downside cushion. Hubble is more venture-shaped than generic vertical SaaS, but less structurally advantaged than Teamshares or Marina Mogilko’s cash-flow-backed instruments.

Prabha Dublish was Grow Therapy’s first product hire, and Aaron Leon built EHR and AI infrastructure serving more than 1 million patients at Amazon One Medical; Hubble therefore has real pre-pitch domain commitment rather than two founders discovering interoperability during YC. Hubble still lacks the public writing, three-year independent obsession, or dismissed non-obvious belief that would move asymmetric conviction into the top tier. The score flips upward if Dublish can show that Hubble began as a manual records-retrieval operation with repeat paying users—or publish a specific argument explaining why HIN- and FHIR-first vendors cannot solve patient-mediated access. Without that evidence, Hubble has excellent résumés and plausible missionary temperament, not yet visible asymmetric conviction.

John Fish / Bookshelved is the clean parallel for owned distribution because the audience existed before the financing and was part of the asset being purchased. Hubble has one modest signal—Dublish’s 5,198 LinkedIn followers—but Hubble shows no newsletter, practitioner community, patient channel, or organically acquired user base comparable to John Fish’s creator distribution. Founder-led demos are sales activity, not owned distribution. Hubble therefore carries a normal enterprise acquisition burden, and the founder-as-brand asset is not meaningfully on the cap table.

Health Gorilla has raised more than $80 million and already holds QHIN/QHIO designations, so my falsifiable 36-month forecast is that by July 2029 Hubble is acquired by Datavant for less than $100 million after building a useful patient-mediated retrieval and workflow layer but failing to become the independent national network. Hubble’s category fit supplies strategic value; Hubble’s absent proprietary dataset, undisclosed coverage completeness, and established-platform substitution risk cap standalone leverage. That outcome could reward founders and still make an oversubscribed pre-seed SAFE an unremarkable angel return—the distinction between predicting a business and underwriting a round.

Aaron Leon’s experience leading 50+ engineers and building infrastructure for 1 million-plus patients is the strongest bull case against my forecast: Hubble could be the Ro exception, where regulatory schleps and capital intensity become the moat, while Dublish’s Grow Therapy experience supplies the distribution map. Hubble’s 40+ live production tools make that case sting because the product is already more than a slide. The evidence that wins today is still structural: Hubble reports no revenue, signed customer, pricing, proprietary data advantage, or breadth benchmark, yet the financing is already oversubscribed and competitors have collectively raised hundreds of millions. I would flip to Invest if Hubble documents at least three paying enterprise customers, 80% or better retrieval completion across their requested records, and entry terms below the Summer 2026 batch median; that combination would establish a Ro-like regulatory moat without asking me to pay for consensus before it exists.
