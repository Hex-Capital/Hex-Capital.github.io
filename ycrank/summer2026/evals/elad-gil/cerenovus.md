# Cerenovus -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: agent-ready enterprise context now lets CEOs, CFOs, private-equity firms, and advisers investigate fragmented operating data, but Cerenovus becomes large only if its findings produce repeatable, auditable financial value that exceeds the cost and friction of data access. The dossier never supplies that controlling threshold. Twenty-six investigative sweeps, impact-ranked findings, and assigned owners describe a mechanism, but there is no measured recovery, deployment time, accuracy rate, renewal, or expansion evidence showing that the mechanism works economically.

The strongest case is that this is already more than a search wrapper. Cerenovus resolves entities across ERP, CRM, accounting, email, contracts, and chat; preserves source permissions and contradictions; links findings to original evidence; and retains reviewer decisions and outcome records. Better models should improve the investigations while the company continues to own customer-specific state and the loop from diagnosis to remediation. That is the company’s best signal and the dimension on which it is furthest ahead.

The problem is market structure. Google Cloud introduced automatic enterprise entity-and-relationship extraction in April 2026, but the dossier does not quantify how this change lowers deployment cost, increases accuracy, or converts previously uneconomic analysis into attractive software margins. Meanwhile, Glean has more than $100 million in ARR and connections to 275-plus tools; Coworker already markets permission-aware organizational memory; Hebbia performs cited analysis; and Workday acquired Sana. This is a crystallizing category in which well-capitalized vendors can bundle much of the enabling infrastructure. Cerenovus must own the operational outcome, not merely a better investigation interface.

Live pilots are directionally useful but weakly specified. There is no current-product revenue, named customer, repeat-usage measure, deployment duration, or evidence that a pilot expanded after producing a finding. Compendium’s 122 Product Hunt points and #16 daily rank belong to a $40-per-seat collaboration product launched in July 2026, not validation of the enterprise diagnostic product now being sold through custom demos. The rapid repositioning may represent useful learning, but it also makes current product-market pull difficult to distinguish from exploratory deployment.

The bull case is that five technically strong founders have stayed lean, converted an internal information problem into a working cross-system product, and reached pilots before the story was polished. If those pilots show that Cerenovus repeatedly finds six-figure operational leakage, the persisted mappings and outcome history could become a valuable workflow asset and a distribution channel into monitoring and remediation. But the claimed proprietary data has no stated reproduction cost, the founders lack documented enterprise-software or operating-transformation experience, and no known-exception override applies: this is neither scarce frontier research nor a demonstrated reconstruction of incumbent unit economics.

My 36-month base case is that Cerenovus narrows into private-equity diligence and portfolio-operations diagnostics, remaining below $3 million ARR rather than becoming a horizontal enterprise knowledge platform. I would pass today. The flip condition is three named pilot customers converting to contracts above $100,000 ACV and renewing or expanding after independently verified findings produced measurable savings; that would validate both the controlling economic variable and the owned-workflow thesis.
