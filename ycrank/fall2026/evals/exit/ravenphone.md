# Raven -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Raven’s strongest path is a private strategic acquisition by a handset maker. A buyer could deploy Raven’s cross-app agent software across its own devices and control how workflows are integrated and released. Ownership would have to deliver a measurable advantage over building or licensing similar capabilities. The prototype demonstrated actions such as flight search and calendar changes and won a People’s Choice award with $50,000 in API credits. Raven reports that test handsets reached early adopters and that more than 1,000 people contacted it. Those signals support a production pilot, while the refundable $199 pre-order and projected $99–$199 monthly price leave paid retention unproven. Hardware specifications, a commercial ship date, and funding for production remain unresolved. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The memo gives no Raven deal price, quantified buyer economics, or applicable shareholder terms. Using the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership dilutes to 3%, with no follow-on checks. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the investor, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

For the sale scenarios, assume all cash at closing, zero net debt, no other enterprise-to-equity adjustments or fees, and a simple pro rata waterfall without senior preferences. Enterprise value therefore equals distributable equity proceeds, and stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation each contribute $0 to the bridge. A modest $50 million sale in year five pays the holder $1.5 million, or 3×. A strong $100 million sale in year seven pays $3 million, or 6×, at 3% ownership and falls short at 2%. These prices are scenario assumptions. In the prolonged-private case, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and Raven reinvests its cash. The investor realizes $0, or 0×, through year eight and retains the shares. The memo leaves transfer rights for the software and essential integrations unverified and supplies no independently supported route to holder cash. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

A handset maker may already have enough of Raven’s functionality to build or license the rest at a price far below the acquisition hurdle. The Galaxy S26 already supports some multi-step tasks, and Phone Harness gives external agents control of existing phones. Raven has yet to demonstrate that its integrated experience reliably completes valuable workflows those alternatives struggle with. The memo establishes meaningful overlap but leaves full-workflow parity and the resulting price ceiling unresolved. That supports Watch. A buyer comparison that establishes an adequate, substantially cheaper substitute would trigger Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a handset maker’s head-to-head production pilot show that owning Raven creates enough incremental value to support an acquisition above the applicable proceeds hurdle? The pilot would need to measure reliable completion of recurring cross-app tasks and quantify the buyer’s rollout economics against its best build or licensing option. If that comparison supports a price that could deliver $2.5 million to the holder and identifies a credible way to transfer the essential software and integrations, move to Reach out.
