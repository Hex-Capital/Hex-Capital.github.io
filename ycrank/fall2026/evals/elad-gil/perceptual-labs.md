# Perceptual Labs -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The missing piece at Perceptual Labs is the market map: which buyer can now accomplish something economically useful because visual intelligence crossed a capability threshold? “Building the next generation of visual intelligence” does not identify that buyer, the task, or the threshold. My controlling belief would be that a specific visual workflow has become reliable and inexpensive enough to automate. The dossier provides no company-specific evidence to test that belief, so I cannot underwrite it with my own money.

The adjacent market shows substantial investment. TwelveLabs announced a $100 million Series B in July 2026; Roboflow supplies annotation, training, and deployment infrastructure. Those facts make the competitive boundary consequential, but Perceptual Labs’ undisclosed scope prevents establishing where it competes. Cheaper multimodal models and edge inference could expand adoption. They could also let existing platforms absorb the intended product. Without a measured change in task cost, accuracy, or delivery speed, the general technology wave earns no company-specific economic premium.

The strongest objection to passing is that this could be exactly when a personal angel should invest: a two-person infrastructure team pursuing an opening before its product and market become public. My pattern of investing across multiple layers of a discontinuity supports taking that possibility seriously. The research-stage exception, however, requires affirmative evidence of scarce technical talent, frontier work, and a credible capital path. Perceptual Labs supplies none of those details. The October 29, 2026 access failures explain the information gap; they do not establish either technical weakness or exceptional capability.

Workflow ownership is equally unresolved. There are no documented integrations, actions, evaluation loops, or proprietary operational state showing that better underlying models would deepen the product. The suggested exclusive visual dataset is explicitly an inference. It cannot earn moat credit without rights, performance evidence, and an estimate of reproduction cost. Conversely, the dossier does not establish that this is a disposable wrapper or that the founders make unsupported data claims. There is also no documented repeat usage, payment, prior startup learning, or costly founder commitment. Each dimension therefore receives its stated missing-evidence default, with no additional deduction for being a small pre-seed team.

I pass on the available evidence. The decisive market gate remains unproven, and the technical-team exception cannot be invoked from undisclosed biographies. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: a named customer’s 90-day deployment record showing repeated paid completion of a defined visual workflow at least 50% below its previous total cost, including inference and human review. That would provide a concrete mechanism to investigate and evidence that someone wants the result.
