# Kanopy -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Kanopy has a seed-stage case for **Industry enabled by superintelligence**. Utility vegetation teams rely on arborist patrols, manual surveys, and repeat visits, sometimes working from field data that is years old. Kanopy reconstructs 3D clearance measurements from ordinary video. The investment scenario is that utilities could refresh clearance data on corridors more often than manual surveys allow, assuming video analysis reduces field labor per accepted mile and utilities can limit follow-up visits to exceptions. Kanopy reports 3,093 cumulative miles mapped, including more than 300 miles across four states in two weeks. That supports the feasibility of collecting and processing footage across multiple sites. The memo provides no evidence of a utility sustaining a higher inspection cadence or of the fully loaded cost at that cadence. Even with comparable models available, buyers would still need corridor footage, validated measurements, and results their crews can use. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Kanopy scores zero on **Building blocks of superintelligence**. Its product addresses vegetation inspection on existing utility networks. The memo identifies no AI workload whose deployment is limited by vegetation inspection capacity or whose access to scarce power would improve through Kanopy’s product. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

ACRT’s Arborcision launch establishes a contractor distribution channel. Kanopy’s hosted reconstruction and credentialed SDK give it a way to embed results in utility workflows, with switching costs still unproven. The reported six LOIs or master service agreements cover 270,000 potential miles, with committed order volumes and paid repeat use unverified. The shipped SDK, reported mapping work, and founders’ utility and computer vision experience support early execution. The memo gives no price or delivery cost per accepted mile, so scale economics receives the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Kanopy may speed up an existing inspection workflow while leaving its cadence unchanged. SCE’s remote-sensing plan calls for comparison against field observations and retains ground inspection where remote methods fall short. If utilities still need routine in-person verification, video analysis may add a step without freeing enough labor to fund additional surveys. Overlapping vegetation and inspection products also give buyers alternatives. The memo establishes no failure of Kanopy’s measurements, so this risk supports **Watch**. Evidence that the workflow only improves existing surveys at the same cadence would zero the industry path and trigger **Pass**. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a utility use Kanopy to fund a recurring clearance survey at a cadence it previously could not afford? A paid deployment on representative corridors would answer this if it meets the utility’s field-validated accuracy threshold, measures fully loaded cost per accepted mile including capture, review, and repeat visits, and leads the utility to order another survey at the higher cadence. That would move the action to **Reach out**. A representative test showing that routine field verification keeps the higher cadence uneconomic would move it to **Pass**. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
