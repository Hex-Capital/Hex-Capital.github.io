# Async -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Two named law-firm deployments make Async a single-miracle implementation business with no index-like distribution position. Async assumes that current models can execute cross-application workflows with approval logic and company memory; the remaining work is productization and GTM, not new science. But Async acquires customers through consultative audits and custom agreements, so category growth does not automatically compound into distribution. That high-single-miracle, low-index pairing describes a potentially useful managed-service business, not yet an index on SMB operations.

**Color Genomics** is the high rung on the single-miracle ladder: workflow and data infrastructure built on commodity sequencing rather than a bet on new chemistry. Async shares that mechanism by building deployment, memory, and approval infrastructure on Claude and other external models rather than training a foundation model. Async diverges because sequencing was already a dependable substrate, while long-horizon agents still require reliable execution, economical outcome pricing, and repeatable integrations simultaneously. I count one primary miracle—dependable cross-system agency—but the unresolved delivery economics keep Async well below the Color pattern.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism on index position: capturing share against better-positioned platforms rather than benefiting automatically from category growth. Async faces Relevance AI, Gumloop, and Anthropic’s May 2026 small-business package, yet Async has no proprietary rail, structural barrier, or demonstrated data moat. The hands-on audit may win accounts, but every Async deployment begins with direct selling and customer-specific labor. Async therefore looks more like an agency competing inside the agent wave than a Stripe-like default that compounds with it.

**Anthropic’s May 2026 release** of 15 SMB workflows and Gumloop’s claim that the prior six months of model improvements enabled integrated agents provide Async with a real recent catalyst. Over the next 18 months, improving models should expand the workflows Async can automate and reduce exception-handling costs. The same mechanism also strengthens Anthropic and Gumloop, so the catalyst reopens demand without reserving the window for Async. Async is skating for the puck in a newly viable market, but the catalyst currently validates the category more strongly than the specific position.

**Clifton Law and the Law Office of Joshua Lowenthal** show that Async has moved beyond a roadmap into deployed intake, conflict-check, engagement-letter, research, and email workflows. The counterfactual is straightforward: if Async disclosed several customers entering paid expansions, revenue compounding above 20% month over month, and stable gross margins, the revenue-velocity score would move into invest territory. Async instead publishes no price, ARR, retention, or usage curve, and outcome pricing can conceal enterprise-pilot tourism. Two named deployments are meaningful at pre-seed, but they do not yet establish that Async is “just working.”

**Perplexity** is the relevant founder parallel because Aravind Srinivas repeatedly shipped discussed features between meetings. Ben Smith and Connor Park have shipped nonprofit fundraising agents, Skope billing software, legal agents, and now Async, which is better evidence than credentials alone. Async’s sequence demonstrates output and willingness to iterate, but it could equally indicate unresolved product selection: Product Hunt reached only #13 with 132 points, and no repeat-usage loop or scaled prior product is documented. Async earns credit for shipping artifacts, not yet for Perplexity-like iteration converging on unmistakable demand.

**Async’s one-or-two-workflow entry model** leads me to predict that by July 2029 Async will have narrowed primarily to managed legal operations, reached roughly $2M–$5M ARR, and remained an independent services-heavy vendor rather than becoming a broad SMB agent platform. Async already has two law-firm stories, while no comparable healthcare or real-estate customers are named. Custom audits, integrations, and outcome agreements should produce revenue, but without a standardized distribution layer they should also pull Async toward the vertical where implementation knowledge is deepest.

**Color Genomics** supplies the strongest bull case against that forecast: services and workflow implementation can be the wedge that creates proprietary memory, action history, and switching costs on top of a commoditized technical layer. Async’s named law-firm deployments and repeated pivots could represent rapid discovery, not thrashing, and outcome pricing could accelerate adoption by removing seat-based procurement friction. The dossier currently loses that argument because Async shows no retention, expansion, proprietary-data evidence, or declining deployment labor, while Anthropic directly overlaps its workflows. I would flip to Invest if Async demonstrates at least 20% monthly recurring-revenue growth for six months alongside repeatable deployments above 70% gross margin; until then, I am neutral and would not write the check.
