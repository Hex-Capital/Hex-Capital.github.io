# Runtime.si -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit with an initial group of robot builders or deployers, Runtime could serve the same recurring job across markets where robots need to adapt to changing manipulation tasks. RoboSkillHub documents reusable manipulation components and records which have been verified on physical hardware. That supports some product reuse. How much work it takes to transfer the current policy across robot platforms and production sites remains uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The observed acquisition path reaches a direct early-access signup. Runtime says access opens in January. The memo gives no usable evidence on how a typical buyer would pay, connect a robot, or complete a first task, so acquisition and onboarding stays at the default. The policy's proposed role in the control loop gives buyers a reason to keep using it as tasks change. A recurring software license and higher spending as buyers add robots are scenario assumptions. Current billing, renewals, and paid expansion remain unknown. (The Idea)

The five-person team launched the current policy on November 4. Launch votes and views show attention. Runtime claims it matched or exceeded unnamed models on tasks including rope threading and egg grasping, with trial counts and success rates undisclosed. Commercial traction stays at the default because the memo leaves the current policy's paid use and revenue status unknown. The Lambda pilot references concern an earlier product. (Runtime.si, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

If Runtime charged a recurring license per production cell, incremental contribution would be that fee less any simulation compute Runtime bears and the engineering and support time needed to activate and serve the cell. Runtime says it is accelerating the planning loop. The memo gives neither a price nor measured deployment or compute costs, so cost economics stays at the default. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Even with strong demand, live simulation search could add too much latency, and differences between simulated and physical contact could require engineers to tune and support each installation. If that work recurs at every site, deployment capacity and cost could rise alongside revenue. The memo identifies those risks, while the commercial integration burden is inferred partly from a competitor's experience. It establishes neither unavoidable bespoke work for Runtime nor a requirement to rebuild the product by market, so the structural downside gate stays open. (Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can two independent buyers in different markets use the same documented integration to bring paid production cells online and then add paid cells with little Runtime engineering time? Deployment logs, recurring invoices, and measured compute and support costs would show whether the motion repeats at positive incremental contribution. That result would support Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 and confidence reaches Medium. Evidence of unavoidable site-by-site engineering that makes faster growth economically infeasible would trigger Pass.
