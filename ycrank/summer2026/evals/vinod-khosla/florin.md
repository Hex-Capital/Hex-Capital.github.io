# Florin -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

A one-minute insurance quote is useful. It is not yet a 100x consequence. Florin points to a $911.7 billion commercial-insurance market and genuinely underserved robotics, space, defense, and advanced-energy companies, but the dossier never quantifies how many firms are denied coverage, how much capital is stranded, or what societal output becomes possible if underwriting improves. A week-to-minute reduction changes workflow speed; it does not by itself establish an order-of-magnitude change in insurance cost or physical-technology deployment.

The technical claim is audacious: read ACORD forms, combine permits, satellite imagery, public records, and social media, simulate tens of thousands of policy years, and bind with no underwriter. But the experiment has not produced a disclosed accuracy benchmark, policy cohort, loss ratio, or claims result. Coterie already advertises sub-one-minute binding, while Corgi, Shepherd, and Nirvana have collectively helped put more than $800 million behind automated commercial underwriting. Florin must prove superior risk selection, not merely a cleaner interface. Faster bad pricing destroys a carrier faster than slow underwriting does.

The largest kill risk is not whether the model can parse forms. It is whether three founders can price unfamiliar physical risks without adverse selection while satisfying licensing, capital, reinsurance, governance, and anti-discrimination requirements. Florin discloses no carrier authority, capacity provider, actuarial validation, or reinsurance structure. Soliciting coverage requests is not evidence against those risks. The cheapest decisive milestone would be a bounded policy cohort with human-shadow pricing and explicit loss-ratio and exception-rate thresholds; no such risk-removal plan is visible.

The team is the real option. Shaurya Aggarwal built TPUs and encryption deployed across more than 100,000 hosts. Amol Pant worked across Tesla Autopilot compute, Orchard Robotics perception, NASA rover prototypes, and Boeing simulators. Aydin Sorensen reports helping scale TerraFirma revenue from $500,000 to $30 million while supporting more than 50 vehicles. That is unusually relevant systems talent for evaluating complex physical businesses. But the three are connected through school, college, and shared housing, and Florin has not yet recruited the actuarial, regulatory, or carrier operator who owns its most unfamiliar risks. The recent banking-to-insurance pivot could demonstrate fast belief revision; without the evidence that caused it, it could equally be category shopping.

The bull case is substantial: a technically strong team enters a giant existing value pool, serves customers poorly represented by historical class codes, and turns every bound policy and claim into proprietary risk data. Execution-heavy fintech can become important without inventing new science, and a mutable route can be an asset. The exception does not apply yet. A large TAM plus strong engineers is insufficient when funded competitors already automate underwriting and Florin has neither a proprietary learning loop in operation nor proof that zero-underwriter pricing survives economic and regulatory reality.

My expectation is that within 36 months Florin will have pivoted from a zero-underwriter full-stack carrier into an MGA or underwriting-software product for hard-technology brokers. That can become a competent business, but it is not presently the one-sided, world-changing option I would fund with my own money. One condition flips the decision: a verifiable 12-month cohort of at least 1,000 bound policies showing loss performance no worse than an appropriate incumbent benchmark while at least 95% of submissions receive no human underwriting touch. Until then: pass.
