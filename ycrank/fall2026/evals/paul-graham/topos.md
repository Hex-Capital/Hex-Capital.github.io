# Topos -- Paul Graham Evaluation

What did these founders do when something broke? Topos’s dossier does not contain that story. It contains two people who have built things together, including Felt and a computational prime-progression project credited to both in September 2026. That is useful evidence of shared execution. But it does not identify an obstacle, their workaround, or how quickly the workaround changed anything. I would not turn Harvard mathematics, a spelling championship, or a powerlifting record into evidence of resourcefulness.

The public mathematics code is the most interesting affirmative signal. It gives their technical ambition an artifact, rather than leaving us with biographies. I give it limited credit because the dossier supplies neither a measured result from that project nor a functioning Topos verifier. The decisive technical question is simple: what can their system reject that an ordinary scientific agent would confidently accept? Agents agreeing with one another do not establish correctness. A formal checker could establish that a conclusion follows from assumptions. Whether those assumptions describe biology remains a separate problem.

The strongest argument for investing is that these founders may be unusually well placed to bridge that gap. Shin has Dana-Farber experience; Sukhatankar researched seizure-localization models at Boston Children’s Hospital. Their mathematical collaboration makes the combination more credible than a generic proposal to apply agents to science. This could become the sort of difficult technical substrate I like funding before its category is obvious. But the dossier does not show that they encountered a particular failure in their research and built Topos to fix it. Relevant experience supports the possibility of an organic insight; it does not establish one.

I would look for one laboratory that needs a crude version desperately. None is identified here. YC participation is not user pull, and neither revenue nor repeat usage is disclosed. Edison already offers scientific analysis and experimental-planning workflows, so a researcher needs a concrete reason to add Topos: a consequential error caught, or a check that makes an existing task substantially easier. Harmonic’s reported 96.8% code-verification result establishes neither Topos’s capability nor biological applicability. The business model is undisclosed, which leaves the buyer uncertain; it does not establish bad economics.

The founders’ departure from Harvard is not itself a red flag. Their existing collaboration also weakens the concern about a team assembled merely to apply to YC. There is no documented dependence on a large partner or refusal to change direction. Equally, there is no evidence-driven product revision to reward. I checked the exceptions for working prototypes and independently validated science: Topos has not supplied the proof needed for either override. Its resemblance to my pattern of backing technical primitives is promising, but the proprietary substrate and actual developer use are still unestablished.

I would pass at present. The missing founder-action evidence leaves my principal investment gate unmet. One verifiable event could change that: a documented laboratory pilot where the founders encounter a specific verification failure, ship a workaround within two weeks, and the researcher independently checks the result and returns to use it again. That would connect the people, the technical claim, and the need. Today those remain three plausible propositions.
