# Agent 37 -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition is Agent 37’s strongest supported route to holder liquidity. Its hosted API combines isolated sandboxes, persistent agent state, and per-customer provisioning. Boon’s case study describes an integration into its provisioning and scheduled-run workflows. Boon reports cutting monthly cost per agent from $85 to $4.94, and ETEC+ reports moving more than 30 clients to its branded agent service. A developer infrastructure platform could acquire Agent 37 to add persistent agent hosting and bring reseller workloads onto its platform. The testimonials establish a reason customers adopt the product. The buyer’s economic benefit remains an inference. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The milestone is sustained, gross-margin-positive usage across several paying production resellers. ETEC+’s rollout shows that Agent 37 can migrate an existing client base. Agent 37 says it powers agents for more than 250 startups, though the memo gives no paid cohort retention, gross margins, or confirmed runway for reaching the milestone. It also leaves IP ownership, contract assignment, and holder participation terms unverified. There is no evidenced independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross return within eight years requires $2.5 million in cash, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale scenarios below assume an eligible holder sells its entire stake for cash within eight years, with no senior preferences or deferred consideration. The prices and deductions are illustrative assumptions. The memo supplies no buyer valuation or actual waterfall. (Key Facts, Market & Traction)

| Scenario | Bridge to distributable equity | Cash received by holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments = $35M | $1.05M, or 2.1× |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in assumed net debt and deal adjustments = $90M | $2.70M, or 5.4×. Selling 80% of the stake at closing would yield $2.16M, or 4.32×. |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share purchase or funded payout within eight years | $0 realized |

### Countercase

A platform buyer could build or source the sandbox layer and replicate the packaging. AWS already offers isolated agent sessions, Boon previously built its own provisioning pipeline, and Agent 37 attributes part of its cost advantage to commercially rented servers and shared host resources. The founder-reported average of $0.37 per Cloud sandbox per month leaves buyer economics highly dependent on retained workload volume and margins, both unquantified. This weakens the case for a price that clears the holder hurdle. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a credible ceiling on proceeds. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an infrastructure platform buyer justify a purchase price that leaves at least $83.3 million distributable to equity holders within eight years? A buyer’s build-versus-buy analysis grounded in retained, gross-margin-positive reseller usage would move the action to Reach out if it supports that price after modeled deal deductions and senior claims.
