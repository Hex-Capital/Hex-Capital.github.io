# Allia Health -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequential business is not the $1.56 billion mental-health EHR market. It is the possibility that one operating system coordinates fragmented practices, measures outcomes, and reallocates a much larger behavioral-care cost pool through value-based contracts. Allia Health already touches 300,000 patients and nearly one million monthly visits, but the dossier quantifies only six clinician-hours saved weekly and $50 million of contracted care capacity. That is useful leverage, not yet evidence of a 10x change in care access, outcomes, or cost.

Execution is unusually strong for pre-seed. Eight thousand clinicians across 1,000 practices use the platform daily; 600 clinicians at 70 locations in 32 states have been activated under signed national payer contracts. Those facts are substantially more informative than app downloads or social following. The founders have assembled technical, healthcare, and clinical perspectives, and Saroosh Khan’s reported 94.2% ARC-AGI-2 result suggests technical ability. But the dossier documents neither a material belief revised after evidence nor a difficult risk-specific recruit, so I cannot infer exceptional learning rate from credentials alone.

The product does not clear the Black-Swan technical test. Six hours saved per clinician per week is promising, but there is no method, baseline workflow, outcome comparison, or evidence that Allia is 5–10x better than Headway, Grow Therapy, or Alma. Headway already offers a free AI-assisted insurance-native EHR with $327.5 million behind it. Combining EHR, billing, referrals, prescribing, and payer contracting may create an effective distribution machine, but integration labor is not a technical discontinuity. The prospective claims-and-outcomes learning loop remains a hypothesis until contracted care begins.

The strongest objection to passing is the **Block** exception: a non-scientific initial wedge can evolve into infrastructure when a small team has entered a giant existing value pool. Allia shares that platform-shaped route and has far more early network activation than most pre-seed companies. The material difference is that Block’s expanding product surface created direct transaction economics, while Allia has not disclosed revenue, payer rates, medical-loss exposure, contribution margin, or whether improved outcomes accrue economically to Allia. Free software plus healthcare consolidation can accumulate volume while destroying capital.

Capital should now attack contract realization, not add features or locations. The September 2026 care launch is a concrete milestone, but the dossier supplies no numerical outcome threshold, experiment cost, stop condition, or branch if claims economics fail. SOC 2 and HITRUST were still in progress while the system handled protected health information, and operating across 32 states compounds compliance risk before the value-based model has been demonstrated. This is the execution-heavy healthcare-network pattern I sometimes fund, but it is also precisely where scale can conceal the largest unresolved risk.

My 36-month forecast is that Allia reaches $15–30 million in annual care revenue by August 2029 but remains better described as a provider-network consolidator than a defensible technical platform. I pass today because the downside is not merely one small technical experiment failing; it is financing a nationwide care operation without proof that clinical outcomes and unit economics improve together. One verifiable condition flips the decision: a national payer renews a live Allia cohort after twelve months at equal or better rates based on audited outcome improvements, while that cohort produces positive contribution margin without subsidizing the free EHR.
