# Markov -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The impressive fact is not the founders’ résumés. It is that two young founders have already assembled 48,478 videos covering roughly 12,300 hours of professional-software use, then built an AutoCAD environment that retains files, screenshots, model turns, and input events. This is a working technical substrate, not a diagram of one. The 17,814 Hugging Face downloads in the preceding month also suggest that computer-use researchers want the raw material now. These are fierce-nerd signals.

But the dossier never says what broke. There is no named customer obstacle, dated workaround, or result produced by the founders’ response. The large corpus demonstrates output, but it does not prove relentless resourcefulness under resistance. The AutoCAD benchmark even exposes a useful unsolved problem: its evaluator can pass visually similar but semantically wrong geometry. What did Mandal and Ashok do after discovering that? Without the next action, I cannot tell whether this is an obstacle they are attacking or merely a limitation they have documented.

The demand evidence is similarly promising but incomplete. Markov has a narrow user type—developers training computer-use agents—and 150,000 cumulative downloads are much better than a waitlist. Yet downloads and 180 Hugging Face likes do not show that a lab repeatedly used the data, improved a model, paid for an application-specific environment, or returned for another project. The public corpus may be attracting researchers while the proposed contracted-data business attracts nobody. That distinction is decisive because Scale AI, Mercor, Turing, and Invisible already possess buyer relationships and much larger operating networks.

The strongest bull case is that the corpus itself is the pre-permission proof spike I often look for. Two founders built 856 GB of usable material before institutional backing, distributed it through the channel their users already inhabit, and received measurable adoption. Their prior AI, GUI-agent, robotics, and tool-automation work also makes this less like a manufactured “AI-for-X” idea. Extreme youth therefore is not disqualifying. But the documented exception for young founders requires more than a large artifact: it requires validated technical superiority and direct exposure to the problem. Markov has neither an independently measured model improvement nor a contemporaneous account of why these founders personally needed this dataset.

The schlep could become the company. Reliable environments, licensed demonstrations, application-specific evaluators, and gold outputs are harder than scraping screen recordings. But the flagship corpus’s internet-sourced provenance creates chain-of-title risk, and synthetic augmentation has already produced results from only 312 human trajectories. If raw trajectories commoditize, Markov must become the system that creates auditable environments and measures semantic success. My 36-month prediction is that it will make that pivot and reach roughly $500,000–$2 million ARR, rather than become a large standalone raw-data supplier.

I would not write the check today. The operational gate fails because the dossier contains no founder response to a named blocker, and user pull remains short of repeated use or payment. The one condition that would flip me is a named AI lab renewing a paid Markov environment contract after the founders ship a documented workaround to a failed evaluator or data-quality requirement. That single event would show both the running back and the narrow well.
