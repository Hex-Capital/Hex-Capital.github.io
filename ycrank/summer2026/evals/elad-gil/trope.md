# Trope -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Trope’s 366-commit desktop-control layer makes Trope a technically credible, single-miracle execution bet whose weakest signal is the absence of any current-product revenue curve. Trope looks like workflow infrastructure on top of Business Central, not a science project, but the July 2026 repositioning from workflow guidance to ERP implementation means I cannot tell whether Trope is “just working” or merely capable of working.

**Color Genomics** is the high rung on single-miracle architecture: workflow and data infrastructure captured value on top of already-commoditized sequencing. Trope shares that mechanism through an in-house agent harness and custom Business Central APIs layered over Microsoft’s validated ERP rather than attempting to replace the ledger. Trope diverges because Color’s underlying technical layer was stable, while Trope must still prove that financial-workflow agents can execute reliably and that principal-consultant approvals do not turn fixed-price delivery into an ordinary labor business. That leaves Trope between the workflow-layer high rung and a multi-dependency services model.

**Mistral’s** low-rung weakness on visible revenue was a pre-revenue, capital-intensive profile that only cleared because ChatGPT had reopened the market and its founders had shipped Llama at Meta. Trope discloses fixed-price implementations and separate managed-agent contracts but no current dollar revenue, signed customer, retention, or growth rate; the nonprofit’s 2–3x speed and 90% error reduction belong to Trope’s prior workflow-guidance product. For current-product revenue velocity, there is no evidence—applying the 8/25 absent-evidence default. Unlike Mistral, Trope does not yet have a catalyst or shipping credential powerful enough to make that missing curve irrelevant.

**Microsoft’s September 2024 GP end-of-support announcement** creates real migration demand, while the February 2026 Business Central agent-designer preview creates the automation surface Trope uses. The counterfactual that would move Trope into the top catalyst tier is a compressed migration deadline or a Microsoft channel mandate that makes thousands of GP customers buy within 24 months. The actual December 2029 deadline is distant, and Microsoft’s agent toolkit simultaneously arms Trope’s SI competitors, so Trope is skating for the puck but not into an Anduril-after-Ukraine discontinuity.

**Perplexity** is the relevant founder parallel because Aravind Srinivas repeatedly converted conversations into shipped artifacts. Matthew Chow and Victor Vannara have produced Trope’s 366-commit CUA repository, and Trope’s earlier product reached a nonprofit system serving 400+ staff; those are substantially better signals than ex-Tesla and ex-Amazon titles alone. Trope still lacks evidence that Chow or Vannara has iterated through multiple ERP deployments, and the claimed 200+ implementations belong to unnamed principal consultants rather than either founder. The founders ship, but Trope has not yet demonstrated the Perplexity cadence inside the market being sold.

**The $2.3B Business Central consulting-services market** can compound for Trope if every migration generates reusable mappings, tests, reconciliation logic, and recurring managed-agent revenue. Over 18 months, Trope’s white-label program could turn SIs and VARs into distribution, making Trope an index on GP-to-Business-Central migration rather than another consultancy acquiring projects one at a time. The opposite loop is equally plausible: Microsoft’s custom-agent designer lets each partner reproduce the automation, while fixed-price implementations leave Trope carrying delivery risk. With no disclosed deployment volume or partner-driven bookings, Trope has a potential index position, not an established one.

**No current-product dollar revenue is disclosed**, so my falsifiable 36-month forecast is that by July 2029 Trope reaches $2M–$5M ARR, with most revenue still coming from implementation and migration services rather than managed agents. Trope’s single-miracle architecture should make delivery faster, but Trope’s weak usage curve and Microsoft-controlled distribution should keep the business closer to a technology-enabled Dynamics consultancy than a Stripe-like index on ERP modernization.

**One nonprofit deployment serving 400+ staff** supplies the strongest bull case: Trope already reported 2–3x faster completion and 90% fewer errors, the founders have shipped 366 commits, and Color Genomics shows that workflow infrastructure can capture a hard category without owning its underlying technology. That bull mechanism would let Trope use fixed-price projects as paid data acquisition, then convert accumulated exception histories into high-margin managed agents. The dossier still assigns those results to Trope’s prior product, names no current customer, and shows Microsoft distributing the same agent-building primitives to incumbent partners. I would flip to Invest if Trope verifies three consecutive months above 20% current-product revenue growth, with at least half of new ARR coming through named SI or VAR partners.
