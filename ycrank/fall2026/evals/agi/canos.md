# Canos -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New Economies.** Canos could make repeat cancer screening practical for owners of asymptomatic dogs who currently go without it. Routine bloodwork rarely identifies cancer, and advanced imaging is costly and requires sedation. Canos uses one veterinary blood draw and lists Guardian at $299 per test. Canos reported 90.6% sensitivity at 95.1% specificity in retrospective cross-validation on 303 dogs. That supports seed-stage assay feasibility. The market-opening test is whether the blood-draw workflow reduces labor and full cost per confirmed, actionable early detection after accounting for collection, sequencing, interpretation, redraws, and follow-up. The memo gives no comparative cost or labor measurements. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Under cheap, equally capable models, clinics could still pay Canos for reliable sample-to-result execution and continuity across serial tests. Canos reports a longitudinal analysis of 117 dogs and offers collection kits. Those are early signs of a repeat-testing workflow. Its advantage depends on whether the workflow improves clinical decisions or saves clinics work compared with an equivalent service. The founder-reported patent filing leaves enforceable protection unresolved. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Canos reports 10 clinics and mobile vets onboarding, an early demand signal with paid and repeat use still unverified. The founders’ prior veterinary and computational pathology work, together with the current benchmark, supports their ability to run the next validation step. Each additional test provides a plausible unit of expansion at the listed price. Realized contribution remains unknown because collection, sequencing, and exception costs are unreported. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Canos could add a paid screening step while sending enough dogs into costly follow-up to erase the delivery savings. In an illustrative scenario assuming 2% cancer prevalence and that the reported sensitivity and specificity hold prospectively, 1,000 screens would yield about 18 true positives and 48 false positives. CanCan already offers sequencing-based monitoring, and VolitionRx already distributes an in-clinic blood test. Those alternatives make model-parity retention uncertain, while their equivalence for Canos’s specific screening use remains untested. Both Path B gates remain open for testing, with insufficient evidence for outreach. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a prospective, paid rollout at independent clinics show that owners whose dogs previously went unscreened will pay for repeat testing, that Canos uses less labor and costs less per confirmed actionable early detection than the usual diagnostic pathway after follow-up, and that clinics keep choosing Canos when offered comparable diagnostic performance? Evidence for all three would move the action to **Reach out**. A representative trial showing that follow-up erases the savings or that clinics can switch and retain the same outcome would close Path B and move the action to **Pass**.
