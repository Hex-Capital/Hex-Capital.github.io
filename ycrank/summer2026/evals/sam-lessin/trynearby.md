# TryNearby -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The proposed post-money valuation is unknown, the competing-term-sheet count is unknown, and the dossier does not establish that this is a genuinely scarce first check. The capital plan is equally blank: no named hypothesis, budget, deadline, or pass/fail milestone explains what the round would prove. Those are not paperwork questions. TryNearby may be a good company at one price and a terrible angel investment at another, while “hire and expand” would turn an operating business into another capital factory.

The strongest evidence is actual purchase behavior inside an owned workflow. More than 120 restaurants pay $399 monthly, implying roughly $47,900 in monthly subscription revenue; reported growth is 37% month over month, retention exceeds 90%, and owner referrals produce approximately half of new customers. Thousands of creators and more than 10,000 agent-managed texts each month generate proprietary preference, availability, travel, and rejection data. The 35 million views are potentially metric theater, but recurring restaurant payments, retention, and referrals are not. TryNearby has already shown that coordinating local creator supply can cause businesses to buy and keep buying.

The founders make the translation layer credible. Yousef Abdelfattah helped build FaZe Clan from adolescence through its public listing and brings direct creator authority and a two-million-follower audience. Obaida Albaroudi and Ahmad Ibrahim previously built Clipping Exe together; Albaroudi reportedly scaled another consumer product from $360,000 to $2 million ARR, while Ibrahim built infrastructure tracking more than 10 billion video views. TryNearby’s 120 restaurant deployments provide operating receipts on the buyer side rather than leaving restaurant access as a résumé inference. The named CEO, COO, and CTO roles reduce—but do not eliminate—the concern that three founders conceal who makes the final product and financing decisions.

The painful bear case is that this remains a geographically constrained agency with automation layered on top. Hyper and Tablevert already offer local-creator products to restaurants, TikTok and Instagram control discovery, and each new city must rebuild two-sided liquidity. The company’s two sales testimonials lack independent attribution, while 2,500 videos and 35 million views do not establish restaurant-level return on spend. The retention and referrals prove value in Southern California; they do not yet prove that creator compensation, support, and failed bookings leave attractive contribution margins or that the workflow travels without the founders’ existing network.

At the disclosed subscription price, current customers represent approximately $575,000 in annualized revenue, which is unusually substantive for pre-seed. But there is no gross margin, creator-payment structure, burn, runway, or break-even calculation. Delete the next financing and I cannot tell whether TryNearby compounds from cash or stalls when it pays to seed the next market. The adjacent $34.2 billion influencer-platform TAM does not answer that question, particularly when no local-restaurant SAM is available. A durable regional business would still be a real business; calling it venture-scale before demonstrating profitable city replication would be narrative substituting for underwriting.

The hard conversion evidence is strong enough to prevent a pass, but not strong enough to override both missing deal terms and a missing venture moment. I am neutral today. One verifiable condition flips me to Invest: a signed financing-and-budget package showing a sub-$10 million post-money first institutional round whose proceeds fund a dated second-city experiment that reaches positive contribution margin without another raise. That would establish that the price preserves convexity and that the capital reveals a discrete truth rather than merely extending growth.
