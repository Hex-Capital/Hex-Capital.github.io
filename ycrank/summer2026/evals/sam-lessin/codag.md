# Codag -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I start with three blanks: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and use of proceeds. For me, those are not administrative omissions. They determine whether Codag is a cheap first check into a disputed belief or an auctioned YC developer-tool story. With no evidence that capital is scarce and no finite experiment assigned to the round, I cannot underwrite the deal or the venture moment.

The product claim is nevertheless crisp. Codag compresses logs before coding agents consume them, preserving context and source lines while reducing token usage by 5.9–7.5× in its hosted benchmark. That is a plausible translation layer between observability infrastructure and agent workflows, and Michael Zhou has relevant receipts: infrastructure work at Okta and Shopify, a 655-star agent-workflow visualizer, and the current deterministic engine, CLI, API, and MCP server. The founder has shipped on both sides more convincingly than the company has sold across either.

The benchmark proves technical possibility, not customer value. Its 51 synthetic incidents and internally run paired evaluations produced 98–99% answer rates, but Codag reports no paid customer, production deployment, retention, or purchase caused by its distribution. Forty-five stars for `codag-drain`, 20 for `codag-cli`, and a prior product’s 655 stars are attention metrics. Moving from a free install to a $19 Pro or $499 Team subscription is the causal action that matters, and the dossier does not show it.

The bear case is especially sharp because the base implementation is MIT-licensed, adapts published Drain3 methods, and sits beside Dash0, Honeycomb, Sentry, and Cribl. Those companies already own telemetry workflows and can bundle overlapping diagnosis. Codag’s inferred future dark pool—PII-scrubbed logs improving its classifiers—is not an asset until customers actually contribute data at scale. Today, the proprietary workflow is a possibility while the open-source machinery is observable and reproducible.

The strongest bull case is that a solo infrastructure engineer has launched quickly, published reproducible artifacts, and discovered a new cost boundary created by coding agents. Five pivots can represent unusually fast contact with reality, and the prior visualizer shows Zhou can attract developers. But this does not qualify for the known-exception override: there is no production accuracy record, pilot-to-paid conversion, retention, or customer economic result comparable to the evidence required to excuse weak deal terms or AI commoditization risk.

The pricing page suggests favorable software economics, but prices are not cash economics. There is no revenue, gross margin, inference cost, burn, runway, or route to break-even without another round. I pass today. The single flip condition is three unrelated companies paying the $499 monthly Team price for at least 90 days after converting from the free CLI, while continuing to submit production logs; that would simultaneously demonstrate purchase causality, workflow ownership, and a credible base business beneath the agent narrative.
