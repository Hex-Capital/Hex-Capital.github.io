# RouterPlus -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

RouterPlus’s strongest asset is what its founders have already done. Lakshmi Narayana spent four years building Samsung’s ML-platform GPU infrastructure; the team shipped EVO Router in August 2026 and RouterPlus in October. The reported dedicated deployment that reduced a customer’s P95 latency from 4.8 seconds to under four seconds matters more than their university. It connects infrastructure knowledge to a production outcome. These founders have earned credibility in the problem. Whether their learning becomes an asset that compounds is the investment question.

Copy RouterPlus’s code tomorrow. What remains? Relationships with 40+ inference providers, according to the founder, but no demonstrated exclusivity. Permission to train on de-identified procurement requirements, but no demonstrated predictive advantage. Workload evaluation, bidding, peak testing, and backup capacity are useful services. OpenRouter already offers overlapping evaluations and dedicated deployments. RouterPlus must show that each deployment makes the next one harder for a competitor to reproduce. The dossier establishes execution before it establishes that advantage.

The strongest bull case is procurement aggregation. RouterPlus reports a largest customer running three million agent conversations daily. If substantial demand actually flows through the platform, pooling workloads could secure capacity and terms that individual buyers cannot obtain. This fits my preference for bringing fragmented suppliers into a market where prices become discoverable. But the customer’s total activity does not establish RouterPlus’s routed volume, payment, or bargaining power. Forty providers constitute supply access; they do not yet demonstrate that another customer receives a better product because an earlier customer joined.

There is room to maneuver. Production teams entering through metered inference can also buy reserved capacity using the same workload evaluations and provider-selection process. OpenRouter’s estimated $160 million annualized revenue supplies independent evidence of existing spend, although it neither belongs to RouterPlus nor sizes the adjacent opportunity. Prepaid token credits provide a direct charging mechanism. Free BYOK inference and separately contracted capacity leave the important economics unanswered: how much RouterPlus retains after paying suppliers, and whether that contribution improves with volume.

My exceptions for exceptional builders and working prototypes deserve consideration here. The August-to-October shipping sequence and production latency result make waiting for mature revenue unnecessary. They do not establish an unusual product advantage strong enough to override the missing compounding mechanism. There is no documented integrity violation or financing-pressure red flag, and the move from autoresearch to routing does not itself prove irresolution. Equally, the earlier product’s 10,000 optimized repositories cannot become marketplace adoption by assertion. Nonconfidential intake terms also create friction precisely where customers must share sensitive requirements to improve procurement.

I am neutral and would keep my money uncommitted today. RouterPlus could build a valuable procurement business, but the evidence currently supports founder capability more strongly than customer-created value. My flip condition is one auditable customer cohort showing that added pooled demand produces better provider terms for existing customers while RouterPlus retains positive contribution margin. That would turn the marketplace claim into an observed economic loop.
