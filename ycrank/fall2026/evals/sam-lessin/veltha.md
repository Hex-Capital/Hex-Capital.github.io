# Veltha -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Veltha is proposing to own the claims operation, which makes the economics more interesting than selling another adjuster tool. But what am I buying, and what does my money prove? The dossier supplies neither post-money valuation nor competing-offer count, and it names no capital-funded experiment with a deadline and a pass/fail result. Those are missing underwriting inputs, not evidence of an auction. I am passing on the documented opportunity, without pretending that financing silence tells me the founders have done something wrong.

The strongest case for Veltha is the founders’ ability to deploy software inside institutions where rules and consequences matter. Edin Citaku led a Palantir AI deployment at Germany’s federal criminal police; Roland Jaloszynski was technical lead on mortgage-underwriting automation. These are operating receipts beyond prestigious job titles. Connecting software builders with regulated operators fits my preference for people who can translate between systems. The remaining gap is specific: neither biography establishes that the founders have run workers’ compensation claims or possess privileged access to the buyers.

Operating as a TPA could close that gap. Veltha could capture the economic benefit of faster processing directly, while licensed adjusters retain responsibility for decisions. That is the bull case that makes this worth watching. The company reports reducing six hours per file to ten minutes and enabling four times the caseload per adjuster. Those are substantial claims, but the dossier supplies no customer identities, measurement methodology, or evidence that the improvement caused a purchase. The July 2026 conference produced over 50 leads; leads establish prospecting activity, not an owned distribution advantage. Nor does preparing claim files establish ownership of the resulting data.

The distinction matters because Sedgwick already combines claims operations, AI, and proprietary data through Omni, while Reserv also offers human-reviewed AI workflows. Veltha needs something customers entrust to it that competitors cannot simply obtain from the same model provider. Source-linked regulatory determinations could help earn that trust. Human approval also addresses part of my concern about automation errors compounding at scale. Neither feature yet establishes a proprietary advantage or shows what happens to costs when files require repeated review. I would not treat the AI label as an automatic rejection; I would refuse to capitalize the promised labor savings as demonstrated profit.

Delete the next financing from the plan. Public pricing, gross margin, burn, and runway are undisclosed, so I cannot tell whether this two-person team can build a self-funding administrator. That absence receives the prescribed default, rather than an invented penalty for being pre-seed. The exceptions that could override my missing price and venture-moment gates—documented conversion, cash economics, proprietary access, or unusually strong operating evidence across both systems—are not established here. The founders’ deployment experience earns credit, but it cannot also stand in for customer access and profitable claims handling.

One verifiable result would reopen this pass: a completed 90-day paid workers’ compensation engagement showing positive contribution after adjuster review, rework, and directly attributable service costs. That would test whether Veltha’s claimed processing advantage survives contact with an actual customer and produces cash. I would still need to underwrite the round price before writing my own check.
