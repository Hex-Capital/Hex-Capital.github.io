# Pomerado -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

By 2032, I can imagine giving an agent permission to handle an insurance renewal and never touching the insurer’s website myself. Pomerado is working on one necessary connection: reaching systems that have no API. But getting through a website is only part of that future. Delegated authority must remain bounded, integrations must survive changes, and someone must own recovery when an action fails. The dossier establishes a present integration problem; it does not yet establish the path through those transitions. I would not confuse a useful connection with ownership of what comes next.

Why does the integration problem exist? The customer’s workflow crosses websites whose owners have not supplied usable APIs, leaving developers to build and maintain the missing connections. Pomerado could return engineering time and let smaller teams deploy services that otherwise require bespoke integration work. That is a concrete benefit. But the dossier identifies neither a quantified integration burden nor a gatekeeper collecting a specific rent. Sometimes a missing API is an artificial restriction; sometimes it reflects difficult authentication and operating requirements. The terms acknowledging interruptions and offering commercially reasonable repairs suggest the founders understand that distinction.

Akshay Manglik gives me more confidence than the category does. Building enterprise agents at Scale AI, including Mayo Clinic deployments, puts him close to the operating problem. His agent-trace diagnostics work is relevant evidence of curiosity about where these systems break. Alan Ma adds enterprise data and AI strategy experience, and their earlier SafeSip collaboration establishes that this partnership predates Pomerado. Those are useful beginnings. They do not yet show years of independently pursued obsession, exceptional recruiting, or repeated commercial execution. I see relevant experience rather than a founder story that makes this particular company feel inevitable.

The strongest argument against my hesitation is that Pomerado fits my quieter infrastructure pattern: repair something irritating underneath a much more visible wave. Its exploration, testing, separate review model, and saved execution could make agents practical in workflows where repeatedly asking a model to navigate is too expensive or unreliable. Open source does not erase that opportunity; trusted hosting, authentication, and maintenance can be valuable businesses. But Browserbase, Browser Use, and Skyvern already overlap with script generation or replay. Pomerado controls its software, not the websites that determine whether its integrations keep working. The scarce asset could become accumulated maintenance knowledge, but the dossier does not establish that asset yet.

I want to find the person who has rearranged work around this product. The 1.1k GitHub stars, 100 forks, and October 16 Product Hunt ranking show attention and some developer effort; they do not establish repeated production use. A $50 monthly plan is an offer, not evidence that someone pays. My pre-revenue exception at **CosmicBrain AI** rested on a functioning prototype, signed industrial proofs of concept, and a lifelong robotics record. Pomerado shares the enabling-software ambition, but the dossier lacks comparable customer commitments and founder history. That exception does not carry this decision. Authenticated actions and optional credential storage also make consent and custody consequential: the separate-agreement requirement for HIPAA data acknowledges the boundary without demonstrating the limiting architecture I would need to inspect.

I pass with my own money today. This is a credible team addressing real friction, but I cannot yet distinguish a durable enabling business from useful tooling whose maintenance obligations grow faster than its advantage. My one flip condition is a referenceable customer deployment operating for 90 consecutive days, with logs showing repeated authorized work and successful recovery through an actual website change. That would turn “we generate integrations” into evidence that someone can entrust part of their working life to Pomerado.
