# Dialogus -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot underwrite the first check because the dossier does not disclose the post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, or whether Dialogus is raising scarce capital rather than running a YC auction. More importantly, it never says what one finite experiment the round buys. “Hire, integrate, and sell more” would be a capital factory; “by March 2027, one named contact center reaches a specified autonomous-resolution rate without increasing error costs” would be a venture moment. Dialogus has not supplied the second formulation.

The strongest evidence is already inside customer workflows. Thousands of production calls for Fortune 500 enterprises—and named deployments serving Papa Johns, KFC, and Totalplay—are materially better than launch votes, followers, or synthetic demos. The system recognizes callers, reads enterprise records, executes constrained actions, and transfers exceptions with context. That proves operational access and real usage. It does not yet prove that proprietary access causes purchases or retention: no contract value, renewal, call growth, or pilot-to-paid conversion is disclosed.

There is a potentially valuable translation layer between probabilistic voice models and enterprises that require deterministic policy, compliance, auditability, and human escalation. SOC 2, policy-limited tools, production-failure traces, and replayable regression tests show that Dialogus understands the ugly part of the system. But this is not yet a demonstrated dark pool. OpenAI supplies SIP, realtime speech, and tool use; Salesforce now bundles telephony, CRM data, agents, and handoffs. If Dialogus’s accumulated traces do not measurably improve resolution or reduce error costs, the “moat” is integration labor attached to commoditizing models.

The founders have enough technical proximity to build the product: Hans Ibarra worked across Gemini, Gmail, and YouTube, while Rodrigo Terán Hernández interned at Meta, Microsoft, and Google. The current production deployment is a real shipping receipt. What is missing is evidence that anyone has previously operated the other side—the contact-center budget, compliance regime, or enterprise buying process—and the dossier cannot explain the three-founder decision structure under pressure. Credentials are not the same as outsider-insider access.

The painful bull case is that public metrics lag private reality. Household-name enterprises generally do not permit thousands of production calls unless a system has cleared meaningful technical and organizational hurdles. Dialogus could resemble **Ando** in the narrow sense that enterprise operation eventually reveals a working base business beneath an unfashionable AI application. The material difference is decisive: Ando documented accuracy, complete pilot-to-paid conversion, and retention; Dialogus reports activity without outcomes. Its production evidence is enough to keep watching, not enough to override missing price, capital purpose, and economics.

I pass today. The one verifiable flip condition is a financing package showing a sub-$10 million post-money valuation and a dated deployment experiment with one named customer, paired with paid conversion or renewal, gross margin, and an error-adjusted cash metric such as dollars saved per successfully resolved call. That would establish scarce pricing, a truth-revealing use of funds, and a plausible business that survives if no Series A arrives.
