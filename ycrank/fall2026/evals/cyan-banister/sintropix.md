# Sintropix -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, a founder running companies across Chile, Mexico, and Brazil should be able to ask how much money she actually has and get an answer without waiting for three accounting teams to finish their spreadsheets. Sintropix points toward that world. But getting there requires reliable country-specific accounting, traceable automated postings, and migration that preserves the financial history. The dossier describes pieces of that machinery; it does not yet establish the transitions that make autonomous accounting dependable. I can imagine the destination. Imagination alone does not earn my check.

The present-day wound is much clearer. Disconnected banking, payroll, invoicing, and expense systems leave finance teams doing the stitching themselves. Sintropix reports moving a monthly close from day 20 to day 1. That claim, if verified, means an operator gets nineteen days of decision-making time back. This is a specific delay with a specific beneficiary, although the dossier identifies neither an artificial restriction nor a gatekeeper collecting a quantified rent. Services starting at 20 UF monthly establish an asking price, not the customer's savings or Sintropix's margins.

The strongest argument against passing is that these founders are learning the accounting business with their hands. They moved from a logistics concept in December 2025 to manual bookkeeping in April 2026 and a proprietary ERP by September. Alfonso's Toku product experience gives him relevant financial-workflow context; Borja brings computing and AI experience. Contacting more than 100 accountants also shows willingness to do the unglamorous work. But this is evidence of recent customer discovery and execution, not a documented accounting obsession predating the company. I would not mistake the pivot for opportunism, or the credentials for first-believer proof.

Sintropix also has more than a narrated demo: the September 19 report identifies ten active clients across four countries. That matters at this stage. Yet it leaves open whether customers repeatedly operate the ERP themselves or primarily receive bookkeeping performed by the founders. The claimed onboarding improvement from thirty days to twenty-four hours is encouraging, but comes through a company-authored LinkedIn snippet without a documented cohort. I recognize my pattern of backing mundane workflow repair, and accounting services could finance a harder software destination. The exception that lets me invest before revenue, however, needs unusually strong founder history or concrete technical validation; neither is established here.

The proprietary ledger could become the useful thing beneath the assistant. Still, “100+ integrations” does not demonstrate control over a scarce dependency, and embedded mappings remain an inferred source of switching costs. Rillet and Campfire already offer overlapping accounting automation. Sintropix must show why serving these countries creates an accumulating asset rather than an accumulating support burden. Financial-data custody also needs explicit permission and access controls before I would entrust it with my own books; the dossier's silence does not establish a privacy violation. Likewise, brothers with identified CEO and CTO roles are not evidence of the equal-control governance problem I avoid.

I pass with my own money today. The evidence supports a useful accounting operation more strongly than a distinctive enabling layer for what comes next. My single verifiable flip condition is a customer-auditable record of three consecutive monthly closes at three named multi-country customers, completed by day one through Sintropix with traceable postings and no founder bookkeeping intervention. That would connect the attractive outcome to machinery customers can depend on—and materially change the bet.
