# Datoric -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Datoric’s contested truth is precise: privately recruited, consented contributors with sample-level provenance will produce training data that serious AI labs value more than data collected through open contributor platforms. The obvious falsifier is equally precise—buyers fail to renew, or independently audited samples show no meaningful improvement in manipulation, rights integrity, or model performance. This is a human secret discovered from the inside: Reddy previously bypassed annotation-platform QA across 15,000 hours of paid work, then built the system meant to close that attack path. It is more than an AI slogan, but Datoric has not yet published the comparative result that would prove the secret.

The product has nevertheless crossed the boundary from assertion to execution. More than 300,000 active contributors operate through private, modality-separated systems; a commercial computer-use dataset contains 250,000 traces and roughly 15,000 hours of screen activity; and Datoric generated nearly seven figures of revenue in the preceding 30 days. Those facts show that the company can coordinate supply and sell output. They do not yet show that provenance caused the purchase, that the data outperforms substitutes, or that customers cannot recreate the workflow elsewhere.

The foundation is promising but incompletely documented. Reddy and Lin previously built Arzule together, so this is not a convenience pairing formed for the current application. Their annotation-security, AI/ML, robotics, and benchmark work is directly relevant, and the two-person team appears to keep the essential product work in-house. Yet the unexplained transition from Arzule’s Winter 2026 announcement to Datoric’s Summer 2026 listing compresses their demonstrated shared history into months, while full-time status, ownership, salaries, and economic exposure remain undisclosed. Missing evidence is not refutation, but it prevents the founding structure from becoming an affirmative gate.

Is 300,000 contributors a network or merely a list? The distinction determines whether this becomes a monopoly or a commodity. Datoric’s rights records, project isolation, and contributor histories could compound into proprietary supply intelligence, but no exclusivity, retention, repeat-purchase, or qualification-performance evidence establishes that mechanism today. Meanwhile Scale AI, Turing, Mercor, and Prolific possess vastly greater resources and overlapping contributor systems. Covering voice, video, computer use, and physical interaction is not a narrow beachhead; it risks being a collection of services inside an already competitive category.

The strongest bull case is the revenue itself. Nearly $1 million in 30 days from a two-person pre-seed company suggests an urgent buyer problem and makes the build-to-spec data engine plausibly power-law large. The strongest bear case stings more because the same number may represent one concentrated, low-margin project. Customers are unnamed, pricing and gross margins are absent, repeat revenue is unknown, and the stated founder-led route is inferred rather than measured. The gated Hugging Face repository has zero likes and one organizational follower, so it does not establish compounding distribution. My 36-month base case is $20–40 million in annual revenue but continued dependence on concentrated custom engagements—a successful supplier, not yet a last mover.

Datoric fits the emerging AI-production stack and the more distinctive pattern of making adversarial information legible. It also triggers the red flag against crowded AI categories without a demonstrated monopoly mechanism. The exception for weak competitive positioning requires either a verified zero-to-one technical result or customer behavior that directly removes the risk; one opaque revenue month does neither. I would investigate rather than invest today. The verifiable flip condition is two consecutive quarters showing at least three unrelated AI labs each repurchasing, with no customer above 30% of revenue and gross margin above 60%. That would connect the secret, monopoly mechanism, revenue engine, and distribution route rather than asking revenue alone to stand in for all four.
