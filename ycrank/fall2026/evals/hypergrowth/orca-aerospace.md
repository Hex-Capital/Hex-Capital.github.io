# Orca Aerospace -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Orca’s strongest path is **channel-led or repeatable sales**. Satellite fleet operators face a recurring job that plausibly travels across markets: responding to faults while spacecraft are out of contact with ground teams. Orca describes onboard software that proposes and checks commands. The memo records a signed Orbital letter of intent worth up to $20 million for a planned fleet and a joint concept study with Lyra for rendezvous and proximity operations. The Orbital letter is the stronger signal of fit for the fleet buyer. Its proposed integration fee and per-satellite licenses provide a pricing structure, with payments undisclosed. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

Orca plans to integrate once with Reflex Aerospace’s flight software and reuse that work across Orbital’s fleet. If the first deployment proves valuable and later satellites can reuse the integration with limited additional qualification, each licensed satellite could add revenue with less engineering effort than the first. The first link likely to break is onboarding the next independent operator. The memo identifies direct briefings and the planned Reflex integration as routes to buyers and deployment. Paid activation, repeat licensing, and marginal rollout costs remain unobserved, so acquisition, expansion, and contribution each receive the absent-evidence default. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Orca could win Orbital and still face a bespoke engineering project with each new operator. Memory and power limits can restrict which spacecraft can host the software, and flight-representative testing and staged command authority can add work even when software carries over. Orbital’s per-satellite upside also depends on its planned fleet actually deploying, with Orbital-1 use planned for 2028. These are concrete constraints, but their effect on repeat deployment cost remains unmeasured. The structural downside gate is untriggered. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a second independent fleet operator reach paid operational use by reusing the Reflex integration with materially fewer engineering and qualification hours than the first rollout? A paid go-live with a side-by-side record of that effort would move the action to **Reach out**. Evidence that even a comparable operator on the same stack requires rebuilding and requalifying the integration, with no supported route to reuse, would move it to **Pass**.
