# Locke -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Parth Badhwar has worked in the White House, Deloitte’s policy practice, a presidential campaign, and public-affairs software, while Locke has no public GitHub trail or named technical artifact. That makes Locke a schlep-rich, hacker-poor startup. Locke knows where government work becomes unpleasant, but the dossier does not yet show that Locke knows how to turn that unpleasantness into software rather than merely staffing it.

**Coinbase** is the high rung here because compliance was not an accessory; Armstrong ran toward the regulatory grind everyone else avoided. Locke has the same promising instinct: lobbying registration, citation-backed regulatory briefs, policymaker outreach, and government-contract proposals are real schleps. But Locke diverges from Coinbase because Locke has disclosed neither its verification process nor its lobbying-compliance machinery, and its “first few partners” remain unnamed. The schlep is present. Evidence that Locke has mastered it is not.

**Airbnb** had little hacker genealogy to point to, but the cereal boxes supplied a different kind of proof: the founders could invent their way through a wall. The dangerous low-rung pattern is having neither a hacker trail nor a compensating stunt. Locke currently has no public repository, competitive-programming history, or shipped technical artifact, and Locke offers no cereal-box story from Badhwar or the unnamed second founder. Hiring technical staff at $100,000–$200,000 plus 0.50%–3.00% equity is not the same as being technical before raising money.

Locke’s “first few partners” could become an animal signal if Badhwar is personally monitoring hearings, writing proposals, and lobbying policymakers until midnight. Over 18 months, that behavior would compound into workflow knowledge, customer trust, and proprietary policy records. Locke instead already advertises two hires with salaries reaching $200,000, before disclosing one named customer or one dollar of revenue. Locke may be doing things that do not scale, but the public tracks presently look more like early hiring than relentless improvisation.

Parth Badhwar’s White House, campaign, Deloitte, and Clipbook experience places Locke close to an organic idea, but not inside one. The dossier says Badhwar understands government affairs; it does not say Badhwar personally needed Locke, built Locke for his own workflow, or used Locke before selling it. The score would flip upward if Badhwar could say: “At Clipbook or in the White House, I repeatedly did this job manually, so I wrote the first agent for myself.” Without that fact, Locke is domain-adjacent rather than Dropbox-organic.

**Stripe** was two brothers who had already built and sold Auctomatic together; the pair itself was evidence. Locke also has two people, which is the right number, but Locke names only Badhwar and provides no history of the founders building, working, or even knowing one another before Locke. A pair can move with the compactness of the Collisons. An undocumented pair receives only neutral credit until Locke shows that the relationship survives the first ugly government-contract deadline.

Locke’s first few partners and consultation-led sales point toward a specific 36-month outcome: Locke reaches $2 million–$5 million in annualized retainer revenue by July 2029, with most revenue still dependent on human lobbying and proposal work rather than self-service software. Locke’s high schlep score should win real clients; Locke’s weak hacker genealogy should keep delivery labor-intensive. The likely result is a good AI-native government-affairs firm, not yet a black swan software company.

Quorum’s more than 2,000 customers make the bull case sting: Locke could use services as the wedge, learn faster than Quorum or FiscalNote, and turn repeated engagements into proprietary policy and stakeholder data. Badhwar’s 4,000 LinkedIn followers, government network, and technical hiring could make Locke look more like an early Stripe than an agency. But Stripe arrived with hacker artifacts, while Locke has no disclosed customer, revenue, dataset, verification system, or technical founder record. I would change my verdict if Locke showed three named customers renewing a productized monitoring contract at greater than 70% gross margin; absent that, the **Airbnb** exception does not apply because Locke has shown no equivalent animal stunt.
