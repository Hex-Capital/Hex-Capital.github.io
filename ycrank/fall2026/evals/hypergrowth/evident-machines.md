# Evident Machines -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B, repeatable sales with account expansion, is the strongest candidate.** The initial buyer is a frontier AI lab evaluating whether its models can make sound decisions on physical electronics. The memo records the launch of FrontierEE Bench. Evident reports that models averaged a 29.3% pass rate on 10 physical tasks they passed at 100% in simulation. That result gives labs a concrete reason to test against hardware. Buyer urgency, paid activation, and repeat use remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The growth scenario assumes direct sales of a standard paid evaluation, with labs buying more model runs or task coverage as they release new models. If the same tasks and bench setup can serve multiple labs, Evident could sell into a second geography from its existing lab, subject to remote delivery and the buyer’s data-handling requirements. The memo provides no cross-market buyer evidence or repeatable customer onboarding. Its public contributor call recruits task creators, leaving buyer acquisition untested. A paid pilot could establish an early signal of fit at this stage. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Facts)

### Countercase

The benchmark could become a custom testing service. Operators handle wiring and probe placement, so additional runs can consume bench time and labor. New task coverage can add paid expert design and documentation, and parallel workloads could require more equipment. Anthropic’s work with Diode shows that a target buyer can build overlapping evaluations with a hardware partner. The Pass gate remains open because the memo establishes neither a structural buyer ceiling nor how operator time and equipment needs change when an existing task is reused for another customer. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
