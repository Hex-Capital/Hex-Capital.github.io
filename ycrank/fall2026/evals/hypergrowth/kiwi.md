# Kiwi -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B, repeatable sales with account expansion, is Kiwi’s strongest candidate.** The initial buyer is a revenue operations or post-sales lead who repeatedly reviews and fixes agent output as source data and workflows change. Chen reports spending 20 hours on that work in one week at her prior employer and consulting more than 100 companies during discovery. Kiwi launched publicly in October. It offers founder-led demos and documents connectors, replayable SQL, and access through existing AI clients via MCP. The memo identifies no buyer who paid, put a recurring workflow into production, or expanded usage. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

In the growth scenario, similar teams buy through a repeatable demo, connect their existing data sources, and pay to run one recurring workflow, such as preparing account reviews. They then add workflows using the same source connections and approval history. This assumes that buyers in a second geography use compatible systems and can deploy the same product under their data-handling requirements. Each new account could add security review, connector setup, and workflow configuration. Each added workflow could increase compute, storage, and support costs. The memo gives no rollout or cost measurements to show whether paid expansion would outpace that effort. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Kiwi may need to rebuild business definitions, SQL joins, and approval rules for each buyer, with customer-cloud deployments adding more work. Replay helps teams inspect a result, but an incorrect join can still reproduce the same error. For CRM-centered workflows, HubSpot already offers cross-tool account summaries, which could make a separate purchase harder to justify. These risks warrant Watch. The memo records no rollout that establishes a structural ceiling on demand or shows that delivery effort scales in proportion to revenue. (*Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent revenue operations or post-sales buyer in a second geography come through Kiwi’s intended sales channel, pay, and run an account-review workflow through its normal cadence using standard connectors? A paid rollout with usage records and a breakdown of sales, setup, security review, and ongoing delivery effort would move the action to **Reach out** if it showed recurring use without a bespoke rebuild.
