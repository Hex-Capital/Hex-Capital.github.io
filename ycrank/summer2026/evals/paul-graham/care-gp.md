# Care GP -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Care GP has proved the product before it has proved the founder. It reached US$200,000 ARR after 3.5 months, converted 85% of pilots, reported zero churn, and grew usage 20% weekly. By August 2026, more than 400 clinic operators had used it to process over 3 million healthcare documents. Those are unusually concrete pre-seed results. Australian GP clinics are not merely saying they like the idea; they are paying for a crude product and feeding it real medical records.

The technical proof is also real, though not complete. Samantha cross-checks patient identifiers, refuses uncertain allocations, and reduced document work at Yarrawonga Medical Clinic from six hours to under 30 minutes. ISO 27001 certification, Australian data residency, and a working Bp Premier integration show that the team has entered the ugly part of healthcare software. But the 5%–20% human edit rate and absence of an independent accuracy comparison matter. Three million processed documents prove deployment at scale; they do not yet prove that Care GP has a technical advantage over Facere.

What is missing is the incident that tells me who Melvin Chen is when something breaks. The dossier gives me excellent credentials and impressive outcomes, but no named obstacle, dated founder-built workaround, or elapsed time. Nor does it show a rejected product hypothesis followed by a changed route. Tripling through referrals is evidence of demand, not evidence that Chen is a running back. The unexplained change from three founders in Startmate’s 2025 cohort to one active founder on YC makes this omission more important, because it leaves the team’s behavior under conflict or setback unknown.

The schlep is attractive, but the origin is less so. Medical identifiers, privacy controls, practice-management integrations, phone calls, faxes, and manual exceptions are precisely the burdens that keep casual competitors away. Yet Chen’s documented history is in investing and company building, not running a clinic or experiencing this workflow personally. Meanwhile, Facere already offers overlapping document and voice automation within Bp Premier. Care GP may win through execution, but the dossier does not show that its insight came from seeing something competitors could not see.

The strongest case for investing is that Care GP matches the concrete-proof pattern almost perfectly: revenue arrived quickly, usage compounded, customers referred one another, and a large proprietary document substrate existed before the YC batch. Most of my usual red flags are absent. Growth does not depend solely on the Bp partnership, there is no ceremonial-launch strategy, and there is no evidence Chen hired salespeople before learning how clinics buy. But the known exceptions do not resolve the central uncertainty. Working products and unmistakable pull can override cofounder, age, or structural concerns; they cannot by themselves show how a founder responds when the current route stops working. The public traction is also mostly company-reported, without a retention cohort or independent accuracy data strong enough to replace that missing evidence.

I would not write the check yet. Care GP looks capable of becoming a valuable Australian healthcare-operations business, but today I know more about the product’s momentum than the founder’s motion. One verifiable fact would flip me: dated customer correspondence and release records showing a serious clinic failure, what Chen personally changed within days or weeks, and retained usage by the affected clinics afterward.
