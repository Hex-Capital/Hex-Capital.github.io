# TrustAI -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

The EU AI Act’s August 2, 2026 deadline makes **TrustAI** a regulated-sector wedge without a definite founder plan—strongest where compliance can create institutional friction, weakest where commitment should replace iteration. **TrustAI** maps agent access across SAP, Oracle, NetSuite, Jira, and ServiceNow, but its recent pivot from browser automation suggests the product is following the wave rather than expressing a secret held before the wave arrived. Regulation can manufacture demand. It cannot manufacture monopoly.

**Palantir** is the high rung because intelligence customers, integration depth, and institutional trust compounded into pricing power competitors could not casually reproduce. **TrustAI** shares the sensitive-system mechanism: ERP blast-radius mapping, four compliance-framework mappings, and analyst-reviewed reports can become embedded in regulated approval processes. **TrustAI** diverges where the analogy becomes decisive—no named regulated customer, certification barrier, proprietary deployment dataset, or production authority is disclosed. The EU deadline creates urgency, but urgency is not a moat; TrustAI sits well below the Palantir rung on regulated-sector structural advantage.

**Quora** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism for definite planning: an indefinite consumer roadmap could be tolerated only because Adam D’Angelo had already been Facebook’s CTO. **TrustAI** has moved from browser automation—with a prior 47× benchmark that does not transfer—to agent governance, while publishing no two-to-five-year technical milestones or architectural commitments it will refuse to abandon. **TrustAI** has built 51 tests and five named integrations, but those describe the present product, not a definite future. Without D’Angelo-level pedigree as an exception, the pivot is evidence of indefinite optimism rather than flexibility.

**Noma Security’s $132 million in funding** sharpens the secret test: **TrustAI** names Noma, Zenity, WitnessAI, and Patronus, yet names no architectural or institutional reason any of them cannot add analyst-reviewed predeployment reports. Over 18 months, **TrustAI** could compound proprietary permission-path and remediation data from repeated assessments; absent customer scale, the opposite mechanism dominates—51 published tests become a checklist competitors copy, while human review becomes a services burden. “Assessment rather than enforcement” is differentiation, but it is not yet the Stripe-like secret that incumbents actively reject.

**SAP’s embedded Joule authorization services** and Oracle’s secure NetSuite MCP connectors make **TrustAI’s** monopoly problem concrete. For the score to flip a tier, TrustAI would need to become the independent system of record for agent authorization evidence across competing ERP vendors, with longitudinal permission graphs, renewal data, and audit-grade switching costs that SAP and Oracle cannot credibly provide themselves. **TrustAI** currently discloses no structural barrier, customer dataset, renewal evidence, or pricing-power hypothesis; order forms containing platform, consumption, implementation, and professional-service fees show monetization options, not durable monopoly economics.

**Stripe** is the single useful founder parallel: Patrick Collison combined unusual technical identity with a product insight that legacy payments companies had structurally missed. **TrustAI** has credible outsider signals—Medha Venkatapathy researched LLM post-training and the Muon optimizer and qualified for the U.S. Physics Team, while Hannah Chung studied MIT computer science and economics and worked at Virtu Financial and the World Bank. **TrustAI** does not yet have Stripe’s shipped-to-scale evidence, acquired side project, or founder-derived incumbent insight. The MIT credentials raise the floor; they do not supply the missing secret.

**TrustAI’s 51-test catalog** supports a specific 36-month forecast: by July 2029, **TrustAI** will be acquired by Vanta for its ERP agent-assessment workflow before reaching $5 million ARR, rather than becoming an independent runtime-governance platform. Analyst-reviewed evidence maps naturally into compliance automation, while SAP, Oracle, Noma, WitnessAI, and Zenity compress the standalone control layer. The regulated wedge has acquisition value; the absent definite plan and weak monopoly mechanism limit power-law value.

**TrustAI’s 679 launch reactions and 105 comments** support the strongest bull case: regulation arrives immediately, enterprises need neutral evidence across rival ERP systems, and two unusually technical MIT founders could turn analyst-reviewed assessments into the authoritative audit layer before incumbents coordinate. Asana is the known exception—weak atoms and secret scores can be overridden by extraordinary founder lineage—but **TrustAI** has no Moskovitz-scale operating history, and its reconstruction-local results are explicitly hypotheses when transferred to live agents. I pass. My verdict flips if TrustAI discloses at least three named Fortune 500 customers renewing paid assessments, more than $1 million ARR, and production telemetry proving that its cross-ERP evidence graph improves with every deployment.
