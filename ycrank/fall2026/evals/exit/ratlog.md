# RATLOG -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a European launch incumbent after RATLOG demonstrates a flight-ready engine. A buyer such as Arianespace could acquire the propulsion technology if testing shows that it lowers manufacturing or refurbishment costs enough to justify buying and integrating it. The memo documents Arianespace’s existing constellation launch business. RATLOG reports a prototype hot-fire in four months for under $30,000 and targets 90% fewer engine parts. Cost savings at flight scale and buyer interest remain unverified. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)

A repeatable flight-ready engine test with measured cost and performance data is the next milestone that could support a buyer’s valuation. RATLOG targets a flight-ready engine in 2027, a first-stage test in 2028, and orbit in 2029 or 2030. The €30,000 grant and YC backing support early work. The memo gives no budget or funding commitments for the scale-up, and launch-site selection remains open. It also leaves IP ownership, transfer permissions, and shareholder participation unverified. (Market & Traction; Key Risks)

The benchmark is a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. At 3%, that requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The following sale prices are illustrative assumptions. Both sale cases assume a cash close within eight years, participation of the holder’s entire stake, and a pro rata waterfall with no additional preference deductions.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $10M in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $40M | $1.2M, or 2.4× |
| Strong sale | $110M enterprise value less the same $10M leaves $100M | $3.0M, or 6.0× at 3%. At 2% ownership, $2.0M, or 4.0× |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested, with no distribution or eligible share sale within eight years | $0 |

RATLOG’s reported $400 million in reservations signals potential launch demand. Undisclosed deposits and cancellation terms leave their economic value uncertain, and the memo provides no buyer pricing or transaction comparables to support the strong sale case. There is no evidenced independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Arianespace already serves constellation customers with Ariane 6, and Blue Origin has recovered an orbital booster. An incumbent can keep using its own platform unless RATLOG demonstrates a material cost advantage at flight scale. The gap between a prototype hot-fire and a funded flight-ready engine also creates dilution risk. These facts support Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a credible price ceiling that would trigger Pass. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would the measured economics of a flight-ready engine support an acquisition price that clears the holder’s eight-year cash objective? A conditional valuation range from a credible strategic buyer, tied to measured engine savings and sufficient to leave the holder at least $2.5 million after dilution, debt, deal adjustments, and senior claims, would move the action to Reach out.
