# Invertix -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a renewable power operator. TotalEnergies is the clearest candidate because it selected Invertix for its accelerator and began testing with its solar teams. Altenia’s CEO reported that some work went from days to minutes. Invertix reports agent coverage of about 2 GW. Together, these signals support a potential reason to buy the software and its integrations: an operator could standardize fault triage and repair coordination across its portfolio. The value of owning that capability remains unmeasured. (The Idea; Market & Traction)

The next milestone is a paid, multi-site production rollout that measures labor savings and recovered generation after integration and support costs. The €1.7 million pre-seed and existing solar tests make that milestone plausible. Engineer-led deployments leave the cost and time to expand uncertain. A whole-company sale is a plausible structure for the deployed software, with IP ownership, contract assignment, and change-of-control consents still unverified. (Market & Traction; The Idea; Key Risks)

Under the shared benchmark, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year goal of $2.5 million in gross cash requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity value. The sale scenarios below assume a closing by year eight, $10 million in net debt and deal deductions, and an eligible holder cashing out its full stake pro rata with no preference diversion. The prices are illustrative. The memo gives custom pricing and workflow time savings, leaving annual buyer benefit and a realizable acquisition price unquantified. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Facts)

### Countercase

An operator could license Invertix or use overlapping tools from Metris Energy or Areg.AI. Customer-specific integrations could also erode the savings as deployment expands. That combination could support a software business while leaving a buyer with little reason to pay the roughly $93.3 million in enterprise value needed under the assumed deductions. The memo supports neither a structural closing block nor a price ceiling that triggers Pass. It provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What incremental value would TotalEnergies capture by owning Invertix instead of licensing it? A paid multi-site rollout with buyer-validated savings, deployment costs, and a quantified benefit from ownership would answer it. If those economics support a purchase price that clears the holder’s hurdle after deal deductions, move to Reach out. Until then, Watch.
