# Kairox Systems -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A, scarce complements, is the stronger case.** Tempest could give military maritime teams a single platform for work that Kairox says currently requires separate aerial and underwater fleets, operators, and logistics. If cheaper autonomy makes it easier to coordinate those operations, buyers would still need hardware that can perform reliably in both environments and clear their qualification requirements. Kairox reports laboratory component validation at TRL 4. The memo provides no integrated field result or comparison with the two-fleet baseline, so bottleneck relief remains a seed-stage hypothesis. (*The Idea*; *Key Risks*)

Kairox reports Marine Corps engagement and concept feedback from U.S. and allied military end users. That supports early buyer interest. Procurement remains a future milestone, and the memo’s unquantified revenue label leaves paid demand for Tempest unresolved. The founders’ hardware, autonomy, and aerospace qualification backgrounds support their ability to pursue the next test. The memo provides no measured unit costs, production capacity, or deployment throughput. The scale case assumes that any staffing and logistics savings would exceed the added hardware and support costs per successful job. (*Market & Traction*; *Key Risks*; *Key Facts*; *Founders & Team*)

### Countercase

Buyers can keep their existing fleets. With cheap intelligence and abundant capital, competitors could improve coordination across those fleets or build a competing dual-environment platform. The memo identifies no enforceable rights, completed buyer qualification, or structural barrier that would protect Kairox’s economics. Qualification could create switching costs if buyers adopt Tempest and require replacements to requalify. The available evidence leaves that advantage unproven and does not establish that separate fleets are equivalent on cost and reliability. Path A therefore remains on Watch. Path B supplies no independent case because the memo shows no previously uneconomic buyer or use becoming viable through measured labor savings. (*The Idea*; *Defensibility*; *Market & Traction*; *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will a target buyer commit equipment time or budget to a comparative field qualification that shows Tempest can meet the same job requirements as separate fleets with materially less operator and logistics burden per successful job? A successful trial under a qualification requirement that replacement suppliers would also have to clear would justify rescoring for Reach out at 65 or above with at least medium confidence. If the trial shows that existing fleets meet the same requirements with comparable burden and reliability, move to Pass.
