# GrayPass -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

GrayPass reports $150,000 raised from 1517 Fund and Boost VC, but I cannot write down the proposed post-money, competing-term-sheet count, or why my check would be scarce. Those are blanks, not evidence of an auction. The backer list does not settle the question either. I am being offered a potentially useful security business without the information that tells me whether its uncertainty is attractively priced.

The product has a concrete point: authentication at login does not establish who controls the session when money moves or permissions change. Short-lived, action-bound proof gives applications something they can enforce. But GrayPass’s own documentation says behavioral continuity remains experimental and unapproved for live enforcement. That creates a possible venture moment—establishing whether behavioral evidence can safely govern consequential actions—but the dossier supplies no capital allocation, dated test, or measurable acceptance threshold. I will not turn a technical question I can formulate into a financing plan the founders have demonstrated.

The strongest argument against passing is that customers are already engaging before that uncertainty is resolved. European pilots, a customer secured during a San Francisco trip, and the CEO’s reported “five figures in revenue” are meaningful for two founders at this stage. Someone appears willing to pay for the problem. Yet founder outbound and demonstrations do not establish an owned distribution advantage, and claimed ownership of an SDK and models does not establish proprietary data. The embedded-workflow advantage remains hypothetical until customers actually depend on the enforcement. TypingDNA and Twosense already address overlapping authentication and remediation needs; GrayPass needs evidence that buyers specifically choose its action-level control.

The founders provide the clearest affirmative signal. Mathur has shipped software, including a separate fashion app that reached 3,000 users in three weeks; Ranjan brings computational neuroscience research from SickKids and an MRI-modeling fellowship. These are actual building and research receipts, although the fashion users belong to another product. The combination fits my interest in people translating between ecosystems. What is missing is operating experience on the security buyer’s side: deploying controls, handling false blocks, and owning the consequences of missed attacks. Their documentation’s explicit compromised-endpoint boundary is sensible. It also makes clear that GrayPass must earn its place inside a larger security system.

At $500 a month to start, the revenue claim supports some economic credit, but its unspecified period cannot establish recurring revenue, and there is no margin, burn, or runway evidence. Delete the next financing: I cannot yet see how this business supports itself. My exceptions for technically exposed businesses require measured conversion and retention; my exceptions for open-ended experiments require a proven operator and a financing structure that captures the upside. Neither override is established here. I pass today. One result would reopen diligence: a customer-verified renewal at positive contribution margin after a paid pilot moves into live action enforcement and meets predefined false-block and missed-takeover thresholds. That would connect the product’s distinctive promise to durable cash. The round would still need an underwritable price.
