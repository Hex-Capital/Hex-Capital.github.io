# Mass Magnetics -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence is strategically important but not yet 100x. Magnets sit inside aerospace, robotics, drones, and motors; China’s April 2025 export controls and the January 1, 2027 DFARS restriction make domestic supply valuable. But a $21.98 billion market is not itself a societal consequence. Mass Magnetics does not quantify how much imported capacity it can displace, how much cheaper recycling becomes, or which resource constraint disappears if its process succeeds. This is supply-chain resilience with potentially large leverage, not yet a demonstrated cost-curve revolution.

The technical claim is more interesting than the market narrative. Machine-vision sorting could manage heterogeneous scrap, and fully dense magnets sintered in 60 seconds could materially change throughput. Yet “60 seconds” is not a 10x benchmark until the company names the incumbent cycle time, reports energy and yield, and demonstrates equivalent magnetic performance. Its published compositions are illustrative, no independent qualification results are available, and Noveon already makes recycled sintered magnets. A fast cycle producing inconsistent or unqualified material is merely a fast way to make scrap.

The bull case is real. Eric Silver has pilot cathode-production experience; Matthew Groll brings manufacturing training plus NASA and Draper systems work. That is a more credible combination than three generic software founders discovering industrial policy. The product also fits my preference for technology compatible with installed infrastructure: customers need not redesign motors around a new chemistry. They can buy familiar magnet forms from a domestic recycled source. Year-end 2026 preorders give the team a near-term opportunity to prove this rather than spending years pontificating.

But capital must first attack qualification and feedstock variability, not factory scale. The dossier supplies no specified grade, independent test protocol, pass/fail threshold, experiment cost, or customer qualification milestone. DFARS does not rescue weak economics because its nonavailability exception permits foreign material when compliant supply is unavailable at reasonable price or quality. Meanwhile, Noveon has raised $315 million, Vulcan $65 million, and Niron may eliminate rare-earth feedstock from the architecture. Regulation creates urgency; it does not create technical superiority.

The team earns credit for complementary domain experience and an MIT MetLab advisor, but there is no evidence yet of a material belief revised after data or a recruited owner for the central qualification risk. Nor does a known-exception case override these gaps. I have tolerated missing milestones when an all-in technical founder targeted a quantified, civilization-scale consequence. Here the mission may become that large, but the dossier establishes neither the magnitude nor the obstinacy required for that override.

My 36-month base case is a qualified custom-lot supplier with less than $5 million in annualized revenue, constrained by customer qualification and variable feedstock rather than sintering speed. I pass today. The single flip condition is a repeat production order from a named aerospace or defense customer after independent qualification, at delivered cost parity with incumbent NdFeB magnets without continuing subsidy. That would prove performance, economics, and demand with one piece of evidence.
