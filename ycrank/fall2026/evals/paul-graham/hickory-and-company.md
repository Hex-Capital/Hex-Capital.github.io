# Hickory & Company -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Joshua Howland has already built a bank-facing business and watched it close. I want to know what he did when something important stopped working. His reported 500,000 accounts and $10 million revenue at Seis establish operating experience, but they don't answer that question. The dossier gives no specific obstacle, workaround, and elapsed time I can examine. That is missing evidence, not evidence of passivity. But resourcefulness is the thing I most need to establish before writing a personal check.

Hickory & Company has a much better starting point than an arbitrary idea for compliance agents. Howland worked at Zions and built Seis. Scott Grow worked with him there and brings more than 20 years of compliance experience. Kevin Anderson adds banking-systems engineering. This is a team plausibly acquainted with the ugly work: collecting partner evidence, reconciling institutional policies, and producing records someone can defend to an examiner. That earns credit for proximity to the problem and willingness to tackle its burdens. It falls short of a documented origin story showing the particular failure that made them build Hickory.

The strongest argument for investing is that these people may already know exactly which small piece of banking bureaucracy to remove. Analyst Studio's source-linked research, acceptance criteria, and human approvals describe a sensible way to enter a consequential workflow. The claimed live-bank integration is provisional technical evidence. It deserves more weight than a diagram, but less than an inspectable deployment with measured results. My preference for concrete proof before a round is only partly satisfied. Parcha's configurable agents also mean the published architecture alone cannot establish what this team has uniquely solved.

Who wants this badly enough to keep using it? Sponsor-bank compliance teams are a specific audience, but the dossier identifies no returning user, paying institution, or customer pulling the product into an unexpected workflow. The $500,000 financing reported for September 2026 answers a different question. So do the 22 launch videos. Humanfold's involvement in outreach raises the question of whether the founders are learning directly from buyers; it does not establish that they have delegated that learning. There is likewise no documented customer observation followed by a revised product route. I would leave both demand and adaptability at their missing-evidence defaults.

The bull case stings because banking experience and an existing working relationship could let Hickory accomplish things a technically impressive outsider would never understand. Continuing after a failed company also fits a pattern I take seriously. But the dossier does not show the same founding team recovering together, and my known exceptions require a convincing prototype, unmistakable user pull, or independently validated proof strong enough to replace the missing evidence. The unnamed integration does not reach that threshold. I would pass today. One verifiable condition would reopen the decision: a bank customer confirms that a specific deployment blocker prompted a founder-built change within weeks, after which its compliance staff repeatedly used and paid for the resulting workflow.
