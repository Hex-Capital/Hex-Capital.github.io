# RealPact -- Elad Gil Evaluation

CourseMe reached 4,000 monthly active users, one million page hits, and roughly 90% penetration at Dartmouth before being acquired. That makes RealPact a repeat-shipping founder bet whose weakest axis is strategic position: Ranvir Deshmukh and Erik Peterson can build and distribute software, but RealPact currently looks like a capable vertical application competing for share rather than an index on a compounding category.

**Perplexity** is the high rung on shipping because Aravind Srinivas repeatedly returned a week later with the discussed product built. RealPact shares the load-bearing mechanism: Deshmukh and Peterson shipped CourseMe, sold it, launched SignPact, pivoted, and put RealPact into the hands of paying New Hampshire brokerages. RealPact diverges because the dossier documents successive products, not rapid iterations driven by specific brokerage feedback between conversations; that keeps the founders closer to Perplexity than credential-only founders, but below the exceptional shipping cadence that independently triggers a check.

**Lyft** is the low-rung warning: entering a market where an incumbent’s distribution advantages make the challenger a structurally weaker number two. RealPact faces a version of that problem in SkySlope, which already supplies AI-assisted transactions, compliance, forms, voice functionality, and back-office software to more than 900,000 professionals, while ListedKit already automates contract intake and timelines. RealPact’s external-record retrieval wedge is real, but RealPact must integrate jurisdiction by jurisdiction and then displace embedded workflow software; it does not yet become more valuable automatically as real-estate transactions grow. This is not Stripe as an index on e-commerce.

LLM-based extraction from real-estate contracts was demonstrated in 2024, giving RealPact a recent technological catalyst rather than a pure TAM story. The counterfactual that moves RealPact into the top catalyst tier is evidence that agentic retrieval—not merely document extraction—recently crossed a reliability threshold that incumbents cannot match, producing materially faster close cycles across multiple states. RealPact instead enters a market where SkySlope has already incorporated AI and where the cited transaction-management category grows at 11.88% annually; the window is open, but it is not yet a Stripe-after-merchant-accounts re-opening.

Paying New Hampshire brokerages and the firm-wide Four Seasons Sotheby’s pilot give RealPact more signal than the typical pre-seed AI workflow pitch, but neither revenue nor usage growth is quantified. Over the next 18 months, RealPact’s curve will compound only if each county integration becomes reusable across many brokerages; otherwise implementation labor, stale public records, SOC 2 work, and contract-liability review expand alongside revenue. RealPact could be “just working,” but undisclosed revenue and an unspecified active-user count cannot yet be distinguished from enterprise-pilot tourism. Harvey-like velocity is easy to recognize because customers keep buying; RealPact has not disclosed that curve.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow infrastructure can capture value on top of a hard underlying layer without requiring a new scientific breakthrough. RealPact similarly relies on already-demonstrated LLM extraction and existing government records, so RealPact does not require new model science. The remaining dependencies—accurate cross-jurisdiction retrieval, legally safe contract population, unfinished SOC 2 compliance, and national brokerage adoption—are substantial, but mostly product and GTM execution rather than independent miracles. The architecture passes the science-project test, although data accuracy and distribution remain two correlated ways for execution to fail.

Four Seasons Sotheby’s gives RealPact a credible design partner, but RealPact’s high-shipping/low-index signature predicts a useful product more readily than an independent platform: by July 2029, RealPact will reach $3 million–$7 million ARR and be acquired by SkySlope for less than $75 million to add external-record retrieval to SkySlope’s transaction suite. RealPact can build quickly enough to establish the wedge, while SkySlope’s 900,000-professional distribution and RealPact’s jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction maintenance burden make acquisition more probable than category control.

CourseMe’s acquisition, RealPact’s paying customers, and RealPact’s Four Seasons Sotheby’s pilot form the strongest bull case: repeat founders may be using New Hampshire as a narrow beachhead before turning external-record retrieval into the mandatory data layer for every brokerage workflow. The **Color Genomics** exception stings because RealPact could indeed become workflow infrastructure on top of a fragmented hard layer, with fragmentation creating the moat rather than merely cost. The evidence that wins today is SkySlope’s overlapping AI and voice product set, RealPact’s lack of brokerage operating history, and no disclosed growth curve or proprietary-data advantage. I would flip to Invest if RealPact demonstrates at least $1 million ARR with six consecutive months above 20% month-over-month growth across three or more states.
