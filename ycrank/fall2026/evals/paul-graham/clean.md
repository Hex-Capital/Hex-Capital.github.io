# Clean -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Clean has evidence that people will pay, but less evidence about what its founders do when something breaks. Those are different questions. Pavan’s handwritten letters and personalized outbound suggest he is doing the selling himself. That is good. But the dossier contains no specific obstacle followed by a founder-built workaround and a dated result. I cannot turn $240K of founder-reported ARR into an invented story about resourcefulness. My most important founder test remains unanswered.

The strongest argument for investing is Vivameda. It reportedly went from roughly 4% replies to 19% and closed $60K in contracts within two weeks ([case study](https://www.tryclean.ai/customers/vivameda)). That identifies a concrete problem: sellers wasting time on stale contacts and poorly timed approaches. The $60K belongs to the customer, not Clean. Separately, Pavan’s claim of $240K ARR before YC suggests buyers will pay for the solution. These are substantial pre-seed signals. Their limitation is that a company-published testimonial and an annualized revenue claim do not establish that customers keep buying once the first useful prospect list is exhausted.

The product has also crossed the boundary from an idea into something usable. Clean assembles shipments, permits, loans and contracts into source-linked company timelines, and it has shipped a Chrome connector. That earns technical credit independently of the revenue. But finding a real event and predicting a purchase are different accomplishments. A shipment can prove that a company imports something without proving that it needs this seller now. With Clay and Apollo adding overlapping research and signal workflows, Clean’s opportunity depends on making that second inference useful repeatedly. Exclusive data rights are unnecessary to begin; dependable answers would matter much more than calling the datasets proprietary.

The summer 2026 move from developer infrastructure to sales intelligence shows willingness to change products ([EO interview](https://www.eomag.io/article/clean-pavan-kumar)). It does not reveal what observation killed the previous hypothesis or whether the underlying goal persisted. I therefore leave adaptability at its missing-evidence default. The earlier developer product’s reported 60,000 interactions and 10,000 sessions cannot establish demand for this one. My pattern of backing useful developer primitives does not transfer automatically across a pivot. Pavan’s Cisco sales experience gives him relevant context, but the dossier likewise does not establish a personal collision with this problem or an unusually neglected operational burden.

I would pass on writing a check today, despite genuine interest. The usual warning about substituting launches or partnerships for founder selling does not appear to apply here. And I allow exceptions when a working product and unmistakable user pull compensate for incomplete founder evidence. Clean has the working product; its reported commercial results make that exception worth considering, but do not yet establish unmistakable recurring pull. My verifiable flip condition is one customer-confirmed episode in which a serious data or workflow obstacle threatened a deployment, the founders shipped their own workaround within two weeks, and the customer subsequently renewed. That would connect the promising commercial evidence to the founder behavior I am actually betting on.
