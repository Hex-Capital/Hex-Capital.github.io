# Dock -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Mike Molinet’s 16,511 LinkedIn followers and Govind Kavaturi’s 10,000 make **Dock** a founder-distribution-rich company trapped inside my least attractive category: consensus AI software. That high/low pairing is Dock’s signature. Dock has operators capable of manufacturing attention, but the asset being financed is still a multiplayer agent workspace with no disclosed round price, no revenue, and one GitHub star. “AI workspaces are worthless” is the provocative version; the operational version is that an AI workspace needs either an absurdly cheap entry or a structural financing edge before I can make money from it.

**John Fish / Bookshelved** is the high rung on founder-owned distribution because the community predated the financing and attachment to John was the asset being capitalized. Dock shares the named-founder reach mechanism: Molinet and Kavaturi have roughly 26,500 LinkedIn followers combined, and Molinet previously built Branch past $100 million ARR. Dock diverges where the analogy becomes load-bearing: LinkedIn followers are rented professional attention, not Bookshelved’s owned literary community, and Dock discloses no conversion, engagement, newsletter, or pre-existing user base. Dock has distribution potential on the cap table, but not yet distribution proof.

**Robinhood’s** low-rung mechanism on category-sleeve fit was consensus fintech outside my preferred crypto, DTC, creator, and Wizard-of-Oz sleeves; the exception worked because Slow entered early enough that later consensus pricing accrued to the position. Dock has the same outside-the-sleeve problem without the exception evidence. Dock is a generic AI-agent collaboration layer competing against Dust, Relevance AI, and CrewAI, which have raised more than $112 million collectively, while model providers can add shared state and permissions themselves. “AI is just more internet” applies almost perfectly here: useful infrastructure, perhaps, but Dock offers no evidence that I am buying it before the crowd or below the crowd.

No public Dock round size, valuation, investor list, or financing instrument is disclosed, so I apply the 9/30 absent-evidence default on price. Over the next 18 months, Dock’s category can compound against Dock: every Dust financing, MCP platform feature, and model-provider workspace makes the product more legible but the equity less scarce. A cheap SAFE could turn that into value-seed underwriting; a hot Summer 2026 “multiplayer agents” SAFE would make Dock a factory-model trade where the narrative markup has already been captured by YC.

**$19 per organization per month** for Pro and $49 for Scale gives Dock a capital-efficient-looking entry point, but Dock has not shown that those prices fund enterprise security, integrations, and support without repeated financing tacks. The counterfactual that flips this dimension is specific: Dock demonstrates that usage-based action limits produce default-alive economics, with paid organizations expanding while the two-person operation remains lean. Until then, Dock looks like a bootstrappable collaboration utility being force-fit into a venture cap table, not a Marina Mogilko-style asset whose unusual cash flows demand an unusual instrument.

**Venmo** is the useful single-anchor parallel for founder conviction: many turns of the crank only work when operators have enough shared history to survive Type 2 fun. Dock gets real credit here because Molinet hired Kavaturi as Branch’s first international employee, they worked together for six years, and both later co-founded Thena. Dock’s repeat-founder history is much stronger than the normal pre-seed résumé, but Dock provides no category writing, named non-obvious belief, or evidence that either founder pursued human-agent coordination for three years before YC. That is operator durability, not yet asymmetric conviction.

**Six public GitHub repositories**, one star on the leading repository, and commercial-beta status point to a concrete 36-month forecast: Dock will pivot from a general multiplayer workspace into an enterprise agent-identity, permissions, and audit-control plane and reach $2 million to $6 million ARR by July 2029, without becoming the default horizontal agent workspace. Dock’s actor attribution, separate API keys, signed webhooks, and event history already form the pivot substrate; Dock’s near-zero public developer adoption makes the broader collaboration-layer outcome unlikely.

**$100 million ARR at Branch** is the strongest case against my forecast and my pass: Molinet and Kavaturi have already scaled distribution together, Dock prices per organization rather than per seat, and MCP gives Dock a neutral layer across Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, and independently sourced agents. The Pinterest known exception could apply if missionary founders and privileged operator-network access outweigh a consensus category. It does not apply yet because Dock shows neither a noncompetitive entry price nor founder-specific customer pull; 26,500 LinkedIn followers have produced only one visible GitHub star. I would flip to Invest if Dock disclosed a sub-$15 million post-money entry alongside at least $50,000 MRR and greater than 120% net revenue retention from the workspace product rather than services.
