# Polemos Industries -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032: reconnaissance drones identify targets, and the units operating them have enough affordable precision weapons to act. Polemos Industries proposes connecting those drones to compact launchers and short-range ballistic missiles made on existing production lines. I can picture the changed behavior. But working backward, the essential transitions remain unverified: the connection must work reliably, and production must deliver the promised economics. The dossier supplies a description of that future, without an observed artifact showing it has begun.

“Existing production lines” is the phrase I would pull on first. Which lines, and what does Polemos control? A manufacturing process that changes the cost of precision weapons could matter enormously. Yet there are no documented manufacturing agreements, yields, or unit costs. My pattern of backing concealed infrastructure attracts me to this question; the evidence does not yet establish the infrastructure. Ares already advertises simplified manufacturing and commercial components, so affordability alone does not distinguish the company. Polemos needs to show where its particular design changes the economics.

The strongest case against passing is substantial: armed forces already describe a mismatch between targets identified and weapons they can afford, and Polemos proposes using existing reconnaissance equipment and manufacturing capacity. It may require fewer new institutions than a completely new defense platform. Lockheed Martin’s March 25, 2026 agreement intended to quadruple PrSM production capacity also makes expanding supply a concrete procurement concern. But that agreement establishes neither demand for Polemos nor its ability to supply it. The present economic wound is legible; who captures an avoidable rent, how large it is, and how much control this product returns remain unspecified.

I can invest before revenue. **CosmicBrain AI** is the relevant exception: physical technology serving a future industrial system, supported by a functioning prototype, industrial proofs of concept, and a founder’s longstanding obsession. Polemos shares the physical ambition, while the evidence that justified that exception is absent here. Its three-person team has no publicly identified founders or documented prior builds. That receives my missing-evidence prior, rather than a judgment about their ability. Likewise, the October 23 blog mention establishes attention; it supplies no completed customer action, repeated testing, or operational commitment.

I would pass with my own money today. The defense and AI labels establish neither independent obsession nor opportunistic trend chasing, and the unnamed leadership leaves accountability unresolved without establishing a governance deception. I also have no evidence of the closed-model dependence that would trigger another red flag. The decision rests on missing affirmative proof across my criteria, rather than an active contradiction of them. One verifiable condition would reopen it: a named military customer independently documenting a successful end-to-end trial and placing a paid repeat order at disclosed unit economics. That would connect the proposed system to behavior costly enough to believe.
