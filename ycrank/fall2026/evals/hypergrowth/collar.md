# Collar -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales with account expansion.** The first buyer is the investor-relations team at a large public company. The recurring job is to reconcile figures, prepare for earnings and investor meetings, and give executives answers drawn from approved materials. Collar advertises a ticker-based preview, a demo, and a pilot. If the preview reliably leads to paid pilots, Collar could reuse the same setup for each issuer, then expand from earnings preparation into more investor meetings and live calls. The memo documents the sales offer. Paid activation and expansion remain hypotheses. (The Idea; Defensibility)

Collar reports $1.4M in deals pending compliance, 30 partners whose commercial status is unspecified, and 4× month-over-month revenue growth without disclosing the months or dollar base. It also presents an anonymized representative case in which earnings preparation fell from three weeks to about a day. That example makes the potential value concrete. The memo gives no named buyer confirmation, pilot conversion, or repeat usage at the earnings-cycle cadence. (Market & Traction; Key Risks)

**Second-market assumption.** An issuer outside the U.S. could use the same document checks and approved-answer workflow if Collar can cover the relevant investor data and adapt its disclosure controls to local requirements. The memo establishes no paid second-market test. At volume, the costs to measure are sales and compliance support per issuer, document setup and validation, and compute and support per live call. Their effect on contribution is unknown. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Compliance could turn each sale into a custom implementation. The pending deals show that approval is already a hurdle, and live answers raise disclosure and accuracy concerns for each issuer. If Collar has to do substantial issuer-specific validation and provide ongoing live-call support, revenue could grow while delivery effort rises in step. The memo establishes the approval hurdle but gives no rollout cost or labor data, so the structural Pass gate is unmet. (Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent public issuer outside the U.S. convert a ticker-preview demo into a paid deployment for an earnings cycle using Collar’s standard workflow? A paid agreement, actual use during the cycle, and a record of the work Collar did to clear compliance and go live would answer it. A successful rollout with limited issuer-specific work would move the action to **Reach out**. An unresolved approval process or an unpaid pilot would keep it at **Watch**.
