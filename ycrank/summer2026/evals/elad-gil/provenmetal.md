# ProvenMetal -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Five LOIs from more than 30 conversations make ProvenMetal an execution-heavy manufacturing workflow with a relatively clean single-miracle architecture, but ProvenMetal’s lowest signal is the one I care about most in the current cycle: no paid orders, retention, or usage curve. The analytical signature is “known process, unproven demand.” ProvenMetal can coordinate domestic components, fabrication, assembly, testing, and records without inventing new physics; the open question is whether seven-day American-made PCBs are just working or merely generating polite LOIs.

**Color Genomics** is the high rung on the single-miracle ladder: workflow and data infrastructure layered over commodity sequencing, with science risk deliberately removed from the architecture. ProvenMetal shares that mechanism because ProvenMetal sits above established PCB fabrication and component supply, turning BOM and Gerber files into a managed order rather than inventing fabrication technology. ProvenMetal diverges where Color owned the operating workflow deeply enough to compound proprietary data: ProvenMetal depends on vetted U.S. partners, publishes no supplier-performance advantage, and offers an unverified 99.8% inspection-confidence claim. One miracle—reliable orchestration—remains plausible, but it has not yet been demonstrated.

**Lyft** is the low rung on the visible-curve ladder because growth that cannot establish an economically advantaged position is a false signal; I passed when the evidence did not show credible share inversion in a winner-take-all market. ProvenMetal sits only slightly above that rung: five LOIs from 30-plus conversations are meaningful at pre-seed, but ProvenMetal discloses no converted order, revenue, repeat build, or named counterparty. The shift from a benchtop X-ray inspection product in June coverage to managed PCB production in July also weakens the signal because ProvenMetal’s LOIs may validate the former product rather than the current service. In an industrial company I do not require Harvey-like velocity immediately, but I do require evidence that buyers cross the line from interest to procurement.

$98.9 billion of global PCBA volume growing at 4.3% annually is steady-state TAM math, not a market re-opening. ProvenMetal has a real directional tailwind—America’s PCB-production share fell from roughly 30% to 4%, while defense and robotics buyers increasingly care about domestic sourcing—but ProvenMetal does not identify a specific policy, procurement mandate, or technological step-function from the last 24 months that changed the purchasing equation. The score would flip upward if ProvenMetal showed that a named 2025–2026 defense rule or supply-chain restriction forced customers away from JLCPCB and produced paid seven-day orders. Without that, reshoring is a theme rather than the Stripe-after-PayPal catalyst I look for.

**Perplexity** is the relevant founder parallel because Aravind Srinivas built the discussed product between meetings; credentials mattered less than iteration velocity. ProvenMetal has better-than-generic founder-market fit: Johnny Doyle shipped IoT building-management hardware, and Will Carkner operated Lumindt’s PCB-testing pipeline after building boards from childhood. ProvenMetal therefore clears the “promoter who has never shipped” red flag, but ProvenMetal provides no dated sequence of customer feedback, product revisions, or artifacts showing that Doyle and Carkner iterate with Perplexity-like cadence. Prior hands-on work earns credit; claimed operational tempo does not.

**MacroFab** already spans more than 100 North American factories, which exposes the index-position problem. Over 18 months, ProvenMetal could compound revision histories, supplier yield data, component traceability, and repeat ordering until every additional domestic hardware program improves routing and quality—an index on U.S. hardware formation. ProvenMetal could instead remain a thin coordination layer whose seven-day promise is constrained by the same partner capacity and component availability available to MacroFab. With JLCPCB processing 21 million annual orders and no structural barrier identified, ProvenMetal currently has to win accounts against scaled platforms rather than benefiting automatically as the category grows.

Five LOIs and an open ordering flow support a falsifiable 36-month forecast: by July 2029, ProvenMetal reaches $3 million–$8 million in ARR as a domestic quick-turn PCBA managed service, but ProvenMetal does not become the default manufacturing rail for U.S. hardware companies. The mechanism is consistent with the signature: a single execution miracle can produce a useful business, while the absent buying curve and partner-dependent delivery prevent index-like escape velocity. ProvenMetal’s likely constraint is not whether boards can be built; it is whether repeat demand and gross margin compound faster than supplier complexity.

**JLCPCB’s** 21 million annual orders make the strongest bull case for ProvenMetal sharper, not weaker: geopolitical procurement could split PCB manufacturing into separate global and trusted-domestic rails, allowing ProvenMetal to become the Stripe-like API for the latter. Doyle’s manufacturing background, Carkner’s PCB-testing experience, five LOIs, seven-day delivery, and revision reuse resemble my unstated preference for workflow infrastructure on top of a hard thing. The **Anduril** exception does not yet apply, however: ProvenMetal lacks a post-Ukraine-style procurement catalyst, scaled contracts, and founders who previously shipped the relevant system at scale. ProvenMetal remains Neutral unless ten named customers generate repeat orders, revenue compounds above 20% month-over-month for three consecutive months, and disclosed gross margin plus on-time delivery demonstrate that the partner network improves rather than dilutes the economics.
