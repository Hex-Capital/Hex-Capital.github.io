# Forward -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Forward’s strongest route is a strategic sale to a private-credit marketplace such as GetLiquid. A buyer could add advisor-sourced deals to its existing funding, settlement, and reporting workflow. The memo records a customer testimonial describing introductions to unfamiliar lenders and help securing term sheets. Forward reports that its first advisor partner went exclusive after the first transaction and that 100 U.S. funds signed agreements to pay a fee on funded loans. These signals support a reason to buy if Forward can prove that its advisor relationships generate repeat funded deals. The benefit to a buyer and its willingness to pay remain inferred. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The announced US$1.5 million pre-seed and two reported transactions progressing toward close support testing for repeat funded, fee-paying loans across multiple independent advisors. The reported US$400 million in originated deals has unspecified funded volume. The reported annualized and contracted revenue figures leave currency and cash collections unspecified. The capital and time needed to scale beyond the initial advisor remain uncertain. Buyer funding, contract assignment, data permissions, and holder participation are unverified. The memo supplies no evidence of an independent route to holder liquidity. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a US$500,000 check at a US$10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires US$2.5 million to the holder, or about US$83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. Both sale scenarios below assume a close within eight years, US$5 million in combined net debt and deal adjustments, no senior claims diverting distributable equity, and an eligible holder selling their full stake for cash at closing.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Holder cash |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | US$40 million enterprise value yields US$35 million distributable equity | US$1.05 million (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | US$100 million enterprise value yields US$95 million distributable equity | US$2.85 million (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested throughout the eight-year horizon, with no funded payout or eligible share purchase | US$0 (0×) |

The sale prices are scenario assumptions. At the stated 25-basis-point fee and an illustrative 5× multiple of annual collected fee revenue, a US$100 million enterprise value would require US$20 million in annual fees, equivalent to US$8 billion in annual funded volume. The memo supports a way to earn those fees, while the funded volume and valuation needed to clear the hurdle remain unproven. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A buyer could build lender matching in-house or use an existing workflow provider if Forward’s advisor access proves hard to replicate beyond its first partner. Lev and Leverest already offer lender discovery and financing workflows, and Lev shut down its own brokerage. Forward’s close-dependent fee and concentration in one rollout partner make it plausible that lender signups and term sheets yield too little recurring revenue to justify the required sale price. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor materially inadequate proceeds under the strongest credible route. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Forward show repeat funded loans across independent advisors at fee collection rates and servicing costs that support a credible path to the acquisition hurdle? A loan-level record matching funded balances to cash receipts and servicing costs by advisor would answer it. Repeatable results beyond the initial partner, with economics that support scaling toward the hurdle, would move the action to Reach out. Evidence still concentrated in the initial partner would keep it at Watch.
