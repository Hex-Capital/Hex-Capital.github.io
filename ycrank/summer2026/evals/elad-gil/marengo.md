# Marengo -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: more than $400 billion of hyperscaler capital expenditure and 17% annual growth in data-center electricity use are forcing builders to redesign power, cooling, and capacity faster; Marengo becomes large if automated designs cut the time and cost of compliant engineering without increasing rework or reliability failures. The company claims the relevant threshold—twice the design speed at half the cost—but has not yet supplied benchmarks showing that its generated concepts survive expert review, construction, or tier-four reliability requirements.

The technical direction is credible. Generating, modifying, comparing, and stress-testing concepts through physics and simulation tools attacks a real engineering loop rather than merely formatting model output. Emil Ares and Gad Marconi also bring control systems, physics, aerospace engineering, and orbital-dynamics software experience. Ares previously built a six-figure business, while Marconi authored HITEN. This is stronger technical-founder evidence than a generic AI team, although neither founder has documented data-center design experience.

The bull case is that scarce engineering capacity has become the constraint on an expanding $21.93 billion design-services market, and Marengo is already working with some of the world’s largest data-center builders. If those engagements involve repeated design iterations, the company could acquire approval feedback and simulation state that improve the product while its first deployments create distribution into adjacent power, cooling, and facility workflows. That would convert a service bottleneck into software economics.

The opposing evidence changes the underwriting. The customer work is unnamed, with no disclosed revenue, repeat usage, completed facility, or capacity constraint. Marengo promoted space-mission design only four months before adopting its current data-center positioning. Entangl already occupies the same customer workflow, while Augmenta, SWAPP, and Hypar have raised between $8 million and $25.6 million and possess more explicit design or BIM positions. This category is crystallizing, so technical breadth alone is no longer enough; Marengo needs a sharp winner mechanism.

The central red flag is that this may remain an engineering consultancy that happens to use AI. No CAD, BIM, or document-management integrations are disclosed, and the proposed proprietary data layer is hypothetical. If experts must revalidate every generated concept and Marengo charges per project, the claimed twofold speed improvement may increase service throughput without producing software margins or reusable distribution. The recent pivot also prevents the aerospace demonstrations from answering this question.

My 36-month base case is that Marengo remains below $3 million in ARR as a project-based data-center engineering firm rather than becoming a scaled software platform. I would monitor rather than invest today. The verifiable flip condition is a named major builder deploying Marengo across at least three facilities and independently confirming twice-fast design cycles with no increase in design errors or rework.
