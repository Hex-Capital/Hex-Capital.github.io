# Deploy -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by a warehouse automation vendor is Deploy’s strongest path. A buyer could use RoboJudge and the data captured during remote interventions to add mixed-item returns, kitting, and packing to its existing installations. Ownership could be worth more than a station subscription if exclusive access to transferable intervention data lowers setup and supervision costs across the buyer’s installed base. Cross-site reuse and the resulting savings remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Deploy publicly demonstrated RoboJudge and reported packing more than 800 pens at a trade show after about 30 minutes of fine-tuning data collection. It advertises station pricing about 50% below labor cost. The memo provides no paying-customer count, realized revenue, or customer-site uptime and recovery measurements. A paid rollout across multiple sites would test whether Deploy can sustain throughput on changing item mixes while reducing remote operator time as it reuses intervention data. The demo and the founders’ relevant experience support attempting that test. Its funding, cost, and timing remain unknown, as do IP ownership and the rights to transfer customer-derived data. The memo supplies no usable acquisition pricing evidence or independently supported fallback that would pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks, Founders & Team, Defensibility)

**Illustrative proceeds.** With no investment terms disclosed, assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash. For the sale scenarios, assume no applicable net debt or other purchase-price adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. Assume a pro rata waterfall with no preference carve-outs and full cash payment at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation each contribute $0.

| Scenario | Timing and equity consideration | Gross cash to investor |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5, $40 million | $1.2 million (2.4×) |
| Strong sale | Year 7, $100 million | $3 million (6.0×) |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested | $0 (0×), shares retained |

At 3% ownership, the sale needs about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds. The hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. The modeled sale prices show the sensitivity to price and dilution. Buyer economics remain unmeasured.

### Countercase

Plus One already routes failed picks to remote operators, and Deploy offers its own capability as a monthly station service. A warehouse automation vendor could extend its existing exception handling or license Deploy’s service and capture enough of the benefit to keep an acquisition bid below the hurdle. Frequent interventions could also erode the advertised labor savings. The memo leaves open whether those substitutes can match Deploy across changing workflows or cap a bid below the hurdle. That gap warrants Watch and leaves the structural Pass override untriggered. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid rollout across multiple customer sites show that owning Deploy’s transferable intervention data would save a warehouse automation vendor enough, compared with building or licensing, to justify a price above the holder’s hurdle? Move to Reach out if the rollout sustains throughput on changing item mixes, shows lower setup and remote-operator costs at each successive site, and documents reusable, transferable data rights, with measured savings that support the required price across a plausible buyer’s installations.
