# Last Accounting Company -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Europe already spends $174.2 billion annually on accounting services. The consequence case is therefore real: turn recurring expert labor into reliable software and an enormous cost pool changes. But Last Accounting Company has not quantified that change. “Agent-native” could mean 20% fewer accountant hours or 100x more businesses served per accountant; the dossier gives neither labor savings nor customer-price compression. A large market is not automatically a large consequence.

The product is more substantial than an interface wrapper. Björn collects evidence, reconciles transactions, prepares filings, and writes to a proprietary general ledger with permissions, audit trails, an MCP server, and a REST API. More than $80 million has passed through that system across dozens of Finnish companies. Those facts establish operational reality. They do not establish a Black-Swan discontinuity: there is no measured accuracy, close-time, labor, or cost benchmark against Procountor, Fennoa, Netvisor, Pilot, Truewind, or Zeni. Human review may be a prudent safety layer—or evidence that the expensive incumbent bottleneck remains.

The strongest bull case is that this is an execution-heavy fintech wedge whose ledger becomes financial infrastructure. The shared mechanism with **Block** is that an initially modest tool can expand into a much larger financial platform; the material difference is that Last Accounting Company has not yet shown either rapid product expansion or a cost curve that compounds with volume. The MCP/API access and reviewed ledger data could become the operating substrate for payroll, tax, banking, and automated finance. That partially activates my known exception for a giant existing value pool, but an exception requires exceptional evidence. Dozens of users and $80 million of transaction flow are encouraging; transaction value is not revenue, retention, or automation performance.

The four founders have an unusually complementary architecture for this risk. Riki Karjalainen and Sami Laakkonen built petabyte-scale financial-data infrastructure at Token Terminal; Veikko Pere brings finance, consulting, and legal exposure; Pietari Virkkunen adds technology and investment experience. That is better than four generic AI résumés. Yet the dossier documents no material belief revision, no difficult specialist recruited to own accounting liability or security, and no risk-sequenced experiment. Encryption, customer-separated databases, validation rules, and accountant review are controls, not evidence that capital is attacking the largest kill risk with a numerical pass/fail threshold.

The likely 36-month outcome is a credible Nordic technology-enabled accounting firm at roughly $3 million to $8 million of ARR, not yet a globally scalable autonomous ledger. Fixed pricing may simplify buying, but no price, gross margin, accountant-hours-per-customer, or path to lower all-in cost is disclosed. This is where my unstated willingness to fund execution-heavy fintech collides with my economic gate: service revenue can grow while the automation thesis remains unproven. Dependence on OpenAI and other third-party systems further weakens the claim that the learning curve belongs to Last Accounting Company.

I pass today. The company has built something real in a real market, and it avoids the worst AI-wrapper red flag by owning the ledger and accounting workflow. But 1x downside against an unquantified upside is not yet an attractive option. My one flip condition is a six-month audited cohort of at least 50 customers showing fivefold fewer accountant hours than a Procountor-plus-accounting-firm workflow, under 0.1% material post-close corrections, and at least 70% gross margin while charging less. That would simultaneously prove the technical discontinuity and the unsubsidized economics now missing.
