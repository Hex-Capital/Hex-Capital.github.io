# HillStar -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

HillStar helps utilities draft solicitations, normalize bids, and check mandatory requirements before committee review. The building-blocks path scores zero. The memo identifies delays in acquiring power and equipment and describes how HillStar could shorten bid review. It supplies no AI deployment tied to a specific power or equipment award, no evidence that bid review controls the date usable power becomes available, and no evidence that equivalent supply is slow to secure. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The industry-enabled path also scores zero. The prior workflow involved manual proposal review and pricing reconciliation. HillStar reports processing 22 engineering-services bids in nine minutes, but the memo provides no fully loaded cost per compliant award, including specialist and committee review, or evidence that a previously excluded buyer or use became viable. Procurement specialists and an auditable record could give buyers a reason to purchase a distinct service under model parity. That remains an inference, especially given Enverus’s overlapping bid-analysis feature. Both path scores are zero, which triggers Pass. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

The strongest commercial case is recurring utility procurement. HillStar says it retains evaluator judgments, exceptions, and award rationale. Those records could make later sourcing events easier to run, although retention and switching costs are unverified. The company reports use on live solicitations, including a power solicitation involving more than 1,500 developers, with no independent buyer commitments or repeat paid use documented. The founders’ prior utility infrastructure and construction software delivery supports seed-stage execution, while dependable commercial delivery remains unverified. The memo gives no pricing or delivery-cost basis for estimating contribution on the next solicitation. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Faster bid review could bring usable power forward if an AI site is waiting on a specific utility award and that award controls the energization date. HillStar reports completing a grid-equipment evaluation in seven days and cites a prior cycle of about six months. The seven days cover evaluation, while the six months cover sourcing and evaluation. The comparison leaves the time saved through award and energization unmeasured, so it does not clear the building-blocks gate. (Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a utility buyer verify that HillStar advanced the expected energization date of an AI site by accelerating a specific power or equipment award?
