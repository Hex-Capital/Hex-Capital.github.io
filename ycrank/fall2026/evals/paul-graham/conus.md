# CONUS -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Twenty hospitals have let CONUS in. I want to know what almost kept it out. Was a manufacturer unwilling to supply documents? Did a hospital refuse to upload its protocols? What did the founders build or do that changed the answer? The dossier records deployments, but no particular obstacle followed by a timed workaround. That leaves my most important founder test unanswered. Paffrath’s AI work and Schulz’s biomedical engineering experience make this team credible; they cannot establish how the founders behave when blocked.

The product supplies better evidence. CONUS has shipped mobile applications and source-linked answers combining device instructions, literature, and hospital knowledge. The reported 500,000 papers and 2,000 manufacturer documents suggest substantial assembly work, although corpus size does not establish retrieval accuracy ([CONUS Intelligence](https://conus-med.com/en/conus-intelligence)). This fits a pattern I like: building a difficult substrate before claiming to be a platform. But the useful substrate here would be accurate mappings between a hospital’s actual implants and their current instructions. The dossier establishes a functioning product more clearly than it establishes that technical advantage.

There is a plausible narrow well of demand. Someone preparing for surgery needs the instructions for the device that will actually be used, under the standards of that particular hospital. Generic medical search may leave that job unfinished. More than 20 European hospitals reportedly live, plus manufacturer services explicitly financing operations, are meaningful early signals ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/conus), [institutional terms](https://conus-med.com/en/institution-terms-of-use)). Yet hospitals receive free licenses, and a deployment count does not tell me whether the same surgeons return. The 26 five-star iOS ratings help a little. They cannot tell me whether CONUS has become part of preparation or remains something an administrator installed.

The strongest argument for writing a check is that the founders have already crossed barriers that an outsider might underestimate. They have shipped software, entered hospitals, and established reported relationships with Johnson & Johnson, Stryker, Zimmer Biomet, and B. Braun. A working product with unmistakable pull can justify an exception to incomplete founder evidence. Here, the product is visible; the unmistakable pull is still unresolved. Nor should I turn those manufacturer relationships into a partnership red flag without evidence that growth depends on them. The sharper question is whether manufacturers pay because clinicians repeatedly need CONUS, or whether manufacturer financing can sustain a service clinicians rarely consult. Those mechanisms produce very different companies.

The founders’ experience makes the ugly integration work plausible, but the dossier does not connect CONUS’s origin to a specific problem one of them personally encountered. Five chief-surgeon founders or advisors add domain access without supplying that missing history. Likewise, the January 2025 app release and May 2026 Intelligence introduction demonstrate product development, but do not document a rejected hypothesis and an evidence-driven change of route. I give neither an invented origin story nor an invented pivot any credit. Competition from C8 Health and Incision makes the distinction consequential: CONUS needs to discover a deeply wanted workflow faster than adjacent products can extend into device information.

I would pass today. This is a shipped product with credible access, but the evidence is weakest precisely where I begin: what these founders do under resistance. One verifiable event could change my decision: a documented hospital rollout in which the founders overcome a named integration blocker within weeks, followed by sustained clinician use and unsolicited referrals to additional hospitals. That would connect resourcefulness to urgent demand. The current dossier gives me the installations without that causal chain.
