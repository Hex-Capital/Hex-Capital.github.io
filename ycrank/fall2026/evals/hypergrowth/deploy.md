# Deploy -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** Deploy targets third-party logistics operators that repeatedly handle mixed-item returns, kitting, and packing at existing stations. It advertises a monthly per-station fee and a pilot before rollout. The growth mechanism is to win a first station through direct outreach or a channel such as Fulfill, then reuse the installation and training process across more stations. If intervention data transfers across sites, RoboJudge could also reduce the remote operator time needed per station. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The memo documents a public RoboJudge demonstration. Deploy reports packing 800-plus pens at a trade-show booth after about 30 minutes of fine-tuning data collection. That is an early technical signal on a narrow task. The memo identifies a vendor listing but records no buyer commitment, paid activation through that channel, recurring production use, or station expansion. Fit with 3PL buyers remains unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Scenario assumption.** In a second geography, the same 3PL buyer could use the same station design if workflows are similar and local installation, safety requirements, and field support can be handled without substantial redesign. If Deploy realizes its advertised fee of roughly half the buyer’s comparable labor cost, that fee must cover equipment and financing, installation, maintenance, remote exception handling, and the sales and support effort for each added station. Local labor costs would affect the price the buyer could justify. The memo provides no second-market buyer evidence or production cost data to test those assumptions. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Deploy could scale as a labor- and capital-intensive deployment business. Each station may require new tuning and equipment upfront, while frequent or simultaneous exceptions could require remote staffing to rise with station count. The booth demo leaves mixed-item throughput, uptime, and recovery workload open. This does not trigger the Pass gate because the memo gives no production evidence that costs rise proportionally and no basis to rule out reuse or lower intervention rates. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paying 3PL expand from one pilot to a second station using the same deployment process and earn Deploy positive incremental contribution? A paid second station that sustains agreed throughput on the buyer’s normal mixed-item workload, with records of installation effort, equipment cost, maintenance, and remote operator time, would move Deploy to **Reach out**.
