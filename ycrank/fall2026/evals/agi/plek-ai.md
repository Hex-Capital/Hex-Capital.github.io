# Plek AI -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** Plek could make workforce diligence economical on smaller deals or early screens that PE sponsors and advisers would otherwise skip. That is a scenario assumption. Plek says manual studies take six to eight weeks and that its software maps employee records to tasks, runs scenarios in parallel, and reconciles overlapping savings employee by employee. It reports matching an adviser’s estimate of $30 million in potential annual EBITDA improvements at a 3,600-person business. That single comparison supports the plausibility of the workflow. It leaves end-to-end labor savings, employee-level accuracy, and realized savings unmeasured. *(The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)*

Plek reports four completed pilots, a deployment at a $2 billion PE fund, and advisory-firm onboarding. Those are early engagement signals. The memo leaves payment, repeat use, and demand from buyers who previously skipped diligence unverified. Bouquet’s published task-level workforce research and Carrier’s prior optimization software work support the team’s ability to build the product. Reliable delivery across customers with messy workforce data remains the execution gap. *(Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Risks)*

Under model parity, the best reason to keep paying Plek would be the ability to reuse validated workforce mappings and scenario history on subsequent studies. That advantage is inferred. The scale scenario assumes a fund or adviser orders additional studies across deals or portfolio companies. Fees from those studies must cover inference, data preparation, interviews, review, revisions, and compliance, plus the cost of free work on abandoned deals. The memo supplies neither delivery costs nor paid study frequency, so contribution per additional account remains unverified. *(Defensibility, The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)*

### Countercase

Plek may speed up diligence that sponsors already buy while leaving smaller deals uneconomic once title-to-task errors, interviews, and review are counted. Orgvue already offers M&A workforce scenarios, and Reejig already maps work at the task level. With equally capable models, an incumbent could offer comparable outputs inside a workflow buyers already use. That overlap makes substitution credible, while the memo leaves buyer switching behavior untested. Path A has no separately evidenced bottleneck that Plek controls. Both Path B conditions remain open, which supports Watch and leaves the outreach gate unmet. *(The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)*

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent PE buyers who previously skipped workforce diligence pay Plek for a second study after comparing it with a credible alternative using equally capable models? A representative trial should measure both workflows at buyer-accepted quality, count all human review and exception handling, and show that repeat fees cover delivery costs. Repeat paid preference for Plek with enough labor savings to open those transactions would support Reach out if the updated score clears the outreach threshold. Evidence that full delivery costs erase the savings or that buyers readily switch at parity would move the action to Pass.
