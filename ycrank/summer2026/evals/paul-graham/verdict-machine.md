# Verdict Machine -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what broke. Verdict Machine began POCs with asset managers and infrastructure providers in its first weeks, but the dossier does not name an obstacle, the founders’ workaround, or the resulting change ([YC profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/verdict-machine)). Starting quickly is promising. It is not yet evidence of being relentlessly resourceful. I cannot substitute Dan Danay’s résumé or the customers’ transaction volume for the story of what the founders did when reality resisted them.

The POCs imply that some product exists, but the important technical claim remains unmeasured. An AI system that blocks institutional transactions must show false-positive and false-negative rates, latency, chain coverage, and behavior under adversarial inputs. Verdict Machine discloses none of these. Meanwhile, Blockaid processes more than 180 million transactions monthly, and Hexagate operates across more than 75 chains with Chainalysis data behind it ([Blockaid](https://www.blockaid.io/about-us); [Hexagate](https://www.chainalysis.com/product/hexagate/)). A live institution-specific model could be a useful technical primitive. Today it is a description attached to POCs, not a proof spike.

The user niche is better defined than the technology. Asset managers, infrastructure providers, and large-bank digital-asset teams have a specific problem: enforce policy before an irreversible transaction executes. Founder-led POCs are the correct way to learn this market. But systems moving billions weekly do not mean Verdict Machine protects billions, and an evaluation is not yet pull. There is no named customer, payment, production deployment, repeat usage, or transaction volume processed by the product.

The strongest evidence is the founders’ prior collision with the problem. Danay and Liran Kogan built blockchain-security products at Check Point, while Danay and Isaac Grossman previously built keyTango together. This is not an “AI for crypto” idea invented from a market report. They know the schlep: custody integrations, institutional policies, compliance review, and the severe cost of an incorrect decision. Their shared history also reduces cofounder risk. But Check Point’s existing AI-assisted blockchain firewall creates both founder-market fit and a serious ownership question. The dossier found no IP dispute, yet it also found no commercial arrangement explaining the overlap.

The bull case is sharp. Experienced repeat collaborators have entered a $6.37 billion market forecast to grow 22.4% annually, just as regulatory barriers to bank crypto activity are falling, and enterprise buyers began evaluating the product within weeks ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/staff-accounting-bulletins/staff-accounting-bulletin-122); [OCC](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2025/nr-occ-2025-16.html)). Perhaps the POCs conceal exactly the technical and customer evidence that is missing publicly. But my unstated-pattern test looks for a concrete pre-round proof spike, and there is none here. The known-exception test does not rescue the company either: no independently impressive prototype or unmistakable user behavior replaces the failed resourcefulness gate. The principal red flag is not competition; it is that a polished enterprise product category may be outrunning evidence of how these founders behave when a customer or the technology says no.

I would pass today. The single flip condition is one current POC becoming a paid production deployment after a named customer blocker forces a documented product change within two weeks, with transaction volume and error rates disclosed. That one event would demonstrate the workaround, the technical proof, and the user pull now missing.
