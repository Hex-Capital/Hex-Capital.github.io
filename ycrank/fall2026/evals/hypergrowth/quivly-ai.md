# Quivly AI -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales or partner distribution with account expansion.** Quivly targets post-sales teams at usage-priced B2B software vendors that repeatedly turn kickoff material into onboarding plans and reconcile usage and support data. The memo lists CEO-led demos, workshops, and a 30-day paid Enterprise pilot. The growth hypothesis is that a repeatable pilot gets the first workflow live, and customers then put more of their accounts and post-sales work through the same product. The memo leaves the route from workshops or demos to paid activation unverified. (The Idea)

Octolane says it onboarded over 100 customers without a dedicated customer engineering hire, a concrete early signal of value for the onboarding job. Mycroft uses Quivly projects to track implementation milestones and billable hours, evidence of repeated workflow use at one buyer. Quivly reports managing more than 18,000 downstream accounts and cites 4.3× average revenue expansion as a customer outcome. Paid retention, upgrades, and realized revenue for Quivly remain unknown. (Market & Traction; Defensibility; Key Facts)

The same job could recur in another geography if buyers can reuse the connectors, launch plans, and approval settings. Language, data approvals, and local support needs remain untested in the memo. In an illustrative upgrade at listed monthly prices, moving from Starter to Growth would add $500 in monthly revenue before discounts and raise the account limit from 50 to 100. Any contribution gain would depend on incremental compute, support, and agent-review effort. For each new buyer, founder sales time and connector setup would also matter. The memo provides no measurements for those costs. (The Idea; Key Risks; Key Facts)

### Countercase

Quivly could deliver real savings for customers while still needing founder-led sales and hands-on integration at every rollout. Connector dependencies and approval requirements could add work as accounts and agent activity grow, while incumbents can offer overlapping automation inside tools buyers already use. These risks leave the paid rollout mechanism untested and support Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural limit on buyer demand nor proportional delivery costs, so the Pass gate does not apply. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent usage-priced software vendor in a second geography come through a documented sales channel, convert from the standard pilot to a paid subscription, and run a second onboarding project with the same setup? A rollout showing payment, repeat use, and recorded sales, setup, and support effort would move the action to **Reach out** if the core workflow carries over with limited custom work.
