# Synara -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a video-security platform is Synara’s strongest route to holder liquidity. Motorola Solutions acquired founder Boris Ploix’s prior video-analytics company, Calipsa, for $39 million net of cash acquired. Synara says its integrator-branded service works with existing cameras and recorders and combines AI flagging, human verification, and live intervention. A buyer could use that service to sell recurring monitoring through its dealer network. That rationale depends on Synara showing that integrators can roll it out profitably across multiple sites. The memo records a public launch and YC backing, with conflicting funding entries and no reported revenue or deployment depth. A paid multi-site rollout that measures operator cost, response performance, and site expansion is a plausible next milestone. Its cost and funding are unverified. (Founders & Team, The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Facts)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale scenarios assume an eligible holder sells the entire stake for cash by year eight and receives a pro rata payout with no senior preference reducing it. Synara’s debt, waterfall, and transfer or consent terms are unverified.

- **Modest sale.** At an illustrative $39 million enterprise value and zero net debt or deal adjustments, $39 million is distributable and the holder receives $1.17 million, or 2.34×. Calipsa’s reported price sets the scale of this scenario. (Founders & Team)
- **Strong sale.** At an assumed $100 million enterprise value less $10 million in net debt and deal adjustments, $90 million is distributable and the holder receives $2.7 million, or 5.4×. If the holder can sell only half the stake at that price, cash received falls to $1.35 million. The memo provides no Synara-specific buyer economics to substantiate the assumed price. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)
- **Prolonged private.** If Synara reinvests cash and no eligible share purchase or funded payout occurs within eight years, the holder realizes $0. The memo identifies no independent route that would pay this holder. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Becklar already sells remote guarding through a wholesale dealer channel, and Immix lets monitoring centers add AI filtering while retaining their operator workflows. Synara advertises under 30 seconds from alert to action, with illustrative footage and no measured field results in the memo. A buyer may have little reason to acquire Synara at a price that clears the holder’s hurdle if it can achieve similar results with its existing service or an add-on. The modest sale falls short, but the evidence establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a price ceiling across plausible buyers. The Pass gate is untriggered. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid multi-site integrator rollout demonstrate enough incremental gross profit over incumbent monitoring to support a strategic sale yielding at least $83.3 million in distributable equity value within eight years? Measured operator costs, response performance, and paid site expansion would answer the operating side. If those results support a credible acquisition price at the required equity value under plausible deal adjustments, move from Watch to Reach out.
