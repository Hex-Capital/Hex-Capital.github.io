# ernd -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

A 6% reward changes who pays the AI bill; it does not establish that AI becomes radically cheaper to deliver. That distinction determines my decision on ernd. The concrete benefit—doubled Firecrawl usage limits—has customer value. But neither that offer nor the advertised 2% back elsewhere demonstrates an orders-of-magnitude expansion in access to expertise or computing. The disclosed proposition is incremental purchasing leverage. There is no quantified consequence large enough to make a very low probability of success attractive to me.

The strongest objection is that purchasing leverage can compound. Ten startups and five vendor partnerships in the first 48 hours, even as company-reported figures, suggest the founders can bring both sides together quickly. More buyers could attract better offers; better offers could attract more buyers. My actual investing pattern includes execution-heavy financial businesses. **Block** is a relevant exception: a narrow payments wedge could expand into financial infrastructure. The shared mechanism is entry through an existing spending relationship. The material difference is that ernd has not established why that relationship becomes a platform it controls, rather than an offer Ramp or Slash can reproduce through their existing distribution. Negotiating individual agreements takes work; the dossier establishes neither exclusivity nor durable switching costs.

The team deserves a hearing. Ruia’s Ripple stablecoin work, Dawson’s Bridgewater AI work, and Wengel’s industrial investing experience connect payments, AI, and finance. Those credentials make the initial execution credible. They do not document a belief changed after contrary evidence or a difficult complementary recruit brought in to own a specific weakness. I give the missing process evidence its default score. Likewise, the rapid launch establishes activity, but the dossier supplies no decisive experiment, cost, or threshold against the central uncertainty: whether purchasing aggregation produces a durable advantage after incentives normalize. There is also no measured technical discontinuity against Ramp or Slash; the disclosed differentiation rests on negotiated benefits.

The economics could break the proposed compounding loop. The October 27 rewards terms say ernd funds OpenAI and Anthropic rewards, with new points potentially pending when budgets run out. Redemption restrictions also make the headline reward less useful than unrestricted cash. These facts do not prove permanent subsidy dependence. They do mean that signups alone cannot establish sustainable demand. Partner-funded rewards offer a possible economic bridge, but retained margins, servicing costs, and customer pricing are undisclosed. I cannot infer unsubsidized parity from a promotional rate.

I would pass with my own money. The active mismatch is the modest disclosed consequence and commercially negotiated differentiation; the missing revenue history is normal for this stage. The known financial-platform exception does not overcome those weaknesses yet. My single flip condition is a verified cohort test showing that pooled volume delivers a repeatable 5x reduction in cost per equivalent AI workload, with positive contribution margin after every reward and servicing cost, sustained for six months without ernd-funded promotions. That would turn purchasing leverage into evidence of a consequential cost mechanism.
