# GrayPass -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — Repeatable sales with account expansion** is the strongest fit. A plausible first buyer is the security engineering team at a payout platform that needs to verify who controls a session before releasing funds. GrayPass documents an intake form and a shadow-mode evaluation for one protected action. If that evaluation shows enough value to justify paid enforcement, the same account could add data exports or permission changes. Pricing metered by assured actions gives GrayPass a way to earn more as coverage grows. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

GrayPass reports more than 900,000 customer-app sessions and $50,000 in October 2026 revenue as of launch. Those are early usage and budget signals. The memo leaves open how many independent buyers paid, which workflows reached live enforcement, and whether any account renewed or expanded. In a hypothetical Canada-to-U.S. rollout, the payout workflow could use the same decision API and action-bound proof. The customer would still have to wire the decision into its authorization flow, and an Illinois deployment collecting covered biometric signals would require affirmative consent and retention and deletion procedures. Buyer demand and implementation effort across markets remain unverified. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

The strongest downside case is that every new account and every added action requires substantial integration work. Customers must wire each protected workflow into server-side authorization, and GrayPass limits its experimental behavioral signals to sandbox and shadow evaluation. If each rollout also requires GrayPass engineering time for integration and policy tuning, expansion could grow delivery labor alongside revenue. Each added workload would also add decision-processing and support costs. Buyers already have substitutes for transaction-time risk decisions, so live deployments need to show what action-bound proof adds. The memo gives no rollout cost or marginal contribution data, so it does not establish the structural ceiling that would trigger Pass. (*Key Risks*, *Defensibility*)
