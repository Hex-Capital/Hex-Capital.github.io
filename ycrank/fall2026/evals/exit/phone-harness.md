# Phone Harness -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A real-device testing provider such as BrowserStack could buy Phone Harness’s hosted iPhone and Android service to add native-app automation for agents. Phone Harness advertises a shared command interface across local and hosted devices, with hosted devices that preserve apps and logins between runs. The memo records the cloud beta launch and 5,100 GitHub stars. The purchase rationale is conditional. If paying teams repeatedly run workflows on dedicated devices, owning the hosted service and those customer relationships could give a buyer recurring usage and save the work of building and operating an equivalent fleet. The memo gives no paying-customer count, retention, or measured buyer benefit. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A cohort still paying and running permitted workflows after 90 days, with revenue covering direct device and operating costs, would make the asset easier to value. The launched beta gives Phone Harness a way to run that test. Fleet costs, burn, and available capital are unknown, so the funding needed to reach meaningful scale is unverified. The memo also leaves the transferability of hosted operations and customer agreements open. It provides no independent evidence of sponsor-ready cash flow, an eligible secondary, public-market readiness, or cash available for shareholder payouts. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

Under the illustrative seed terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-ons. The eight-year target is $2.5 million in gross cash. It requires $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. With $5 million of assumed net debt, the 3% case requires about $88.3 million in enterprise value before any fees or senior claims. A $25 million sale at year eight yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), while a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). These are scenarios. The memo’s cloud prices provide no basis for an acquisition price. If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested throughout the eight years, the holder realizes $0 and retains the shares. (Key Facts, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

A buyer can fork the MIT-licensed local harness and pair it with its own devices or an existing fleet provider. Mobilerun already bundles hosted phones with an agent task runner, and third-party app rules can narrow the set of workflows customers may automate. That makes the purchase case depend on operating value beyond the code, especially recurring paid workloads. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported sale price that leaves the holder materially short, so the Pass gate does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
