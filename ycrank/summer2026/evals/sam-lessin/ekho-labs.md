# Ekho Labs -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

I cannot price this check because Ekho Labs discloses neither the proposed post-money valuation nor competing offers nor whether YC is the first scarce capital. That is not evidence of an auction, but it leaves my primary gate unanswered. More importantly, there is no use-of-funds plan naming the finite experiment this round buys. “Improve forecasts and sell enterprise SaaS” is a company plan, not a venture moment. I need the specific route, customer cohort, deadline, and pass/fail threshold that turn $500,000 into newly revealed truth.

The product has a potentially real technical wedge. Ekho combines more than 10,000 public and licensed sources and claims a 12-day median warning lead time, while its Hormuz case study reports seven confirmed forecasts and one ceasefire call 21 days early. Those figures suggest that Alina Park can build intelligence machinery, but they do not yet establish decision value. The case study is company-authored; false-positive and false-negative rates are absent; and there is no evidence that a customer rerouted a PO, avoided an emergency-freight charge, or renewed because of a forecast. Prediction without an observed economic action is still a research product.

The strongest bull case is the founder. Park was founding technical lead at Inca Digital, built enterprise financial-intelligence systems, and previously developed multilingual text-geolocation technology. Ekho therefore is not three people wrapping a news feed in an LLM. She has shipped on the data-ingestion and enterprise-intelligence side of the bridge. But the other side remains thin: the dossier shows no prior ocean-freight operation, carrier deployment, procurement ownership, or routing budget under her control. Advisor Renée Ure’s Lenovo operating history helps access; it does not substitute for the founder having owned freight decisions.

The claimed clients and enterprise customers provide limited evidence that the system has entered real workflows, and read-only TMS, ERP, and visibility integrations could eventually create a proprietary decision-and-outcome loop. Today that loop is hypothetical. ZEBOX Club membership, 71 LinkedIn followers, a biweekly brief, and a RANE podcast appearance are distribution surfaces, not proof that owned access causes purchases. Meanwhile Everstream already reported more than 200 enterprise customers and offers predictive and prescriptive supply-chain analytics. Ekho’s narrower PO-level routing action must produce measurable customer behavior before the broader intelligence stack becomes a defensible dark pool.

Delete the next financing and the underwriting breaks. There is no disclosed revenue, pricing, gross margin, burn, runway, or controllable cash metric. Licensed data and stack-specific enterprise onboarding may create meaningful costs before repeatable sales emerge. The ugly 36-month outcome is not necessarily death; it is Ekho becoming a bespoke geopolitical-intelligence service with software attached, because forecasts are easier to demonstrate than automated routing decisions are to integrate, trust, and monetize.

I am passing at this evidence level. The case can flip on one verifiable result: by a dated six-month checkpoint, at least three named freight customers must have paid for Ekho and documented PO-level actions attributable to its alerts—such as reroutes or avoided emergency-freight costs—with renewal or expansion tied to those savings. That would simultaneously validate the workflow, establish the venture experiment, and begin answering whether this can finance itself. Forecast accuracy alone will not do it.
