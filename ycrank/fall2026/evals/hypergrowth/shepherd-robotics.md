# Shepherd Robotics -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit, Shepherd’s strongest path is to automate repeatable rack cable routing for data center operators and expand across sites within the same account. The buyer and workflow plausibly recur across markets. Shepherd’s modular hardware, pretrained manipulation model, and local adaptation pipeline give it a reusable starting point, while equipment variation leaves the amount of site-specific work uncertain. The memo records an October product demonstration, and Shepherd reported 95% cable-routing success in a bench test. Paid production use and the pace of commercialization are unknown, so initial traction receives the 7-point default. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

Shepherd seeks introductions and takes direct inquiries. Technician demonstrations and supervised setup make onboarding high-touch, even with a reusable adaptation process. Locally adapted policies could support continued use on recurring work. A deployment fee plus recurring support is a scenario assumption, and expansion revenue would depend on customers paying for additional sites or capacity. Under that scenario, contribution per added site would be deployment and support revenue less attributable selling, hardware, installation, local training, supervision, and maintenance costs. Reusing hardware designs and reducing adaptation and exception-handling time could improve contribution. The memo provides no measured site-level economics. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Even with strong demand, each site could require fresh demonstrations, annotation, integration, and sustained technician oversight. Shepherd reports that some customer equipment is available only on site, and its 60% bench success on cable insertion shows how sharply performance can vary across related tasks. A broader commissioning deployment could therefore add labor and hardware capacity with every sale. The structural gate stays open for the narrower routing path because the shared hardware and adaptation process give Shepherd a concrete way to limit repeat-site work. The amount and cost of that work remain uncertain. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paying operator expand rack cable routing to additional sites using the same hardware and a standard setup, with limited technician time and positive contribution at each added site? A paid multi-site rollout with recorded setup hours, ongoing interventions, and site-level contribution would answer it. Repeatable deployment would justify rescoring and a move to Reach out if the score and confidence clear that threshold. If recurring site-specific work makes faster growth economically infeasible, move to Pass.
