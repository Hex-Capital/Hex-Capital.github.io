# Definite -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Definite’s market opportunity is that agents make more finance work automatable, while finance buyers still need consistent definitions across ledgers, operational systems, and spreadsheets. The controlling variable is how much additional work each customer’s financial model supports without additional implementation labor. If every reconciliation requires another custom mapping exercise, Definite inherits consulting economics. If the same retained model supports successive workflows cheaply, the first deployment becomes distribution for the next product. The dossier identifies the underlying problem but does not establish that reuse or its economic threshold. I would pass with my own money today.

The strongest argument against that decision is the architecture. Definite documents customer-specific mappings, deterministic calculations, source-to-cell lineage, retained rules, and historical snapshots. These are meaningful workflow assets: a stronger model could improve mapping and explanations while leaving the financial definitions and audit history inside Definite. This fits my pattern of funding infrastructure around a technology discontinuity. But read-only access and human approvals also limit how much completed work the company currently owns. The evidence supports durable context more strongly than responsibility for an end-to-end financial outcome.

The economic discontinuity remains unproven. The earlier regulatory-reporting product reportedly beat GPT-5 on FinBalance at approximately 50× lower cost using DeepSeek V3 and a verification engine. That is a company-reported benchmark for a different product configuration, not evidence of current deployment costs or customer savings. Scoped connector development could consume the apparent automation benefit. Meanwhile, Workato already offers connectivity and semantic context, and SAP’s May 2026 n8n integration brings orchestration into incumbent distribution. Definite needs to show that its finance specialization changes implementation economics enough to overcome that distribution advantage. Competitor funding totals do not answer that question.

The September 2026 founder interview contains more decision-relevant demand evidence than the 297 LinkedIn followers: demos failed to convert, and earlier compliance-product LOIs did not establish purchase commitment. Those are adverse sales observations, although they do not establish churn in the current product. Contract-first selling is a sensible response. Monthly terms, no deployment fee, and a two-month opt-out describe the offer; they do not establish paying adoption or repeat usage. The strongest bull case is that those terms force Definite to deliver value quickly, while retained mappings make each subsequent task cheaper. No current customer cohort demonstrates that mechanism yet.

The founders’ experience at BitGo, Optiver, Boosted.ai, and Meta provides relevant technical context, particularly around controls and financial-analysis agents. It does not document costly personal commitment or prior startup learning sufficient for a founder premium. Nor does Definite qualify for my exceptions based on frontier research, scarce model-building talent, or demonstrated reconstruction of incumbent margins. The relevant red flag is implementation-heavy delivery claiming scalable software economics; it remains an unresolved risk rather than a proven defect. My single flip condition is a verified cohort of three unrelated paying finance teams, each running two recurring workflows on the same retained model for 90 days, with deployment and support labor below 20% of contract revenue. That is my underwriting threshold, not a reported company result.
