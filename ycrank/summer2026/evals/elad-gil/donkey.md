# Donkey -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: tariff complexity and opaque intermediary pricing push US importers toward a factory-direct counterparty, and Donkey becomes large only if its delivered-cost advantage remains material after inspection, freight, customs, credit, and claims. The governing belief is therefore falsifiable: identifying the original factory must produce repeatable net savings—not merely a cheaper initial quote—while leaving Donkey an attractive risk-adjusted margin.

There is early evidence for that mechanism. Donkey produced one quote 15% below a restaurant supplier’s price for goods from the same factory, against claimed intermediary markups of 15–30%. Its product also owns more than a sourcing interface: the company identifies factories, takes title, inspects goods, clears customs, extends qualified buyers net-45 terms, and delivers to the dock. Better entity resolution and accumulated supplier histories could improve this entire transaction loop. This is an integrated trading operation, not an AI output formatter.

The missing number is contribution margin. A 15% customer saving does not establish how much remains after ocean freight, inspections, credit insurance, replacements, and classification errors. Donkey’s physical execution and working-capital exposure could recreate trading-company economics with better software attached. The tariff argument is also less reliable than presented because its illustrative 37.5% calculation still includes a temporary surcharge whose authority expired July 24, 2026. The relevant technology shift is public-record entity resolution, but the dossier does not yet show that it changes industry margins or scales operations nonlinearly.

Demand is similarly promising but unproven. Donkey quoted $110,000 in its first sales week and described purchase orders as “signing now”; neither is recognized revenue, repeat usage, or evidence that importers will entrust recurring orders to a seven-person counterparty. The stronger signal belongs to the founders rather than the current product: Prelude OS acquired 31 paying Chinese factories in 39 days and became cash-flow positive. Martindale’s Mandarin, factory-sales experience, and distributor visits from Vermont to San Francisco combine with Tan’s customs-data work to show unusually direct learning and commitment.

The bull case is that this team has already sold successfully into one side of the market and has now crossed the transaction boundary, turning supplier access into a reusable distribution channel for procurement, financing, quality control, and logistics. Inspectors across seven provinces make the operating claim more credible than a typical pre-seed sourcing marketplace. But none of the known exceptions rescues the underwriting gap: this is not frontier research with scarce technical talent, and the physical-market exception requires demonstrated product-style economics. The claimed data moat is also presently reproducible in principle; Donkey has not quantified how many orders, dollars, or months a well-funded competitor would need to recreate its supplier histories.

I would monitor rather than invest today. The company clears the initial market gate, but founder strength and workflow breadth are running ahead of current-product pull and verified economics. My flip condition is 20 importers completing at least two orders each while Donkey demonstrates positive contribution margin after freight, inspection, credit losses, and claims. That would show that the same-factory savings compound into a scalable trading system rather than being consumed by the risks Donkey assumes.
