# Proto -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Proto says product designers have moved into developer IDEs to build interactive prototypes. The memo describes a workflow that turns prompts and inputs such as Linear tickets or Notion requirements into prototypes designers can compare, map into flows, and share. Assuming strong fit with those designers, the same workflow could serve teams in multiple markets that use similar tools. Supported languages and setup requirements across different codebases remain unclear. (The Idea)

The memo records a November 6 early-access announcement, alpha signup promotion, and about 161,000 views on the founder’s launch post. Those observations show attention for a two-person team. The steps from signup to first value and payment remain unverified, and the memo leaves paid use and revenue status unknown. Sharing could bring collaborators into Proto, and saved variants and flow maps could give teams a reason to return across projects. Both are inferences. Pricing and same-account spending remain unknown. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Facts; Defensibility)

Under a hypothetical self-serve model, incremental contribution would depend on what the next team pays relative to the cost of generating and hosting prototypes and providing any setup or support it needs. The memo gives too little information to estimate that contribution. (The Idea; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Figma’s Code Layers already puts code-backed prototypes where design teams work. Proto could win initial trials for variant comparison and still lose repeat use if designers prefer to review prototypes in Figma or if handing off generated code creates extra engineering work. Proto says it splits prototypes into smaller pull requests, but the memo leaves the review burden unresolved. The structural gate stays open because teams can plausibly get value from comparing and sharing prototypes before production handoff, and the memo gives no specific evidence that serving those teams requires substantial rebuilding by market or economically prohibitive work per buyer. (Defensibility; Key Risks; The Idea)

### Decisive diligence question

Can design teams that discover Proto through a shared prototype become repeat paying users without founder help? A cohort across multiple markets that independently creates and shares a first prototype, pays, and returns for a second paid project would test the proposed growth path. Move to Reach out if the cohort evidence brings the score to at least 65 and confidence to Medium or High, with the structural gate still clear.
