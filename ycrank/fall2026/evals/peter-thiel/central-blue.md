# Central Blue -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

What does Central Blue know about supplying spacecraft power that other capable engineers believe is wrong? The investable proposition would be that purchased orbital energy beats additional onboard generation and storage for a particular spacecraft mission, after accounting for receiver mass, heat and interrupted line of sight. That is my proposed test; the dossier supplies neither a named opposing belief nor a completed experiment establishing the answer. HELION’s targets—300 kWh of storage and 10 kW delivered for 4–5 hours—describe an intended machine. They do not yet reveal a secret.

The distinction matters because Central Blue must persuade customers to change spacecraft designs before its network exists. Dedicated receivers introduce an integration dependency; customers also retain generation and batteries between deliveries. Star Catcher’s existing-array approach offers a competing answer to that dependency, and its reported October 1, 2026 demonstration-payload launch puts competitive execution into the present tense. These systems are technically differentiated, so I would not dismiss them as interchangeable commodities. But Central Blue identifies no bounded customer segment it can dominate, and neither exclusive technology nor an operating network is demonstrated. Receiver switching costs remain a hypothesis until customers adopt receivers.

Brian Wu and Jeewoo Choi provide the strongest affirmative evidence. Their collaboration on planetary-exploration robotics predates the public company launch, giving the partnership some substance beyond complementary résumés. That supports modest credit for founding alignment. It does not establish years of shared execution, full-time status or economic exposure; those remain unknown, with no evidence of part-time founders, outsourced core engineering or investor-dominated ownership. Their autonomy and aerospace-testing backgrounds also make the strongest opposing case credible: this team could complete a difficult prototype and discover a valuable application through actual engineering.

Central Blue’s energy-infrastructure ambition fits my preference for neglected physical frontiers. Panthalassa is the relevant exception to caution about coordinating several difficult systems: both proposals combine energy production, hardware deployment and infrastructure demand. The material difference is that the exception rested on a specific pilot-factory and deployment path tied to an immediate compute-power constraint. Central Blue reports an incomplete terrestrial prototype, a planned Wave Motion demonstration and a 2027 Blackwing demonstration of CORE software rather than power delivery. There is no completed technical event or persistent customer behavior here that removes the coordination risk. The earlier standalone CORE positioning also makes autonomy software a credible eventual destination if orbital power proves uneconomic.

The commercial proposition remains similarly undefined. Mission-specific power contracts are inferred; pricing, delivery costs and margins are undisclosed. A power-buyer intake form establishes an invitation to discuss a mission, without demonstrating a repeatable acquisition route or implementation economics. I give no deduction merely for having no revenue at pre-seed. The investment problem is that Central Blue has not yet connected a particular customer’s avoided costs to an economically deliverable service, so neither power-law arithmetic nor distribution earns affirmative credit.

I would pass today. The strongest bull case is that complementary founders turn the nearly completed prototype into proof that receiver integration buys customers something sufficiently valuable to justify redesigning a spacecraft. My single condition for reopening diligence is a named operator’s paid acceptance of an end-to-end power-delivery trial, against agreed delivered-power, availability and integration-cost requirements showing an advantage over its onboard alternative. That would test the reason for this company to exist. Completing a demonstration without that economic test would leave the investment question open.
