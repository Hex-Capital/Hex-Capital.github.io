# WonderTx -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is what broke. WonderTx reports four experimentally confirmed binders against zero-training-data targets, but the dossier never names an obstacle, the founders’ workaround, or the time from blockage to changed result. Abraham Heifets, Saulo de Oliveira, and Cameron Ferroni have formidable résumés and previously worked together at Atomwise. That proves relevant experience and an established working relationship. It does not prove relentless resourcefulness. I would not infer behavior from credentials.

The technical proof is much stronger. WonderTx has connected extrapolative models to a design-make-test loop and obtained binders across four biologically and structurally diverse targets, with evidence ranging from screening hits to crystallographic structures. The reported 10× reduction in compound-selection time is also a measured operational result. This resembles the mechanism behind the Bindwell exception: deep exposure plus numerical laboratory validation can justify taking an unfamiliar or unusually risky scientific claim seriously. The important difference is that WonderTx has not disclosed independent reproduction, selectivity, oral bioavailability, toxicity, or a compound ready for clinical development. It has proved that the engine can find binders, not that it can make pills.

The narrow well remains indistinct. Internal and partner programs imply that someone beyond the founding team has used the platform, but the partners are unnamed and there is no disclosed payment, repeat usage, program count, or decision made because of a WonderTx result. “Patients who need pills instead of injections” identifies the eventual beneficiary, not the immediate customer willing to tolerate the crude product. The likely buyer is a pharmaceutical discovery organization, yet the dossier does not show one pulling the product back through repeated experiments.

The origin is more convincing than the demand. All three founders worked at Atomwise, and their combined experience covers computational drug discovery, structural biology, automated experimentation, and biotechnology operations. This is not a generic AI-for-drugs idea manufactured from a $200 billion TAM slide. They have lived near the problem. The wedge is also full of work competitors would prefer to avoid: wet-lab cycles, chemistry, selectivity, manufacturing, regulatory development, and eventually clinical trials. But WonderTx has not documented the particular failure at Atomwise or elsewhere that caused this approach, so I give less credit than I would to a dated founder collision followed by a new method.

The strongest bear case is that four binder results are a scientific demo inside a field where Terray has measured more than five billion interactions, Iambic is already clinical-stage, and Isomorphic Labs has raised $2.7 billion. WonderTx could be technically clever without becoming a company: binders may fail on potency, selectivity, pharmacokinetics, safety, or economics, while pharmaceutical partnerships become the distribution plan rather than evidence of distribution. The $200 billion opportunity does nothing to resolve this. Nor does the dossier show the running-back behavior required to navigate the many failures between a hit and a drug.

I am neutral rather than passing because the concrete proof spike, repeat technical team, and schlep-heavy origin create a plausible exception to ordinary pre-seed uncertainty. But they do not override the operational gate yet. The verifiable flip condition is simple: disclose one named target program in which a failed initial hypothesis caused the team to ship a materially different approach within weeks, producing a partner-validated compound with quantified potency and selectivity. That would demonstrate both the founder behavior and external pull currently missing. I would not write the check today.
