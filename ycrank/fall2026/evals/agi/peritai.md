# Perit.AI -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** Perit’s strongest case is qualified capacity for live, multilingual voice evaluation. Perit says static test sets miss failures that surface during interruptions, accent variation, and noisy calls. If cheaper models lead labs to test more voice agents across more locales, recruiting speakers, securing consent, and calibrating reviewers could become a bottleneck. Labs would bear that cost in staff time or delayed evaluations. The memo describes a concrete testing workflow, with the effect on lab timelines still unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Perit reports a roster of 650 transcribers and aligners and uses Foundry to calibrate reviewers and check batches. A roster that repeatedly clears a lab’s acceptance criteria could shorten future delivery, though the memo gives no evidence of contributor exclusivity or retention. Perit reports six lab customers and multimillion-dollar contracts. The memo gives two unreconciled revenue figures for the same 4.5 months, $11M+ from Perit and $9M+ in YC’s announcement. Those figures support early commercial activity, with repeat purchases and the share attributable to live voice evaluation still unknown. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The founders bring localization and model-data quality experience, and Perit reports 95%+ accuracy on speech QA samples. The expansion unit is an accepted audio hour. Perit quotes by delivery unit, itemizes contributor pay, and reports delivering 50 audio hours per working day. Scaling across locales would require enough paid utilization for the realized price per accepted hour to cover contributor pay, reviewer time, consent handling, and rework. The memo supplies throughput figures, with realized unit prices and delivery costs still unknown. (Founders & Team, The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Perit could be a capable data vendor in a market where labs can switch suppliers or assemble equivalent capacity in-house. The memo identifies no structural barrier, describes non-exclusive licensing as the default, and lists established alternatives in adjacent robotics and RL data services. Those observations weaken the case for durable control. A matched comparison on live voice quality and turnaround is missing, so the evidence leaves the scarcity gate open and supports Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Move to **Reach out** if Perit alone meets the lab’s agreed quality and turnaround requirements and the repeat order covers full delivery costs.
