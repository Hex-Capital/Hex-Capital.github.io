# GrayPass -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a fraud or identity platform. LexisNexis’s acquisition of BehavioSec shows that this buyer class has bought adjacent behavioral-risk technology. GrayPass documents short-lived, signed proof tied to a specific application action. The acquisition rationale is that a platform could use that capability to help customers secure payouts, exports, and permission changes after login. The memo shows a precedent for buying adjacent technology and leaves GrayPass’s incremental value to a buyer unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

GrayPass reports processing more than 900,000 sessions and generating $50,000 in October 2026 revenue as of launch. Its shadow-mode evaluation gives it a concrete path to a paid production test that measures whether enforcement reduces unauthorized actions without excessive friction for legitimate users. The behavioral signals remain experimental, the core API is in beta, and customers have to integrate enforcement into each protected workflow. The disclosed $100,000 investment provides a funding signal, with runway and rollout costs unquantified. A software acquisition is plausible, with customer contract transfer terms and permissions for continued use of customer-scoped biometric data unresolved. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks, Defensibility)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in gross cash by year eight, or roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The memo provides neither a transaction price for BehavioSec nor quantified buyer economics for GrayPass, leaving the proceeds dimension at its absent-evidence default. The sale cases below are sensitivities. Both assume the holder is eligible to sell the full stake for cash by year eight and receives 3% after the stated deductions, with no additional senior claims. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

- **Modest sale.** $40 million in enterprise value less $5 million in assumed debt and deal adjustments leaves $35 million for equity and $1.05 million in holder cash, or 2.1×.
- **Strong sale.** $100 million in enterprise value less $10 million in assumed debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million for equity and $2.7 million in holder cash, or 5.4×. An additional $10 million senior claim would reduce the common equity pool to $80 million and holder cash to $2.4 million, or 4.8×.
- **Prolonged private.** If GrayPass keeps operating and reinvests available cash through year eight, the holder realizes $0. The memo supplies no evidence of an independent route to holder liquidity.

### Countercase

Incumbents may already cover enough of the workflow to make buying GrayPass uneconomic. Castle offers transaction-time policy decisions, LexisNexis already bundles behavioral signals with device and identity intelligence, and GrayPass’s founder says incumbents could build the capability. GrayPass also depends on customers integrating enforcement workflow by workflow. Together, these facts support Watch while GrayPass’s incremental production value remains unmeasured. The Pass gate would require a demonstrated structural blocker or supported proceeds materially below the hurdle. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can paid production deployments show enough incremental value over existing transaction controls to make acquisition economically attractive to a fraud or identity platform? Repeatable reductions in unauthorized actions at an acceptable challenge rate, together with buyer economics that plausibly support the holder’s cash hurdle after deal adjustments, would move the action to Reach out.
