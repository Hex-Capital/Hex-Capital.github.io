# Canos -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A veterinary diagnostics distributor such as Antech could pay to own Canos’s canine cancer assay and longitudinal dataset. Antech’s distribution of Nu.Q establishes a relevant sales channel. The potential benefit is incremental testing revenue if Canos can use the same assay for both screening and repeat monitoring. Ownership could be worth more than licensing if control of the assay and rights to the growing dataset materially improve that benefit. The memo provides no evidence of buyer interest or a measured advantage over the distributor’s existing options. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Canos reports 90.6% sensitivity at 95.1% specificity in retrospective cross-validation and says 10 clinics and mobile vets are onboarding. These are early signals for a prospective rollout in paying clinics. The operating milestone is to show that results change veterinary management and generate paid repeat testing at viable margins. The memo gives no funded validation plan, cost, or timeline. The founder reports a patent filing, while ownership and transferability of the assay IP and study data remain unverified. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility)

**Proceeds bridge.** Actual investor terms are unavailable. The scenario assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million for the holder, or $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership. That hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% and falls to $62.5 million at 4%.

| Year-eight scenario | Gross holder cash at 3% |
|---------------------|-------------------------|
| $25 million enterprise-value sale, less $5 million illustrative net debt | $600,000 (1.2×) |
| $100 million enterprise-value sale, less $5 million illustrative net debt | $2.85 million (5.7×) |
| Growth plateaus, buyer interest fails to emerge, public scale remains out of reach, and all available cash is reinvested | $0, with shares still held |

The sale scenarios assume pro rata distribution before investor taxes. Company-specific debt, fees, and senior claims are unverified. At 3% ownership, the illustrative debt puts the enterprise-value hurdle at $88.3 million. Listed test prices establish what Canos charges, while paid volume, margins, buyer economics, and comparable deal prices remain unevidenced. The memo also provides no basis to underwrite a cash-generating sponsor buyout, an eligible secondary, public resale, or a funded dividend or buyback. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Antech already distributes a canine blood-screening test, and CanCan already offers sequencing-based monitoring. A distributor could cover both uses through commercial partnerships. PetDx’s comparable sequencing business stopped processing samples after raising $72 million, which makes Canos’s ability to turn retrospective performance into sustained paid demand the central risk. The memo does not establish that existing tests can match Canos’s combined workflow, that a transfer is blocked, or that the strongest supportable sale price would leave the holder short. The Pass gate is therefore untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a prospective rollout in paying clinics give a veterinary diagnostics distributor enough incremental value to justify buying Canos at a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? Evidence that results change veterinary management and drive repeat orders at viable margins, paired with distributor economics that favor ownership over licensing and support a price range above the modeled hurdle, would move the action to Reach out.
