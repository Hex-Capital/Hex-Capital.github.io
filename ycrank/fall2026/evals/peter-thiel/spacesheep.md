# Spacesheep -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Spacesheep’s potentially important truth is that agent-generated work needs an independent collaboration surface, separate from the agents that produce it. That proposition is testable: do teams continue reviewing work in Spacesheep when their existing workspace offers comparable publishing and commenting? The dossier establishes a working product, but neither a rejected prevailing belief nor an experiment demonstrating this preference. A plausible secret remains to be discovered.

The ownership problem is more immediate. Spacesheep accepts multiple agents and publishes individually designed browser surfaces. Yet Notion already supports external agents and interactive HTML blocks, while Anthropic provides artifact creation, hosting, sharing, and modification. The competitive overlap is documented; the proposed switching costs are inferred. No bounded customer segment already exhibits proprietary technology, network effects, or another observable monopoly mechanism. Being the neutral surface between agents could become valuable. Neutrality alone does not explain why Spacesheep keeps that value.

The strongest objection to passing is Michael Makarov. He built internal deployment tooling and the first CC Artifacts version at Anthropic, after engineering roles at Pinecone and Twitter. He has worked directly on the infrastructure whose limitations Spacesheep addresses. That makes the product thesis substantially more credible than a generic productivity pitch. It also invites my operator-network pattern, although the dossier does not establish a company assembled through a durable founding network. Team size is two; the second person’s role, shared working history, full-time commitments, and ownership remain unspecified. Those are unknowns, not founding red flags. His technical experience cannot substitute for evidence of economic alignment.

Spacesheep has made distribution concrete enough to examine: connectors, CLI installation, browser extensions, public sharing, and a YC offer. James Briggs is a named user of coding plans, explanations, and reviews. These are better evidence than merely saying “product-led growth.” But the October 27 snapshot—22 Chrome users and 180 Product Hunt upvotes—does not establish repeat acquisition or paid conversion. At $10 per member per month, $100 million in annual subscription revenue would require roughly 833,000 paying seats before discounts. Published pricing identifies one revenue engine; customer expansion and margins have yet to establish its power-law potential.

I checked the exceptions before concluding. Spacesheep has neither an independently demonstrated technical breakthrough nor persistent paid transaction behavior that directly resolves the monopoly concern. Its shipped product and founder expertise make the bull case serious: an independent workspace could accumulate review history across competing agents and become the place teams actually work. The dossier does not yet demonstrate that accumulation or its effect on retention. My pass rests on documented competitive convergence, not the absence of mature-company metrics. One verifiable result would change my decision to investigate: a paid cohort of teams using multiple agents that retains Spacesheep for six months despite access to comparable incumbent features, with usage records showing accumulated review history drives that retention.
