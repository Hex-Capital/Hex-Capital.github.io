# STASIS -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by an eyewear or smart-glasses maker is the strongest private-company path. The potential asset is an integrated system that uses inward-facing sensors to estimate eye fatigue and adjust lens tint or connected screens. If production use shows sustained symptom improvement and the models and data rights can transfer, ownership could give a buyer an exclusive feature and shorten its development time across multiple product lines. The founder reports discussions with eyewear brands. Those discussions establish a potential buyer class, with acquisition interest and willingness to pay still unverified. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*)

The memo records prototype coverage, an open reservation checkout with a refundable $99 deposit, and confirmed YC backing. March 2027 shipment is the company’s target. STASIS also reports over 90% accuracy for fatigue and cognitive load, with the reference measure and validation protocol undisclosed. A production pilot measuring sustained use and symptom improvement is a plausible next milestone. Paid conversion, manufacturing funding, and runway through the pilot remain unverified. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Key Risks*)

**Proceeds bridge.** The memo supplies no terms for this investor’s check, so the illustrative case uses $500,000 at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

These scenarios are assumptions for testing the hurdle. The sale cases assume enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration, with zero net debt, no other closing adjustments, and simple pro rata allocation without a preference adjustment. All consideration is cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each $0.

| Scenario | Timing | Cash to holder | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale at $40 million enterprise value | Year 5 | $1.2 million | 2.4× |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value | Year 7 | $3.0 million | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private hold with growth plateauing, buyers staying away, public scale out of reach, and available cash reinvested | Through year 8 | $0, with shares retained | 0× realized |

At 2% ownership, the strong sale yields $2.0 million, or 4.0×. The memo supplies no buyer economics or relevant transaction prices to support either sale value. It also supplies no independent route to cash for this holder.

### Countercase

An eyewear maker could source inward-tracking components, use published blink-tracking work, and deliver a narrower feature in-house. Software-only blink training already has evidence of symptom improvement for dry eye, one of the problems STASIS targets. A license or partnership could also let a buyer offer the integrated feature without paying for the company. STASIS’s reported training dataset could strengthen the ownership case, but exclusivity, transfer rights, and incremental performance remain unverified. This countercase supports Watch. The memo leaves open whether the substitutes can match the integrated product’s benefit or cap the acquisition price below the hurdle, so the structural Pass override does not apply. ### Decisive diligence question

Can a production pilot with an eyewear maker show that owning STASIS’s technology would create enough incremental value over building or licensing to support roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds? A pilot showing sustained symptom improvement against a credible substitute, combined with documented transfer rights and a buyer-backed economic case that clears the hurdle, would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that a readily available substitute delivers comparable value and caps the price below the hurdle would move it to Pass.
