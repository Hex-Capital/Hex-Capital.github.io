# Streamline Systems -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Streamline Systems could make cheap aircraft useful where satellite navigation fails. That matters. But how much more useful, for how many missions, at what cost? The dossier’s $154.38 million navigation-market estimate measures a component category; it does not quantify the operational value unlocked by dependable flight under jamming. I will accept a high probability of technical failure for an enormous consequence. Here, the consequence remains unmeasured. I pass on the current evidence.

The strongest argument against passing is that two founders have reportedly flown the hardware, demonstrated GPS handoff, and integrated with ArduPilot, INAV and PX4. This is meaningful pre-seed evidence. Streamline’s advertised absence of an RF signature could also matter in contested environments. A working module might conceal a substantial invention whose details should remain private. But secrecy does not establish superiority. Against ANELLO’s photonic gyroscopes or Advanced Navigation’s sensor fusion, the dossier provides no matched accuracy, drift, endurance, weight or power measurements. The reported prototype earns limited technical credit; it cannot establish a discontinuity.

Signed LOIs and scheduled government trials make that bull case more credible: someone apparently wants to test the product. They do not yet demonstrate disciplined risk retirement. The decisive uncertainty is whether navigation remains accurate throughout an actual denied-GPS mission, including the advertised day/night and land/water conditions. The recruitment listing identifies hardening and manufacturability work, but gives no test threshold, experiment cost or failure response. I cannot infer that capital is attacking the largest risk first merely because trials are scheduled. Equally, I would not penalize this team for having no disclosed revenue at this stage.

Poraj’s engineering studies and software experience, paired with Jessett’s RAF reserve exposure, provide relevant starting knowledge. They do not document rapid belief revision or the successful recruitment of someone who owns the hardest sensing and manufacturing risks. Advertised engineering vacancies are intentions, not recruits won. Streamline fits my willingness to back physical systems through difficult qualification cycles. The exception that could overcome missing benchmarks would require a documented instigator addressing a demonstrably enormous value pool; this dossier does not establish that combination. Nor does it justify accusing the founders of incremental ambition, exit fixation or resistance to evidence.

The economic gate is equally unresolved. A drop-in module for expendable aircraft needs a credible delivered cost, including production yield, calibration and integration. Streamline discloses neither pricing nor a bottom-up cost path; government interest does not answer that question. **My flip condition is one independently witnessed comparison showing equivalent mission performance to a named incumbent at one-tenth the fully burdened delivered cost, supported by a credible production quotation.** That would connect the prototype to an economic discontinuity. Until then, I am being offered an interesting navigation experiment without enough evidence to price the upside option.
