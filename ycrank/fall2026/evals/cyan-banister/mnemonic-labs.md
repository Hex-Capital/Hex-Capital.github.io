# Mnemonic Labs -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a small trading team teaching a model from market activity rather than assembling strategies one hypothesis at a time. But what has to happen between here and there? Training must produce generalizable predictions; those predictions must survive execution costs; additional capital must not erase the advantage. Mnemonic Labs reports a training run exceeding 25TB of tokens in November 2026. That establishes effort at scale, not yet a leak from that future. The proposed scaling law is the experiment. It cannot also serve as its result.

Why do trading desks organize research this way today? The founders point to short profitability horizons. That could leave room for patient research, but it does not establish an artificial gatekeeper or a rent Mnemonic removes. The commercial choice matters: proprietary trading would concentrate the advantage inside Mnemonic; licensing might distribute it to smaller teams. Neither model is established. I cannot credit returned agency before knowing who gets access and on what terms. The broader $26.3 billion algorithmic-trading market does not answer that question.

The strongest reason to write a check is the brothers’ experience inside the machine. Videh worked at Citadel and Walleye; Kalki worked at Tower Research, where the company attributes $20 million in annual PnL to his work at 22. That is potentially consequential evidence of commercial judgment, although it is company-reported prior-employer performance, not Mnemonic revenue. Their earlier robotics research suggests technical curiosity beyond this fashionable category, but the dossier supplies no dated sequence of experiments establishing longstanding obsession with this particular problem. These are credible domain practitioners; the first-believer story remains partly unwritten.

My pre-revenue exception deserves a serious hearing here. Experienced builders can discover something important before customers exist, and a pipeline and architecture reportedly built in under six weeks suggest execution speed. Missing revenue is not my objection. The missing bridge is a demonstrated capability: no disclosed scaling curve, transaction-cost-adjusted result, or repeated user action shows what this training buys. A training run is a working research process; it is not evidence that someone repeatedly stakes money or changes their workflow because the output works.

Mnemonic also resembles the concealed infrastructure I often like beneath a visible wave. Resemblance earns no infrastructure premium, though. An owned architecture does not yet establish a scarce bottleneck, exclusive data, or several downstream businesses dependent on it; Prodigy is already pursuing overlapping foundation-model research. Nor would I manufacture red flags: brothers do not imply equal voting control, named CEO and Chief Scientist roles provide some accountability, and undisclosed data rights do not establish a severe privacy violation. Those gates remain unverified. I pass with my own money today because the strongest evidence concerns the researchers, while the central claim concerns what their model can do. **My flip condition is an independently audited, timestamped experiment showing that successive increases in training compute improve live net returns under fixed execution rules and risk limits.** That would connect the proposed future to an observable present result.
