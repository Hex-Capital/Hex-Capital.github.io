# Sona8 -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Sona8 has evidence that its founders can get inside a consulting engagement. I cannot yet underwrite what my check buys. The reported $500,000-plus raised tells me neither the proposed post-money valuation nor how much competition there is for the round. There is also no documented experiment tied to the proceeds, with a deadline and a result that changes the business’s value. Those are missing answers, not evidence of an auction or a bad capital plan. But they leave my two largest underwriting questions unanswered.

The strongest asset is the route into customers. Initial business came through founder networks; one unnamed top-three consulting firm has signed; and Sona8 reports more than 10,000 employee interviews. Pacific Southwest Container’s strategy director supplies a concrete example of previously unseen efficiency and safety opportunities emerging from the product. Together, these establish more than a contact list: relationships have produced deployments and useful output. The interesting mechanism is a consulting partner repeatedly bringing Sona8 into client engagements. What remains unproven is whether Sona8 owns that recurring access or depends on a consultant deciding to include it in each new project. Interview volume alone cannot answer that.

The founders have a credible position between consulting and enterprise AI. Hantel and Tamme developed the idea from employee-interview work at BCG; Malmsten brings principal-engineer experience at McKinsey QuantumBlack; Tamme adds conversational-AI research. That fits my preference for teams connecting two operating worlds. Still, the dossier supplies considerably more evidence of relevant roles than of prior shipped deployments or commercial outcomes. I give the team credit for direct access and context without turning their employers into a substitute for operating receipts. Naming a CEO also does not establish decision rights, but three founders do not establish an equal-founder committee either.

The strongest objection to passing is that Sona8 may already be collecting the operational knowledge enterprise systems miss. Employee workarounds could become valuable proprietary context, and the consulting channel could distribute that context layer repeatedly. Fixed codebooks and excerpt traceability show attention to research quality. But the live workflow graph remains a development goal, and customer-specific information does not automatically become an asset Sona8 can reuse or defend. Celonis already competes for enterprise context; Salesforce’s announced Listen Labs acquisition adds distribution pressure. The founders’ study finding singleton concerns omitted in 86% of 45 summaries makes reliability economically consequential, although it does not establish Sona8’s production error rate. Ten-agent agreement alone cannot prove that important minority observations survive.

I would pass today. The exception case is credible customer pull strong enough to outweigh missing deal information, but a signed unnamed firm and substantial interview usage do not establish paid conversion, retention, or cash economics. Platform and usage fees describe how Sona8 charges; they do not show what remains after inference, implementation, and support. Delete the next financing and the dossier cannot tell me whether the business sustains itself. My one verifiable condition for reconsideration is a consulting partner renewing a paid deployment and expanding it into a second client engagement at positive contribution margin, including those delivery costs. That would test whether the relationship produces repeatable cash rather than another bespoke project. I would still need to price the check.
