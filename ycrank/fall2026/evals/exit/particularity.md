# Particularity -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition by an investigation software vendor. Particularity says its browser agents test live checkout and cancellation flows, preserve timestamped and hashed captures, and connect findings to complaints and statutory elements for staff review. Case IQ’s intake and case-routing product is a plausible fit. An incumbent could buy Particularity to add proactive detection and evidence collection to the workflow it already sells. That rationale is inferred. The memo documents adjacent products and leaves buyer interest, agency adoption, and measured savings unverified. (The Idea; Market & Traction)

The memo records an October 2026 launch and reports $500,000 in YC financing. A concrete milestone is a paid agency deployment that measures browser-flow completion, evidence accepted by investigators, and staff time saved, followed by replication at a second agency. The reported financing gives the team a starting point for testing. Runway, pilot costs, and the time needed to clear procurement and security reviews are unknown. Ownership and transfer rights for the product and agency evidence, along with any required consents, remain unverified. The memo gives no basis to underwrite an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction; Key Facts; Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million for the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale scenarios below are illustrative assumptions. Both assume a cash closing within eight years, full participation by the holder, and pro rata distribution after the stated deductions, with no further preference or other senior claim.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Holder cash |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $30M enterprise value less $5M in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $25M distributable equity | $750K (1.5×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M distributable equity | $2.7M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | The company reinvests available cash and the holder has no eligible share sale or payout within eight years | $0 (0×) |

The strong case clears the hurdle only if enough of the value reaches this holder in cash. At the same per-share price, selling half the stake yields $1.35 million. The memo supplies no revenue, acquisition pricing, or buyer economics that would support the strong sale value. (Market & Traction; Key Facts)

### Countercase

An incumbent could build browser screening into its existing product. The memo identifies a publicly documented approach to browser automation, and a separate audit’s incomplete runs illustrate the reliability problem Particularity would need to solve. Agencies may also require corroboration beyond timestamps and hashes. If those issues limit investigator use, an incumbent has less reason to pay for the product. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the evidence establishes neither a ready substitute for the full workflow nor a supported ceiling on holder proceeds. (Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could an investigation software incumbent justify using measured results from paid agency deployments? Repeatable staff savings and accepted evidence, paired with buyer-side economics that support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity for a fully participating holder after deductions and senior claims, would move the action to Reach out. A supported price ceiling that leaves the holder materially short would move it to Pass.
