# Verdun -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Verdun’s founders have already shipped in the system they want to replace. The financing is much less legible: proposed post-money, competing term sheets, and whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital are all undisclosed. YC participation establishes neither a bargain nor an auction. I would pass on the evidence available. I can underwrite the founders’ relevant experience; I cannot yet underwrite what my money buys.

The strongest argument against passing is unusually concrete. Sonny Tai founded Actuate, which Verdun’s leadership page credits with deployment across more than 110,000 cameras at 15,000-plus sites. Tung Hoang built Actuate’s first gun-detection model. These are operating receipts across physical security and machine learning, rather than adjacent job titles. Their prior collaboration also matters: Verdun is attempting a difficult handoff between automated interpretation and human intervention with founders who have worked together inside that handoff. This fits my pattern of backing people who can translate between technical systems and messy physical operations.

But the asset Verdun owns remains unclear. Its cascade combines computer vision, multimodal reasoning, temporal analysis, and site protocols; Hakimo already offers contextual verification and autonomous responses. The potentially durable asset is site-specific operating knowledge and correction data that improve service economics as deployments accumulate. The dossier establishes an advertised feedback loop, without establishing exclusive data or customer behavior caused by it. Charlie Warren soliciting security buyers and Tai’s roughly 20,000 LinkedIn followers create a plausible route to conversations. Neither establishes a proprietary route to purchases.

The venture moment could be proving that contextual filtering reduces human review costs while preserving threat detection and response quality. Verdun identifies nuisance queues exceeding 20 minutes, so solving that tradeoff would have tangible customer value. However, that is my proposed experiment. The dossier supplies no round-funded test, deadline, or measurable pass/fail result. Two engineering vacancies do not establish how capital reveals a discrete truth. I give the missing capital plan the specified default, without treating ordinary pre-seed silence as evidence of a bad plan.

Delete the next financing and the same operating question becomes decisive. Tai explicitly recognizes that model selection and video ingestion can produce negative unit economics; cheaper initial filters are a sensible architectural response, not demonstrated margins. There are no disclosed prices, burn figures, or cash economics. My AI red flag is relevant because contextual reasoning is already competitive and model improvements are broadly available, but Verdun’s human fallback does not support accusing it of assuming errors disappear. My known exceptions permit overriding missing financing evidence with demonstrated conversion and cash economics, or unusually strong external validation. The founders’ prior deployments make that override plausible; those deployments belong to Actuate and cannot establish Verdun’s current business.

One verifiable flip condition would be a paid 90-day deployment cohort producing positive contribution profit per monitored site-month, including inference and human intervention costs, while meeting contracted detection and response standards. That would demonstrate a functioning base business beneath the AI option and could justify overriding the missing-gate defaults. Until then, Verdun has substantial founder credibility and a credible operating hypothesis, but insufficient evidence for me to commit personal capital.
