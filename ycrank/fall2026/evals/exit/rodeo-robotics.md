# Rodeo Robotics -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest private-company path. An automotive service-equipment manufacturer could buy Rodeo to offer a system that inspects vehicles, diagnoses faults, and performs routine maintenance. The case for ownership is control over the hardware, models, and any transferable data from technician interventions. That control could be worth paying for if it lets the buyer expand task coverage across its installed base faster than building or licensing a system. PitPro’s tire-changing installation at Kal Tire shows that shops will deploy narrower automation. Rodeo’s broader workflow and 96% autonomous execution time on initial tasks are company claims. The memo gives no customer-site results or evidence of buyer intent. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Rodeo targets January 2027 customer deployments. A mixed-vehicle deployment could measure technician time saved and jobs completed after supervision, interventions, and downtime. The memo lists a $500,000 convertible note and leaves its terms, available cash, burn, and deployment budget unresolved. It also leaves IP ownership, data rights, and transfer consents unverified. There is no usable evidence of a separate route that could pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Proceeds bridge.** The memo provides no terms for the proposed check, so the illustration uses one $500,000 investment at a $10 million post-money valuation. A 40% relative dilution takes the initial 5% stake to 3%, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, requiring $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale scenarios assume zero net debt, no other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, and simple pro rata distribution with no senior claims. Under those assumptions, enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration. All consideration is cash at closing, with zero stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation. The actual waterfall and the note’s treatment are unknown. The memo supplies no usable pricing evidence, so the sale prices and timing are scenario assumptions. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Assumed outcome | Gross cash to holder at 3% |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest | $50 million sale in year 4 | $1.5 million (3×) |
| Strong | $100 million sale in year 7 | $3 million (6×) |
| Prolonged private | Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested through year 8 | $0 (0×), with shares retained |

The strong scenario clears the objective at 3%. At 2%, the same sale yields $2 million (4×).

### Countercase

A buyer could assemble a narrower offering from available systems or license Rodeo’s technology. PitPro and Automated Tire already cover overlapping tasks, Rodeo uses off-the-shelf hardware where available, and technician interventions could absorb much of the expected labor savings. If the combined workflow adds little net throughput, ownership may command too small a premium to clear the proceeds hurdle. The memo establishes overlap in tire service and leaves both broader substitutability and the price ceiling unresolved. That supports Watch. The structural Pass override remains untriggered. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a customer deployment show enough repeatable economic value from owning Rodeo’s full workflow to support a sale that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Move to Reach out if mixed-vehicle operating logs show sustained gains in completed jobs and net technician time after interventions, and those gains support buyer economics at plausible deployment scale after accounting for what the buyer could get through a license or narrower systems. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
