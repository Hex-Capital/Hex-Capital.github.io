# Outworld Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a robotics foundation-model developer or simulation platform. Such a buyer could pay for Horizon’s manipulation engine and a reusable library of calibrations if owning them gave it a durable lead in training and evaluating policies that work on physical robots. The memo records a public launch and demo, and both founders have robotics research experience. Outworld says Horizon fits simulated behavior to physical measurements and reports the agreement. The acquisition case depends on an inference that those calibrations can be reused across robots or tasks and that ownership offers an advantage over licensing. The memo supplies no customer benchmark, funded pilot, or runway for reaching that milestone. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

The memo supplies no usable acquisition price evidence, investment terms, documented IP or calibration-data transfer rights, or independently supported fallback that pays this holder. The scenarios use the illustrative seed terms of a single $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% initial ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in holder cash, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The threshold is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The sale prices below are scenario assumptions. Both sales assume enterprise value equals distributable equity consideration, with zero applicable net debt or other adjustments and a simple pro rata waterfall with zero senior preference deductions. All consideration is cash at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be zero. Holder cash is before investor taxes.

| Scenario | Timing and outcome | Holder cash | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5 at $50 million | $1.50 million | 3.0× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 at $100 million | $3.00 million | 6.0× |

Even the $100 million scenario falls to $2 million and 4.0× at 2% ownership. The memo gives no basis for treating either sale price as achievable.

### Countercase

MuJoCo Playground already offers hardware-trajectory fitting, SuperDex offers open tactile contact simulation, and Isaac Sim / Newton covers overlapping simulation workflows. If a buyer can combine those tools or license Horizon on ordinary terms, ownership may offer little incremental value. Repeating physical calibration for each robot or task could also consume much of the time Horizon aims to save. The memo establishes overlap and a plausible cost risk. It provides no head-to-head result or buyer economics that would establish an adequate substitute or a price cap below the hurdle. The structural Pass override therefore does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a prospective buyer’s production pilot show that owning Horizon creates enough incremental value over licensing it or building with available tools to support roughly $83 million in distributable equity proceeds? A buyer-validated pilot that measures hardware hours saved at comparable on-robot task success across multiple setups, accounts for calibration effort, and translates the gains into an ownership-specific price case would move the action to **Reach out**. A result showing that licensing or available tools capture essentially the same benefit would move it to **Pass**. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
