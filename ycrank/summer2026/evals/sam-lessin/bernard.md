# Bernard -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I need are missing: post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and whether this is genuinely Bernard’s first scarce check. That is not a routine disclosure gap. I am investing my own money, and price tells me whether I am underwriting neglected uncertainty or buying a consensus AI story after YC has auctioned it. With no terms or competition data, the round receives the explicit low prior, not the benefit of the doubt.

The strongest evidence is operational. Bernard is reportedly live with large authorized service companies, handles thousands of calls daily, and supports SMS operations for Mr. Appliance of West Loop. Pre-ID also attacks a specific cash leak: technicians arriving without the correct appliance, diagnosis, or parts. That is materially better than followers, downloads, or an AI demo. It suggests Bernard has entered a real workflow, although the dossier does not establish that these deployments are paid, retained, or improving first-visit completion.

The bull case fits an important unstated pattern: software reorganizing ugly physical-world infrastructure. Completed repairs could create a proprietary symptoms-to-parts-to-outcomes dataset, while appliance-specific integrations give Bernard more depth than a generic dispatcher. Katzman has operated partnerships and service workflows; Goodfellow has managed fraud, geo-compliance, and enterprise automation products. But they have shipped primarily on the software side of the bridge. Nothing documents either founder running an appliance-servicing operation, parts network, or technician workforce.

The opposing case stings: thousands of daily calls at large servicers may conceal exactly the paid conversion and embedded usage that public data cannot show. If those customers renew because Bernard measurably increases first-visit completion, the narrow vertical could produce a real dark pool before the horizontal platforms react. But the known-exception bar requires measured accuracy, pilot-to-paid conversion, and retention—not deployment language. Bernard publishes none of them, while acknowledging probabilistic diagnoses and dependence on Compass, Encompass, Marcone, Early Repair, and Samsung GSPN.

This also activates the generic-AI red flag. ServiceTitan already combines customer records with AI voice and automated dispatch, and Probook has raised $40 million around an AI operating system for home services. Bernard’s specialization may win a wedge, but its shared-data rights are not yet a moat: there is no dataset size, diagnostic accuracy, repair-outcome lift, or evidence that incumbents cannot reproduce the feature. Paid-subscription terms prove a monetization mechanism, not revenue, gross margin, or a business that survives without another round.

Most importantly, the dossier names no venture moment. I cannot tell what this capital will test, by what date, or what result would dramatically change Bernard’s value. A nine-person pre-seed team makes the absence of burn, runway, and contribution economics more consequential. My 36-month base case is a useful appliance-repair product reaching $3 million–$7 million in ARR but remaining a feature-scale vertical rather than an independent system of record. I pass today. One verifiable flip condition is a financing data room showing a sub-$10-million post-money round alongside ten unrelated paid servicer cohorts with greater than 90% twelve-month retention and positive contribution margin after human exception handling.
