# Resin Technologies -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Resin has shipped something substantial, but the dossier leaves out the founder story I most want to hear: what failed, what Anmol and Koosha did about it, and how quickly it worked. Building an electronics editor is evidence of technical effort. It does not by itself establish resourcefulness under resistance. I would ask for one troublesome board and the sequence of decisions it forced. Without that sequence, I cannot distinguish unusually determined founders from competent engineers pursuing a plausible idea.

The product earns real credit. Resin offers editable schematics, datasheet-to-footprint generation, and placement and routing inside one workspace ([product](https://resineda.com/product)). Connecting those pieces requires engineering beyond attaching a chat box to an existing tool. But the proprietary routing claim has no independent comparison, and beta availability does not establish that a fabricated board works. The distinction matters here: a schematic can look convincing while containing an expensive mistake. Resin itself warns about incorrect wiring and unsuitable components ([terms](https://resineda.com/terms)). Engineering review is sensible; the unanswered question is how much work remains after that review.

The strongest case for investing is the combination of these particular founders. Koosha has built electronics and robotics at Intelligent Haptronic Solutions, A&K Robotics, Bosch, and Kodak; Anmol brings enterprise AI deployment experience from You.com. They also met studying Engineering Physics, rather than assembling a relationship for an application ([founders](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/resin-technologies)). They could understand both the physical constraints and the software needed to remove tedious work. Yet relevant employment does not document a personally encountered design failure that led to Resin, and the dossier gives no evidence-driven product revision. Those are unanswered questions, not evidence against their character.

I would look for the small group that already needs this badly. The October launch offers founder onboarding, which is a useful way to find them, but identifies no engineer repeatedly using Resin to finish boards ([launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/VG7-resin-codex-for-electronics)). The $199 monthly Pro price is an offer, not revenue. It could be cheap if Resin reliably eliminates days of work; it could be expensive if checking its output recreates the original task. Flux already offers an integrated editor and AI assistance, so the decisive advantage must appear in an engineer’s actual workflow. LinkedIn followers cannot answer that question.

I would pass on the present evidence. Resin resembles the programmable tools I like backing, but there is no documented developer appropriation or numerical proof spike to support that pattern. Nor does the dossier establish launch dependence, partner dependence, or premature delegation; I would not invent those red flags. I have considered the working-prototype exception to my founder gate, but product availability without demonstrated correctness or unmistakable user pull is insufficient to invoke it. My single flip condition is a documented customer case in which the founders fix a blocking design failure within a week and that same engineering team returns to produce a second functioning board with Resin. That would connect founder behavior to a result someone actually wants again.
