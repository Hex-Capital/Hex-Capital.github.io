# Cyclon -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B, New economies, is the strongest path.** Cyclon says its OS agent can run tasks across signed-in Android apps in the background and build and test requested apps before installing them. If that works reliably, a user who would never pay a developer for a small personal tool could get one on demand. The memo’s contract-sharing example illustrates a workflow the agent could take over, but it describes work users already do manually. The market-opening case depends on users repeatedly delegating work or requesting tools they previously went without. The memo gives no comparison of human time per successful outcome after corrections, retries, and identity checks. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

The memo documents a public launch and a $199 refundable reservation offer. It gives no reservation count, conversion data, or realized revenue. The founders’ Android and hardware backgrounds and their prior collaboration on an SMS resale agent support seed-stage execution. Custom handset delivery still depends on FCC authorization, and the memo gives conflicting shipment estimates. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*)

At the estimated $699 handset price, each additional sale would need to cover hardware, fulfillment, returns and warranty, plus inference and support over the period included in the purchase. Cyclon includes AI usage during the closed beta. The memo leaves those costs and post-beta pricing unquantified, so contribution per handset remains unknown. (*The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Buyers may get the outcomes they value on a phone they already own. Gemini already automates supported tasks on existing Android devices, and Nothing offers user-generated apps. Cyclon’s saved rules and custom apps could make switching costly if buyers come to rely on them, but the memo provides no retention or evidence of preference under comparable model capability. Existing substitutes for parts of the experience make this a credible countercase. Whether they match the recurring outcomes buyers would pay for remains open, so the Path B override has yet to trigger. Path A has no separate supported case because the memo identifies no scarce input or capability Cyclon controls. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

### Decisive diligence question

In a paid trial with users who previously went without these tools or workflows, does Cyclon deliver recurring outcomes with materially less human time per successful task, including fixes and exceptions, and do users still choose to pay for it after trying comparable automation on their existing phones? A positive result at a viable price would support Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 with at least medium confidence. If buyers get equivalent outcomes on their existing phones and decline to pay for Cyclon, move to Pass.
