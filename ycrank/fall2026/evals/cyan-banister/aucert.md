# Aucert -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a two-person team shipping mobile software while another team, made of software, tries to break everything before customers do. Getting there requires agents that discover unfamiliar flows, distinguish intended behavior from defects, and accumulate reliable release knowledge. Aucert’s remembered paths and approved expectations point toward that world. But those appear in draft terms; the dossier does not establish that they work reliably or compound across releases. I can picture the destination. I cannot yet identify the little artifact that proves Aucert has started arriving there.

Why is mobile verification painful? A mistake can remain installed on someone’s phone while a correction waits for an update and store review. That makes checking before release valuable. Aucert proposes returning engineering time through autonomous exploration and specialist checks for functionality, accessibility, design, and store policies. There is a specific institutional constraint here, but the product does not remove store review, and the dossier supplies no measured reduction in verification time or release delay. The free scan is a sensible invitation to try it; undisclosed subscription prices leave the customer economics untested.

The founders are the strongest reason to keep looking. Vivek Soneja was PhonePe’s founding mobile architect and worked at WhatsApp; Rajesh Kumar spent 11 years at PayPal, including patented automated-testing work. That is operating tuition in precisely the systems Aucert wants to inspect. I would take their understanding of hidden complexity seriously. Still, employment history and a relevant patent establish more than credentials but less than independently pursued obsession or demonstrated first-believer recruitment. **CosmicBrain AI** is a useful exception to test: domain commitment can justify investing before revenue when a functioning prototype and signed industrial proofs of concept make the technical journey tangible. Aucert has relevant builders; this dossier does not provide equivalent prototype or customer evidence.

The strongest bull case is that these founders know which release failures generic testing tools miss, and organization-specific app knowledge becomes indispensable through use. That fits my preference for unglamorous workflow repair and concealed infrastructure. Customer ownership of that knowledge is compatible with a good business; exclusivity is not required for usefulness. But switching costs remain an inference, and BrowserStack already combines device infrastructure, accessibility checks, and testing agents. Aucert needs evidence that its autonomous exploration finds consequential defects those alternatives miss. An October 2026 launch and 317 LinkedIn followers do not show anyone repeatedly trusting the product with release work. Missing revenue is ordinary at this stage; missing observed behavior leaves the artifact question unanswered.

The screen-data path matters more to my check than the launch attention. Aucert’s security disclosure says screenshots and UI hierarchies reach AI providers without screen-content redaction. Encryption and tenant isolation address useful boundaries, but they do not prevent sensitive information inside a screen from reaching an external processor. This is a concrete privacy concern, although the dossier does not establish unauthorized collection or a severe incident. Nor does it establish a sufficiently bounded deployment that would let me dismiss the concern. Clear CEO and CTO roles help identify accountability; they do not establish the founders’ control arrangements, so I would neither award governance credit nor invent a governance failure.

I pass today at **37/100**. Aucert has credible builders attacking a real operating burden, but the evidence supports a testing product more clearly than a scarce enabling layer for the world I am imagining. My one condition for reopening is an independently referenceable four-week pilot at a named mobile customer, showing repeated pre-release discovery of consequential defects while keeping sensitive screen content out of external model inputs. That single observed deployment would test both the product’s usefulness and whether customers can safely inhabit it.
