# Praxis AI -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Praxis AI reports access to 60,000 workers across five continents and 150-plus environments, yet none of Dev Karpe, Tommy Li, or Rohan Seelamsetty is shown suffering this problem personally. Praxis AI is therefore a schlep-rich, origin-poor startup: strongest where factories, consent, calibration, and multimodal capture repel ordinary software founders; weakest where an organic idea should leave a story as clear as Drew Houston forgetting his USB stick. That pairing makes Praxis AI look more like an unusually well-connected data broker than a tool its founders could not help building.

**Coinbase** is the high rung for schlep blindness because compliance was not incidental work; it became the asset. Praxis AI has a similar mechanism in employer-mediated access, worker consent, privacy redaction, rights clearance, and quality control across 150-plus environment types. Those are good schleps. Praxis AI diverges from Coinbase because the dossier names no collection volume, consent system, rejection rate, exclusive agreement, or downstream model improvement. The founders have found the swamp, but have not yet shown they can drain it. **13/15.**

**Loopt** exposed the danger of an idea arriving before usable infrastructure and real need, with Sam Altman’s force of personality providing the exception. Praxis AI has an adjacent organic-idea problem: Li has robotics research, Karpe worked in supply-chain operations, and Seelamsetty claims factory experience, but the dossier never says any founder needed this dataset or built the capture system for his own work. Praxis AI therefore gets the absent-evidence default rather than credit for biography-shaped inference. No cereal-box exception rescues a missing origin story. **10/25.**

**60,000 workers** could be Praxis AI’s animal track, but only if that reach came from founders personally persuading reluctant employers rather than inheriting introductions through finance and venture networks. Over 18 months, Praxis AI’s resourcefulness will compound if every workplace deployment makes the next one faster, cheaper, and easier to permission; it will evaporate if “access” means a spreadsheet of possible participants. The July 2026 sample offer and claimed embedding inside unnamed public and unicorn-scale conglomerates are stronger than credentials alone, but no paid dataset or completed collection proves the stunt. **25/35.**

**Tommy Li’s IEEE research** associated with NASA JPL and PerceptIn Robotics is the one real hacker trace in Praxis AI. The counterfactual is simple: if Praxis AI had already shipped an open capture pipeline, published synchronization benchmarks, or shown a working dataset that improved a robot policy, the hacker score would jump a tier. Instead, all three founders have no public GitHub repositories, while two biographies lean heavily on Morgan Stanley, a16z, Wharton, and Oxford. Technical credentials are not the same thing as an artifact. **7/15.**

**Reddit** had founders whose prior relationship and hacker culture made the pair more than two names on an application. Praxis AI has the preferred three-founder configuration and useful coverage across robotics research, supply-chain operations, and laboratory sales. But Praxis AI offers only a shared University of Pennsylvania affiliation, not evidence that Karpe, Li, and Seelamsetty built together for years, survived something difficult together, or even knew one another before choosing the idea. Three is the right number; the relationship is still unproven. **6/10.**

**Human Archive’s 499.9-hour sample** shows what Praxis AI must beat, while MobileEgo shows that commodity smartphones can make raw collection cheaper. By July 2029, I expect Praxis AI to have narrowed from “turns every company into a data vendor” into a managed, rights-cleared industrial collection service for two to four robotics laboratories, producing $3 million to $7 million in annual revenue but not becoming the default robotics-data platform. Praxis AI’s schlep capacity can sustain a real service business; the weak organic origin and missing hacker artifact make a broad technical platform less likely.

**Airbnb’s 1,000 cereal boxes at $40 each** are the strongest objection to waiting: perhaps Praxis AI’s 60,000-worker reach is the same animal signal hiding behind an initially dubious idea, and the unnamed conglomerates conceal extraordinary founder hustle. The bull mechanism is that employer access compounds into proprietary longitudinal data before Human Archive or Cortex AI can reproduce the relationships. But Praxis AI has disclosed no named buyer, paid contract, exclusive workplace right, collected-hour count, or model-performance gain, while every founder lacks a public GitHub trail. I would not write the personal check yet. One named robotics laboratory renewing a dataset contract above $500,000 after publishing a measurable policy-performance improvement would flip my verdict.
