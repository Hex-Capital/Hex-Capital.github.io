# machine0 -- Elad Gil Evaluation

The market map is straightforward: models now sustain six-to-eight-hour coding and research runs, agent operators need persistent remote environments, and machine0 becomes large only if a meaningful share of agent compute migrates from laptops and raw VPSs into a retained control plane. The governing variable is therefore persistent workload density—how many agents remain active long enough that static endpoints, credentials, snapshots, and managed lifecycle matter. The dossier identifies this causal mechanism, but supplies neither a threshold nor evidence that current customers consistently cross it.

The technology shift is real but the economic step change is not yet demonstrated. machine0 sells CPU from $0.013 per hour and GPUs from $0.836 per hour while sourcing every VM from DigitalOcean. That establishes a working product and transparent pricing, not differentiated margins. Daytona already reports $5 million in annualized revenue, Modal exceeds $300 million, and both funded competitors are converging on persistent or full-VM execution. If agent workloads become longer, the category expands; it does not follow that machine0 captures the expansion.

The strongest product argument is that better models should increase machine0’s usefulness rather than erase it. Root access, static IPs, HTTPS endpoints, credential profiles, snapshots, NixOS provisioning, and CLI or MCP control surround the model with persistent state and permissions. This is more durable than formatting model output. But machine0 still manages the computer rather than the completed agent workflow: it owns no disclosed evaluation loop, fleet history, or operational system of record, and competitors can reproduce most lifecycle features. The likely durable company is an agent-fleet control plane; the current product has not yet shown that it becomes one.

Demand evidence is suggestive rather than decisive. PACIFIC is displayed as trusted, Geoffrey Négiar is identified as a customer, and the June 2026 Hacker News launch reached 95 points and 36 comments. Those facts establish interest and at least some use, but not payment, repeat workloads, retention, or a capacity constraint. At pre-seed I do not require mature revenue, yet these signals cannot show that persistent agent infrastructure is the controlling need rather than a convenient alternative to a VPS.

Barnaby Malet is the clearest affirmative signal. His cloud-resource research, prior CTO/CPO role at Upflow, infrastructure and security responsibility, and willingness to pivot the earlier Director product show relevant second-time learning. A solo founder who can already ship this technical surface deserves attention. The bull case is that an experienced infrastructure founder has entered before agent-compute demand is legible, with a product whose consumption grows automatically as agents run longer. The objection is that this is not a frontier-research exception: scarce research talent or proprietary technology does not overcome weak product pull when well-capitalized competitors and the underlying cloud supplier can converge on the same features.

I would monitor rather than invest my own money today. The market may amplify machine0, but the dossier does not yet show why value accrues to this control plane instead of DigitalOcean, Modal, Daytona, or E2B. My flip condition is one retained cohort producing at least $25,000 MRR for three consecutive months at greater than 50% gross margin, with most compute hours coming from VMs that remain active longer than 24 hours. That would validate persistence as both the customer need and the economic variable.
