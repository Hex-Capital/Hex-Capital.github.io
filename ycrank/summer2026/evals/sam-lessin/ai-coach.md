# COACH -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

$500,000 ARR after five months makes COACH an execution-machine founder bet trapped inside my least attractive sleeve: consensus AI sales SaaS. COACH scores highest on founder conviction because the Perez brothers previously built Alsace Test to €3.5 million in 14 months, survived six pivots, and personally worked door-to-door sales meetings; COACH scores lowest on category because Rilla already sells substantially the same field-conversation product at an estimated $70 million ARR. Great operators can build valuable software here. The harder question is whether my own-money check buys mispriced exposure or merely rents a seat in an already-resolved race.

Pinterest is the high rung on asymmetric conviction: Ben Silbermann’s missionary patience outweighed a category that investors initially struggled to read. COACH sits between Pinterest and Venmo on that ladder because Mathieu and Thomas Perez have already taken six tacks, built a prior revenue-producing business, and embedded themselves alongside Réseau ACEH representatives before automating the work. COACH diverges from Pinterest because the dossier shows recent LinkedIn writing about the product, not a multi-year public obsession that predates the pitch; the conviction looks operationally real, but the thesis may still be post-LLM opportunity recognition rather than a belief the founders could not stop pursuing.

Fin’s failure mechanism was that an intelligent-assistant layer could demonstrate compelling Wizard-of-Oz behavior without becoming a durable category, eventually forcing a pivot into workplace analytics. That low-rung pattern appears uncomfortably clearly in COACH: 2026 transcription and agent harnesses enabled the product, while Rilla already records, transcribes, analyzes, and coaches the same in-person sellers. COACH has the favorable unstated Wizard-of-Oz pattern—the founders personally joined field meetings—but COACH converts that pattern into a generic subscription layer rather than one of my convicted sleeves such as crypto, DTC, creator infrastructure, or a genuinely service-led software transition.

$500,000 ARR, 100% reported pilot conversion, and 0% reported churn could make COACH default-alive much faster than the usual YC regatta. Over the next 18 months, COACH can compound customer-specific recordings into workflow switching costs while forward-deployed pilots finance product learning; that is a real capital-structure advantage, not merely a data-moat slogan. COACH loses points because the instrument appears to be a standard SaaS cap table, the $100,000–$200,000 design role suggests burn is arriving early, and no customer count or cohort period establishes whether the revenue base provides a genuine downside cushion.

No public financing terms were found for COACH, so I apply my price-discipline default rather than pretending $500,000 ARR answers a valuation question. COACH flips from a weak value-seed score to a strong one if the SAFE is below the S26 batch median, no tier-one bidding process exists, and the founders can fund the next tack largely from customer cash. A $25 million-plus post-money price would reverse that conclusion: COACH would become the canonical hot YC deck—AI, SaaS, sales enablement, 120% monthly growth—with no asset-class mispricing left for me to underwrite.

John Fish / Bookshelved is the clean founder-distribution parallel: owned attention existed before the financing and was inseparable from the asset being purchased. COACH has approximately 17,000 combined LinkedIn followers across Mathieu Perez, Thomas Perez, and Nicolas Sebag, which gives COACH credible founder-led reach and validates that the founders can write publicly. The analogy stops there because no evidence ties those followers to the reported $500,000 ARR, no customer community predates COACH by two years, and the product does not require enduring attachment to a named founder.

Rilla’s estimated $70 million ARR and COACH’s 120% reported monthly growth point to a falsifiable outcome: by July 2029, COACH will reach $10–20 million ARR and be acquired by Gong for less than $200 million rather than become an independent category leader. COACH’s high-conviction founders can execute through several regatta tacks, but COACH’s low category differentiation leaves Gong or another revenue platform as the natural downstream buyer once field-meeting data becomes a required feature.

COACH’s strongest bull case stings: repeat founders produced €3.5 million in 14 months before COACH, then reached $500,000 ARR in five months with reported 100% pilot conversion and 0% churn after manually living the customer workflow. That resembles the Robinhood known exception—later consensus does not matter if entry economics capture the curve—and the Wizard-of-Oz unstated pattern could let COACH outrun Rilla through superior execution. The evidence that wins today is structural: COACH discloses neither price nor customer cohorts, every headline metric is founder-reported, and Rilla already validates both the market and the direct product overlap. I do not write the check today; a sub-$12 million post-money SAFE plus customer-verified twelve-month retention above 90% would flip the verdict.
