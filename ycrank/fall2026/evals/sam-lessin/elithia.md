# Elithia -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Elithia reports sales, but I cannot price the investment: post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and whether my money would be the first scarce check are undisclosed. That leaves my price gate unresolved; it does not establish an auction. The second gate is equally important. There is no documented use of proceeds tied to one hypothesis, a deadline, and a measurable result. Automating medical review describes the product. It does not explain what truth my capital buys.

The strongest argument for Elithia is its narrow buyer. Physician expert witnesses must reconstruct histories from thousands of pages, and the company reports selling to those physicians and medical-legal firms. Deduplication, source-linked findings, and Word/PDF exports fit an existing professional task. This is more substantial than a waitlist. But purchasing useful software does not establish that Elithia owns the customer relationship, an embedded workflow, or proprietary data that competitors cannot access. The proposed expert-correction dataset remains an inference with unverified collection rights. I give the sales credit as evidence of demand without converting them into an invented dark pool.

Sarthak Jain has shipped a product that the company says customers buy, supported by unstructured-data engineering experience at Winton. That earns modest credit beyond credentials alone. His family medical background and conference speaker listing under Minerva Labs suggest access to the other ecosystem, but neither establishes direct experience operating a clinician’s review practice. Elithia resembles the translation layers I like; the evidence currently demonstrates the software side more clearly than the medical operating side. Oxford and MIT Sloan do not fill that gap.

The bull case is that expert witnesses become repeat buyers because verifiable chronologies save expensive professional time while leaving judgment with the physician. That could support a compact, profitable business. It also challenges a reflexive dismissal of the company as another AI application. Yet DigitalOwl already offers chronologies and evidence-linked record chat, and Datavant owns retrieval infrastructure alongside the analysis. Elithia must retain value after the underlying models improve and competitors bundle similar outputs. My known exceptions to missing financing gates depend on unusually strong conversion, retention, economics, or founder operating receipts. Reported sales without those details do not establish that override.

I would pass on the evidence available. The first 1,000 pages are free, paid rates are undisclosed, and there is no margin, burn, or runway information from which to remove the next financing from the plan. That is missing underwriting evidence, not proof of bad economics. My one verifiable condition for reconsideration is a completed paid repeat-use cohort showing positive contribution profit per reviewed case after inference and human correction costs. It would test whether the narrow professional workflow produces cash that survives competition. Round price would still determine whether that business is an attractive investment for my money.
