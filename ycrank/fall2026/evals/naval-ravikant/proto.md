# Proto -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Maayan Albert has earned the right to build this. In 2020, he co-created Stamper, an artboard-oriented programming environment; he later launched Browserbase’s Director. Those are artifacts at the intersection of visual expression and executable software, precisely where Proto operates ([CMU](https://www.design.cmu.edu/news/design-alumni-develop-creative-coding-tool); [DevTools Meetup](https://luma.com/pmsrfa9o)). Automatically handling empty, loading, and error states reflects knowledge of what makes prototypes expensive to finish. I give substantial credit to that accumulated specific knowledge. Employer names add little beside it.

The harder question is what Albert’s knowledge becomes inside the business. Proto’s state-restoring comments could preserve the relationship between a design decision and the interface that produced it. That is a useful product mechanism. The dossier does not establish that those decisions become an exclusive asset, improve a proprietary model, or make migration costly. Meanwhile, Figma opened its canvas to agents in March 2026, and Magic Patterns introduced overlapping context and handoff capabilities in October. These facts make durability the central question; they do not establish that Proto’s particular implementation is trivial to reproduce. I assign the missing-evidence default rather than inventing either a moat or a commodity architecture.

The strongest argument against waiting is that Albert may be developing a better language for designing software. State-aware comments and prototype decomposition into pull requests could make the workspace substantially more useful than a collection of generation features. Proto shares with the early **Notion** exception a founder-built visual workspace whose potential may precede observable distribution. The material difference is that the exception rested on a demonstrated distinctive prototype and unusual capital efficiency; this dossier supplies demonstrations of individual capabilities without establishing the corresponding product advantage. Proto also fits my pattern of tools that let individuals turn knowledge into output without an employer gatekeeper. That makes the upside credible, but it does not yet establish compounding value.

There is room beyond the first wedge. Proto starts with product designers and connects Linear tickets, Notion requirements, interface states, and submitted pull requests. That supplies a concrete path into adjacent product and engineering workflows through the same design-to-code capability. Figma’s reported $370.1 million quarterly revenue establishes substantial spending around design work; it is neither Proto’s revenue nor proof that Proto can capture that budget. I give intermediate market credit. Alpha access through founder DMs and 65 company-account followers establish no user-to-user distribution loop. Pricing and realized revenue remain unknown, so monetization receives its default as well. These are uncertainties appropriate to pre-seed, not evidence of failure.

I would keep the relationship open and hold my check. Proto has affirmative founder evidence and a plausible expansion path, while the asset that could compound remains unproven. There is no documented integrity problem, artificial financing deadline, or other behavioral veto. The unresolved issue is whether excellent product judgment accumulates into an advantage after competitors ship similar features. My single flip condition is a longitudinal customer study showing that retained prototype states and decision history measurably improve repeat-task performance, and that this advantage disappears when the same teams use a feature-equivalent alternative. That would demonstrate accumulated product value beyond the code.
