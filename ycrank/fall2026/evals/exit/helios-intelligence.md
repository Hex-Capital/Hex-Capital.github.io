# Helios Intelligence -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A diagnostics company could buy Helios to own a validated dataset linking repeat at-home tests to health outcomes, along with a model that improves risk predictions. Ownership could justify a premium if the buyer can use those assets across its products and would take materially longer to reproduce the results through licensing or internal development. The memo documents two acquisitions of diagnostics companies co-founded by the Helios team. Helios reports training on repeat draws and linked records, with dataset size, exclusivity, and predictive performance undisclosed. The previous acquisitions establish precedent for the buyer class, while Helios’s ownership advantage remains an inference. (Founders & Team, Defensibility)

The operating milestone is a comparative pilot showing that home-collected samples produce reliable measurements and that the linked data materially improve predictions over a commercially available profiling approach. The reported waitlist and financing entries provide early signals. The memo gives no pilot budget, confirmed funding total, or runway, and Helios’s rights to transfer the linked data remain unverified. Roon’s storefront establishes a product on sale, with sales and cash generation undisclosed. The memo provides no separately supported fallback that could pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Defensibility)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** The memo supplies no usable deal price or investment terms. Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%.

The sale scenarios assume zero net debt and other price adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. They assume a simple pro rata waterfall with no senior claims and all cash paid at closing. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each modeled at $0.

### Countercase

VO Health already uses a third-party platform for broad protein profiling from a small self-collected sample, and the memo reports commercial profiling priced from $50 per sample. A buyer could license that capability and build its own dataset. If that route can match Helios’s predictive performance and cap the price below the holder’s hurdle, the structural Pass gate would apply. The memo establishes a substitute for profiling and leaves the economics of reproducing the linked data and model unresolved. That gap supports Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a buyer-backed pilot show that owning Helios’s transferable data and model creates enough incremental value over licensing broad profiling to justify a sale that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A pilot that measures the performance gain, verifies data transfer rights, and supports that price with the buyer’s economics would move the action to Reach out.
