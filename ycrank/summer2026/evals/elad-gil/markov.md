# Markov -- Elad Gil Evaluation

150,000 Hugging Face downloads make Markov a shipping-heavy, monetization-unproven infrastructure bet: Markov’s strongest signal is demonstrated product cadence, while its weakest is the absence of any revenue curve. Dev Mandal previously took doubt.ai to 700 users and 5,000 questions in four weeks; Markov then assembled 48,478 videos and roughly 12,300 hours of computer-use data. Those are real artifacts, not founder narrative. I would invest, but this is skating for the puck on execution and market timing—not evidence that Markov is already “just working.”

**Perplexity** is the high rung for iterative shipping because Aravind Srinivas repeatedly returned with the prior week’s discussion built. Markov shares the load-bearing mechanism: Dev Mandal shipped doubt.ai, Harish Ashok previously built Zenith, and Markov released a substantial computer-use corpus before YC. Markov diverges because the dossier shows discrete artifacts rather than a repeated customer-feedback cadence; 150,000 downloads prove distribution, but Markov has not yet shown that Dev and Harish can turn model-lab requests into improved environments week after week.

**Lyft** exposed the low-rung failure mechanism of mistaking visible activity for sufficiently attractive economics in a structurally disadvantaged position. Markov has 45,000 first-month downloads for Computer Use Large and more than 150,000 downloads overall, but Markov discloses no paying customer, contract, price, or revenue. Free research consumption is a false signal until a model developer pays for a proprietary trajectory, returns for another workflow, and expands the contract. The usage evidence moves Markov above the absent-evidence default, but not close to the Harvey pattern of dollars compounding visibly.

Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google launched computer-use products during 2024–2025, creating a specific recent catalyst for Markov rather than a generic $16.3 billion TAM story. Over the next 18 months, that catalyst can compound because every new agent failure produces demand for more demonstrations, harder tasks, and evaluation environments; Markov can become the data exhaust layer for improving computer-use models. The mechanism fails if synthetic trajectories improve faster than agents themselves or laboratories internalize collection, and Turing’s delivery of more than 10,000 supervised GUI tasks shows Markov does not own the window.

Computer Use Large’s CC-BY-4.0 license prevents Markov’s current public corpus from functioning as an index on computer-use AI. Markov could flip this dimension upward by converting Hugging Face distribution into a rights-cleared contributor network and proprietary RL environment catalog that every model developer must touch across Salesforce, AutoCAD, Blender, Photoshop, Excel, and VS Code. Without that conversion, Markov must repeatedly win projects against Scale AI, Surge AI, and Turing; Markov rides the category only weakly because the underlying asset can presently be copied.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow infrastructure on top of commodity sequencing avoided a simultaneous bet on new chemistry, regulation, and customer behavior. Markov similarly assumes that computer-use models already exist and concentrates on the workflow layer—recording, annotation, action metadata, and RL environments—so Markov does not require a new foundation-model miracle. The residual dependency chain is manageable but real: contributor rights, workplace privacy filtering, paid procurement, and continued demand for human rather than synthetic trajectories all must work. The favorable unstated pattern is infrastructure on top of the hard thing; the drag is that Markov has not yet isolated proprietary workflow infrastructure from a public dataset business.

10,000-plus hours of recordings give Markov enough raw material to pivot toward closed, customer-specific RL environments, and my 36-month forecast is that Markov reaches $5 million–$12 million ARR by July 2029 after proprietary evaluation environments—not public datasets—become its primary product. Markov’s shipping velocity should produce the environment catalog, while the missing revenue curve implies at least one business-model reset before the buying behavior becomes legible. That is a smaller, earlier version of the Decagon pattern: workflow infrastructure becomes valuable once customers buy repeated units of cognition rather than download a research artifact once.

**Scale AI** at $870 million of 2024 revenue is the strongest bear mechanism: a scaled generalist can bundle GUI trajectories into existing lab contracts, while Markov gives away its flagship corpus and publishes no consent, privacy-review, or sensitive-data filtering process. Markov also triggers my AI red flag—no disclosed growth in dollars—and the Mistral exception does not rescue Markov because Dev Mandal and Harish Ashok have not shipped Llama-scale infrastructure or secured a hyperscaler partner. The evidence that keeps Markov investable is the speed and scale of the asset already produced by two people: 48,478 videos, approximately 12,300 hours, and 150,000-plus downloads. I would flip to Pass if Markov cannot show, by March 2027, signed model-lab contracts totaling at least $500,000 ARR for rights-auditable proprietary environments.
