# Greypoint Industries -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Darcy Zhang’s Canadian Armed Forces infantry experience and Greypoint Industries’ signed first ARROW contract make Greypoint Industries a missionary defense-hardware operator trapped inside a financially illegible round. Founder conviction is the strongest dimension: Jordan Khoo has General Dynamics, TerraSense, and UAS experience, while Steven Xu has already built embedded controls. Price discovery is the weakest dimension: no valuation, SAFE cap, round size, burn, contract value, or investor-pass evidence tells me whether Greypoint Industries is value seed or merely the latest hot defense YC deck.

**Drop.io** is the high rung for asymmetric conviction: I kept building a file-sharing primitive against free incumbents because the operating obsession existed before the financing narrative. Greypoint Industries shares that mechanism through Darcy’s infantry background, Jordan’s defense-systems work, and the founders’ 2025 Defence Tech Hackathon win before Summer 2026 YC. Greypoint Industries diverges from Drop.io because the dossier contains no founder writing, public technical artifacts, or disclosed ARROW test results; the signed contract proves action, but unpublished range, accuracy, and test volume leave conviction more visible than technical truth.

The canonical **$30 million post-money vertical-AI-agent seed** is the low rung on round mispricing because consensus enthusiasm has already consumed the return. Greypoint Industries is not that generic software pitch, but defense autonomy became a hot category while Anduril reached approximately $2.2 billion in 2025 revenue and Canada launched its Defence Drone Initiative in July 2026. Greypoint Industries provides no evidence of a discounted cap, a tier-one pass, or unusual financing terms, so I apply the 9/30 absent-evidence default: illegible pricing is not automatically mispricing in my favor.

Greypoint Industries’ first ARROW contract can make the capital structure compound over the next 18 months if customer-funded development pays for qualification, threat-data collection, and manufacturing learning. Greypoint Industries instead becomes founder-drainage if each interceptor revision requires equity-funded hardware, export compliance, and procurement demonstrations before production revenue appears. The Canadian controlled-goods rules and competitive qualification process create real tacks, while the missing contract value, burn, unit cost, and production yield prevent me from calling Greypoint Industries default-alive.

Jordan Khoo’s approximately 4,000 LinkedIn followers are reach, not owned distribution; Greypoint Industries has only 582 company followers and no verified founder newsletter, podcast, GitHub audience, or Twitter community. Greypoint Industries would flip a tier on founder-as-brand if Jordan’s audience generated measurable procurement introductions, recruiting leverage, or an engaged defense-operator community that predated the company. Until then, Greypoint Industries sells through government briefings and institutional partnerships, not a person already sitting on the cap table as a distribution asset.

**Sublime Security** is the single-anchor parallel for backing an unsexy operator in a security category before consolidation makes the asset obvious. Greypoint Industries has a similar operator-first profile and avoids my explicit anti-sleeve of foundation-model infrastructure or generic LLM SaaS. Greypoint Industries nevertheless sits outside my revealed crypto, DTC, prescription-rail, creator, and Wizard-of-Oz sleeves, while Anduril, Fortem, and Airobotics already possess deployment histories; physical defense earns partial sleeve credit, not borrowed conviction from Sublime.

Canada’s July 2026 low-cost counter-drone procurement window leads me to predict that within 36 months Greypoint Industries will remain independent, hold one to three Canadian defense development contracts totaling $2 million to $6 million annually, and reposition its center of gravity from manufacturing ARROW interceptors toward LEGION sensing and command integration. Greypoint Industries can reach that outcome because software-and-sensor integration requires less production capital and fits existing military procurement paths, while Fortem’s 4,500 reported captures and Anduril’s deployed Anvil make scaled interceptor displacement much harder.

**Ro** is the strongest objection: regulatory complexity and heavy follow-on capital can be the moat when only venture financing clears the licensing and operational schleps. Greypoint Industries could be the defense version of that Known Exception—Darcy’s infantry context, three founders with UAS or embedded-systems experience, a signed contract, and Canada’s new supplier process could turn qualification records into an accumulating barrier. The objection loses today because Greypoint Industries discloses no performance advantage, production economics, deployment volume, or structural protection against Anduril and Fortem; a current financing cap at or below $12 million post-money would be the single verifiable condition that flips my verdict to Invest.
