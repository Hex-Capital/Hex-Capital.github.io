# Shepherd Robotics -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Feifei Duan has deployed factory robots at Chef Robotics. I want to know about the deployment that went wrong. What broke, what did she build to get around it, and how long did that take? Shepherd Robotics’ dossier supplies relevant experience, but no such episode. That distinction controls my decision. Waymo, Cruise, Dyna, and Rivian suggest these founders understand difficult engineering. They do not establish how this particular team behaves when blocked.

The proposed work is attractive because the difficulty lives in the details. Routing a wire and inserting a terminal sound simple until equipment varies and failures require a technician. Shepherd proposes to absorb the hardware, models, integration, and support. Duan’s factory experience makes that choice more credible than a robotics idea invented from a market report. But the dossier never connects a specific problem she encountered to Shepherd’s founding. I would retain the default for problem origin rather than manufacture that connection.

The strongest case for investing is that these founders have already learned expensive lessons elsewhere and can apply them to one narrow electrical task. Human supervision could help them start serving customers before solving general manipulation. The decisive measurement would be technician time saved after interventions, recovery, and setup. Shepherd publishes no production benchmark, and its advertised gains are described as illustrative. The architecture earns no substitute credit for a demonstrated technical result. Standard Bots already reports data-center deployments; Shepherd needs to show which awkward job its own system handles unusually well.

The October 2026 claim of working with hyperscalers and manufacturers could conceal the most important fact about the company: a customer repeatedly asking the founders to bring the robot back. But the customers, usage, and commercial status are undisclosed. That leaves demand at its default, alongside technical proof. The 425 LinkedIn followers and reported $500,000 YC investment do not answer who needs the crude product. Nor does the dossier show a rejected hypothesis followed by a shipped change, so adaptability receives its default too. These are missing observations, not evidence of rejection or rigidity.

I would pass on the evidence available. Shepherd fits my interest in technical founders tackling neglected operational work, but lacks the measured proof or unmistakable pull that could justify an exception to my founder-action gate. There is no documented dependence on a major partner, premature delegation, or resistance to feedback; requesting buyer introductions does not establish those red flags. The concern is simpler: almost everything decisive remains unobserved. My flip condition is one customer-verified trial documenting a named deployment failure, a founder-built fix within two weeks, and repeated customer use with measured net technician hours saved. That would reveal the company much more clearly than another description of its models.
