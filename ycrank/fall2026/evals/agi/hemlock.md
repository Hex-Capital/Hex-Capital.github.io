# Hemlock -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path A, scarce complements, is the strongest fit. Robotics labs training force-sensitive manipulation need grip-force and contact-timing information that first-person video omits. Hemlock’s demonstrated prototype pairs wrist EMG with video to estimate and label those signals. Hemlock reports that adding force data to the same nine minutes of demonstration video raised egg pick-and-place success from 10% to 100%, and that Bracelet data raised lightbulb-screwing success from 30% to 80% with under six minutes of demonstrations. These are promising seed-stage comparisons on two narrow tasks. Independent validation on a production task remains open. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

If cheaper intelligence expands the range of manipulation tasks worth automating, labs could need more force-labeled demonstrations. Hemlock could capture that demand by supplying datasets that meet a buyer’s training requirements at a lower all-in cost. In that scenario, each additional collection would need to generate enough usable labeled hours to cover worker time, calibration, equipment, quality checks, and rejected recordings at a price buyers will pay. The memo gives no measured collection economics or buyer commitments. It also identifies no previously uneconomic buyer or transaction that Hemlock has opened under Path B. (The Idea; Key Risks; Key Facts)

Olschwang’s prior work on an assistive sensing glove and generative models for robot data supports seed-stage execution. Running a buyer qualification and a repeat collection remains an open test for the solo founder. (Founders & Team; Key Facts)

### Countercase

The force signal may be valuable while its supply remains interchangeable. A well-funded lab could build on ForceBand’s published wrist-EMG design or buy direct tactile capture. If either option yields equivalent policy performance at comparable all-in cost, Hemlock’s proprietary Bracelet gives buyers little reason to keep paying it. Worker-specific calibration could further narrow any cost advantage. The memo establishes credible alternatives and leaves equivalence on a buyer’s task unresolved. That keeps Path A open for Watch while leaving its value-capture gate unmet. (Defensibility; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent robotics lab commit to a paid follow-on collection after qualifying Hemlock on a production task against its best feasible force-data alternative? The trial should hold the training setup constant and measure task success and cost per usable labeled hour, including calibration and rejected data. A paid follow-on at a price that covers repeat delivery, backed by a meaningful performance or cost advantage, could move the action to Reach out if the updated score clears the outreach threshold. An equivalent substitute at equal or lower cost, with the buyer choosing it, would move this case to Pass.
