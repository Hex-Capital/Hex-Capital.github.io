# Almanac -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Public computer-use models now let an assistant operate fragmented company systems without bespoke APIs; the buyer is a small team paying to replace manual retrieval and coordination, and the controlling variable is whether Almanac can complete cross-application work reliably enough to justify $250 per user before established platforms bundle the same capability. The dossier does not establish a reliability, retention, or usage threshold—or provide evidence against one. That leaves the central market belief unproven in a category where product availability is no longer the bottleneck.

The product is meaningfully deeper than a chat interface. Connected accounts continuously populate a source-linked wiki, while Slack and iMessage provide access to an assistant with a terminal, integrations, and a managed browser. Confirmations before sending, spending, or committing recognize the operational risk. Better models could improve task completion while Almanac retains company context and workflow state. This is the strongest part of the case, although public computer-use primitives also reduce the cost for competitors to reproduce it.

The bull case is the team’s prior execution. Kushagra Chitkara, Rohan Sharma, and Divit Sheth combine ML research, data science, and production engineering, and CodeAlmanac attracted 791 GitHub stars and 71 forks. Expanding from developer knowledge into company-wide context may reflect real product learning. If those users become a distribution channel for an assistant that performs work across tools, the maintained wiki could compound rather than remain a retrieval feature.

The market evidence currently cuts the other way. CodeAlmanac’s adoption cannot be transferred to a newly repositioned product, while Almanac’s present signals are 846 LinkedIn followers and a three-day trial—not revenue, repeat usage, or renewals. Glean already reports more than $100 million ARR and 250 connectors; Dust serves more than 3,000 organizations with 300,000 agents; Sana reached a $1.1 billion Workday acquisition. This market is crystallizing around well-capitalized platforms. Almanac has not identified the variable that lets a new entrant overcome their distribution, security, and integration advantages.

The proposed data moat also fails the reproduction-cost test. The dossier suggests that sources, relationships, and corrections may create switching costs, but provides no accuracy, migration, retention, or proprietary-data evidence. A continuously maintained wiki plus browser control is an integrated workflow, but iMessage access and a dedicated computer are packaging differences until customers demonstrate that they produce materially better outcomes. The founders’ technical credentials do not qualify for a frontier-research exception: the company is assembling increasingly public capabilities rather than supplying scarce foundational technology.

I pass. By August 2029, I expect Almanac to have pivoted back toward developer-agent knowledge infrastructure rather than reaching $1 million ARR with the broad company-assistant product. The verifiable flip condition is a cohort of at least 10 independent companies renewing after six months at the stated $250-per-user price while using Almanac weekly to execute—not merely answer questions about—cross-application work.
