# Dreamscale Labs -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A GPU cloud or robotics infrastructure platform could buy Dreamscale’s robot-specific transport, inference optimizations, and deployment integrations to capture recurring fleet compute demand. Ownership could be worth paying for if the integrated stack brings fleets onto the buyer’s infrastructure faster or serves them at lower cost than building, licensing, or partnering. That buyer rationale is an inference. The memo documents a HUD research integration, and Dreamscale reports running DreamZero 14B at 320 ms versus 350 ms for NVIDIA’s implementation using half as many GPUs. The company-run benchmark gives a buyer a specific cost advantage to test, while production savings and acquisition pricing remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is repeat paid usage across multiple live fleets that meet customer tail-latency and reliability targets and generate positive contribution margin after regional GPU and serving costs. The HUD integration and A$100,000 grant support early testing. The cost and runway needed to reach that production milestone remain open. Commercial model-license restrictions also leave the scope of a transfer uncertain, and the memo leaves ownership and assignment rights for the core stack unverified. The memo provides no evidence of buyout-grade cash generation, eligible secondary demand, public-market readiness, or distributable cash for dividends or buybacks. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The proceeds bridge uses the shared scenario because actual holder terms are unavailable. A $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-ons. The 5× gross cash target within eight years is $2.5 million. At 3%, that requires $83.3 million of distributable equity, or $88.3 million of enterprise value with an illustrative $5 million of net debt. The equity hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% ownership and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. Using that same debt assumption and simple pro rata allocation before investor taxes, a $25 million sale yields $600,000 (1.2×) and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). Actual debt, fees, and senior claims remain unverified. Dreamscale’s posted rates provide no basis for underwriting either sale price without paid volume, margins, or buyer economics. If growth plateaus below public-market scale, acquisition and eligible secondary demand fail to emerge, and available cash is reinvested, eight years of private operation yields $0 realized cash with the shares still held. A wind-down would pay only the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo does not quantify. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A prospective acquirer could serve the same demand through general-purpose GPU infrastructure, partner with a hosted robotics provider such as Abstract, or use on-device inference where offline operation is essential. Dreamscale’s reported packet-recovery gain came from synthetic frames and simulated compute on one home Wi-Fi link. It leaves open whether the transport advantage persists across production fleets and is valuable enough to own. These alternatives weaken acquisition pricing, but the memo establishes neither a production-equivalent substitute nor a supported sale range that leaves the holder materially short. The Pass gate remains untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price could a GPU cloud justify for owning Dreamscale’s stack based on repeat paid fleet usage and measured serving-cost savings? A buyer-side build-versus-buy analysis grounded in production fleet data would move the action to **Reach out** if it supported a price range that clears the holder’s 5× hurdle after dilution and senior claims. If the strongest supported price leaves the holder materially short, move to **Pass**.
