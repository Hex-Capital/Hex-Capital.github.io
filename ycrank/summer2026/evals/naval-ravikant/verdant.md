# Verdant -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Verdant’s strongest asset is not its software. It is the founders’ accumulated contact with government systems. Aidan Ng worked across DOJ, USDA, Capitol Hill, and Deloitte Government and Public Services; Jason Yi built government HR and legislative-tracking software and researched public-benefits automation and privacy ([Y Combinator, 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/verdant); [Jason Yi](https://jason-yi.vercel.app/)). Interviewing more than 200 local-government employees is further evidence of disciplined problem selection. But neither founder is shown shipping or selling a permitting product, administering a planning department, or carrying a deployment through municipal procurement. This is credible adjacent knowledge, not yet specific knowledge earned inside the exact transaction.

The product addresses real friction. Verdant guides applicants through jurisdiction-specific intake, checks submissions against local rules, and drafts staff reports while leaving approvals with government personnel ([Verdant FAQ, 2026](https://www.verdantapp.com/faq)). The timing is also real: local governments moved into live AI-permitting pilots and contracts during 2025–2026 ([Axios, September 2025](https://www.axios.com/local/san-francisco/2025/09/30/san-jose-ai-permitting-potholes-sf-lessons); [Axios, May 2026](https://www.axios.com/local/miami/2026/05/19/how-florida-cities-are-using-ai-to-streamline-building-permits)). Yet a painful workflow plus timely technology creates a market, not an advantage. GovWell already offers AI completeness and code checks, Clariti owns CivCheck, and PermitFlow reports 12 million permitting datapoints. If they copy Verdant’s interface, the dossier identifies no asset they cannot copy with it.

Jurisdiction-specific ordinance and GIS configuration could eventually become scarce deployment knowledge. Today it cuts both ways. Every new municipality may require mapping another body of ordinances, forms, data, and hearings; without a reusable proprietary corpus, each customer adds implementation labor rather than leverage. No cohort, referral, contribution, or matching evidence shows one jurisdiction improving the product or distribution for another. Verdant is presently selling software one government at a time through demonstrations and direct introductions. The system does not yet compound while the team sleeps.

The market leaves limited room for error. The cited global permit-management software category was only $307 million in 2025, projected to reach $567 million by 2032, and the dossier supplies no U.S. SAM or demonstrated path into adjacent government transactions ([MarketResearch.com, January 2026](https://www.marketresearch.com/GlobalInfoResearch-v4117/Global-Permit-Management-Software-Company-43315832/)). Verdant’s modular architecture could extend across intake, review, hearings, and reporting, but those remain stages of the same permitting workflow. A broad label such as government infrastructure does not create a broad market; a shared capability that unlocks licensing, inspections, benefits, or other regulated transactions would.

The strongest opposing case is that the 200 interviews uncovered an unusually painful entry point, the founders combine policy access with technical ability, and early municipal configuration could quietly produce a proprietary ordinance graph before competitors notice. At pre-seed, revenue and published pricing are not required. But exceptions for an unmonetized product require some other asymmetric proof—obsessive repeat use, an unmistakable prototype, unusual capital efficiency, or a self-reinforcing network. Verdant discloses a July 3, 2026 marketing-site launch and 470 LinkedIn followers, but no live jurisdiction, completed transaction, repeat usage, price, or revenue. Research has crossed into product framing; it has not crossed into adoption.

I see no integrity, founder-conduct, artificial-deadline, or morale red flag. The pass is structural, not personal. Verdant resembles conventional enterprise software in a crowded procurement market precisely when code is becoming cheaper and incumbents possess customers, integrations, and data. I would reopen the decision if five named jurisdictions were live and their deployments had produced an exclusive, reusable ordinance-and-review corpus that measurably reduced each successive implementation time. Until then, I would not invest my own money.
