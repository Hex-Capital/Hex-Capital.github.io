# hiloop -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Karan Brar led ML infrastructure at DynamoAI, while Thomas Boser built production ML systems at Discord, SoFi, Crosswise, and Reducto. hiloop is an insider-built machine whose highest signal is lived technical proximity and whose lowest is unresolved two-founder authority. hiloop feels less like tourists discovering “recursive self-improvement” in a market memo than two mechanics building a better engine bay—but I still cannot tell who holds the wrench when they disagree.

**Anduril** is the high rung because Palmer Luckey’s biography made the product almost inevitable before defense became fashionable. Brar and Boser similarly met while doing ML, post-training, and platform work at Reducto, so hiloop emerged from repeated contact with the machinery it now orchestrates. The divergence is depth and destiny: Luckey had already shipped Oculus from a garage, while hiloop’s dossier shows relevant employment but no prior product taken to scale, no autobiographical moment, and no evidence that either founder was compelled to build hiloop rather than merely well-qualified to build it.

**HQ Trivia** is the low rung because a vivid product temporarily concealed a co-founder structure that eventually became the fracture. hiloop names Brar as Founder/CEO, which is better than shared titles, but Boser’s role is unspecified and hiloop discloses no equity split, vesting protocol, or tiebreaker authority. “CEO” on a YC page is a rebuttal, not proof. With two former Reducto colleagues and no documented conflict mechanism, hiloop has not cleared the lesson HQ Trivia taught me with my own money.

Prime Intellect’s $145 million in announced funding and more than 6,000 reported customers make hiloop’s “magically weird” loop vulnerable to becoming a feature inside somebody else’s cloud. Over 18 months, hiloop’s weirdness compounds only if 4,188 experiments create reusable knowledge about which interventions work across customers; otherwise, hiloop merely spends 50 B200s to generate traces that Prime Intellect, OpenPipe, or Together AI can reproduce. The recursive-research framing is delightful, but hiloop is entering an increasingly consensus category with large, obvious neighbors rather than walking into an industry other investors refuse to touch.

Fifty Nvidia B200 GPUs running for two days gives hiloop capital intensity without yet giving hiloop a capital-intensive moat. The score flips upward if hiloop controls scarce compute, develops proprietary systems below the orchestration layer, or proves that its snapshot-and-fork architecture produces learning competitors cannot cheaply reconstruct. Today the private monorepo, brokered secrets, and SQL-queryable lineage are thoughtful infrastructure engineering, but the artifact remains software riding rented frontier hardware rather than a SpaceX rocket or Crusoe container nobody else wants to finance.

**Flock Safety** could be detected in a hotel Wi-Fi scan before its market was obvious; hiloop can be observed only by a developer reading traces, installing an SDK, or comparing 0.9016 validation bits-per-byte against 0.9109. The 12.54% NanoGPT runtime improvement makes hiloop more tangible than a slide-deck AI wrapper, but hiloop still fails my normal-human test: I cannot ride it, photograph it, or hand it to a friend. It is an instrument panel for specialists, not an artifact wandering through my physical environment.

The 4,188-experiment demonstration predicts a competent infrastructure outcome rather than an independent defining platform: within 36 months, hiloop will pivot toward enterprise post-training evaluation orchestration, reach $3 million–$8 million ARR, and be acquired by Together AI for less than $100 million. hiloop’s founders know the substrate well enough to build something useful, but the invisible product, incumbent bundling risk, and compute dependence pull hiloop toward becoming a valuable subsystem inside a larger cloud.

The 0.9016 benchmark result is the bull case that stings: hiloop already beat the cited 0.9109 result, improved NanoGPT runtime by 12.54%, and built a working system capable of coordinating thousands of experiments before public customer traction. **DeepMind** reminds me that an abstract product and credentialed technical founders can transcend my usual origin test when genuine polymath compounding appears. hiloop has not shown that exception yet—seven public repositories top out at one star each, no revenue is public, and Prime Intellect already overlaps directly. I do not write the check today; three named customers each completing a second paid hiloop campaign would flip my verdict by proving that the experimental lineage compounds rather than evaporates.
