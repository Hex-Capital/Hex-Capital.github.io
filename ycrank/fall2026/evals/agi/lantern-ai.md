# Lantern AI -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B, New economies.** Lantern’s strongest seed case is that automated screening and interviews could make it economical for employers to assess applicants they previously left unreviewed. One customer reported screening more than 1,000 candidates and hiring an engineer. That establishes use in a completed search. The prior screening cutoff, assessment quality at equivalent standards, and recruiter time saved remain unknown. Lantern’s terms require human review of every final employment decision. The market-opening scenario therefore depends on savings after review, retries, exceptions, and compliance work. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Lantern describes reusing employer-approved criteria and debrief feedback across agents on top of an existing ATS. If that history makes hiring decisions more consistent across workflows, customers could have a reason to keep paying when competing models are equally capable. Buyer preference under parity remains untested, and the reported billion-person graph leaves control of a scarce input unproven. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Lantern reports $1M in annual enterprise contracts, an early willingness-to-pay signal whose buyer mix leaves demand for the newly viable use unresolved. The founders shipped the original interviewer before launching the broader platform, showing relevant execution. A separately reported account expansion from about $11K to $92K annually suggests room to sell more within an account. Repeatable expansion depends on the cost of serving the next account after ATS integration, inference, and support. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Lantern could be selling faster screening to employers with existing recruiting budgets while ATS vendors absorb the same workflow. Ashby’s native interviewer and Workday’s embedded grading show how incumbents can deliver overlapping functionality inside systems customers already use. Human review and compliance could also consume enough of the labor savings to leave previously skipped assessments uneconomic. These risks leave both Path B conditions unproven. The memo contains no like-for-like test that establishes either failure, so the override leaves room to Watch. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will independent employers pay to repeat a screening workflow they previously skipped for cost reasons after trying a functionally comparable ATS-native alternative? A paid head-to-head pilot should verify the prior screening cutoff, measure fully loaded labor at equivalent assessment quality, and record which product employers choose at viable pricing on the next search. Verified labor savings that open the use and repeat paid preference for Lantern would support Reach out if the updated score clears the outreach threshold. A credible trial showing either that screening remains uneconomic or that buyers readily switch under parity would trigger Pass.
