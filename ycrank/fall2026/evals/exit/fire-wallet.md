# Fire Wallet -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a payments platform is Fire’s strongest supported route. A platform could buy Fire to add private per-request settlement and spending controls for agents. AWS’s AgentCore Payments preview with Coinbase and Stripe shows that established platforms are already building for this workflow. Fire reports that an e402 mainnet test returned a paid response in under two seconds and that MobileCoin was acquired in 2026. The memo gives no buyer, rationale, or price for that acquisition. The evidence supports a reason to evaluate Fire, with buyer savings and willingness to pay still unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

The milestone is recurring production use that demonstrates what a buyer would gain over its existing stack. The memo records $500,000 in YC financing, and Fire reports a signed Trustboop LOI and an enterprise customer in onboarding. The customer’s $1.5 million in monthly volume is its existing business volume. The estimated $100 million in annual GMV represents potential volume from introductions. Fire’s processed volume, retention, and the capital needed to reach the milestone remain unknown. Fire advertises zero merchant and e402 fees, so those volume figures establish no buyer revenue or payout capacity. The memo also leaves transfer rights, required consents, and holder eligibility open. It provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at full pro rata participation. The sale cases below use illustrative prices. Both assume an all-cash close within eight years, eligibility to sell the entire position, and a pro rata waterfall with no senior preference reducing the holder’s share.

- **Modest sale.** At $30 million in enterprise value, $5 million in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $25 million in distributable equity and $750,000 in gross holder cash, or 1.5×.
- **Strong sale.** At $100 million in enterprise value, $10 million in assumed net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90 million in distributable equity and $2.7 million in gross holder cash, or 5.4×. If the holder can sell only 90% of the position on those terms, cash falls to $2.43 million, below the objective.
- **Prolonged private.** If Fire keeps operating, reinvests available cash, and completes no eligible share sale or funded payout within eight years, the holder realizes $0.

The memo gives no supported price range or deal terms. The scenarios establish how much a buyer would need to pay and how sensitive the outcome is to participation.

### Countercase

A buyer could assemble much of the functionality in-house or through existing vendors. Crossmint already offers programmable agent wallets and per-request payments, and Hinkal offers confidential merchant settlement. Fire identifies no structural barrier, and merchants must implement and verify e402 payments. If production use fails to show a material advantage, a platform may have little reason to pay the price required for the holder’s return. The evidence makes substitution credible while leaving the economics of the combined product unresolved. It establishes neither a structural closing block nor inadequate proceeds across the strongest plausible route, so the Pass gate remains untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a payments platform pay enough to clear the holder’s $2.5 million cash objective once Fire demonstrates recurring production use and measurable savings over the platform’s own stack? Buyer-side feedback grounded in those results, with an indicative price range that clears the objective after deal adjustments and the holder waterfall, would move the action to Reach out. If the strongest credible buyer economics still imply a materially lower payout, the action would move to Pass.
