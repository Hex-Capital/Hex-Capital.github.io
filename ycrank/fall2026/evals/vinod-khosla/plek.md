# Plek -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Plek identifies 36 FTEs performing potentially AI-addressable tasks inside a 250-person organization. That is 14.4% of the workforce exposed to an opportunity, not 14.4% of payroll eliminated or productive capacity added. The distinction determines my investment decision. Mapping work could become the starting point for making expertise radically cheaper. This dossier quantifies task exposure but establishes neither the achievable cost reduction nor the population that benefits. There is enough substance to investigate, insufficient consequence demonstrated to purchase the option.

Bouquet is the strongest argument against passing. His MIT workforce research directly addresses the problem, and YC reports that his previous startup, Biot, sold in 2022. A researcher who can map work and then implement automation could accumulate a valuable connection between predicted opportunities and actual results. That fits my preference for scientific workflows becoming repeatable platforms, and my willingness to back execution-heavy software deserves consideration here. But the proposed learning loop remains an inference. Research credentials and a prior sale also do not establish how Bouquet revises beliefs or recruits complementary people against specific risks; those dimensions receive their missing-evidence defaults.

The technical question is whether organizational records contain enough information to prescribe useful automation. Plek begins with titles and an ontology, then uses interviews to replace directional estimates. Skan observes workflows directly; Workhelix already assesses task-level AI opportunities. Plek could win through much cheaper discovery, but there is no comparative measurement of assessment cost, accuracy, or successful implementation. Its proprietary ontology is a concrete research asset, not yet evidence of a 5–10x discontinuity. I cannot establish that Plek is merely an interface wrapper. Equally, including implementation does not establish proprietary capability.

Spend the next dollar testing whether the map predicts what actually works. The reported 735-task assessment demonstrates activity, but the dossier supplies no predicted-versus-realized outcomes, decisive threshold, experiment budget, or stop condition. The displayed 13,500-employee and $50 billion portfolio examples cannot fill that gap: Plek’s terms describe case studies as illustrative. Enterprise access also requires resolving the conflicting SOC 2 disclosures and handling unredacted interview text. These are concrete execution risks; they do not prove deception or technical failure.

I pass on the present evidence. Negotiated statements of work establish a commercial mechanism, but reveal no delivery labor, gross margin, or customer savings from which to calculate unsubsidized economics. My exceptions for an extraordinary instigator or overwhelming societal consequence do not override that here: Bouquet has relevant accomplishments, while the transformative outcome remains unmeasured. The red flag to watch is implementation labor growing with every deployment before the ontology’s predictive value is established; the dossier does not show that this has happened. One verifiable result would reopen my decision: an independently audited customer deployment showing a 10x reduction in fully loaded cost for a material workflow selected by Plek, at equivalent output quality, including assessment and implementation expense.
