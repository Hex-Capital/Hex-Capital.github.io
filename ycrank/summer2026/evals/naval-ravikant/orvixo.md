# Orvixo -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Ethan Phillips and Kolya Schwinge have Cambridge physics, applied-ML, Attio, and IFS credentials, but Orvixo has no mechanism by which one factory makes the product more valuable to the next. That is Orvixo’s signature: capable, high-energy technical founders building a single-sided enterprise tool. Intelligence can create software. Networks create compounding. Orvixo shows the former and has not designed the latter.

**Stack Overflow** is the high rung for long-term founders: Jeff Atwood and Joel Spolsky were builders serving builders, with shipped work and public reasoning that exposed intelligence, energy, and integrity before the investment. Orvixo’s founders clear the intelligence screen through quantum-computing research, applied ML, and Kolya’s senior ML role at IFS; creating a factory-planning system also signals energy. Orvixo diverges because neither founder has a prior venture, factory-planning operating history, or public technical artifact establishing how they behave when systems and relationships break. Integrity is quiet, which is better than advertised honesty, but the 10-year-partner evidence remains incomplete.

**Perplexity’s** network failure is that each new user does not directly improve the product for previous users; exceptional specific knowledge and permissionless distribution overcame it. Orvixo has the same single-sided weakness without the exception’s compensating strength. A factory encoding its labour, tooling, and scheduling constraints does not automatically help another factory, and Orvixo provides no evidence that constraint libraries transfer across customers. Oden Technologies, Sight Machine, and Siemens can improve centrally too. That is ordinary software learning, not an **Uber**-style network in which added supply creates immediate demand-side value.

Founder-led scheduling reviews reveal Orvixo’s leverage mechanism over the next 18 months: each deployment connects ERP, MRP, shop-floor data, planner knowledge, and factory-specific operating rules. Orvixo can replicate its code cheaply, but implementation and distribution remain permissioned by plant managers, procurement, integrations, and founder labour. The UK automotive-supplier map and manufacturing articles add media leverage at the edge; they do not remove the enterprise gate. Unless Orvixo converts factory modeling into self-serve configuration, every sale creates another service obligation rather than another distribution node.

Kolya Schwinge’s IFS experience and Ethan Phillips’s optimisation-adjacent physics put Orvixo above a generic “ex-FAANG PM found a TAM” founder profile, but neither biography establishes non-trainable manufacturing knowledge. Orvixo would flip this criterion into the top tier if Kolya had repeatedly shipped production scheduling inside IFS or if Ethan had operated a factory and encoded a problem he personally lived. Today Orvixo combines transferable ML intelligence with a domain that punishes missing tacit knowledge: machine constraints, material latency, labour rules, and planner overrides are learned on the floor, not at Cambridge.

**Uber** had room to move from black cars to mass-market rides, logistics, delivery, and a global marketplace without changing its fundamental architecture. Orvixo addresses a $1.744 billion APS segment growing 20.4%, with adjacent vectors across demand, material, capacity, and production planning. Orvixo therefore has some pivot-room, but the expansion path runs directly into Siemens, Dassault Systèmes, Asprova, and Sight Machine. The market is large enough for a useful business. It is not yet obviously broad enough for 10,000x geometry.

IFS already employs the distribution, integrations, and enterprise trust that Orvixo lacks. By July 2029, I expect Orvixo to be acquired by IFS after narrowing into an IFS-centric scheduling copilot producing $1 million to $3 million in ARR. Orvixo’s strong technical founders can build a valuable planning layer, but its lowest-scoring network architecture prevents users from pulling in users; incumbents own the channels, so the natural endpoint is product acquisition rather than independent compounding.

**Perplexity** is the strongest bull objection: a single-sided AI product can become enormous when deep technical knowledge and code-native distribution make the missing network irrelevant. Orvixo also has two technical founders, a 20.4%-growth market, natural-language model configuration, and incumbents burdened by rigid implementations; reusable constraint libraries could eventually turn deployments into data leverage. The exception does not yet hold because Orvixo distributes through founder-led reviews, requires live factory integrations, and has no public customer, deployment, performance result, or reusable proprietary dataset. I would flip to Invest if three named factories each reported at least a 20% scheduling improvement and Orvixo deployed each new site in under 14 days without founder-led customization.
