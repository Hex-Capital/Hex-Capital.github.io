# Hemlock -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Hemlock gives me a reason to study the technology, but no basis for pricing the investment. Proposed post-money, competing term sheets, and whether my money would be the first scarce check are all undisclosed. That does not establish an auction. It leaves my primary gate unanswered. I am passing on the evidence available, while taking the underlying technical proposition seriously.

The interesting asset is force information that ordinary demonstration video cannot see. Hemlock’s wrist EMG Bracelet could collect that information without putting gloves between workers and their tasks. Its reported 10× improvement in egg handling and 2.7× improvement in lightbulb installation suggest the signal may matter to robot performance. That is the strongest objection to passing: a small capture advantage could become an indispensable input across many labs. But these are company-reported results on two tasks, without aggregate validation across workers and environments. MANUS, StretchSense, and the independent FACT research also make clear that useful force supervision does not automatically become an exclusive business.

Julian Olschwang is the strongest part of the underwriting. He built the Talk To The Hand sensing glove, received a $25,000 Davidson Fellowship in 2024, and worked on GANs for military robotics data at UCLA’s Biomechatronics Lab. Those are concrete technical receipts connecting wearable sensing with robotic training data. Hemlock fits my preference for founders who can translate between two systems, and for software reorganizing difficult physical infrastructure. The remaining distinction is commercial: the dossier establishes relevant building experience, without establishing a deployed collection operation inside customer workflows.

Who owns the access? Hemlock calls the Bracelet proprietary, but the four claimed wearable patents lack identifiers and demonstrated company ownership. Exclusive worker relationships, collection agreements, and dataset rights are also undocumented. The YC launch and commissioned WERZ film provide visibility; neither demonstrates that proprietary access caused a purchase. I would not count the same two task improvements again as distribution evidence. Hemlock could build a valuable private corpus, but that corpus is still a hypothesis.

The financing needs to resolve a similarly specific uncertainty. A plausible experiment would test whether an independent lab can reproduce the benefit across unseen workers and tasks at an acceptable collection cost. Hemlock has not disclosed that as its capital plan, supplied a deadline, or attached a pass/fail threshold. I cannot award proceeds credit for an experiment I invented. Dataset licensing itself is inferred, and pricing, collection costs, margins, and burn are unavailable. Missing revenue is ordinary at this stage; it still cannot establish a route to operating without another round. There is no evidence of financing pressure, founder deadlock, or economics premised on disappearing AI errors, so I apply the missing-evidence defaults rather than additional penalties.

I can override missing financing evidence when external demand or cash economics are unusually decisive. Hemlock’s technical demonstrations are promising, but they do not supply the paid conversion, durable purchasing behavior, or alternative financing structure behind those exceptions. My one verifiable condition for reopening this pass is an independent robotics lab placing a repeat paid dataset order at documented positive contribution margin. That would connect the sensing advantage to a customer-controlled cash driver. Until then, I see a capable technical founder and a potentially useful signal, with the investable transaction still undefined.
