# Touchmark -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The missing fact is not market size. It is what broke. Touchmark moved from quality-based output pricing in July 2026 to forward token-capacity contracts by August, but the dossier does not identify the observation that forced this change or what the founders did when a buyer, provider, or settlement mechanism failed. A fast change can be resourcefulness, or it can be two adjacent ideas tried in succession. Without the blocker and resulting workaround, I cannot tell which. That leaves the most important test at its missing-evidence default.

There is nevertheless a real artifact here. Touchmark lists live books for GLM 5.2 and MiniMax-M3, supports prepaid balances, promises delivery through an OpenAI-compatible key, and permits contracts to be resold before consumption. This is more than a diagram. It combines market design with inference delivery, which fits Ilia Bolgov’s financial-product experience and Roman Yanushevskyi’s quant and AI-engineering background. But a displayed order book is not yet measured technical proof. No completed trade, delivered token block, successful resale, participant count, or volume is disclosed.

The hard part is not constructing the interface. It is making a market at the intersection of model, delivery month, quantity, and price while inference costs are falling extraordinarily fast. Buyers may not want to prepay $3,800 for a billion GLM 5.2 tokens if the equivalent spot cost falls before delivery, and resale only helps if another buyer appears. Ornn has raised $33 million, SF Compute reportedly manages over $100 million of hardware, and OpenRouter has roughly $50 million in annualized revenue. Those figures do not make Touchmark impossible. They make liquidity evidence more important than investor legibility.

The strongest bull case is the founders themselves. An IOI gold medalist with Citadel quantitative-research and Lovable AI-engineering experience, paired with a mathematics-and-finance founder who worked in Revolut’s trading unit, is an unusually plausible team for this exact combination of pricing, software, and market structure. Their rapid product transition may eventually prove they are running backs rather than tourists. Bindwell is the relevant exception: very young technical founders overcame uncertainty with numerical proof. The material difference is that Touchmark has not yet produced the corresponding measurement. A live book without a completed transaction is like a program that compiles but has never been run.

Nor is there yet a narrow well of users pulling the product back. The dossier names “AI companies” and “model providers,” not a specific buyer with an urgent capacity problem. There is no usage, payment, LOI, repeat purchase, or requested workflow. The founders’ backgrounds make the schlep-heavy wedge plausible, but they do not show that either founder personally encountered this problem. The disclosed evidence therefore misses the operational gate: resourcefulness is unproven, technical proof remains provisional, and user pull is absent.

I would pass today. My base case is that within 36 months Touchmark either shuts down or pivots into conventional inference procurement software because thin model-and-month liquidity prevents a durable resale market. The single condition that would flip me is $100,000 of publicly verifiable completed contract volume from at least five independent buyers, including one completed resale. That would simultaneously show that the machinery works and that users want the peculiar thing Touchmark has built.
