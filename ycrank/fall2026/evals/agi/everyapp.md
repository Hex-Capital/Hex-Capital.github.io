# EveryApp -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — New economies.** EveryApp could make shared, customizable business apps economical for small teams priced out of per-seat SaaS or unable to maintain their own stack. Its Gateway gives ready-to-install apps shared sign-in, centrally managed API keys, and agent access inside the customer’s Cloudflare account. That architecture gives a seed-stage team a credible way to reduce setup labor. Whether it lowers total delivery labor at equivalent quality, including maintenance, retries, and support, remains unmeasured. (The Idea, Key Risks)

EveryApp’s 906 GitHub stars show interest. Senescu reports hundreds of paying customers for standalone OpenSEO. That claim supports early demand for the SEO product, while paid conversion to EveryApp and demand from previously excluded teams remain unknown. Senescu’s prior work building compliance agents and the progression from a Gateway-only release through standalone OpenSEO to a relaunch with installable apps show relevant execution. Operating a managed service across multiple customers remains untested in the memo. (Market & Traction, Founders & Team, Key Facts)

The expansion unit is a managed team account, with additional apps and usage as potential expansion. **Scenario assumption:** At the announced $10 monthly starting price and an assumed $30 per hour in loaded labor cost, 20 minutes of monthly onboarding and support labor per account would consume the full fee before any infrastructure or inference costs EveryApp bears. The scale case depends on measured support time, conversion, and usage revenue. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Cheap models could help buyers deploy equivalent apps themselves and competing hosts reuse the MIT-licensed Gateway. OpenSEO already runs independently through Docker or Cloudflare, so its users can get the core SEO workflow without adopting EveryApp. The proposed catalog has a publishing mechanism, with no measured evidence that more contributors improve buyer utility or that buyers prefer EveryApp under functional parity. Cloudflare supplies the deployment infrastructure, and the memo identifies no separate scarce capability EveryApp controls for Path A. These facts weigh against durable capture. Paid preference under parity remains testable, so the evidence supports Watch while the Reach out gate remains unmet. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will small teams that previously went without a shared, customizable app renew a paid EveryApp plan after trying a functionally equivalent alternative powered by comparably capable models, while EveryApp delivers the workflow with materially less total human labor? A paid cohort trial should measure setup, retries, exceptions, maintenance, and support at matched quality and record renewals after buyers try the alternative. Material labor savings and repeat paid preference would support Reach out if the updated evaluation clears the score, confidence, and mandate gates. If a representative trial shows that full delivery labor erases the savings or buyers readily switch at parity, move to Pass.
