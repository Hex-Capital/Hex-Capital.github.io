# Halmos Labs -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** If cheaper AI lets research teams propose experiments faster than qualified external labs can run them, timely access to lab capacity becomes more valuable. Physical Validation has launched a catalogue and project-specific lab quoting. Halmos identifies external testing access as a bottleneck and reports that physical demonstrations are underway, with results scheduled for December 10. Those claims leave open whether buyers face meaningful delays and whether Halmos can secure capacity they cannot obtain directly. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

A completed lab order is the unit of expansion. In a viable scenario, Halmos keeps enough of the buyer’s payment after lab charges to cover scoping, supplier verification, coordination, and rework. The memo documents project-specific quotes but gives no platform fee or realized order costs. It also describes an earlier experiment-design collaboration in which a team revised its plan. Buyer commitment to Physical Validation is undisclosed, so customer demand takes the absent-evidence default. The launch and the founders’ relevant research experience support seed-stage execution credit. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Halmos may offer a sourcing interface that buyers can bypass. Its catalogue terms restrict bulk redistribution, while its supplier terms ask buyers to verify provider details directly. Science Exchange already offers lab sourcing and an AI assistant, and Medra and Ginkgo operate their own labs. Those are credible alternatives, but the memo contains no buyer comparison showing equivalent access. That leaves the path on Watch. The earlier product’s reported reduction in simulated evaluations supplies no evidence of a newly viable paid use for Path B. (*Defensibility*, *The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Will an independent research team pay Halmos for a qualified lab slot it cannot obtain on comparable terms through direct booking or an incumbent? A paid order backed by repeatable priority access from the lab would move the action to **Reach out**. A representative comparison showing equivalent access through those alternatives would close Path A and move the action to **Pass**.
