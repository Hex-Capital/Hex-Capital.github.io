# Fleety -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Fleety checks bookings against call transcripts and equipment records, assigns technicians, and revises schedules as jobs change. This serves plumbing and HVAC operators and leaves the building-blocks path unsupported because the memo identifies no scarce input Fleety supplies to AI workloads. The industry-enabled path also scores zero. Fleety’s reported results show potential labor savings in an existing dispatch workflow. The evidence leaves open whether those savings make dispatch affordable for a previously excluded shop or enable a scheduling cadence the shop could not previously afford. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Shop-specific rules and direct writeback to ServiceTitan give Fleety a workflow advantage, while ServiceTitan’s native technician matching and control of board access limit its pricing power. A Panther Services Group operations manager’s appearance in a customer video shows buyer engagement. Paid commitments and repeat use remain unverified. Both founders worked as trades dispatchers, and Fleety has launched with a dispatcher override. Reliable exception handling across shops remains unproven. (Defensibility, Key Risks, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

Subscription pricing, on-site setup labor, and ongoing support costs are undisclosed, leaving scale economics at the default. Confidence is medium because the product and buyer engagement are documented, while the strongest operating results come from Fleety and lack a comparable cost baseline. (The Idea, Key Facts, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

Fleety reports handling about 92% of assignments at Albert Nahman Plumbing, Heating & Cooling, enabling one dispatcher to manage more than 30 technicians and saving about $60,000 annually in overhead. If similar shops could previously afford only periodic schedule updates, automating reassignment as jobs change could make continuous dispatch viable. Comparable models would still need access to shop records and a way to write assignments to the dispatch board. The reported deployment leaves the prior affordability limit and the cost per correctly dispatched job unmeasured, so the Pass gate still applies. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Defensibility)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a shop that previously could afford only periodic schedule updates sustain paid, event-driven dispatch with Fleety? A paid deployment would move the action to Reach out if it documented that prior staffing limit and showed that Fleety’s integrated workflow made continuous reassignment viable against the shop’s native alternative. The comparison would measure fully loaded cost per correctly dispatched job, including setup, subscription fees, dispatcher review, and rework.
