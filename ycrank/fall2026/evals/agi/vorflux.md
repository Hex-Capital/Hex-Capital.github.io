# Vorflux -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Vorflux runs parallel planning, implementation, and review agents on dedicated compute and attaches browser-test evidence to their work. That identifies a specific AI workload, but the memo leaves compute scarcity unestablished. The broader coding-tools forecast gives no evidence of procurement delays, limited supply, or difficulty substituting equivalent capacity. The building-blocks path scores zero. (The Idea)

Vorflux says more predictable coding models made recurring AutoBots roles practical. Under the prior workflow, engineers initiated tasks, coordinated agents, and brought changes back for testing and review. AutoBots could reduce that labor, but the reported customer outcomes establish that existing engineering work got done. The memo leaves open whether any team could now afford a previously deferred use or sustain a previously impractical cadence. The industry-enabled path scores zero, triggering Pass. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Persistent repository and test context and role-specific setup could make a configured deployment harder to replace. That advantage is inferred, with retention unmeasured and competing products already offering overlapping automation and verification. TrashLab and Supermemory report concrete customer use, with payment and repeat use unverified. The public launch and the founders’ infrastructure and engineering leadership support execution credit. The posted machine-hour prices establish a billing model, while delivery costs and contribution per successful task remain unknown. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Founders & Team, The Idea)

### Countercase

The strongest case for outreach is that AutoBots could make routine cross-repository maintenance affordable at a cadence that engineers previously could not staff. Supermemory’s reported fixes across six repositories and its data backfill show that Vorflux can handle work spanning repositories, and recurring Slack roles with budgets and approval routing provide a plausible way to reduce supervision. Those signals leave the adoption threshold open, so the Pass gate still applies. (Market & Traction, The Idea)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paying AutoBots customer sustain recurring maintenance it previously deferred because the engineering time was too costly? A repeat deployment that documents the prior workflow and compares fully loaded cost per accepted change, including compute, review, and rework, would answer it. Evidence that the cost fell below the customer’s adoption threshold and that repository setup, testing, and approval routing still mattered with comparable models would move the action to Reach out.
