# Shire Intelligence -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong fit with full-service restaurants, Shire’s best growth path is to reuse existing CCTV feeds and distribute through POS resellers. Tracking table readiness and server workload is a recurring job that plausibly transfers across markets. The camera-based workflow gives Shire a reusable starting point, with POS compatibility, camera setup, and footage governance still uncertain. Shire publishes quote-based plans and offers demos and reseller inquiries. Those are concrete routes to buyers, with a sales step visible today and the amount of installation help unknown. (The Idea, Key Risks)

Shire reports 13 live restaurants at its October launch and a 12% average reduction in table-turn time. The outcome claim lacks a comparison baseline and measurement window. The deployments show early adoption, with paid status, realized revenue, and commercial growth unknown. Use during every shift could make live alerts and seating decisions part of the restaurant’s routine. The advertised POS transaction fees suggest a possible way for spend to rise with volume, but the memo leaves Vision billing, renewals, and same-account expansion unclear. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

If Vision is sold per location, incremental contribution would be that site’s fees less attributable selling or reseller costs, camera and POS setup, video inference, and support. Existing cameras could reduce equipment costs at sites with adequate coverage. The memo gives too little pricing and deployment cost evidence to judge whether that contribution is positive. (The Idea, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Camera blind spots and classification errors could make each restaurant a hands-on deployment. Toast’s POS-linked table status already covers part of the host workflow, which could limit what restaurants will pay for Shire’s added visibility. A reseller could bring in more leads while leaving the same setup burden at each site. The structural gate clears because the memo leaves open a standard deployment path and does not establish that site-specific work is unavoidable or uneconomic. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a POS reseller repeatedly take restaurants in a second market from first contact to paid live use with a standard setup and positive incremental contribution? A dated cohort with first invoices, continued paid use, and site-level setup and support costs would answer it. That result could move the action to Reach out if it lifts the score to at least 65 with Medium or High confidence. Evidence that each site requires bespoke work that makes incremental growth uneconomic, with no credible way to reduce that work, would trigger Pass.
