# Melty -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Melty’s proposed post-money is unknown. Competing term sheets are unknown. Whether my check would supply genuinely scarce capital is unknown. The conflicting $125,000 and $500,000 funding-database entries establish none of those things. I will not infer an auction from YC membership, but I also cannot underwrite a cheap, non-consensus entry from financing silence. The second gate is equally unresolved: the dossier describes what Melty builds, but no round-funded hypothesis, deadline, or pass/fail milestone. “Mashup any game” is a product ambition. What specific truth does my money buy?

The interesting asset is the bridge between people who make game modifications and people unwilling to spend an evening configuring them. Melty supplies dependency installation, game launching, multiplayer invitations, and agent-assisted publishing. David Miller’s reported experience coaching millions of gamers gives him relevant operating context, and the downloadable Windows product is a shipping receipt beyond a résumé. That earns some founder credit. It does not yet establish that he has operated both a creator economy and reliable software distribution across publisher-controlled environments. Nexus Mods and Overwolf already occupy important parts of that infrastructure.

The strongest objection to passing is that Melty may already possess the difficult half of a consumer marketplace. Its officially linked Discord reportedly has **10,611 members**, and a founder who understands gamers has shipped a tool that removes concrete friction. A creator publishes something entertaining; friends invite friends; players return for another mashup. If that loop works, external coding agents could expand supply while Melty owns discovery and customer relationships. But the membership figure comes from a third-party snapshot, and the dossier shows no cohort connecting community membership to installation, repeat play, creator publishing, or payment. Contractual exclusivity extending twelve months after removal can restrict distribution; it cannot manufacture demand. My owned-access pattern requires observed behavior, so the community earns no conversion premium yet.

Delete the next financing. What pays for compatibility fixes, support, distribution, and eventual creator payouts? Melty and its current mashups are free, while paid content and tips remain possibilities without prices. That is perfectly ordinary at pre-seed; it also supplies no evidence of a self-financing path. Publisher and anti-cheat restrictions can limit what the launcher delivers, and successful execution on a creator’s own PC does not establish reliable customer delivery. The recording controversy adds a trust question, although the allegations are unverified and should not be treated as established misconduct. The economic concern is that every attractive mashup might add maintenance obligations before it adds cash.

I pass at this evidence level. The exceptions in my framework require something concrete to override missing financing gates: demonstrated conversion, cash economics, unusual external validation, or a security that captures an already durable asset. Melty’s shipped product and gaming background make the bull case credible, but neither establishes those overrides. My one verifiable condition for reopening underwriting: **100 customers acquired through the existing Discord each purchase a second mashup within 60 days, with positive cohort contribution after creator payouts, delivery, and support.** That would show the community can produce repeat cash rather than merely attention. I would then have a business mechanism worth pricing and a sharper question about what additional capital must prove.
