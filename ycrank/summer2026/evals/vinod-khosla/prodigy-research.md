# Prodigy Research -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

A machine that consistently out-trades elite quantitative researchers could alter a large expert-labor and capital-allocation system. Prodigy Research has not quantified that consequence. Its disclosed objective is proprietary trading profit, not a 10x reduction in the cost of financial research, broader access to trustworthy expertise, or demonstrably better allocation of capital. A 108% return enriches the owner of the strategy; it does not by itself produce a 100x societal consequence. That distinction is fatal to the current option-value case.

The technical claim is more interesting than the mission. Prodigy reports that its proprietary foundation model and agent harness outperform GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Fable, and a 90th-percentile Jane Street trader. Michael Wang reportedly combines DeepMind model training with Jane Street trading, while Yuhua Wang built AI infrastructure at Apple and Salesforce. This is unusually relevant founder-market fit: model architecture, quantitative finance, and production infrastructure sit inside two people. But the benchmark datasets, evaluation protocol, capital assumptions, and statistical significance are undisclosed. Two months of live trading is a prototype result, not evidence of a durable 5–10x discontinuity.

The 108% delta-neutral return with no down week is either extraordinary evidence or evidence that has not yet encountered its regime. Without daily returns, volatility, drawdown, leverage, transaction costs, or an independent audit, I cannot distinguish model intelligence from concentrated exposure, favorable market conditions, or measurement choices. The largest kill risk is not whether the system can generate a spectacular two-month chart. It is whether its edge survives new regimes, larger capital, market impact, and competitors using correlated models. Prodigy has spent real dollars in live markets, which is better than another benchmark-only AI company, but it has disclosed neither a pass/fail threshold nor the next experiment against that kill risk.

The strongest bull case is the known exception for execution-heavy fintech: an exceptional small team can begin with a narrow instrument and expand into infrastructure around an enormous financial value pool, as Block did. Prodigy has the technical complementarity and live-product usage that make that exception worth checking. The material difference is that Block expanded participation in commerce, while Prodigy currently captures private trading alpha in a crowded, largely zero-sum activity. The dossier supplies no commercial customer, platform roadmap, or mechanism by which the proprietary strategy becomes broadly valuable infrastructure.

The proposed data flywheel could become real—trade outcomes and execution data might improve the model—but two months cannot establish it, and Standard Signal, EquiLibre, and Numerai already pursue adjacent learning systems with more disclosed capital or scale. More troubling, nothing public establishes architectural novelty beyond training a finance-specific foundation model and harness. That resembles the red-flag pattern of applying prevailing AI architectures to a fashionable interface or workflow until the company publishes an auditable capability frontier. Strong résumés do not substitute for evidence of belief revision, risk-tuned recruiting, or a learning advantage competitors cannot purchase.

Economics are also undefined. Proprietary trading has no conventional subsidy problem, but it has capacity, compute, liquidity, and market-impact constraints that serve the same economic function: returns can disappear as deployment scales. My verifiable flip condition is an independent 12-month audit showing at least a 3.0 net Sharpe ratio, below 10% maximum drawdown, and comparable performance after scaling to at least $50 million of deployed capital. Until then, this is a potentially exceptional research team attached to an unverified trading result, not yet a consequential company. I pass.
