# TrueMile -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

TrueMile’s strongest path is **New Economies**. Independent owner-operators typically book one load at a time. TrueMile combines fleet-wide planning with agents that call brokers and coordinate dispatch, while a person still approves every load. TrueMile reports that one dispatcher can manage nine times as many trucks. If dispatcher hours, wages, and completed loads per truck are comparable, that implies roughly one-ninth of the dispatcher payroll per completed load, before software and exception costs. The market-opening hypothesis is that this cost reduction lets TrueMile profitably offer recurring service to shippers on lanes where uncertain backhauls made manual dispatch uneconomic. The memo establishes a plausible labor-saving mechanism and leaves the cost per completed load and newly viable demand unmeasured. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Under model parity, TrueMile could still earn shipper business by coordinating leased-on trucks across outbound and return loads and taking responsibility for service as the carrier. That gives it some control over execution. Durable access to trucks on specific lanes and shipper preference for that service remain unproven. TrueMile reports $57K in freight bookings over three weeks. The memo records solicitation for two dedicated-lane partnerships, with paid completion, repeat orders, and demand from previously underserved shippers still open. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Dhaliwal wrote the solver and put it to work in TrueMile’s fleet. His reported experience running his family fleet adds relevant operating experience. The next execution gap is handling disruptions across more linked loads. The expansion unit is a recurring outbound lane with viable return freight. TrueMile’s reported 10% increase in monthly miles per truck suggests higher utilization. Contribution per completed load still depends on freight charges after operator pay, empty miles, dispatch and exception labor, software, and carrier overhead. The memo gives no lane-level contribution or evidence that the economics repeat across lanes. (Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Risks, The Idea)

### Countercase

TrueMile may be making existing trucking work cheaper without making any new service viable. Other providers already offer freight matching, route planning, or dispatch, and competitors with comparable models could reproduce much of the workflow. Thin backhaul availability and cancellations could also erase the savings by adding empty miles and manual rework. The memo leaves both New Economies gates open. It shows neither a newly viable use nor buyer preference under model parity, and it contains no independently supported scarce complement. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can TrueMile win repeat paid freight on dedicated lanes where shippers previously lacked economically viable recurring service, even when carriers with comparable automation compete for the work? A representative cohort with documented prior service economics, lower fully loaded cost per completed load, positive lane contribution, and repeat orders won on service would move the action from Watch to Reach out. If the cohort shows that automation serves only already-viable lanes or buyers readily switch to equivalent carriers, move to Pass.
