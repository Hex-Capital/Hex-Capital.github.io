# Raven -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Raven’s strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a handset maker seeking to bring cross-app agent workflows to its existing devices. The prototype demonstrated flight search and calendar changes, and its People’s Choice award came with $50,000 in API credits. Raven reports that test handsets reached early adopters and that more than 1,000 people contacted the company after the prototype debut. The refundable $199 preorder and projected $99–$199 monthly membership leave paid retention and unit economics unmeasured. A commercial pilot could establish whether Raven reliably saves users time on recurring tasks and whether an OEM could capture enough value to justify buying the software. Raven has yet to publish final hardware specifications or a ship date, and the capital needed for that pilot is unknown. (*The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks*)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. Reaching 5× gross cash within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity. The sale scenarios below are illustrative. Both assume closing within eight years, cash at closing, an eligible holder selling the entire stake, and pro rata distribution after the stated debt and deal adjustments, with no additional senior claims.

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Cash to holder |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in debt and deal adjustments leaves $35M | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or distribution | $0 |

The strong case clears the objective by $200,000. Selling half the stake at that price would yield $1.35 million. The memo supplies no buyer economics or comparable sale prices to support either valuation, no evidence on rights or consents for an acquisition, and no independent liquidity route. (*Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Facts*)

### Countercase

An OEM could add the workflows users value most to its existing phones. The Galaxy S26 already delegates some multi-step tasks through Gemini, and Phone Harness gives agents control of existing or cloud-hosted phones. Raven’s potential switching costs from saved workflows remain an inference, with usage and retention undisclosed. These substitutes weaken the reason to buy Raven. The memo establishes neither equivalent performance across Raven’s recurring workflows nor a supported ceiling on sale proceeds, so the Pass gate remains unmet. (*Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Would an OEM’s buy-versus-build analysis support a price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? An OEM pilot that measures repeat usage and time saved against its current phone assistant, paired with the OEM’s estimate of the value it could capture, would answer it. Evidence supporting that price would move the action to Reach out. A pilot showing a workflow advantage without a supported buyer price would keep it at Watch.
