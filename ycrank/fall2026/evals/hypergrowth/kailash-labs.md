# Kailash Labs -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Conditional on strong product-market fit, Kailash’s best growth path is to deploy the same robot station for similar assembly, inspection, packing, and kitting jobs across factories. The arm, gripper, vision, and safety configuration is a reusable starting point for manufacturers with existing conveyors. Kailash says the station learns parts on the line and can be installed in days. Those claims make transfer to comparable factories abroad plausible, with the work required to adapt the station to each line still uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility)

The public demo and open pilot bookings establish an offer that buyers can evaluate. The seven-day keep-or-return pilot gives Kailash a repeatable sales step, with a CEO discovery call, an on-site line walk, and station-specific commissioning still required. The memo lists a two-person team and shows the robot offer being marketed by November after video-model research earlier in the year. Paid robot deployments, realized revenue, and confirmed pre-revenue status are all unknown, so initial traction receives the absent-evidence default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

A station embedded in a production line could earn repeat use because it performs a recurring job. Under an assumed managed-service model, a customer could expand by adding stations. The advertised no-capex pilot leaves recurring billing and the monetization of expansion uncertain. (The Idea, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Even with strong fit at an initial factory, each added station requires hardware and site work. If Kailash charges a recurring station fee, incremental contribution would be that fee less equipment financing or depreciation, sales and site assessment, freight, commissioning, maintenance, and engineering support. Across a cohort of pilots, paid stations would also need to cover installation and retrieval costs for returned units, plus idle time before redeployment. Reusing station designs or setup data could lower those costs, but the memo shows no cross-site results or realized service rate. The structural downside gate stays open because the documented dependencies do not establish that comparable foreign sites require market-by-market rebuilding or that repeat deployments are economically infeasible. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can one station design reach sustained paid production across independent factories in two markets with limited site adaptation and positive incremental contribution? A dated rollout record showing paid conversions and repeat billing, commissioning hours, and contribution after hardware, support, and returned-pilot costs would answer it. A fast, repeatable rollout would support Reach out if the updated evaluation reaches at least 65 points with Medium or High confidence. Evidence that unavoidable site work or capacity costs make faster rollout uneconomic would trigger Pass.
