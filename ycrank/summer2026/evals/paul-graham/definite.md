# Definite -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Mazin Al-Ani, Farhan Ur Rehman, and Gurshabd Singh Varaich met in their first year at Waterloo, but Definite’s dossier names no pre-funding stunt, customer, or independently shipped artifact. That pairing is Definite’s signature: a credible, longstanding technical trio attached to founders who have not yet left animal tracks. The configuration resembles the people who might build a good startup; the evidence does not yet show the relentlessly resourceful behavior that makes me write a check.

**Stripe** is the high rung for co-founder configuration: two brothers with years of shared history and complementary ability attacking software they could build themselves. Definite shares the important mechanism because its three founders met in first year at Waterloo and bring Optiver, Meta, and BitGo experience across financial systems, machine learning, and transaction controls. Definite diverges because the dossier describes credentials rather than something the three built together before YC. I give Definite most of the configuration points, but friendship plus internships is not yet the Collisons hacking commerce together.

**Kiko** exposed the danger of treating a salvage outcome as evidence of resourcefulness; the eBay sale mattered less than what Kan and Shear did afterward with Twitch. Definite has an even earlier version of the problem: Definite has no named demo launch, manual reconciliation operation, revenue improvisation, customer rejection overcome, or public artifact attributable to a founder. The website and documentation show that a product has been assembled, but not how Mazin, Farhan, or Gurshabd forced it into existence. No evidence means applying the absent-evidence default. Animals leave tracks.

Definite’s immutable snapshots, per-value lineage, deterministic verdicts, and hash-chained receipts put Definite in a real schlep zone. Over 18 months, mappings across NetSuite, Workday, ADP, Stripe, Bill.com, and Ramp could compound: each ugly exception teaches the canonical model something the next integration can reuse. The danger is that read-only access also lets Definite avoid the worst part of the schlep—posting entries and moving money—while Numeric, Rillet, and FloQast already own adjacent workflows. This is promising schlep blindness, but not yet Coinbase-scale appetite for the whole unpleasant problem.

Mazin’s Optiver and Boosted.ai work, Farhan’s Meta recommendation systems, and Gurshabd’s BitGo approval-controls experience establish domain proximity, not an organic idea. Definite would move up an entire tier on this dimension if one founder could say, precisely, “I reconciled these systems every week, built Definite for myself, and coworkers kept using it.” Definite’s shift from bank regulatory reporting in its YC launch to a broad finance-agent layer makes the opposite interpretation plausible: the founders may be searching for a market around their technical machinery. Relevant employment is not the same thing as Drew Houston repeatedly forgetting his USB stick.

**Dropbox** had the hacker-genealogy signal in its pre-seed demo: Houston put a working artifact in front of Hacker News before asking investors to believe him. Definite’s technical documentation suggests real systems work, and BitGo wallet policies plus Optiver trading infrastructure are better signals than CS degrees alone. But Definite has no public repositories, competitive-programming result, open-source contribution, Hacker News trail, or founder-linked demo. The unstated hacker pattern is therefore weak, and the red flag—technical credentials standing in for something visibly shipped—remains active.

Numeric’s $89 million and Rillet’s more than $100 million make the horizontal finance-agent layer a crowded place to discover distribution after building the machinery. By July 2029, I expect Definite to have pivoted back toward regulatory reporting for banks and credit unions and to remain below $1 million ARR. Definite’s strong trio should be capable of producing a technically respectable system, but the absence of organic founder pain and pre-funding hustle makes a narrow, sales-led retreat more likely than becoming the live model of every finance department’s books.

Deloitte’s finding that trust is the main barrier to finance-agent adoption is the strongest case that Definite has chosen exactly the right architecture: read-only connections, deterministic checks, human review, lineage, and hash-chained evidence. Definite could be early in the way Loopt was early, and the Waterloo trio’s long relationship could let Definite survive until finance teams are ready. But Loopt’s timing exception was overridden by Altman’s animal quality; the dossier supplies no equivalent override here, while the bank-reporting-to-horizontal-agents transition and absence of even one named customer reinforce the bear case. I would flip to Invest if Definite reached $1 million ARR from at least five named financial institutions, with three renewing after twelve months.
