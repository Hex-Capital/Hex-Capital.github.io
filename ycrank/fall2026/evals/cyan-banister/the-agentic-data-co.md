# The Agentic Data Co. -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I want to be able to interrupt a machine without negotiating whose turn it is. Getting there requires models that learn conversational timing, then preserve that timing outside clean recordings. The Agentic Data Co. is collecting something relevant: conversations between acquaintances, recorded on separate tracks, including hesitation and interruption. NVIDIA’s PersonaPlex shows simultaneous listening and speaking already exists. That makes the future plausible; whether this particular collection method supplies the missing ingredient remains partly unproven.

The strongest signal is people actually spending time talking through the product. The released corpus contains **1,000.245 hours across 1,602 conversations from 239 speakers**, according to the [October 2026 dataset documentation](https://theagenticdatacompany.com/open-yap-1k). Recruiting a friend and recording a conversation costs more effort—and exposes more of yourself—than clicking a waitlist. This is a functioning collection system with substantial completed participation. The 3,022 monthly Hugging Face sample downloads show developer attention, but they do not establish full-corpus adoption or paying demand. Private licensing supplies a business model; unnamed customers and undisclosed contract values leave its economics unresolved.

What attracts me is the company’s position underneath voice applications. Its collection platform and corpus are assets it operates, and its [October turn-taking research](https://theagenticdatacompany.com/research/turn-taking) reports first place for end-of-turn prediction among 24 entries and second for interruption prediction among 23. That fits my pattern of looking for concealed infrastructure beneath an emerging behavior. The objection bites precisely here: David AI, Defined.ai, and Appen can also collect audio. A useful dataset can become a replaceable procurement item. These company-reported rankings do not yet establish performance in noisy environments or meetings, and the dossier demonstrates neither exclusive supply nor a durable collection advantage. I see an enabling layer, but cannot yet call it a scarce one.

Christian has built a company before; Matias brings growth and operations experience from Female Invest. Those are useful ingredients for recruiting contributors and running collection. They are less informative about an independently developed obsession with speech: the dossier supplies no dated sequence of pre-company experiments establishing that history. I would not infer opportunism from that silence. My willingness to back a pre-revenue technical company still applies, but the exception requires affirmative substitutes for commercial proof. Here there is a working artifact; the accompanying lifelong domain record and named industrial commitments are absent.

I also want to know whose control improves. The Agentic Data Co. addresses a concrete shortage of natural conversational audio, but the dossier identifies no quantified toll, artificial restriction, or gatekeeper whose removal returns agency to a speaker or developer. That criterion receives my missing-evidence default. Privacy deserves separate attention because the product’s usefulness comes from natural conversation. Explicit consent, pseudonymous identifiers, and exclusion of flagged recordings are meaningful safeguards. Automated screening can still miss identifying information; that is an unresolved custody question, not evidence that the company has crossed my severe-privacy red line. Likewise, two co-founders do not establish a 50/50 governance problem, and using Deepgram for transcription does not establish dependence on one closed model supplier for the entire business.

I would keep my own money on the sidelines today, with real interest in the next result. The collection behavior is stronger than the evidence that this becomes indispensable infrastructure. My verifiable flip condition is **a named commercial customer renewing a private-data license because a controlled production evaluation attributes a material conversational-timing improvement to this company’s data**. That would connect the interesting artifact to a dependency buyers choose to preserve.
