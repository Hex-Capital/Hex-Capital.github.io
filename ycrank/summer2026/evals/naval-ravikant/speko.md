# Speko -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Beknazar Abdikamalov has real specific knowledge. He has worked in voice technology since 2019 and spent four years manually comparing voice providers at prior products before building Speko. The problem was learned through repeated action, not inferred from a market report. A working API, 17 public repositories, initial voice-agent partners, and one commercial pilot show that he converted that knowledge into a product.

The asymmetry breaks at durability. Speko routes among other companies’ STT, LLM, and TTS models using public benchmarks, while refusing to train on customer audio or transcripts. If a competitor copies the routing code, the dossier identifies no proprietary model, scarce dataset, hardware, exclusive rights, or customer-created network that remains. LiveKit already routes inference, OpenRouter supports speech and transcription, and Vapi permits provider switching. Implementation proves energy; it does not yet prove leverage.

The bull case is real. Speko tracks 23 STT models, with different winners across nine languages, in a voice-agent market projected to grow from $2.5 billion in 2025 to $35.2 billion by 2033. Frequent model releases make neutral selection valuable, and the founder experienced the pain personally. Pricing is direct—provider cost plus 5%, or $0.09 per managed minute—and the commercial pilot may become evidence that customers prefer an independent routing layer over bundled platforms.

But independence without accumulated advantage is a feature, not a company. Public benchmarks help acquire developers, yet each new account does not make Speko measurably better for the next account. The principal platforms possess more traffic, infrastructure, capital, and adjacent workflow control; they can subsidize routing to sell telephony, runtime, or enterprise automation. Speko currently bears the dependency while its providers capture the underlying model improvement.

The unstated pattern is therefore absent: users are not contributing supply, distribution, or proprietary knowledge to one another. No known exception overrides that absence. The founder’s domain depth raises the option value, but there is no documented obsessive usage, capital-efficient breakout adoption, or self-reinforcing behavior. I see no integrity or founder red flag; the failure mode is structural commoditization, not character.

I would not invest today. My one flip condition is verifiable: ten paying production customers whose workload outcomes create a private routing dataset that delivers at least a 20% accuracy, latency, or cost advantage over static provider selection and improves as aggregate routed volume grows. Until then, Speko is an experienced founder standing on rented models with public information.
