# Cesara -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Cesara’s strongest exit path is a strategic sale to a revenue-cycle platform. The public demo shows a workflow that prices claims at submission and prepares appeals tied to payer contract clauses. Adonis and Trek Health already address reimbursement variances. A platform could have a reason to buy Cesara if adding that workflow produces incremental recoveries for customers faster or more cheaply than building it internally. The memo provides no buyer interest or evidence of what a buyer would pay. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

The CEO reports that pilots identified over 90% of valid underpayments and recovered over 70% of those identified. Customer counts, dollar recoveries, and realized fees are undisclosed. The next milestone is converting service-line pilots into paid production at multiple hospitals, with customer-verified incremental recoveries after integration and operating costs. Getting there requires capital for integrations, security review, and contract validation. The conflicting funding figures leave runway uncertain. A software sale appears feasible in principle, with customer-contract assignment and continued access to claims data subject to diligence. The memo provides no evidence of an independent liquidity route. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. Both sale cases below assume a close within eight years, a fully eligible holder selling the entire stake, cash at closing, and pro rata distribution after the stated deductions, with no further preference or escrow deductions. The prices and deductions are scenario assumptions. The memo gives no basis for underwriting either sale price. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Enterprise-to-equity bridge | Holder cash |
|---|---|---|
| Modest strategic sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M in debt and deal adjustments leaves $45M | $1.35M, or 2.7× |
| Strong strategic sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M | $2.70M, or 5.4×. At 2.5% ownership, $2.25M, or 4.5× |
| Prolonged private operation | Cash is reinvested, with no payout or eligible share sale within eight years | $0 realized |

### Countercase

Adonis and Trek Health already ingest overlapping data and flag reimbursement variances. If they can add clause-backed appeals to their existing workflows, Cesara’s differentiation may be too narrow to justify an acquisition at a price that clears the hurdle. The pilot percentages leave open whether Cesara recovers materially more cash than those tools once integration and review costs are included. The memo establishes neither a structural barrier to closing nor a supported price range that falls materially short. The unresolved buyer economics support Watch. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Do paid deployments show enough incremental recovery over an incumbent workflow to support an acquisition that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity value? Customer-verified results across multiple hospitals, paired with a prospective buyer’s quantified build-versus-buy analysis, would move the action to Reach out if they support that threshold. If they show that the strongest credible sale would leave the holder materially short, move to Pass.
