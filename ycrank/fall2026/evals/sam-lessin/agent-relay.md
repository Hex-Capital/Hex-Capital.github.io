# Agent Relay -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Agent Relay has built something useful, but I cannot price the investment: proposed post-money, competing term sheets, and whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital are all undisclosed. The displayed backers establish financing relationships; they do not establish an auction or a bargain. The dossier also names no experiment the round will finance, with a deadline and a result that changes enterprise value. Those are missing underwriting inputs, not accusations against a two-person pre-seed team. Both receive my stated defaults.

The strongest reason to keep looking is the founders. Will Washburn reports 14 years helping build Tailwind to eight-figure ARR and more than 50,000 paying customers. Those receipts belong to his operating history, not Agent Relay’s revenue column. Khaliq Gant brings integration experience from Nango, and the founders report running more than 50 parallel agents themselves. This is a credible bridge between building commercial software and operating the new tools used to build it. Their long relationship and explicit CEO/CTO roles also give me no affirmative basis for the equal-founder committee red flag, although final decision rights remain unverified.

The product’s most interesting evidence is one person using it with teammates for months. Agents addressing one another across machines and accessing session transcripts can remove a recurring human coordination bottleneck. That is an actual workflow action, worth more to me than approximately 1,000 GitHub stars or 229,600 downloads with an unspecified measurement period. But one testimonial, available through a mirror, establishes limited repeat usage—not a proprietary distribution channel. The searchable history might eventually own the customer relationship. Today that switching-cost argument is explicitly inferred, while the Apache-2.0 core gives other vendors substantial implementation access. Conductor and agent providers have reasons to absorb this coordination function.

Delete the next financing from Agent Relay’s plan and the dossier cannot yet tell me what survives economically. Hosted access starts free; paid rates, revenue, margins, burn, and runway are undisclosed. That earns the economics default, without assuming the business is uneconomic. The specific concern is that enterprise monetization may require operational work beyond messaging: SSO and customer-visible audit logs remain pending, while cloud deletion requires manual handling. Coordination could become valuable enough to support that work, but downloads cannot tell me whether customers will pay its full cost.

The bull case stings: experienced builders have shipped a bridge that an external team keeps using, and ownership of cross-provider context could become more durable than any individual agent. That fits my translation-layer pattern. It does not yet meet the exception standard of documented paid conversion, retention, or cash economics that would override missing financing gates. Nor does the dossier prove the generic-AI red flag applies—the product connects agents rather than claiming a superior model—but provider bundling threatens its chargeable boundary. I pass at **36/100**. My one verifiable condition for reopening underwriting is **five independent teams renewing paid deployments after 90 days at positive contribution margin, including support costs**. That would establish a functioning base business beneath the coordination option.
