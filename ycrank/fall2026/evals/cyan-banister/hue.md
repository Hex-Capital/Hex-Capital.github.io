# Hue -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine an agent rehearsing a messy workday before anyone gives it permission to touch the real business. An API fails halfway through. A record changes. The agent must recover without making the mess larger. Hue’s production-context reconstruction and resettable environments point toward that world. But the destination is my extrapolation: the dossier does not establish the intervening unlocks or demonstrate that these simulated worlds reliably predict production behavior. I will not award Hue points for a future I supplied.

The interesting part is the plumbing. Recorded answers cannot fully describe a task that changes its surroundings; Hue proposes preserving those changes inside simulated APIs and MCPs. That puts it beneath agent applications, where an unglamorous dependency could become valuable. It fits my pattern of looking for concealed infrastructure under a fashionable market. The public SDK is a concrete technical artifact, but control over instrumentation is not yet control over a scarce bottleneck. Uninstrumented provider calls remain invisible, and the dossier supplies no validated fidelity advantage. Patronus already targets simulation and training, so “agents need worlds” does not establish why those worlds belong to Hue.

The strongest argument against passing is Aneesh Kethini’s work building and launching Datadog’s AI SRE. He has shipped into the problem’s neighborhood; Josh Le brings engineering experience from Apple and Gusto. Together, they could recognize operational details that a generic evaluation vendor misses. The Python package progressed from its September 15 release to version 0.14.0 on October 25, 2026. There is something here to inspect. But release activity belongs to product evidence, not proof of lifelong founder obsession or customer devotion. The dossier documents neither pre-company experiments nor a team repeatedly spending money, labor, or reputation on Hue. Zero GitHub stars tells me little at this stage. Repeated use would tell me much more.

Why does the present system waste anyone’s time, and who benefits from keeping it that way? Hue identifies a testing limitation, but the dossier names no specific toll, imposed restriction, or quantified delay whose removal gives a worker control. That leaves my broken-system criterion at its missing-evidence default. My pre-revenue exception also deserves a real hearing: revenue need not precede conviction when founder obsession, a functioning prototype, and committed counterparties independently support the bet. Hue has relevant founder experience and a released SDK, but the other supporting evidence is absent. Missing revenue is not the reason I pass.

Production capture also makes data custody part of the product. Custom redaction does not inspect arbitrary OpenTelemetry attributes or logs; broader filtering requires customer configuration. That is a documented boundary, not evidence of a severe privacy violation, but I cannot assume consent and custody are solved. The named CEO and CTO provide no affirmative governance red flag, and the dossier establishes no dependence on a single closed model supplier. I pass with my own money because Hue has a credible infrastructure direction without enough evidence for my central future-and-founder bet. My verifiable flip condition is one named customer’s paid, repeated use showing that Hue prospectively catches a state-dependent production regression its existing evaluator misses.
