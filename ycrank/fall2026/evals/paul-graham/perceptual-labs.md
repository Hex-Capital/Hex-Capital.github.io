# Perceptual Labs -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The important missing fact about Perceptual Labs is what its founders have done when blocked. The dossier lists a two-person team, but neither founder is identified. There is no obstacle followed by a workaround, and no observation followed by a changed product. I therefore use the missing-evidence defaults for resourcefulness and adaptability. These founders may be exceptionally good. I simply have no instance of their behavior on which to bet my money.

“Building the next generation of visual intelligence” doesn't tell me what they have built. There is no inspectable software, measured experiment, or demonstrated technical threshold. Perceptual Labs could fit my pattern of backing useful programmable primitives, but an infrastructure classification doesn't establish that developers can compose anything with it. Nor does the hypothetical proprietary dataset establish a difficult substrate. Roboflow and TwelveLabs show that adjacent capabilities exist; their funding tells me nothing about whether this team has discovered something those companies overlook.

The next question is who needs the crude version now. The dossier identifies no user niche, repeated usage, payment, or customer workflow. That leaves demand untested. It also gives no founder history connecting the team to a particular visual problem or an ugly integration burden. An undisclosed business model prevents assessing the economics, but it doesn't establish that the economics are bad. Each of these omissions stays at its specified default; I won't turn missing research into evidence of customer rejection.

The strongest argument against passing is the research failure itself. Both the website and YC profile were inaccessible during the October 29, 2026 checks. Almost everything I most want to know could be behind those failed requests. A two-person team could already have a working product and a few devoted users without leaving much public evidence. But the known exceptions require something affirmative: a convincing prototype, unmistakable user pull, or validated technical proof. None is documented here. Equally, there is no evidence of the red flags that would justify a harsher judgment—waiting for a partner, delegating product work prematurely, or refusing contrary customer evidence.

I would pass on writing a check from this dossier. Perceptual Labs hasn't demonstrated the founder-action gate, and there is no documented exception that replaces it. One verifiable event would change my decision to investigate: a named customer confirms that a founder resolved a specific deployment blocker within two weeks and that the resulting working product has been used weekly for a month. That would connect founder behavior, technical execution, and actual need. At present, I have a description of an ambition where that causal chain should be.
