# COACH -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Mathieu and Thomas Perez built a €3.5 million-revenue business before personally joining door-to-door sales calls, while Nicolas Sebag spent ten years building software. COACH is a specific-knowledge business trapped inside a weak network architecture: exceptional founder-problem fit is the high, customer-isolated SaaS value is the low. Replit is the high rung because Amjad Masad’s decade-long obsession became the product and its distribution. COACH shares the lived-problem mechanism, but COACH diverges because no public repository, technical community, or founder-built artifact predates the sales motion. Specific knowledge is present; permissionless proof is not.

Perplexity’s single-sided architecture failed my network test and survived only because founder-specific knowledge, product distribution, and pre-consensus AI timing were overwhelming. COACH also makes one customer better without automatically making the next customer more valuable: each recorded meeting enriches a customer-specific knowledge base, not a shared network. COACH therefore sits near Perplexity’s low rung on this dimension, but COACH lacks Perplexity’s research-native founder edge and category timing. Customer data creates switching costs. It does not yet create compounding user-added value.

$500,000 ARR after five months proves COACH can sell code, but the forward-deployed pilot model reveals where the leverage resides. Over eighteen months, COACH’s software should reduce marginal analysis cost while its customer-specific closer models improve retention; yet enterprise pilots, consent workflows, and implementation labor will scale alongside revenue. The founders’ combined 17,000 LinkedIn followers provide media leverage, but COACH has disclosed no open-source adoption, viral loop, or self-serve product pull. Code is producing the service. Labor is distributing it.

A $2.96 billion conversation-intelligence market growing 20.8% annually gives COACH room, but not the nearly unbounded pivot surface of developer tools or consumer media. COACH could move from field-sales coaching into compliance, onboarding, hiring assessment, or broader frontline-work intelligence. The score flips into the top tier only if COACH’s recording and behavioral layer generalizes beyond sales without rebuilding distribution for every vertical. Until then, Rilla’s estimated $70 million ARR shows that the category is real while also showing that COACH has entered an already-legible lane.

Mathieu and Thomas Perez previously built Alsace Test together, endured six pivots, and recruited Nicolas Sebag before reaching COACH’s reported $500,000 ARR. Notion is the useful single-anchor parallel: repeated rebuilding matters because energy is visible through survival, not slogans. COACH shows intelligence through speed and energy through pivots, while the brothers’ prior operating history reduces co-founder risk. COACH has no disclosed evidence about treatment of prior employees, customer data practices, or completed partnerships, so integrity reads clean but remains unproven. Play long-term games with long-term people; the dossier supports the first half of that test more strongly than the second.

$500,000 ARR and 120% monthly growth make my falsifiable forecast straightforward: by July 2029, COACH will reach $12 million–$20 million ARR as an independent field-sales coaching vendor, but COACH will remain a conventional enterprise SaaS product rather than becoming a compounding network. Founder-specific knowledge should keep COACH ahead of generic model wrappers, while the absence of cross-customer network effects should cap the upper-bound geometry. A valuable business can still be the wrong angel architecture.

Rilla’s $70 million estimated ARR is the strongest case against even that forecast: Rilla already occupies the exact recording-and-coaching category, Gong can bundle adjacent functionality, and COACH must procure sensitive recordings without disclosed consent or security infrastructure. The bear mechanism is brutal—incumbent distribution plus compliance friction turns COACH’s forward-deployed pilots into expensive labor leverage. COACH’s reported 100% pilot conversion, 0% churn, and 32% conversion lift rebut immediate product irrelevance, although the undisclosed cohorts prevent those claims from carrying the check. I would change to Invest if COACH demonstrates that at least 30% of new customers arrive through product-led referrals and that anonymized cross-customer learning measurably improves outcomes as usage grows.
