# Radley -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The revealing fact is not that Radley has chosen radiology. It is that the founders sold $2.2 million of contracts for radiology software, put that product live, and then abandoned the easier software-company shape for the uglier job of operating a practice. That is evidence of a running back changing routes. But the dossier omits the crucial middle of the story: what failed in the software model, what the founders learned, and how quickly they converted that observation into the acquisition strategy. I see flexibility and a shipped artifact. I do not yet see the named blocker and founder-built workaround required to call them relentlessly resourceful.

The earlier deployment is nevertheless real technical proof. This is not a pair of executives proposing to hire engineers after financing. Seak Meng Lay built software while Carbon Health expanded from 31 to more than 100 clinics, and Maryam Jahed led healthcare products through clinical growth, an insurer acquisition, and an FDA breakthrough designation. They also worked together during Carbon’s expansion. That is unusually relevant shared execution for a two-person pre-seed team. The missing proof concerns Radley’s present claim: no disclosed agent has yet improved report turnaround, radiologist capacity, billing collections, or clinical quality inside an owned practice.

The narrow user well is partially visible. Healthcare buyers signed $2.2 million in contracts for the prior product and it went live, which is stronger than a waitlist or an LOI. But Radley does not disclose whether those customers used the system repeatedly, renewed, or caused the pivot. The prospective $4.3 million of annualized practice revenue is even easier to misread. It belongs to the practice until the acquisition closes; it is not yet revenue created or retained by Radley. The company has found buyers, but it has not shown that radiologists or facilities pull the new full-stack product back when it is crude.

The founders did encounter the general healthcare schlep before starting this company. They have operated through clinic expansion, EHR infrastructure, clinical workflows, insurance, and FDA processes. Acquiring a practice adds precisely the burdens software founders normally avoid: credentialing, multistate licensure, payer contracts, clinical governance, and integration. That can be a good wedge. It can also turn a startup into a small leveraged services roll-up. Radiology Partners already combines practices with proprietary technology across more than 25 million annual examinations, while Rad AI and Sirona possess large installed software footprints. Radley must show that ownership produces a compounding software advantage, not merely access to labor and contracts.

The strongest case for investing is that Radley matches an important unstated pattern: experienced technical founders produced paid, deployed evidence before raising around a more ambitious route. Its founders are not relying on press, a ceremonial launch, a newly assembled cofounder relationship, or hired salespeople, so my usual red flags are mostly absent. The objection still stings: the concrete proof belongs to the route they left, while the acquisition, agent performance, proprietary data, and integration playbook are all prospective. The known exceptions do not rescue this gap; exceptions require a working product or unmistakable pull strong enough to substitute for the failed gate, and Radley has not yet demonstrated either for the operating-practice model.

I would not write the check today. This is a promising team with a credible reason to enter an unpleasant market, but the decisive founder-action sequence is missing and the present product remains a transaction thesis. One verifiable condition would flip me: close the practice and publish six months of same-facility results showing retained revenue plus a material agent-driven improvement in radiologist throughput or turnaround time without worse clinical quality. That would connect the acquisition, the software, and the founders’ behavior into one logical proof.
