# Dydema -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A, product-led adoption**, is the strongest candidate. A plausible first buyer is a desktop agent user who frequently confirms prompts while their hands are occupied. The memo documents a research-cap demo that used three imagined movements to type, play chess, and answer email. Dydema reports that its pretrained decoder works on new users after brief calibration. The demo establishes that a small command set can trigger useful actions. Buyer urgency, willingness to pay, and recurring use remain untested. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

**Scenario assumption.** If the planned consumer headset preserves that performance and users can calibrate it unaided, agent integrations could bring in buyers, online ordering could convert them, and buyers could map more actions on the same device. The launch post drew 329.7K views, but the memo gives no conversion data. Wizard’s published research API prices give no evidence of consumer headset revenue or paid expansion. A second market could plausibly use the same command-mapping software, with local checkout, shipping, returns, and neural-data handling still to verify. Each additional buyer would require a manufactured and shipped headset and could add returns, setup support, and decoding costs. The memo provides no per-buyer cost or evidence that those costs fall with volume. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

The three-command demo may draw curiosity while offering too little recurring value to justify buying, wearing, and calibrating a headset. It used a research cap, and Dydema is still working on cheaper sensors, more commands, and faster decoding. If cheaper sensors make commands unreliable, the same weakness could depress repeat use and raise support and return costs. The memo establishes neither a structural ceiling on demand nor proportional delivery effort, so the Pass gate is unmet. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of independent desktop agent users buy the headset through the planned online channel, calibrate it on the intended consumer sensors without founder help, and keep using it for the same hands-free action in their regular workflow? Paid orders and per-user activation and repeat-use records would move the action to **Reach out** if they show that pattern across the cohort. A demo, waitlist, or founder-assisted trial would keep it at **Watch**. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)
