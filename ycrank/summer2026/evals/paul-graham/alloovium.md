# Alloovium -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The impressive fact is that Alloovium is deployed across $400 million of active construction projects. The missing fact is what broke while the founders were getting it there. The dossier describes a contractor spending $2.5 million and assigning 40–50 people to a management-plan submission, but that is the customer’s obstacle, not evidence of how Zander and Cielo respond to resistance. I cannot find a dated sequence in which a contractor refused, an integration failed, or an output proved unreliable and the founders shipped a workaround. The current US plan—seeking introductions to large contractors and expecting each to expose the product to 50 subcontractors—makes this worse. It describes leverage through partners before showing that the founders can recruit the first users individually.

The product itself is much more convincing. Alloovium has working integrations with Procore, Autodesk, SharePoint, and other construction systems; sentence-level citations; organization-specific correction memory; review-gated routines; and SOC 2 Type I certification. These are artifacts, not diagrams. Source-linked answers and mandatory human sign-off also address the most obvious failure mode of using AI for contractual paperwork. Deployment on live projects puts the technical proof in the high band, though not at the top: there is no accuracy benchmark, independently measured improvement, or disclosed threshold showing how often the system replaces work safely.

There is a real narrow well here. General contractors have painful document and compliance workflows, a BMD subsidiary is using the product, and an ISO-compliance beta is being developed with a 30-year auditor. But “$400 million of projects” measures the projects, not Alloovium’s usage. The reported tenfold increase in construction professionals omits the starting number, and subscription terms prove that the company can charge, not that anyone has paid or renewed. I see credible deployment, but not yet users pulling the crude product back through repeated use, expansion, or referrals.

The origin is better than a manufactured “AI for construction” thesis. Zander worked as a site engineer on civil-marine projects, while Cielo had already been a founding engineer at four startups. The work is full of schleps: permissions, document provenance, contractor-specific management systems, liability, and integrations with entrenched software. But the cofounders were recruited together only after Startmate Launch Club, and the dossier contains no before-and-after product route showing what they learned from the field. One product version without a documented setback receives the default for flexibility; it does not prove rigidity, but it does not prove running-back behavior either.

The strongest bull case is that the public numbers understate a difficult enterprise deployment. If contractors are already trusting Alloovium across $400 million of live work, correction memory and embedded routines could create switching costs before better-funded competitors catch up. That would match the pattern of a concrete product assembled before the financing story. It could also qualify for an exception to weak founder-history evidence. But the exception is not earned merely by having working software: Trunk Tools, Document Crunch, and Nomic already have overlapping products, while Alloovium discloses neither revenue nor retention nor a numerical technical result. Project value and press attention are especially dangerous substitutes for weekly product behavior.

My 36-month base case is that Alloovium reaches roughly $2–5 million in ARR by August 2029 as a useful contractor-document workflow product but does not become the independent system of record. I would not write the check today because the people-first gate remains unproven. One verifiable event would flip me: a dated case study showing that a named contractor rejected or curtailed an initial deployment, the founders personally shipped a material workaround within two weeks, and that contractor then renewed or expanded paid usage.
