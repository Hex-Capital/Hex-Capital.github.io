# Aikido Technologies -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Repeatable direct sales** is the strongest path. Aikido targets neoclouds and AI labs that need a recurring supply of compute capacity where onshore grid connections are constrained. Its YC launch and capacity inquiry form give buyers a route to discuss dedicated GPUs or colocation, with price, capacity, and delivery negotiated separately. Aikido reports prospective GPU renters and final platform permits for its A1DC pilot retrofit. The memo identifies no buyer-funded test, paid go-live, or subsequent paid expansion. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks; Key Facts)

The buyer’s need plausibly travels across markets. **Scenario assumption:** If the same floating design can meet local permitting and buyer uptime requirements with bounded changes, Aikido could reuse the platform and deployment process for similar buyers. The first link likely to break is converting an inquiry into a paid, operating deployment with a delivery process the next buyer can reuse. The planned Norway pilot offers a test of deployment, while the reported sub-40-hour structural assembly of the earlier wind platform measures only one step in a predecessor product. The memo gives no usable estimate of the cost to bring the next data center online and keep it operating. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Each deployment could require substantial site-specific engineering and capital before a buyer can use it. Full-scale certification is still planned, and variable wind and vessel-dependent servicing create continuity risks. Backup power and offshore maintenance could absorb the savings from reusing the platform design. The structural downside gate remains open because the memo contains no operating deployments that establish whether these costs rise in proportion to capacity or can improve with repetition. (The Idea; Market & Traction; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can an independent neocloud or AI lab go from a capacity inquiry to a paid, live deployment using a process Aikido can repeat? A signed agreement, payment, and an operating record showing that the deployment met the buyer’s agreed uptime target, with documented site-specific work and delivery costs, would move the action to **Reach out** if the record shows a credible route to repeating the rollout.
