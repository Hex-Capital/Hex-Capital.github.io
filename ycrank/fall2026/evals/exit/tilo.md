# Tilo -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Tilo’s strongest private exit path. An agent sandbox provider could pay to own Tilo’s integrated workspace if it materially reduces the cost of running multi-app workflows and ownership gets the provider to production faster than building, partnering, or adopting the public code. The memo documents a public implementation of shared app state, typed actions, attributed undo, and workspace permissions, and reports that Tilo lists about 50 native apps on that model. Tilo claims about 1/29 of the cost and 90× the speed on one same-model task, but the comparison run failed. The claim gives a provider a reason to test the product. Buyer savings and willingness to pay remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The operating milestone is a paid deployment that repeatedly completes a customer’s multi-app workflow and measures cost per completed run against a working alternative. The existing desktop and SDK make a pilot plausible. Tilo’s early-alpha backup guidance and the potential need for third-party integrations leave the production work uncertain. YC backing and a listed pre-seed round establish a funding signal, while the round size and runway available for the pilot are unknown. The memo leaves IP ownership, license terms, and transfer mechanics unverified and supplies no usable evidence for an independent route to holder cash. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** A single $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-on checks. The 5× objective requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds under a simple pro rata waterfall before investor taxes. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% ownership and $62.5 million at 4%. For both modeled sales, assume zero net debt, other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, fees, and senior preference payouts. Enterprise value therefore equals equity consideration paid at close in cash. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each $0. The sale prices are scenario assumptions. The memo supplies no usable basis to price a transaction. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

| Scenario | Timing and company outcome | Cash to holder at 3% |
|---|---|---:|
| Modest sale | Year 5, $40 million enterprise value | $1.2 million, 2.4× |
| Strong sale | Year 7, $100 million enterprise value | $3 million, 6× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and Tilo reinvests available cash | $0, 0×, shares retained |

The strong sale clears the hurdle at 3% but returns only $2 million, or 4×, at 2%. Watch is warranted because the memo leaves both a hurdle-clearing price and the likelihood of the prolonged-private outcome unresolved.

### Countercase

A sandbox provider may be able to use Tilo’s public code under its applicable license, integrate open MCP interfaces, or partner for the capabilities it needs. Existing desktop agent features and legacy-app APIs give customers another way to keep their current workflows. If these options deliver comparable results in production, the incremental value of owning Tilo could fall well below the acquisition hurdle. The memo supports this substitution risk but leaves production equivalence and the resulting price ceiling unresolved, so the Pass override does not apply. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would an agent sandbox provider’s build-versus-buy analysis, grounded in a paid production deployment, show enough incremental value from owning Tilo to support roughly $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds? A repeatable comparison against the provider’s best available alternative, tied to its deployment costs and expected workflow volume, would answer it. Buyer-validated economics that support the hurdle would move the action to Reach out.
