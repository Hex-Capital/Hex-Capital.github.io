# Hesper AI -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Hesper AI has raised a founder-reported $1.5 million led by Afore, but I cannot price my own check: post-money valuation, competing offers, and remaining capital need are undisclosed. That earns the financing default. YC admission does not establish an auction. More consequentially, the dossier never identifies the finite experiment this money buys. What truth will $1.5 million reveal, by when, that materially changes the business’s value? An investigation product and a financing announcement leave that question open.

The strongest answer is hiding in the founders’ product change. Their April 2026 fraud-scoring software reportedly failed to sell; they rebuilt around completing investigations and subsequently reported an MGA production deployment. That is an economically meaningful change: a score creates another task, while a completed evidence file can remove work. The reported 82% reduction in adjuster time over two weeks and 10,000-plus claims investigated give Hesper actual workflow adoption. For a pre-seed company, this is substantial evidence that someone wants the output. Payment, repeat deployment, and ownership of the access remain unproven.

My concern is where the durable asset accumulates. Customers supply database licences, credentials, and permissible-purpose determinations; contracts prohibit pooling their data for shared model training. Those provisions may help win trust, but they leave important investigative inputs controlled by the buyer. Hesper fits my pattern of software connecting difficult operating systems, yet the proprietary dark pool is still unidentified. Shift already offers overlapping agents and has Guidewire distribution. Customer-specific configuration could become valuable embedded infrastructure; the dossier establishes no retention or switching-cost evidence yet.

The bull case stings because these founders have shipped and sold document automation before. Dhariwal developed Nanonets Flow to $200,000 ARR, and Badu reports helping scale Nanonets revenue to $4 million ARR. Those are previous-employer receipts, allocated to founder capability, rather than Hesper revenue. Their engineering and distribution experience makes the move into completed investigations credible, although direct insurance operating experience is thinner. My exception for AI businesses with demonstrated enterprise value is relevant here: the reported customer savings deserve attention. It does not yet override the missing gates because paid conversion, retention, and Hesper’s delivery economics remain undisclosed. Nothing establishes the unusual acquisition evidence or financing structure that supported other exceptions.

I pass on this check. The reported $15 million in potential fraud identified measures customer findings; it supplies no evidence of Hesper’s cash generation. The first 200 claims free and 90-day platform-fee waiver make usage especially important to separate from purchasing. Human approval for adverse decisions is sensible, and the contract acknowledges errors rather than assuming they vanish. We still need the cost of producing an accepted investigation, including review and support, to understand whether the business survives without another round. My single verifiable flip condition is a documented financing package at a sub-$10-million post-money whose proceeds fund a dated, customer-validated test of positive contribution margin per paid, accepted investigation and a credible route to break-even. That would connect the promising workflow to an investment I can underwrite.
