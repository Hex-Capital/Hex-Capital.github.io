# Sinter -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic sale to a manufacturing platform such as Fictiv or Xometry. The memo documents Sinter’s online quoting workflow and automated manufacturability checks. Sinter says its pilot uses photopolymer tools in existing presses to deliver molded parts in 48 hours at one-tenth the time and cost of traditional methods. If that cost advantage holds on repeat orders for short runs, a platform could acquire the workflow to serve those jobs profitably through its existing distribution. The buyer rationale is an inference. The memo gives no buyer feedback or repeat-order cost data. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is repeat paid molding runs that measure tool life and cost per accepted part against quick-turn aluminum tooling. Public prototype ordering and molding pilot recruitment show a way to run that test. Sinter reports producing more than 500 kg with customers in its first month, with no breakdown of how much came from repeat molding work. Using existing presses could limit test capital if the process works across relevant parts. The memo gives no test budget or funding commitment. It also leaves ownership and transfer rights for the workflow unverified and gives no evidence of a separate route to holder liquidity. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% starting ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return requires $2.5 million within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. Both sale scenarios below assume an all-cash close by year eight, $5 million in combined net debt and deal deductions, no further senior claims, and an eligible holder selling all shares at the pro rata price. The sale prices are illustrative sensitivities. The memo supplies no buyer valuation or holder terms. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Protolabs advertises one-day molding with aluminum tools, and mold shops can trial commercially available resin inserts. If Sinter’s tools need frequent replacement, a buyer may already have a faster option or be able to reproduce the process cheaply. That could erase the economic reason to buy. The memo documents the speed substitute and the general risk of tool wear. Sinter’s tool life and cost per accepted part remain open, so the evidence establishes neither a structural block nor a sale-value ceiling below the proceeds hurdle. The Pass gate remains untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can repeat paid molding runs demonstrate enough savings per accepted part for a strategic buyer to justify a price that leaves at least $83.3 million in distributable equity? Matched runs against quick-turn aluminum tooling, with tool replacement and scrap included, would establish the savings a buyer could apply to its own job volume. Move to Reach out if those economics support a credible price range above the holder hurdle. Move to Pass if they show that even the strongest credible buyer case leaves the holder materially short.
