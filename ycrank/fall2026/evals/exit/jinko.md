# Jinko -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic sale to a travel distributor is Jinko’s best-supported route. A distributor could buy Jinko to add budget-led discovery across destinations and bring agent bookings across flights, hotels, and cars onto its platform. Hopper’s acquisition of the founders’ prior travel company, Mowgli, establishes a relevant precedent for a travel buyer acquiring a product from this team. The value of Jinko’s booking flow to a buyer remains an inference. (The Idea, Founders & Team)

The memo records a public API launch and a live Exa Connect listing. A testimonial published by Jinko reports that one startup reached live bookings in four hours. Those signals make a seed-stage test plausible using existing integrations. The milestone is repeat paid bookings from multiple integrations with positive contribution profit after search and servicing costs. The memo gives no booking volumes, realized revenue, or estimate of the time and capital needed to reach that milestone. Jinko reports operating through its own accreditations, and its supplier agreements restrict fare markups. Whether those arrangements would survive a sale and whether the holder could participate remain open. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Defensibility, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× return requires $2.5 million in cash within eight years, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3%. The sale cases assume an eligible holder sells their entire stake for cash within eight years, with no senior preference claims, escrow, or deferred consideration. The values are illustrative. Jinko’s published pricing and undisclosed realized revenue leave the achievable sale price unsubstantiated. (Key Facts)

| Scenario | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Holder cash |
|-----------|------------------------------------------|-------------|
| Modest sale | $50M less $5M in net debt and deal adjustments = $45M | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M less $10M in net debt and deal adjustments = $90M | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no eligible share sale or payout | $0 |

### Countercase

Etraveli already offers agent-facing flight search, booking, and servicing through its own MCP. That gives a potential strategic buyer a concrete build alternative and puts the burden on Jinko to show that its broader discovery and checkout flow generates profitable bookings the buyer would otherwise miss. The modest scenario misses the return objective, but the memo establishes neither a credible ceiling on buyer price nor a structural closing barrier. The Pass gate remains untriggered, and the strong scenario is too speculative to justify outreach. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would buying Jinko generate enough incremental profit for a travel distributor to justify a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? Repeat paid booking data across integrations, measured against the buyer’s existing workflow and net of search and servicing costs, would let a buyer quantify the case for buying versus building. Move to **Reach out** if that analysis supports a price range that could deliver $2.5 million to the holder after deal adjustments and senior claims. Move to **Pass** if the strongest credible range falls materially short.
