# Agent Relay -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Agent Relay’s founders encountered a stupid dependency: their agents could keep working, but communication between them required a human. They built a relay to remove it. That is a sensible response to a real obstacle. The February 2026 launch supplies evidence of action, though it does not establish how quickly the workaround changed the team’s output. I give Washburn and Gant credit for building through the problem, with less certainty about the resourcefulness test than a dated account of something breaking and being fixed would provide.

The software is the strongest evidence. There is an open-source implementation, a desktop app, and a concrete interface: agents get addresses such as `name@machine` and communicate across existing tools. Those are inspectable artifacts, rather than an architecture waiting for funding. Agent Relay fits my recurring interest in programmable interfaces that technical users can compose into systems. But the distinction matters: shipping the primitive establishes technical ability; it does not establish that developers depend on it. The roughly 1,000 GitHub stars and 95 forks show attention. The reported 229.6K downloads, without a measurement period, cannot tell me how many teams returned.

Preslav Mihaylov’s report of using Agent Relay with teammates’ agents for months is more valuable than those larger numbers. It identifies a narrow user and repeated behavior around the intended job. The evidence comes through a mirrored post, and one team cannot establish widespread pull, but this is enough to take the demand seriously. Free hosted access makes experimentation easy; whether the product saves enough coordination work to support payment remains untested publicly. I would want to know which conversation this team would otherwise have had to interrupt a person to complete.

The founders’ history helps explain why they might find that conversation. Their reported use of 50-plus parallel agents supplies firsthand exposure, although the origin account deserves less weight than a contemporaneous record. Gant’s integration experience makes the awkward work between tools credible territory. Washburn’s prior SaaS experience is useful founder evidence, not Agent Relay revenue. Their relationship since high school also removes the concern that this is a cofounder pairing assembled for an application. What I cannot establish is how they change direction when users contradict them. There is no documented rejected hypothesis or resulting product revision, so that dimension stays at its absent-evidence default.

The strongest case against waiting is that the team has already built a useful primitive and found someone who keeps using it. Those facts resemble the substance behind my working-product exceptions. But they do not yet supply an override: the dossier shows neither independently measured exceptional performance nor unmistakable user pull. Meanwhile, the bearish mechanism is concrete. Providers can add messaging, competitors can adapt the Apache-2.0 core, and customers may prefer coordination inside their existing workspace. Agent Relay’s pending SSO, audit logs, and emergency controls expose the operational work required to become a trusted team dependency. There is no evidence of partner-dependent distribution or founders avoiding that work; there is also no evidence they have completed it.

I would hold off on a personal check. This is a promising tool with stronger proof of construction than proof of founder learning under resistance. My flip condition is one documented deployment where a customer’s specific coordination or security blocker leads to a founder-shipped change within two weeks, followed by that customer paying and using it weekly for eight weeks. That would connect the missing founder evidence to the demand already beginning to appear.
