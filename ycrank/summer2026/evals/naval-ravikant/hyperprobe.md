# HyperProbe -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

The founders have earned the right to work on this problem. Karan Raina and Shailendra Singh previously built HyperTest, an SDK-based integration-testing product using application traces, reportedly serving more than 100 product teams before the Summer 2026 pivot. HyperProbe is not résumé knowledge applied to a fashionable market; it is adjacent knowledge acquired by building, selling, and then changing direction. The current product also has real artifacts: CheQ Digital found a production mismatch on its first attempt, while Housing.com fixed a race condition within an hour.

The probe mechanism is useful but not yet durable. An agent can place a read-only hook on a specific production line, capture live variable state without redeployment, and receive it through MCP. Yet Lightrun, Multiplayer, and Dynatrace/Rookout already possess overlapping runtime-debugging capabilities. PII redaction, audit logs, approval gates, and private-VPC deployment make the product admissible to enterprises; they do not make it non-copyable. If a competitor reproduces the interface and probe orchestration, the dossier identifies no proprietary model, exclusive data, hardware, or accumulated workflow asset that remains.

The strongest exception case is founder-specific knowledge outrunning an initially weak moat. The same team has already pivoted once, the old product reached more than 100 teams, two named companies describe sharp current-product outcomes, and developer AI-tool usage reached 80% in 2025. Those facts could let HyperProbe become the runtime layer through which coding agents learn from production. But that learning loop is still hypothetical. No evidence shows investigations entering an exclusive incident-to-root-cause corpus, improving diagnosis across deployments, or creating switching costs. Customer results prove utility; they do not yet prove compounding leverage.

The market and economics remain legible but shallow. HyperProbe charges $99 per instrumented service monthly, or $79 annually with a three-service minimum, and offers enterprise contracts. That is direct pricing, but neither testimonial identifies a paying customer, completed transaction, recurring revenue, or sound unit economics. Production debugging is widespread, yet the dossier names no adjacent buyer or transaction unlocked by the current probe capability. Competitor scale establishes demand for observability; it does not establish HyperProbe’s path beyond its initial wedge.

I see no integrity veto, artificial financing pressure, or adverse founder conduct. The unstated-pattern test is less favorable: customers do not contribute useful supply or distribution to one another, and adoption currently appears to require SDK installation, proofs of concept, and security review. Value grows through company labor rather than permissionless participation. My 36-month base case is that Dynatrace or another observability incumbent acquires HyperProbe for under $50 million before it reaches $5 million ARR, absorbing the probe layer as a feature.

I would pass with my own money today. The founders are the asset; the company has not yet created one. My flip condition is verifiable: demonstrate that a proprietary, cross-deployment incident-to-fix corpus measurably improves automated root-cause resolution for a cohort of at least 20 paying teams. Then copied code would reproduce the interface but not the intelligence accumulated behind it.
