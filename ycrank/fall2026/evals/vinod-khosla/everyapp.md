# EveryApp -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

EveryApp could make software ownership cheap enough that businesses stop renting each application separately. That is a consequential direction. But the dossier does not quantify the subscription spending displaced, the operating labor eliminated, or the population newly served. Free source code does not establish a 100x consequence when somebody still maintains the applications. I would pass on investing my own money today. The missing evidence concerns the mechanism of transformation, not the company’s age.

The technical question is whether shared infrastructure makes running many applications radically cheaper. EveryApp places authentication, permissions and credentials behind one Gateway on Cloudflare Workers, D1, R2 and KV. That is a working architectural choice. Cloudron already centralizes users and application operations; Coolify deploys across customer servers. No measured comparison establishes that EveryApp removes their binding constraint by 5–10x. Cloudflare specialization might simplify operations, but it also restricts application compatibility. Neither implication is a demonstrated technical discontinuity.

The strongest argument against my pass is Senescu’s actual building record. He launched the Gateway in January, built standalone OpenSEO, and returned to the broader platform on October 26. That sequence earns some credit for changing tactics and shipping useful software. OpenSEO’s 25.3K GitHub stars and claimed 3,000-plus entrepreneurs give him a substantial audience from which to recruit users and developers. This activates a pattern I take seriously: a tiny team assembling a technical community around a platform wedge. But EveryApp itself has 351 stars and 16 forks. The earlier application’s popularity does not establish commitment to the shared platform, and the dossier documents neither a belief revised after measured feedback nor complementary recruiting against a named weakness.

The economics could still make this interesting. Self-hosting is free, infrastructure is billed separately, and managed service is announced from $10 monthly. Those are customer prices, not a cost model. I need the combined infrastructure, support, upgrades and customer operating time compared with the software being replaced. The explicit absence of a backward-compatibility promise makes maintenance a particularly important variable. MIT licensing is compatible with building a valuable company, but it permits competing hosts to reproduce the software; durable advantage must emerge elsewhere.

My exceptions allow an unconventional mechanism when a documented instigator confronts an enormous existing value pool, or when the consequence alone justifies buying a highly uncertain option. EveryApp has not yet established either override. Nor would I classify the broader platform as a prohibited advertising business merely because OpenSEO supplied its initial audience. The relevant concern is whether standard infrastructure and distribution become the whole advantage. There is no evidence of premature organizational spending, but there is also no disclosed experiment with a budget, threshold and stop condition that attacks that uncertainty.

One result would flip my decision: an independently auditable, 90-day deployment study showing ten unrelated businesses operating at least three EveryApp applications each at one-tenth their previous total cost, including migration, maintenance and support, while paying EveryApp at a positive contribution margin. That would demonstrate a repeatable collapse in the cost of owning software. Until then, Senescu has demonstrated an ability to build and attract attention; the platform’s transformative leverage remains unproven.
