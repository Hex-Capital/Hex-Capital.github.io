# Petrarch -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case does not clear my first gate. A $4.44 billion training-dataset market growing 22.9% is a market forecast, not a quantified societal transformation. Petrarch may release valuable construction, manufacturing, and logistics data, but the dossier does not show which expert workflow becomes 10x cheaper, which population gains access, or what new model capability becomes possible. As documented, the upside is a successful data brokerage—not a 100x change in the economy.

The founders have earned more attention than the thesis alone would. Their experience spans data compliance, training-data quality, annotation, computer vision, and scientific datasets. More importantly, the three-person team has completed transactions involving construction plans, autonomous-driving recordings, and codebases; several thousand plans were reportedly redacted and transferred within one week. That is real pre-seed execution. It proves an ability to find and process unusual supply. It does not yet prove that the resulting data improves a buyer’s model, commands repeat spending, or creates proprietary learning.

The present mechanism is operational rather than technical: direct outreach, restructuring contacts, cleaning, PII removal, legal review, and fellows sourcing buyers and sellers. No result is benchmarked against a named incumbent, and the public cases report transfer throughput rather than a 5–10x improvement in model accuracy, cost, or capability. Defined.ai, Troveo, Human Native, and Datarade already cover adjacent parts of the workflow. Without exclusivity or repeat purchasing, Petrarch can introduce counterparties and then be removed from the second transaction.

The largest kill risks are dataset utility and chain of title. Petrarch’s privacy policy puts submission rights and legal bases substantially on providers, while no disclosed experiment establishes that a transferred dataset improves buyer-model performance. Completed transfers are milestones, but they do not attack either uncertainty with a numerical threshold. The Constellation fellowship suggests investment in supply and sales before the dossier shows a decisive utility or legal-risk test. Money spent finding more datasets is not the same as risk removed.

The strongest bull case is that these transactions are merely the wedge into a rights-clearing exchange for scarce industrial data. The relevant exception would be Block: a technically incremental starting product could evolve across an enormous existing value pool. The material difference is that Petrarch currently documents a $4.44 billion category, an inferred transaction spread, and no repeat-demand or proprietary-learning loop. My 36-month base case is a $2–5 million ARR managed data-sourcing and compliance service, not a defensible marketplace. I would reverse the decision if three independent AI buyers each renewed contracts worth at least $250,000 annually for exclusive datasets whose measured model-performance improvement exceeded 5x their next-best data source.
