# Anywhere -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032: my assistant remembers the tiny restaurant a friend recommended, knows which places we actually enjoyed, and helps turn “we should go somewhere” into Saturday afternoon. Anywhere’s interesting artifact is the API through which agents can read and modify a personal map. That makes this future more tangible than a conversational search box. But two transitions remain unproven: people must entrust the map with their lived history, and assistants must use that history well enough that people keep contributing. The dossier documents the interface, not those behaviors.

Charly Delaroche gives me a reason to take the experiment seriously. Building Zenly’s cross-platform mapping engine and rebuilding Life360’s map experience are specific accomplishments inside the problem. Co-founding amo adds entrepreneurial experience; Zenly was an employer, not his exit. This is substantially stronger than a founder discovering maps after discovering conversational AI. Still, the record does not establish the independently pursued experiments, recruiting magnetism, or commercial command that would make me believe he can carry users through those remaining transitions. His mapping experience earns conviction about execution, with less evidence about why this particular social product must exist.

Why does someone need another map? Anywhere combines places, notes, visits, and trips, but the organizing burden is inferred rather than measured. There is no identified fee, restrictive rule, or gatekeeper whose removal clearly returns control to an individual. It does resemble the unglamorous workflow repair I like, yet Mapstr already holds saved places and Wanderlog organizes trips. The agent connection must make the combined record useful enough to overcome that fragmentation. A free download and a connector directory entry do not establish that advantage, and neither tells me how Anywhere captures economic value.

The strongest argument against waiting is that someone with this much mapping experience has already shipped both a consumer app and an agent-accessible interface. A useful personal context layer could emerge through a playful consumer beachhead before anyone recognizes its infrastructure value. I am comfortable investing before revenue when working technical proof and founder obsession carry the bet. Here, however, the app launched publicly on November 1, 2026, and the dossier contains no repeated, effortful user behavior. The 2.0/5 rating across four reviews is far too small a sample to pronounce failure; the reported verification problem does matter because someone who cannot enter cannot develop a habit. Anywhere has an artifact. It has not yet shown the behavior that makes the artifact matter.

Location history also makes custody part of the product. The documented permissions, revocation, and deletion controls are constructive; this dossier does not establish a severe privacy violation. It also does not establish safe boundaries across every connected agent. Nor do dependencies on Apple Maps, Google Maps Platform, and OpenAI prove exclusive supplier captivity—but the API alone does not demonstrate control of a scarce enabling bottleneck. I would hold my personal check today. My verifiable flip condition is one six-week, opt-in cohort where at least 20 of 30 users use Anywhere weekly to have an agent update their map and then actually undertake the resulting outing. That would show the future reaching into ordinary life.
