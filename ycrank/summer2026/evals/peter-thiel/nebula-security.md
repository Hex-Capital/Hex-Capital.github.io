# Nebula Security -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Eten Zou’s cybersecurity PhD, Microsoft research record, and share of $400,000 in Google bounties make **Nebula Security** a pedigree-heavy exploit laboratory with a software-thin structural moat: founder lineage is the strongest dimension; atoms or regulatory protection is the weakest. **Nebula Security** has proved that its founders can discover difficult vulnerabilities. It has not proved that those discoveries form a monopoly rather than an unusually capable security service.

**Anduril** is the high rung for founder lineage: Palmer Luckey combined outsider status with the verified experience of shipping Oculus to a billion-dollar exit. **Nebula Security** shares the credentialed-outsider mechanism—Eten Zou researched kernel vulnerabilities, Yuan Tan contributed to Linux, and Frank Wu led DARPA program-repair work—but diverges where the analogy becomes load-bearing: none has shipped a commercial product to scale. The 1,162 validated findings and eight Chrome zero-days put **Nebula Security** above mere academic pedigree, but below the operator proof that made Luckey’s history an override rather than a résumé.

**Asana’s** low-rung weakness on the atoms axis was pure-software replicability; Dustin Moskovitz’s Facebook history supplied the exceptional override. The same weakness appears more severely at **Nebula Security**: VEGA is repository-centered software, its enterprise customers are not identified as regulated buyers, and human review is labor rather than a capex or certification barrier. **Nebula Security** has no Moskovitz override, while OpenAI’s Codex Security demonstrates that a model provider can enter the workflow without constructing a factory, winning a clearance, or launching a rocket.

**OpenAI’s Codex Security** exposes the missing secret. **Nebula Security** could claim that exploit researchers—not generic models—are required to validate multistep business-logic vulnerabilities, but the dossier never identifies an architectural reason OpenAI, Semgrep, Gecko, or XBOW cannot hire researchers or reproduce that workflow. Over 18 months, researcher judgment compounds only if it becomes proprietary machine advantage; otherwise **Nebula Security** compounds payroll and review queues. A secret names why the incumbent cannot follow. “We execute better” is competition, and competition is for losers.

**Nebula Security’s** 1,162 validated findings and 98 public CVEs create the raw material for a monopoly, not the monopoly itself. The score would move an entire tier if customer scans produced an exclusive vulnerability graph whose measured detection advantage grew with every repository and imposed material switching costs. The present evidence points the other way: public exploits educate competitors, human-written patches constrain throughput, and per-repository pricing describes monetization rather than pricing power. **Nebula Security** may become an excellent scanner while Gecko, ZeroPath, Semgrep, XBOW, and OpenAI ensure that excellent scanning earns commodity margins.

**SpaceX** had a definite sequence—reach orbit, reduce launch cost, achieve reusability—and each milestone deepened the final monopoly. **Nebula Security** reports an enterprise private beta, pay-as-you-go access, and a plan to limit report volume, but no two-to-five-year technical commitment explains how VEGA escapes human-validation throughput. The contrast matters: **Nebula Security** has a definite craft and an indefinite company plan. The dossier says what the product does today; it does not say what irreversible architecture the founders intend to own in 2029.

**Nebula Security’s** $400,000 in bounty rewards supports a specific 36-month forecast: by July 2029, **Nebula Security** reaches roughly $1–3 million ARR and is acquired by Semgrep for under $75 million, primarily for its exploit researchers and vulnerability corpus. Exceptional lineage produces valuable detection technology; weak structural protection prevents independent pricing power. In Tolkien’s world, many possess useful rings. Only one ring governs the others.

**Nebula Security’s** 98 public CVEs form the strongest objection to that forecast: the bull case is that elite exploit output becomes the security analogue of Facebook’s early social graph, with researcher feedback creating a data loop before larger vendors understand its value. The Known Exceptions do not quite rescue **Nebula Security**—Asana and Quora had founders who had already shipped Facebook to scale, while Nebula’s evidence is technical rather than commercial. No disclosed revenue, no named enterprise customer, and no demonstrated customer-data compounding outweigh the exception case today. One verifiable condition would flip the verdict: **Nebula Security** reaching $5 million ARR by July 2028 without proportional growth in human-review headcount.
