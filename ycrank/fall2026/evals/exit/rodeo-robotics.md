# Rodeo Robotics -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a vehicle inspection platform such as UVeye. The memo reports that UVeye sells drive-through inspection systems. Rodeo says its system combines inspection, diagnosis, and physical service, including oil changes. Owning Rodeo could let an inspection platform earn revenue from work its systems identify. That rationale depends on Rodeo proving that its robots can complete enough work at a lower all-in cost than the buyer’s alternatives. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

Rodeo reports 96% autonomous execution time on initial tasks and planned customer deployments for January 2027. The milestone is repeatable performance across shops and vehicle types, measured in completed jobs, technician minutes saved, and cost per job after interventions and downtime. The memo lists a $500,000 convertible note. Its terms, available cash, and the cost of installing and supporting enough units to reach the milestone are unknown. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3% ownership.

| Illustrative scenario | Proceeds to the holder |
|---|---:|
| Modest sale at $40 million enterprise value, less $5 million in net debt and deal adjustments | $1.05 million at 3% (2.1×) |
| Strong sale at $100 million enterprise value, less the same $5 million | $2.85 million at 3% (5.7×), or $2.38 million at 2.5% (4.75×) |
| Prolonged private operation with cash reinvested through year eight | $0 realized |

Both sale scenarios assume a full cash acquisition within eight years, an eligible holder selling its entire stake, and pro rata distribution after the stated deductions, with no additional senior claims. The note’s conversion or repayment treatment, the actual waterfall, and closing permissions are unverified. The memo gives no Rodeo revenue or buyer pricing evidence to support either sale value, and it identifies no independent route that would pay this holder. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Technician supervision could erase the labor savings that make Rodeo valuable to an acquirer. The reported autonomy figure covers initial tasks, and interventions are planned for unsupported jobs, hazards, and low-confidence actions. PitPro’s tire robot is already deployed at Kal Tire, and Automated Tire offers overlapping tire and inspection workflows. A buyer could cover enough of its needs with narrower systems and assign little value to Rodeo’s broader workflow. The evidence leaves a specific gap in buyer economics. It establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported sale-price ceiling that triggers Pass. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Would the economics of paid shop deployments give an inspection-platform buyer a reason to pay enough for Rodeo to clear the holder’s $2.5 million cash hurdle? Repeated results across shops showing completed jobs, technician time, downtime, and all-in cost per job would let a buyer compare acquiring Rodeo with building or partnering. A buyer-validated price range that clears the hurdle after dilution and senior claims would move the action to Reach out. Evidence that the strongest credible sale falls materially short would move it to Pass.
