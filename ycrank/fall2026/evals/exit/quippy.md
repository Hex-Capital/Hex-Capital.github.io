# Quippy -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Quippy’s strongest path. A learning or coaching platform could buy its consumer rehearsal product and subscriber base to add daily practice for dating, friendships, and workplace conflict. Quippy says it uses saved practice history to tailor later sessions. Ownership could be worth more than building or licensing voice roleplay if that personalization measurably improves paid retention and the buyer can preserve the benefit after transfer. The memo provides no measured lift or buyer interest. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

Quippy has shipped on iOS and Android, and its iOS app had 92 U.S. ratings averaging 4.7. The 10,000-plus lifetime iOS installs are a third-party estimate. The live subscription product makes a cohort test of 90-day paid retention with and without history-based personalization plausible. The memo leaves paid cohort size, burn, and runway unknown, so the cost and timing of that test remain open. Quippy describes deletion and integration controls, while the permissions a buyer would need to retain or use practice history and connected-app context remain unverified. (*Market & Traction*, *The Idea*, *Key Risks*)

Under the illustrative terms, a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation buys 5%, diluted to 3% with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× target is $2.5 million in gross cash before investor taxes. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. Assuming $5 million of net debt for illustration, the 3% case requires about $88.3 million in enterprise value. A $25 million sale yields $600,000 (1.2×), and a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). The listed subscription prices establish a way to charge, with no disclosed paid revenue, buyer economics, or comparable sale price to support either valuation. Actual holder terms and senior claims are unknown. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

If paid growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and Quippy reinvests all available cash, the holder realizes $0 over eight years and still owns the shares. The memo also leaves sponsor financing capacity, eligible secondary demand, a path to public scale, and funded dividends or buybacks unevidenced. A wind-down would pay only the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo gives no basis to estimate. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

A buyer could build or license voice roleplay. The founder identifies external tools Quippy uses, and Talkville and Volo already offer consumer conversation simulations. Quippy’s strongest reason for a buyer to own the product is the potential retention benefit of accumulated practice history. That benefit is unmeasured, and the memo leaves open whether a buyer could use the underlying data. These gaps warrant Watch. The memo establishes neither a binding transfer barrier nor a supported price range that falls materially short of the holder’s target, so the Pass gate does not apply. (*Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Quippy show that history-based coaching produces enough incremental profit from retained subscribers to justify a strategic buyer paying the price required for a 5× holder return? A 90-day paid-cohort test, paired with buyer-specific build-versus-buy economics that support a price range clearing the hurdle on plausible holder terms, would move the action to Reach out.
