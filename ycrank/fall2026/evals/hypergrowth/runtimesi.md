# Runtime.si -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B — repeatable sales with account expansion** is the strongest plausible path. The initial buyer is a robot builder deploying manipulation systems whose tasks change often enough that collecting demonstrations and fine-tuning policies slows deployment. If Runtime can handle those changes through the same control-stack integration, direct signups could lead to paid deployments, and buyers could add tasks or robots as they see savings. The same product could serve builders abroad if they can reuse the integration and validation workflow. Software licensing is a scenario assumption. (The Idea)

The memo documents a direct early-access signup, 69 launch votes, and a skill library that records physical verification. Runtime reports matching or exceeding unnamed robot foundation models on tasks including rope threading and egg grasping, with trial counts and success rates undisclosed. These are early signals of technical feasibility and attention. Buyer activation, payment, repeat use, and demand in a second market remain unverified. The pilot references concern the earlier data-center product. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Runtime could replace per-task fine-tuning with a different set of recurring costs. The next customer may require control-stack integration and on-site validation. Each added workload may require more planning compute and physical testing, and the next geography may add field support. Runtime says it is still accelerating the planning loop for live operation. If scene reconstruction or contact errors require engineers to intervene whenever tasks change, the buyer’s savings and Runtime’s contribution could both shrink as usage grows. The memo leaves these costs unmeasured, so the structural Pass gate is unestablished. (The Idea, Key Risks)
