# Tire Swing -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Tire Swing could make affordable housing available sooner by removing certification delays. That is consequential if compliance is the binding bottleneck. But the dossier does not establish how many occupied units, vacancy days, or dollars of expert labor its system could release. Automating paperwork does not automatically expand housing supply. I can see the mechanism; I cannot yet price the consequence of success. That earns the missing-evidence default, rather than a penalty for being early.

The strongest bull case is that Tire Swing is assembling executable regulatory expertise. Its reported thousands of deterministic and AI checks, document extraction, missing-information requests, and source-linked findings could replace substantial consultant work. My portfolio patterns include regulated workflows and execution-heavy software; a scientific invention is not mandatory. But the decisive distinction is whether expertise becomes dramatically cheaper. Yardi already retrieves payroll and banking information, while Pronto Housing automates qualification and compliance. Tire Swing supplies no comparative accuracy, reviewer-time, or throughput benchmark. The checks demonstrate implementation, but their quantity does not establish a technical discontinuity or a proprietary learning loop. Equally, the dossier does not justify dismissing the product as an off-the-shelf wrapper.

Witten’s memory-forensics work and Irvine’s Cloud Custodian and Instacart engineering experience are relevant to handling sensitive documents and implementing rules reliably. The documented move from healthcare compliance, described in February 2026, to affordable housing shows that the founders can change direction. It does not establish what evidence changed their beliefs, how quickly they responded, or whether they recruited complementary housing expertise. I give that criterion its default. My known exceptions would require an exceptional instigator paired with an enormous demonstrated value pool, or technical progress whose consequence overwhelms the missing business evidence. Neither override is established here.

The largest kill risk is whether Tire Swing removes expert work after accounting for verification and correction. Its terms require qualified human approval; that is a sensible operating constraint, not evidence that automation fails. It does make end-to-end reviewer effort the essential measurement. The reported $500,000 financing says nothing about how cheaply the team is resolving that uncertainty. There is no disclosed decisive experiment, threshold, or spending sequence, so milestone discipline also receives its default. HOTMA enforcement beginning January 1, 2027 creates urgency for covered housing authorities, but urgency cannot substitute for proving that the product works economically.

I pass with my own money today. Subscription billing is documented; pricing, delivery costs, and the labor remaining per certification are not. There is no evidence of permanent subsidy dependence, but there is also no demonstrated cost-parity model. My verifiable flip condition is an independent, blinded comparison across multiple operators showing at least a 10x reduction in total certification cost—including software, review, corrections, and rule maintenance—with no increase in material eligibility errors. That would establish a discontinuity in delivering regulated expertise and materially change this decision. Missing revenue, a two-person team, and absent press are not what keep me out. The unproven multiplier is.
