# Osmaura -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The most revealing event is the two-month negotiation with a major law firm. The founders eventually withdrew, but the dossier does not say what blocked the contract, what they built to get around it, or what changed afterward ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/tselote), July 2026, via search snippet). Walking away may have been good qualification. It is not yet evidence of resourcefulness. The central question is what Osmaura shipped during those two months that made the next firm easier to close.

There is a real product, not merely a diagram. Agents monitor public and regulatory events, combine them with firm context, and customers reportedly log in daily ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/osmaura), August 2026). That is the strongest evidence here. But Osmaura gives no customer count, precision benchmark, attributable opportunity, or example of an alert producing a conversation or matter. A product trusted with a lawyer’s professional relationships needs measured proof. “Daily” is promising; without a denominator or outcome, it is not yet a proof spike.

The narrow well is plausible. Corporate-law partners spend substantial time pursuing business, and Osmaura is selling directly to lawyers and business-development leaders rather than waiting for a distribution partner ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/osmaura), August 2026). Yet the dossier never identifies the particular partner, practice area, or recurring event type that pulls the crude product back. The 1,144 reactions to the YC announcement are investor-legible attention, not demand. Anonymous daily usage deserves provisional credit, but not the same credit as named firms repeatedly acting on alerts.

The bull case is that Kali Abeje and Jity Woldemichael are two strong technical founders who left MIT, spent months interviewing lawyers, built working software, and are personally making sales calls. They also rejected a distracting large contract instead of allowing one enterprise buyer to dictate the company. This could be a young team learning quickly in an ugly market. But the known exception for unusually young founders requires numerical technical proof and direct exposure to the problem. Osmaura currently has neither in the public record: Abeje’s policy research, debate, and mock trial explain interest in law, not a founder-originated collision with law-firm business development.

The schlep is substantial: noisy signals, confidential firm data, integrations, access controls, partner trust, and slow contracting. That can hide a good company. It can also trap a generic AI product. Nexl already serves more than 150 law firms and can add proactive monitoring to an existing relationship-intelligence surface, while Darrow already derives legal opportunities from public data ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251008092790/en/Nexl-Secures-%2423M-Series-B-Funding-to-Accelerate-Law-Firm-Growth-Platform-Innovation), October 2025; [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/rkeopxvkt), September 2023). Osmaura has disclosed no proprietary dataset or measured feedback loop that makes its ranking compound. This fails the pre-round-proof pattern I care about, and the founders’ friendship from MIT does not yet establish a history of shared execution under conflict.

I would pass now. The flip condition is verifiable: five named law firms using Osmaura weekly for 90 days, with at least three signed matters attributable to its alerts and a documented founder-built workaround that shortened deployment after the failed major-firm negotiation. That would simultaneously prove resourcefulness, a narrow well of demand, and a product that incumbents cannot dismiss as another feature.
