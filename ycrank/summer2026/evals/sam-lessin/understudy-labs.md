# Understudy Labs -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The first three numbers I want are absent: post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and whether this is genuinely the first scarce check. YC participation does not prove an auction, especially at pre-seed, but it does not prove cheap non-consensus capital either. With no round terms or investor-competition data, I have to underwrite the deal as unknown rather than pretend that an attractive technical story makes the price irrelevant.

Understudy has built a truth-revealing mechanism inside the product: capture production traces, construct held-out evals, train a smaller model, and promote it only when it beats the incumbent. The published bench is unusually candid—four reasoning tasks, seven CRM-action tasks, ten runs per promoted configuration, and evidence that generic adapters fail across slices. But that is a product experiment, not yet a capital plan. The dossier names no round-funded hypothesis, dated milestone, or pass/fail result. “Hire engineers and acquire design partners,” if that is the unstated plan, would be another capital factory rather than a venture moment.

The founders are the real signal. Luis Manrique has operated across Google advertising ML, Instacart AI product, and Gumloop commercialization, where he closed approximately $2 million in the first year. Aamir Poonawalla spent roughly a decade building Instacart’s ads-serving, auction, experimentation, and ML infrastructure and previously founded a YC company. Together they have shipped on both sides of the bridge: production ML systems and the commercial adoption of AI tooling. The CEO/CTO split also gives me more confidence than an undifferentiated two-founder committee, although the dossier does not establish who has final authority when product and financing choices collide.

The supposed dark pool is weaker. Understudy can observe customer traces and operational metadata, but its terms prohibit using one customer’s payloads to train or evaluate another customer’s models. That is sensible for procurement and limiting for compounding advantage. A small group of unnamed design partners demonstrates access to workflows, so this is better than a slide deck; it does not demonstrate that the access caused a purchase, retained production usage, or measurable savings. Twelve GitHub stars and five forks are distribution activity, not economic behavior.

The bear case is not merely that Fireworks, Together AI, Baseten, and Braintrust have raised more money. It is that each can absorb pieces of Understudy’s loop while upstream model vendors make smaller models, caching, routing, and evals native features. The strongest exception precedent would be **Ando**: both businesses use AI optimization inside enterprise workflows, but Ando earned the exception with greater than 90% forecast accuracy and 100% pilot-to-paid conversion and retention. Understudy’s limited benchmarks and unnamed preview partners do not provide that override. Nor do order-form billing provisions establish revenue, gross margin, burn, or a route to profitability after deleting the next financing.

I am passing today. My 36-month base case is that Understudy retreats from full autonomous model replacement into narrower evaluation-and-routing infrastructure, serving fewer than 15 enterprise customers and remaining below $3 million ARR as the larger inference platforms bundle the surrounding workflow. The one verifiable flip condition is a signed financing plan for a sub-$10 million post-money round whose proceeds are explicitly tied to converting at least ten production workloads to paid open-model deployments by a dated milestone. That would simultaneously establish scarce pricing and a real venture moment; without it, I would be investing in an excellent team attached to an unspecified deal and an unproven economic loop.
