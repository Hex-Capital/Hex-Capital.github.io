# Hesper AI -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by a P&C claims software platform is Hesper’s strongest path. Hesper’s agents gather missing evidence, verify it, and return a source-linked case file to the claims system. A platform could pay to own that workflow if embedding it across its installed base gives it a measurable advantage over licensing it or building it. Hesper reports that one MGA cut average adjuster time per claim by 82% and identified $1.2 million in suspected fraud over two weeks. The memo gives no recovered-dollar figure or evidence that the result holds across customers. The result supports customer value. The value of ownership remains an inference. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The operating milestone is sustained time savings at acceptable investigation accuracy across multiple paid deployments. Reported paying production customers, pilots across carriers, MGAs, and TPAs, and a $1.5 million pre-seed round make that test plausible. Burn, runway, and the cost of reaching the milestone are undisclosed. The memo also gives no deal-relevant evidence on IP ownership or assignment of customer and data rights, and it supports no independent route to cash for this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** The memo gives no terms for the evaluated check or usable acquisition-price evidence. Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-on checks. The eight-year objective is $2.5 million in gross cash, or 5×. That requires $83.3 million of distributable equity proceeds at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

For both sale scenarios, assume zero net debt and zero other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, with all consideration paid in cash at closing and allocated pro rata. Assume zero preference effects, stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation. Actual terms and the waterfall are unknown.

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Holder cash before taxes | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $50M EV → $50M equity | $1.5M | 3× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100M EV → $100M equity | $3.0M | 6× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No sale or payout | $0, with shares retained | 0× |

The strong sale clears the hurdle at 3% ownership and falls short at 2%, where the holder receives $2 million. Both prices are scenario inputs. In the prolonged-private case, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public-market scale remains out of reach, and the company reinvests its cash.

### Countercase

A claims platform could capture much of the workflow value through a license or its own product. Duck Creek launched native infrastructure for deploying and governing insurance agents, and FRISS offers Guidewire-integrated investigation referrals and case management. Those offerings make the ownership premium uncertain. The memo leaves open whether they can match Hesper’s evidence gathering and verification at comparable cost and accuracy. That gap supports Watch. A structural Pass would require evidence that substitutes cap the realizable price below the holder’s hurdle, or that every credible route otherwise leaves the holder materially short. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Would a claims platform gain enough by owning Hesper to justify a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? A buyer-side comparison of acquisition, licensing, and internal development, grounded in sustained time savings and measured error rates across paid deployments, would answer it. Economics that support a price with room for dilution and senior claims would move the action to **Reach out**. Evidence that a comparable substitute caps the price below the hurdle would move it to **Pass**.
