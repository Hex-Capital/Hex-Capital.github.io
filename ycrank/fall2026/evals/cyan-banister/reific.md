# Reific -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

An agent that answers correctly after reading the whole history can still be useless while that history is happening. By 2032, I can imagine personal assistants waiting for permission, industrial agents revising decisions as sensor records arrive, and scientific agents managing experiments over days. Getting there requires training on incomplete information, then proving that the learned timing survives unfamiliar situations. Reific has built a concrete artifact for the first step: records released on a clock, with outcomes checked against what actually happened. The second step remains unproven. That distinction determines whether I am buying a useful training ingredient or an elaborate obstacle course.

The most attractive part is underneath the demo. Reific describes acquiring longitudinal records and constructing evidence-checked answer keys across personal, industrial, and scientific tasks. Those are assets that require work, even when the underlying records are nonexclusive. This fits my pattern of looking for concealed infrastructure beneath a visible wave: many agent products could need this capability. Nonexclusive licensing limits scarcity, but it does not erase the value of turning messy histories into executable, gradable environments. I give the enabling layer substantial credit without treating a possible exclusive-data moat as an existing one.

Antanas’s first-author EVEREST research on rare-event time-series forecasting matters more to me than the Imperial prize. Temporal dependencies and uncertainty are relevant technical tuition for this problem. That is a real research artifact, beyond a founder announcing an interest in reliable AI. But the dossier does not establish a long sequence of independent experiments, recruited believers, or commercial decisions predating Reific. I see relevant depth; I cannot yet see the full obsession-to-business bridge.

Why does the present system produce agents that fail on timing? Reific identifies one institutional failure: evaluations hand models completed histories and thereby conceal the difficulty of deciding before the answer becomes knowable. That is specific and useful. The dossier does not establish who collects an avoidable rent, what delay costs a user, or whose economic control Reific restores. Better laboratory measurement could eventually return agency to people delegating work, but that remains a proposed consequence. It earns no additional credit today.

The November 2 results—31.6%, 43.1%, and 29.7% of available reward across the three task families—show room for improvement, not customer demand or successful training transfer. Likewise, 64 simultaneous sandboxes without reported failures is internal infrastructure evidence. Nobody in this dossier has repeatedly paid, committed scarce researcher time, or rearranged a training program around Reific. I use the absent-behavior default here; missing revenue itself is not a strike against a pre-seed company.

The strongest case for writing a check is that outcome-verified temporal records become an essential curriculum, and this technically relevant founder starts accumulating grading expertise before buyers recognize its importance. My pre-revenue exception makes that possibility worth taking seriously. But the exception needs affirmative conviction from the prototype, founder history, and committed counterparties together. Reific currently supplies only part of that combination. Bespoke, Patronus, and internal lab teams can pursue overlapping environments, while Reific has not published the magnitude or held-out durability of its claimed training gains. Difficulty alone cannot resolve that objection.

I am **Neutral at 49/100**, with genuine interest in the infrastructure. Longitudinal personal records make consent and custody a signing gate; licensing records does not by itself demonstrate subject consent, although the dossier establishes no severe privacy violation. It also supplies no evidence of the founder-control shell game or dependence on one closed model supplier that would actively repel me. My one verifiable flip condition is a named external lab publishing a controlled, compute-matched result showing that training on Reific improves performance on unseen temporal tasks. That would connect the carefully built obstacle course to agents that actually behave better.
