# Voyager -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Voyager’s strongest path to private-company liquidity. A creative software platform could buy Voyager to bring cross-app editing into its own product. Voyager says its desktop agent can execute edits in installed tools such as Blender, DaVinci Resolve, and Ableton Live. Runway’s existing video workflows and disclosed $315 million financing make it a plausible example of the buyer class. The ownership case depends on Voyager’s integrations saving a buyer enough time or cost, or enabling workflows it could only capture by owning the technology. The memo provides no buyer interest or quantified advantage over building or partnering. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*)

A customer reported completing a short film using Voyager alongside Meshy and ElevenLabs, with $128.99 in generation spend across the workflow. That is an early production signal. Repeat paid use and comparative savings remain unverified. A measurable next milestone is repeat paid production work with documented editing-time savings across installed apps. Voyager has shipped the product, but its funding and runway to reach that milestone are unclear. The memo also leaves ownership and transfer rights for the core code and integrations unverified. No independent fallback has usable evidence of cash reaching this holder. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

Voyager’s investment terms are unverified, so the proceeds bridge uses the thesis’s illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation. The holder starts at 5%, dilutes to 3%, makes no follow-on checks, and needs $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years. That requires $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%.

| Illustrative outcome | Timing | Enterprise value to distributable equity | Holder cash at 3% | Gross multiple |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | Year 5 | $50M to $50M | $1.5M | 3.0× |
| Strong sale | Year 7 | $100M to $100M | $3.0M | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private | Through year 8 | No sale or distribution | $0, shares retained | 0.0× realized |

The sale prices and dates are scenario assumptions. Both sales assume all-cash consideration, zero net debt and deal costs, and a simple pro rata waterfall with no preference adjustments. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation each contribute $0. At 2% ownership, even the strong sale returns $2.0 million, or 4.0×. In the prolonged-private scenario, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public-market scale remains out of reach, and Voyager reinvests its cash. The memo supplies no usable acquisition pricing or buyer economics to establish that the required sale price is attainable. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Adobe already offers a native agent with third-party models, and Runway and Krea offer overlapping workflow tools and connectors. A buyer could build the workflows its customers need or obtain them through an ordinary integration. If either option delivers equivalent production results at a cost that caps bids below the holder’s hurdle, the strategic route fails. The memo establishes overlap while leaving production parity and the price ceiling unresolved. That supports Watch. The structural Pass override remains untriggered. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

Does a buyer-validated production pilot show that owning Voyager’s cross-app editing capability creates enough incremental value over building or integrating it to support a price that clears the holder’s cash hurdle? Repeat paid workflows and a platform buyer’s quantified comparison would answer it. Move to Reach out if that comparison supports a credible price above the applicable hurdle.
