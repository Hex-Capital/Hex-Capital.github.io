# Melty -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Coding agents could expand the supply of game mashups; PC gamers become the audience, and repeat play determines whether Melty becomes a distribution business. Cheaper creation alone does not establish that market. The controlling belief is that players will repeatedly discover and play experiences through Melty rather than install one novelty and leave. The dossier provides neither a retention threshold nor evidence testing that belief. I can formulate the investment hypothesis, but I cannot count my hypothesis as company evidence.

The strongest affirmative evidence is that Melty handles actual execution. Its Windows app downloads mashups, installs dependencies, launches owned games, and supplies multiplayer invitations. That is a meaningful operational loop around generated code. Better coding agents could improve the content flowing through it while leaving installation and coordination valuable. This is Melty’s strongest dimension. However, creator-PC release checks do not establish reliable execution across players’ machines, and the dossier documents no accumulated compatibility advantage. Publisher and anti-cheat restrictions also limit which experiences the launcher can deliver.

There is a plausible technology discontinuity here. Coding agents became more accessible following Claude Code’s May 2025 general availability, and Melty incorporates them into creation. But the economic step change remains unmeasured: no before-and-after development time, publishing success rate, or creator output is supplied. Existing distribution already runs through Nexus Mods, Overwolf, and mod.io. Melty needs agent-created content to produce a new reason to aggregate players, because easier content production could also benefit those established channels. Free mashups and contemplated future payments do not yet establish how much value the platform captures.

The bull case is stronger than “another AI gaming demo.” A functioning launcher, multiplayer invitation links, and a reported Discord community of 10,611 members could connect creation, discovery, and social play. If that loop works, the first product becomes distribution for subsequent experiences without reacquiring every player. That is the portfolio pattern I would want to see. The difficulty is that the October 26 community figure comes from a third-party snapshot; it establishes audience interest, not repeated play. There are no disclosed player cohorts, repeat creators, or transactions. I treat demand as unproven, rather than treating free usage or pre-seed status as adverse evidence.

David Miller’s reported history coaching millions of gamers supplies audience context, but it does not document costly commitment or lessons from operating a prior startup. His listed prior projects are insufficient to infer either. I also do not see grounds for a known-exception override: Melty has neither documented frontier research and scarce model-building capability nor evidence that exceptional consumer engagement compensates for uncertain durability. Contractual catalogue exclusivity, extending twelve months after removal, cannot substitute for a demonstrated network effect. The potential compatibility-data advantage likewise remains a hypothesis without scale or reproduction-cost evidence.

I would pass with my own money today. The product owns useful workflow steps, but the market gate remains unresolved: does abundant mashup supply create durable player demand? One verifiable result would make me reopen the decision: an independently inspectable cohort of at least 1,000 newly activated players in which 30% return in week eight to play a different creator’s mashup without staff assistance. That would connect Melty’s operational strengths to the distribution mechanism the investment requires.
