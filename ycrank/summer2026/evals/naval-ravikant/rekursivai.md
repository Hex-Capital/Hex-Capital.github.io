# rekursiv.ai -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Joshua V. Dillon created TensorFlow Probability and Dan Kondratyuk authored an ICML 2024 Best Paper, yet rekursiv.ai has no user-added network. That pairing is the signature: rekursiv.ai is concentrated specific knowledge packaged as a single-sided research engine. The intelligence is rare; the distribution is ordinary. Great researchers can create knowledge, but great architectures make every new user increase the value received by every prior user. rekursiv.ai has demonstrated the first and not the second.

**Stack Overflow** is the high rung for specific knowledge: developers with years of lived frustration built for other developers. Dillon’s 13 years at Google Research and DeepMind, plus Kondratyuk’s Google and Luma world-model work, put rekursiv.ai near that rung. Hypothesis formation, experiment design, and evidence tracing are not features imagined by product managers; rekursiv.ai’s founders lived inside those loops. The divergence is that Stack Overflow’s knowledge became a public artifact used and improved by a community, while rekursiv.ai’s public GitHub organization has 14 followers and its largest repository has 25 stars. Specific knowledge is proven. Translation into a movement is not.

**Perplexity** exposes the low-rung network mechanism: a useful single-sided product where one customer does not automatically make the next customer’s experience better. Naval’s exception applied because permissionless distribution, Aravind Srinivas’s specific knowledge, and pre-consensus AI timing were all overwhelming. The same missing mechanism appears in rekursiv.ai: no marketplace, no creator graph, and no evidence that one laboratory contributes value to another. rekursiv.ai might accumulate a proprietary corpus of failed experiments, but the dossier marks that as inferred; private learning curves are a data moat, not a network. The Perplexity exception is relevant, but rekursiv.ai has not yet earned all three override conditions.

sagent’s 25 GitHub stars show the tension in rekursiv.ai’s leverage architecture. Apache-2.0 components, technical reports, and reproducible ARC and Sudoku results can compound through code and media; 235 ARC experiments and a 99,768-puzzle stress test are excellent distribution objects for technical audiences. Yet rekursiv.ai currently gates access through email and appears to rely on founder-led design partnerships. Over 18 months, self-serve experiments, APIs, and third-party extensions could turn rekursiv.ai into code leverage. Without them, every new deployment requires trust, compute, and human selling. Software is permissionless leverage only when adoption is permissionless too.

$8.60B of autonomous-agent spending growing at a forecast 28.4% CAGR gives rekursiv.ai room beyond one benchmark, but the literal Sudoku algorithm failed to improve ARC. For rekursiv.ai to move into the highest market rung, one research method would need to transfer across ML domains and support at least two adjacent products—research infrastructure, model optimization, or automated evaluation—without rebuilding the system each time. The $5M financing buys more pivot-room than a normal pre-seed round, while experiments lasting up to 48 hours consume that room. rekursiv.ai is operating in a large landscape, but the reusable platform underneath the first application remains unproven.

**Twitter** passed the long-term-founder test because Jack Dorsey shipped and Ev Williams had completed Blogger’s journey cleanly. rekursiv.ai offers a narrower but favorable parallel: Dillon and Kondratyuk worked together at both Google and Luma, then shipped eight system iterations and hundreds of autonomous experiments. rekursiv.ai also publishes the expected four-to-six-point semi-private ARC accuracy decline rather than hiding the benchmark caveat. That reads as intelligence, energy, and epistemic integrity. It does not establish how the founders treat employees, allocate authority, or behave under financial stress, so I would verify the 10-year-person test before doubling down.

The 235 ARC experiments point to a falsifiable 36-month outcome: by July 2029, rekursiv.ai will have narrowed from a generalized “AI scientist” into an enterprise ML experiment-optimization platform producing $3M-$8M ARR, without becoming a meaningful marketplace or user network. rekursiv.ai’s founders know enough to build a valuable machine even if they do not build a compounding ecosystem. High specific knowledge plus low network effects usually produces a strong tool, not a protocol.

Sakana AI’s $412M, Periodic Labs’ $300M, and Lila Sciences’ $550M form the strongest case against that forecast: rekursiv.ai may be open-sourcing its differentiation into a market where better-capitalized laboratories can copy the software, subsidize compute, and hire the same researchers. rekursiv.ai has no public revenue, no disclosed customer moat, and only 14 GitHub followers; capital and labor could overwhelm code leverage before distribution compounds. The rebuttal is that 71.4%-75.5% ARC-AGI-1 accuracy and 100% Sudoku-Extreme accuracy were produced by two founders who have already built frontier systems at Google, DeepMind, and Luma. Specific knowledge buys the option. If rekursiv.ai cannot name three external research organizations running repeated weekly experiments by July 2027, I flip from Invest to Pass.
