# Risklytics -- Elad Gil Evaluation

January 2026 AI exclusions make Risklytics a well-timed specialty brokerage with no privileged position in the resulting market. Risklytics has the strongest part of the Stripe/Coinbase pattern—a specific catalyst inside a 24-month window—but the weakest part of the pattern: Risklytics currently controls neither carrier capacity nor customer distribution. The analytical signature is a re-opening bet without an index position.

**Anduril** benefited from a concrete catalyst—Ukraine exposed defense-software inadequacy—and converted that catalyst into contracts through existing procurement expertise. Risklytics similarly identifies an actual market change: carriers began attaching explicit AI exclusions to standard policies in January 2026, creating renewal deadlines for robotics, autonomy, energy, and construction companies. The analogy breaks where it matters: Anduril had Palmer Luckey and Trae Stephens plus visible contract velocity, while Risklytics has pending producer licenses, no disclosed carrier agreements, and founders Samuel Gold and Alexander Risio have no documented insurance or commercial-risk experience. Risklytics found a real reopening, but has not shown unique access to the window.

**Lyft** exposed the failure mode of occupying a valuable market without owning the advantageous position inside it: being number two in a network-effect market is not an index on rideshare. Risklytics has a related structural problem. Risklytics must win individual accounts while Newfront already places $3.1 billion in annual premiums and holds licenses across all 50 states, and Vouch or Founder Shield can add AI-exclusion reviews to established brokerage relationships. A specialist intake form is a feature until Risklytics proves proprietary carrier appetite, exclusive capacity, or renewal data that incumbents cannot reproduce.

Risklytics reports 91 LinkedIn followers, no current-product revenue, and no named customer, LOI, repeat user, or bound policy. Over the next 18 months, revenue velocity would compound into defensibility because every Risklytics placement could improve carrier routing, policy-language knowledge, and renewal retention. Without placements, the loop runs backward: pending licensing prevents transactions, absent transactions prevent carrier data, and absent data leaves Risklytics selling expertise that Newfront can hire. For an AI-adjacent business, no disclosed curve is the “false signal” side of the distinction, so I apply the absent-evidence default rather than treating pre-seed silence as traction.

Risklytics did ship Ember into beta before pivoting from natural-disaster scoring to brokerage, which is better evidence than two Harvard biographies alone. Risklytics would move up a full tier on founder execution if Samuel Gold or Alexander Risio could show weekly product iterations driven by broker and insured feedback—specific exclusion extraction, carrier-routing automation, and repeat submissions—not merely a fast change in positioning. Today there are no public repositories, prior independent products beyond Ember, or evidence that either founder has completed the brokerage workflow end to end. The pivot proves motion; it does not yet prove the Perplexity pattern of shipping the discussed artifact between meetings.

**Color Genomics** worked because workflow and data sat on top of commodity sequencing; Color did not require new chemistry, new regulation, and new customer behavior simultaneously. Risklytics is directionally similar because Risklytics does not need new actuarial science or new insurance capacity to exist. Yet Risklytics still depends on three operational gates aligning: producer licensure, carriers willing to preserve or affirm physical-AI coverage, and frontier-tech buyers switching brokers. Those are not independent scientific miracles, so the architecture is survivable, but carrier willingness is outside Risklytics’s control and weakens the single-miracle case.

Newfront’s $3.1 billion of annual premiums makes my 36-month forecast specific: by July 2029, Risklytics will have broadened from physical-AI coverage into a general frontier-technology E&S brokerage and will remain below $2 million in annualized commission revenue. Risklytics can use the January 2026 exclusions to open doors, but insufficient proprietary capacity will make narrow specialization hard to defend; broadening becomes the rational way to monetize licenses and founder-led distribution. Risklytics may build a useful brokerage, but the current mechanism does not produce an index on physical AI.

Ember’s beta, a two-person team, and a freshly created exclusion category form the strongest bull case: Risklytics could be Color Genomics for physical-AI insurance, building workflow infrastructure above carriers while incumbents dismiss a small segment that compounds with robotics deployment. That Known Exception stings because licensing is procedural, not scientific, and early carrier-appetite data could become valuable before Newfront reacts. The dossier still shows no defensibility, no insurance-domain founder history, no licensed transaction, and no carrier commitment, so the bull case currently requires access, distribution, and execution to arrive together. I would flip to Invest if Risklytics secures producer licensure, names at least two carriers that have bound affirmative physical-AI coverage, and compounds commission revenue above 20% month over month for four consecutive months.
