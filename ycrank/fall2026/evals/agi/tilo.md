# Tilo -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Pass  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Tilo’s strongest commercial case is a shared desktop for teams already automating multi-app work. The memo describes an early-alpha product with typed actions, shared app state, attributed edits, undo, and permission checks. For **building blocks of superintelligence**, the workload is desktop agents executing multi-app tasks. Tilo says separate virtual machines and screenshot-and-click controls add cost and latency. That identifies execution friction, while the case for a scarce input that would constrain AI deployment as demand grows remains unsupported. (The Idea, Key Risks)

For **industry enabled by superintelligence**, the potential new use is frequent, supervised multi-app work that would be too costly to run through isolated desktops. That prior cost barrier is a scenario assumption. Tilo reports about 1/29 of the cost and 90× the speed on one same-model task whose comparison run failed. The result leaves the relative cost per successful outcome unresolved, and the memo identifies no buyer or workflow newly able to operate at the required price or frequency. With both AGI paths unsupported, the rubric calls for Pass. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The common state-and-action model across roughly 50 listed native apps gives Tilo a specific integration advantage. Switching costs from accumulated integrations and edit history are plausible but untested. The founders have shipped an alpha, and the CTO has relevant patent-workflow automation experience. The memo documents no Tilo buyer trial or paid deployment. The company’s reported laptop test of 50 agents at about 60 MB each measures memory use, while hosted workspace pricing and delivery costs remain unknown. (Defensibility, Founders & Team, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Tilo could make high-frequency, human-supervised automation practical by letting agents and people act on the same live state, with permissions and undo handled by the workspace. Even with comparable models, buyers would still need app integrations and shared controls. The company’s concurrency test supports the feasibility of running many agents in one workspace, but leaves successful workflow completion, operator effort, and buyer adoption unmeasured. It falls short of the threshold for Watch. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paying team use Tilo to run a recurring multi-app workflow at a frequency it previously could not afford? A first paid deployment would move the action to Reach out if it showed that Tilo’s shared state and controls enabled that frequency, with a buyer-verified comparison of cost per successful outcome that includes integration, human review, retries, and recovery.
