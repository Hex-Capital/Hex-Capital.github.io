# Orgo -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

The strongest path is scarce complements. As more agents run recurring workflows in graphical software, reliable desktops that preserve files and session state could become more valuable. Orgo offers dedicated VMs with persistent disks and live takeover across Linux, Windows, and macOS. Orgo says building this infrastructure in-house can take months. An agency operator reported that a client saved about 12 hours preparing a Squarespace shop with an agent running on Orgo. That report shows a concrete use for the desktop, while the share of the savings attributable to Orgo and the scarcity of equivalent infrastructure remain unmeasured. The same report leaves the New Economies gate open because it gives neither the former cost per successful job nor evidence that the cost change brought a previously excluded buyer or use into the market. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

Orgo says it operates its own U.S. infrastructure. Persistent disks and custom templates could make migration costly, an inference the memo leaves untested by retention or migration data. Orgo reports more than 1,000 teams and thousands of production desktops, and the memo records a revenue signal without an amount. Repeat payment for workflows that specifically require persistent desktops remains unverified. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The founders have relevant software and commercial computer-use experience and have shipped a multi-OS service. Orgo’s status page reported 99.760% fleet uptime over 30 days, leaving reliability for unattended workloads to test. The running VM hour is a clear expansion unit. At the posted Small Linux rate of $0.023 per running hour, a hypothetical 730-hour month generates $16.79 in usage revenue before any discounts. Fleet costs, utilization, and support costs are unreported, leaving contribution per desktop unknown. (Founders & Team, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

AWS WorkSpaces already offers Linux and Windows agent desktops with AWS identity integration, and Daytona exposes graphical desktop control through its sandbox API. Buyers may be able to move the same recurring workflows to overlapping products at comparable reliability and cost, making Orgo’s persistence features widely available. The memo establishes product overlap while leaving workflow-level parity and switching behavior untested. The path stays open under Watch. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

When independent paying teams run the same recurring, stateful workflow on Orgo and their best available alternative using the same model, does Orgo deliver a sustained advantage in cost per successful run, including recovery and human intervention, that leads them to renew? A matched trial across billing cycles showing a material advantage and repeat payment would move the action to Reach out. Comparable results and easy migration would move it to Pass.
