# Orca Aerospace -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong product-market fit, Orca’s strongest growth path is to integrate its onboard software once per flight-software stack, then earn license revenue as operators deploy it across more satellites. The memo records a signed Orbital letter of intent worth up to $20 million, with proposed integration and per-satellite fees and first use planned for Orbital-1 in 2028. Orca describes an onboard planner and safety layer that address a recurring job for fleet operators abroad as well as domestically. The planned Reflex integration could make the core software portable across compatible fleets. The amount of adaptation across stacks remains uncertain. (The Idea; Defensibility; Market & Traction)

Orca acquires prospects through direct briefings, and first use requires engineering-led avionics integration and testing. Reuse could reduce that work for later satellites on the same stack. Within an existing account, onboard fault response gives operators a reason to keep using the software, and deploying it on more satellites could increase license spend. The proposed terms leave billing cadence, renewals, and the ability of fleet expansion to offset churn unresolved. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

The Orbital letter and Lyra concept study establish early buyer interest. Payments are undisclosed, and the memo gives neither realized sales nor a confirmed companywide pre-revenue status, so the commercial traction score uses the default. (Market & Traction; Key Facts)

In the reuse scenario, the integration fee would offset direct sales, integration, and initial qualification costs. Each additional satellite would add a license fee, with incremental costs for any vehicle-specific testing, telemetry review, and support Orca must provide. Reusing a qualified integration is a concrete way to improve contribution. Fee amounts, engineering effort, and any added capacity needs are unquantified, so positive contribution remains unproven. (The Idea; Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Countercase

Even fleets on the same stack may need substantial vehicle- and mission-specific qualification. The memo identifies memory and power limits, hardware-in-the-loop testing, and planned human telemetry review before command authority expands. Orbital must also deploy its planned fleet for the license opportunity to scale. These dependencies make slow, engineering-heavy rollouts plausible. The structural downside gate stays open because stack-level reuse is a credible lever and the memo leaves unresolved how much work must recur for each buyer or satellite. (Defensibility; Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a second fleet operator reach paid flight operations on the Reflex stack using the Orbital integration with bounded qualification work? A paid deployment with recorded engineering and support costs would test reuse and incremental contribution. Successful reuse would keep Orca on Watch pending enough commercial and retention evidence to meet the Reach out threshold. If even a compatible fleet requires a rebuild whose costs overwhelm the expected fees with no credible improvement lever, move to Pass. (Defensibility; Key Risks)
