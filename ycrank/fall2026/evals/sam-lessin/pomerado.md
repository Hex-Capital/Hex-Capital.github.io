# Pomerado -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Pomerado has built something tangible, but I cannot yet underwrite the check. Post-money valuation: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. Whether my money enables an experiment that otherwise cannot happen: undisclosed. YC membership establishes none of those answers. I am not assuming an auction, and I am not treating financing silence as evidence that capital is scarce. The dossier also names no funded hypothesis with a deadline and a measurable result. Those are my two largest criteria, and neither has affirmative evidence.

The technical architecture deserves a more careful reading than “another browser agent.” Pomerado uses models to explore, generate, test, and review an integration, then executes saved operations without further model requests. That directly addresses the expense and variability of reasoning through every execution. The problem is who captures the resulting savings. Browserbase and Browser Use already offer reusable scripts; Skyvern describes compilation and repair. With Pomerado’s core available under an MIT license, generating the script is unlikely to remain scarce. The potentially valuable business is maintaining authenticated workflows when websites change. That requires operating proof beyond the published architecture.

The strongest argument against passing is Akshay Manglik’s experience deploying enterprise agents at Scale AI, including Mayo Clinic deployments. He has encountered the gap between an agent demonstration and a functioning enterprise system. Together with the shipped integration tooling, that earns meaningful founder credit; Alan Ma’s enterprise data and AI strategy work adds context but supplies fewer operating receipts. This fits my pattern of backing people who connect systems that do not naturally cooperate. It does not yet establish a proprietary pool of customer access. The 1.1k GitHub stars, 100 forks, and October 16 Product Hunt #5 ranking demonstrate attention. The dossier documents no purchase, pilot, or retained workflow caused by that attention.

There is a plausible small, useful business here. At $50 a month for Premium, Pomerado offers up to 1,000 calls a day and 100 new integrations a month. But a price list is not revenue, and avoiding runtime model calls does not establish profitability. Browser execution, authentication failures, and repairs still consume resources; the terms acknowledge interruptions without a universal restoration deadline. I would track cash contribution per retained customer after those costs. Without margin, burn, or runway data, I cannot tell whether enterprise maintenance supports the business or consumes it. I give the missing economics their specified default rather than inventing either favorable margins or losses.

I pass at this evidence level. The concern is competitive replication without demonstrated ownership of the customer workflow, rather than an assumption that better models must make the product disappear. The strongest exceptions to my usual gates require actual conversion, retention, cash economics, or unusual proprietary access; launch attention does not establish that override. My single condition for reconsideration is a documented seed proposal below $10 million post-money funding a fixed-budget, six-month test in which three enterprise customers renew at prices that cover execution and maintenance costs. That would make the price and the truth this capital buys concrete.
