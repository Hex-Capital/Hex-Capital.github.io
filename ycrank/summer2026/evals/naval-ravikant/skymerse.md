# Skymerse -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Damian Szumski is a licensed flight dispatcher who owned Flightradar24’s API product, and Skymerse has no user-added network effects. That pairing is Skymerse’s signature: exceptional specific knowledge applied to a single-sided, permission-dependent aviation product. Skymerse may become valuable software. It does not yet have the architecture of a 10,000x network.

Stack Overflow is the high rung for specific knowledge: developers building for developers, with public artifacts preceding the equity story. Szumski shares the load-bearing mechanism—dispatcher license, aeronautics degree, LOT and Lufthansa Systems experience, then Flightradar24 API ownership. Skymerse diverges because Szumski has no visible technical writing or original public repositories; Skymerse’s GitHub contains four zero-star FAA SWIM forks. The knowledge is unusually specific. Its expression remains private and therefore harder to distinguish from industry experience alone.

Perplexity is the low rung on network compounding: a single-sided utility where one user does not automatically improve the next user’s product. Skymerse has the same weakness without Perplexity’s top-quartile permissionless distribution. LOT Polish Airlines adopting Notamify and 3,500 registered users validate utility, but an additional airline does not automatically make Skymerse more useful to LOT. Operator manuals and corrections might create a data flywheel, yet Skymerse provides no evidence that customers contribute exclusive data or that learning crosses operator boundaries.

Notamify’s $24.90 monthly Pro plan gives Skymerse code leverage at the product layer, but Skymerse distributes the larger product through demonstrations, pilots, integrations, and enterprise contracts. Over 18 months, the software can replicate cheaply while every airline still demands security review, workflow integration, official-data reconciliation, and human verification. Skymerse avoids the premature-SDR red flag today, but it contradicts my unstated pattern: distribution depends on gatekeepers rather than open source, organic media, or viral product pull. Code is leverage. Enterprise aviation procurement is permission.

Jeppesen’s 2026 Airflow launch shows a large aviation-software market, but it also shows that Skymerse is entering through a narrow operational wedge controlled by incumbents. Skymerse can move from NOTAM interpretation into dispatch, monitoring, cockpit support, cargo, medevac, and business aviation; those are adjacent products, not yet independent pivot vectors. The score flips upward if Skymerse demonstrates that its aeronautical model generalizes into an operator-independent decision layer across airlines, airports, and unmanned aviation. Without that, Skymerse remains vertical software whose first major pivot may require new certifications, integrations, and capital.

Notion is the useful founder parallel: Ivan Zhao’s obsession survived multiple product rebuilds because the founder possessed the problem before the market possessed the category. Szumski has a comparable lived-problem origin, and Skymerse has already processed more than three million NOTAMs rather than starting from a TAM slide. Intelligence and energy read strongly. Integrity and the 10-year-partner test remain unproven because Skymerse provides no history of prior-founder relationships, employee treatment, or completed ventures; silence is neutral, not absolution.

More than three million processed NOTAMs and Jeppesen’s direct product overlap produce a specific forecast: by July 2029, Boeing/Jeppesen will acquire Skymerse for its aeronautical model and airline integrations before Skymerse exceeds $10 million ARR. Skymerse’s deep founder knowledge can build a strategically useful product, while Skymerse’s low network compounding and permissioned distribution make acquisition geometry more likely than independent platform geometry.

LOT Polish Airlines is the strongest case against that forecast and against my neutral verdict: Skymerse already has a named production customer, thousands of daily Notamify users, 3,500 registrations, and proprietary operating context before a normal pre-seed company has traction. Anchorage Digital proves I can accept permissioned architecture when permission becomes the moat. The analogy breaks because Skymerse’s terms say its output cannot be authoritative for safety-critical decisions, while Jeppesen owns embedded distribution and can supply aeronautical data to AI agents. I write the check if three unrelated airlines adopt Skymerse as their planning-to-cockpit decision layer and proprietary correction data produces a measurable accuracy advantage over Jeppesen; until then, Skymerse is a strong founder-product-market fit trapped inside ordinary enterprise leverage.
