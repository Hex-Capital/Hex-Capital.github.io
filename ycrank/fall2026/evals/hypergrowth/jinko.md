# Jinko -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Reach out  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

Jinko’s strongest path is pay-as-you-go flight booking for developers building AI travel agents. Under the thesis’s strong product-market-fit assumption, the same REST API, MCP connector, and CLI can serve the recurring job of finding live fares, booking with traveler approval, and handling changes and refunds across markets. Jinko’s reported IATA and ARC accreditations let customers book through its credentials. That supports portability, while booking eligibility by market remains uncertain. (The Idea, Defensibility)

Exa Connect and agent plugins provide concrete discovery channels. Self-serve API keys, quickstarts, and published usage pricing give developers a path from discovery to billable use. One founder reports reaching live bookings in four hours, which supports fast activation in one case. The six-person team publicly launched the current API in November 2026. Realized revenue, paying-customer growth, and billable volume remain unverified, so initial traction receives the default. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

The 1% flight booking fee gives retained accounts a concrete way to spend more as their booking volumes grow. Checkout, changes, and refunds give developers a reason to keep the integration in place. Expansion could offset churn if customers’ agents generate more bookings over time, but repeat paid usage and net revenue retention remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility)

For an illustrative cost bridge, assume a $400 flight booking and 2,000 billable-eligible searches per booking at the account’s monthly ratio. Jinko would earn $4 on the booking and $2.50 on the 500 searches above the threshold. That $6.50 is revenue before attributable acquisition and onboarding, search infrastructure, ticketing, and post-booking support. Automated onboarding can limit labor, and excess-search billing can recover some high-query costs. Actual contribution across workloads remains uncertain. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Search-heavy agents could generate few bookings while consuming substantial search capacity, and changes and refunds could add support work to a thin booking fee. Supplier restrictions limit fare markups, and Etraveli’s overlapping flight MCP gives developers an alternative that could weaken retention. The structural downside gate clears because the documented integration and pricing provide credible scaling levers, with the cost of serving ordinary production accounts still unresolved. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a cohort of self-serve flight customers grow paid booking volume while generating positive incremental contribution? Account-level results across successive months showing booking fees, search-to-book ratios, and attributable acquisition, onboarding, infrastructure, ticketing, and servicing costs would answer it. Positive contribution as repeat booking volume grows would sustain Reach out. Weak contribution with a credible improvement path would move the action to Watch. Persistent losses driven by unavoidable per-booking work would trigger Pass.
