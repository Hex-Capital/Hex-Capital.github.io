# Canos -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

A strategic acquisition by an established veterinary diagnostics distributor is the strongest path. The memo documents that Antech distributes a blood-based canine cancer test, and Canos lists OncoTrack at $399 per test. A distributor could use its clinic network to sell repeat monitoring. Ownership could be worth a premium if exclusive control of Canos’s assay and longitudinal data gives the buyer a durable advantage in guiding treatment. That advantage and the buyer’s willingness to pay are inferences. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

Canos reports a longitudinal analysis of 117 dogs across 514 time points and says 10 clinics and mobile vets are onboarding. Its reported diagnostic benchmark is retrospective. A prospective rollout in paying clinics could measure whether repeat OncoTrack results change treatment decisions and sustain paid use. The memo gives no study budget, runway, or schedule. The founder-reported patent filing leaves ownership and transfer rights for the assay and data unverified. The memo supplies no independent fallback that could pay this holder. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Proceeds bridge.** With no deal terms supplied, use the illustrative $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, diluted from 5% to 3%, with no follow-ons. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in holder cash, or $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds at 3%. The hurdle is $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios below assume zero net debt and no other enterprise-to-equity adjustments, so enterprise value equals equity consideration. They assume all cash at close and pro rata allocation with no preference adjustment. Stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, and compensation are each assumed to be $0. The sale prices and dates are illustrative. The memo gives test prices but supplies no buyer economics or transaction values to support an exit price. (The Idea, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

Antech’s distribution of Nu.Q shows that a distributor can enter this category through a commercial agreement, and CanCan already offers sequencing-based canine cancer monitoring. PetDx stopped processing samples after raising $72 million, which adds caution about reaching commercial scale. If a distributor can get comparable clinical and commercial results through an existing product or a license, Canos may command too little to clear the proceeds hurdle. The memo leaves clinical equivalence and any buyer price ceiling unresolved, so the structural Pass override does not apply. (Defensibility, Market & Traction)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a prospective rollout in paying clinics demonstrate an advantage in repeat monitoring that a veterinary diagnostics distributor can value at a price that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Verified treatment decisions and paid repeat orders, tied to a distributor’s quantified economics for owning the assay and data, would move the action to Reach out if those economics support the required price.
