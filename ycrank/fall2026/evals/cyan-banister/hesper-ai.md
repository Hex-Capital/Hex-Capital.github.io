# Hesper AI -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. Fabricating a convincing accident photograph is easy; establishing what actually happened is the scarce service. I can imagine Hesper AI assembling verifiable event histories that let honest people resolve claims without weeks of document chasing. Getting there requires reliable access to evidence, dependable reconciliation of conflicting records, and insurers trusting the resulting files. Hesper’s document checks and timeline reconstruction point in that direction. But the dossier establishes an investigation workflow today, not a distinctive path toward that future. I should not award the company points for a destination I supplied.

The most encouraging artifact is an unnamed MGA putting the product into production. The founders report an **82% reduction in adjuster time per claim over two weeks**, alongside 10,000-plus claims investigated across customers ([founder announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nitish-badu-529b28165_five-months-ago-i-quit-my-job-to-build-something-activity-7505656789254823936-8xCr)). Entrusting live claim files to a young company costs operational effort and reputation. That matters more to me than the $1.5 million financing announcement. Still, these are founder-reported results without independently established retention or payment. The $15 million of potential fraud identified is neither Hesper revenue nor confirmed customer savings.

Why does the investigation queue exist? Someone must gather documents, obtain records, reconcile discrepancies, and write a defensible account. Hesper attacks that specific labor burden. This fits my attraction to unglamorous workflow repair, but the agency returned is narrower than the pitch might suggest: adjusters get time back; we do not yet know whether legitimate claimants get faster payment or greater ability to challenge a finding. Usage pricing could make investigation capacity accessible to smaller MGAs and TPAs. The introductory 200 free claims demonstrate an acquisition offer, not that customers have escaped an entrenched rent.

Dhariwal and Badu have relevant operating tuition. Building Nanonets Flow to a reported **$200,000 ARR** and helping Nanonets reach **$4 million ARR** are evidence of shipping and selling document automation; those revenues belong to their former employer ([YC profiles](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/hesper-ai)). Their April 2026 fraud-scoring product failed to sell, and they rebuilt around completed investigations. I like that correction: customers wanted work finished, not another score to interpret. It establishes commercial learning, though not a documented, longstanding obsession with insurance investigations.

The strongest argument against passing is that this is exactly how an essential operating layer can begin: two experienced founders discover the actual purchasing unit and win production access before outsiders understand the opportunity. Pre-revenue status would not stop me from backing that. What stops the argument here is control. Customers supply database licenses and access permissions; no proprietary enabling asset is established, and Shift and FRISS already address overlapping workflows. Restrictions on pooled customer training protect custody but also mean I cannot assume a shared-data advantage. The contract’s human approval requirement, retention controls, and training restrictions address important privacy concerns; they do not establish that false fraud indicators are harmless. Useful security work does not erase that gate.

I pass with my own money today. Hesper AI has more evidence of useful execution than of owning something the next insurance system must depend on. There is no documented leadership shell game or dependence on one closed model supplier, so I am not treating those red flags as present. Nor does the dossier supply the exceptional founder history and technical destination that would override my weak future-conviction score. **One verifiable event would reopen this decision: a named carrier renewing a paid deployment after an independent six-month audit shows materially faster investigations without increased false fraud referrals.** That would demonstrate durable trust in the completed work—the promising part of Hesper’s story.
