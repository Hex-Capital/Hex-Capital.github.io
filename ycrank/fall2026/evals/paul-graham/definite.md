# Definite -- Paul Graham Evaluation

Definite learned that an LOI can be a polite way of postponing a purchase. The useful evidence is what the founders did afterward: the September 28 interview describes moving from demos and compliance-product LOIs toward contract-first selling. That is a specific correction prompted by rejection. It earns credit for changing the route. But the dossier does not show a resulting customer deployment or purchase. I can see the founders revising their hypothesis; I cannot yet see them overcoming the obstacle ([founder interview](https://buildfromhere.substack.com/p/mazin-al-ani-on-selling-before-you)).

The technical idea has substance. Financial agents need consistent definitions of what the numbers mean. Definite proposes a shared model, deterministic calculations, and source-to-cell lineage beneath the agents. The earlier regulatory-reporting product also supplied a company-reported FinBalance result exceeding GPT-5 using DeepSeek V3 and a verification engine. That is more evidence than an architecture diagram, though an unreproduced benchmark from the earlier product deserves partial credit. It does not establish that the current system reconciles a customer's actual books correctly ([YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/RWQ-definite-turn-weeks-of-regulatory-reports-into-hours), [security documentation](https://www.usedefinite.com/security)).

The strongest argument for investing is that these three founders may already understand where financial automation breaks. Their work includes BitGo approval controls, Optiver trading systems, Boosted.ai financial-analysis agents, and Meta recommendations. Customer-specific connectors and definitions are precisely the tedious work that can conceal an opportunity. Workato's existing connectivity and semantic context make a broad platform claim less persuasive, but they need not prevent Definite from solving one finance team's problem exceptionally well. The missing connection is autobiographical: the dossier never establishes which recurring problem these founders encountered themselves and then built Definite to solve. Relevant employment earns my attention; it cannot supply that origin story ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/definite), [integrations](https://www.usedefinite.com/integrations), [Workato](https://www.workato.com/platform)).

Who needs the crude version enough to keep using it? Finance VPs are an identifiable buyer, but still a broad answer. Monthly contracts, no deployment fee, and a two-month opt-out lower the cost of trying Definite. They also make continued payment after the opt-out particularly informative. The dossier supplies no such customer behavior. Earlier LOIs failed to establish purchase commitment, and the current contract terms do not establish purchases either. I leave demand at the untested default rather than treating missing public revenue as rejection ([founder interview](https://buildfromhere.substack.com/p/mazin-al-ani-on-selling-before-you)).

Definite partially matches my unstated preference for technical founders building a useful underlying primitive. It has not demonstrated the numerical proof spike or developer appropriation that makes that pattern compelling. The founders are selling personally, so the red flag of delegating customer learning does not apply. Nor does their distribution depend on a promised major partner. Those are useful distinctions, but they do not satisfy my resourcefulness gate. I also checked the exceptions: a convincing working prototype or unmistakable user response could outweigh missing founder evidence. The historical benchmark and current product descriptions do not provide that override.

I would pass on Definite today. Its best evidence is that the founders can change their minds after a commercial disappointment. My largest uncertainty is whether they can turn the next disappointment into a working installation. One verifiable deployment would flip my decision: the founders resolve a named integration blocker within two weeks, and that finance customer subsequently pays to continue beyond the two-month opt-out. That would connect resourcefulness, technical delivery, and real need in a way this dossier currently cannot.
