# Kailash Labs -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, I can imagine a small factory owner buying a productive robot shift as casually as renting a forklift. Kailash Labs offers one piece of that arrangement today: an arm, gripper, vision and safety, with no customer capex and a seven-day pilot. But work backward. Someone must make unfamiliar tasks reliable, finance the equipment, and absorb failures without destroying the margin. The dossier does not yet connect Kailash’s specialized video models to those transitions. That connection is the investment question; I cannot award points for the future I supplied myself.

Shubham Sharma gives me a reason to investigate it. His NeurIPS 2022 spotlight predates this 2026 company, and his robot-learning research at KAIST, offline-RL work at Tsinghua and precision-welding motion planning put him close to the actual machinery. The shared autonomous-drone leadership at IIT Bombay adds evidence of building with other people. This is substantially more informative than an AI credential collected for a fundraising biography. What remains less established is commercial command: who turns a technically successful station into a deployment whose maintenance, financing and support economics work?

Why does a factory leave a station unstaffed? Is the constraint hiring, equipment cost, changeover time, or automation that breaks whenever the parts change? Kailash’s no-capex offer addresses the upfront equipment bill and could return operating flexibility to a manufacturer. That earns some credit for repairing an economic constraint. But the dossier identifies neither a quantified burden nor a gatekeeper extracting a rent. Formic already bundles equipment, deployment, maintenance and financing into recurring payments. Kailash needs to reveal which painful dependency it removes better; changing who carries the equipment cost does not establish that.

The strongest argument for writing a check is that this team has already put something into other people’s hands. Marlin was released on May 14, 2026; the October snapshot shows 934 monthly downloads and nine Hugging Face Spaces using it. Those integrations matter more to me than its 610 likes. Downloadable Apache-2.0 weights also offer an alternative to dependence on a closed model API. This brushes against my preference for concealed enabling infrastructure, but there is no demonstrated downstream dependency yet: neither the benchmark ranking nor the public CLI establishes that Marlin makes a robot station cheaper or more dependable. Developer activity belongs to the earlier product, and a removable pilot offer is not evidence that a factory has completed one.

The pre-revenue exception is tempting. **CosmicBrain AI** shared the physical-AI direction and technically grounded founder story, but its functioning robotics prototype and signed industrial proofs of concept supplied the bridge that is missing here. Kailash’s lack of revenue is not my objection. Its unresolved product connection is. The founder listings also deserve a direct answer: Sharma is identified as CEO, while Mete’s present involvement is unclear. That does not establish a leadership shell game or equal-control deadlock, but I cannot assume the advertised team is available to execute. My lean is **Neutral**: genuine technical roots, with the enabling-layer thesis still unproven. One verifiable result would change my lean toward investing: an independently referenceable factory completing a paid repeat deployment, with operating logs showing that Kailash’s own video models reduce intervention enough to cover equipment, maintenance and financing costs.
