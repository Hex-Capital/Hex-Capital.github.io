# Care GP -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Care GP’s 175% monthly usage growth over five consecutive months makes Care GP a revenue-velocity business with a weak index position: Care GP is already “just working” inside Australian GP operations, but 400+ clinic operators do not yet make it an index on healthcare administration. The 85% pilot conversion, zero churn, and reported profitability matter far more than the $187.7 billion TAM slide. The central question is whether Care GP owns a durable workflow rail or merely a fast-growing feature cluster.

Harvey is the high rung on revenue velocity because its growth became legible in dollars, with valuation moving from $3 billion to $5 billion to $8 billion as buying behavior compounded. Care GP shares the load-bearing mechanism: 20% weekly growth, 140% average monthly usage growth, 85% pilot conversion, and minimal outbound indicate pull rather than enterprise-pilot tourism. Care GP diverges because revenue remains undisclosed; profitability and three million processed documents are meaningful, but usage can conceal low prices, trial volume, or poor gross margins. Care GP sits immediately below Harvey’s rung until the curve is expressed in ARR and cohort retention.

Lyft’s low-rung failure mechanism was occupying a structurally inferior position in a market where the leader captured the network effects. Care GP is not yet trapped in that exact structure, but Care GP depends on Best Practice Premier while Heidi, Lyrebird, Parallel, and VoiceCare can expand into overlapping administrative workflows. No structural barrier or exclusive dataset is established, and a 5%–20% human edit rate limits autonomous lock-in. Care GP’s back-office wedge is distinct from Heidi’s clinician-documentation center, which keeps Care GP above the low rung, but the platform owner—not Care GP—currently controls the choke point.

Multimodal extraction and voice agents becoming deployable during 2024–2026 provide Care GP with a credible technological catalyst, even though the dossier labels that catalyst inferred rather than sourced. Over the next 18 months, Care GP can compound the opening by adding workflows, corrections, and practice-management integrations to every clinic relationship; Australian data residency and ISO 27001 reduce deployment friction in a regulated market. Care GP loses that compounding effect if Samantha and Veronica remain separable features that incumbent practice software can reproduce. The market has reopened, but Care GP has not yet shown that the window belongs uniquely to Care GP.

Melvin Chen interviewed more than 150 healthcare providers, launched Samantha, expanded into additional agents, and reached 400+ clinic operators, which is stronger shipping evidence than his Oxford, Goldman Sachs, or private-equity credentials. Care GP still lacks the Perplexity-style evidence I value most: named product changes shipped between specific customer conversations and the resulting usage response. Care GP’s founder score would move into the top tier if Chen showed a recurring weekly release cadence—for example, a clinic-requested workflow shipped within days and adopted across a measurable share of the 400 operators. Until then, Care GP demonstrates execution, but not yet the unusually fast iterative rhythm that independently triggers a check.

Color Genomics is the single-anchor parallel: build workflow infrastructure on top of a hard capability that has already become commodity rather than financing the hard science itself. Care GP follows that unstated pattern by applying available multimodal and voice models to document allocation, calls, billing, and practice-system workflows. The Color known exception therefore applies: healthcare exposure is acceptable when Care GP is selling operational infrastructure rather than taking biology risk. Care GP’s 5%–20% edit rate, compliance obligations, and Best Practice integration are difficult execution problems, but they are not three independent miracles; the remaining miracle is achieving reliable enough autonomy to become the operating layer.

Care GP’s 400+ operators and three million processed documents point to A$12–20 million ARR by July 2029, followed by an acquisition by Telstra Health for roughly A$120–250 million rather than emergence as a standalone global platform. Care GP’s usage velocity should support regional expansion and additional agents, while Care GP’s weak index position and dependence on Australian practice-management infrastructure make a strategic consolidation outcome more likely than a Stripe-like category default. Fast adoption plus limited control of distribution usually produces a valuable workflow asset, not an independent global rail.

Heidi’s nearly US$100 million of funding, Lyrebird’s A$12 million, Care GP’s undisclosed ARR, and Care GP’s 5%–20% edit rate form the strongest bear mechanism: funded substitutes absorb the back-office wedge before usage becomes durable revenue, while Best Practice captures the integration economics. That red flag stings because AI businesses with explosive growth ordinarily disclose revenue, and “three million documents” may be a false signal if processing is inexpensive. The evidence that presently wins is Care GP’s reported profitability, 85% pilot conversion, zero churn, organic demand, and growth from 150 to 400+ operators. I would flip to Pass if disclosure shows Care GP below A$1 million ARR at 400 operators or six-month clinic retention below 80%; absent that contradiction, I would invest my own money.
