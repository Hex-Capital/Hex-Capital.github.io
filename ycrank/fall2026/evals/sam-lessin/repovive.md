# Repovive -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Repovive has something worth underwriting: $100K in reported contracts across two formalization RL environments. The separate $200K coding-data LOI is a prospective order, and neither figure establishes cash collected. But the contracts suggest a concrete business beneath the chip-verification ambition: assemble scarce expert work into training environments that AI labs will buy. My first financing questions remain unanswered—post-money valuation, competing term sheets, and what my check specifically enables. Unknown terms are not evidence of an auction. They also give me no basis for calling this a cheap bet.

The potential dark pool is the relationship between Repovive’s reported 12,000 medalists and its lab customers. If that community reliably supplies difficult formalization work unavailable through ordinary recruiting, the company could own a useful translation layer between expert talent and model training. The contracts establish commercial demand for the environments; they do not establish that community access caused the orders. There is no documented exclusivity, contributor retention, or repeat purchasing. I give meaningful credit for contracted demand, limited credit for the proposed distribution advantage, and none for LinkedIn followers.

Shayan’s Lean-verified dataset work and Amir’s Gemini post-training experience make that translation layer more credible. These are relevant operating activities on both sides of the proposed bridge, beyond academic and employer names. This fits my unstated pattern of backing people who connect systems whose participants cannot easily do each other’s work. It does not establish that Repovive can verify production chips. The reported 8.3-percentage-point improvement is an internal result with publication pending, while specification hacking can make verifier acceptance diverge from useful correctness. Harmonic, Axiom, and &Truth also make it difficult to assume that formal-verification capability itself will remain scarce.

The strongest objection to passing is that Repovive may already be selling the scarce input while better-funded companies compete to build the eventual application. Two environment contracts at pre-seed are substantive evidence for that bull case. My known exceptions permit hard commercial proof to override missing financing gates, but the dossier does not yet show the complete conversion, retention, cash economics, or external validation that would justify that override. Nor does it supply a person-level financing structure with an explicit capital plan. I see no evidence of fundraising pressure or an equal-founder decision committee; those red flags should not be invented. The live concern is whether model progress expands demand for Repovive’s expert work or makes that work easier for customers to reproduce.

I pass on the current evidence. No use-of-proceeds plan identifies a dated experiment, and no delivery-cost, burn, or runway figures show whether the contracts support a business without another round. That is missing underwriting evidence, not proof of bad economics. My verifiable flip condition is a documented financing package below $10 million post-money that fully funds one dated test of whether repeat environment orders can bring Repovive to cash break-even. That would connect the price, the scarce asset, and the truth my money buys.
