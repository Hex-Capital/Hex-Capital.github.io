# Last Accounting Company -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Riki Karjalainen and Sami Laakkonen built petabyte-scale financial-data infrastructure at Token Terminal, while Last Accounting Company began onboarding customers roughly ten weeks after conception. Last Accounting Company is a Wizard-of-Oz accounting service whose highest signal is the service-with-software sleeve and whose lowest signal is entry-price differentiation: operationally interesting, financially illegible, but packaged inside the hottest possible YC wrapper—an AI agent for a large enterprise workflow. “AI accounting is uninvestible” is too strong; the operational point is that Last Accounting Company may be a good accounting business before it becomes a good venture security.

**Fin** is the high rung because manual assistants performed the difficult work before software absorbed the repeatable pieces. Last Accounting Company shares that load-bearing mechanism: Björn performs accounting processes, certified accountants approve the output, and a proprietary ledger records the workflow rather than merely placing an LLM over QuickBooks. Last Accounting Company diverges from Fin because ten weeks of operation is not enough Wizard-of-Oz time to reveal which exceptions dominate labor, whether fixed pricing survives review costs, or whether accounting-error liability makes automation asymptotic rather than compounding. The sleeve fit is real, but the manual process has not run long enough to earn the word “system.”

**Robinhood** exposes the low-rung failure mechanism: a consensus-priced YC darling only worked for Slow because entry preceded the competitive markups. Last Accounting Company supplies no round size, SAFE cap, tier-one pass, or pricing discount, so I cannot locate an equivalent early-entry exception; the appropriate absent-evidence default is punitive. Last Accounting Company also combines Artificial Intelligence, Fintech, and Finance while Truewind has raised more than $17 million, Pilot more than $150 million, and Balance already offers AI bookkeeping with human review. That is not illegibility—it is an increasingly legible factory category—and without a visibly mispriced security I have no underwriting edge.

Last Accounting Company’s fixed pricing, which reportedly does not scale with headcount or business size, determines whether the capital structure compounds over the next 18 months. If daily agent evaluations lower correction labor faster than customer complexity rises, Last Accounting Company can use service revenue to finance automation and become default-alive without five SaaS rounds; if certified-accountant review remains proportional to transactions and jurisdictions, fixed pricing converts growth into founder-drainage. No revenue, price, gross margin, retention, or accountant-utilization data is public, so the proprietary ledger is not yet evidence that the regatta has fewer tacks—it is merely the proposed boat.

Riki Karjalainen and Sami Laakkonen have credible financial-data depth, while Veikko Pere and Pietari Virkkunen encountered accounting workflows through BCG, Intera Partners, private equity, and venture capital. Last Accounting Company nevertheless originated approximately ten weeks before first onboarding, and Last Accounting Company offers no founder essays, repositories, or three-year trail of obsession that would distinguish missionary conviction from four capable people recognizing a timely market. The score flips an entire tier if Riki or Sami can show that Token Terminal’s accounting exceptions produced a documented, non-obvious ledger thesis years before YC; absent that artifact, relevant competence is not asymmetric conviction.

**John Fish / Bookshelved** had the asset on the cap table before the financing: a named creator, an existing audience, and distribution that survived independently of paid acquisition. Last Accounting Company has 430 company LinkedIn followers, roughly 1,000 followers for Veikko Pere, 965 for Pietari Virkkunen, and founder-led direct messages to Nordic operators. Last Accounting Company therefore has reach, not owned community; those followers neither constitute a pre-existing accounting audience nor demonstrate an acquisition channel that compounds without continued founder labor.

**Balance** already occupies the nearest market position—AI bookkeeping, human accountant review, and flat pricing—so my 36-month forecast is that by July 2029 Last Accounting Company will have abandoned the claim that it replaces both accounting software and the accounting firm, and will instead operate as a Nordic AI-enabled outsourced accounting service producing $2 million to $5 million in annualized revenue on third-party ledger rails. Last Accounting Company can reach that range because the Wizard-of-Oz sleeve supports a real service business; Last Accounting Company is unlikely to preserve proprietary-ledger replacement because migrations, jurisdiction-specific certifications, and review liability slow the software-style compounding required for a venture-scale Series A.

**Ro** is the objection that stings: regulatory complexity can be the venture moment rather than the capital-efficiency failure, and Last Accounting Company has four founders spanning financial infrastructure, finance, law, consulting, and investing who reached initial customers in ten weeks. Last Accounting Company could use Nordic jurisdictional fragmentation, proprietary audit trails, and accountant-reviewed agents exactly as Ro used prescription rails—turning operational burden into a moat competitors cannot cheaply replicate. The dossier currently names no supported jurisdictions, accountant certifications, error rate, migration result, revenue, or retention, while Balance already demonstrates that the service design is not unique. I would flip the verdict if Last Accounting Company reaches $1 million ARR across Finland and Sweden at greater than 60% gross margin without raising beyond the YC financing.
