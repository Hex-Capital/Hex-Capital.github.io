### Company case

Repeatable sales is the strongest path. Frontier labs and medical AI developers could license reusable test environments to check whether an agent completes a clinical workflow, including placing an order in a sandboxed EHR. The memo documents a public data-provider listing focused initially on nephrology. Archangel reports use by frontier labs and medical AI developers, with buyer identities, payment, and rollout details undisclosed. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

Assuming strong fit, buyers could rerun the same scenarios as they release new models. The first likely bottleneck is taking the same package from a sales lead to a paid rollout with another buyer. The job plausibly travels across markets, but license scope and differences in clinical workflows leave the required adaptation uncertain. The listing gives buyers a way to discover Archangel. The memo provides no buyer-level path to first value and payment, concrete budget or workflow commitment, repeat paid use, or measured delivery effort. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Archangel may have to assemble a new evaluation package for each account. Negotiated licenses, custom evaluations, and physician grading create plausible sources of per-account work, and the rights to reuse longitudinal records are undisclosed. A public EHR benchmark also gives labs an alternative for some testing and could limit what they pay for a standard package. The structural downside gate remains open because the memo provides no rollout evidence showing that custom work is unavoidable or that delivery costs rise in proportion to sales. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

Can a second independent medical AI developer buy and run the same nephrology evaluation package through the same sales and onboarding process?
