# Last Accounting Company -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

In 2032, the accounting system should not wait for month-end. Agents continuously gather evidence, reconcile transactions, update the ledger, and present humans with exceptions rather than chores. Last Accounting Company has a convincing leak from that future: dozens of Finnish companies have already passed more than $80 million through its proprietary general ledger. The missing causal chain is institutional. The dossier does not show how customers, auditors, or tax authorities move from accepting accountant-reviewed output to trusting an agent-native system of record.

The most valuable object here is not Björn. It is the ledger beneath him: evidence links, permissions, accounting controls, customer-separated databases, and MCP and REST interfaces that expose reviewed records to other software. That hits an important unstated pattern in my investing—an ordinary service can conceal infrastructure needed by a much larger downstream wave. This is materially stronger than a fashionable interface wrapped around OpenAI because Last Accounting Company controls the accounting record and workflow, although its dependence on OpenAI, Google Cloud, banks, and communications platforms still limits that control.

Customers are doing something costly and revealing: they are entrusting statutory books and substantial transaction volume to a four-person pre-seed company. I count that as real product behavior, but only cautiously. The $80 million figure is company-reported, and the dossier supplies no pricing, revenue, retention cohort, completed-close count, or rate of human correction. Last Accounting Company identifies a genuine mess—accounting firms, ledger software, payroll, banks, tax systems, and document channels—but it does not quantify the rent or show who gains meaningful new agency. “Less coordination” is useful; it is not yet the same as transferring occupational or economic control.

The team has unusually relevant ingredients. Riki Karjalainen and Sami Laakkonen built petabyte-scale financial-data infrastructure at Token Terminal; Veikko Pere brings finance and law; Pietari Virkkunen brings technology and investing experience. Their childhood relationship is evidence that this team was recruited through trust rather than assembled for a hot round. What I cannot find is the pre-company wound or independently built artifact that proves accounting was an obsession before agent accuracy became consensus. Four co-founders also make one red flag impossible to ignore: the dossier identifies no accountable CEO, control arrangement, or contractual tiebreaker.

The severe-data-access red flag is real as well. Gmail, bank, payroll, invoice, tax, and OAuth access create an unusually rich failure surface. Read-only Google grants, encryption, least privilege, portal-only approvals, and separated databases demonstrate that the founders recognize the problem, but controls described on a privacy page are not evidence of how the system behaves during compromise. My Known Exceptions do not erase this gate, and the pre-revenue-prototype exception is irrelevant: Last Accounting Company already claims live usage, so the next proof must be operational outcomes.

My 36-month forecast is that Last Accounting Company reaches €3 million–€8 million ARR by selling its ledger and agent infrastructure to Nordic accounting firms rather than fully eliminating them. The painful bear case is that Pilot, Truewind, Zeni, or established Nordic ledger vendors reproduce the automation while retaining distribution, regulatory trust, and human review, leaving this team operating a low-margin accounting service atop interchangeable models. The proprietary ledger and existing transaction volume keep that from being my base case, but they do not yet justify investing my own money. I would flip to Invest on one verifiable condition: ten paying customers must migrate off both their prior accounting firm and ledger, complete three consecutive statutory closes, and report the human-adjustment and filing-correction rates for that cohort.
