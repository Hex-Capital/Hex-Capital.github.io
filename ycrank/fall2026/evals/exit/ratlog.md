# RATLOG -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. An established European launch operator such as Arianespace could pay for RATLOG’s propulsion technology and control of its development if owning the design would save more time or cost than building a comparable system or licensing it. The memo documents Arianespace’s operating launch capability. RATLOG claims its engine uses 90% fewer parts and reports a prototype hot-fire in four months for under $30,000. The potential buyer benefit is an inference. The memo leaves production savings, buyer interest, and ownership of transferable propulsion rights unverified. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

The next relevant milestone is a funded, flight-ready engine test that demonstrates a manufacturing or refurbishment cost advantage a buyer can quantify. The prototype and €30,000 grant support early execution. The company targets a flight-ready engine in 2027 and an orbital launch in 2029 or 2030. The scale-up budget and launch site remain open. A sponsor buyout lacks evidence of predictable cash generation, a secondary lacks evidence of eligible buyer demand, an IPO lacks evidence of RATLOG’s public-market readiness and holder resale, and dividends or buybacks lack evidence of distributable cash. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*, *Key Facts*)

The proceeds bridge assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and zero follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, before investor taxes. The equity hurdle rises to $125 million at 2% ownership and falls to $62.5 million at 4%. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% case requires $88.3 million in enterprise value, before any transaction fees or senior claims. A $25 million sale in year eight returns $600,000 (1.2×). A $100 million sale returns $2.85 million (5.7×). The company’s claimed $400 million in signed launch reservations provides no usable sale-price evidence because deposits and cancellation terms are undisclosed. If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public-market scale remains out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, the holder realizes $0 after eight years and retains the shares. (*Market & Traction*, *Key Risks*)

### Countercase

Arianespace already flies constellation missions, SpaceX sells rideshare capacity, and Blue Origin has recovered a booster after an orbital mission. A strategic buyer could keep using its existing platform or license RATLOG’s design if it proves useful. The reported hot-fire establishes prototype progress, while the economic advantage of owning the technology remains unmeasured. This countercase weakens the reason to buy. The Pass gate remains untriggered because the memo establishes neither a structural barrier to sale nor a credible sale price that leaves the holder materially short. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Market & Traction*)

### Decisive diligence question

Can RATLOG show that owning its propulsion design would save a strategic buyer enough to justify a sale clearing the holder’s cash target? Evidence from a funded flight-ready engine test, paired with a buyer-backed estimate of savings and willingness to pay that supports at least $83.3 million in distributable equity at 3% ownership, would move the action to Reach out.
