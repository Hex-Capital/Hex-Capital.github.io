# Fabraix -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

The consequence case is real but inadequately specified. Manual red-teaming can take weeks and cost six figures, while customer-facing agents are gaining access to messages, files, permissions, and confidential data. Yet Fabraix gives me an $8.65 billion category estimate, not the cost pool it can collapse, the population affected, or a mechanism producing a 10–100x societal consequence. Preventing agent failures matters. The dossier does not establish that this particular product rewrites the economics of security rather than improving one testing workflow.

Nyx is more than a presentation-layer wrapper. It executes adaptive, multi-turn attacks across websites, files, messages, and SaaS replicas; the 10,000-strategy library and self-learning loop could become a proprietary production system. The best technical evidence is a 78% AgentHarm attack-success rate against 67% for GPT-5.6 Sol, plus a claimed first exploit in under one hour. That is measurable prototype evidence, but it is a 16% relative gain—not a 5–10x discontinuity—and the company has not shown transfer to blinded production environments. Finding failures in agents operated by dozens of Fortune 500 companies proves capability, not customer value or superiority over Virtue AI, HiddenLayer, Garak, or PyRIT.

The bull case is stronger than the revenue line suggests. More than 50,000 Playground attempts indicate genuine adversarial engagement, and Ahmed Aly brings fraud systems experience from Monzo and Two while Ibrahim Abdu has built agent infrastructure at Meta and production systems at Two and TradingHub. This combination fits the pattern I like: several disciplines assembled around an expensive workflow, with normal usage potentially enlarging a proprietary attack corpus. The known-exception case would be a small, unusually capable team entering a large existing value pool with a wedge that can expand into security infrastructure. Fabraix has the team and possible wedge; it has not yet demonstrated that the wedge escapes feature status.

The largest kill risk is benchmark transfer. Capital should first answer whether Nyx finds materially more exploitable production failures per dollar and per hour than both human teams and incumbent automated tools. Instead, the public record gives a company-reported benchmark, unspecified Fortune 500 findings, Product Hunt rankings, and SOC 2 Type II in progress. There is no declared pass/fail threshold, experiment cost, timeline, or stop-and-pivot branch. That is not a pre-seed traction penalty. It is missing evidence that the founders know which uncertainty can kill the company.

Platform bundling makes this omission dangerous. HiddenLayer has raised $56 million, while Check Point and Zscaler have already acquired overlapping products and can distribute testing inside broader security suites. Fabraix therefore needs an architectural advantage or learning loop that bundled vendors cannot reproduce. The red flag is not competition; it is competing through a better attack library and open-source attention without yet proving a nonlinear capability advantage. Seventy-one GitHub stars and 934 Product Hunt followers do not resolve that question.

Economically, automating a six-figure, weeks-long service could create attractive software margins, but custom pricing reveals no cost curve, utilization model, or customer savings. I pass today. One check could buy an interesting security option, but the uncapped consequence and technical discontinuity remain assertions. I would reverse this decision on one verifiable result: a blinded production bake-off across three named enterprises showing Nyx discovers at least five times as many confirmed exploitable failures per tester-hour as the best incumbent automated tool.
