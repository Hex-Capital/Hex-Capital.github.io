# Caveman -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Caveman’s possible secret is that teams will delegate inference optimization once savings can be demonstrated against their own workloads without degrading task success. That proposition is testable: compare provider-billed totals and completed tasks under controlled replay and production canaries. But the dossier identifies no prevailing belief that named peers reject. Edgee already combines compression and routing. Caveman’s evaluation workflow is a concrete product distinction; it has not yet established a contrarian technical truth.

The evidence also warns against confusing token reduction with economic improvement. JetBrains measured 8.5% fewer output tokens from the earlier skill while aggregate billed cost increased 11.6%, driven by an outlier. This does not refute the Cloud product launched on October 13. It does mean the earlier product cannot validate the new promise. Likewise, company-reported 89% token removal with 94% evidence retention does not establish unchanged task performance. Caveman’s decision to measure workloads and gate deployment addresses the right problem, but a method for discovering savings is not itself proof of savings.

The strongest argument for investing is distribution. Approximately 115.5K GitHub stars, 6.7K forks, and 20,000 users of the earlier browser extension constitute substantial developer attention for one founder. GreenPT’s integration supplies an actual route into another product. Caveman could use that attention to become the trusted authority on which agent configurations work economically. Yet the adoption spans earlier products, and the referral arrangement pays Caveman when users become paying GreenPT customers. Neither establishes repeatable acquisition economics for Caveman Cloud. More decisively, attention has not produced a bounded market Caveman controls: LiteLLM and Edgee already occupy the traffic path, while Apache-2.0 licensing makes the published implementation available to them.

Julius Brussee’s agent-infrastructure experience makes the product credible. Founding alignment remains an unanswered question rather than a demonstrated defect. A solo founder needs no invented co-founder history, and studying at Leiden does not establish that he works part-time. The dossier leaves current commitment and ownership unresolved, so I retain the stated default. Caveman fits the broader investment pattern of supplying the AI production stack, but that thematic fit cannot override the competitive red flag. The relevant exception would resemble OpenAI’s independently observable technical breakthrough despite uncertain monopoly economics: Caveman has a shipped optimization process, but no comparable independent result establishing a discontinuous capability.

The business model is coherent enough to investigate: consumption charges for events, agent minutes, and routing decisions, supplemented by $250 and $750 monthly governance packages. Those are prices, not revenue. There is no documented usage distribution or gross-margin structure supporting a fund-scale outcome. At $750 monthly, roughly 11,100 customers would produce $100 million in annual revenue; consumption could change that arithmetic substantially, but the dossier supplies no basis for estimating how much. My concern is not the absence of pre-seed revenue. It is that Caveman’s strongest demonstrated asset attracts developers while the proposed economic engine requires enterprises to entrust it with recurring infrastructure spend.

I pass. Caveman has earned attention before establishing a mechanism for retaining the value that attention might create. The bull case is serious: workload-specific evaluations could become an indispensable accumulated asset, turning an optimization tool into a durable control point. Today that switching-cost mechanism remains inferred. My single verifiable flip condition is a documented cohort in one defined customer segment renewing paid Cloud contracts because their accumulated workload evaluations make competing gateways materially costly to substitute. That would connect developer distribution to monopoly economics.
