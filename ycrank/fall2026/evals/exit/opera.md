# OpEra -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition by an ERP, warehouse-management, or supply-chain planning vendor. The memo documents a public launch and a one-site deployment offer. OpEra describes an optimizer that connects to existing systems and adapts to each site's operating rules. The founders report 38% lower delivery costs and 10% more on-time deliveries in their MIT retailer project. OpEra separately reports a 10.4% higher premium over market for a sugar producer and 54% fewer unfulfilled orders for a services marketplace. These results suggest a buyer could add valuable replanning capabilities to its existing products. The memo leaves paid deployment volumes and sustained production performance undisclosed. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

A paid rollout across multiple sites would test whether OpEra can reproduce savings while keeping integration and support costs manageable. The founders’ optimization experience and the proposed sequence of backtesting and live-data pilots make that milestone plausible. The cost, funding, and time required to reach it remain open. Transaction feasibility and independent alternatives stay at their defaults because the memo leaves IP and contract transfer rights, closing conditions, and alternative liquidity routes unaddressed. (The Idea, Founders & Team, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, with 5% ownership diluted to 3% and no follow-ons. The target is $2.5 million in gross cash within eight years, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at full pro rata participation. The sale cases below assume the holder is eligible to sell the entire stake for cash within eight years and receives 3% after net debt and deal adjustments, with no senior preference claims diverting proceeds. The memo provides no revenue, buyer pricing, or deal terms to anchor a realizable payout range, so proceeds stay at the default. (Key Facts, Market & Traction)

### Countercase

A planning vendor could combine its existing product with optimization APIs and build the overlapping features internally. Timefold already offers routing and workforce-scheduling APIs, and OpEra’s site-specific rules and data mappings could make each deployment costly to replicate. If those costs absorb much of the reported customer benefit, buying OpEra could offer too little advantage over building. The memo establishes neither a ready substitute for the full workflow nor a credible price range that falls materially short of the hurdle. The Pass gate therefore remains untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can OpEra show that a strategic buyer could create enough value from its product to support at least $83.3 million in distributable equity value? A paid multi-site rollout with customer-verified savings, measured integration and support costs, and a buyer-specific estimate of what owning the product would add would move the action to Reach out if the economics plausibly clear that hurdle.
