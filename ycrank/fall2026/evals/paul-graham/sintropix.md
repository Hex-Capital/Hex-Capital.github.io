# Sintropix -- Paul Graham Evaluation

The most interesting thing about Sintropix is that the founders started doing the accounting themselves. Manual bookkeeping in April 2026 preceded the proprietary ERP described in September. That is a sensible way to discover what to automate. Alfonso also contacted more than 100 accountants, and the first customers arrived through referrals. These founders have gone looking for work rather than waiting for a distribution partner to deliver it ([Diario Financiero, September 19](https://www.df.cl/df-mas/punto-de-partida/hermanos-ingenieros-son-los-nuevos-chilenos-en-y-combinator)).

The best resourcefulness evidence is the reported reduction in onboarding from 30 days to 24 hours through AI-assisted migration. Historical-data migration is a specific obstacle, and that is a substantial claimed improvement. But the evidence comes from a company LinkedIn snippet. We cannot see what broke, what the founders built, or how long building the workaround took. I give this meaningful partial credit. Separately, the reported ERP is evidence of shipped software, though the advertised 100-plus integrations establish neither accounting accuracy nor technical depth. The question is how much of the accounting the software actually performs.

Ten active clients across Chile, Mexico, Brazil, and the United States are the strongest signal here. Someone needs the crude version enough to work with a two-person company. Referrals strengthen that evidence because an existing relationship has produced another customer. But customers buying bookkeeping and customers repeatedly using an autonomous accounting product imply different businesses. Services start at 20 UF monthly; disclosed pricing does not establish collected revenue, margins, or software adoption. The claimed movement from a day-20 close to a day-1 close would be powerful evidence if attributable to the product rather than additional founder labor ([Sintropix services](https://sintropix.com/servicios)).

The strongest argument for investing is that this distinction may resolve in their favor precisely because they started with service. Country-specific accounting and migration are unpleasant work. Learning those details customer by customer could produce software that broader competitors handle badly. Alfonso’s payments-product experience and Borja’s computing background make that plausible, while being brothers removes the concern that they met merely to submit an application. Yet neither credentials nor kinship demonstrates execution under resistance. The dossier also never connects the December logistics concept to a falsified hypothesis, or explains what personal encounter originally led them into accounting. I cannot award evidence-driven persistence or an organic origin merely because the sequence could fit that story.

Sintropix does not show the launch dependence, unspecified major partnership, or premature sales delegation that would make me reject it outright. It does have some resemblance to my pattern of technical teams building a difficult substrate, but there is no independently measured proof spike or developer appropriation to establish that pattern. I also checked the working-prototype and validated-proof exceptions: the reported ERP warrants attention, but does not yet supply an override for the incomplete resourcefulness evidence. With Rillet and Campfire already offering overlapping workflows, the decisive question is whether these founders can turn their particular customers’ problems into reliable software faster than competitors can. I would hold off on a check. One verifiable result would change that: an auditable rollout showing a named reconciliation blocker, a founder-built fix shipped within two weeks, and five existing clients completing two consecutive closes without material posting errors or founder bookkeeping.
