# Libra Robotics -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path A — Scarce complements.** Libra’s strongest case is that reusable robotic skills could expand scarce installation capacity. Solar is the clearest entry point. The memo documents a live solar demonstration and reports that 58% of surveyed contractors working on data centers faced increased competition for skilled workers. The demonstration shows early technical progress in solar, while the survey establishes labor pressure in a potential expansion market. Libra claims its software can reuse skills such as alignment and fastening across jobs. If that reuse cuts setup and supervision across materially different sites, contractors could buy more accepted installation work per scarce crew-hour. That capacity gain remains unmeasured in the memo. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Sunstall’s reported provision of jobsites for testing is a meaningful seed-stage commitment of site access. Libra also reports signed solar agreements and data-center pilots. Agreement terms, realized revenue, and repeat orders remain undisclosed, and the reported 7+ GW consists of letters of intent. The founders bring complementary experience in field robotics deployment and reusable construction skills. A California-based team with this field-test profile plausibly fits the seed-led mandate. (Market & Traction, Key Risks, Key Facts, Founders & Team)

The relevant expansion unit is the next solar site. In a favorable scenario, reuse reduces site-specific engineering enough for installation fees to cover equipment, transport, setup, supervision, downtime, and rework at the contractor’s required quality. Libra’s claimed 10× reduction in task-to-deployment time has no independent cross-site benchmark in the memo and leaves cost per accepted installation unknown. Path B remains unproven because the memo describes automation of existing installation work and identifies no newly viable buyer or transaction. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

With ample capital and comparable models, competitors could assemble crews from commercially available hardware and compete for the same contractors. Cosmic already targets solar installation, and Sunstall has worked with both companies. Libra’s reported physical testbeds and tracker compatibility give it a starting point for qualification, but the memo leaves cross-site skill transfer, switching costs, and any durable advantage in accepted work unproven. The evidence leaves the outreach gate unmet. It also leaves a feasible seed-stage test of whether Libra can expand capacity and retain the economics. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Will a solar contractor award Libra paid work at a second, materially different site after benchmarking its first deployment against a credible alternative? Contractor-verified records should show more accepted installations per skilled labor-hour at equivalent quality and safety, including setup, supervision, and rework. A repeat award at a price that covers those delivery costs, with the buyer citing a Libra-specific qualification or deployment advantage, would support moving to Reach out if the updated score and confidence clear the outreach gate. If the comparison shows readily substitutable delivery and no capacity gain after rework, move to Pass. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)
