# TrueMile -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest path is a strategic acquisition by a carrier or virtual carrier that could deploy TrueMile’s fleet-wide planning across more trucks. The memo describes a founder-built solver operating in TrueMile’s own fleet and coordinating outbound loads, backhauls, and driver hours. TrueMile reports that one dispatcher can manage 9× as many trucks. If that capacity gain holds on a buyer’s fleet, owning the solver and its operating workflow could reduce dispatch costs. The memo provides no buyer interest or measured savings for a prospective acquirer. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

TrueMile reports 17% higher revenue per mile on AI-selected dry-van loads and 8% higher on refrigerated loads versus the market. Those figures suggest potential buyer value, but the memo gives no realized carrier margin or evidence that supports an acquisition price. TrueMile also reports $57,000 in freight booked over three weeks, and the memo records solicitation for two dedicated-lane partnerships. A paid multi-truck pilot on recurring lanes is a plausible next milestone. Signed lane commitments, the capital needed to run the pilot, and the working capital needed to expand remain unverified. A sale of the carrier and solver is plausible at seed, with software and data rights, contract consents, and treatment of carrier liabilities still open. The memo gives no evidence of an independent route that would pay this holder. (Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and a $2.5 million gross cash objective within eight years. The sale scenarios assume $5 million in net debt and other deal deductions, eligibility to sell the entire stake, and an all-cash closing within the horizon with no preference or escrow reducing the holder’s pro rata share. The enterprise values are illustrative assumptions.

| Scenario | Enterprise value | Distributable equity | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modest sale | $50M | $45M | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M | $95M | $2.85M (5.7×) |
| Prolonged private operation with cash reinvested | — | No distribution | $0 |

The target requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity, or $88.3 million in enterprise value under the assumed deductions. Even in the strong sale, eligibility to sell only 75% of the stake would reduce cash at closing to about $2.14 million. The memo leaves a specific gap for Watch: whether repeatable carrier-level gains can justify a price that clears the holder’s hurdle.

### Countercase

A carrier could assemble dispatch, route planning, and freight matching from existing providers. Manual approval of every load, disruptions to linked routes, and carrier liability could erode the savings or make the operating business less attractive to acquire. The reported revenue-per-mile gains apply to selected loads, leaving open whether fleet-wide margins improve after those costs. This weakens the case for an acquisition premium, but the memo establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a credible price ceiling below the proceeds hurdle. The Pass gate is therefore untriggered. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can TrueMile demonstrate recurring-lane gains large enough to support a strategic sale above the roughly $88 million enterprise-value hurdle? A paid multi-truck pilot against comparable dispatch operations would need to show sustained contribution-margin gains after empty miles, manual dispatch, and carrier costs. Gains that a strategic buyer could plausibly value above the hurdle would move the action to Reach out. A repeatable result that supports materially lower proceeds even under favorable sale terms would move it to Pass.
