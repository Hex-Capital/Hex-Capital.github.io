# Outworld Labs -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Outworld Labs is asking the right question: how closely does the simulator match the machine? The valuable discovery would be a calibration system that improves with every physical deployment. A new physics engine alone remains reproducible software. The investment turns on whether measuring reality creates an asset competitors cannot cheaply reconstruct.

The founders have relevant knowledge acquired through research. Matei Gardea worked in robotics at BAIR; Daniel Grant’s work includes legged-robot control and computational fluid dynamics, alongside research at BAIR, Google DeepMind, NASA, and Oxford. That is substantive proximity to the problem. It earns more credit than degrees alone. But the dossier does not identify a dated result demonstrating that either founder has solved dexterous contact simulation or transferred those policies successfully onto hardware. I see domain experience; exceptional execution remains unestablished.

The strongest argument for investing is that Outworld’s measurement-and-fitting approach could produce precisely the physical-world knowledge that generic coding agents cannot supply. Its founder-reported joint first place at the September 2026 YC Group 1 Showcase gives some evidence that the team can demonstrate something compelling. I have made exceptions for products before revenue or observable network effects when the prototype and founder behavior carried the conviction. That exception deserves consideration here. It does not yet carry the decision: there are no numerical transfer results, exclusive calibration datasets, or customer deployments establishing what survives a competitor copying the engine. Free MuJoCo and NVIDIA’s Isaac Sim make that distinction economically consequential.

Outworld fits my recent interest in infrastructure bottlenecks beneath physical AI. It has not yet demonstrated the other mechanism I look for: participants making the system more valuable for one another. Calibration data could eventually do that, but the dossier establishes neither reuse rights nor improvement across customers. Foundation-model training and evaluation identify real workflows; they do not yet establish a large adjacent market unlocked by this wedge. Commercial simulator access is also an inferred business model, with no disclosed payer, price, or revenue. Those are uncertainties receiving their defaults, rather than evidence against a pre-seed team. The dossier likewise supplies no adverse evidence about founder integrity, artificial financing deadlines, or dependence on investor reassurance.

I pass today. The founders’ relevant research is the strongest signal; the missing compounding asset is the deciding gap. My one verifiable flip condition is a customer-validated benchmark showing that calibration learned from prior deployments materially improves transfer on a new customer’s hardware, with contractual rights allowing Outworld to retain and reuse that learning. That would demonstrate that each deployment buys the company something the next competitor cannot obtain merely by writing code.
