# Blinka -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Blinka’s possible secret is that foreign-operated US businesses constitute a systematically misserved banking market that can be served profitably. Juan Pablo’s account closure establishes the problem. It does not establish that competitors misunderstand it: exclusion might reflect poor service, unfavorable compliance economics, or both. The proposition would fail if reliably serving these customers costs more than they will pay. Blinka has not disclosed that test or identified a prevailing belief its results overturn. I assign the secret the absent-evidence default, without treating an incomplete dossier as a refutation.

The more immediate difficulty is ownership of the opportunity. Blinka combines partner-bank accounts, payments, cards, treasury, invoicing, and accounting. Slash already offers much of that bundle; Mercury and Airwallex approach the same customer’s financial needs from adjacent positions. “US companies operated abroad” identifies a customer segment, but geography alone does not establish a monopoly mechanism. Dependence on Portage Bank, Third National, and Mural Pay also leaves important capabilities outside Blinka’s control. This is affirmative competitive pressure, rather than merely an undisclosed moat.

The strongest objection to passing is the customer reportedly managing more than 200 entities through one portal. That suggests an operating problem sufficiently complicated to support a substantial business. Combined with company-reported payment volume exceeding $10 million in two months, it makes dismissing Blinka as an interchangeable interface premature. But managing complexity creates durable value capture only if customers cannot readily reproduce the arrangement elsewhere. There is no retention, migration, exclusive-access, or proprietary-technology evidence establishing that. My exceptions for demonstrated customer behavior require that behavior to remove the particular risk being overridden. Transactions establish use; they do not yet establish captivity or pricing power. Nor is there an independently demonstrated technical breakthrough warranting a separate override.

The founding structure is considerably more encouraging. Juan Pablo and Sebastián are brothers who collaborated on business-credit software before this product. That is substantive shared history, rather than a team assembled around a fundraising opportunity. Their legal and engineering backgrounds also fit the work. Blinka resembles my observed preference for financial businesses formed around an operator’s own problem, although the dossier establishes neither a proprietary founder-discovery network nor a previous operating exit. Full-time status, ownership, compensation, and responsibility for core engineering remain unverified. Those omissions are not evidence of the part-time-founder or investor-dominated-capitalization red flags.

Distribution has begun, but its economics remain unresolved. Twenty-five clients before official launch and named customers including Bricksave and Dapta are meaningful at this stage; the 150 account-opening figures should not be counted as 150 active customers. Founder conversations, LegalOS, and Mexico Tech Week provide identifiable acquisition routes, without establishing repeatability or acquisition cost. Separately, service fees are authorized, but pricing, net revenue, partner costs, and margins are unavailable. Payment volume cannot supply the missing arithmetic for a power-law outcome. The earlier lending products are historical iterations, not evidence that the current plan requires several unrelated businesses to succeed.

I pass on investing my own money today. Blinka has a credible founding relationship and evidence of customer demand, but its weakest primary gate is the mechanism for retaining the value it creates. The inconsistent public eligibility descriptions also need reconciliation; they do not establish unlawful activity or an enforcement failure. One verifiable result would change my judgment: a documented cohort of ten multi-entity customers renewing at a fee premium after testing a functionally comparable competing service. That would turn the strongest anecdote into evidence that Blinka controls something customers cannot easily replace.
