# Limnion -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition by an observability or incident-response platform is Limnion’s strongest supported route. Siglake gives agents read-only query access to telemetry stored in customers’ own Iceberg tables. Limnion says high-volume telemetry is costly and investigation agents need direct access. A platform could buy Siglake to add a self-hosted storage option and bring its engineering team in-house. Snowflake’s acquisition of Observe and ServiceNow’s acquisition of founder Todd Persen’s prior company, Era Software, establish adjacent buyer activity. The memo provides no measured customer savings, buyer interest, or transaction prices that would support a Limnion valuation. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Founders & Team)

A paid production deployment that passes the 72-hour endurance test, demonstrates multi-replica failover and tenant isolation, and measures telemetry costs against the incumbent would strengthen the acquisition case. Limnion’s own 1 TB test and bounded single-node smoke tests show progress toward that milestone. The failed endurance test leaves meaningful engineering work, and the reported $500,000 convertible note leaves runway and the cost of reaching production qualification uncertain. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. The holder needs $2.5 million in cash within eight years for a 5× gross return, requiring about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3% pro rata. The sale cases below are illustrative sensitivities. Both assume a sale of 100% of the equity for cash paid within eight years, an eligible holder, and pro rata distribution after the stated net debt and deal adjustments. Actual note conversion, preferences, and participation rights are unknown. (Market & Traction)

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Holder cash |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $50M enterprise value less $5M in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $45M for equity | $1.35M (2.7×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in net debt and deal adjustments leaves $90M for equity | $2.7M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Continued operation with cash reinvested and no funded payout or eligible share sale | $0 (0×) |

The strong case clears the hurdle under those assumptions. The memo leaves the achievable sale price, closing and participation terms, and any independent liquidity route unevidenced. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An incumbent could ship the relevant capability internally or reuse Siglake’s Apache-2.0 code. Observe already combines OpenTelemetry ingestion with customer-owned Iceberg storage, and the memo gives no evidence of production adoption that would make Siglake harder to replace. That weakens the case for an acquisition premium. The evidence leaves the Pass gate untriggered because it establishes neither a structural closing barrier nor a supported ceiling on sale proceeds. (Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

What would make an observability platform pay enough to acquire Siglake for this holder to receive $2.5 million within eight years? A buyer-specific case grounded in measured savings from a paid production deployment, with a defensible price range that clears the hurdle after deal adjustments and the payout waterfall, would move the action to Reach out.
