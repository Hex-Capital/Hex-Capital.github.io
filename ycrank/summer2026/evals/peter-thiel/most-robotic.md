# Most Robotic -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

What important truth does Most Robotic know that Genesis AI, Skild AI, FieldAI, and NVIDIA do not? The dossier supplies no answer. “Research lab for physical AI” is a category label, not a falsifiable secret: there is no disputed proposition, target task, model architecture, embodiment, dataset, benchmark, or experiment that could prove the founders wrong. NVIDIA’s May 2026 release of open world models and action-generation tools makes the category timely, but timing without a secret is consensus.

The founders are the strongest part of the company. Two MIT computer scientists with robotics research and collective experience at NASA JPL, SpaceX, and AWS have credible technical proximity to the problem. More importantly, they have been friends since middle school; this is a real foundation rather than a team assembled for YC. But shared history does not establish that both are full-time, economically exposed, or performing all core engineering in-house. The evidence supports alignment, not yet total commitment.

Where is the small market Most Robotic can monopolize? None is named. Skild AI has raised $2.215 billion, reported approximately $30 million in 2025 live revenue, and trains across multiple robot types. FieldAI reports deployments in hundreds of industrial environments. Genesis AI has disclosed a foundation model, a simulation-and-real-data engine, and a dexterous hand. Most Robotic discloses no customer, robot, data advantage, or technical result. This is not merely missing traction at pre-seed; it is explicit entry into a crowded category without a visible wedge or monopoly mechanism.

The strongest bull case is that secrecy conceals the substance: a long-aligned, unusually technical team may already possess a natural secret whose disclosure would compromise it. A genuine zero-to-one result could override today’s competitive objection, just as OpenAI’s verified technical event mattered despite uncertain monopoly durability. The material difference is proof. Most Robotic has disclosed no demonstration, benchmark, independent validation, dataset, or repeated use. Stealth can protect a secret, but it cannot serve as evidence that one exists.

The business remains indefinite downstream. There is no dominant product, price, buyer, gross-margin structure, or expansion path from which a power-law outcome can be calculated. Distribution consists only of `founders@mostrobotic.com`; no acquisition channel, sales owner, implementation burden, or sales cycle is known. My 36-month base case would therefore be a pivot from a general physical-AI laboratory into a narrower robotics-data or evaluation tool, rather than the emergence of an independent foundation-model company.

I pass, but one verifiable event would flip the judgment to investigation: a working system must outperform Skild AI, FieldAI, or NVIDIA’s open stack by at least 10× on a named industrial task and produce repeat paid use from one narrowly defined customer segment. That would simultaneously reveal the secret and the beachhead. Until then, Most Robotic is talented founders inside a fashionable frontier—founder signal without company proof.
