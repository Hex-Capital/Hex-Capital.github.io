# Tsenta -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Nearly 8,000 users, up from roughly 1,100 in two months, makes Tsenta a high-velocity consumer product trapped in a low-leverage distribution position. Tsenta’s analytical signature is real usage acceleration without evidence that Tsenta becomes an index on AI-mediated hiring: applicants can arrive quickly, but Jobright, Simplify, Teal, or the next Claude Code-built entrant can offer the same workflow. The curve registers; the structure does not yet.

**Harvey** is the high rung because its revenue curve made “consensus pays” rational: customers were buying, valuations moved from $3B to $5B to $8B, and the signal was denominated in dollars. Tsenta shares the important mechanism—usage rose more than 7x in approximately two months—but Tsenta diverges where the analogy becomes load-bearing: no paid conversion, retention, revenue, or application-to-interview cohort is disclosed. Interviews reportedly reached Goldman Sachs, Nvidia, and Netflix, yet Tsenta cannot verify those outcomes. Tsenta is closer to “just working” than most pre-seed AI products, but 8,000 registrations are not Harvey-style buying behavior.

**Lyft** is the low rung because entering a networked market behind a stronger incumbent can leave a good product structurally stuck at #2. That failure mechanism appears differently at Tsenta: Jobright already finds, customizes, and submits applications, while Simplify owns a visible Chrome workflow and Teal spans the broader career lifecycle. Tsenta has no exclusive data, documented switching cost, or structural barrier, and its 50,000-career-page coverage can be reproduced because Agnay Srivastava says the MVP was generated with Claude Code in three months. Tsenta’s web, mobile, messaging, Chrome, and MCP/CLI surfaces are product breadth, not an index position; Tsenta must repeatedly win users against category peers rather than automatically compound with online hiring.

Agentic browser automation and modern coding agents created Tsenta’s credible recent catalyst: two student founders could automate forms across 15+ ATS platforms in three months, which was materially harder before the current agent stack. Over the next 18 months, Tsenta’s catalyst compounds only if execution reliability across those ATS platforms improves faster than competitors can copy it; otherwise the same catalyst lowers entry barriers for Jobright-style substitutes. LinkedIn’s prohibition on scraping and automation and Indeed’s restrictions on outside automation further prevent Tsenta from owning the workflow path. This is a real re-opening, but it re-opened the market for everyone.

Agnay Srivastava and Pulkit Gupta shipped Tsenta from a dorm, supported multiple client surfaces, and rewrote the earlier autojobs.me architecture after users said the local-browser installation “looks like a virus.” Tsenta therefore has better evidence of iterative shipping than the founders’ internship credentials or zero-star GitHub repositories suggest. The counterfactual that moves Tsenta into the top founder-shipping tier is a documented cadence showing that Agnay and Pulkit repeatedly converted user feedback into higher completion, interview, and paid-retention rates—not merely additional interfaces. Without that loop, Tsenta demonstrates fast construction but not yet the Perplexity pattern of shipping the exact thing that changes adoption between conversations.

**Color Genomics** is the useful single-anchor parallel: Color captured workflow value on top of commodity sequencing rather than requiring new biology. Tsenta similarly builds workflow on top of already-valid LLMs, browser automation, and existing ATS infrastructure; Tsenta does not require a scientific breakthrough, so the science-project red flag does not apply. The Color exception only partially rescues the architecture, however, because Tsenta still needs three nontechnical dependencies to hold simultaneously: platform tolerance, user trust with resumes and email OTPs, and sufficiently accurate matching without direct employment-outcome data. The core technology needs one miracle at most, but the business needs several counterparties not to close the door.

$19 for 600 applications is why I predict that by July 2029 Tsenta will have pivoted from mass consumer auto-apply into a B2B university-career-center or outplacement workflow and will remain below $5 million ARR. Tsenta’s usage curve can create a meaningful applicant funnel, but low consumer willingness to pay, weak outcome attribution, and platform-policy exposure make the current high-volume model difficult to compound. A B2B buyer could value administration and placement analytics even where individual applicants will not, making a workflow pivot more probable than Tsenta becoming the Stripe of job applications.

50,000+ monitored career pages, 15+ ATS integrations, a 7x user increase, and a founder-led architectural rewrite form the strongest opposing case: Tsenta may be the Perplexity-style cold-start product where shipping velocity outruns an apparently crowded market, while application-response data eventually becomes the defensible matching layer. Tsenta’s founders also lived the problem through more than 3,000 applications, which is stronger founder-market fit than a generic “AI for jobs” pitch. The bull case still loses today because Tsenta lacks direct employment outcomes, public revenue velocity, exclusive data rights, and protection from LinkedIn or Indeed policy changes. I would flip to Invest if Tsenta demonstrates at least six consecutive months of paid revenue growth above 20% month over month with independently measurable interview outcomes.
