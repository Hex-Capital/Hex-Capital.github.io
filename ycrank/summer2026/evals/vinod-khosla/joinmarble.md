# Marble -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Marble shows a useful product, not yet a consequential option. One customer reports 40% lower food waste, eliminated stockouts, and more than 30 manager hours saved weekly. Those are unusually informative pre-seed results. But the dossier quantifies neither the restaurant cost pool being displaced nor a mechanism for changing it by an order of magnitude. A $6.6 billion software TAM describes vendor spending; it does not establish a 100x societal consequence.

The technical claim is also ahead of its proof. Computer-vision inventory capture and location-specific memory could remove recurring manual work, but 99% counting accuracy and 97% forecast accuracy are company-reported without sample sizes, protocols, or error distributions. Marble provides no head-to-head benchmark against Nory, Restaurant365, or manual counting. Live use across A&W, The Kati Roll Company, Sophie’s Cuban, Kokomo NYC, HolyShred, and Dead Letter No. 9 demonstrates that the system operates outside a demo. It does not demonstrate a 5–10x discontinuity.

The best bull case is execution, not science. Aakar Khanna has managed family restaurants and worked in restaurant finance and operations; Arjun Chaliha led AI/ML engineering for electronic trading. That is a sensible pairing around an expensive, fragmented workflow. Every count, correction, and order could create a proprietary learning loop, and the six named deployments suggest the founders can sell before building a large organization. Execution-heavy software can become valuable without a scientific breakthrough.

But the known exception for an initially incremental merchant tool required an enormous adjacent value pool and a credible route from wedge to infrastructure. Marble has not shown that route. Nory already combines forecasting, inventory, workforce, payroll, and autonomous ordering; Restaurant365 serves more than 50,000 locations with over 100 POS integrations. Marble has disclosed neither an integration catalog nor evidence that its location-specific context compounds across customers. An incumbent can add computer vision. A proprietary learning system is possible, but possibility earns no moat credit.

Capital should now attack the largest kill risk: whether better capture produces durable restaurant economics after integration and support costs. The dossier supplies no ranked risk, experimental threshold, cost, stop condition, pricing, contract structure, or gross-margin model. My 36-month base case is therefore $3–7 million in ARR as a useful inventory-and-ordering layer, still competing for system-of-record status rather than becoming the autonomous back office.

I pass today. The single flip condition is a controlled 90-day deployment across at least 50 locations, benchmarked against an incumbent workflow, showing at least 5x lower inventory-counting labor, a sustained 300-basis-point restaurant margin improvement, and paid renewals. That would convert attractive testimonials into a technical and economic discontinuity.
