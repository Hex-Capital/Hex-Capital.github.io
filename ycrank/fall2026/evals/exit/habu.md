# HABU -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

The strongest route is a strategic acquisition of HABU’s development platform by a multifamily sponsor seeking a repeatable way to originate and underwrite infill projects. The memo reports that HABU put its first Phoenix site under contract and records $1.126 million in corporate securities sold. HABU reports a working screening and underwriting module. Its targets of five times as many projects per manager and up to 20% faster delivery remain unvalidated on a completed project. A financed project that reaches stabilization with measured labor savings, delivery time, and net fee and promote economics would give a buyer a basis for valuing the platform. The corporate raise and site contract support progress toward that milestone. Construction debt, project equity, and the capital and time required to reach stabilization remain unverified. The CEO’s reported investor interest concerns project checks. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

The shared benchmark assumes a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million, or about $83.3 million in distributable equity value at 3%. The sale scenarios below assume a corporate acquisition within eight years, with the holder eligible to sell the full stake for cash at closing and no senior preferences diverting proceeds. Enterprise value reflects HABU’s retained project economics after project-level obligations. The deductions shown are assumed corporate net debt and deal adjustments.

| Scenario | Proceeds bridge | Gross cash to holder |
|---|---|---|
| Modest sale | $40M enterprise value less $5M in deductions leaves $35M in distributable equity | $1.05M (2.1×) |
| Strong sale | $100M enterprise value less $10M in deductions leaves $90M in distributable equity | $2.70M (5.4×) |
| Prolonged private | Cash is reinvested throughout the eight-year horizon, with no funded payout or eligible share purchase | $0 |

These are illustrative sensitivities. The memo supplies no basis for either sale price and leaves corporate transfer rights, the payout waterfall, the eventual sale fraction, and closing timing unverified. Project fees and promotes describe potential company cash flows. The memo supplies no evidence of a funded payout or another independent route to cash for the corporate holder. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An incumbent could reproduce much of HABU’s first-pass screening using public parcel data and its own underwriting history. HABU still gathers local approval knowledge manually, and every sponsored project requires separate financing. Phoenix’s reported 10.8% multifamily vacancy and declining asking rents also put lease-up and promote income at risk. Together, those factors could leave a buyer valuing HABU as a local developer with a project pipeline and limited reason to pay for the technology. The memo establishes neither a structural closing block nor a supported sale range that falls materially short under the strongest credible route, so the Pass gate does not trigger. (Defensibility, The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a strategic buyer underwrite at least $83.3 million in distributable corporate equity value within eight years using HABU’s measured project economics? A buyer’s indicative valuation tied to verified delivery savings and retained fees and promotes from a stabilized project, with a proceeds bridge showing at least $2.5 million in cash to the benchmark holder, would change the action to Reach out.
