# Click -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

The proposed post-money valuation, competing-term-sheet count, and status of this as the first scarce check are all absent. That earns Click 6/28, not because silence proves an auction, but because I cannot tell whether I am buying an unfashionable belief or paying YC consensus pricing for “infrastructure for agents.” Exa has raised $357 million, Parallel $230 million, Tavily $25 million, and Linkup $10 million. The category is emphatically funded; the company still has to show why this particular round is scarce.

The product aggregates Exa, Crustdata, Google Maps, Reddit, X, YouTube, Apify, and other providers behind one MCP installation. That is useful packaging, but the dossier names no finite experiment the round will fund, no deadline, and no result that dramatically changes value, so the venture-moment score remains the 6/24 default. “Add connectors and acquire users” would be a capital plan, not a truth-revealing test. The sharper hypothesis might be that multi-source routing creates materially better agent outcomes than native OpenAI search, but Click has not supplied a benchmark, customer workflow, or dated test of it.

Aditya Asgaonkar is the strongest part of the case. Ethereum Foundation research, distributed-systems publications, and work on browser-agent context engineering are genuinely relevant technical receipts, not a résumé assembled around this month’s market. But Click joins two sides: agents seeking information and external providers controlling access, pricing, and permissible use. The dossier does not show that Asgaonkar has shipped a scaled product or operated commercially on both sides of that boundary. I give him 5/10: above a biography-only case, below evidence of the outsider-insider translation layer the business requires.

The dark pool is missing. Click has 157 company followers; Asgaonkar has roughly 4,000 followers on X and 2,566 on LinkedIn; the paid plan is listed at $49 per month for 500 credits. None of those facts shows a purchase, retained workflow, referral, or even repeat prototype use. Meanwhile the privacy statement confirms that requests flow through upstream providers, and the dossier identifies no proprietary dataset or structural barrier. This is rented access assembled into a convenient interface, with native OpenAI search and remote MCP support capable of compressing the interface layer. That is 4/20, below the missing-evidence default because the disclosed architecture affirmatively exposes the dependency.

The strongest bull case is that a solo protocol researcher has entered at exactly the moment MCP crossed 10,000 active public servers, and breadth across professional, financial, mapping, travel, and social data could become a routing dataset before host platforms reproduce every vertical integration. Click has also changed positioning from assistant frontend to operations automation to research connectors; that could be intelligent search for the right wedge rather than drift. But there is no NanoCo-style exception here: no thousands of users, enterprise demand, acquisition offer, or other hard external evidence capable of overriding a crowded market and absent ownership.

At $49 per month, a one-person company could reach sustainability without enormous revenue, but Click reports no customers, revenue, gross margin, provider cost per query, burn, or runway. I therefore assign the economics criterion its exact 7/18 default. My 36-month base case is that the horizontal connector subscription is discontinued or pivoted back into a narrower operations-automation product rather than exceeding $1 million ARR. The verifiable flip condition is 100 paying businesses with six-month cohort retention above 80%, accompanied by gross-margin data showing that connector usage becomes more profitable—not less—as customers deepen usage. Until that exists, I am passing: there may be a useful product here, but I am being asked to finance aggregation without owned access, demonstrated conversion, or a named experiment.
