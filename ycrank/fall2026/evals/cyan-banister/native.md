# Native -- Cyan Banister Evaluation

Move the clock to 2032. A shop owner tells software what kind of business she wants to build, and it learns which customers to reach without borrowing her evenings. Native could help create that world. But generating and scheduling posts is only the first step; learning which ideas deserve publication is the harder transition. Norn names that problem clearly. The dossier does not yet establish the predictive improvement or sequence of unlocks that connects today’s product to that future. I will not give my own daydream the weight of company evidence.

The founders have earned more belief than the category label suggests. Benjamin Bekken made content before Native; Trond Hauga operated a marketing business; Håkon Gråsberg brings applied AI experience. More tellingly, they built Wepost together and reached a reported NOK80,000 MRR in September 2025 before winding it down. These people have already persuaded businesses to pay for help with this problem. That is meaningful operating tuition, although the record stops short of documenting years of independent technical experiments or exceptional recruiting.

Customers are also putting something real at risk: their businesses’ public voices. Native reports $2M ARR within six months, 2,200-plus paying customers, and over 40,000 published posts in an unspecified month. The named Briggs on Homes example covers August 20–October 2, 2026, making actual product use more tangible than a subscription count alone. I give that behavior substantial credit. I do not turn the reported 5.5% versus 2.9% engagement comparison into proof that Norn chooses better ideas; Native’s own research acknowledges selection and survivorship biases.

Here is the strongest argument for writing a check anyway: a mundane marketing tool could finance the collection of something much more valuable. Approval, editing, rejection, and subsequent performance might teach Native how different businesses exercise taste. That fits my pattern of finding concealed infrastructure inside an ordinary commercial beachhead. But the distinction matters: the terms document a collection mechanism, not a scarce asset or demonstrated predictive advantage. The API is a waitlist, and Blaze already offers a comparable strategy-through-publishing loop. Native has evidence of a useful business; its position beneath other marketing products remains unproved.

I also want to know what the agency bill buys before calling it a rent. The dossier identifies work owners would like to delegate, but no artificial restriction, quantified gatekeeper toll, or mechanism transferring bargaining power back to them. The $79 monthly plan may return time; that alone does not establish the institutional repair I look for. Nor does the evidence establish a fatal red flag. Training opt-outs and approval controls are constructive, and reliance on frontier models and Ayrshare does not demonstrate dependence on one closed model supplier. Founder control and data custody remain diligence questions, not findings against the team.

**I am neutral at 47/100 and would keep my money uncommitted today.** CosmicBrain AI illustrates the relevant exception: missing revenue could be overcome by a working prototype, longstanding founder obsession, and a concrete manufacturing thesis. Native has stronger commercial evidence, but the missing bridge is predictive capability; sales cannot supply it. My one verifiable flip condition is a prospective, independently auditable customer test showing that Norn’s accumulated feedback produces sustained gains in qualified leads per dollar against the same generation-and-publishing workflow without its ranking. That would make the learning layer something I could underwrite.
