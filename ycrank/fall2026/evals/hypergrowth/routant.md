# Routant -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Assuming strong initial product-market fit, Routant has a credible path to serving the same buyer across markets. The company describes a single MCP endpoint for software vendors and outbound-only gateways that let enterprises connect private systems and apply their own access policies. The same architecture could support the recurring job of governing vendor agents across markets. The extent of adaptation across customer environments remains uncertain. (The Idea, Key Risks)

The observed sales path leads to a demo. Vendors can reuse the MCP integration, and each enterprise still has to connect its systems and configure permissions before going live. Once deployed, ongoing access controls, approvals, and audit logs could make Routant a recurring part of production workflows. If Routant charged vendors for each deployment, a vendor could spend more as it added customers. The subscription model is an inference, and billing and account expansion remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

The public launch establishes visibility. Paid adoption and its pace remain unknown, so initial traction uses the default. Incremental contribution would depend on what Routant earns from the next deployment after gateway operations, logging, and any attributable sales, setup, and support work. Pricing and Routant’s share of that work are unresolved, so cost economics also uses the default. (Market & Traction, The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Countercase

Each new enterprise could require Routant to help connect private systems, configure permissions, and maintain the gateway in production. If that work recurs at similar intensity for every rollout, vendor reuse could generate more deployments while leaving Routant’s delivery costs tied to each one. The memo establishes customer-side setup and policy work, while the extent of Routant’s involvement remains unknown. The structural gate stays open because the evidence leaves room for customers to complete that work through a standard deployment process. (The Idea, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a software vendor use one Routant integration to bring successive enterprise customers into paid production through the same setup process? Deployment records and invoices showing that customer teams completed setup with minimal Routant help, that each rollout added recurring revenue to the vendor account, and that the revenue covered attributable rollout and serving costs would support moving to Reach out if the updated score reaches 65 with at least Medium confidence. Evidence that every rollout requires economically prohibitive bespoke work with no credible improvement path would change the action to Pass.
