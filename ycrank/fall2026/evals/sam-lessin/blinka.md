# Blinka -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Blinka reports moving more than $10 million in its first two months. What does buying a piece of that business cost? The dossier supplies neither the proposed post-money nor competing-term-sheet count. Pygma’s April 2024 SAFE and subsequent Antler participation establish financing history, not today’s price or capital scarcity. I will not invent an auction because YC is involved. I also cannot underwrite a cheap first check from undisclosed terms.

The strongest evidence is the customer reportedly managing more than 200 entities through one portal. That is a reason to consolidate financial operations, and potentially a painful workflow to unwind. Together with 25 pre-launch clients and named customers including Bricksave and Creditop, it gives Blinka more substance than an account-opening funnel. The reported $10 million establishes transaction activity; the 150-plus companies opening accounts do not establish retained customers. Blinka has evidence of workflow adoption, but not yet that it owns the relationships or differentiated access causing that adoption ([October reporting](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/asi-es-la-empresa-que-dos-colombianos-crearon-en-ee-uu-y-que-ya-ha-procesado-mas-de-us10-millones-en-pagos/)).

The founders fit my pattern of connecting systems that do not understand each other. Juan Pablo has legal experience and personally encountered the account-access problem; Sebastián has built lending software, and the brothers have now shipped a functioning payments product. Those are operating receipts beyond school names. But the dossier does not establish deep operating experience inside US bank underwriting or compliance. That matters because Blinka’s promise depends on Portage Bank, Third National, and Mural Pay continuing to support these customers. The inconsistency between advertised non-US access and the US-entity requirement in its terms is a concrete eligibility issue to resolve, not evidence of misconduct.

The bull case is uncomfortable for a pass: a neglected customer segment may already be pulling the product into a valuable operational position. If the 200-entity customer is representative, Blinka could own the daily interface while buying interchangeable financial infrastructure underneath. That is precisely the sort of translation layer I like. The counterweight is that Slash already bundles global accounts, payments, invoicing, and accounting automation. Blinka needs customer trust and workflow depth that survive a competitor matching the feature list. Founder-event sponsorships, the LegalOS partnership, and LinkedIn followers do not establish that advantage.

I checked the grounds on which I make exceptions to missing financing evidence: demonstrated conversion, cash economics, proprietary access, and unusually strong operating proof. Blinka’s live usage makes that check serious, but does not carry it. Payment volume is not revenue, and authorized service fees disclose neither realized take rate nor contribution profit. There is no margin, burn, runway, or seed-funded break-even plan. Equally, nothing establishes that another round is necessary. Economics therefore receive the missing-evidence default. The AI tag supplies no basis for either an automation premium or an AI-wrapper penalty.

I pass on this investment as documented, while taking the product seriously. No disclosed use-of-proceeds plan names the hypothesis, deadline, and measurable result this check would buy. My single verifiable flip condition is an executable round below $10 million post-money whose funded operating plan demonstrates cash break-even within that round’s runway, using actual customer-cohort economics. That would connect Blinka’s promising workflow adoption to both an acceptable purchase price and a business that can survive without another investor.
