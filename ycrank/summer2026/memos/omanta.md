## The Idea

- **Problem:** [Inferred]: Omanta targets patients who have exhausted standard options, face recurrence, or lack a diagnosis and who otherwise must coordinate evidence, laboratories, specialists, and treating clinicians themselves (provided YC company record, Aug 2026).
- **Approach:** Omanta operationalizes patient-specific programs spanning tissue logistics, diagnostic follow-up, data integration, multiomic analysis, therapeutic-strategy development, and a living evidence roadmap for the treating clinician (provided YC company record, Aug 2026).
- **Differentiation:** Omanta describes an end-to-end research program, compared with xCures’ medical-record structuring, Tempus’ molecular testing and AI insights, and Massive Bio’s clinical-trial matching ([xCures](https://xcures.com/company/), [Tempus](https://www.tempus.com/oncology), [Massive Bio](https://massivebio.com/), accessed Aug 2026; provided YC company record, Aug 2026).
- **Business Model:** [Inferred]: The labor, laboratory coordination, and case-specific analysis described suggest per-patient program fees or institutional service contracts, but no public pricing was found.
- **TAM/SAM:** The adjacent global precision-oncology market was estimated at $115.8 billion in 2024 with an 8.1% CAGR through 2030, while rare-disease diagnostics was estimated at $21.6 billion in 2024 with a 9.9% CAGR through 2029 ([Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/precision-oncology-market-report), accessed Aug 2026; [Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/report/global-rare-disease-diagnostics-market), accessed Aug 2026).
- **GTM / Distribution:** [Inferred]: Initial distribution likely depends on referrals from oncologists, rare-disease clinicians, patient advocates, and families seeking research beyond standard-care pathways.

## Key Risks

- **Clinical-decision-support classification:** Omanta’s described therapeutic-strategy and clinician-roadmap functions may fall inside or outside FDA device oversight depending on intended users and whether clinicians can independently review the recommendation basis ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software), Jan 2026); no public regulatory classification was found.
- **Clinical and laboratory validation:** Omanta describes multiomic analysis and diagnostic follow-up, but no public validation studies, laboratory accreditations, or laboratory partners were found, while CMS requires regulated human-testing laboratories to satisfy CLIA quality standards ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/quality/clinical-laboratory-improvement-amendments), accessed Aug 2026).
- **Patient-data handling:** The service requires medical, tissue, and genomic information, and HHS classifies individually identifiable genetic information held by covered entities or business associates as protected health information ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/354/does-hipaa-protect-genetic-information/index.html), reviewed Dec 2022); no public security or privacy documentation was found.
- **Website and domain availability:** Website not accessible at time of research, and the domain appeared in a recent verified-owner sale listing ([Atom](https://www.atom.com/name/Omanta), crawled Jul 2026), creating uncertainty about the public product-access point.
- **Adjacent-platform overlap:** Tempus, xCures, and Massive Bio already cover molecular testing, record integration, AI-derived insights, or trial access, while Omanta’s broader operational scope is the only publicly described mitigation ([Tempus](https://www.tempus.com/oncology), [xCures](https://xcures.com/company/), [Massive Bio](https://massivebio.com/), accessed Aug 2026; provided YC company record, Aug 2026).
