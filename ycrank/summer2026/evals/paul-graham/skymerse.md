# Skymerse -- Paul Graham Evaluation

I looked first for the moment something blocked Damian Szumski and what he did next. The dossier does not supply it. His experience as a licensed dispatcher and former airline-software operator explains why he saw the problem, and Skymerse’s functioning product proves he can build, but neither shows a named obstacle, a dated workaround, or the speed of his response. That is missing evidence rather than evidence of passivity. It still leaves the most important founder test at its default.

The technical proof is much stronger. Notamify has processed 3 million NOTAMs over 487 days and extracted 2,005,126 affected-element exposures. LOT Polish Airlines deployed it across its network, where processing takes approximately 30 seconds and produces 15–20 filtered alerts per day with delivery in under three minutes ([Notamify](https://notamify.com/lot-polish-airlines-notamify)). This is not a diagram for software that might exist after financing. It is a difficult aviation-data system running against real operational inputs. The measurements are company-reported rather than independently validated, but this is nevertheless Skymerse’s best evidence.

There is also a narrow well of users. Notamify reports more than 3,800 registered users and thousands of daily users, while LOT supplies one named enterprise deployment ([Notamify](https://notamify.com/)). Those users are dispatchers and aviation operators trying to extract applicable information from an intolerable volume of notices, not consumers casually experimenting with an AI wrapper. But the depth of the well remains uncertain. Skymerse discloses a $24.90 monthly plan without subscriber counts, revenue, retention, renewals, or growth rates, and its new iOS app has too few reviews for Apple to summarize ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/notamify/id6790844947)). Repeated use deserves substantial credit; registration totals do not prove willingness to pay.

The origin and the schlep fit. Szumski encountered flight operations through LOT, Lufthansa Systems, FLYR, Flightradar24, and his own dispatcher work. The product must reconcile NOTAMs, weather, procedures, aircraft limits, minimum-equipment lists, schedules, and operator manuals. That is precisely the sort of ugly integration work that keeps superficially similar products shallow. But ugliness alone is not a moat. Jeppesen ForeFlight introduced an aviation AI engine backed by its existing data and distribution, while Overwatch AI already spans many of the same operational inputs. Skymerse needs proprietary operator workflows, not merely a cleaner interpretation layer.

The weakest evidence concerns changes of route. The company publicized an ATC–pilot transcription assistant in 2025, centered the business on Notamify, and then expanded to end-to-end flight operations in July 2026. The dossier names no customer observation, failed hypothesis, or technical discovery that caused those changes. This could be rapid search, or it could be an expanding product noun in search of a sale. The earlier co-founder’s disappearance from the current YC listing adds another unresolved founder question. Skymerse avoids the more serious red flags—it has shipped before a ceremonial launch, the founder knows the buyer, and LOT appears to be a user rather than a hypothetical distribution partner—but I cannot award adaptability merely because the product changed.

The strongest objection to waiting is that the known-exception logic nearly applies: a working artifact plus unmistakable real-world pull can replace missing founder-process evidence. Few pre-seed companies have processed 3 million domain records or reached a network-wide airline deployment. The problem is that one named airline and self-reported daily usage do not yet tell me whether Skymerse is becoming an operating system or a useful feature that an incumbent can absorb. My 36-month base case is an independent aviation decision-support business at $2–5 million ARR, still constrained to advisory rather than authoritative flight decisions. I would not write the check today. I would flip if two additional named airlines paid for and renewed network-wide deployments after a documented product change triggered by their operational feedback.
