# Silica -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

Silica is attacking a place where better software cannot manufacture the missing information. If a robot estimates contact force from motor current, its learning system inherits that approximation. Measuring torque at the joint output could improve the physical signal available during demonstrations and operation. That is a sensible bottleneck to own. The investment question is whether Enrico Milletti can turn that insight into an actuator advantage that survives competing suppliers.

Milletti has relevant experience doing physical work: developing materials and manufacturing processes at Apple and serving as Artefact’s founding engineer ([founder profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/founders/enrico-milletti)). That earns more credit than degrees or investor endorsements. But the dossier does not identify a torque-sensing actuator he previously shipped, a manufacturing process with measured results, or customers who used his work in this exact application. His background supports adjacent specific knowledge; it does not yet establish mastery of this particular problem. There is no documented integrity or behavioral red flag. Uncertainty about execution should remain uncertainty.

Hardware fits my current investment direction, but physical existence alone does not establish defensibility. Silica’s specifications remain design targets; prototypes are scheduled for December 2026 and production for January 2027 ([Silica Buy](https://www.silicamachines.com/buy)). HEBI already sells integrated actuator modules, and Sensodrive offers torque-controlled joints with safety certification. Copying Silica’s code would leave its mechanical engineering intact, but the dossier has not established what competing manufacturers would struggle to reproduce. Calibration knowledge, yield, and durability could compound with production. Scheduled production earns no credit for those assets today.

The strongest objection to passing is that Silica could make useful force sensing affordable just as robot-learning software expands demand. Milletti’s manufacturing background is relevant to precisely that cost problem. This fits my pattern of backing physical infrastructure beneath expanding software capability. Yet the $18.62 billion robotics-actuator estimate describes a broad category, while Silica has not established an expansion path from its initial robot developers into specific adjacent buyers. Nor does the dossier show customers improving the product or distribution for one another. Each sale currently has a direct supplier relationship; a shared learning loop remains unproven.

I would pass at this point. Open preorders establish a route to purchase, but the dossier reports neither prices nor completed transactions, so direct value capture receives the absent-evidence default. My exceptions for financing before monetization depend on compensating evidence: a demonstrated exceptional prototype, unusually revealing founder actions, or an emerging user network. Silica has not yet supplied those overrides. Its small team and lack of revenue history are ordinary pre-seed conditions; the decisive gap is an unverified advantage against existing hardware. **I would reopen the decision on one independent robot-maker qualification report showing that a delivered Q Series matches a comparable HEBI module on torque accuracy and durability at a lower delivered cost.** That would turn affordability from positioning into evidence.
