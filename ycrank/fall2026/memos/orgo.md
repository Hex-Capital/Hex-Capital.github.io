## The Idea

- **Problem:** Developers deploying persistent AI workers must otherwise assemble virtual machines, desktop streaming, storage, isolation, and scaling themselves, which Orgo describes as months of infrastructure work ([YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/WBl-orgo-computers-for-agents), accessed October 2026).
- **Approach:** Orgo provisions isolated cloud desktops with browsers, terminals, persistent files, screen viewing, and programmatic mouse-and-keyboard control through its API, SDKs, CLI, and MCP ([Orgo](https://www.orgo.ai/), October 2026).
- **Differentiation:** Compared with Fly.io’s application microVMs and DigitalOcean’s VPSs, Orgo supplies desktop interaction primitives and persistence by default, according to its own comparison ([Orgo documentation](https://docs.orgo.ai/guides/migrations/overview), October 2026).
- **Business Model:** Monthly subscriptions cost $29 Starter, $99 Growth, and $399 Scale, with additional capacity purchases and enterprise volume pricing ([Orgo About](https://www.orgo.ai/about), October 2026).
- **TAM/SAM:** No public TAM/SAM data found for this specific segment.
- **GTM / Distribution:** The founder identifies demonstration videos, Greg Isenberg podcast appearances, and agencies selling managed business agents as acquisition channels ([founder interview](https://www.listennotes.com/podcasts/konstantin/10-billion-ai-workers-will-fFWBjY0k7z8/), October 7, 2026).

## Defensibility

- **Moat today:** [Inferred]: Persistent environments and reusable deployment templates could impose migration work, although public evidence establishes the functionality rather than measured switching costs ([Orgo templates](https://www.orgo.ai/blog/orgo-templates-and-triggers), October 19, 2026).
- **Future moat:** [Inferred]: Customer-specific templates, application configurations, and embedded desktops could deepen integration dependence, but retention attributable to these mechanisms remains unverified ([Orgo](https://www.orgo.ai/), October 2026).
- **Market structure:** [Inferred]: No structural barrier identified at this stage, given competing desktop sandboxes from E2B and Daytona ([E2B](https://e2b.dev/about); [Daytona](https://www.daytona.io/dotfiles/daytona-raises-60m-series-b-to-build-the-agent-cloud)).
- **Commoditization risk:** Open-source Cua Driver and Lume provide desktop-control and virtualization components that developers can deploy on their own computers or cloud infrastructure ([Cua](https://cua.ai/), October 2026).

## Key Risks

- **Competitive overlap:** Daytona offers overlapping persistent agent computers across operating systems, reducing feature exclusivity ([Daytona announcement](https://www.daytona.io/dotfiles/daytona-raises-60m-series-b-to-build-the-agent-cloud), October 2026).
- **Operational reliability:** Orgo disclosed payment-webhook errors that froze paid accounts and MCP failures, alongside subsequent fixes ([platform changelog](https://www.orgo.ai/changelog/orgo-ai), July–August 2026).
- **Sensitive-data exposure:** Hosted AI features can transmit prompts and screenshots to model providers; Orgo offers customer-owned provider keys and describes VM isolation as controls ([subprocessors](https://www.orgo.ai/subprocessors); [privacy policy](https://www.orgo.ai/privacy), October 2026).
- **Name ambiguity:** An unrelated member-management product also uses Orgo, requiring domain checks when attributing funding or customer metrics ([orgo.space](https://orgo.space/ask-orgo-ai/), October 2026).
