# OpenRelay -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

OpenRelay’s contested truth is that heterogeneous accelerator capacity can become fungible enough for an independent routing layer—not a cloud or chip vendor—to control inference delivery. What would prove this wrong? Production customers would continue pinning workloads to one provider because security, reliability, and model-performance differences overwhelm any routing savings. Processing more than 100 billion tokens weekly across 22 locations and 11 accelerator SKUs is a real experiment, and the reported B300 deployment cut costs by more than 25%. But the dossier does not name the prevailing belief being rejected or report cross-hardware reliability, retention, or latency results. The secret is plausible and partially tested, not yet demonstrated.

The founders are the strongest evidence. Prashant Patel built managed inference at Amazon Bedrock and orchestrated clusters exceeding 10,000 GPUs at Voltage Park; Jaden Wang built a half-megawatt data center and worked on HPC deployment at TensorDock and Voltage Park. They previously worked at the same company and cover both sides of the system: inference software and physical capacity. This resembles a company assembled around an operating thesis, one of my unstated positive patterns. Their current founder titles suggest commitment, and there is no disclosed part-time founder or outsourced core engineering. Still, prior collaboration duration, ownership, salaries, and secondary liquidity are absent. Missing evidence is not a red flag, but it cannot establish economic alignment.

Can this activity become a monopoly rather than merely efficient brokerage? Continuous benchmarking across 11 accelerator types could produce proprietary routing data, while provider density could improve availability and pricing. Yet 100 billion tokens measures throughput, not ownership. There are no provider counts, exclusive supply agreements, customer-retention figures, or results showing routing quality compounds with scale. RunPod, Vast.ai, Together AI, and the hyperscalers can all attempt the same abstraction, while OpenAI compatibility makes adoption easier but departure equally easy. OpenRelay fits the shift toward the complete AI production stack, but category importance does not confer value capture. At present the mechanism is operationally impressive and structurally unproved.

The economic engine is coherent: prepaid token pricing, per-minute dedicated GPUs, and a usage share for hardware providers. The projected $26.62 billion GPU-as-a-Service market is large enough, and the documented cost reduction gives customers a reason to try the service. But public prices as low as $0.05 per million input tokens require enormous volume, and neither revenue nor gross margin reveals how much of the savings OpenRelay retains. Distribution is weaker still. Self-service APIs, a $5 deposit match, enterprise demos, and provider recruitment name activities rather than a repeatable channel; there is no conversion rate, sales cycle, sales owner, or evidence that product usage lowers acquisition cost.

The strongest bull case is that the disclosed throughput already represents hidden product-market fit: a two-person pre-seed company does not casually operate across four continents and produce a quantified customer saving. If those tokens come from unaffiliated, recurring production customers rather than owned capacity, benchmarks and liquidity may already be compounding faster than the dossier shows. The known exception for a verified zero-to-one technical event does not apply yet, however. OpenRelay has shown scale and optimization, not a result competitors cannot reproduce. Nor does it trigger the cleantech-style coordination red flag outright—the system is already operating—but security certification, provider liquidity, enterprise distribution, and reliable failover still must mature together.

My 36-month base case is $12 million to $30 million in ARR by August 2029 as a capable multi-provider inference broker, but without evidence yet that it becomes the enduring control point. I would investigate rather than write a personal check today. The verifiable flip condition is a cohort of at least 20 unaffiliated production customers sustaining greater than 130% net revenue retention over 12 months while OpenRelay maintains gross margins above 60%. That would show distribution compounding into ownership rather than merely passing commodity compute through a useful interface.
