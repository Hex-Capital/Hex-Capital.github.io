# Mantle Machines -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition** is the strongest path. An equipment OEM could buy Mantle to bring machine-specific repair histories, remote diagnostics, and field dispatch into its aftermarket service operation. The founders say travel alone can exceed $5,000 when an OEM flies in an engineer. That claim identifies a potential saving. Ownership could be worth a premium if transferable repair histories and technician coverage deliver savings across the OEM’s installed base that it could only partly capture through a service contract. The memo documents Mantle’s launch and attributes the live customers and 250+ field technicians to the company. It gives no measured savings, first-visit fix rates, or evidence of buyer willingness to pay. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

A paid CNC deployment across several customers could show whether Mantle repeatedly reduces downtime and repeat visits while earning a positive contribution margin after technician time, travel, and parts. The reported deployments suggest a starting point. The memo leaves the test budget, runway, and rights to transfer customer contracts and repair records open. The adjacent maintenance buyout had an undisclosed price and supplies no basis for underwriting a sponsor bid for Mantle. The memo supplies no evidence of an eligible secondary, a path to public scale, or cash available for shareholder payouts. (Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Illustrative proceeds.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, and no follow-ons. A 5× gross cash return within eight years requires $2.5 million to the holder, or $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3%. At 2% and 4%, the equity hurdles are $125 million and $62.5 million, respectively. With illustrative net debt of $5 million, the 3% case requires about $88.3 million of enterprise value before any fees or senior claims. The memo provides no Mantle-specific terms or sale-price evidence. (Key Facts)

| Eight-year scenario | Gross holder cash |
|---|---:|
| $25 million sale less $5 million of net debt | $600,000 (1.2×) |
| $100 million sale less $5 million of net debt | $2.85 million (5.7×) |
| Growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public scale remains out of reach, and all available cash is reinvested | $0 realized, with shares still held |

A wind-down would pay the holder only from any residual after obligations and preferences. The memo gives no basis to estimate that residual. (Key Facts)

### Countercase

Roboworx already offers robot field service with telemetry-based predictive maintenance, giving OEMs a documented alternative to buying Mantle for robot support. Frequent visits and expensive parts could also erode Mantle’s flat-fee margins and leave an OEM better off contracting for CNC service. The evidence supports Watch because the documented substitute covers robot service and the CNC acquisition case remains untested. The Pass gate would apply if the strongest credible acquisition case proved structurally blocked or fell materially short of the holder’s cash target. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can Mantle show that a paid CNC deployment creates enough value for an OEM to support an acquisition near or above the illustrative $88.3 million enterprise-value hurdle? Measured service savings across several customers, backed by an OEM’s quantified case for ownership, would move the action to Reach out if the resulting price range could clear the holder’s target.
