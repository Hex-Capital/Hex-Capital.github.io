# Perceptual Labs -- Naval Ravikant Evaluation

“Building the next generation of visual intelligence” tells me the ambition. It tells me nothing about the founders’ specific knowledge. Perceptual Labs has two people, but the dossier identifies neither founder nor anything they have built, researched, or sold. I cannot distinguish two people who have discovered a difficult technical truth from two people exploring a category. Founder knowledge receives the missing-evidence default. The inaccessible website on October 29, 2026 supplies no evidence against their ability or integrity.

The decisive question is what survives when a competitor copies the implementation. Perceptual Labs could own a proprietary visual model or exclusive training data. Neither is established. The suggested dataset advantage is explicitly hypothetical; there are no verified data rights, performance results, or deployments. I also cannot classify the company as a commodity wrapper without knowing its architecture. Both dismissing the technology and awarding it a moat would require inventing the product.

Roboflow and TwelveLabs establish that substantial companies operate in adjacent territory. TwelveLabs’ July 2026 announcement of a $100 million Series B does not establish Perceptual Labs’ opportunity. Without an initial buyer, task, or deployment model, there is no causal path from its wedge into adjacent spending. Nor is there evidence that one customer improves the product or distribution for another. The permissionless contribution, fragmented-supply aggregation, and individual knowledge becoming scalable output that attract me are all unverified here. A visual-intelligence label establishes none of those mechanisms.

The strongest objection to passing is that this dossier measures public accessibility more than company substance. Two founders could have built something important before publishing it, and visual intelligence could involve precisely the proprietary models or physical-world learning I now prefer. My exceptions allow investing before revenue or a visible network when demonstrated founder ability and a working prototype carry the argument. Perceptual Labs might qualify privately, but the dossier contains neither substitute. Missing pricing and transactions therefore receive their defaults; they are not evidence of failed monetization.

I pass on the available evidence at **24/100**. There is no documented dishonesty, artificial financing pressure, or founder instability triggering a veto. There is also no affirmative basis for risking my own money. One verifiable milestone would reopen the decision: a customer-confirmed deployment of a founder-built visual model showing a measurable advantage attributable to training data Perceptual Labs exclusively controls. That would connect specific knowledge to an asset that can compound. Today, that connection is unknown.
