# Invertix -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Medium

### Company case

**Strategic acquisition by a large solar operator is the strongest path.** The memo documents TotalEnergies On accelerator selection and reports testing with TotalEnergies’ solar teams. Altenia’s CEO reported that work that previously took days took minutes with Invertix. Invertix claims its agents cover about 2 GW. A buyer could acquire the software and integrations to standardize fault triage, repair coordination, and production verification across its fleet. The ownership case is an inference. It depends on whether control of the software and its development would create incremental value beyond a license or an internal build. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

A paid rollout across multiple solar sites that measures reduced downtime, recovered generation, and deployment costs would make that case testable. Existing trials and the €1.7 million pre-seed make the next step plausible, while engineer-led integration leaves rollout cost and runway uncertain. IP ownership and the transferability of customer contracts and data rights remain unverified. The memo supplies custom quoting as a revenue signal and leaves acquisition pricing, buyer economics, and investor terms unknown. It provides no independently supported fallback that would pay this holder. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility, Key Risks, Key Facts)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** A $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation starts at 5% ownership and falls to 3% after 40% relative dilution, with no follow-on checks. The eight-year 5× objective requires $2.5 million in gross cash. At 3%, that takes $83.3 million in distributable equity proceeds, versus $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. The sale scenarios assume no net debt or other enterprise-to-equity adjustments and a simple pro rata waterfall with no preference claims. Equity consideration is cash paid at closing, with zero stock, escrow, earnouts, rollover, or compensation. The prices and timing are scenario assumptions.

| Scenario | Timing | Enterprise value → equity consideration | Gross cash to holder | Gross multiple |
|----------|--------|------------------------------------------|----------------------|----------------|
| Modest strategic sale | Year 5 | $40M → $40M | $1.2M | 2.4× |
| Strong strategic sale | Year 7 | $100M → $100M | $3.0M | 6.0× |
| Prolonged private ownership | Through year 8 | No sale or payout | $0 | 0× |

The strong sale clears the hurdle at 3% ownership and falls short at 2%, where it returns $2.0 million, or 4.0×. In the prolonged-private case, growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public-market scale remains out of reach, and cash is reinvested. The investor retains the shares and realizes $0 within the horizon.

### Countercase

An operator may get the same workflow improvements through a license. Invertix already deploys inside customers’ existing tools, and Metris Energy and Areg.AI offer overlapping workflows. Combined with engineer-led integration, that makes it plausible that a buyer could reproduce the benefit without paying an acquisition premium. The memo leaves open whether those substitutes can match Invertix’s results or cap what a buyer would pay. That gap supports Watch. The evidence establishes neither a structural price ceiling nor a probable prolonged-private outcome. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid multi-site solar rollout show that ownership creates enough incremental value over licensing or building to support a price that clears the holder’s proceeds hurdle? Buyer-validated results on recovered generation and net operating savings, together with a credible estimate of the additional value of owning the software, would move the action to Reach out if they support about $83.3 million in distributable equity under the 3% scenario. A rollout that verifies customer savings alone would keep it at Watch.
