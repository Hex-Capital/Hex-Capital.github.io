# Petrichor -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is Petrichor’s strongest path. A frontier AI lab could buy the operating team and workflow to secure dedicated capacity and shorten the time between submitting an experiment and receiving usable training records. Petrichor says its staff run experiments in parallel, customers own the resulting records, and building an in-house lab can take years. The acquisition case therefore rests on control of the workflow and capacity. The memo documents a public launch and YC backing. It leaves buyer savings, paid repeat use, and transfer rights for the workflow unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Defensibility)

A paid repeat production run benchmarked against the customer’s current provider would test whether Petrichor can deliver faster while maintaining reproducibility. The launch and described staff-run service make a limited test plausible. The memo gives no pilot budget, burn, or runway with which to underwrite expansion beyond that test. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

**Illustrative proceeds.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted to 3%, no follow-ons, and a 5× gross cash target within eight years. The holder needs $2.5 million, which requires $83.3 million of distributable equity at 3% ownership, $125 million at 2%, or $62.5 million at 4%. Using $5 million of illustrative net debt and a simple pro rata payout before investor taxes, a $25 million enterprise-value sale in year eight yields $600,000 gross (1.2×). A $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). The memo provides no acquisition pricing evidence or actual debt, fee, or preference terms. The modeled sale prices are scenarios. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

If growth plateaus, buyers stay away, public-market scale remains out of reach, and Petrichor reinvests available cash for eight years, the holder realizes $0 and retains the shares. The memo gives no basis to estimate a wind-down residual after obligations and preferences. It also leaves all four alternative routes unsupported by company-specific evidence of buyout cash flow, eligible secondary demand, IPO readiness, or distributable cash. (Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

A frontier lab can outsource and retain ownership of its training records under Petrichor’s stated model. Ginkgo reports that OpenAI already uses its cloud lab, and Emerald captures structured experimental metadata. Those alternatives give a buyer a concrete reason to compare service contracts before paying for ownership. Staff-run throughput and reproducibility could further erode the advantage at scale. This supports Watch. The memo establishes neither a structural barrier to acquisition nor a supported sale-price ceiling that would trigger Pass. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid, renewed production engagement show that owning Petrichor’s workflow would be worth more to a frontier AI lab than continuing to outsource? Move to Reach out if the customer’s benchmark shows a repeatable turnaround advantage at comparable reproducibility and its quantified benefit from ownership supports a sale range that could clear the modeled holder hurdle.
