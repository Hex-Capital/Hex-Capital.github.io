# RouterPlus -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Three million agent conversations a day could be an excellent place to discover expensive infrastructure failures. It does not yet tell me what RouterPlus makes radically cheaper. The consequence-of-success case needs a baseline: customer inference spending, the fraction eliminated, and the workloads that become economically possible afterward. None is quantified here. I see an existing need and potentially meaningful usage. I cannot yet price the upside as an option on an orders-of-magnitude change.

The technical evidence is narrower than the ambition. One customer reportedly moved from 4.8-second P95 latency to under four seconds after a dedicated deployment. That is useful production evidence, deserving more credit than a routing demo. It does not establish a 5–10x discontinuity, and the comparison is not against OpenRouter under equivalent workload, quality, and capacity requirements. OpenRouter already offers customer-specific evaluations and dedicated deployments. RouterPlus must demonstrate that its procurement machinery produces an advantage competitors cannot reproduce by adding another supplier.

The strongest argument against passing is that procurement itself could become the platform. Forty-plus provider relationships, pooled demand, and a reported customer operating at substantial volume could generate negotiating leverage and proprietary information about actual capacity. Narayana’s four years building Samsung GPU infrastructure and Bishoyi’s edge-AI deployment experience make this a credible team to pursue that possibility. My willingness to back execution-heavy marketplaces means scientific novelty is not an absolute requirement. But supplier access is not exclusive, the learning advantage remains inferred, and the customer’s payment status is undisclosed. The progression from EVO autoresearch to EVO Router in August and RouterPlus in October demonstrates product evolution; it does not document which belief changed after evidence or which complementary recruit removed a critical weakness.

The next dollar should establish whether this marketplace creates durable customer savings while retaining a margin. Prepaid token credits describe collection mechanics. Free BYOK inference describes pricing. Neither supplies provider costs, support burden, reservation utilization, or contribution economics. Held-out evaluations, customer approval, peak testing, and backup providers are sensible controls, but the dossier gives no budgeted experiment with a numerical threshold that could kill the business thesis. I would not interpret public hiring posts as proof of premature scaling. I also would not award risk-removal credit for a hiring plan.

I pass at this evidence level. RouterPlus has credible operators and an operational foothold; the missing link is a demonstrated mechanism that compounds into consequential cost reduction. The exceptions for extraordinary instigators or overwhelming societal consequence do not resolve that gap: neither is established here. One result would reopen my decision: an independently auditable, 90-day comparison across three paying production customers showing 10x lower delivered inference cost than an equivalent OpenRouter setup, with matched quality and service requirements and positive RouterPlus contribution margin. That would turn procurement access into evidence of a discontinuity.
