# Perit.AI -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Perit.AI could help make voice models reliable enough to handle consequential transactions. That is a worthwhile bottleneck: a model that misunderstands an interruption or transaction detail cannot safely replace an expert operator. But the dossier quantifies audio production, not the resulting expansion of reliable expertise. The $27.6B labeling market measures supplier spending; it does not establish how much human work Perit can eliminate or how many people gain access. I can see the potential consequence. I cannot yet underwrite its magnitude.

The strongest argument for investing is unusually concrete for this stage. Six major AI labs and “multiple millions in ARR,” although self-reported, suggest buyers already pay for the output. The company also reports 4,000+ delivered audio hours across nine locales. This deserves substantially more attention than an untested data-platform pitch. My broader investing pattern includes execution businesses, and a supplier embedded in frontier-model development could earn the right to build something much larger. Passing here risks overlooking an operating company while demanding a science experiment.

The decisive question is what improves as Perit delivers its next 4,000 hours. Its disclosed differentiation rests on recruiting practitioners, calibrating reviewers, and managing production through Foundry. Those activities can produce valuable data. They do not yet demonstrate a technical discontinuity against Mercor, Turing, or an AI lab’s internal process. Non-exclusive licensing is the default, exclusive contributor access is unproven, and the proposed accumulation of rubrics and reviewer histories remains an inferred moat. The public dataset—45 conversations totaling 5.10 hours—makes some output inspectable, but its scripted, clean English conversations cannot establish transfer to spontaneous multilingual deployment. The concern is the disclosed dependence on expert labor as differentiation, rather than merely an absent benchmark.

Kshitij’s prior profitable $2M-ARR business and Poorva’s engineering experience at Google, Atlassian, and Uber provide credible operating foundations. They do not document how the founders revise beliefs or recruit complementary owners for their hardest risks. I give that dimension its missing-evidence default. The same discipline applies to milestones: production throughput demonstrates delivery, but the dossier supplies no ranked kill risk, decisive experiment, budget, or stop condition. Eleven open roles do not establish premature scaling; I would need to know which uncertainties those hires will remove.

Perit’s custom quotes and separately itemized contributor compensation also leave the economic mechanism unresolved. There is no disclosed cost per accepted audio hour, reviewer utilization, rework burden, or customer saving at equivalent model quality. I cannot infer poor margins from that silence. I also cannot infer that software will make expert production progressively cheaper. The relevant red flag is an AI business whose advantage remains implementation labor. The known exceptions would require evidence of an exceptional instigator addressing a quantified giant value pool, or demonstrated exponential capability improvement. Neither override is established here.

I pass with my own money today. Perit may build a substantial supplier business; the evidence does not yet purchase an option on an orders-of-magnitude change in expertise costs. **My flip condition is one customer-audited, costed comparison showing that Perit’s reusable evaluation-and-data loop delivers at least a 5x reduction in total cost to reach the same prespecified transaction-error rate on unseen, spontaneous multilingual calls versus that customer’s existing workflow.** That result would connect the current commercial demand to a technical and economic mechanism worth backing.
