# Glen -- Elad Gil Evaluation

MCP’s November 2024 release makes Glen a catalyst-rich, curve-poor infrastructure bet: Glen is skating for a real puck, but its highest signal is market re-opening while its lowest is demonstrated revenue velocity. Support from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and AWS created a common interface for shared agent memory; Glen’s production deployment and phased waitlist still disclose neither revenue nor usage growth. That combination describes a plausible new control point, not something “just working” yet.

**Stripe** is the high rung on catalyst: APIs replaced merchant-account friction and turned a market shift into a distribution surface. Glen shares the mechanism because MCP can let Glen serve many agent clients through one standard rather than integrate separately with every interface. Glen diverges where it matters: Stripe’s catalyst connected immediately to paid transactions, while Glen’s MCP thesis is explicitly inferred and the dossier provides no agent-call volume, customer count, or evidence that organizations treat shared memory as a budget line.

**Lyft** is the low-rung warning on velocity: adoption does not rescue a platform when the economic curve cannot establish a winning position. Glen offers even less evidence on this axis—teams are admitted “in waves,” but Glen reports no revenue, repeat-usage cohort, named customer, or month-over-month figure. At pre-seed I do not require $1 million ARR, but an AI product with no disclosed curve receives my absent-evidence default; the distinction between a founder-managed waitlist and actual buying behavior remains unresolved.

Nikos Dritsakos sold SalesBop for $500,000 after 18 months, worked on agent tooling at Composio, and shipped AI products at FliteHouse. That is materially better than credential-only founder evidence. Over the next 18 months, Glen’s solo-founder structure could compound speed because Nikos is the unambiguous decision-maker, but Glen has not shown the Perplexity pattern—specific artifacts built between customer conversations and measured through user-feedback loops. Glen gets credit for prior independent shipping, not for an iteration cadence the dossier does not document.

**Glean’s $300 million ARR** defines Glen’s index-position counterfactual. Glen becomes an index on enterprise agents only if agents across engineering, sales, and support continuously read from and write to Glen while Glean, Dust, and model platforms remain destinations rather than owners of that memory. Glen would move into the top tier here if usage across multiple MCP clients created a permission-aware system of record; today Glen’s organization-wide sharing model, optional private mode, and absent migration or retention data make it an exposed middleware layer that well-funded platforms can absorb.

**Color Genomics** is the relevant single-anchor parallel: workflow infrastructure on top of a validated hard capability can avoid science risk. Glen similarly assumes that LLMs and MCP already work, leaving product, retrieval, security, and distribution execution rather than new model science. Glen therefore passes the single-miracle test better than most AI infrastructure pitches, although permission granularity and stale-context propagation could require simultaneous breakthroughs in trust and enterprise adoption if they are architectural rather than ordinary product work.

Glean’s $300 million ARR and existing enterprise distribution lead to my 36-month forecast: by July 2029, Glen is acquired by Glean for agent-memory technology while still below $5 million ARR, rather than becoming an independent enterprise platform. Glen’s catalyst attracts strategic interest, but Glen’s missing revenue curve and organization-wide permission model slow direct enterprise scaling; the same MCP interoperability that expands distribution also makes Glen easier for a larger search platform to replicate or purchase.

Nikos Dritsakos’s $500,000 SalesBop exit, Composio experience, solo authority, production deployment, Slack integration, and phased waitlist form the strongest bull case: Glen could become the shared-memory index for every enterprise agent, and **Mistral** shows that platform coupling can be a feature rather than a moat-killer. That case stings because Glen is positioned at the right layer during a real standards transition. The current dossier still loses on the Harvey test—no revenue, named customers, usage curve, retention, or evidence that Glen’s memory survives enterprise permission requirements. Six consecutive months above 20% month-over-month net revenue growth would flip my verdict.
