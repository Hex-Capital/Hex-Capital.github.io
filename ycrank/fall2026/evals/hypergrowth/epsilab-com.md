# Epsilab -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

**Path B, direct sales with account expansion,** is the strongest path. Frontier lab teams are the initial buyer. Epsilab says those teams need reliable ways to train and evaluate agents on multi-step work, and reports engagements with unnamed labs. The memo documents a project lead opening for scoping and delivering environments around lab research needs. Buyer budgets, paid activation, and repeat orders remain unverified. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

**Growth scenario.** If an initial paid environment reliably measures performance on real work, a lab could buy simulations for additional workflows. The same simulation tooling, tasks, and graders could help win and onboard another lab in a second geography if the workflow is similar and the data rights permit reuse. Local workflows or tighter data permissions could require fresh practitioner input, new tasks, and grader validation. The memo leaves cross-market demand, reuse rights, and delivery costs unmeasured. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Epsilab could scale as a custom research supplier. The project lead posting calls for automating environment creation, task generation, and evaluation. If each new workload still requires fresh data, practitioner time, engineering, and grader validation, delivery effort could rise in proportion to revenue. The memo also documents an example of a frontier lab building work-based evaluations internally. These risks warrant Watch. The Pass gate would require observed evidence of a structural demand ceiling or proportional costs without a supported path to improvement. (Key Risks)

A buyer-confirmed deployment, with a record of what was reused and the engineering, practitioner, and validation costs relative to the fee and the earlier rollout, would move the action to Reach out.
