# Dydema -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Dydema must make a useful command radically easier to produce before “map to anything” becomes consequential. Its demonstration has a theoretical ceiling of approximately 1.3 bits per second, with errors and filtering reducing actual throughput. What valuable activity becomes possible at that rate, for whom, and against which existing interface? The dossier does not establish that consequence. A $2.77 billion estimate encompassing medical BCI applications cannot answer the question for a consumer product that refuses clinical tasks.

The technical concern is specific. Wizard selects and hosts licensed pretrained models and classical classifiers; the dossier supplies no measured advantage over EMOTIV, Neurosity, or a developer assembling those components. Commissioned recordings could change that equation, but collecting data does not automatically produce defensibility. Dydema has not established corpus scale, reuse rights, or an improvement attributable to its recordings. The current throughput constraint is adverse evidence for everyday control. The missing incumbent benchmark leaves the proposed discontinuity unproven.

The most encouraging evidence is how the team handles a failed capability. Wizard reports 52% zero-shot imagined-movement accuracy across nine datasets against 50% chance and declines to offer that zero-shot task. That is a completed experiment followed by a product boundary. I give it credit for exposing a limitation rather than selling through it. But this establishes neither reliable calibrated control nor a plan for eliminating the largest remaining risk. There is no disclosed next experiment with a cost, deadline, and threshold that determines whether to continue.

The strongest argument against passing is that these founders could turn a modest developer service into the recording infrastructure the field lacks. Weaver combines neuroscience, computer science, and EEG/fMRI research; Oosthuizen brings machine-learning research and startup engineering. They previously built together, and a live API plus control demonstrations show execution. This fits my less public preference for scientific workflows that can become production platforms. Yet the decisive learning loop remains hypothetical: commissioned studies must generate reusable data that improves subsequent customers’ results. Their credentials also do not establish the particular founder behavior I score—material belief revision and recruiting complementary owners for named risks. I leave that dimension at its missing-evidence default.

The economics require equal discipline. At $0.003 per prediction window, one prediction each second would cost $10.80 per hour in API charges alone. That is an illustrative usage assumption, not Dydema’s disclosed operating requirement; overlapping windows and sampling frequency could change it substantially. The $1 creation and monthly hosting charges tell me little about the delivered cost per reliable command. Hardware, calibration time, inference expense, and recording costs remain unspecified. I cannot infer either economic parity or permanent economic failure from this price list.

I pass at 27/100. My exceptions allow an extraordinary consequence or an exceptional instigator attacking a giant established spending pool to outweigh an incomplete initial product. Dydema has relevant founders, but neither override is established here. Nor does the dossier establish deliberate imitation, irresponsible scaling, or misconduct; those red flags should not be invented. My single flip condition is an independently reproducible, held-out-user study showing that Dydema’s proprietary recordings enable a named valuable task at least 5x better than the best available alternative on fully loaded cost per reliable command, including hardware and calibration. That would connect its data business to a consequential technical advantage.
