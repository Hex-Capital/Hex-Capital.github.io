# Axonnic -- Sam Lessin Evaluation

Axonnic has shown me something worth testing, but I cannot yet identify the investment I would buy. Proposed post-money: undisclosed. Competing term sheets: undisclosed. Whether my check supplies genuinely scarce capital: unknown. YC, Boost VC, Sterling Road, and Oxford Seed Fund backing establishes neither an auction nor a bargain. I give financing silence the prescribed low prior. The more interesting question is what truth my money would reveal beyond the demonstration the founders have already built.

There is a credible bridge here between muscle sensing and industrial robotics. Williams has HD-sEMG research experience plus PCB and supply-chain work at Voxblock; Patankar has developed software for Oxford’s OxRAM quadruped. Those are operating receipts beyond Oxford credentials. Their company-reported demonstration—4.58° average joint-angle error, 50 predictions per second, and 78 minutes of training data—is concrete technical progress. This fits my willingness to fund people translating between disciplines inside difficult physical systems. But accurate human finger tracking and successful autonomous sewing are separate accomplishments. The dossier establishes the former; the latter still requires retargeting, policy training, and reliable execution.

The Italian manufacturing LOI and reported Perloro Packing Srl pilot make the bull case materially stronger. Axonnic has reached people with an actual manufacturing problem, and its Proception integration puts the software against real robotic hardware. At pre-seed, that is meaningful customer action. The potential asset is privileged access to demonstrations of difficult work: muscle signals could capture information that ordinary video misses. Yet customer-specific data rights are negotiated individually, and Hemlock already offers overlapping EMG capture. A useful pilot can become a bespoke engineering engagement without creating an owned data advantage. I credit the industrial engagement; I cannot credit a cross-customer learning business that the contracts have not established.

The strongest objection to passing is that an early check could finance exactly the right experiment: show that adding EMG produces an economically meaningful improvement in autonomous sewing over an otherwise comparable video-only policy. If that works, manufacturers could pay for results while each deployment improves the underlying system. But that is my proposed experiment, not Axonnic’s documented capital plan. There is no stated use of proceeds, deadline, or customer acceptance threshold. Nor are there disclosed margins, burn, or a route to break-even. These are missing facts, rather than evidence of bad economics. The known exceptions do not bridge the gap: this dossier supplies neither exceptional paid conversion and retention nor a proven founder-level financing structure. Nothing establishes auction pressure, equal-founder paralysis, or an assumption that automation errors disappear; I will not invent those red flags either.

I pass on the evidence available. Axonnic’s strongest signal is the founders’ ability to build across sensing and robotics; its weakest primary gate is the undocumented deal. The verifiable condition that would change my decision is a sub-$10 million post-money seed agreement funding a dated, customer-contracted sewing trial with a paid acceptance threshold based on autonomous task performance. That would connect price, a finite experiment, and an actual buyer. Today, the tracking demonstration and industrial interest justify attention, but they do not establish what my money buys or how the business survives without another round.
