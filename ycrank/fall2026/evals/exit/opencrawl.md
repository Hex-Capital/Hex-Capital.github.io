# OpenCrawl -- Exit Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Strategic acquisition is the strongest path. A coding-agent platform could buy OpenCrawl’s shared board and searchable session history to serve engineering teams that use multiple agent clients. Inference: ownership could be worth paying for if OpenCrawl brings retained customers and integrations that would take meaningful time to replicate. The memo documents a launched product, a public repository with 57 stars and 9 forks, and self-hosting instructions. The CTO reported a cross-user messaging interaction. Those signals establish early product activity and leave buyer benefit, paid retention, and acquisition pricing unmeasured. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Defensibility*)

A measurable milestone is sustained paid use by teams running multiple clients, with production data showing fewer duplicate investigations. The shipped product and the founders’ data infrastructure and agent experience make that test plausible. The memo records a $125,000 seed entry before the current product launched. Current runway, burn, and the capital needed to reach the milestone remain unknown. A product acquisition is conceivable, with commercial IP rights and customer permissions to transfer hosted transcripts still unverified. (*Market & Traction*, *Founders & Team*, *Key Risks*)

**Illustrative proceeds bridge.** Assume a $500,000 check at a $10 million post-money valuation, 5% initial ownership diluted by 40% to 3%, and no follow-ons. The 5× gross cash target within eight years is $2.5 million. At 3%, that requires about $83.3 million in distributable equity, versus $125 million at 2% and $62.5 million at 4%. Assuming $5 million of net debt and a simple pro rata distribution before investor taxes, the 3% case requires about $88.3 million in enterprise value. A $25 million sale yields $600,000 gross (1.2×), while a $100 million sale yields $2.85 million (5.7×). These are scenario prices. Actual ownership, debt, fees, preferences, and a supported sale price are unknown.

If paid adoption plateaus, buyer interest fails to materialize, public scale stays out of reach, and available cash is reinvested, eight years of private operation yield $0 realized and the holder keeps the shares. Wind-down proceeds would depend on the residual after obligations and preferences, which the memo gives no basis to estimate. Hosted pricing alone leaves sponsor financing and distributable cash unevidenced. The memo also supplies no evidence of an eligible secondary or a path to public resale. (*The Idea*, *Market & Traction*, *Key Facts*)

### Countercase

Agent Relay already offers cross-tool messaging and searchable sessions. Claude Code covers messaging and shared tasks for teams working within its own client. OpenCrawl’s listener-dependent push mentions weaken its advantage in mixed-client teams, and team-visible transcripts with incomplete secret masking can make enterprise adoption and transfer harder. A platform may be able to capture much of the benefit through native features, integration, or self-hosting. The overlap is documented, while its effect on buyer willingness to pay remains an inference. The memo establishes neither a structural block nor a credible sale price that leaves the holder materially short, so the Pass gate does not apply. (*The Idea*, *Defensibility*, *Key Risks*)

### Decisive diligence question

What acquisition price range would a coding-agent platform support after a production pilot shows sustained paid use across clients and a measurable reduction in duplicate work? Buyer-backed pricing tied to those results would change the action to Reach out if the implied distributable proceeds could clear $2.5 million for the holder within eight years. Pilot usage alone would leave the action at Watch.
