# Greypoint Industries -- Elad Gil Evaluation

Canada’s July 2026 Defence Drone Initiative created a newly funded buyer for low-cost counter-UAS systems; the controlling variable is whether Greypoint can convert passive emitter geolocation into reliable, economical interceptions under contested conditions. The dossier contains one successful geolocation test, a Canadian Armed Forces demonstration, and a signed government contract. That is enough to make the variable real, but not enough to establish the threshold: no range, accuracy, jamming resilience, swarm throughput, interceptor cost, or engagement success rate is disclosed.

The strongest evidence is institutional pull before the system is fully proven. A four-person pre-seed team has moved beyond prizes and briefings to a government contract and military demonstration. The contract’s undisclosed value and absence of repeat deployment prevent me from treating it as product-market fit, but defense procurement is sufficiently difficult that it carries more information than an ordinary pilot. My 36-month base case is Greypoint reaching $2 million to $8 million in annualized contract revenue through Canadian deployments while remaining an independent, specialized supplier.

LEGION-to-ARROW is also more substantial than a software-aware hardware product. Greypoint proposes a closed operational loop: detect emitters, fuse observations, geolocate the source, and cue an autonomous interceptor. Better sensing, autonomy, and field data could deepen that loop. The adverse counterfactual is severe, however. RF-silent or waypoint-controlled drones bypass the system’s distinctive sensing mechanism, while Anduril, Fortem, and Epirus already field alternative effectors backed by far more capital and validation. Greypoint has demonstrated one component, not the integrated engagement cycle at swarm scale.

The bull case is the known physical-market exception: a government procurement opening, a productized architecture, and a team with infantry, General Dynamics, TerraSense, Bosch, and uncrewed-aircraft experience. That is the same broad mechanism that made Anduril unusual, but the material difference is that Greypoint has not shown a margin reconstruction or production advantage. There are no disclosed interceptor costs, manufacturing yields, delivery times, or product-style gross margins. The $14.41 billion counter-UAS market and 22.4% projected CAGR establish urgency, not Greypoint’s value capture.

The founders have credible proximity to the problem, but the biography does not yet demonstrate the desperation or second-time learning I weight separately. They are students and early operators with relevant projects and internships; the dossier documents neither prior startup lessons nor a costly commitment that distinguishes this from a technically ambitious university spinout. I would not penalize them for being first-time founders, but I also would not convert domain adjacency into evidence of sustained execution under procurement and manufacturing pressure.

I would monitor rather than invest today. Greypoint clears the market-change gate narrowly because Canada has created an actionable procurement channel and the company has already entered it. It does not yet clear the economic or technical proof required for a physical defense business competing against validated platforms. One condition flips the decision: a paid CAF follow-on order issued after an instrumented exercise in which the integrated LEGION-to-ARROW system neutralizes at least eight of ten mixed RF-active and RF-silent targets under jamming.
