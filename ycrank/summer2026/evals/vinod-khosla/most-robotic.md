# Most Robotic -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

“Research lab for physical AI” describes a technology domain, not a consequence. Most Robotic has not named the physical task, labor pool, industrial cost, or population whose economics it intends to change. Robotics can address enormous value pools, but category-level possibility is not company-level evidence. I cannot price a 10% chance of a 100x outcome when neither the outcome nor its beneficiary is defined.

The two founders have the right raw material: MIT computer science, robotics research, and collective experience at NASA JPL, SpaceX, and AWS. Friends since middle school may also have unusual trust under technical stress. This is the strongest fact in the dossier. It supports the possibility that substantive work exists behind the silence, but it does not demonstrate fast belief revision, complementary recruiting, or ownership of a named risk.

The technical case is presently unauditable. There is no disclosed architecture, robot embodiment, dataset, benchmark, prototype, or deployment. Meanwhile, Skild AI reports an omni-bodied model and approximately $30 million in 2025 revenue; FieldAI reports testing across hundreds of industrial environments; Genesis AI has disclosed its foundation model, data engine, and robotic hand. NVIDIA’s open Cosmos 3 stack reportedly reduces training and evaluation cycles from months to days. Most Robotic therefore needs a discontinuity above increasingly capable infrastructure, not merely competence assembling it.

The strongest bull case is that secrecy is rational at this stage and that a technically exceptional team has chosen a market where success could replace large amounts of physical labor. A pre-product research lab can justify an option before revenue when exponential technical evidence is visible. But the dossier contains no company-specific evidence of that exponential: no measured capability, proprietary learning loop, committed research coalition, or difficult experiment already completed. Well-funded competitors make this omission more consequential because implementation alone will not survive commoditization.

Capital allocation is equally undefined. Real-world data acquisition and testing are acknowledged kill risks, yet Most Robotic supplies no cheapest decisive experiment, numerical threshold, cost, timing, or stop condition. There is also no bottom-up path from training, inference, hardware, supervision, and deployment costs to a price below the human or incumbent system being replaced. Physical AI without task economics can become an indefinitely financed science project.

I pass today. This is not an adverse judgment on two pre-seed founders for lacking revenue; it is a refusal to buy an option whose underlying claim has not been specified. One verifiable condition flips me: demonstrate one named physical task on customer-relevant hardware at least 5x better than the incumbent method on cost-adjusted throughput, with repeat performance and a credible path to unsubsidized deployment economics.
