# Agent 37 -- Peter Thiel Evaluation

Agent 37 has demonstrated that customers will buy cheaper agent hosting. What important truth does that establish? The candidate secret is that persistent, customer-specific agents can run economically on infrastructure incumbents systematically overprice. But the dossier names no competitor belief that Agent 37 has disproved. Lower prices are observable; a superior underlying cost structure remains unproved. I give the secret its missing-evidence default rather than mistake the research dossier’s silence for a founder’s lack of insight.

The monopoly question has a more adverse answer. E2B already offers state-preserving pause/resume; Daytona supplies broader execution environments. Preinstalled harnesses simplify deployment, but the dossier establishes no proprietary technology, exclusive data, or network effect. Agent 37 also uses Daytona for template builds and image imports. That dependency does not make independence impossible, but it sharpens the question: which essential layer does this company own? Open-source repositories with 651 and 191 stars establish attention, not control over a market. The documented overlap earns a below-default monopoly score.

The strongest objection to passing is that Agent 37 may be discovering distribution while competitors finance infrastructure. Boon deployed in three days after spending six months building on Render. ETEC+ migrated more than 30 existing clients onto a branded product. Those are concrete delivery outcomes supporting the white-label starter kit and founder-assisted onboarding. The founder-reported 100-plus companies strengthen the case that this solves an actual problem. My unstated preference for infrastructure serving the AI production stack applies here: an unglamorous deployment layer could become strategically important. But onboarding speed has not yet been shown to compound into exclusive access, customer dependence, or a repeatable acquisition channel with measured economics.

The price creates a second difficulty. At the published $4.76 monthly configuration, $100 million of annual compute revenue requires roughly 1.75 million continuously billed instances, before considering other resource consumption. A customer operating thousands of agents could change that arithmetic substantially; the dossier supplies no such expansion curve or gross-margin evidence. The company-wide “100k ARR” claim has neither a disclosed currency nor a Cloud allocation, so it cannot establish this engine’s economics. Memory-pressure restarts and the need to keep polling agents awake also identify where cheap capacity must be tested against production requirements. Agent 37 has one intelligible revenue engine, but its path to power-law value remains weakly supported.

Vishnu Krishnaprasad’s Amazon engineering and Quantcast management experience make infrastructure execution credible. They do not establish the founding structure I require. Full-time status, ownership, compensation, and responsibility for core engineering are undisclosed; the duplicated YC founder listing and zero team-size field establish no adverse conclusion. I retain the exact founder default. Nor do the known exceptions rescue this investment: customer deployments validate usefulness, but they do not directly remove the failed monopoly gate. There is no independently established technical discontinuity or realized economic result demonstrating that competitors cannot match the offering.

I would pass with my own money. Agent 37’s strongest evidence concerns getting customers operational; its weakest concerns retaining the value created afterward. One verifiable result would reopen the decision: an independently audited production comparison demonstrating a sustained tenfold cost advantage over E2B and Daytona at matched workload, durability, and availability requirements, while earning positive gross margins. That would turn a published discount into evidence of proprietary economics. Today, the customer benefit is clearer than the ownership advantage.
