# Jinko -- Hypergrowth Evaluation

**Action:** Watch  
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Usage-based expansion is Jinko’s strongest path. Developers building AI travel agents can integrate one API for live search, traveler-approved checkout, and post-booking servicing. Flights carry a published 1% booking fee, so an initial integration could generate more revenue as the customer routes more bookings through it. The workflow is portable in principle across buyers Jinko can serve under its existing supplier and ticketing arrangements. Jinko reports IATA and ARC accreditations and broad inventory coverage. Buyer geography and the work required to serve a second market remain unverified. (The Idea, Defensibility, Market & Traction)

The memo records a live search listing in Exa Connect, and a customer testimonial reports reaching live bookings in four hours. Those are early signs of a workable route to activation. The announced Twin booking integration separately suggests that a target buyer was willing to commit engineering time to test the workflow. For the API’s November launch stage, these signals make the path plausible. The first unresolved link is whether activated customers return and generate paid bookings on their normal cadence. The memo provides no repeat-use records, realized revenue, or measured marginal costs. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts)

### Countercase

An agent could integrate quickly and run many searches while completing too few bookings to cover search and servicing costs. In an illustrative scenario, a $300 flight yields $3 in booking fees. At a monthly average of 1,500 searches per booking, no excess-search fee applies, leaving at most $0.002 per search before checkout, servicing, and support costs. Supplier restrictions on fare markups limit one way to improve that economics. Actual search ratios and costs are unknown, so the structural downside gate remains untriggered. (The Idea, Key Risks, Key Facts)

### Decisive diligence question

Can two independent travel-agent startups use the documented onboarding flow and then generate repeat paid flight bookings over their normal booking cadence at positive marginal contribution? Booking and cost records covering search, checkout, servicing, and support would move the action to **Reach out** if both customers clear that milestone. Persistent unprofitable usage at representative volumes, with no supported way to improve it, would move the action to **Pass**.
