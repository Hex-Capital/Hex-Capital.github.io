# STASIS -- AGI Evaluation

**Action:** Watch
**Evidence confidence:** Low

### Company case

Path B, New Economies, is the strongest plausible path. STASIS aims to automate the work of noticing eye fatigue and deciding when to change tint, adjust a screen, or take a break. The potential new buyers are screen workers for whom manually monitoring symptoms and adjusting their setup throughout the day takes too much effort. The memo documents prototype coverage and compact eye-tracking hardware in another consumer product, which supports seed-stage feasibility. STASIS claims over 90% accuracy for fatigue and cognitive load, with the reference measure and validation protocol undisclosed. Both labor savings at equivalent symptom relief and demand from newly served buyers remain unproven. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

Under model parity, buyers could still value continuous on-face sensing and automatic tint and screen control in one device. STASIS reports training on 550 billion eye-data points, with ownership, exclusivity, and any resulting performance advantage unverified. The founders’ previous eye-sensing prototype and complementary signal-modeling and hardware experience support their ability to build and test the product. San Francisco and reported seed backing suggest a plausible mandate and stage fit. (The Idea, Defensibility, Founders & Team, Market & Traction)

The launch drew about 1.62 million views. Buyer commitment remains unknown because the memo gives no reservation count or completed sales, and the $99 deposit is refundable. In a first-batch scenario at the listed $299 price, contribution per delivered pair depends on hardware, prescription lenses, fulfillment, returns, and support. Those costs and production throughput are unknown, as is uptake of the optional $10 monthly subscription. (The Idea, Market & Traction, Key Facts, Key Risks)

### Countercase

Software-only blink training has shown symptom improvement for one problem STASIS targets. With cheap models and abundant capital, competing eyewear makers could also source inward-facing sensing components and build similar features. If those alternatives deliver comparable relief with similar effort, buyers have little reason to buy STASIS, and the company has yet to show control of a separate scarce complement under Path A. The evidence establishes a credible substitute for part of the outcome. Whether it matches the full workflow remains open, so the Path B override has yet to trigger. (Defensibility, Market & Traction, Key Risks)

### Decisive diligence question

Can a paid first-batch field trial show that screen workers who previously skipped active fatigue management spend materially less time monitoring symptoms and adjusting their setup for equivalent or better relief, and still choose to keep STASIS at the full first-batch price when given an alternative with comparable model capabilities? Measure relief against a defined reference and count setup, false alerts, manual corrections, and support. A positive result would support Reach out if the updated evaluation clears the score and confidence gates. Comparable outcomes and effort with the alternative, or weak paid retention despite a measured benefit, would move the action to Pass absent a separate supported Path A case. (The Idea, Defensibility, Key Risks)
