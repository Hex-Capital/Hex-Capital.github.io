# Waybill -- Vinod Khosla Evaluation

Waybill is attacking real friction, but the dossier does not quantify a consequential cost collapse. Hardware teams lose time across catalogs, resellers, WhatsApp, spreadsheets, freight, and customs, yet the only market baseline is a broad $10.1 billion procurement-software category growing 10% annually. There is no estimate of engineering hours recovered, inventory eliminated, procurement cost reduced, or factories unblocked. This is an operating headache with an unproven societal multiplier, not yet a 100x consequence.

The product combines quoting, payment, freight, customs, delivery, inventory, and invoice reconciliation. Breadth is useful; it is not technical discontinuity. Cofactr, Forge, Jiga, and Luminovo already cover overlapping workflows, while Waybill publishes no cycle-time, landed-cost, accuracy, or reliability benchmark against them. Its supplier network and accumulated price and lead-time histories could eventually create a learning loop, but two weeks of operation cannot establish one. Today the differentiation is implementation labor and responsibility transfer.

The founders are the strongest evidence. Rishi Laddha handled aerospace procurement, supply chain, and government relations at LAT Aerospace; Tushar Goyal built inventory and demand-forecasting systems used across more than 400 Indian D2C brands. That is complementary domain architecture around the actual workflow, not generic AI résumés. But the dossier contains no observed belief revision, difficult recruit already won, or evidence that the team changed its approach after disconfirming data. An advertised founding-engineer role is intent, not recruiting proof.

The requests for an obsolete RF component, 20 tons of aerospace-grade titanium, and four Cessna aircraft show unusual demand breadth after only two weeks. They also expose the kill risk: Waybill may be accepting commodity, authenticity, working-capital, customs, and fulfillment risks simultaneously. Broker vetting, licensed customs brokers, fallback suppliers, and shipment tracking are sensible mitigations, but there is no ranked risk, decisive experiment, failure threshold, or capital budget. More transactions could amplify liabilities faster than they create proprietary data.

The bull case is stronger than the score alone suggests. An execution-heavy procurement service spanning India, China, and Southeast Asia could use difficult orders to build supplier intelligence, then become the system of record for hardware teams. That resembles the relevant **Block** exception: an operationally modest initial wedge can expand into consequential infrastructure. The material difference is that Waybill has not demonstrated a giant captured value pool, repeat usage, or platform economics. The unstated portfolio pattern supports operational marketplaces, but the red flag is scaling sales and fulfillment before proving the largest risk-removal mechanism. No known exception overrides that absence today.

I would pass. My verifiable flip condition is a cohort of at least 20 repeat hardware customers showing a measured 5x reduction in procurement cycle time at positive contribution margin, including freight, customs failures, broker losses, and working-capital cost. That single result would convert Waybill from a capable managed service into evidence of a compounding procurement platform.
